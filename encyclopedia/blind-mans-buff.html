Blind man’s buff

A blindfolded player tries to catch the others and guess who they are. Goya painted it, and it was already played in ancient Greece.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Blind man’s buff is one of the oldest known group games. The Greek writer Julius Pollux, in the 2nd century, describes similar games played by Greek children, such as the “bronze fly”, in which a blindfolded child tried to catch the others.

In 17th- and 18th-century Europe it also became fashionable among adults as a pastime at country parties. Francisco de Goya painted Blind Man’s Buff (La gallina ciega) in 1788 and 1789, a tapestry cartoon now in the Prado Museum, in which a group of young people play in a circle on the banks of the river Manzanares.

Before starting, the blindfolded player is spun round while a traditional song is sung, so that they lose their bearings. Then they have to catch someone and guess who it is by touching their face.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 18th century the European aristocracy idealised country life, and popular games like blind man’s buff appeared in paintings, tapestries and fans. Goya painted this cartoon as a model for a tapestry intended for the royal palace of El Pardo, near Madrid.

Today it is still a typical game at children’s parties and birthdays, and a fun way to exercise the senses of touch and hearing.

📖 How to play

  • One player is blindfolded; in Spanish they are the “blind hen”.
  • The others spin them round and sing: “Little blind hen, what have you lost?”.
  • The blind hen tries to catch someone while the others come close and dart away without being caught.
  • When they catch someone, they have to guess who it is by touching their face or hair.
  • If they guess right, the player caught becomes the blind hen.

🎭 Anecdotes

In Goya’s painting, elegant young people play dressed as majos, in the popular Madrid style, which was very fashionable among the aristocracy of the time.

The full Spanish song goes: “Little blind hen, what have you lost? A needle and a thimble. Then turn round three times and you’ll find it.”

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Goya’s painting, the players form a circle holding hands and the blind hen holds a wooden spoon to touch someone.
  • In Spanish it is called gallina ciega, “blind hen”, and in German Blindekuh, “blind cow”.
  • In France it is called colin-maillard, after the name of a supposed blind warrior.
  • In some versions the blind hen cannot move and the others have to pass within reach.
