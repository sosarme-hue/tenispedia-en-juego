Bottle caps (chapas)

A street game with bottle caps flicked with a finger in races or football matches drawn on the ground.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Bottle caps, or crown corks, the ones on soft-drink and beer bottles, were invented in 1892 in the United States. Very soon children began to use them to play, because they were free and everywhere.

In Spain bottle caps were one of the most popular street games of the 20th century. A circuit was drawn in chalk on the pavement and the caps were flicked with a finger, as if they were cars or cyclists in the Vuelta.

Children decorated the inside of the caps with the faces of cyclists or footballers cut out of trading cards and newspapers, and filled them with wax or plasticine to make them heavier.

🏛️ Historical context

In post-war Spain and in the 1960s toys were expensive and children played in the street with whatever was to hand: bottle caps, marbles, spinning tops, elastics and trading cards.

Bottle-cap races imitated the Vuelta a España and the Tour de France, which were followed on the radio and later on television, and many caps carried the faces of cyclists such as Bahamontes or Induráin.

📖 How to play

  • A circuit is drawn in chalk on the ground.
  • Taking turns, you flick your cap with a finger.
  • If the cap leaves the circuit, it goes back to where it was.
  • The first to reach the finish wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The inventor of the crown cork, William Painter, advised a young salesman to invent something that would be used and thrown away. That young man, King Camp Gillette, invented the disposable razor blade.

Some villages still organise bottle-cap cycling races, with stages and a jersey for the winner.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The crown cork has 21 teeth, a number that has stayed the same for more than a century.
  • In Argentina and other countries they are called “tapitas”.
  • The best caps came from glass soft-drink bottles.
  • Bottle-cap football was also played, with goals made of wire.
