Old Maid (la mona)

A children’s pairs game: you have to get rid of all your cards and not be left with the one without a pair, the “mona”.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Old Maid is one of the first card games children learn. It is based on making pairs and drawing cards from your neighbour, and whoever is left at the end with the unpaired card loses.

Very similar games exist all over Europe: in England it is called Old Maid, in Germany Schwarzer Peter (“Black Peter”) and in France Vieux garçon or Pouilleux. In the 19th century special illustrated decks were published for playing these versions.

In Spain it is played with the Spanish deck with one card removed, so that its partner is left alone; the Spanish name, la mona, means “the monkey”. It is a game of pure luck and poker faces: you have to keep a straight face when you hold the mona so that your neighbour takes it.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 19th century, with colour printing and the boom in toys, card makers began selling children’s decks with drawn characters for playing Old Maid and other simple games.

For many Spanish children it was their first contact with the deck, before moving on to brisca or cinquillo.

📖 How to play

  • One card is removed from the deck, for example a jack, and all the others are dealt.
  • Each player discards any pairs they have (two cards of the same number or face).
  • Taking turns, each player draws a card, without looking, from the hand of the player on their right. If it makes a pair, they discard it.
  • Whoever runs out of cards is safe.
  • The player left with the last card, the mona, loses.

🎭 Anecdotes

Experienced players put the mona in a particular spot in their hand, sticking out a little, to tempt their opponent to take it. Clever opponents do exactly the opposite.

The Spanish expression “quedarse con la mona” (to be left with the monkey) is sometimes used outside the game to say that someone has drawn the short straw.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In the German version, Schwarzer Peter, the loser sometimes has a moustache or a smudge painted on their face with a burnt cork.
  • It can be played with any deck, as long as only one card is removed.
  • It is a game of pure luck: the player’s decisions hardly affect the result.
  • In English it is called Old Maid because the card removed is a queen.
