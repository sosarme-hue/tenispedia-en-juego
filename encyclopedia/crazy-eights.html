Crazy Eights and colour-matching discard games

A family of games in which you get rid of your cards by playing one of the same suit or number as the last.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Discard games in which each card must match the previous one in suit or number were played in Europe with ordinary decks. In Germany it is known as Mau-Mau, and in the United States as Crazy Eights, because eights act as wild cards to change suit.

In 1971 Merle Robbins, a barber from Reading, Ohio, created with his family a version with its own four-colour deck and special cards. He sold it in 1972 to a group of businessmen from Illinois and, after passing through several companies, it was bought by the toy maker Mattel in 1992. It became one of the best-selling card games in the world.

The rule of warning when you have only one card left, with a penalty if you forget, is one of the most fun moments of these games and has been copied in many variants.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 20th century discard games became the most popular among children and young people because they are quick. Crazy Eights was played with the French deck by American soldiers in the Second World War, and in Germany Mau-Mau was the typical playground game.

Merle Robbins’s version arrived just when American families were looking for simple, colourful games. By the 1990s it was sold all over the world and had been translated into dozens of languages.

📖 How to play

  • Each player receives several cards (usually 7).
  • You play a card that matches the top of the pile in colour or number.
  • Special cards make others draw, skip turns or reverse direction.
  • Wild cards let you change colour.
  • The first to run out of cards wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Merle Robbins and his family mortgaged their house to make the first 5,000 decks, which they sold in the family barbershop and at petrol stations.

Arguing about house rules, such as whether draw cards can be stacked, is a classic of these games. A big toy company once posted on social media that stacking was not in the official rules, causing a huge stir.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Merle Robbins sold the rights to his game for 50,000 dollars plus a share of sales.
  • In Crazy Eights an eight lets you change to any suit.
  • In Germany Mau-Mau has its own phrases you must say when you win.
  • There are versions played with the Spanish deck.
