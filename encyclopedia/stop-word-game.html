Stop (Tutti frutti, Basta)

A pencil-and-paper game in which you write words from several categories that begin with a given letter.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Stop, also called Basta, Tutti frutti or Mercado, is a pencil-and-paper game very popular in Spain and Latin America. A letter is chosen at random and each player must write a word for each category (name, animal, thing, country, fruit…) beginning with that letter.

Its origin is not documented, but similar games appear in Germany (Stadt, Land, Fluss) and other European countries at the beginning of the 20th century. It was played at school and at family gatherings.

In 1988 a board game based on the same idea, with letter dice and category cards, was sold in the United States, and today there are mobile versions too.

🏛️ Historical context

In 20th-century schools pencil-and-paper games were the entertainment of rainy days and long journeys. All you needed was a sheet of paper, a pencil and a willingness to think.

The game builds vocabulary and general knowledge, which is why many teachers use it in language classes.

📖 How to play

  • A letter is chosen at random.
  • Each player writes a word for each category with that letter.
  • The first to finish says “Stop!” and everyone stops writing.
  • Points are counted: more if nobody else wrote the same word.

🎭 Anecdotes

To choose the letter, in many places one person recites the alphabet under their breath and another says “stop!” at any moment.

Difficult letters, such as K, Ñ or X, are usually removed beforehand so that the game is not impossible.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Germany the game is called Stadt, Land, Fluss: city, country and river.
  • In Argentina and Uruguay it is called Tutti frutti.
  • In Mexico it is known as Basta.
  • Some classic categories are first name, surname, animal, fruit, country, colour and thing.
