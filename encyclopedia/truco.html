Truco

A card game of bluffing and signals, very popular in Argentina, Uruguay and other countries, and a sibling of Valencian truc.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Truco descends from truc, a card game played in Valencia, Catalonia and the Balearic Islands since the Middle Ages. It is already mentioned in 15th-century Valencian texts, and it is still very popular in the Valencia region today.

Spanish emigrants took it to the River Plate, where it became the national card game of Argentina and Uruguay. It is played with the 40-card Spanish deck, without eights or nines.

It is a game of bluffs, signals and witty sayings: players call “truco”, “retruco”, “envido” or “flor”, and accompany their moves with verses and jokes to unsettle their opponents.

🏛️ Historical context

In 15th-century Valencia truc was a tavern game, like dice. Some documents of the time mention fines for those who played it for money.

In the 19th century, in the Argentine and Uruguayan countryside, truco was the pastime of the gauchos, along with the payada, improvised song. That is why it has so many verses and set phrases.

📖 How to play

  • Three cards are dealt to each player.
  • Three hands are played, and whoever wins two takes the round.
  • You can call “envido” for the points of your cards and “truco” to raise the stakes.
  • The cards have a special ranking, with the ace of swords as the highest.

🎭 Anecdotes

In the gaucho poem Martín Fierro (1872), one of the most important works of Argentine literature, there are games of truco in the pulperías, the country shop-taverns.

Truco signals, such as raising your eyebrows for the ace of swords or winking for the ace of clubs, are allowed between partners, but opponents may try to spot them.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Argentine truco the four highest cards are the ace of swords, the ace of clubs, the seven of swords and the seven of coins.
  • “Flor” is called when you hold three cards of the same suit.
  • Valencian truc has somewhat different rules from Argentine truco.
  • A game is normally played to 30 points.
