Snake

Steering a snake that grows every time it eats, without hitting the walls or its own tail.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The idea of Snake was born in 1976 with Blockade, an arcade machine from the American company Gremlin in which two players left a trail behind them and had to avoid crashing into it.

In the 1980s and 1990s many versions appeared for personal computers, in which a snake ate dots and grew. One of the best known was Nibbles, included with the MS-DOS operating system in 1991.

In 1997 the Finnish engineer Taneli Armanto programmed Snake for the Nokia 6110 phone. It was one of the first mobile games, and it was installed on hundreds of millions of phones, making it one of the most played games in history.

🏛️ Historical context

In the late 1990s mobile phones began to reach everyone. They had no colour screen or internet, but they came with a simple game or two to pass the time.

Snake was many people’s first contact with video games, and it opened the door to mobile games, which today are the biggest part of the industry.

📖 How to play

  • The snake keeps moving in one direction.
  • The player changes direction with the arrow keys.
  • Every time it eats, the snake grows.
  • The game ends if it hits a wall or its own body.

🎭 Anecdotes

Taneli Armanto programmed Snake with very little memory and a screen of just 84 × 48 pixels. He has said he never imagined that so many millions of people would play it.

In 2017, when Nokia relaunched a classic phone model, one of the most talked-about pieces of news was that it came with a new version of Snake.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Snake on the Nokia 6110 had a two-player mode over infrared between two phones.
  • Filling the board completely with the snake is almost impossible.
  • On Ludicopedia you can play our own version of Snake.
  • Many programmers make it as their first exercise when learning to program video games.
