Tarot (card game)

Before it was used for fortune-telling, tarot was a card game of the Italian nobility.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Tarot decks were born in the courts of northern Italy around 1440, such as that of the Visconti-Sforza in Milan. They added to the ordinary deck a series of illustrated cards called trumps, used for playing tricks.

For centuries tarot was just a game, very popular in France, Austria and central Europe, where it is still played (in France it is called jeu de tarot).

Its use for fortune-telling does not appear until the end of the 18th century, when the Frenchman Antoine Court de Gébelin claimed, without evidence, that it came from ancient Egypt.

🏛️ Historical context

Tarots were born in the courts of northern Italy, in Milan and Ferrara, around 1440. They were luxury hand-painted decks for powerful families such as the Visconti and the Sforza, with a series of allegorical cards added to the four suits: the trumps, which represented virtues, planets and characters.

For three centuries tarot was just a trick-taking card game, very popular in Italy, France and central Europe. Its esoteric use began in Paris in 1781, when the scholar Antoine Court de Gébelin claimed, without evidence, that the cards held the secret wisdom of ancient Egypt.

📖 How to play

  • The deck has 78 cards: 56 ordinary cards, 21 trumps and the Excuse.
  • Trumps beat ordinary cards.
  • It is a trick-taking game, with one player against the others.
  • Court cards and some trumps score points.

🎭 Anecdotes

The Visconti-Sforza decks, none of which survives complete, are spread between museums in New York, Bergamo and Milan, and are considered works of Renaissance art.

In France, Austria and Hungary tarot is still played as a card game, with no connection at all to fortune-telling. French tarot even has a federation with championships.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The Visconti-Sforza tarot cards are among the oldest surviving playing cards.
  • The tarot deck has 78 cards: 22 trumps or major arcana and 56 suit cards.
  • The Fool, the unnumbered card, is the ancestor of the joker.
  • Tarocchi was the Italian name of the game in the 16th century.
