🎄 Games for Christmas

Christmas is the time of year when families play together most. There is free time, several generations gather round the table and everyone feels like doing something together after the meal. It is a very old custom, with roots going back to ancient Rome.

🏛️ A very old tradition

In ancient Rome, the Saturnalia were held at the end of December, a festival in which gifts were exchanged and people were allowed to play dice for money, something forbidden for the rest of the year. Many customs of that festival passed into the winter celebrations of Christian Europe.

In Spain, the Christmas Lottery draw has been held since 1812, and it has its origin in the lottery, a game born in Italy. For many families, the morning of 22 December, listening to the children sing out the numbers, is the real start of Christmas.

🎲 Board games for the whole family

The game of the goose and parcheesi are the kings of the Christmas table, because grandparents and grandchildren play together and nobody has an advantage. Dominoes and bingo, with cards and chickpeas to cover the numbers, are other classics of the season.

For those who prefer something more modern, trivia games, draw-and-guess games and forbidden-word games work very well with large groups and cause lots of laughter.

🃏 The holiday deck

In many Spanish homes, Christmas is the season of julepe, seven and a half and tute, played with chickpeas, beans or counters instead of money. The youngest play Old Maid and el burro, which can be learned in a minute.

Chinchón and brisca also have their place, and in Aragon there is always a game of guiñote.

🕎 Spinning tops and other traditions

During the Jewish festival of Hanukkah, also celebrated in December, children play with a four-sided spinning top called a dreidel, with a Hebrew letter on each side. Depending on the letter that comes up, the player wins, loses or shares out the chocolate coins in the pot.

In many Latin American countries, picture-card lotería is played during the Christmas festivities, and in some regions of Spain villages organise card or skittles tournaments as part of their winter festivals.

The games in this article

🪿
16th century

Game of the Goose

A 63-square spiral track with geese, bridges, a well and a skull. Pure chance and tradition.

🎲
16th century

Parcheesi (ludo)

A race game with dice and four pieces per player; in Spain, parchís is the great family classic.

🁫
12th century (China); 18th century (Europe)

Dominoes

A game of rectangular tiles with spots, joined end to end by matching numbers.

🎱
1530

Bingo and lotteries

Games of chance with cards and drawn numbers, heirs to the Italian lottery.

🎟️
15th–16th centuries

Lottery

Number draws with prizes, from Renaissance lotteries to Spain’s famous Christmas draw.

🥃
19th century

Julepe

A trick-taking card game in which anyone who plays and fails to win at least two tricks “takes a julepe” and has to pay.

7️⃣
19th century

Seven and a half

The “twenty-one” of the Spanish deck: you have to reach seven and a half without going over.

🐒
19th century

Old Maid (la mona)

A children’s pairs game: you have to get rid of all your cards and not be left with the one without a pair, the “mona”.

🐴
20th century

Donkey (el burro)

A fast, noisy card game: you have to collect four matching cards by passing them to your neighbour, and whoever loses collects letters until they spell BURRO (donkey).

🧠
20th century

Trivia games

Knowledge games and quizzes, from 1930s radio to the great board games.

🎨
1985

Draw-and-guess games

One player draws a word without speaking or writing and their team has to guess it before time runs out.

🤐
1989

Forbidden-word games

You have to get your team to guess a word without saying the word itself or the five forbidden words that go with it.

🌀
c. 3500 BC

Spinning top

A wooden top with a metal tip that spins when you throw it with a string.
