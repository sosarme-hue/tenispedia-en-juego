Pétanque

A game of throwing metal balls as close as possible to a small ball, invented in Provence in France.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Games of throwing balls at a target are very old: the Greeks and Romans already threw stones and balls. In Provence people played jeu provençal, in which you had to take three steps before throwing.

In 1907, in the town of La Ciotat, a player called Jules Lenoir, who had rheumatism and could not run, began throwing without moving, with his feet together. Thus pétanque was born; its name comes from the Provençal pè tancat, “feet together”.

In the 20th century it spread throughout France and the world. In Spain it is very popular among older people, in parks and squares, and there is a federation with championships.

🏛️ Historical context

At the beginning of the 20th century, Provence was a region of small towns where social life revolved around the square and the café. Ball games were the men’s main entertainment on Sundays.

After the Second World War pétanque became a symbol of the easy-going life of the south of France, and emigrants and holidaymakers took it to other countries.

📖 How to play

  • A small ball, the jack, is thrown 6 to 10 metres away.
  • Each player throws their metal balls trying to get them close to the jack.
  • You can hit your opponent’s balls to knock them away.
  • The team with balls closest to the jack scores points, and the game is played to 13.

🎭 Anecdotes

Losing 13–0 at pétanque is called “faire Fanny” in France. According to tradition, the loser must kiss the bottom of a figure of a woman called Fanny that hangs in many clubs.

The first balls were wooden studded with nails; metal balls appeared in 1927.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • You throw with your feet together inside a circle drawn on the ground.
  • The jack is called cochonnet, “piglet”, in French.
  • The balls weigh between 650 and 800 grams.
  • There have been world championships since 1959.
