Seven and a half

The “twenty-one” of the Spanish deck: you have to reach seven and a half without going over.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Seven and a half (siete y medio) is a betting game with the Spanish deck in which you have to get as close as possible to seven and a half points without going over. It is related to twenty-one and to the Italian sette e mezzo.

Cards from the ace to the seven are worth their number, and the face cards (jack, knight and king) are worth half a point. One of the face cards, often the king of coins, can act as a wild card.

It is a classic Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve game in many Spanish families, played for cents, chickpeas or sweets.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 19th century, betting games like seven and a half were very common in village casinos and at fairs. The law pursued them, but at home and at Christmas people played without money or for small stakes.

Its simplicity made it one of the first card games children learn in Spain, along with brisca and cinquillo.

📖 How to play

  • One person acts as the bank.
  • Each player receives a card and asks for more until they stand.
  • Face cards are worth half a point and the rest their number.
  • Whoever gets closest to seven and a half without going over wins; an exact seven and a half is paid double.

🎭 Anecdotes

In many homes the role of bank rotates, and whoever is the bank can win or lose a lot in a single round, which gives the game its Christmas excitement.

In Italy it is a tradition to play it on Christmas Eve after dinner, along with tombola; many Spanish and Italian families share this almost identical custom.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Italy it is played a lot at Christmas, just as in Spain, and it is called sette e mezzo.
  • It is played with 40 cards.
  • The king of coins is often the “wild card”.
  • Going over seven and a half is called “going bust”.
