Jigsaw puzzle

A picture cut into interlocking pieces that you have to put back together.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The first jigsaw puzzle was created around 1766 by the London engraver and mapmaker John Spilsbury, who mounted a map on wood and cut it along the borders of the countries to teach geography.

During the 19th century they were cut by hand with fretsaws (jigsaws, hence the name). From the 1930s they were made of die-cut cardboard and became cheaper.

Sales soared during the Great Depression and the 2020 lockdowns.

🏛️ Historical context

The first jigsaw was made in London around 1766 by the mapmaker John Spilsbury, who stuck a map onto a wooden board and cut it along the countries’ borders. He sold it as material for teaching geography to the children of wealthy families.

In the 19th century puzzles were made with drawings, scenes and landscapes, and in the 20th century die-cut cardboard made them cheap. During the Great Depression of the 1930s they were an enormously popular pastime, because they were inexpensive and gave hours of fun.

📖 How to play

  • Start with the edges, which have a straight side.
  • Group the pieces by colour.
  • Fit the pieces together until the picture is complete.
  • Some puzzles have more than 50,000 pieces.

🎭 Anecdotes

The 15-piece sliding puzzle caused a sensation in 1880. Sam Loyd, who falsely claimed to have invented it, offered 1,000 dollars to anyone who could solve a version with two tiles swapped. Nobody managed it because, as was proved mathematically, it was impossible.

In English it is called a jigsaw puzzle, after the jigsaw used to cut the wooden pieces.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The first jigsaw puzzles were called “dissected maps”.
  • The largest commercial puzzle has more than 50,000 pieces.
  • There are team jigsaw championships.
  • Doing puzzles helps visual coordination and patience.
