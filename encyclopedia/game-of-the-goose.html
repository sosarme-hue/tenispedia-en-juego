Game of the Goose

A 63-square spiral track with geese, bridges, a well and a skull. Pure chance and tradition.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The Game of the Goose is documented in Italy at the end of the 16th century. It is said that Francesco de’ Medici, Grand Duke of Tuscany, gave a board to King Philip II of Spain, which helped it spread through the courts of Europe.

Its spiral board of 63 squares became a widely copied model: there were educational goose games about history and geography, and propaganda ones. Some authors have given it symbolic or esoteric meanings, though there is no evidence for them.

In Spain it is one of the most popular children’s games, and phrases like “de oca a oca y tiro porque me toca” (“from goose to goose, and I throw again because it’s my turn”) are part of everyday language.

🏛️ Historical context

The Game of the Goose was born in Renaissance Italy, at the height of the fashion for printed games of chance. Printing and engraving made cheap paper boards possible, and the goose became a kind of template: thousands of different goose games were made on themes of history, geography, morals, politics or advertising.

In the 18th and 19th centuries the goose game was widely used for education and propaganda. France had goose games of the Revolution and of Napoleon; Spain had ones about its kings and national history and, in the 20th century, ones advertising chocolate brands, banks and school campaigns.

📖 How to play

  • Throw a die and move forward.
  • If you land on a goose, jump to the next one and throw again.
  • There are penalty squares: inn, well, maze, prison and skull.
  • The first to land exactly on square 63 wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Some scholars have tried to see the goose game as a secret map of the Camino de Santiago, with the geese as a symbol of the Templars and stonemasons. It is a nice theory but without evidence: the game was born in Italy and has no documented link with the Camino.

In France, during the Revolution of 1789, goose games were published in which the squares were revolutionary events and the final square was the Constitution instead of the goose.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The number 63 was considered special because it is 7 × 9, two numbers full of symbolism.
  • Square 58, the skull or death, sends you back to the start.
  • The bridge takes you from square 6 to square 12.
  • The geese come every nine squares, starting at 5 and 9.
