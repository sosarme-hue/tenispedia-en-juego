Tangram

A Chinese puzzle of seven pieces that can make thousands of shapes.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The tangram, called qīqiǎobǎn (“seven boards of skill”) in Chinese, appears in China at the end of the 18th century or the beginning of the 19th. The first books of shapes were published around 1813.

It reached Europe and America on trading ships and in 1817 caused a sensation in England, France and the United States. Napoleon and Lewis Carroll are said to have been fans.

Today it is widely used at school to teach geometry.

🏛️ Historical context

The tangram, called qiqiao ban in China, “board of seven clevernesses”, appears in Chinese books of the early 19th century. It is based on sets of modular furniture from the Song dynasty.

Sailors and merchants took it to Europe and America around 1815, and it caused a real craze: books with hundreds of shapes were published, and famous people such as Napoleon on St Helena and the writer Lewis Carroll were fans.

📖 How to play

  • There are 7 pieces: 5 triangles, a square and a parallelogram.
  • You must use all 7 pieces without overlapping them.
  • You have to reproduce a silhouette.
  • More than 6,500 shapes are known.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1903 the famous puzzle maker Sam Loyd published a book claiming that the tangram was 4,000 years old and had been invented by a Chinese god called Tan. It was all made up, but the story was repeated for decades.

Napoleon is said to have had a tangram in his exile on St Helena, and the writer Edgar Allan Poe had one too.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The 7 pieces can form 13 different convex polygons.
  • It has seven pieces: five triangles, a square and a parallelogram.
  • Thousands of different shapes can be made.
  • All the pieces together form a square.
