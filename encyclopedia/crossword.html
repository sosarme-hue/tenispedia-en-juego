Crossword

A puzzle of filling a grid with words that cross each other, using clues.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The first modern crossword was published by the British journalist Arthur Wynne on 21 December 1913 in the supplement of the newspaper New York World. It was diamond-shaped and he called it a word-cross.

In the 1920s it became a real craze: in 1924 the publisher Simon & Schuster brought out the first book of crosswords and it was a huge success. Spanish newspapers began publishing them in the 1920s and 1930s.

There are two main styles: the American, with few black squares, and the British “cryptic”, with clues that are wordplay.

🏛️ Historical context

The first crossword was published by the British journalist Arthur Wynne on 21 December 1913 in the supplement of the American newspaper New York World. It was diamond-shaped and called a word-cross.

In 1924 the publisher Simon & Schuster brought out the first book of crosswords and hundreds of thousands of copies were sold. A craze broke out: there were crosswords on trains, at weddings and in advertisements, and some newspapers complained that people were “wasting their time”.

📖 How to play

  • Each word has a numbered clue.
  • Words go across or down.
  • Shared letters must match in both words.
  • Spanish crosswords are usually written without accents.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1944, shortly before the Normandy landings, several Daily Telegraph crosswords included secret code words of the plan, such as Omaha, Utah, Mulberry and Overlord. British intelligence questioned the compiler, a headmaster, but it turned out to be a coincidence: his pupils had heard the words from soldiers.

In 1942 the Daily Telegraph organised a competition to solve a crossword in under twelve minutes. Some of the winners were recruited to break German codes at Bletchley Park.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • During the Second World War, the British army recruited good crossword solvers to break codes.
  • In Spain there are also autodefinidos, crosswords with the clues inside the grid.
  • At first it was called a word-cross; a typesetting change in the newspaper turned it into cross-word, the name that stuck.
  • British cryptic crosswords have clues built on wordplay.
