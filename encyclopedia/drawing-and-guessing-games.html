Draw-and-guess games

One player draws a word without speaking or writing and their team has to guess it before time runs out.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Guessing words from gestures is a very old parlour game: charades were already played in European homes in the 18th and 19th centuries. Draw-and-guess games replace the gestures with a pencil and paper.

The most famous is Pictionary, created in 1985 by Rob Angel, a waiter in Seattle (United States) who played at drawing words taken from the dictionary with his friends. He first made the boxes by hand, and then the game sold in the millions.

The idea has been adapted to all sorts of formats: television quiz shows, mobile apps and online games in which you draw with your finger or mouse and other players type what they think it is.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 1980s board games for groups of friends and parties became fashionable, like Trivial Pursuit. Pictionary joined that wave with a game in which you did not need to draw well: bad drawings are part of the fun.

With the arrival of mobile phones, draw-and-guess games enjoyed a second youth. In 2012 one app of this kind reached tens of millions of players in a few weeks.

📖 How to play

  • It is played in teams. On each turn, a player takes a card with a word.
  • They have to draw it without speaking, gesturing or writing letters or numbers.
  • Their team tries to guess the word before the sand timer runs out.
  • If they guess right, they move forward on the board or score points.
  • In some rounds, all the teams draw the same word at once.

🎭 Anecdotes

The hardest words to draw are usually abstract ones, such as “nostalgia” or “truth”, which force players to come up with clever tricks.

Many players invent codes with their team, such as drawing an ear to mean “sounds like…”.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Rob Angel got the idea for his game by leafing through a dictionary, and that is exactly how he chose the first words for the cards.
  • You do not need to be able to draw: often the winner is whoever draws fastest and most simply.
  • These games are good practice for visual communication and quick thinking.
  • In online versions you can play with strangers from all over the world.
