🤖 Games invented by machines

Almost as soon as the first computers existed, someone used them to play. Games served to show what machines could do, to test programs and, above all, to have fun. Over time, computers not only created new games: they also learned to play the old ones better than anyone.

⚙️ Before computers

The first successful gaming machine was the slot machine, which became popular in San Francisco at the end of the 19th century with Charles Fey’s Liberty Bell: three reels with symbols and a lever. It was a game of pure chance, but it introduced the idea that a machine could be your opponent.

Pinball machines, table football and the first mechanical arcade games filled amusement arcades in the first half of the 20th century, paving the way for video games.

🖥️ The first computer games

In 1952, the British scientist Alexander Douglas programmed tic-tac-toe on the EDSAC computer at the University of Cambridge, as part of his thesis. In 1958, the physicist William Higinbotham created Tennis for Two on an oscilloscope to entertain visitors to his laboratory, a forerunner of Pong.

In 1962, some MIT students created Spacewar!, a spaceship duel that was copied at universities all over the United States. It was the first video game to really spread, and its influence reached the first arcade machines.

🕹️ The golden age of arcades

In the 1970s and 1980s, Pong, Space Invaders, Pac-Man and Donkey Kong filled bars and arcades all over the world. Donkey Kong was the first appearance of the character who would later be called Mario, the hero of Super Mario Bros.

In 1984, in the Soviet Union, Alexey Pajitnov created Tetris on a computer at his research centre, and in 1991 Street Fighter II turned duels between players into a spectacle.

💻 Office and pocket games

Personal computers brought simple games that almost everyone played: Windows Solitaire and Minesweeper taught millions of people to use a mouse. At the end of the 1990s, Snake on mobile phones became one of the most played games in the world.

The internet and smartphones allowed a single programmer to publish a game and reach millions of people, as happened with 2048 in 2014 or with Minecraft, which began as a personal project and ended up as the best-selling video game in history.

🧠 Machines learn to win

At the same time, computers learned to play the classic games. In 1997, IBM’s Deep Blue computer beat the world chess champion Garry Kasparov. In 2016, the AlphaGo program defeated the South Korean champion Lee Sedol at Go, a game that was considered too complex for machines.

Today there are artificial intelligences that play poker, Stratego or video games at the level of the best humans. But games are still, above all, a way of having a good time with other people.
