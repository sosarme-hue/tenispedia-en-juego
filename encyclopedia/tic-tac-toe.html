Tic-tac-toe

The simplest pencil-and-paper game: three symbols in a row on a 3 × 3 grid.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

3 × 3 grids carved in stone have been found in Egyptian temples from around 1300 BC, although we don’t know for sure how they were played. The Romans had a game called terni lapilli, with three pebbles per player that moved around the board.

The version with crosses and circles on paper became popular in England in the 19th century under the name noughts and crosses. In the United States it is called tic-tac-toe.

In 1952 the Briton A. S. Douglas programmed OXO, a noughts and crosses game for the EDSAC computer at the University of Cambridge: it is one of the first video games in history.

🏛️ Historical context

Games of lining up three pieces are among the most universal: they appear in Egypt, China, ancient Greece and medieval Europe, almost always as children’s games or quick pastimes drawn on the ground or on a stone. Grids carved by monks, stonemasons or pilgrims survive on the stone benches of many medieval cloisters and churches.

In the 20th century tic-tac-toe was computing’s “laboratory”: being so simple, it was used to teach a machine to play without mistakes and to demonstrate the first ideas of machine learning.

📖 How to play

  • One player uses X and the other O.
  • Players take turns to mark an empty square.
  • Whoever gets three in a row wins.
  • If both play well, it is always a draw.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1961 the British researcher Donald Michie built MENACE, a machine made of 304 matchboxes filled with coloured beads. Each box represented a position; the beads stood for the moves. After each loss or win, Michie removed or added beads, and so the “machine” learned to play without losing. It is an ancestor of today’s artificial intelligence.

In the film WarGames (1983), a military computer discovers by playing tic-tac-toe against itself that there are games nobody can win, and so decides not to launch a nuclear war.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • There are only 765 essentially different positions in tic-tac-toe.
  • There are 255,168 different possible games if you count every sequence of moves.
  • Starting in the centre or a corner is the safest choice.
  • The name tic-tac-toe imitates the sound of pencils marking the paper.
