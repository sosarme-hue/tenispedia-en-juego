Donkey (el burro)

A fast, noisy card game: you have to collect four matching cards by passing them to your neighbour, and whoever loses collects letters until they spell BURRO (donkey).

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

El burro (“the donkey”) is a children’s and family card game widespread in Spain and Latin America. There are several versions with the same name, but the best known consists of passing cards at full speed until someone gets four of a kind.

Whoever loses each hand gets a letter: first B, then U, and so on until the word BURRO is complete. Whoever completes it loses the game.

It is very similar to the English-speaking game Spoons, in which instead of touching your nose you have to grab a spoon from the middle of the table, and there is always one fewer spoon than players.

🏛️ Historical context

It is a typical game for family gatherings and outings, because it can be learned in a minute and suits any age. Laughter and shouting are part of the game.

There are other versions of el burro in which cards are played in turns and whoever is left holding cards loses, but the card-passing one is the best known in Spain.

📖 How to play

  • You use as many sets of four matching cards as there are players (for example, the four kings, the four aces…). Each player receives four cards.
  • At the same time, everyone passes one card face down to the player on their right and picks up the one passed from the left.
  • Whoever gets four matching cards touches their nose (or puts their hand in the middle of the table) without saying anything.
  • The others must copy them as soon as they notice. The last to do so gets a letter.
  • Whoever completes the word BURRO loses.

🎭 Anecdotes

In the English version called Pig, instead of touching your nose, whoever gets four cards puts a finger on their nose like a piggy, and the last to notice becomes the pig.

The craftiest players touch their nose very slowly and discreetly, to see how long the others take to notice.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In some homes it is played with spoons or objects in the middle of the table, and there is always one fewer than players: whoever is left with nothing gets the letter.
  • There are no turns: everyone plays at the same time, which is why it is so fast.
  • The word to complete can change: in some places it is BURRO and in others ASNO or MULO.
  • It works with any deck, Spanish or French.
