Memory (pairs)

A game of finding matching pairs of cards by remembering where they are.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

An ancestor of the memory game is the Japanese kai-awase, from the Heian period and very popular in the 16th century, in which you had to match shells painted inside with the same scene.

The modern version with cards face down became popular in Europe in the 20th century. In 1959 the German publisher Ravensburger brought out Memory, which became a classic children’s game.

It is widely used by teachers because it trains attention and visual memory.

🏛️ Historical context

In Heian-period Japan people already played kai-awase, which consisted of matching clamshells painted on the inside: only the two halves of the same shell fitted together perfectly. It was a game of the aristocracy and formed part of brides’ trousseaux, as a symbol of fidelity.

The version with face-down cards became popular in Europe in the 20th century. In 1959 the German publisher Ravensburger launched Memory, based on a game invented by a Swiss teacher, and it became one of its greatest successes.

📖 How to play

  • The cards are laid face down; each picture appears twice.
  • You turn over two cards per turn.
  • If they match, you keep them.
  • Whoever collects the most pairs wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The trousseaux of brides from noble Japanese families included lacquered boxes with 360 painted shells, carried in the wedding procession.

The game that inspired the German version had been created by the Swiss Heinrich Hurter for his children, with pictures stuck on cardboard. After his death his family took it to Ravensburger, which sold it all over the world.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Kai-awase shells were given as wedding presents because each shell fits only its own partner.
  • Pairs games help to train short-term memory.
  • The record for memorising a deck of cards is under 15 seconds.
  • In kai-awase only the two halves of the same shell fitted together.
