Forbidden-word games

You have to get your team to guess a word without saying the word itself or the five forbidden words that go with it.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Forbidden-word games are an evolution of age-old guessing games. The best-known version is Taboo, published in 1989 in the United States and created by Brian Hersch.

Each card has a word the team has to guess and a list of words that cannot be said, precisely the ones that would help most. For “beach”, for example, “sand”, “sea”, “summer”, “sun” and “towel” would be forbidden.

A player from the other team keeps watch with a buzzer: if the person explaining says a forbidden word, it buzzes and the card is lost. It is a game of quick thinking, vocabulary and plenty of nerve.

🏛️ Historical context

The late 1980s were a golden age for party board games, designed for large groups and quick games. Taboo became one of the best sellers and was translated into dozens of languages.

Language teachers use it a lot in class, because it forces you to look for synonyms and explain things in a different way.

📖 How to play

  • It is played in teams. A player takes a card and tries to get their team to guess the main word.
  • They cannot say that word or any of the forbidden words on the card, or parts of them.
  • Nor can they gesture or draw.
  • An opponent watches and calls out if a forbidden word is said.
  • Each word guessed before time runs out scores a point.

🎭 Anecdotes

When someone gets a very difficult word, they sometimes go blank, and their team laughs more than if they had got it right.

Some expert players use shared references, such as films or songs, so that their team guesses in a second.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In many homes people play a home-made version: everyone writes words on slips of paper and puts them in a bowl, with no cards or board.
  • The word taboo comes from a Polynesian word meaning “forbidden” or “sacred”.
  • It helps build vocabulary, which is why it is widely used in language classes.
  • There are versions in which you can pass a card with a penalty.
