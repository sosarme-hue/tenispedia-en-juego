Lottery

Number draws with prizes, from Renaissance lotteries to Spain’s famous Christmas draw.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The first public lotteries in Europe were organised in the Low Countries in the 15th century to raise money for town walls and charitable works. In the town of L’Écluse there is a document from 1445 describing a lottery with cash prizes.

In Spain, King Charles III created the Royal Lottery in 1763, inspired by the one in Naples, to raise money for the Treasury without raising taxes. In 1812, in the middle of the Peninsular War, the Cortes of Cádiz created the Modern Lottery, which is today’s National Lottery.

The Extraordinary Christmas Draw was held for the first time in 1812 and is today one of the biggest draws in the world. The children of the San Ildefonso school in Madrid have sung out the numbers since the 18th century.

🏛️ Historical context

From the 15th to the 18th century, states did not have taxes that were easy to collect, and lotteries were a “voluntary” way of raising money. They were used to finance wars, ports, universities and even the founding of colonies, such as Virginia in America.

In the 19th century the lottery became a social phenomenon in Spain: shares were bought among friends, workmates and families, and the Christmas draw marked the start of the festive season.

📖 How to play

  • You buy tickets or tenth-shares with a number.
  • In the draw, balls are taken at random from a drum.
  • The winning numbers receive an amount according to the prize.
  • In the Christmas draw the top prize is called “el Gordo” (the Fat One).

🎭 Anecdotes

In 2011 the Christmas Gordo went to Sodeto, a village in Huesca of about 250 inhabitants where almost everyone had the number. Only one household missed out: a filmmaker who had moved there shortly before, and who later told the story in a documentary.

The Christmas lottery advert is almost a tradition in itself: the “lottery bald man” of the 1990s and the emotional adverts of recent years are eagerly awaited every year.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The children of San Ildefonso sing out the numbers of the Christmas draw with a distinctive chant that the whole country recognises.
  • The Christmas draw lasts several hours.
  • The drums of the Christmas draw are made of brass and can be seen at the Teatro Real in Madrid.
  • Lotteries have funded works such as the building of some American universities.
