Dominoes

A game of rectangular tiles with spots, joined end to end by matching numbers.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The oldest domino tiles come from China, where they are mentioned in 12th-century texts. Chinese tiles represent the 21 combinations of two dice and were used for games similar to card games.

European dominoes, with blank tiles and the 28 pieces of a double-six set, appear in Italy, in Venice and Naples, at the beginning of the 18th century, and from there spread through France, Spain and the Americas. The name may come from the black hooded cloak with a white lining worn by priests, called a domino.

Today it is one of the most popular games in Spain, Latin America and the Caribbean, where it is played in pairs in bars and squares.

🏛️ Historical context

Chinese domino tiles, known since the Song dynasty, represented every possible combination of two dice and were used both for trick-taking games and for divination. They had no blank tiles.

European dominoes appear in Italy at the beginning of the 18th century, in Venice and Naples, and from there pass to France and the rest of Europe. They may have arrived with merchants and missionaries who travelled to China, although the European rules are different. In Spain, Cuba and the Caribbean it became the game of squares, bars and doorways.

📖 How to play

  • The double-six set has 28 tiles.
  • Each player receives 7 tiles.
  • You place tiles that match the open ends of the line.
  • Whoever runs out of tiles, or has the fewest points when the game is blocked, wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The name may come from the black hood with a white lining worn by priests in winter, the domino, or from the Venetian carnival costume of the same name, because of the contrast between the black tiles and white spots.

In 2009 a record was set in the Netherlands with more than 4.4 million tiles in a domino topple. The domino effect, toppling tiles in a chain, has become an art form with its own championships.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The record for dominoes toppled in a chain is close to four and a half million.
  • A double-six set has 28 tiles.
  • In Latin America it is often played in pairs, with “capicúas” and “tranques” (blocked games).
  • The word “capicúa” comes from the Catalan cap i cua, “head and tail”.
