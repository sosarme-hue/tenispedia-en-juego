Hopscotch

A game of hopping on one foot through squares drawn on the ground.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

An often-repeated tradition says that Roman soldiers ran courses of squares to train in armour, and that children imitated them; it is not proved, but hopscotch is very old and is played all over the world.

In Spain it is known as rayuela, tejo, truque, charranca or avión, depending on the region. You throw a stone or marker into a square and hop through the drawing on one foot without stepping on the lines.

The Argentine writer Julio Cortázar called his most famous novel Rayuela (Hopscotch, 1963); it can also be read by “hopping” between chapters.

🏛️ Historical context

A much-repeated theory says that Roman soldiers played something like hopscotch on their roads to train, but there is no evidence. What is certain is that it was already played in Europe in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, and that in the 17th century it appears in English books of games.

Some historians link the squares to the soul’s journey from earth to heaven, because the last square is called “heaven” in many languages. In the 20th century it was the playground game par excellence, especially among girls.

📖 How to play

  • Numbered squares are drawn on the ground.
  • You throw a stone into the square whose turn it is.
  • You hop through the drawing on one foot without touching the lines or the square with the stone.
  • Whoever completes all the squares wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The Argentine writer Julio Cortázar called his most famous novel Rayuela (1963) and structured it as a game in which readers can jump from chapter to chapter in different orders, just like the squares of the game.

In many countries it has its own names: avión or avioncito in several places, golosa in Colombia, luche in Chile, xarranca in Catalonia, and charranca or tejo elsewhere.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In France hopscotch is called marelle, and in Spain rayuela.
  • You play hopping on one foot and throwing a stone or marker.
  • The final square is called heaven or moon, depending on the place.
  • You may not step on a line or on the square with the stone.
