Elastics (la goma)

The game of jumping over an elastic band held by two friends, higher and higher, to the rhythm of songs.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

La goma, also called “the elastic” or “the ribbon”, was one of the most popular playground games in Spain from the 1960s to the 1990s, especially among girls. All you need is a long elastic tied at the ends and three players.

Two players stand inside the elastic, a few metres apart to stretch it, and the third jumps following a sequence of steps: in, out, on top, treading on one side, crossing… If she makes a mistake, it is her turn to hold the elastic.

Very similar games are known all over the world. In English it is called Chinese jump rope, because it is thought to have reached the West from Asia, and in German Gummitwist. In the 1970s and 1980s it became a craze in schools all over Europe.

🏛️ Historical context

In Spain in the 1970s and 1980s, elastics shared the playground with skipping, hopscotch and trading cards. It was cheap, fitted in a pocket and needed no more space than a corner of the playground.

Many of the songs sung while jumping were passed from one girl to another and changed from school to school, as happens with skipping songs.

📖 How to play

  • Two players hold the elastic with their legs, first at ankle height.
  • The jumper performs a set series of steps on the elastic, often to the rhythm of a song.
  • If they complete the series without mistakes, the elastic goes up: to the knees, the thighs, the waist…
  • If they tread wrongly or get tangled, they lose their turn and hold the elastic.
  • Whoever gets highest wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Elastics were often made at home, by joining pieces of sewing elastic bought at the haberdasher’s, the same kind used for trouser waistbands.

At break time there were real champions who jumped the elastic at waist height while spinning in the air, and crowds would gather to watch them.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • At the hardest level the elastic is raised to armpit or neck height, and only the best jumpers manage to hook it with a foot.
  • Each school had its own sequence of jumps and its own names for each step.
  • Jumping elastics improves coordination and balance, which is why it is used in PE.
  • If a player was missing, the elastic was tied to a chair or a tree.
