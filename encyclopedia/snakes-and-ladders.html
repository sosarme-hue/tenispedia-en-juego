Snakes and ladders

A dice game in which ladders take you up and snakes take you down, born as a moral lesson in India.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Snakes and ladders comes from an Indian game called gyan chaupar or moksha patam, the “game of knowledge” or “of liberation”. It is sometimes credited to the 13th-century poet and saint Gyandev, although the oldest surviving boards date from the 18th century.

It was a moral lesson: the ladders represented virtues, such as generosity or faith, and the snakes vices, such as anger or theft. Reaching the last square meant achieving the liberation of the soul, moksha.

The British took it to England in the 19th century as snakes and ladders, and in the United States it was sold from 1943 as Chutes and Ladders, with slides instead of snakes.

🏛️ Historical context

India has a long tradition of race games with religious content: Jainism, Hinduism and Sufi Islam had their own versions of the game, with squares representing the heavens, the hells and the stages of the soul.

In Victorian England the game was adapted to the Christian morals of the time, with ladders of “obedience” or “penitence” and snakes of “idleness” or “disobedience”. Later it lost its moral meaning and remained a children’s game.

📖 How to play

  • The board has 100 numbered squares.
  • You roll a die and move forward.
  • If you land at the foot of a ladder you climb up; if you land on a snake’s head you slide down.
  • The first to reach the last square wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In Jain versions of the game the final square represented total liberation, and some had 84 squares, for the 8.4 million births that, according to tradition, a soul passes through.

The game is so old and well known in India that the expression “snakes and ladders” is used to talk about life, with its unpredictable ups and downs.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Old Indian boards had more snakes than ladders: it was easier to fall into vice than to climb by virtue.
  • It is a game of pure chance: there are no decisions to make.
  • Mathematicians use it to explain Markov chains.
  • In Spain it is also called “the ladder game”.
