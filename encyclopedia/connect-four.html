Connect Four

A game of dropping discs into an upright grid until you line up four.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The idea of lining up four discs in an upright grid was marketed by the Milton Bradley company in 1974, designed by Howard Wexler and Ned Strongin. In Spain it is known as cuatro en raya or conecta 4.

Similar games of lining up pieces already existed, but the upright frame in which the discs fall by gravity made it very easy to understand for all ages.

In 1988 the computer scientists James D. Allen and Victor Allis independently proved that the first player can always win by starting in the middle column and playing perfectly.

🏛️ Historical context

The 1970s were the golden age of plastic family board games, easy to understand and with eye-catching components. The American toy industry bet on quick games to play as a family while television took up more and more free time.

Connect Four was also one of the first games studied in depth by 1980s computer science, which sought to solve simple games to understand how machines “think”.

📖 How to play

  • The grid has 7 columns and 6 rows.
  • Each player drops a disc of their colour into a column.
  • Whoever lines up 4 discs horizontally, vertically or diagonally wins.
  • If the grid fills up, it’s a draw.

🎭 Anecdotes

Victor Allis presented the solution in his thesis, and his program, called VICTOR, never lost if it went first. A legend says that the explorer James Cook played something similar on his voyages, and so in some places it was sold as “the Captain’s Mistress”, but there is no evidence for it.

The upright grid prevents a typical cheat of paper games: you can’t place a disc “in mid-air”, only on top of another, which forces you to think about what each move leaves open.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • There are 4,531,985,219,092 possible positions in Connect Four.
  • If the first player starts in a side column, the second can force at least a draw.
  • The standard grid is 7 columns by 6 rows.
  • There are variants with five in a row and with discs that can be pulled out from the bottom.
