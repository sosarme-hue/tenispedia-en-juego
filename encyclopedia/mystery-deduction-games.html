Mystery and deduction games

Games in which you have to find out who committed a crime, with which weapon and where.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

During the Second World War, the British musician Anthony E. Pratt, who was working in a factory, devised with his wife Elva a detective game inspired by mystery novels and the “murder” parties of the time. He patented it in 1947 and the Waddingtons company published it in 1949 under the name Cluedo (Clue in the United States).

The mechanism is deduction: three secret cards hold the solution, and players rule out possibilities by questioning the others.

Its success inspired films, series and many later deduction games.

🏛️ Historical context

The 1920s to 1940s were the golden age of the British detective novel, with authors such as Agatha Christie and Dorothy L. Sayers. Country-house “murder” parties, in which the guests acted out a crime, were a fashionable pastime among the well-to-do.

Pratt devised the game during the bombing of Birmingham, when people spent many hours at home and in shelters. Post-war paper rationing delayed its publication until 1949.

📖 How to play

  • A suspect, a weapon and a room are hidden in an envelope.
  • Players move around the mansion and make suggestions.
  • The others secretly show a card that disproves the suggestion.
  • Whoever makes the complete correct accusation wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The 1985 film was released with three different endings depending on the cinema, so that audiences had to go to several cinemas to find out who did it. The video release included all three one after the other.

The original design had more weapons and characters, such as a syringe and an axe, which were removed before publication. The name combines clue and Ludo, the English race game.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The name Cluedo combines the English word clue with Ludo, a very well-known game in the United Kingdom.
  • There are 324 possible combinations of suspect, weapon and room in the classic game.
  • The mansion in the original game has nine rooms.
  • Elva Pratt designed the board.
