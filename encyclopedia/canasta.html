Canasta

A partnership melding game with two decks, created in Montevideo.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Canasta was invented in 1939 in Montevideo by the lawyer Segundo Santos and the architect Alberto Serrato, who were looking for a game to pass the afternoons at the Jockey Club.

It spread quickly through Argentina and reached the United States at the end of the 1940s, where in 1950 it became a real craze.

Its name, meaning “basket”, refers to the main play: collecting seven cards of the same rank, gathered as if in a basket.

🏛️ Historical context

Canasta was invented in Montevideo, Uruguay, in 1939, in the setting of upper-class social clubs, as a melding game quicker than bridge. In the following decade it spread to Argentina and then to the United States.

In the 1950s it became a craze in the United States, where it even displaced bridge in the clubs. Special decks, books and card tables were sold, and magazines spoke of the “canasta craze”.

📖 How to play

  • It is played with two French decks and four jokers.
  • You form melds of cards of the same rank.
  • A canasta is 7 matching cards.
  • The first pair to reach 5,000 points wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The name “canasta” comes from the tray or basket in which discarded cards were placed at the Jockey Club in Montevideo, where it was first played.

Life magazine and other major American media ran features on the “canasta craze” at the beginning of the 1950s, when it was the fashionable card game at parties and clubs.
