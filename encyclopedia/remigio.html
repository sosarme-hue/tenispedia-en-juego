Remigio

A melding game with two French decks and jokers, related to rummy and chinchón.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Remigio is the Spanish variant of rummy played with the French deck. It uses two decks and jokers, which allows games with many players.

As in chinchón, the aim is to form runs and sets with the cards in your hand by drawing and discarding in turn, and the first to manage it with all their cards “closes”.

It is very popular at family gatherings, in village social clubs and in cafés.

🏛️ Historical context

Remigio is one of the Spanish forms of rummy, with several decks and jokers. It was often played at family gatherings of several generations, because it is easy to learn and allows many players.

Like other melding games, it grew in importance in the 20th century with the spread of cheap playing cards and the culture of cafés and conversation circles.

📖 How to play

  • It is played with two French decks and jokers.
  • Each player receives 7 cards.
  • Each turn you draw and discard to form runs and sets.
  • Whoever closes wins the hand; the others add up points and are out at 100.

🎭 Anecdotes

In many Spanish homes remigio was the game for Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve, when grandparents and grandchildren played together betting chickpeas or sweets instead of money.

As in every game of the rummy family, the trick is not to be left with many unmatched cards when someone closes, because those cards count against you.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The joker is the most wanted card, but also the one that costs the most points if you’re caught holding it.
  • It is usually played with two decks shuffled together.
  • You have to lay down melds of sets and runs.
  • The joker can stand in for any card.
