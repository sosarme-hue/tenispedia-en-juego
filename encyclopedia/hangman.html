Hangman

A game of guessing a word letter by letter before the drawing is complete.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The origin of hangman is unclear. It is mentioned in England at the end of the 19th century, in Alice Bertha Gomme’s book Traditional Games (1894), under the name Birds, Beasts and Fishes.

It is a pencil-and-paper game that is ideal for learning vocabulary and spelling, which is why it is widely used in schools.

There are gentler versions that replace the drawing with a melting snowman or a flower losing its petals.

🏛️ Historical context

Hangman is a Victorian pencil-and-paper game. In Alice Bertha Gomme’s 1894 book of games it appears under the name Birds, Beasts and Fishes, in which you had to guess the name of an animal.

Its drawing reflects a time when hanging was still a public punishment, something that seems strange today in a children’s game. That is why there are alternative versions with a melting snowman or a flower losing its petals.

📖 How to play

  • One player thinks of a word and draws a dash for each letter.
  • The other suggests letters.
  • Correct letters are written in their places.
  • Each miss adds a part to the drawing.

🎭 Anecdotes

In many Spanish schools hangman is used to revise English vocabulary and spelling, because it makes you think about the most common letters.

The TV game show Wheel of Fortune, in which phrases are guessed letter by letter, is based on the same idea as hangman.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The most common letters in English are E, T, A and O.
  • In English the most common letters are E and T.
  • A good strategy is to ask for the vowels first.
  • Words with rare letters such as J, Q, X or Z are the hardest.
