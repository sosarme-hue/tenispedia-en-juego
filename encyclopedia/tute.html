Tute

A trick-taking game with declarations: the twenty and the forty, and the tute of four kings or four knights.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Tute reached Spain from Italy in the 19th century and became one of the most popular games with the Spanish deck. Its name comes from the Italian tutti, “all”, after the play of collecting the four kings or the four knights.

It is related to brisca, but with declarations: if you hold the king and knight of the same suit, you can declare “the twenty”, or “the forty” if they are in the trump suit.

The Spanish expression “cantarle a alguien las cuarenta” (to sing someone the forty), meaning to give them a piece of your mind, comes from this game.

🏛️ Historical context

Tute appears in 19th-century Spain and draws on Italian trick-taking games with declarations. It was the card game of village cafés and social clubs, and its name is linked to the Italian tutti, “all”, from the declaration of the four kings or knights.

There are many regional variants: tute habanero, tute cabrón, auction tute, tute arrastrado… Each part of Spain has its own rules, and arguing about them is part of the fun.

📖 How to play

  • The whole 40-card deck is dealt.
  • You must follow suit and beat the card if you can.
  • King and knight of the same suit can be declared: 20 or 40 points.
  • Whoever scores the most points from tricks and declarations wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Declaring the forty, which in tute means announcing the king and knight of the trump suit, became a Spanish idiom: “cantarle las cuarenta a alguien” means telling someone exactly what you think of them.

Having “tute” means collecting the four kings or the four knights, and it wins the game on the spot. Some link the Spanish expression “darse un tute”, meaning to make a huge effort, to the game.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Whoever collects the four kings or the four knights “declares tute” and wins the game outright.
  • Declaring a king and knight of the same suit is worth 20 points (40 in the trump suit).
  • The “ten for last” are the points for whoever wins the last trick.
  • Auction tute is the variant most played in championships.
