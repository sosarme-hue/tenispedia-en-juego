Chinchón

A melding game of the rummy family, very popular in Spain and Argentina.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Chinchón belongs to the rummy family, games of forming runs and sets that became popular in the 20th century from Mexican conquian and American gin rummy.

Its name is the same as that of the town of Chinchón near Madrid, although nobody knows for sure if it was born there. It is played with the 40- or 48-card Spanish deck.

The best possible hand, a run of seven cards of the same suit, is itself called “chinchón” and gives an instant win.

🏛️ Historical context

Chinchón belongs to the large family of rummy-type melding games, which arose in Mexico and the United States at the end of the 19th century from conquian. In Spain it was played mainly after the Civil War, in bars and social clubs, with the Spanish deck.

The name matches that of the town of Chinchón near Madrid, known for its main square and its anise liqueur, although it has not been proved that the game was born there.

📖 How to play

  • Each player receives 7 cards.
  • Each turn you draw a card and discard another.
  • You form runs in the same suit or sets of the same number.
  • When someone closes, the others add up their unmatched cards; whoever reaches 100 is out.

🎭 Anecdotes

In Argentina and Uruguay chinchón is one of the most popular card games, and it is often played “to 100 points”: whoever goes over that score is out.

Making chinchón, that is, a run of seven cards of the same suit, wins immediately. It is so rare that many players remember it for years.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Making chinchón (a run of 7 in the same suit) wins the whole game at once.
  • Seven cards are dealt to each player.
  • Closing with all your cards melded takes points off.
  • Wild cards are sometimes the two of coins or cards agreed beforehand.
