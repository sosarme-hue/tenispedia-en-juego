Alquerque

A game of capturing by jumping on a board of lines, the direct ancestor of checkers.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Alquerque, from the Arabic al-qirq, is played on a board of 5 × 5 points joined by straight and diagonal lines. It is mentioned in the Kitab al-Aghani, a 10th-century Arabic book, although similar boards carved in stone could be much older.

It reached Europe through al-Andalus. Alfonso X the Wise describes it in his Book of Games of 1283, which contains the first written rules we know.

In the late Middle Ages someone had the idea of playing with its pieces on a chessboard: that is how checkers was born. Alquerque went on being played in Spain, North Africa and, taken by the Spanish, in the Americas.

🏛️ Historical context

For centuries al-Andalus was the great cultural bridge between the Islamic world and Christian Europe. Through it came chess, alquerque, playing cards, paper, Arabic numerals and many gaming words, such as “hazard” (from the Arabic az-zahr, “the die”).

At the court of Alfonso X in Toledo, Christian, Jewish and Muslim scholars worked together, and the Book of Games reflects that mix of cultures, with miniatures of players of every religion.

📖 How to play

  • Each player has 12 pieces placed on their half of the board.
  • Pieces move to an empty point along the lines.
  • You capture by jumping over an opponent’s piece to an empty point, and jumps can be chained.
  • Whoever captures or blocks all the opponent’s pieces wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The monastery of El Escorial keeps the original manuscript of Alfonso X’s Book of Games, with 150 full-colour miniatures of people playing chess, dice, tables and alquerque.

There are alquerque boards carved into the stones of Spanish cathedrals and churches, probably made by stonemasons in their spare time.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The Zuni people of New Mexico play a game called kolowis awithlaknannai that comes from the alquerque brought by the Spanish.
  • In some parts of Spain the word “alquerque” is also used for a game similar to tic-tac-toe.
  • The board has 25 points.
  • Fanorona from Madagascar is a larger relative of alquerque.
