Trivia games

Knowledge games and quizzes, from 1930s radio to the great board games.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Quiz shows were born on American radio in the 1930s and jumped to television in the 1950s. In Spain, programmes such as Un, dos, tres, Saber y ganar and Pasapalabra have turned questions into family entertainment.

In 1979 two Canadian journalists, Chris Haney and Scott Abbott, invented in Montreal a board game of questions on six topics, with a wheel-shaped board and coloured wedges: Trivial Pursuit, now a registered trademark. It went on sale in 1981, and in 1984 it sold more than twenty million copies.

🏛️ Historical context

Quiz shows were born when radio reached every home. In the 1950s, in the United States, television turned them into mass spectacles with huge prizes, until a scandal broke in 1958: some contestants had been given the answers in advance.

The 1979 board game reached the shops in 1981 and was a runaway success in the 1980s, the era of house parties and get-togethers with friends. In Spain, public television made cultural quiz shows popular, and they are now part of the collective memory.

📖 How to play

  • There are questions on several topics.
  • Players answer in turn.
  • Right answers earn points, tokens or moves forward.
  • Whoever first completes every topic or gets the most right wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Chris Haney and Scott Abbott had the idea when they found pieces missing from a letter game. To fund their own game they sold shares to friends and family, and those who invested a few hundred dollars ended up making fortunes.

In the 1980s a writer sued the creators of the trivia game because several answers matched mistakes he had deliberately planted in his encyclopedia of trivia. He lost the case, because facts cannot be copyrighted.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The word “trivial” comes from the Latin trivium, the meeting of three roads where people chatted about everyday things.
  • The six classic topics are geography, entertainment, history, art and literature, science and nature, and sport and leisure.
  • In Spain the wedge-shaped pieces came to be called “quesitos” (little cheeses).
  • Saber y ganar, on TVE since 1997, is one of the longest-running quiz shows on Spanish television.
