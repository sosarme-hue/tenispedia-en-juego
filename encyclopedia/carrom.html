Carrom

The “finger billiards” of South Asia: you flick a disc to send pieces into the corners of the board.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Carrom is a very popular tabletop game in India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and Nepal. It is played on a square wooden board with pockets in the corners, and instead of cues you use your fingers to flick a bigger disc, the striker.

Its exact origin is unknown. It is said to have been invented by the maharajas of India, and in a palace in Patiala, in the Punjab, there is a 19th-century glass board.

In the 20th century it spread around the world with South Asian emigration, and today there are federations and international championships in Europe, Asia and the Americas.

🏛️ Historical context

In India carrom is a neighbourhood and family game played at home, in clubs and in the street. It is a typical pastime of long, hot summer afternoons and of the monsoon season.

It is similar to other flicking games, such as Canadian crokinole or pichenotte, and probably all of them are inspired by billiards.

📖 How to play

  • There are 9 white pieces, 9 black and one red, the “queen”.
  • You flick the striker with your finger to pot your own pieces.
  • You have to pot the queen and “cover” it by then potting one of your own pieces.
  • The first to pot all their pieces wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In India some carrom players are so skilled that they pot all their pieces in a single turn, which is called a “white slam” or “black slam”.

During the 2020 lockdowns, sales of carrom boards soared in India because families were spending so much time at home.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Boards are dusted with talcum or boric acid powder so that the pieces slide.
  • The board is about 74 centimetres across.
  • It is played only with the fingers: cues are not allowed.
  • The queen is worth more points than any other piece.
