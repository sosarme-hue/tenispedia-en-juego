Tetris

The falling-blocks puzzle, one of the best-selling video games in history.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1984 the engineer Alexey Pajitnov, who worked at the Computing Centre of the Academy of Sciences in Moscow, programmed Tetris, inspired by pentominoes, a puzzle of pieces made of five squares. He used pieces of four squares (tetrominoes) and his favourite sport, tennis, for the name.

The game was copied all over the Soviet Union and, after a complicated battle over the rights, in 1989 it was bundled with Nintendo’s Game Boy handheld, which turned it into a worldwide phenomenon.

Pajitnov received no royalties until 1996, when he founded The Tetris Company. Tetris is now a registered trademark.

🏛️ Historical context

Tetris was created in 1984 by the Soviet engineer Alexey Pajitnov at a research centre of the Academy of Sciences in Moscow, on an Elektronika 60 computer with no graphics: the pieces were text characters.

At the height of the Cold War, the game was copied on floppy disks throughout the Soviet Union and reached Hungary and the West. The rights were controlled by the Soviet state, and selling them to Western companies was a real spy story, with negotiations in Moscow in 1989.

📖 How to play

  • Pieces of four squares fall in 7 different shapes.
  • You rotate and place them to complete rows.
  • Complete rows disappear.
  • You lose if the pieces reach the top.

🎭 Anecdotes

In December 2023 the American Willis Gibson, aged 13, became the first person to make classic NES Tetris crash after reaching level 157. Nobody had managed it in 34 years.

Tetris’s most famous music is a 19th-century Russian folk song, Korobeiniki, which tells the story of a pedlar.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • A “Tetris effect” has been described: after playing a lot, people see pieces fitting together when they close their eyes.
  • The name comes from “tetra” (four, for the four squares in each piece) and “tennis”, Pajitnov’s favourite sport.
  • There are seven different pieces, called tetrominoes.
  • Psychologists have called dreaming of falling pieces after playing a lot the “Tetris effect”.
