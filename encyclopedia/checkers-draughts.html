Checkers (draughts)

A strategy game in which you capture by jumping diagonally and men become kings.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Checkers was born around the 12th century in southern Europe by playing the pieces of alquerque, a very old Arab game, on a chessboard. The rule that capturing is compulsory appeared around the 16th century, and the Spanish name, damas, comes from the chess queen (dama).

One of the first books on the game is by the Valencian Antonio de Torquemada, printed in 1547. Later many variants developed: Spanish, English (checkers or draughts), Italian and Russian checkers, and international draughts on a 10 × 10 board.

In 2007 a team at the University of Alberta proved with the program Chinook that in English checkers, if both sides play perfectly, the game ends in a draw.

🏛️ Historical context

Checkers was born in the late medieval Europe of towns and trade, when chess was already well established. Combining the pieces of alquerque with the chequered board gave a simpler, faster game, played in inns and town squares.

Spain played a leading part in its history: after Torquemada, the Aragonese Pedro Ruiz Montero published another treatise in 1591, and in 1597 Lorenzo Valls wrote a book that circulated around Europe. Spanish checkers, with a king that moves several squares, keeps those old rules.

📖 How to play

  • Each player has 12 pieces on the dark squares.
  • Men move forward diagonally one square.
  • You capture by jumping over an opponent’s piece; capturing is compulsory.
  • A man that reaches the far side is crowned king and can move further.

🎭 Anecdotes

Marion Tinsley, an American mathematics teacher, was almost unbeatable: in a 45-year career he lost only seven games. In 1992 he beat the program Chinook, and when they met again in 1994 he had to withdraw through illness. He died a few months later.

Boards that some call “Egyptian checkers” have been found in ancient Egypt, but they have no direct link with the European game: the idea of jumping to capture seems to have arisen in several places.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • English checkers was the first game of this complexity to be “solved” by computer.
  • In British English the game is called draughts, and in American English checkers.
  • The board is placed with a dark square in each player’s left-hand corner.
  • In Russian checkers men can capture backwards.
