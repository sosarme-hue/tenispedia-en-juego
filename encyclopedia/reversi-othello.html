Reversi

A game of two-coloured discs that flip over when they are surrounded.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Reversi was invented in England around 1883; it is credited to Lewis Waterman and John W. Mollett, who disputed who had invented it. It was very popular at the end of the 19th century.

In 1971 the Japanese Goro Hasegawa launched a version with slightly different rules that was sold with great success around the world.

It is a classic game for programming artificial intelligence: in 1997 the program Logistello beat the world champion Takeshi Murakami 6–0.

🏛️ Historical context

Reversi was born in Victorian England, a society fascinated by parlour pastimes, puzzles and patented games. Newspapers and magazines published problems and competitions, and the dispute over authorship between Waterman and Mollett reached the press.

Its modern version, from the 1970s, benefited from the boom in board games in Japan after the “economic miracle”. Its trade name was inspired by Shakespeare’s Othello, the story of a Black general and a white woman.

📖 How to play

  • It is played on an 8 × 8 board with discs that are white on one side and black on the other.
  • Each disc you place must trap a line of opposing discs.
  • The trapped discs are flipped over and become yours.
  • Whoever has more discs of their colour at the end wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Goro Hasegawa said he came up with his version while playing as a child in a shelter during the Second World War, using go stones. Today we know the game had already existed in England for almost a century.

Japanese players have dominated the world championships for decades; they are played on a green board, like the cloth of a billiard table.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Its advertising slogan was “a minute to learn, a lifetime to master”.
  • The four central squares start with two discs of each colour placed diagonally.
  • Corners are the most valuable squares because they can never be flipped.
  • A game lasts at most 60 moves.
