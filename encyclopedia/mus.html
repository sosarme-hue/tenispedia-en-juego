Mus

Spain’s most popular partnership card game, full of signals, all-in bets (órdagos) and bluffs.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Mus was born in the Basque Country and Navarre. Its first written mention appears in 1745, in a dictionary by the Jesuit Manuel de Larramendi. From there it spread throughout Spain, where today it is played in bars, homes and tournaments.

It is played with the Spanish deck in pairs, and its charm is that partners may communicate through permitted facial signals: winking, biting your lip or sticking out your tongue show which cards you hold.

Mus vocabulary has passed into everyday Spanish: “echar un órdago” means to stake everything at once.

🏛️ Historical context

Mus was born in the Basque Country and Navarre in the 18th century, a time of close contact between the Cantabrian ports, fishermen and merchants. It was played in taverns, cider houses and pelota courts, and spread throughout Spain with the Basques and Navarrese who emigrated to Madrid and the Americas.

It is a game of partnership and communication: the signals, permitted gestures that tell your partner which cards you have, make it a very social game. In the 20th century it became the card game of universities, barracks and social clubs.

📖 How to play

  • Four players in two pairs, with 4 cards each.
  • Bets are made on four rounds: high cards (grande), low cards (chica), pairs (pares) and points (juego).
  • Players can call “mus” to swap cards.
  • Partners use signals to tell each other what they hold.

🎭 Anecdotes

Some say the name comes from the French mouche, “fly”, and others from the Basque musu, “kiss” or “face”, but nobody knows for sure.

A legend says that mus was born among Basque fishermen who, bored on the high seas, invented the signals so as not to be found out by the other boats. It’s a nice story without evidence, but it explains why the game relies so much on gestures.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • There has been an International Mus Championship since 1978, with countries from all over the world taking part.
  • An órdago means staking everything on a single round; if it is accepted, the winner takes the game.
  • The four rounds of mus are grande, chica, pares and juego.
  • Biting your lower lip is the signal for holding two kings.
