Bridge

Considered the most intellectual card game, with an auction and tricks played in pairs.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Bridge descends from whist, an English trick-taking game that was very popular in the 18th century. At the end of the 19th century biritch or bridge whist appeared, probably with influences from the Ottoman Empire and Russia.

In 1925, on a cruise through the Panama Canal, the American millionaire Harold Vanderbilt set the rules of contract bridge, the version played all over the world today.

It is a mind sport recognised by the International Olympic Committee, and famous people such as Bill Gates and Warren Buffett are keen players.

🏛️ Historical context

Bridge comes from whist, the great trick-taking game of 18th- and 19th-century England, played in gentlemen’s clubs. At the end of the 19th century bridge whist appeared, with an auction, and in 1925 the American millionaire Harold Vanderbilt set the rules of contract bridge during a cruise through the Panama Canal.

In the 1930s bridge was a real craze in the United States and Europe: newspapers had daily columns, there were radio programmes and championships filled ballrooms. It was also one of the few games in which men and women competed together on equal terms.

📖 How to play

  • Four players in two pairs, with 13 cards each.
  • First there is an auction to decide the contract and the trump suit.
  • Then 13 tricks are played.
  • You win if you make your contract.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1929, in Kansas City, Myrtle Bennett shot her husband during a game of bridge because he had lost a hand she thought was easy. She was acquitted, and the case became famous throughout the United States.

Well-known figures have been bridge fanatics, such as Bill Gates and Warren Buffett, who play together and have donated money to teach it in schools.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The pair that wins the auction plays with one partner’s cards face up on the table: the “dummy”.
  • It is played in pairs: North-South against East-West.
  • The “dummy” shows their cards and their partner plays them.
  • In duplicate bridge every table plays the same hands, to reduce luck.
