Word games with letter tiles

Games of making crossword-style words with scored letter tiles, like the famous Scrabble.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1938 the American architect Alfred Mosher Butts, out of work during the Great Depression, invented a game called Criss-Crosswords. To decide how many tiles of each letter to include, he counted the frequency of letters on the front pages of the New York Times.

In 1948 James Brunot bought the rights, simplified the rules and renamed it Scrabble, a name that is now a registered trademark. From 1952 it became a best-seller and was translated into dozens of languages, with letter distributions adapted to each one.

There have been international championships in Spanish since 1997. On Ludicopedia you can play Word Rack, our original version with its own board, scoring and dictionary.

🏛️ Historical context

The 1930s were the years of the crossword craze in the United States, and Butts wanted to combine the crossword with letter games and games of chance. His first attempt, Lexiko, had no board; when he added the 15 × 15 grid, the modern game was born.

Success came in the 1950s, in the age of prosperity and suburban family life. It is said that the president of Macy’s department store tried it on holiday and, delighted, placed a huge order that sent sales soaring.

📖 How to play

  • Each player has 7 letter tiles.
  • Taking turns, players form words on the board, crossing the ones already there.
  • Each letter is worth some points; special squares multiply them.
  • Whoever has the most points when the tiles run out wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 2015 the New Zealander Nigel Richards won the French-language Scrabble world championship without speaking French: he had memorised the dictionary in about nine weeks.

For the letter distribution, Butts counted how often each letter appeared on the front pages of the New York Times. That is why the S was deliberately given few tiles: he didn’t want plurals to be too easy.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The inventor of the original game worked out the letters by counting those in the newspaper by hand.
  • The Spanish version has its own letters, such as Ñ, LL, RR and CH on single tiles.
  • A move that uses all seven tiles earns a 50-point bonus.
  • The board has 225 squares.
