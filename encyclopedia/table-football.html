Table football

Football on a table, with figures fixed to rods that you spin.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

There were several table football patents in Europe in the 1920s, such as that of the Briton Harold Searles Thornton in 1923 and others in Germany and France.

In Spain it is usually credited to the Galician poet Alejandro Finisterre, who in 1937, wounded in the Civil War, designed a table football game for injured children who could not play football, and said he patented it in Barcelona. Spanish futbolín has its own features, such as figures with joined legs and a goalkeeper rod with three players.

For decades it was king of the bars, amusement arcades and youth clubs of Spain.

🏛️ Historical context

Table football has several claimed inventors. In England, Harold Searles Thornton patented a table football game in 1923. In Spain, the Galician Alejandro Finisterre designed a futbolín in 1937, during the Civil War, while in hospital at Montserrat, so that wounded children who could not play football could have fun.

In the 1950s and 1960s table football became king of the bars and arcades of Spain, France, Italy and Germany. Each country developed its own style: the Spanish with joined-feet figures and a smaller ball, the French with wooden figures, the German with heavier tables.

📖 How to play

  • Each player or pair controls several rods with figures.
  • You turn the rod to strike the ball.
  • The first to score an agreed number of goals wins.
  • In many games “spinning” (whirling the rod round without control) is not allowed.

🎭 Anecdotes

Alejandro Finisterre lost the patent for his invention while fleeing to France across the Pyrenees at the end of the Civil War: the papers got soaked in a storm. Years later he saw table football games all over the world without having earned a penny.

In Spain, futbolín has its own vocabulary: “gancho”, “remolino”, “pared” or “bote”. And the most famous rule in bar games: no spinning the rod all the way round, the notorious “molinillo”.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Alejandro Finisterre said he lost his patent papers while fleeing to France during the war.
  • Spanish table football has one-piece figures with joined legs.
  • A game is usually played to seven goals.
  • In France and Belgium it is called baby-foot, and in Italy calcio balilla.
