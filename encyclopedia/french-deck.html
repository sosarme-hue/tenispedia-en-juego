The French deck

The deck of spades, hearts, diamonds and clubs, the most widely used in the world.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Around 1480 French card makers simplified the German suits and created the four suits we use today: spades, hearts, diamonds and clubs. Being simple one-colour shapes, they could be printed with stencils, and the deck became much cheaper.

It has 52 cards with three court cards: the J (jack or knave), the Q (queen) and the K (king). In the 19th century corner indices and the joker were added; the joker was born in the United States for the game of euchre.

It is the deck used for poker, bridge, blackjack, canasta, rummy and solitaire.

🏛️ Historical context

In late 15th-century France, card makers in Lyon and Rouen were looking for cheaper ways to produce cards. The simple shapes of hearts, spades, diamonds and clubs could be stencilled in a single colour, making production faster and cheaper than with the complex Italian and Spanish suits.

The court cards of the French deck were given the names of heroes: in the Paris pattern, the king of hearts is Charlemagne, of spades David, of diamonds Julius Caesar and of clubs Alexander the Great. The English deck, derived from the Rouen pattern, spread around the world with the British Empire.

📖 How to play

  • 52 cards in four suits: ♠ ♥ ♦ ♣.
  • Each suit has an ace, 2 to 10, J, Q and K.
  • Two jokers are usually added.
  • Two suits are red and two are black.

🎭 Anecdotes

The English ace of spades has a very ornate design because for centuries it carried the playing-card tax stamp. Forging it was punishable by death: in 1805 a man called Richard Harding was hanged for it.

During the French Revolution the kings and queens of the deck were seen as symbols of the Ancien Régime and were replaced by figures such as “geniuses”, “liberties” and “equalities”. Napoleon restored the traditional figures.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In the French tradition each court card had a name: the king of hearts is Charlemagne and the king of spades is King David.
  • The king of hearts is the only king without a moustache in the English pattern, and he seems to be sticking his sword into his head: that is why he is called the “suicide king”.
  • A properly shuffled deck is almost certainly in an order that has never existed before.
  • The corner indices let you hold the cards in a fan.
