Pong and the first video games

The first big video game hit: table tennis with two lines and a ball.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1958 the physicist William Higinbotham created Tennis for Two on an oscilloscope for a laboratory open day. In 1962 MIT students programmed Spacewar!, one of the first digital video games.

In 1972 Nolan Bushnell founded Atari and the engineer Allan Alcorn programmed Pong, a very simple table tennis game that was installed in bars and arcades. It was an instant hit and the beginning of the video game industry.

That same year the Magnavox Odyssey, the first home video game console, designed by Ralph Baer, went on sale.

🏛️ Historical context

In 1958 the American physicist William Higinbotham created Tennis for Two on an oscilloscope at the Brookhaven laboratory, to entertain visitors on its open day. It is one of the first video games in history.

In 1972 Ralph Baer launched the Magnavox Odyssey, the first home console, which included a tennis game. That same year Nolan Bushnell founded Atari and asked Allan Alcorn for a simple tennis game: that is how Pong, the first big video game hit, was born.

📖 How to play

  • Each player moves a paddle up and down.
  • You must return the ball to the other side.
  • If the ball gets past your paddle, your opponent scores a point.
  • The first to reach 11 points wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The first Pong machine was installed at Andy Capp’s Tavern in Sunnyvale, California. A few days later the owner called Atari because the machine had broken down: it was so full of coins that no more would fit.

Ralph Baer, considered the father of consoles, sued Atari because Pong was too similar to the tennis game on his Odyssey. They reached a financial settlement.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The first Pong machine broke down because it was stuffed with coins: people couldn’t stop playing!
  • Pong had only one instruction: “Avoid missing ball for high score”.
  • The Pong ball was a square.
  • Allan Alcorn programmed Pong as a practice exercise.
