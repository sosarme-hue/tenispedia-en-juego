Churro, media manga, mangotero

One team plays the donkey and the other jumps on top. Then you have to guess: churro, media manga or mangotero?

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Churro, media manga, mangotero is a traditional Spanish game for two teams, very popular in neighbourhoods and schools for much of the 20th century. It is a game of strength, balance and luck.

One team forms a line of bent-over players, each with their head between the legs of the one in front, and the first leaning against a wall or on the “mother”, who stands upright. The other team jumps one by one onto their backs, trying to get as far as possible.

When everyone has jumped, the last one up touches their arm and asks: “churro, media manga or mangotero, which will it be?”. Churro is the wrist, media manga the elbow and mangotero the shoulder. If the team underneath guesses right, the roles swap.

🏛️ Historical context

It was a typical street game for children from the 1950s to the 1980s, in neighbourhoods and school playgrounds. All you needed was a wall and a willingness to jump.

Today it is banned in many playgrounds because jumping on people’s backs can cause injuries. That is why it is remembered mainly as a game from another era.

📖 How to play

  • One team plays the donkey, bent over in a line. The mother stands against the wall to support the first one.
  • The players of the other team jump one at a time onto their backs, without touching the ground once they are up.
  • If someone falls or touches the ground, or if the donkey collapses, the round is lost.
  • The last to jump points to their wrist, elbow or shoulder and asks: “churro, media manga or mangotero?”.
  • If the team underneath guesses right, they become the jumpers. If they get it wrong, they are the donkey again.

🎭 Anecdotes

The first jumper was always the most agile in the team, because they had to get as far as possible and leave room for the others.

In many gangs of friends, the heavier and stronger kids played the donkey, and the slim, light ones were the jumpers.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In other regions the game is called “el potro” (the vaulting horse), “chorro, morro, pico, tallo, qué” or “la uva” (the grape), and the guessing words change.
  • The “mother” is not part of either team and acts as referee.
  • It is a cousin of leapfrog, another game in which you jump over your friends’ backs.
  • Some 19th-century treatises on children’s games already describe similar games.
