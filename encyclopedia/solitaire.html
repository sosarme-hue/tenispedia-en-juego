Solitaire

Card games for one person; the most famous is Klondike.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Solitaire games, also called patience, appeared in northern Europe, probably in Germany or Scandinavia, at the end of the 18th century. The first known book about them was published in Germany in 1788. In the 19th century they became fashionable in France and England.

The Klondike variant owes its name to the Klondike gold rush in Canada at the end of the 19th century, where according to tradition it was played in the miners’ camps.

In 1990 Microsoft included solitaire in Windows 3.0 so that users could learn to use the mouse by dragging cards. It became one of the most used programs in history.

🏛️ Historical context

Solitaire appeared in northern Europe at the end of the 18th century, perhaps linked to fortune-telling with cards. In the 19th century it became fashionable in the drawing rooms of the French and English middle classes, and books with dozens of variants were published.

In 1990 Microsoft included a solitaire game in Windows 3.0 so that users could learn to use the mouse by dragging cards. It became one of the most used programs in the history of computing.

📖 How to play

  • 28 cards are laid out in 7 columns.
  • The columns are built down in alternating colours.
  • Four piles are built up from Ace to King, one per suit.
  • You win when all four piles are complete.

🎭 Anecdotes

It is said that Napoleon played solitaire during his exile on St Helena, and several solitaire games bear his name, although there is no evidence that he invented them.

Windows Solitaire was programmed by an intern, Wes Cherry, in 1988. He was never paid royalties for it, despite its being installed on hundreds of millions of computers.
