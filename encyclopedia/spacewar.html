Spacewar!

One of the first video games in history: two spaceships shooting at each other around a star.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Spacewar! was created in 1962 by a group of students and staff at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), led by Steve Russell, on a PDP-1 computer that cost about $120,000 at the time.

In the game, two ships, the “wedge” and the “needle”, fire torpedoes at each other while a star in the centre pulls them in with its gravity. There was a real starfield background, programmed from a map of the sky, and a “hyperspace” button for escaping.

The game was never sold, but it was copied onto almost every university computer in the United States. It inspired the creators of the first arcade machines, such as Computer Space (1971) by Nolan Bushnell, who would go on to found Atari.

🏛️ Historical context

In the early 1960s computers were huge, very expensive and used only in universities, the military and big companies. The space race between the US and the USSR was in all the news, and science fiction was very popular among students.

The students of MIT’s Tech Model Railroad Club, who created Spacewar!, are considered the first “hackers”, in the original sense of people who explore technology for fun.

📖 How to play

  • Each of the two players controls a ship.
  • The ships turn, thrust and fire torpedoes.
  • The gravity of the central star pulls the ships in.
  • Whoever destroys the opposing ship wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The game’s starfield was called Expensive Planetarium because it accurately reproduced the night sky on a very expensive computer.

Steve Russell said that he took months to start programming the game and that his colleagues had to “force” him to finish it, putting in front of him the mathematical routines he needed.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The players built the first video game controllers in history because the computer’s switches were awkward to use.
  • The PDP-1 had a round radar-style screen.
  • Spacewar! can be played today on emulators and, on a restored PDP-1, at the Computer History Museum in California.
  • In 1972 Rolling Stone magazine organised the first video game olympics with Spacewar!.
