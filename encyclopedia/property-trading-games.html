Property trading games

Board games of buying streets, building and collecting rent, whose origin is The Landlord’s Game.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1904 the American Elizabeth (Lizzie) Magie patented The Landlord’s Game, a game on a square board in which properties were bought and rents collected. Her aim was critical: to show how the accumulation of land impoverishes tenants, following the ideas of the economist Henry George.

The game was copied and changed by hand for years in universities and Quaker communities. In 1935 the Parker Brothers company marketed one of those versions under the name Monopoly, which became one of the best-selling games in history. Lizzie Magie received only 500 dollars for her patent.

Since then many original property trading games have been created. On Ludicopedia you can play Skyscrapers, our own version with Spanish cities.

🏛️ Historical context

Lizzie Magie was a follower of Henry George, an economist who argued for a single tax on land so that landlords would not grow rich without working. At the beginning of the 20th century, with industrialisation and the great railway and oil monopolies, inequality was a burning issue in the United States.

The 1935 version was a hit in the middle of the Great Depression: millions of people with no work or money enjoyed pretending to get rich with paper banknotes. Paradoxically, a game designed to criticise monopolies ended up celebrating them.

📖 How to play

  • Players move round a board of squares with two dice.
  • They can buy the properties they land on.
  • Whoever lands on someone else’s property pays rent.
  • Collecting groups of properties lets you build and charge more.

🎭 Anecdotes

During the Second World War, the British secret services hid silk maps, compasses and real money inside property-game boards sent by the Red Cross to Allied prisoners of war, to help them escape.

For decades it was said that the game had been invented by Charles Darrow, an unemployed salesman from Philadelphia. The true story of Lizzie Magie only became known in the 1970s thanks to the lawsuit of the economist Ralph Anspach.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Monopoly is a registered trademark; its origin was a game created to criticise landlords.
  • The streets of the original Monopoly are those of Atlantic City, New Jersey.
  • Lizzie Magie’s game had two sets of rules: one in which the richest player won and another that rewarded sharing.
  • In the classic Spanish edition, with Madrid streets, the most expensive property is the Paseo del Prado.
