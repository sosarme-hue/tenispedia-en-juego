Cinquillo

A simple game of laying cards out in order, starting from the fives.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Cinquillo is a traditional Spanish game known internationally as sevens (with other decks you start from the sevens). It is one of the first card games children learn because you only have to put cards in order.

Whoever has the five of coins starts, and from the fives the four suits are built up and down.

It is a game of patience and blocking: holding back the card another player needs can win you the game.

🏛️ Historical context

Cinquillo belongs to the family of “dominoes with cards” games, in which cards are laid out in order starting from a middle card. In France it is called “sept” or “domino”, and in England “Fan Tan” or “Sevens”.

In Spain it is a classic for family afternoons, because children learn it very quickly and it requires a little strategy to avoid being left with blocked cards.

📖 How to play

  • The whole 40-card deck is dealt.
  • Whoever has the five of coins starts.
  • You play cards next in sequence from the fives, or a new five.
  • The first to run out of cards wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

A classic strategy is to hold on to fives and end cards to block the others. Some players are so expert that they seem to know everyone’s cards just from what they pass on.

In England the similar game, Sevens, starts from the seven, the middle card of the French deck. In Spain you start from the five, the middle of the forty-card deck.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • If you can’t play a card you must pass: that is why it sometimes pays to block your opponent.
  • Whoever has the five of coins starts.
  • If you can’t play, you pass.
  • The first to run out of cards wins.
