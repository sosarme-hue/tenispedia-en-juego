♟️ Board games (21)

🪲
c. 3100 BC

Senet

One of the oldest known board games, played in Egypt for almost three thousand years.

🐍
c. 3000 BC

Mehen (the snake game)

A very ancient Egyptian race game played on a board shaped like a coiled snake.

🏺
c. 2600 BC

Royal Game of Ur

A Sumerian race game whose rules could be reconstructed thanks to a Babylonian clay tablet.

⚫
c. 2000 BC – 4th century BC

Go

A Chinese strategy game of surrounding territory with black and white stones. One of the deepest games in the world.

🌽
c. 200 BC – 16th century

Patolli

A Mesoamerican race game with marked beans as dice, much loved by the Aztecs.

🏛️
1st century BC

Ludus latrunculorum

The Romans’ “game of little soldiers”, a capture strategy game played on a grid.

⭕
1st century (or earlier)

Nine men’s morris

A strategy game in which you form lines of three pieces, “mills”, to capture your opponent’s pieces.

🛡️
4th–11th centuries

Hnefatafl (Viking game)

The Vikings’ board game: a king and his defenders try to escape from an army that surrounds them.

♟️
6th century

Chess

The king of strategy games: two armies of 16 pieces and the aim of giving checkmate.

🥚
6th–7th centuries (or earlier)

Mancala

A family of sowing games in which seeds are shared out among pits on a board.

🐘
8th–11th centuries

Xiangqi (Chinese chess)

Chinese chess, with a river across the middle of the board and pieces placed on the intersections.

✴️
10th century (or earlier)

Alquerque

A game of capturing by jumping on a board of lines, the direct ancestor of checkers.

🏯
10th–16th centuries

Shogi (Japanese chess)

Japanese chess, in which captured pieces can come back into play on your side.

⛀
12th century

Checkers (draughts)

A strategy game in which you capture by jumping diagonally and men become kings.

🪜
13th century

Snakes and ladders

A dice game in which ladders take you up and snakes take you down, born as a moral lesson in India.

🎲
16th century

Parcheesi (ludo)

A race game with dice and four pieces per player; in Spain, parchís is the great family classic.

🪿
16th century

Game of the Goose

A 63-square spiral track with geese, bridges, a well and a skull. Pure chance and tradition.

🎲
17th century (modern form)

Backgammon

A race game with dice and 15 pieces per player, heir to Persian and Roman games.

⚪
1883

Reversi

A game of two-coloured discs that flip over when they are surrounded.

⭐
1892

Chinese checkers

A jumping game on a six-pointed star board that, despite its name, was invented in Germany.

🔴
1974

Connect Four

A game of dropping discs into an upright grid until you line up four.

Tarot (card game)

18th century

Spain’s most popular partnership card game, full of signals, all-in bets (órdagos) and bluffs.

🧹
18th century

Escoba

A card game of adding up to fifteen by picking up cards from the table, a classic of the Spanish deck.

4️⃣
18th century

A four-player version of tresillo, the most elegant card game of 18th- and 19th-century Spain.

🂡
18th century

Solitaire

Card games for one person; the most famous is Klondike.

🌸
18th century

Hanafuda

The Japanese “flower cards”, with twelve suits that represent the months of the year.

🃏
19th century

Poker

The great betting card game, won with good hands or with good bluffs.

🥃
19th century

Julepe

A trick-taking card game in which anyone who plays and fails to win at least two tricks “takes a julepe” and has to pay.

👑
19th century

A trick-taking game with declarations: the twenty and the forty, and the tute of four kings or four knights.

7️⃣
19th century

Seven and a half

The “twenty-one” of the Spanish deck: you have to reach seven and a half without going over.

🤥
19th century

Truco

A card game of bluffing and signals, very popular in Argentina, Uruguay and other countries, and a sibling of Valencian truc.

🐒
19th century

Old Maid (la mona)

A children’s pairs game: you have to get rid of all your cards and not be left with the one without a pair, the “mona”.

5️⃣
19th century

Cinquillo

A simple game of laying cards out in order, starting from the fives.

😉
19th century

Guiñote

The great card game of Aragon: played in pairs, with trumps, declarations of twenty and forty and plenty of signals between partners.

🐴
20th century

Donkey (el burro)

A fast, noisy card game: you have to collect four matching cards by passing them to your neighbour, and whoever loses collects letters until they spell BURRO (donkey).

🌉
1925 (contract bridge)

Bridge

Considered the most intellectual card game, with an auction and tricks played in pairs.

🌈
20th century

Crazy Eights and colour-matching discard games

A family of games in which you get rid of your cards by playing one of the same suit or number as the last.

🧺
1939

Canasta

A partnership melding game with two decks, created in Montevideo.

🃏
20th century

Chinchón

A melding game of the rummy family, very popular in Spain and Argentina.

🎴
20th century

Remigio

A melding game with two French decks and jokers, related to rummy and chinchón.
