Bingo and lotteries

Games of chance with cards and drawn numbers, heirs to the Italian lottery.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Bingo descends from Lo Giuoco del Lotto d’Italia, the national lottery organised in Italy around 1530. In the 18th century it passed to France as le lotto, and in 19th-century Germany it was used to teach children multiplication tables and vocabulary.

The version with cards reached the United States in the 1920s under the name beano, because the numbers were marked with beans. According to tradition, the salesman Edwin Lowe renamed it bingo after hearing an excited player shout it.

In Spain bingo halls were legalised in 1977. The Christmas Lottery, with the children of San Ildefonso school singing out the numbers, has been held since 1812.

🏛️ Historical context

Bingo comes from the Italian lottery Lo Giuoco del Lotto d’Italia, first organised around 1530 after the lotteries of Genoa were unified. In the 18th century it passed to France, where it was played in aristocratic salons with cards and counters.

In the 19th century it was used in Germany to teach children to multiply and spell. In 1929 the American toy salesman Edwin S. Lowe saw “beano” being played at a fair in Georgia and marketed it under the name bingo.

📖 How to play

  • Each player has one or more cards with numbers.
  • Numbered balls are drawn at random.
  • Whoever completes a row calls “line”.
  • Whoever completes the card calls “bingo” and wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

Edwin Lowe said that a friend, on completing a card for the first time, shouted “bingo” instead of “beano”, and that he decided to keep that name. Nobody knows if it’s true, but the story has survived.

In the United Kingdom each number has a nickname: 88 is “two fat ladies”, 22 “two little ducks” and 11 “legs eleven”.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The Spanish Christmas Lottery is one of the oldest draws in the world still held without interruption.
  • Spain and the UK play with 90 balls; the US with 75.
  • 90-ball bingo has three-row cards.
  • At Christmas in Italy people play tombola, a relative of bingo.
