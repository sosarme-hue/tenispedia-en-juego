Dice

Dice are among the oldest gaming objects of humankind.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Before dice, people used knucklebones, animal bones that land on one of four sides. The oldest known cubic dice are about 5,000 years old and have been found at Shahr-i Sokhta (Iran) and in the Indus Valley.

On a modern die opposite faces add up to seven, a custom that already existed in antiquity. The Romans were such fans that there were laws against dice games, except during the Saturnalia festival.

In the 16th century the Italian mathematician Gerolamo Cardano wrote the first study of probability by analysing dice, and in the 17th century Pascal and Fermat founded probability theory from a gambling problem.

🏛️ Historical context

Dice are among the oldest gaming objects in the world. Before cubes, people used astragali, the ankle bones of lambs or goats, which have four different sides. The oldest known six-sided dice were found at Shahr-i Sokhta in Iran and are about 5,000 years old.

In Rome dice (alea) were a passion. Gambling was banned except during the Saturnalia, but the law was broken all the time. In the Middle Ages dice makers formed guilds, and the laws against loaded dice were very harsh.

📖 How to play

  • A normal die has six faces numbered 1 to 6.
  • Opposite faces add up to 7.
  • With two dice, the most likely total is 7.
  • There are dice with 4, 8, 10, 12 and 20 faces for role-playing games.

🎭 Anecdotes

When Julius Caesar crossed the River Rubicon with his army in 49 BC, defying the Senate, he said “alea iacta est”, “the die is cast”.

In 1654 the Chevalier de Méré, a French gambler, asked the mathematician Blaise Pascal why he was losing money on certain dice bets. Pascal wrote to Pierre de Fermat to solve it, and probability theory was born from those letters.
