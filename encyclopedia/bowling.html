Bowling (skittles)

Knocking down pins by rolling a ball: from the Egyptians’ game to León skittles and modern bowling alleys.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The British archaeologist Flinders Petrie found a set of stone pins and balls in an Egyptian tomb from about 3200 BC, which look like a forerunner of the game. In medieval Europe skittles was very popular in Germany, England and Spain.

The religious reformer Martin Luther is said to have fixed the number of pins at nine in Germany. In the Iberian Peninsula there are dozens of traditional forms: bolo palma in Cantabria, León skittles, Asturian bolos, birlo, Canarian bolo…

In the United States people switched to ten pins in the 19th century, and bowling alleys with indoor lanes and automatic machines spread around the world from the 1950s.

🏛️ Historical context

In the Middle Ages skittles was the Sunday and holiday sport in village squares, and kings sometimes banned it because people stopped practising archery, which was needed for war.

In the 20th century, in the United States, bowling alleys became a social meeting place. The invention in 1952 of the machine that resets the pins automatically changed the game for ever.

📖 How to play

  • The pins are set up in a formation.
  • Taking turns, the ball is thrown from a fixed distance.
  • The pins knocked down are counted.
  • In modern bowling, knocking down all ten at once is a strike.

🎭 Anecdotes

An English legend says that in 1588 Admiral Francis Drake was playing bowls when he was told the Spanish Armada was approaching, and said there was time to finish the game before fighting.

Ten-pin bowling is said to have been invented in the United States to get round a law banning nine-pin bowling, although it is not clear whether this is true.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Cantabria bolo palma is almost a regional sport, with skittle alleys in nearly every village.
  • A perfect game in modern bowling is worth 300 points.
  • León skittles uses a wooden ball.
  • There are forms of skittles on grass, on earth and on wooden lanes.
