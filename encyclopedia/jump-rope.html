Skipping rope

Skipping, alone or in a group, with songs that keep the rhythm: a playground game of every age.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Jumping with a rope or a vine is a very old activity. There are depictions of similar games in Egypt and ancient China, and in medieval Europe children skipped with hemp ropes or flexible branches.

In the 17th and 18th centuries skipping was mostly a boys’ game. In the 19th century, in Europe and the United States, it became a girls’ game, accompanied by songs and rhymes that kept the rhythm and decided who jumped in or out.

Today, as well as a children’s game, it is a training exercise for boxers and athletes, and there is a competitive sport, rope skipping, with world championships.

🏛️ Historical context

The streets and squares of the 19th and 20th centuries, with little traffic, were the great playground of childhood. There, songs and games were passed from one child to another with no need for expensive toys.

Folklorists who studied children’s games in the 20th century collected hundreds of skipping songs, some centuries old, such as fragments of medieval ballads.

📖 How to play

  • Two people turn a long rope and the others jump.
  • A song is sung that marks when to jump in, skip or jump out.
  • If someone treads on the rope or stops, it is their turn to “turn” the rope.
  • You can also skip alone with a short rope.

🎭 Anecdotes

Many skipping songs preserve lines from old ballads that were sung centuries ago, so children have unknowingly acted as carriers of folk poetry.

In double dutch, a variant born in New York, two ropes turn in opposite directions, and there are championships with spectacular acrobatics.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Spain, songs like “Al pasar la barca” and “El cocherito leré” were sung while skipping.
  • The Spanish word comba comes from the curved shape the rope makes as it turns.
  • Boxers skip to improve their coordination and stamina.
  • In American English it is called jump rope.
