Space Invaders

A Japanese arcade machine in which you shoot at waves of aliens descending the screen.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Space Invaders was created in 1978 by the Japanese designer Tomohiro Nishikado for the company Taito. He was inspired by Breakout and by science fiction films, and he had to design his own hardware because the computers of the time could not move so many enemies.

It was a gigantic hit: in Japan arcades opened devoted entirely to Space Invaders, and it is said to have caused a shortage of 100-yen coins. It was the first great worldwide success of arcade video games.

Its influence was enormous: it introduced saved high scores, music that speeds up and enemies that react to the player. Many video game designers say it was the game that made them want to make games.

🏛️ Historical context

In the late 1970s Japan was a technological and electronics powerhouse, with companies making televisions, calculators and computers. Arcade machines were a growing business in bars and amusement arcades.

Space Invaders arrived at the height of the science fiction craze, a year after the release of Star Wars (1977), and its pixelated aliens became a pop culture icon.

📖 How to play

  • The player controls a cannon at the bottom.
  • The aliens come down in rows and shoot.
  • You have to destroy them before they reach the ground.
  • Shields protect the player but are gradually destroyed.

🎭 Anecdotes

The story of the coin shortage in Japan has been repeated many times, but according to some researchers it is a myth: the Bank of Japan increased production of 100-yen coins for other reasons.

The aliens were designed in the shape of an octopus, a squid and a crab. At first Nishikado wanted human soldiers, but Taito felt that shooting at people would be frowned upon.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The aliens move faster the fewer there are left, because the computer had less work to do: a “bug” that became the essence of the game.
  • The sound of the aliens’ footsteps, four repeating notes, is one of the first continuous background soundtracks in video games.
  • The flying saucer that crosses the top gives extra points.
  • The French street artist Invader has stuck mosaics of the aliens in cities all over the world.
