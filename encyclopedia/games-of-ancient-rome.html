🏛️ What games did the ancient Romans play?

The Romans were keen players. They played in taverns, in the baths, in army camps and even on the steps of public buildings, where boards carved into the stone can still be seen today. Many of their games have come down to us under other names.

🎲 A passion for dice

The most widespread game in Rome was dice. People played with six-sided dice, the tesserae, and with knucklebones, the tali, made from lamb bones. The best throw with four knucklebones was called “Venus”, when all four different sides came up, and the worst “the dog”.

Gambling for money was forbidden by law, except during the Saturnalia, the December festival, when everyone was allowed to play, slaves included. Even so, people were so keen that the ban was often ignored. The historian Suetonius tells us that the emperor Augustus played dice with his family and friends, and that the emperor Claudius even wrote a book about the game.

To prevent cheating, the Romans used dice cups and dice towers: little towers into which the dice were dropped, bouncing around inside before coming out. Knucklebones are still used today in many children’s games.

♟️ Board games

Ludus latrunculorum, the “game of soldiers”, was a strategy game in which you had to surround your opponent’s pieces to capture them. It was the Romans’ great thinking game, and boards have been found all over the empire, including Hispania.

Another favourite was tabula, the direct ancestor of backgammon, in which pieces moved according to dice throws. Terni lapilli was very similar to our tic-tac-toe and to nine men’s morris, and its boards appear carved into the steps of the Basilica Julia, in the Roman forum: that is where citizens waited for trials to begin.

🌰 Children’s games

Roman children played with nuts just as children today play with marbles: they threw them, stacked them in little towers and tried to knock them down. The Latin expression “to leave the nuts behind” meant to stop being a child and grow up.

They also played with spinning tops, hoops, rag or bone dolls and wooden hobby horses, and at hide-and-seek. Some of these games already existed in Greece and Egypt, and the Romans took them to every corner of the empire.

🏟️ Games and society

For the Romans, games had two sides: they were healthy fun at festivals and in the family, but also a vice when people gambled too much. Writers of the time criticise gamblers who lose everything in taverns, and at the same time fondly describe the emperors’ games.

What is most surprising is how little some things have changed: the Romans already cheated with loaded dice, already got cross when they lost and already carved boards into any flat stone to play a game while they waited.
