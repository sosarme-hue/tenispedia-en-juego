Toad in the hole (juego de la rana)

An aiming game of throwing metal discs into the mouth of an iron frog and the holes of a table.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The frog game consists of throwing metal discs, called tejos or chapas, at a wooden table with holes and an iron frog with its mouth open. Each hole is worth points, and getting a disc into the frog’s mouth gives the top prize.

Its origin is unclear. A Peruvian legend says that the Incas threw gold stones at the frogs in lakes, and that if a frog jumped with the stone, a wish came true. Other theories place it in the taverns of 18th-century France.

In Spain it was very popular in taverns, open-air eateries and fairs in the north and in Madrid from the 19th century, and it is still played in some areas. In Peru it is almost a national game, the “sapo” (toad).

🏛️ Historical context

In the 19th century, taverns and open-air eateries were where workers met on holidays. Aiming games, such as the frog, skittles or pétanque, went along with food and drink.

The game travelled to the Americas with emigrants and there mixed with local legends, such as that of the Incas and the sacred frogs of Lake Titicaca.

📖 How to play

  • The discs are thrown from a fixed distance.
  • Each hole has a different score.
  • The frog’s mouth gives the most points.
  • The first to reach the agreed score wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In early 20th-century Madrid there were open-air eateries on the outskirts where people played the frog game on Sundays, as the novels and comic plays of the time describe.

The Inca legend of the frog, often repeated in Peru, says that the big prize for getting the disc into the mouth recalls the Incas’ ancient wish for the sacred frog to swallow their offering.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In Peru the game is called sapo, and there are national championships.
  • The frog is usually made of iron or brass.
  • The discs are metal discs the size of a large coin.
  • Some tables also have little windmills and bridges with special scores.
