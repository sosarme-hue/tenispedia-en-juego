World conquest games

Military strategy board games on a map of the world, such as Risk.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1957 the French filmmaker Albert Lamorisse, director of the short film The Red Balloon, created La Conquête du Monde, a game of conquering territories on a world map with armies and dice. In 1959 Parker Brothers published it in the United States as Risk.

The game popularised the idea of dividing the world into territories grouped by continent and of settling battles with dice, something many later games have copied.

It is a classic for long afternoons: a game can last several hours.

🏛️ Historical context

War games with maps have a long tradition: in the 19th century the Prussian army used Kriegsspiel to train its officers, and in 1913 the writer H. G. Wells published Little Wars, a set of rules for battles with toy soldiers.

The world conquest game was born in 1957, at the height of the Cold War, when the world was divided into blocs and the threat of war between the great powers was very real. Its success opened the door to the wargames of the 1960s and 1970s.

📖 How to play

  • The map is divided into 42 territories and 6 continents.
  • Each turn you receive armies according to your territories.
  • You attack neighbouring territories by rolling dice.
  • Whoever conquers the world or completes their secret mission wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The game’s creator, the Frenchman Albert Lamorisse, was a film director: his short film The Red Balloon won the Oscar for best original screenplay in 1957, the same year he published his conquest game.

For years the secret mission cards allowed shorter, more mysterious games; in France the original game already came with secret missions, while the 1959 American version could only be won by conquering the world.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The game’s creator won an Oscar for his short film The Red Balloon.
  • The classic map divides the world into 42 territories and six continents.
  • Australia is usually considered the easiest continent to defend because it has only one way in.
  • Some games can last more than eight hours.
