Street Fighter II

Capcom’s fighting game that packed the arcades in the 1990s, with eight fighters from around the world and their special moves.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Street Fighter II: The World Warrior came out in arcades in 1991, made by the Japanese company Capcom. The first game, from 1987, had had little success, but the second became a worldwide phenomenon.

It let you choose between eight fighters from different countries, each with their own style and special moves: Ryu and Ken, Chun-Li, Guile, Blanka, Zangief, Dhalsim and E. Honda. Special moves were made with combinations of joystick movements and buttons, such as the famous Hadouken.

Its great novelty was the duel between two people on the same machine. Arcades filled with crowds around the machines, and the culture of fighting game tournaments seen today in esports was born.

🏛️ Historical context

In the early 1990s arcades were beginning to feel the competition from home consoles. Street Fighter II gave them a new lease of life, because the experience of playing face to face against another person could not easily be repeated at home.

Its success led to dozens of fighting games imitating it, and made one-on-one fighting one of the most important video game genres.

📖 How to play

  • Two fighters face each other in best-of-three-rounds bouts.
  • A round is won by draining your opponent’s energy, or by having more energy when time runs out.
  • There are light, medium and heavy punches and kicks.
  • Special moves are made with joystick movements and a button.
  • In single-player mode you have to beat all the fighters and four final bosses.

🎭 Anecdotes

Combos, chaining hits so that your opponent cannot defend, appeared by accident: they were a small bug in the game that the programmers decided to keep because players liked them.

In 1990s arcades, players placed a coin on the machine to claim the next turn and challenge whoever was playing. It was known as “calling next”.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • One of the four final bosses is Vega, a Spanish fighter who combines bullfighting with ninjutsu, and his stage is a tavern with flamenco dancing in the background.
  • Ryu and Ken share almost all their moves, but each has his own personality.
  • Chun-Li was one of the first female lead characters in a fighting game.
  • The Super Nintendo version sold more than six million copies.
