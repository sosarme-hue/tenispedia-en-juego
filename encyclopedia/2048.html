2048

A puzzle of sliding numbered tiles to join matching pairs until you reach 2048.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

2048 was created in March 2014 by the Italian Gabriele Cirulli, a 19-year-old student, over a weekend, as an exercise to learn programming. He published it free online with open source code.

It was based on earlier, very similar games, such as 1024 and Threes!, released a few weeks before. Within days it went viral: millions of people played it in the first week, and hundreds of versions appeared.

Its simplicity, with four directions and a single rule, made it perfect for mobile phones. It has also been widely used to teach programming and the idea of powers of two.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 2010s smartphones took video games to everyone, and simple games with short sessions, called casual games, boomed.

2048 is an example of how the internet and open source allow a game made by one person at home to become famous around the world in days.

📖 How to play

  • The board has 4 × 4 squares.
  • All the tiles slide at once in one direction.
  • Two equal tiles that collide merge into one with their sum.
  • The aim is to make a 2048 tile before running out of moves.

🎭 Anecdotes

Cirulli made no money from 2048 because he published it free and without adverts. He said he did not think it right to make money from an idea that was not entirely his own.

The creators of Threes!, who had spent more than a year designing their game, published a letter explaining that 2048 was almost a copy of theirs.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The highest tile theoretically possible on a 4 × 4 board is 131,072.
  • All the tiles are powers of two.
  • A good strategy is to keep the highest tile in a corner.
  • There are versions with 3 × 3, 5 × 5 and 6 × 6 squares.
