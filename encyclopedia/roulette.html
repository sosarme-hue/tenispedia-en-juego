Roulette

A casino game in which a ball decides the winning number on a spinning wheel.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Modern roulette appears in Paris at the end of the 18th century; in 1796 a very similar game was already described at the Palais Royal. Its name means “little wheel” in French. Some link it to Blaise Pascal’s experiments with perpetual motion, although there is no evidence.

In 1843 the brothers François and Louis Blanc introduced single-zero roulette in Bad Homburg (Germany), and later took it to the Monte Carlo casino, which made it famous.

In the United States the wheel with 0 and 00, worse for the player, was kept. On Ludicopedia you only play with pretend chips.

🏛️ Historical context

Modern roulette was born in 18th-century Paris from Italian and English wheel games such as biribi and roly-poly. In 1796 it was already described in a French novel with its red and black pockets and the zero.

In 1843 the brothers François and Louis Blanc introduced single-zero roulette, which gave the bank a smaller edge, at the casino in Bad Homburg, Germany. When Germany banned gambling, François Blanc went to Monte Carlo, where he ran the famous casino that made Monaco the capital of gambling.

📖 How to play

  • European roulette has 37 numbers: 0 to 36.
  • You bet on numbers, colours, odd or even, dozens…
  • A single number pays 35 to 1.
  • The zero means that, in the long run, the house always wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1913, at the Monte Carlo casino, the ball landed on black 26 times in a row. Many players lost fortunes betting on red, convinced it was “due”. That is the origin of the name “gambler’s fallacy” or Monte Carlo fallacy.

A legend says that the roulette numbers add up to 666, the “number of the beast”, and that François Blanc made a pact with the devil. The sum is true, but the pact is just a story.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The numbers on a roulette wheel add up to 666, which is why it is sometimes called “the devil’s game”.
  • European roulette has 37 pockets: 1 to 36 and the zero.
  • On a roulette wheel, all the numbers add up to 666.
  • The numbers are not in order on the wheel, so as to spread reds, blacks, odds and evens.
