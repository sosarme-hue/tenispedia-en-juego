Kite

A flying toy of paper or cloth held by a string, invented in China 2,500 years ago.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Kites were invented in China around the 5th century BC. They are credited to the philosophers Mozi and Lu Ban, who are said to have built wooden and bamboo birds that flew. With the invention of paper they became lighter and cheaper.

In China they were used for military purposes, such as sending signals or measuring distances, and also in religious ceremonies. From there they spread to Korea, Japan, India and South-East Asia, where there are great kite festivals.

They reached Europe in the Middle Ages and were much used in science: in 1752 Benjamin Franklin used a kite to show that lightning is electricity, and at the beginning of the 20th century the Wright brothers tested their ideas about flight with kites.

🏛️ Historical context

In ancient China kites were prestigious objects, and they were believed to have magical powers: in some regions the string was let go so that the kite would carry bad luck away.

In Enlightenment Europe the kite went from toy to scientific tool, and it was used to measure temperature and wind at altitude, and to raise radio aerials.

📖 How to play

  • You hold the kite by a string and run into the wind.
  • When the wind lifts it, you let out the string little by little.
  • You steer it by pulling and releasing the string.
  • In some countries there are kite fights in which you have to cut your opponent’s string.

🎭 Anecdotes

A Chinese legend says that General Han Xin used a kite to measure the distance to the wall of an enemy palace, so that he could dig a tunnel to it.

In 1901 Guglielmo Marconi received in Newfoundland the first radio signal sent across the Atlantic, using an aerial held up by a kite.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • In India, Pakistan and Afghanistan kite fights are held with strings coated in ground glass.
  • The Spanish word cometa comes from the comets in the sky, because of its long tail.
  • In Argentina it is called barrilete and in Mexico papalote.
  • There are kites of more than 1,000 square metres.
