Generala and poker dice games

Games of rolling five dice to make poker hands.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Generala descends from Spanish poker dice games, very popular in bars, in which five dice showing card faces (A, K, Q, J, 10 and 9) are rolled up to three times to get the best hand.

In Latin America generala is played with ordinary dice and a score sheet. In 1956 an American company marketed a very similar game called Yahtzee, now a registered trademark, which became very popular.

The best combination, five matching dice, is called a “generala”.

🏛️ Historical context

Generala descends from 19th-century combination dice games such as poker dice, played in taverns and barracks with leather cups. It is very popular in Argentina, Uruguay and Paraguay.

In the 1950s a Canadian couple created a similar dice game on their yacht, which they later sold to an American toy maker under the name Yahtzee, and it became very popular around the world.

📖 How to play

  • Five dice are rolled up to three times, keeping the ones you want.
  • You try to make a straight, full house, four of a kind or generala.
  • Each hand is scored in a box on the sheet.
  • Whoever has the most points when the sheet is full wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

The name “generala” comes from the best combination, five matching dice: it is the “general”, as if it were the highest rank in the army.

In Argentina it is played a lot on holiday and at family gatherings, and some people always keep a dice cup in the glove box of their car.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The chance of rolling five matching dice in a single throw is 1 in 1,296.
  • Rolling a generala on the first throw, a “generala servida”, wins the game.
  • It is played with five dice and up to three throws per turn.
  • The straight can run from 1 to 5 or from 2 to 6.
