La quiniela (football pools)

The game of predicting football results with a 1, an X or a 2, a Spanish tradition since 1946.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The Spanish quiniela was created in 1946, when the state set up pool betting on the matches of the football League. The first round was on 22 September 1946, and the coupon had seven matches.

The system is simple: for each match you mark 1 if you think the home team will win, X if you expect a draw and 2 if the away team will win. Those who get most results right share the prizes.

For decades filling in the quiniela was a weekend ritual all over Spain. Today the coupon has fourteen matches plus the Pleno al 15, in which you predict each team’s goals.

🏛️ Historical context

In post-war Spain, the quiniela gave many people the dream of changing their lives with a stroke of luck. The results were followed on the radio on Sunday afternoons, coupon in hand.

Part of the money raised goes to social causes and to sport. Betting with money is only allowed for adults.

📖 How to play

  • The coupon shows a list of that round’s football matches.
  • For each match you mark 1 (home win), X (draw) or 2 (away win).
  • You can make multiple bets by marking two or three signs on some matches.
  • In the Pleno al 15 you predict each team’s goals in one particular match.
  • There are prizes from 10 correct results, and the special prize is for getting all 14 matches and the Pleno al 15 right.

🎭 Anecdotes

For years some people filled in the quiniela with curious methods: rolling a die, asking the children or following the tips of the bar’s betting syndicate.

On Sunday afternoons, many families listened to the match results on the radio and checked their coupon sign by sign.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The name quiniela comes from an old Basque pelota game in which people bet on five players or pairs, from quina, five.
  • 1, X and 2 have become part of the language: in Spanish, “making an X” means drawing.
  • In other countries it has other names, such as Totocalcio in Italy or Toto in Germany.
  • Syndicates pool money to place multiple bets with better chances.
