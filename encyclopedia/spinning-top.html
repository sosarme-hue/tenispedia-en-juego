Spinning top

A wooden top with a metal tip that spins when you throw it with a string.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Spinning tops are among the oldest toys: clay tops from around 3500 BC have been found at Ur in Mesopotamia, and Plato and Virgil mention them in their works.

In Spain it is called peonza, trompo, baldufa (in Catalan) or peón, and there are many traditional games: making it spin on your hand, knocking coins out of a circle, or “killing” your opponent’s top.

Its stable spin is explained by the gyroscopic effect, the same one that keeps a moving bicycle upright.

🏛️ Historical context

Tops appear in almost every culture, from ancient Mesopotamia to China and Mesoamerica. In Greece and Rome whipping tops were used, and the poets Virgil and Callimachus mention them.

In 20th-century Spain the top was the street toy par excellence, along with marbles. It was played with a wound string, and there were games of “killing” an opponent’s top or catching it in your hand while it spun.

📖 How to play

  • Wind the string from the tip upwards.
  • Throw it with a flick of the wrist, pulling the string.
  • Whoever keeps theirs spinning longest wins.
  • There are games of hitting a circle or knocking objects out.

🎭 Anecdotes

In the 1990s and 2000s, battling-top toys inspired by the Japanese tradition of beigoma caused a sensation around the world, proving that a 5,000-year-old game still worked.

Engineers use the principle of the top, the gyroscope, to stabilise aircraft, satellites and mobile phones. An ancient toy lies behind the most modern technology.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The top is also a scientific instrument: the gyroscopes in aircraft work on the same principle.
  • In different parts of Spain it is called trompo, peón, pirindola or bailarina.
  • The metal tip of the top is called the púa or rejón.
  • A well-thrown top can spin for more than a minute.
