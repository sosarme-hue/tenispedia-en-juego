Rubik’s Cube

The best-selling three-dimensional puzzle in history.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

In 1974 the Hungarian architecture lecturer Ernő Rubik created a cube of rotating pieces to teach his students to think in three dimensions. It took him a month to solve it himself.

It was patented in Hungary in 1975 and from 1980 was sold worldwide under his name, which is now a registered trademark. More than 450 million are estimated to have been sold.

There are speedcubing competitions in which the best solve it in under four seconds.

🏛️ Historical context

Ernő Rubik was a lecturer in architecture and design in Budapest, in 1970s communist Hungary. He built the cube as a tool to explain movement in three dimensions to his students, and it took him a month to solve it for the first time.

Leaving an Eastern Bloc country was not easy, but in 1980 the cube reached the West and caused a sensation: more than 100 million were sold in three years. It was a symbol of the eighties, with books, competitions and even a cartoon series.

📖 How to play

  • Each face has 9 squares of one colour.
  • You turn the faces to mix the colours.
  • The aim is to make each face a single colour again.
  • There are step-by-step methods for solving it.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 2010 a group of mathematicians, with the help of Google’s computers, proved that any scrambled cube can be solved in 20 moves or fewer. That number is called “God’s number”.

The American Max Park and the Chinese Yusheng Du have set world records of under four seconds. There are championships in which it is solved blindfolded or one-handed.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Any position of the cube can be solved in 20 moves or fewer: this is known as “God’s number”.
  • The cube has 43 quintillion possible combinations.
  • It is one of the best-selling toys in history.
  • Rubik thought it would be impossible to scramble it and put it back in order.
