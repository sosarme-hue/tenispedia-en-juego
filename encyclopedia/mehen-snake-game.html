Mehen (the snake game)

A very ancient Egyptian race game played on a board shaped like a coiled snake.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Mehen is, together with senet, one of the oldest board games of Egypt. Its board is a stone or pottery disc shaped like a coiled snake, whose body is divided into spaces leading from the tail to the head, in the centre.

It was played mainly in the Predynastic period and the Old Kingdom, between 3000 and 2300 BC. Then it disappeared from Egypt, although it went on being played in Cyprus and elsewhere in the eastern Mediterranean.

Mehen was also the name of a snake god who protected the sun god, Ra, on his night journey through the underworld. As with senet, the game probably had a religious meaning.

🏛️ Historical context

In the time of mehen the first kingdoms of the Nile valley and hieroglyphic writing were taking shape. Games already appear in elite tombs as valuable objects that the deceased wished to take with them.

In the paintings of the tomb of Hesy-Ra, a high official of the pharaoh Djoser, a senet board, a mehen board and a board of twenty squares appear together: a small games library for eternity.

📖 How to play

  • The original rules are unknown; these are a reconstruction.
  • Each player has lion-shaped pieces and small balls.
  • Pieces move from the tail towards the snake’s head according to a throw.
  • On reaching the centre, the pieces come back and the lions can capture.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 1920 the British ethnographer Harold MacMichael described the hyena game of the Baggara of Sudan: players took their “mother” from the village to a well in the centre of a spiral and back; the first mother to return released a hyena that ate the others’. It is thought to be a distant descendant of mehen.

Many mehen boards have a different number of spaces, from a few dozen to several hundred, which suggests there were several versions.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • A very similar game, called the hyena game, was played in Sudan until the 20th century.
  • The lion-shaped pieces are among the oldest known figurative game pieces.
  • The god Mehen was shown as a snake coiled around Ra.
  • Some boards were made of limestone with the snake’s head carved in relief.
