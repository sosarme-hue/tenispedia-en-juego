⏳ The history of games

Five thousand years of games, from the senet of ancient Egypt to video games. Tap any game to read its full history.

c. 5000 BC

🦴 Knucklebones (astragali)

One of the oldest games in the world: throwing lamb ankle bones, the ancestors of dice. (Ancient world (Greece, Rome, Asia))

c. 3500 BC

🌀 Spinning top

A wooden top with a metal tip that spins when you throw it with a string. (Mesopotamia and Greece)

c. 3200 BC (forerunners)

🎳 Bowling (skittles)

Knocking down pins by rolling a ball: from the Egyptians’ game to León skittles and modern bowling alleys. (Egypt and Europe)

c. 3100 BC

🪲 Senet

One of the oldest known board games, played in Egypt for almost three thousand years. (Ancient Egypt)

c. 3000 BC

🎲 Dice

Dice are among the oldest gaming objects of humankind. (Mesopotamia, India and Egypt)

Antiquity

🔮 Marbles

Little balls flicked with a finger to hit others or knock them into a hole. (Ancient Egypt and Rome)

c. 3000 BC

🐍 Mehen (the snake game)

A very ancient Egyptian race game played on a board shaped like a coiled snake. (Ancient Egypt)

c. 2600 BC

🏺 Royal Game of Ur

A Sumerian race game whose rules could be reconstructed thanks to a Babylonian clay tablet. (Mesopotamia (modern Iraq))

Antiquity

🪢 Skipping rope

Skipping, alone or in a group, with songs that keep the rhythm: a playground game of every age. (Egypt, China and Europe)

Antiquity

❌ Tic-tac-toe

The simplest pencil-and-paper game: three symbols in a row on a 3 × 3 grid. (Ancient Egypt and Rome)

c. 2000 BC – 4th century BC

⚫ Go

A Chinese strategy game of surrounding territory with black and white stones. One of the deepest games in the world. (China)

c. 500 BC

🪀 Yo-yo

A disc that goes up and down a string: a toy from ancient Greece that became a worldwide craze in the 20th century. (Ancient Greece and the Philippines)

5th century BC

🪁 Kite

A flying toy of paper or cloth held by a string, invented in China 2,500 years ago. (China)

Antiquity

🐔 Blind man’s buff

A blindfolded player tries to catch the others and guess who they are. Goya painted it, and it was already played in ancient Greece. (Ancient Greece and Europe)

c. 200 BC – 16th century

🌽 Patolli

A Mesoamerican race game with marked beans as dice, much loved by the Aztecs. (Mesoamerica (Aztecs and earlier peoples))

1st century BC

🏛️ Ludus latrunculorum

The Romans’ “game of little soldiers”, a capture strategy game played on a grid. (Ancient Rome)

1st century (or earlier)

⭕ Nine men’s morris

A strategy game in which you form lines of three pieces, “mills”, to capture your opponent’s pieces. (Roman Empire and medieval Europe)

2nd century

🙈 Hide and seek

One player counts with eyes closed while the others hide. (Ancient Greece)

4th–11th centuries

🛡️ Hnefatafl (Viking game)

The Vikings’ board game: a king and his defenders try to escape from an army that surrounds them. (Scandinavia)

Antiquity or Middle Ages

🦶 Hopscotch

A game of hopping on one foot through squares drawn on the ground. (Roman Empire (tradition))

6th century

♟️ Chess

The king of strategy games: two armies of 16 pieces and the aim of giving checkmate. (India)

6th–7th centuries (or earlier)

🥚 Mancala

A family of sowing games in which seeds are shared out among pits on a board. (Africa)

8th–11th centuries

🐘 Xiangqi (Chinese chess)

Chinese chess, with a river across the middle of the board and pieces placed on the intersections. (China)

10th century (or earlier)

✴️ Alquerque

A game of capturing by jumping on a board of lines, the direct ancestor of checkers. (Near East and al-Andalus)

10th–16th centuries

🏯 Shogi (Japanese chess)

Japanese chess, in which captured pieces can come back into play on your side. (Japan)

12th century

⛀ Checkers (draughts)

A strategy game in which you capture by jumping diagonally and men become kings. (Southern France and the Iberian Peninsula)

12th century (China); 18th century (Europe)

🁫 Dominoes

A game of rectangular tiles with spots, joined end to end by matching numbers. (China)

13th century

🪜 Snakes and ladders

A dice game in which ladders take you up and snakes take you down, born as a moral lesson in India. (India)

14th century

🎴 The Spanish deck

The deck of coins, cups, swords and clubs, heir to the first playing cards that reached Europe. (Iberian Peninsula)

Middle Ages

🦘 Leapfrog (pídola)

The game of vaulting over a crouching friend, with your hands on their back, known in English as leapfrog. (Europe)

15th century

🔮 Tarot (card game)

Before it was used for fortune-telling, tarot was a card game of the Italian nobility. (Northern Italy)

15th–16th centuries

🎟️ Lottery

Number draws with prizes, from Renaissance lotteries to Spain’s famous Christmas draw. (Italy and the Low Countries)

15th century

🎱 Billiards

A game of striking balls with a cue on a table covered in green cloth. (France)

around 1480

🂡 The French deck

The deck of spades, hearts, diamonds and clubs, the most widely used in the world. (France)

1530

🎱 Bingo and lotteries

Games of chance with cards and drawn numbers, heirs to the Italian lottery. (Italy)

16th century (kai-awase)

🧩 Memory (pairs)

A game of finding matching pairs of cards by remembering where they are. (Japan and Europe)

16th century

🎲 Parcheesi (ludo)

A race game with dice and four pieces per player; in Spain, parchís is the great family classic. (India (pachisi))

16th century

🪿 Game of the Goose

A 63-square spiral track with geese, bridges, a well and a skull. Pure chance and tradition. (Italy)

17th century (modern form)

🎲 Backgammon

A race game with dice and 15 pieces per player, heir to Persian and Roman games. (Persia and the Roman world)

18th century

🎴 Brisca

A simple, quick trick-taking game with the Spanish deck, in which the ace and the three are worth the most. (France and Italy)

17th–18th centuries

🂮 Blackjack (twenty-one)

A casino game in which you must get as close to 21 as possible without going over, playing against the dealer. (Spain and France)

18th century

🤫 Mus

Spain’s most popular partnership card game, full of signals, all-in bets (órdagos) and bluffs. (Basque Country and Navarre)

18th century

🧹 Escoba

A card game of adding up to fifteen by picking up cards from the table, a classic of the Spanish deck. (Italy and Spain)

18th century

4️⃣ Cuatrola

A four-player version of tresillo, the most elegant card game of 18th- and 19th-century Spain. (Spain)

1766

🧩 Jigsaw puzzle

A picture cut into interlocking pieces that you have to put back together. (England)

18th century

🂡 Solitaire

Card games for one person; the most famous is Klondike. (Northern Europe)

18th century

🌸 Hanafuda

The Japanese “flower cards”, with twelve suits that represent the months of the year. (Japan)

18th century

🎡 Roulette

A casino game in which a ball decides the winning number on a spinning wheel. (France)

around 1800

🔺 Tangram

A Chinese puzzle of seven pieces that can make thousands of shapes. (China)

18th–19th centuries

🐸 Toad in the hole (juego de la rana)

An aiming game of throwing metal discs into the mouth of an iron frog and the holes of a table. (Spain, France and Latin America)

19th century

🃏 Poker

The great betting card game, won with good hands or with good bluffs. (United States)

19th century

🥃 Julepe

A trick-taking card game in which anyone who plays and fails to win at least two tricks “takes a julepe” and has to pay. (Spain)

19th century

👑 Tute

A trick-taking game with declarations: the twenty and the forty, and the tute of four kings or four knights. (Italy)

19th century

🀄 Mahjong

A Chinese game of 144 tiles, with rules similar to rummy, very popular in Asia. (China)

19th century

🎲 Generala and poker dice games

Games of rolling five dice to make poker hands. (Spain and Latin America)

19th century

7️⃣ Seven and a half

The “twenty-one” of the Spanish deck: you have to reach seven and a half without going over. (Spain and Italy)

19th century

🤥 Truco

A card game of bluffing and signals, very popular in Argentina, Uruguay and other countries, and a sibling of Valencian truc. (Spain and the River Plate)

19th century

🥢 Mikado (pick-up sticks)

A game of skill that consists of removing sticks from a pile without moving the others. (Europe (with roots in Asia))

19th century

🎯 Carrom

The “finger billiards” of South Asia: you flick a disc to send pieces into the corners of the board. (India)

19th century

🐒 Old Maid (la mona)

A children’s pairs game: you have to get rid of all your cards and not be left with the one without a pair, the “mona”. (Europe)

19th century

5️⃣ Cinquillo

A simple game of laying cards out in order, starting from the fives. (Spain)

19th century

😉 Guiñote

The great card game of Aragon: played in pairs, with trumps, declarations of twenty and forty and plenty of signals between partners. (Aragon (Spain))

1883

⚪ Reversi

A game of two-coloured discs that flip over when they are surrounded. (England)

1883

🗼 Tower of Hanoi

A puzzle of moving a tower of discs from one peg to another without ever putting a large disc on a small one. (France)

19th century

🪢 Hangman

A game of guessing a word letter by letter before the drawing is complete. (England)

late 19th century
