Hanafuda

The Japanese “flower cards”, with twelve suits that represent the months of the year.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Playing cards reached Japan in the 16th century with Portuguese sailors, who played with Latin-suited decks like the Spanish one. The Japanese created their own decks, but the shogunate banned them several times because of gambling.

To get round the ban, at the end of the 18th century a deck without numbers was created, with the flowers and plants of each month of the year: hanafuda, “flower cards”. It has 48 cards, four for each month.

In 1889 Fusajirō Yamauchi founded a small company in Kyoto to make hanafuda. That company was called Nintendo, and almost a century later it would become one of the world’s great video game companies.

🏛️ Historical context

Edo-period Japan was closed to the outside world and tightly controlled by the shogunate, which frequently banned gambling games. Card makers responded by changing the designs so that they did not look like gaming decks.

Hanafuda reflect the Japanese culture of contemplating nature: the cherry blossoms of March, the full moon of August, the deer and the maple leaves of October.

📖 How to play

  • The deck has 12 suits, one per month, with 4 cards each.
  • Each card is worth points according to its type: animals, ribbons, brights or plain.
  • You match cards in your hand with cards on the table from the same month.
  • Points are scored by forming special combinations, called yaku.

🎭 Anecdotes

Nintendo means something like “leave luck to heaven” in Japanese. Before video games, the company tried taxis, hotels and instant rice.

Hanafuda became associated with the yakuza, the Japanese mafia, because they were used in illegal gambling houses. The name yakuza is said to come from a losing hand (8-9-3) in a card game.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Nintendo still makes and sells hanafuda decks in Japan.
  • The cards are small and thick, made of hard card.
  • The most popular game with hanafuda is called koi-koi.
  • In Korea a game called go-stop is played with these cards.
