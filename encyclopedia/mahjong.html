Mahjong

A Chinese game of 144 tiles, with rules similar to rummy, very popular in Asia.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Mahjong developed in China in the middle of the 19th century, probably in the Ningbo region, from earlier card games. Its tiles have three suits (circles, bamboos and characters), winds, dragons, flowers and seasons.

In the 1920s it became a craze in the United States and Europe. Today it is the most popular table game in China, Japan (which has its own variant, riichi) and much of Asia.

Note: the computer game of matching tiles that many people call “mahjong” is actually a solitaire game created in 1981, and has nothing to do with the rules of the original mahjong.

🏛️ Historical context

Mahjong arose in China at the end of the Qing dynasty, in the middle of the 19th century, probably around Ningbo and Shanghai, from card games with suits of coins, strings and myriads. It was a China in crisis, with the Opium Wars and the arrival of Western merchants in its ports.

In the 1920s the American merchant Joseph Park Babcock exported it to the United States with simplified rules, and a craze broke out: luxury sets were sold, people dressed up in Chinese costume to play, and there were even songs about the game.

📖 How to play

  • Four players with 13 tiles each.
  • Players take turns to draw and discard.
  • You form sets of three, runs and a pair.
  • The first to complete a 14-tile hand wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

During China’s Cultural Revolution mahjong was considered a bourgeois vice. It is said that many people played in secret, with blankets over the table so the neighbours would not hear the clatter of the tiles.

In Japan, mahjong became very popular in the 20th century, with its own rules (riichi), and there are manga and anime devoted entirely to it.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The name mahjong means “sparrow” in some Chinese dialects.
  • A complete set has 144 tiles.
  • In Chinese, the sound of shuffling the tiles is called “the twittering of the sparrows”.
  • Mahjong solitaire, in which you match tiles, is a different game invented for computers in 1981.
