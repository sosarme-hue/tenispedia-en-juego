Julepe

A trick-taking card game in which anyone who plays and fails to win at least two tricks “takes a julepe” and has to pay.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Julepe is a traditional Spanish trick-taking game with trumps played with the 40-card deck. It is documented from the 19th century and was very popular throughout Spain, especially in Galicia, Asturias, Castile and León and Madrid.

It is a game of risk: in each hand players decide whether to play or drop out depending on the cards they hold. Whoever plays commits to winning at least two of the five tricks. If they fail, they “take a julepe” and have to pay into the pot.

The word julepe used to mean a medicinal syrup, and from there it came to mean a great effort or a good telling-off: to “give someone a julepe” is to teach them a lesson, which is exactly what happens in the game to anyone who plays and loses.

🏛️ Historical context

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, julepe was one of the favourite games of Spanish cafés and social clubs, along with tresillo and tute. It was almost always played for small stakes of cents or with counters.

In many families it was played at Christmas with chickpeas or beans instead of money, and so it was passed down from generation to generation as an after-dinner game.

📖 How to play

  • Each player receives five cards, and the last card dealt sets the trump suit.
  • Looking at their cards, each player decides whether to play or pass. Those who play may exchange some cards for others from the pack.
  • Five tricks are played. You must follow suit and, if you cannot, play a trump.
  • Whoever wins two tricks or more takes a share of the pot, according to how many they won.
  • Anyone who plays and wins fewer than two tricks is “julepe’d” and pays into the pot for the next hand.

🎭 Anecdotes

The game gives us the expression “llevarse un julepe” (to take a julepe), which in many parts of Spain means to be exhausted or to get a real fright.

In Galicia some say that a good game of julepe is the best way to end a long Sunday lunch, and some villages organise tournaments.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The ranking of the cards in julepe is ace, three, king, knight, jack, seven, six, five, four and two, just as in tute.
  • Players can drop out without paying if their cards are bad: knowing when not to play is the great skill of julepe.
  • In some variants the dealer is obliged to play.
  • It resembles English loo and other European trick-taking games with a pot.
