Sudoku

A puzzle of filling a 9 × 9 grid with the numbers 1 to 9 without repeating any.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Although the name is Japanese, modern sudoku was invented by the American architect Howard Garns, who published it in 1979 in Dell Pencil Puzzles magazine under the name Number Place. His idea was based on the Latin squares studied by the Swiss mathematician Leonhard Euler in the 18th century.

In 1984 the Japanese publisher Nikoli published it under the name sūji wa dokushin ni kagiru (“the digits must be single”), which was shortened to sudoku.

In 2004 the New Zealander Wayne Gould persuaded the British newspaper The Times to publish it, and within a few months it became a worldwide phenomenon.

🏛️ Historical context

Modern sudoku was invented by the American Howard Garns, a retired architect, who published it in 1979 in Dell Pencil Puzzles magazine under the name Number Place. Its forerunners were the magic squares and Latin squares studied by the mathematician Leonhard Euler in the 18th century.

In 1984 the Japanese publisher Nikoli published it under the name sūji wa dokushin ni kagiru, “the digits must be single”, shortened to sudoku. In 2004 the New Zealander Wayne Gould persuaded the British newspaper The Times to publish it, and within months it became a worldwide craze.

📖 How to play

  • The grid has 81 squares in nine 3 × 3 boxes.
  • You must fill in the numbers 1 to 9.
  • No number may be repeated in any row, column or box.
  • Every well-made sudoku has a single solution.

🎭 Anecdotes

Wayne Gould discovered sudoku in a Tokyo bookshop and spent six years programming a computer to generate puzzles. He offered them free to newspapers in exchange for his name being printed, and became famous.

In 2012 mathematicians used a computer to prove that a sudoku needs at least 17 starting numbers to have a single solution.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • It was proved in 2012 that a sudoku needs at least 17 starting numbers to have only one solution.
  • There are about 6.67 sextillion different completed sudoku grids.
  • Sudoku needs no maths: you could use letters or colours.
  • The first world championship was held in 2006 in Lucca, Italy.
