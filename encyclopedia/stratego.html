Stratego

A strategy game in which each army hides the rank of its pieces. You have to capture your opponent’s flag without knowing where it is.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

Stratego originates from a French game called L’Attaque, patented at the beginning of the 20th century by Hermance Edan. In it, two armies faced each other with cardboard pieces whose identity only their owner knew.

In the Netherlands a revised version was created under the name Stratego, which became very popular in Europe from the 1940s and 1950s. In 1961 it reached the United States and became a classic family board game.

Its charm lies in hidden information: each player sees the backs of the opposing pieces, but not their rank. You have to deduce what each piece is from how it moves, set traps with bombs and protect your flag.

🏛️ Historical context

L’Attaque was born in the years before the First World War, when tabletop war games were very popular in Europe. Its pieces showed soldiers in the uniforms of the time.

Stratego sits halfway between chess and bluffing games like poker, because it mixes strategy with deception and memory.

📖 How to play

  • Each player has 40 pieces: a marshal, generals, colonels, a spy, scouts, miners, bombs and a flag.
  • They are set up secretly, facing the player, in the first four rows of the board.
  • Each turn you move one piece one square. Scouts can travel several squares in a straight line.
  • When a piece attacks another, both are revealed and the higher rank wins. The spy beats the marshal if it attacks first.
  • Bombs do not move and destroy any attacker except miners. Whoever captures the opposing flag wins.

🎭 Anecdotes

In 2022 the company DeepMind presented an artificial intelligence able to play Stratego at expert level. It was a difficult challenge, because unlike chess there is a lot of hidden information and you have to know how to bluff.

Expert players usually surround their flag with bombs, but the craftiest sometimes leave it unprotected in an unexpected corner.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • The spy is the weakest piece in the game, but it is the only one able to beat the marshal, the strongest, as long as the spy is the one attacking.
  • The rank numbers of the pieces changed in some editions: in some the marshal is 10 and in others 1.
  • There has been a Stratego world championship for years.
  • There are versions themed on films and fantasy.
