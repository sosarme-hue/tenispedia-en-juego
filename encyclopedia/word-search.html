Word search

A puzzle of finding words hidden in a grid of letters.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The modern word search is credited to two authors: the Spaniard Pedro Ocón de Oro, who published puzzles in magazines, and the American Norman E. Gibat, who published it in 1968 in the Selenby Digest newspaper in Oklahoma.

Thanks to its simplicity it became a very popular puzzle in magazines, newspapers and school workbooks, and it is widely used to learn vocabulary.

Words can be hidden horizontally, vertically or diagonally, and also written backwards.

🏛️ Historical context

The modern word search is credited to the American Norman E. Gibat, who published it in 1968 in an Oklahoma newspaper. Within a few years it had spread to puzzle magazines all over the world.

In Spain it became one of the classic sections of puzzle magazines and school holiday workbooks, because it helps you learn vocabulary and sharpen your visual attention.

📖 How to play

  • There is a list of words to find.
  • Each word lies in a straight line in any direction.
  • You mark each word when you find it.
  • You finish when you have found them all.

🎭 Anecdotes

In Spanish it is called sopa de letras, “alphabet soup”, after the soup with letter-shaped noodles that children love; in other countries it is called “word find” or “seek and find”.

In Spain the invention is credited to the puzzle writer Pedro Ocón de Oro, who is said to have published word searches in the 1960s, before Gibat. As with many puzzles, the idea probably arose in several places at once.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Pedro Ocón de Oro was one of Spain’s most prolific puzzle makers, author of thousands of brain teasers.
  • Words can be written from right to left or diagonally.
  • Themed word searches have all their words on the same topic.
  • Some hide a message in the leftover letters.
