Knucklebones (astragali)

One of the oldest games in the world: throwing lamb ankle bones, the ancestors of dice.

📜 History🏛️ Historical context⏳ Timeline📖 How to play🎭 Anecdotes💡 Fun facts

📜 History

The knucklebone is the astragalus, an ankle bone of lambs, goats and other animals. It has four different sides and, when thrown, lands unpredictably. Knucklebones used for play have been found at sites more than 7,000 years old.

In Greece they were called astragaloi and in Rome tali. They were used for drawing lots, for telling the future and for games of skill: throwing one knucklebone and picking up others before it falls.

Knucklebones are still played in Spain and Argentina: in the Argentine countryside taba is a traditional betting game among gauchos, and in Spain it was a girls’ game with four bones, sometimes painted in colours.

🏛️ Historical context

In the Greek and Roman world, knucklebones were children’s toys, objects of divination in temples and also a game for adults in taverns. There are statues and painted vases of young people playing knucklebones.

Herders and shepherds all over the world, from Mongolia to Patagonia, have played knucklebones, because the material was easy to come by. In Mongolia shagai is still a national game.

📖 How to play

  • There are two main ways of playing: chance and skill.
  • In the game of chance you throw the bone and win or lose according to the side that lands face up.
  • In the game of skill you throw one bone into the air and pick up those on the ground before catching it.
  • Each side has a name, which changes from region to region; in the Argentine game of chance they speak of “suerte” (luck) and “culo” (bottom).

🎭 Anecdotes

According to the Greek historian Herodotus, the Lydians invented knucklebone games to endure an 18-year famine: they ate one day and played the next to forget their hunger.

In ancient Rome the best throw with four knucklebones, all showing different sides, was called “Venus”, and the worst, all the same, “the dog”.

💡 Fun facts: did you know…?

  • Cubic dice were probably invented to imitate knucklebones.
  • The astragalus is the only foot bone that joins the leg.
  • In Mongolia there is a game of flicking knucklebones with a finger at a target.
  • The Louvre Museum holds a giant bronze knucklebone from the Persian Empire.
