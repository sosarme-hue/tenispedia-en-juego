Myth and religion

The worship of the gods of Ancient Greece in the modern world

Hellenism

Hellenism is a modern religious movement based on the religion of Ancient Greece, devoted above all to the twelve Olympian gods. Its followers describe it as the continuation or revival of an enduring tradition, while most historians see it as a modern reconstruction. Its core values, piety, excellence and hospitality, draw on myths and ancient texts.

Trivia

A name with history. The term 'Hellenism' appears as early as the 4th century, when Emperor Julian the Philosopher used it in a letter to refer to the traditional religion. Some followers today adopt it as a tribute to him.
Divine guests. Theoxenia is a mythical theme in which a humble host welcomes a stranger who turns out to be a disguised god, who then rewards the host's generosity.
A papyrus from 1962. The Derveni Papyrus, discovered in 1962, contains an Orphic cosmology in which Zeus appears as the creator of the world. Scholars continue to debate its interpretation.
An edict from 380. The Edict of Thessalonica, issued in 380, banned the religions called pagan and established Christianity as the sole religion of the Roman Empire.
