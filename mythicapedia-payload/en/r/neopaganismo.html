Myth and religion

Contemporary pagan movements and their relationship with ancient myth

Modern paganism

Modern paganism, also called neopaganism, brings together new religious movements inspired by the beliefs of pre-modern peoples in Europe, North Africa and the Near East. It is a diverse field with no shared creed or sacred texts, ranging from careful revivals of ancient cults to free blends of many traditions. Its relationship with ancient myth is the thread that links these groups, although each understands that relationship differently.

Trivia

A contested term. The prefix “neo” was first used in the nineteenth century for classical revivalism. Many practitioners today reject it, feeling it separates them from their pre-Christian ancestors.
The horned god. The male figure in Gardnerian Wicca draws on Cernunnos, a Celtic deity shown with antlers on objects and monuments from pre-Roman Europe.
Borrowed structures. Latvia’s Dievturība, which seeks to revive the pre-Christian religion of the Latvian people, borrowed a monotheistic focus and ceremonial structure from Lutheranism.
