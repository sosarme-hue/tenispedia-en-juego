Six Corto Maltese adventures between Celtic legend and the First World War

Celtic Tales

Celtic Tales (Les Celtiques) is a 1971 volume of Hugo Pratt's Corto Maltese series. It gathers six comic stories set in Europe between 1917 and 1918, during the First World War. Several of them draw on Celtic, Germanic and Irish legends, and one reworks a Shakespeare comedy.

Celtic Tales (Les Celtiques in French) is a 1971 volume of the series created by Hugo Pratt around Corto Maltese, a sailor born in Malta. It collects six stories first published between 1971 and 1972 in the French magazine Pif Gadget. Corto moves between Venice, Ireland, England and northern France, and in several stories myth provides the thread that ties the adventure together.

The first story, The Angel in the Window to the Orient, is set in Venice. Corto seeks a map drawn on skin that, according to the tradition the comic follows, could lead to the mines of the Inca Atahualpa and, by extension, to the Seven Cities of Cibola and El Dorado, two European legends of American riches. One of the seven cities is missing from the map, and the search leads to a rabbi's diary and a mysterious paralysed woman. In Under the Flag of Gold, the royal treasure of Montenegro is hidden on the Italian front, and Corto arranges its recovery with allies of several nationalities.

Concerto in O Minor for Harp and Nitroglycerin takes place in Ireland, amid the conflict between Sinn Féin and the Black and Tans. The title refers to the Celtic harp, a traditional symbol of the island. The widow of the movement's hero is called Banshee, a name that recalls the banshee, a spirit of Irish folklore said to foretell death. She refuses to travel with Corto, believing she brings bad luck. The headstone of Pat Finnucan bears the word Fianna, the name of the semi-independent warriors of Irish mythology.

A Midwinter Morning's Dream is set at Stonehenge on 21 December 1917. Creatures from Celtic folklore gather there and, in the story, Puck, in the form of a raven, guides Corto. It pays homage to Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream and revisits the legend of Vortigern and Rowena, which tradition places in Britain before Arthur. The idea came to Pratt when he found, in a cemetery in northern France, the tomb of a Canadian named Julius César.

Côtes de Nuits and Picardy Roses follows the Red Baron, Manfred von Richthofen, Germany's celebrated ace pilot, in April 1918. The last story, Burlesque Between Zuydcoote and Bray-Dunes, features Cain, a character from Ballad of the Salt Sea, who is accused of trying to kill De Trecesson, although it turns out he was not responsible. In 2003, Canal+ France adapted the album as one of four television films.

Author: Hugo Pratt

Trivia

Serialised first. The stories first appeared in the French magazine Pif Gadget between 1971 and 1972 before being collected as an album.
A real inspiration. The character of Moira O'Danann was physically inspired by Patricia Frawley, a friend of Pratt of Irish-American descent.
Shakespeare reimagined. The Stonehenge story features Oberon and Puck, characters from Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream.
