Pierre Grimal's guide to the myths of Greece and Rome

Dictionary of Greek and Roman Mythology

The Dictionary of Greek and Roman Mythology is a work by the French Latinist Pierre Grimal, first published in 1951. It gathers in alphabetical order the stories of gods, heroes and places from classical antiquity, without offering a fixed explanation of the legends. Its Spanish edition has made this material available to Spanish-speaking readers since 1965.

The Dictionary of Greek and Roman Mythology was written by the French Latinist Pierre Grimal and published in 1951 by Presses universitaires de France. Its original French title is Dictionnaire de la mythologie grecque et romaine. As its format suggests, it lists the names of gods, heroes, places and figures who appear in the stories of classical antiquity and, for each one, summarises what the ancient texts say.

The Spanish edition was translated by Francisco Payarols and includes a prologue and revision by Pedro Pericay, a professor at the University of Barcelona. It first appeared with Editorial Labor in 1965 and has been published by Paidós since 1981. Through these editions, the book has reached generations of Spanish-speaking readers interested in classical mythology.

In his introduction, Grimal makes clear that the dictionary does not claim to be original or scholarly, and that it offers no explanatory system for the legends. In his view, interpretive theories grow old, while the data preserved in the ancient texts endure. His aim is therefore to gather, condense and present that material so that readers can examine it for themselves.

Grimal openly acknowledges his debt to the Lexikon of Wilhelm Heinrich Roscher, the Ausführliches Lexikon der griechischen und römischen Mythologie. For readers who want to pursue the scientific study of myths in depth, Grimal himself points to that large reference work. In this way, his dictionary serves as a more accessible entry point into a very broad field of scholarship.

Trivia

A Latinist at the helm. Pierre Grimal was a Latinist, a specialist in the language and literature of Rome. His training explains the work's focus on the ancient texts.
A dictionary without a system. In his introduction, Grimal explains that he does not seek to impose a theory on the legends. He prefers to present the data from the texts, which he regards as more lasting than any interpretation.
An acknowledged debt. The author acknowledges that his work builds on the large Lexikon of Wilhelm Heinrich Roscher, and refers readers who want a deeper scientific study of myths to it.
