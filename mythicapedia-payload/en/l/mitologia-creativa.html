Creative Mythology

Creative Mythology is the fourth and final volume of The Masks of God, Joseph Campbell's comparative mythology series, written in 1968. Creative Mythology is the fourth and final volume of The Masks of God, the comparative mythology series written in 1968 by American mythologist, writer and professor Joseph Campbell. While the earlier volumes survey the myths of ancient and traditional cultures, this book turns to something different: how, in modern culture, literature, art, philosophy and science have given rise to a new awareness of the human being as the creator of his or her own mythology.

Campbell traces this European mythology of individualism from the Middle Ages to our own time. His examples include the medieval stories of the Knights of the Round Table, the Grail and Tristan and Isolde, as well as works by Dante, Nietzsche, Wagner, Thomas Mann and Joyce, and Picasso's Guernica. In these, myths do not come from the theological rulings of priestly authorities, as they do in most religions, but from the intuitions, feelings and visions of artists and writers.

To explain this idea, Campbell returns to the phrase creative mythology in the Shakespearean sense: a mirror that shows virtue and vice their true faces, and shows each age its authentic form and features. A creative myth begins with a personal experience of order, horror, beauty or simple exhilaration, which the author tries to communicate through signs. If that experience has enough depth, its communication can carry the force of a living myth for those who receive it and recognize it freely.

The book is organized into four large parts, titled The Ancient Vine, The Waste Land, The Way and the Life, and New Wine. Through them, Campbell suggests that myth has not vanished from modernity but has changed hands: it is now individual creators who renew it, and readers and viewers who decide whether to make it their own.

Trivia

A Shakespearean mirror. Campbell borrows from Shakespeare the idea of myth as a mirror that shows virtue and vice their true faces.
A painting among myths. Picasso's Guernica appears in the book as an example of modern mythmaking, alongside medieval tales and twentieth-century novels.
Closing the series. Creative Mythology completes The Masks of God, the four-volume comparative mythology series Campbell wrote as a whole.
