Stephen Fry's retelling of Greek myths

Mythos

Mythos is a 2017 book by Stephen Fry that retells a selection of ancient Greek myths in a warm, lively voice. Fry presents it as an easy entry point that requires no prior knowledge of classical mythology. It was the first in a series that later continued with several further volumes.

Mythos is a book by British author Stephen Fry, published in 2017, that retells a selection of myths from ancient Greece in a conversational and playful style. Fry does not aim for a scholarly handbook. At the start of the book he insists that no background knowledge is needed to enjoy these stories, which he describes as addictive, entertaining, approachable and astonishingly human.

Most of the tales draw on three major ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, which describes the origins of the cosmos and the gods; Ovid's Metamorphoses, a collection of stories about transformation; and Apuleius's The Golden Ass. Fry takes this material and reshapes it in his own voice, so readers meet gods, Titans and mortals in narratives that keep the spirit of the originals while reading easily today.

Mythos was the first of a series. Heroes, focused on Greek heroes, followed in 2018, and then Troy (2020) and Odyssey (2024). The first three volumes were published in Spanish by the publisher Anagrama, translated by Rubén Martín Giráldez. In 2018 the play Mythos: A Trilogy premiered at the Shaw Festival in Ontario, Canada, drawing on the same stories.

The reception has been mixed. The Telegraph gave it four stars out of five and praised its wit and entertainment value. The Scotsman admired the book's accessibility and Fry's knowledge and enthusiasm, but criticised a style that shifts between the erudite and the deliberately downmarket. The British scholar Edith Hall, writing in The Guardian, argued that the selection of myths is limited and that the absence of a contents page or index gives no warning of the book's incomplete coverage. Even so, she praised Fry for putting his fame to constructive use, and for bringing humour and intimacy to the psychological side of myth.

For readers who want to approach Greek myth without preparation, Mythos works as a first stop. Those looking for exhaustive coverage or philological analysis will need to turn to other books.
