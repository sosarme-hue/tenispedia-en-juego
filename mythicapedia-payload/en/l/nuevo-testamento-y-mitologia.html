Rudolf Bultmann's essay on how to read the Gospels

New Testament and Mythology

In 1941, the German theologian Rudolf Bultmann published an essay arguing that the New Testament, especially the Gospels and their account of Christ, should be understood as mythological in nature. His proposal, known as demythologizing, aimed to separate the mythical form of the message from its meaning. The essay remains one of the most influential and debated theological works of the twentieth century.

Rudolf Bultmann (1884-1976) was a German Protestant theologian who taught at the University of Marburg. In 1941 he published an essay whose full title refers to the problem of demythologizing the message of the New Testament, and which is usually known as New Testament and Mythology. It appeared during the Second World War, at a time of intense intellectual pressure in Germany, and it soon became a standard reference for anyone thinking about the relationship between faith, history and religious language.

Bultmann's starting point is that the texts of the New Testament are written in the categories of their time. They describe a world arranged in three levels, with heaven above, the earth in the middle and a underworld below, and they treat supernatural interventions, miracles and invisible beings as an ordinary part of reality. For Bultmann, a modern reader shaped by science and by a different picture of the world cannot accept that imagery as a literal description of what happens.

However, he did not think the answer was to abandon the message. The real task, in his view, was to distinguish between the mythological form and its content. He proposed to demythologize the texts, a term taken from the German Entmythologisierung, by asking what they meant about human existence, the call to faith and personal decision, rather than reading them as reports of events in the physical world. This approach drew on existentialist philosophy, particularly the thought of Martin Heidegger, whose influence on Bultmann is widely recognised.

The essay provoked a lively debate. Some theologians saw it as a necessary way of making the message intelligible to modern readers. Others feared that by treating myth as language about human experience, the Christian tradition would lose its historical substance. The argument continued for decades and went well beyond theology, reaching biblical scholarship, hermeneutics and the academic study of religion.

From the perspective of mythology, the essay is especially interesting because it reverses the usual approach. Instead of studying myths belonging to traditions far from the reader's own, it examines a religious text that many believers regard as historical and analyses it as a narrative with mythic structure. Bultmann thereby drew a line between what the text says about the world and what it seeks to communicate about human beings.

Trivia

A term that entered theology. The German expression Entmythologisierung became a technical term in theology and biblical scholarship, and it is still used in academic debates.
Written during wartime. The essay was published in 1941, while Europe was in the middle of the Second World War, which adds a historical context to its reflection on faith and language.
A debate that lasted decades. Bultmann's ideas generated responses and criticism for many years, both within and beyond Protestant circles.
