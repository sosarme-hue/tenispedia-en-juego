Robert Graves's speculative novel about who wrote the Odyssey

Homer's Daughter

Homer's Daughter is a 1955 novel by the British writer Robert Graves, best known for I, Claudius and The White Goddess. It begins from a bold premise: that the Odyssey was not composed by a man named Homer but by a princess living in the Greek settlements of Sicily.

No documentary evidence survives for such an author, so the novel is an entirely speculative reconstruction of who she might have been and why she would have written a poem like this. In the story, she reshapes the legends of her own day so that they partly mirror a crisis in her own life. The myth thus becomes less an inherited store of tales than a mirror held up to the person telling them.

The notion that a woman stood behind the poems attributed to Homer was not Graves's invention. Samuel Butler first developed it in The Authoress of the Odyssey, and Graves built his novel on that theory, turning a critical argument into fiction.

From a mythological standpoint, the book's real interest lies in how stories pass from voice to page. The Odyssey gathers voyages, monsters and homecomings that belonged to an oral tradition, and Graves imagines a narrator who arranges them according to her own needs. It is a way of thinking about how myths get fixed in writing: someone decides what to tell and how to tell it.

Homer's Daughter is not as famous as Graves's novels about the Roman emperors, yet it has its admirers. Readers who come to Greek myth through it may find themselves asking old questions afresh: who first told these stories, who wrote them down, and whose interests the telling served.

Author: Robert Graves Date: 1955-

Trivia

An earlier theory. The hypothesis of a woman author behind the Odyssey was put forward by Samuel Butler in his book The Authoress of the Odyssey. Graves took it as the starting point for his novel.
A writer of two famous works. Robert Graves is remembered above all for I, Claudius and The White Goddess. This novel is less known than either, though it has its devoted readers.
Fiction filling a gap. The novel's author has no documentary evidence behind her. Graves reconstructs her entirely as a speculative hypothesis.
