Odd and the Frost Giants is a short novel by Neil Gaiman, published in 2008 as a World Book Day book. It draws on Norse mythology and, to a lesser degree, on the historical Vikings, blending adventure, humour and a wistful tone aimed at young readers.

The story begins with Odd, a young Norseman who loses his father and then his leg. He leaves his village and heads into the forest, where he meets three animals in trouble: a fox, an eagle and a bear with a paw caught in a tree. He soon learns that they are not ordinary animals but the gods Loki, Odin and Thor.

According to the tale, a Frost Giant tricked Loki into handing over Thor's hammer by taking the form of a woman. With it, the giant seized rule over Asgard and brought on an endless winter, and the gods were cast out of their home. Odd cannot keep feeding them for long, but he realises that they have nowhere to go and cannot feed themselves, so he decides to travel with them to Asgard.

There, Thor leads him to Mimir's Well, where he drinks and receives wisdom, along with a vision of his parents in their youth. He later speaks with the Giant, who explains that his brother built the walls of Asgard but was cheated out of his payment and killed by Thor. Odd persuades the Giant to go home. In return, the goddess Freya heals his leg, though not completely, and Odin gives him a staff. As winter ends he returns to Midgard, somewhat bigger for having drunk from the well, and is reunited with his mother.

The book was well received. Kirkus Reviews gave it a starred review and called it a "sweet, wistful, slyly funny novella", comparing it with George R.R. Martin's The Ice Dragon. Publishers Weekly thought children would enjoy it but found it simpler and less original than Gaiman's earlier Coraline. Booklist praised his deft humour and agile imagination, and The Horn Book Magazine admired Helquist's illustrations for evoking his wintry Norse world. In December 2024 a stage version was staged at the Unicorn Theatre in London.

Author: Neil Gaiman Date: 2008-

Trivia

A World Book Day book. It was published as part of the World Book Day initiative, which distributes books to young readers. That is why its format is short and accessible.
Myths and Vikings. The novel mixes Norse mythology with elements inspired by the historical Vikings, although its core comes from the myths of the northern gods.
From page to stage. In December 2024 the story reached the theatre in a version staged at the Unicorn Theatre in London.
