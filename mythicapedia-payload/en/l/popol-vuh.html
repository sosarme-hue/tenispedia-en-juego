The sacred book of the K'iche' people and its Hero Twins

Popol Vuh

The Popol Vuh is a sacred narrative of the K'iche' people of Guatemala, recounting the creation of the world, the origins of humankind and the adventures of the Hero Twins Hunahpú and Xbalanqué. It was passed down orally for centuries and is known today largely through a transcription made by a Dominican friar in the eighteenth century. Its origins and authorship remain debated among scholars.

Trivia

A name with history. The name Popol Vuh was coined by Abbé Brasseur de Bourbourg in 1861, when he published his French translation.
A book found in Chicago. Ximénez's manuscript ended up at the Newberry Library in Chicago, donated by the collector Edward E. Ayer.
A very ancient mural. The El Mirador panel, dated to around 200 BCE, is one of the oldest depictions of the Hero Twins.
