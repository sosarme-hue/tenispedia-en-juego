A thirteen-volume comparative series

The Mythology of All Races

The Mythology of All Races is a series of thirteen volumes edited by Louis Herbert Gray between 1916 and 1932, with George Foot Moore serving as consulting editor. Its ambition was to bring together myth traditions from many different peoples within a single work. It remains a useful record of how mythology was studied in the early twentieth century.

The Mythology of All Races is a series of thirteen volumes edited by Louis Herbert Gray between 1916 and 1932. Its title reflects the ambition of its time: to gather the myth traditions of very different peoples into one project, so that readers could compare them side by side.

Gray led the work with the help of George Foot Moore, who served as consulting editor. Together they shaped an undertaking that stretched over more than fifteen years, a pace typical of the large comparative projects published in the first half of the twentieth century.

The context helps explain it. Mythology as an academic field was expanding rapidly, and comparative studies looked for connections between stories from distant regions. Today the series is read with a critical eye. Many of its approaches reflect the categories and assumptions of its era, and the way it presents non-European peoples has since been reassessed by anthropology and the history of religions.

Still, the work has value as an entry point. Comparative studies of this kind often return to recurring themes, such as the origin of the world, primordial floods, journeys to the land of the dead, or beings that combine human and animal traits. By bringing different traditions together under one project, readers can see how each culture answered these same questions in its own way.

For modern readers, the series is also a record of mythology's own history as a field of study. It shows what scholars were looking for, what they often overlooked, and how the approach has changed from the early decades of the twentieth century to the present.

Trivia

Thirteen volumes. The series runs to thirteen volumes, a considerable length for a work devoted to comparing mythologies from around the world.
Sixteen years of publication. The volumes appeared between 1916 and 1932, so the project spanned more than a decade and a half.
