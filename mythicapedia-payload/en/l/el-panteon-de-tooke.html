The schoolbook that taught classical myths to generations of students

Tooke's Pantheon

Tooke's Pantheon is the informal name of The Pantheon, a book that retells the stories of the heathen gods and the most illustrious heroes in a brief, plain and familiar style. It is Andrew Tooke's translation of a Latin original by François-Antoine Pomey, published in 1659. For more than a century it was a popular text for students of Greek and Roman mythology.

Tooke's Pantheon is the informal name of The Pantheon, a book that retells the fabulous histories of the heathen gods and the most illustrious heroes. First published in 1694, it takes a short, plain and familiar approach, presenting its stories by way of dialogue, which made them easy for readers to follow.

The book has an earlier source. It is Andrew Tooke's translation of the Pantheum mythicum, seu Fabulosa deorum historia, written in Latin by François-Antoine Pomey and first published in 1659. Both the English version and its Latin original stayed in use with students of Greek and Roman mythology for well over a century.

The attribution of the work changed over time. Tooke never mentioned Pomey in his introductory note «To the reader», and Pomey's name vanished from the title page of the sixth edition in 1713, while Tooke's stayed. As a result, the book came to be known in the eighteenth century as Tooke's Pantheon, although Tooke's name did not become part of its printed title until the nineteenth century.

Its reach was considerable. The work was reprinted twenty-three times by 1771, and as late as 1859 it was still being issued in the United States, described as adapted for the use of students of every age and either sex. Today it is most valued as a window onto how classical mythology was taught for centuries, with its gods and heroes presented as an ordered collection of stories for learning.

Author: François-Antoine Pomey Date: 1659-
