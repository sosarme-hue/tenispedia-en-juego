The Trojan War told from the shadow of a hero

The Song of Achilles

The Song of Achilles is Madeline Miller's debut novel, published in 2011. It retells the Trojan War through the eyes of Patroclus, Achilles' companion, and brings to the centre a relationship that ancient texts only hint at. In 2012 the book won the award then known as the Orange Prize for Fiction.

Madeline Miller's novel is set in the Greek Heroic Age and takes the Trojan War as its backdrop, but it does not tell the story through the great warriors. It follows Patroclus, a boy whom his father, King Menoetius, sees as weak and timid. In Homer's Iliad Patroclus is a secondary figure, a man who dies in battle and whose death sets Achilles' vengeance in motion. Miller gives him a voice of his own.

The story begins in Patroclus's childhood. After he accidentally kills the son of one of his father's nobles, he is exiled to Phthia, where he meets Achilles, son of King Peleus and the sea nymph Thetis. A friendship grows between them and, as the years pass, turns into love. Thetis, convinced that a mortal of low standing is no fit companion for her son, separates them, yet the pair are reunited on Mount Pelion. The tradition of Achilles being educated by the centaur Chiron survives in the novel, though here it is told with an intimacy the ancient sources do not explore.

The plot then follows the familiar path: Agamemnon's call to the Achaean kings, the prophecy that Achilles will die if he fights, his hiding at the court of Lycomedes on Skyros, the sacrifice of Iphigenia at Aulis, the quarrel over Chryseis and Briseis, and Achilles' withdrawal from battle. When Patroclus puts on his friend's armour to rally the troops, Hector kills him. Achilles then kills Hector and, shortly afterwards, dies at the hands of Paris, guided by Apollo.

The novel introduces important changes. Miller makes Patroclus and Achilles the same age, unlike the Iliad, where Patroclus is older. She also drew on other ancient writers, among them Ovid, Virgil, Sophocles, Apollodorus, Euripides and Aeschylus, to shape a narrative voice far removed from the epic tone. Her Achilles is not invulnerable: the later legend of the heel, found in some ancient sources, plays no part here.

The book was widely read and sold millions of copies, gaining new attention in 2021 through social media. Critics such as Natalie Haynes praised it as a deeply affecting version of the Achilles story. Beyond its commercial success, the novel shows how classical myths remain open to new perspectives, in which secondary characters can take centre stage.

Author: Madeline Miller Date: 2011-

Trivia

A prize-winning debut. It was Madeline Miller's first novel; she had worked as a teacher of Latin and ancient Greek.
A minor character. In the Iliad Patroclus has little prominence, yet his death is decisive for the outcome of the war.
