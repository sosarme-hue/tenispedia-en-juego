Percy Jackson's second quest for the Golden Fleece

The Sea of Monsters

The Sea of Monsters is the second novel in the Percy Jackson & the Olympians series by Rick Riordan, published in the United States on April 1, 2006. Drawing on Greek myth, it follows a rescue mission for a satyr, a quest for the Golden Fleece, and an attempt to save Camp Half-Blood from ruin.

The Sea of Monsters is the second book in Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson & the Olympians series. The American author published it on April 1, 2006, and it continues directly from The Lightning Thief. Like its predecessor, it borrows characters and places from Greek mythology and blends them with humour, action and a young hero's search for his place in the world, with themes of friendship and family loyalty running underneath.

The story opens with a dream. Percy sees his friend Grover, a satyr who has looked out for him since childhood, running from an unseen enemy along the Florida coast. When he wakes, his mother tells him that he cannot return to Camp Half-Blood this summer, the training ground for the children of the gods, because it has become dangerous. On his last day at school, Percy spends time with Tyson, a huge boy whom most of the other students avoid. In gym class a game of dodgeball turns into a fight with the Laestrygonians, the man-eating giants of Homer's Odyssey. They hurl flaming balls at the boys, yet Tyson shields Percy, and the fire seems to do him no harm. Once the monsters are beaten, the school blames Percy for the damage, and Annabeth, another demigod, arrives to help them escape.

At camp the situation is grave. The tree of Thalia, a young demigod transformed into a pine to save her from monsters, has been poisoned. Her spirit keeps the camp's magical borders intact, so its decline leaves the place exposed. Bronze bulls that breathe fire attack the boundaries, and Chiron, the centaur who runs the activities, is dismissed on suspicion of poisoning the tree. His replacement is Tantalus, a figure who in Greek tradition was punished in the underworld with eternal hunger and thirst, as food and drink always drift out of reach. In the novel he becomes a cruel and capricious director who makes life miserable for the campers.

Percy keeps dreaming of Grover, who appears dressed as a bride and held captive by a cyclops who wants to marry him, mistaking him for a female because of his poor eyesight. Annabeth realises that the cyclops may have the Golden Fleece, the golden pelt of the myth of Jason, which is said to have healing powers. Tantalus ignores Percy and his friends and sends Clarisse, daughter of Ares, to find it. At the same time, Poseidon claims Tyson as his son, and Percy has to face his own prejudice towards his half-brother. With the help of hippocampi sent by the sea god, the trio reach the cruise ship Princess Andromeda, which belongs to Luke, a former friend now gathering an army to restore Kronos. Later, Clarisse's ship is destroyed by Charybdis and Scylla as it enters the Triangle, and Percy and Annabeth believe Tyson has died.

Adrift, Percy and Annabeth reach an island with a seemingly perfect spa run by Circe, the sorceress of the Odyssey who turns men into animals. Percy ends up transformed into a guinea pig, and Annabeth saves him with vitamins that Hermes had given them. They then escape on the ship of the pirate Blackbeard, who turns out to be a son of Ares. Approaching the sirens, Annabeth remembers the old story that their song lures listeners to their deaths, though those who survive gain great wisdom. She asks Percy to tie her to the mast, but she breaks free and throws herself toward the sirens. Percy follows her and drags her back.

They arrive at the island of Polyphemus, in the Sea of Monsters, known to mortals as the Bermuda Triangle. There they find Clarisse fighting the cyclops and Grover dressed as a bride. When Clarisse reveals that Grover is a satyr, the cyclops captures him too. Annabeth uses a cap of invisibility given to her by her mother, Athena, to distract the monster, and Tyson arrives to help them seize the fleece. Percy decides to let Clarisse take it back to camp by plane, but Luke captures them again. Percy then sends a message through Iris and persuades Luke to admit that he poisoned the tree, which clears Chiron. Centaurs arrive to rescue them, and the fleece heals Thalia's tree. Its power proves so strong that it brings Thalia back to life, a result that Percy later realises suits Kronos's plans.

The novel also fulfils, in its own ironic way, a prophecy from the Oracle of Delphi addressed to Clarisse. Her voyage on an iron ship crewed by the spirits of Confederate soldiers, the search for the fleece, the rocks that sink her vessel, and her return by plane without friends all match the lines of the oracle.

The book was well received and nominated for several awards, including the Mark Twain Award in 2009. It has also been adapted to other media: a film came out in 2013, and the book became the basis for the second season of the Disney+ television series, which premiered on December 10, 2025.

Trivia

A cyclops with poor eyesight. In the novel, Polyphemus mistakes Grover for a female cyclops because of his poor eyesight, a nod to the episode in Homer's Odyssey where Odysseus tricks the giant with a false name.
Monsters of the sea lanes. Charybdis and Scylla appear in Greek tradition as dangers in a narrow strait. The novel places them at the entrance to the Bermuda Triangle, turning an old maritime peril into a modern obstacle.
A prophecy with a double meaning. The Delphic oracle's lines addressed to Clarisse come true in the novel, but in unexpected ways, reflecting the ironic character of ancient oracles.
