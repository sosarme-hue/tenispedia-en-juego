Joseph Campbell's unfinished survey of how myths changed over time

Historical Atlas of World Mythology

The Historical Atlas of World Mythology is a multi-volume series that Joseph Campbell conceived to trace how humanity's mythic symbols and stories developed from prehistory onward. It was left unfinished at his death in 1987, and only one of its volumes was completed during his lifetime.

Joseph Campbell, the American mythologist and teacher, is best known as a comparativist, someone who looked for the themes and motifs that recur across very different cultures. He began shaping the Historical Atlas in the late 1970s as a kind of extension of two of his earlier books, The Mythic Image and The Masks of God. As in those works, his aim was to show how these universal themes were voiced in different ways by different peoples, in different eras and places.

The project rested on an idea he had first set out in The Hero with a Thousand Faces: that myth changes over time, passing through four stages. The first, the Way of the Animal Powers, belongs to Paleolithic hunter-gatherers, with shamanism and animal totems at its centre. The second, the Way of the Seeded Earth, covers Neolithic agrarian societies, organised around a mother goddess and fertility rites. The third, the Way of the Celestial Lights, describes the myths of Bronze Age city-states, where pantheons of gods ruled from the heavens under a masculine god-king. The fourth, the Way of Man, concerns religion and philosophy after the Axial Age, when older mythic imagery came to be read consciously as metaphor for psycho-spiritual matters rather than as literal history. Campbell traced this shift in Buddhism, Vedanta and philosophical Taoism, and in the Mystery Cults, Platonism, Christianity and Gnosticism.

The series was meant for scholars and general readers alike. It was heavily illustrated and annotated, with many charts and maps intended to highlight both the similarities and the variations among cultures.

Only the first volume was finished in Campbell's lifetime. It appeared as a single book in 1983 and, after his posthumous fame grew with the television series The Power of Myth, Harper and Row reissued it in 1988. The second volume, which Campbell was working on up to the day he died, was completed in three parts by his longtime editor, Robert Walter, and published in 1989. Two further parts of the Way of the Seeded Earth remained unfinished; Campbell left rough text for them, along with notes for the last two volumes. Both published volumes are now out of print. The Joseph Campbell Foundation has said it intends to republish the existing books and, if possible, complete the work. Since 2014 it has also been developing a digital edition that takes advantage of multimedia possibilities.

Trivia
