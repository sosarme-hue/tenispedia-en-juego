A comparison of the world's mythologies according to Joseph Campbell

The Masks of God

The Masks of God is a four-volume work written between 1959 and 1968 by the American mythologist Joseph Campbell. It compares myths and religions from many peoples to argue that they all draw on a shared set of motifs. Its title suggests that religions and mythologies are metaphors for God.

Joseph Campbell, an American mythologist, writer and professor, spent much of his career studying the myths of the world. His most ambitious work, The Masks of God, was written between 1959 and 1968 and is included in his Complete Works. It runs to four volumes and more than 2,600 pages, and the author needed over a decade to write it. The account stretches from the earliest beginnings of the human species down to relatively recent times.

The title captures the book's central claim. For Campbell, religions and mythologies are metaphors for God, that is, different masks through which each culture tries to name what it cannot fully grasp. From this starting point, he carries out a systematic comparison of traditions that are far apart in time and place. His conclusion is that themes appearing everywhere on the planet come from a single common stock of mythological motifs. Each people selected, organized and interpreted them according to its own needs, and ritualized them in different ways, yet all of them have revered these motifs in one form or another.

From this comes the idea of the unity of the human race, in both its biological and its spiritual history, which Campbell defends throughout the work. The differences between mythologies do not erase these parallels. Instead, they show how the same human concern takes shape in different settings. The structure of the book reflects this approach. It is divided into four volumes, titled in turn Primitive Mythology, Oriental Mythology, Occidental Mythology and Creative Mythology. The first three organize the material by broad traditions and cultures, while the last turns to the human capacity to create new stories and symbols.

The book remains a useful reference for anyone who wants to approach mythology from a comparative perspective. Its length and ambition make it a wide tour of humanity's mythic thought, in which gods appear again and again as faces of the same underlying search.

Author: Joseph Campbell

Trivia

More than 2,600 pages. The complete work fills four volumes and runs to over 2,600 pages. Campbell needed more than a decade to write it.
A title built on a metaphor. The title alludes to the idea that religions and mythologies are metaphors for God, as if each culture showed a different mask of one reality.
Four volumes, four approaches. The volumes are titled Primitive Mythology, Oriental Mythology, Occidental Mythology and Creative Mythology, and they cover very different traditions.
