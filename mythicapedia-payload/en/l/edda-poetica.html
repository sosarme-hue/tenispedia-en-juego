Poetic Edda

The Poetic Edda, also known as the Elder Edda or Sæmundar Edda, is a collection of anonymous Old Norse poems about the Scandinavian gods and heroes of the Germanic tradition. Its main source is the Codex Regius, a medieval Icelandic manuscript, although its poems are considerably older than the copy. Together with Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, it is the most important source for Norse mythology.

Date: 1260-

Trivia

A manuscript named after a gift. The Codex Regius takes its name from the Danish royal family, to which Bishop Brynjólfur Sveinsson gave it in the seventeenth century. It stayed in Copenhagen until 1971, when it returned to Iceland.
Shared lines. Eyvindr skáldaspillir used two lines in his Hákonarmál that also appear in Hávamál. It is unknown whether he quoted them or whether the Eddic poem came later.
Wolves without wolves. Iceland has no wolves, yet its poets knew these animals well. For that reason, the fauna mentioned in the poems is not always a reliable clue to where they were composed.
