Apollo, exiled in New York

The Hidden Oracle

The Hidden Oracle is a 2016 fantasy novel by Rick Riordan and the first book in The Trials of Apollo. The god Apollo, turned into a mortal teenager, must regain control of the oracles of Ancient Greece to win back his father's forgiveness.

The Hidden Oracle is a fantasy novel published in 2016 by the American author Rick Riordan. It is the first book in The Trials of Apollo, the second spin-off of the Percy Jackson & the Olympians series. Riordan draws on Greek and Roman mythology, but this time the story follows Apollo himself. According to the tradition Riordan builds on, Zeus punished the god by turning him into a mortal, and the author chose to explore that punishment from the inside.

The story opens with Apollo tumbling into a dumpster in a New York City alley. He remembers little except his father's anger, and he learns that he now goes by Lester Papadopoulos, a sixteen-year-old with acne and little interest in exercise. A young demigod named Meg McCaffrey rescues him and claims his services until he pays off his debt to Zeus. Together they reach Camp Half-Blood, where Apollo finds that the Oracle of Delphi, housed in Rachel Elizabeth Dare, can no longer prophesy, and that campers keep vanishing into the woods.

To earn his father's pardon, Apollo must regain control of the five oracles of Ancient Greece, beginning with the Grove of Dodona, hidden in the camp's forest. The novel shows Dodona drawing campers toward itself, and reveals that a company called Triumvirate Holdings is behind the threat, aiming to control every oracle. Its leader turns out to be the Roman emperor Nero, who has become a "god-emperor" thanks to the worship he received across history. Historically remembered for his tyranny, Nero becomes here a symbol of power that feeds on devotion.

Critics praised Apollo's narration above all. His voice is witty and self-mocking, a clear shift from earlier books told by demigods. The nod to his role as god of poetry appears in the chapter titles, which are "bad haiku." In classical mythology Apollo also presides over music, archery, healing and prophecy, and the novel plays with each of these as the character tries to win them back.

The book was published in the United States on May 3, 2016, and sold about 62,000 copies in its first week. Riordan had announced the series in 2015 and promised that characters from his earlier series would return.

Trivia

A punishment repeated. Riordan said he decided to subject "poor Apollo" to that punishment for a third time, drawing on two myths in which Zeus turns him into a mortal. The series tells the story from the viewpoint of that mortal.
Deliberately bad poetry. Because Apollo is the god of poetry, each chapter title is a "bad haiku." The game fits the ironic tone of the narrator.
Gifts depending on the store. For the first printing, the publisher offered different gifts depending on where the book was bought, such as a letter from Apollo to Zeus at Barnes & Noble or an illustrated map of his trials at Target.
