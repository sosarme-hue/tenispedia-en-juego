Boccaccio's great genealogy of the pagan gods

Genealogia deorum gentilium

The Genealogia deorum gentilium is a vast Latin mythography by Giovanni Boccaccio, begun around 1360 and revised until his death. Across fifteen books it traces the tangled family ties among the gods of Greece and Rome and offers an allegorical reading of many of them. For centuries it was the most consulted mythological handbook in Europe.

Hugh IV of Cyprus commissioned the work, and Boccaccio began it before 1350. He kept revising it for the rest of his life, so several different versions survive in separate manuscript traditions. The title, usually rendered as "Genealogy of the Pagan Gods", states its aim: to arrange in family trees the crowd of gods, Titans, heroes and monsters inherited from antiquity, whose stories often contradicted one another.

In the prologue Boccaccio admits his doubts. The subject is immense, many ancient sources had been lost, and collecting names was not enough; the fables also had to be read allegorically. He even suggests that Petrarch would be a better choice, but accepts the task knowing the result will be incomplete. He likens himself to a sailor gathering the wreckage of a shipwreck: he will pass on what he finds and offer his interpretation where he can.

The first book opens with a cosmogony in which Demogorgon, a being without origin, stands at the beginning as father of the earliest gods. His companions are Eternity and Chaos, and his children include the Fates, Pan and Erebus. Each receives a symbolic explanation: Chaos is disordered matter, Pan stands for harmonious nature and the Fates for destiny. Later books descend through the lines of Jupiter, Caelus and the Titans, weaving the families of Cadmus, Agenor, Danaus and Prometheus together with heroes such as Oedipus, Perseus and Odysseus.

The central books follow the Titans, the giants and the Pleiades into new branches. The final books trace the descendants of Dardanus, Troy and Aeneas, down to the kings of Latium. Boccaccio closes with lengthy defences of poetry, a section that surprised many of his readers.

The work is humanist in spirit and medieval in structure. Boccaccio drew on earlier mythographers such as the author called Albricus, the Vatican Mythographies and, through them, late antique writers like Fulgentius, as well as Ovid and Statius. His method of arranging material had no real successor, yet the book kept its prestige until the late sixteenth century, when Giraldi published his own great compilation in 1548.

The Genealogia also left a lasting mark on art and literature. Boccaccio spread the idea that Demogorgon was the ancestor of all the pagan gods, a notion that reached Milton and Shelley. He also included family-tree diagrams, which are considered among the earliest non-biblical uses of that kind of graphic.

Author: Giovanni Boccaccio Date: 1360-

Trivia

A god without a father. Boccaccio presents Demogorgon as the first god, with no parent, and as the ancestor of the other pagan gods. The idea came from a misread scholium on Statius and had a notable literary afterlife.
Family trees. Some manuscripts contain diagrammatic family trees, thought to be among the earliest non-biblical uses of this kind of graphic, which already appeared in medieval art in the Tree of Jesse.
