Joseph Campbell's essays on the mythological dimension

The Flight of the Wild Gander

The Flight of the Wild Gander is a collection of essays by Joseph Campbell, written between 1944 and 1968 and published in 1969. It gathers the mythologist's scattered early writings on the origins, transformation and symbolic meaning of myths. Its title refers to an image from Hindu tradition.

Joseph Campbell, the American mythologist, writer and teacher, gathered over more than two decades a series of essays and forewords that had appeared in scattered form. In 1969 they were brought together in a single volume, The Flight of the Wild Gander. It came before The Mythic Dimension and can be read as an early overview of his thinking about myth.

The title comes from the Hindu concept of the paramahamsa, a great spiritual teacher of exalted illumination, able to rise above the mundane. The image rests on the hamsa, a bird which, according to tradition, can fly over the steep peaks of the Himalayas. For Hindus this is a living religious reference rather than a story from the past, and Campbell used it as a metaphor for the wider view that myth can offer from above.

The essays work on several levels. Some examine the psycho-biological sources of mythic forms and symbols, asking what human stories share. Others follow a single Native American legend from its rise to its decline and transformation. Others still look at the secularization of myths in the modern age, when symbols keep their shape but lose the meaning they once held for those who told them.

Taken together, the collection describes the symbolic content of these stories and the way they connect with human experience. Campbell argues that this connection changes over time as people and their circumstances change. That is why the book deals as much with the past as with the role myth can play in everyday life and the forms it might take in the future.

The book was first published by Viking Press in 1969. Harper and Row reissued it in 1990, and New World Library published a third edition in 2002. It is now part of the Collected Works of Joseph Campbell series issued by the Joseph Campbell Foundation, a sign of the lasting interest it holds for readers and scholars of myth.

Trivia

A title taken from Hinduism. The gander in the title comes from the hamsa, a bird that in Hindu tradition symbolizes the ability to rise above the mundane. Campbell linked it to the paramahamsa, the spiritual teacher who reaches exalted illumination.
A book that came before another. The Flight of the Wild Gander appeared in 1969, before The Mythic Dimension, which followed it in Campbell's publishing career.
