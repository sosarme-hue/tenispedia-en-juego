Greek myths of love between men

Lovers' Legends: The Gay Greek Myths

A 2002 book by Andrew Calimach that gathers Greek myths featuring love between men, from Pelops to Achilles and Patroclus. The stories are framed by a dialogue attributed to Pseudo-Lucian.

Lovers' Legends: The Gay Greek Myths is a 2002 book by Andrew Calimach that brings together Greek myths in which love between men plays a central role. The author addresses homosexuality and paederasty, a practice he examines within its Greek context and places at the heart of his reading of the myths.

The book is organised into chapters that revisit some well-known figures. It opens with "Beloved Charioteers" and moves through Tantalus and the Olympians, Pelops in Pisa, Laius, Zeus and Ganymede, Hercules and Hylas, Orpheus, Apollo and Hyacinthus, Narcissus, and Achilles and Patroclus. Between these sections, four parts titled "Different Loves" act as a running thread. The whole collection is framed by a dialogue attributed to Pseudo-Lucian, also called "Different Loves", which serves as the starting point for the discussion.

As with other Greek myths, the stories vary depending on the ancient author who records them. For that reason, the book is best read as a selection and a modern interpretation rather than as a single authoritative version of the tradition.

Beyond the printed page, the material also took theatrical form. Lovers' Legends Unbound was directed by Agnes Lev and performed by Timothy Carter, with incidental music composed and performed by Steve Gorn. In 2004, Haiduk Press released the work as an audio CD accompanied by an illustrated libretto.
