A tragic tale by Tolkien drawn from the Kalevala

The Story of Kullervo

The Story of Kullervo is a prose retelling of one of the tales in the Kalevala, the Finnish epic poem compiled by Elias Lönnrot. Tolkien wrote it between 1914 and 1915, while he was an undergraduate at Oxford. Published much later, in 2010 and 2015, it remains one of the least known works connected with his name.

The Story of Kullervo is a prose version of one of the tales in the Kalevala, the Finnish epic that Elias Lönnrot assembled from folk songs collected in Karelia and Finland. Tolkien wrote it between 1914 and 1915, when he was a student at Exeter College, Oxford. It was an unsettled time in his life, and many readers feel that mood in the story's dark tone.

The narrative follows Kullervo, a young man who is unattractive, short-tempered and hard to control. Raised as a captive in the household of the man who had killed his father, he survives every attempt on his life and ruins each task he is given. Sold into slavery and cruelly treated by his master's wife, Kullervo takes revenge by having wolves and bears devour her alive. On his way home he meets a girl and carries her off, and the two live together for a while until they discover that they are brother and sister. She drowns herself, and the ending makes the sense of doom running through the tale feel complete.

For Tolkien, Kullervo was also an exercise in writing. According to the editorial material, it was the first time the future author of The Lord of the Rings began writing prose, having until then been a poet. He described his aim as turning one of the stories of the Kalevala, which he called a very great and most tragic tale, into a short story. The piece is counted, among other sources, as an influence on the story of Túrin Turambar in The Silmarillion.

The book published by HarperCollins in August 2015 contains more than the story. It includes two essays by Tolkien from the same period, one on the Kalevala and its land of heroes, along with notes and commentary. Verlyn Flieger edited the text, which had first appeared in 2010 in the Tolkien Studies series, and she adds an essay of her own. Reviewers have noted that the book can be a demanding read for those who came to Tolkien through his novels, but it is rewarding for students of folklore and of his work.

Trivia

A work that took a long time to appear. Tolkien wrote it as a student in 1914 and 1915, but the text was not published until 2010, in the Tolkien Studies series, and as a book in 2015.
A first attempt at fantasy. The review magazine Kirkus Reviews called the book Tolkien's first effort at fantasy, surrounded by scholarly apparatus, and noted that serious students of his work and of world folklore would value it most.
