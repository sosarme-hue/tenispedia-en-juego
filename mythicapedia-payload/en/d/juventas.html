Roman goddess of youth

Juventas

Juventas, also known as Iuventus or Juventus, is the Roman goddess who embodies youth and watched over young people as they left childhood behind. She was later identified with the Greek Hebe, though she kept her distinctly Roman traits.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Juventas was a daughter of Jupiter and Juno and a sister of Libertas. The First Vatican Mythographer tells a different version, in which Juno conceived her without a male partner after eating a lettuce. In another account, Juventas was a nymph, also Libertas's sister, whom Jupiter turned into a spring. Its waters were miraculous, restoring youth to anyone who bathed in them.

As a divinity, she watched over adolescents and shielded them from Senectus, the personification of old age, equivalent to the Greek Geras.

Worship and religion

Her main rite took place when a young man set aside his childhood clothing and took up the toga virilis, around the age of 16 or 17; at that moment he left a coin as an offering to the goddess. She had a chapel within the temple of the Capitoline Triad, in the part dedicated to Minerva, and it predated the temple itself. According to Dionysius and Livy, she and the god Terminus refused to be moved when Tarquin wished to rebuild the Capitoline precinct, and both were incorporated into the new structure. After the lectisternium of 218 BC, during the Second Punic War, she was worshipped in the Greek manner, alongside Hercules. Under the Empire she presided over the youth associations, pre-military groups that supported imperial policy.

Ancient sources: Hyginus and the First Vatican Mythographer place her in her genealogy; Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Livy record the legends about the Capitoline temple and the offering linked to Servius Tullius.

Trivia

An offering of passage. The young man who received the toga virilis left a coin as an offering, a gesture tied to the passage from childhood to adult life.
Crucified dogs. A procession called supplicia canum passed between the temples of Juventas and Summanus, and in it Romans carried crucified dogs.
