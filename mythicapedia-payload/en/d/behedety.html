Egyptian god of the winged sun

Behedety

Behedety is an Egyptian god associated with the city of Behedet, the main centre of his worship. Depicted as a winged sun, he symbolised royal sovereignty and the protection of the pharaoh.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Behedety protected the king and kingship from early times; his image appears on the underground panels of the funerary complex of Hor Netcheryjet at Saqqara. From the Fourth Dynasty he was shown as a winged solar disk, with two outspread falcon wings in Heb Sed scenes.

According to the stories, he fought alongside his father Ra when Ra was pursued by enemies, and he protected Harsiese, son of Osiris and Isis, against Seth. Later he became subordinate to Horus and appeared as Hor-Behedety.

Worship and religion

His cult centre was Behedet, a place that existed in several versions: Northern Behedet, Southern Behedet (today Edfu) and Eastern Behedet. In the Greco-Roman period he was the chief god of two nomes, and his temples at Edfu, Dendera, Karnak and Medinet Habu show him frequently.

Trivia

Winged sun. His image as a winged solar disk appears already on the panels at Saqqara and remained in royal iconography for centuries.
Many epithets. He received numerous epithets, one for each of his attributes, such as "the one of colourful plumage".
