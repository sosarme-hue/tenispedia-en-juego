wise old singer of Finnish mythology

Väinämöinen

Väinämöinen is an old, wise singer and demigod in Finnish mythology and the main character of the national epic Kalevala. Part shaman, part hero and part creator of the world, he is one of the most important figures in Finnish tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition, Väinämöinen was conceived when Ilmatar, the daughter of nature, met the wind and the waves. For seven hundred and thirty years he grew inside her while she floated on the sea. A wild duck built its nest on her knee, which rose above the water, and laid seven eggs, six of gold and one of iron. When she shook her body, the eggs fell into the sea and broke, and from them came the earth, the sky, the moon, the stars and the clouds.

Ten years later, Ilmatar had made islands and continents, but Väinämöinen waited thirty more years to be born. He then drifted for eight years across the sea before reaching dry land. There, he asked his son Pellervoinen to sow the ground with trees and flowers, which also brought the first harvest. As the eternal sage, he brought order to chaos and founded the land of Kaleva.

His restless search for a wife drew Kaleva into a first friendly, then hostile, contact with Pohjola, the dark and dangerous northern land ruled by Louhi. That long epic conflict ended with the winning and theft of the Sampo, a magical artifact forged by the smith Ilmarinen.

Legacy

He is the hero of the Finnish national epic Kalevala, compiled by Elias Lönnrot from folk songs. Janne Saarikivi has suggested that his name may be linked to early Germanic forms of Odin's name, though this remains a hypothesis.

Ancient sources: The first written mention appears in a list of Tavastian gods recorded by Mikael Agricola in 1551, and his deeds survive in the runic songs of Ostrobothnia, Karelia and Savonia.

Trivia

A name with a possible Germanic echo. Janne Saarikivi proposed that the name might connect to early Germanic forms of Odin's name, though this is only a hypothesis.
An egg on the knee. In some runic songs, the bird lays the egg that starts the world on Väinämöinen's knee. When he moves it, the eggs fall and break, forming the earth and the sky.
An old hero who weeps. When he falls into the sea or hurts his knee, Väinämöinen cries, and the Mistress of Pohjola remarks how unusual it is to hear an old bearded hero weep.
