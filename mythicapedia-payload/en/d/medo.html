eponymous hero of the Medes, son of Medea

Medus

Medus is a hero of Greek mythology, the son of the sorceress Medea, whose name is linked to the land of Media and the Medes. He matters because the stories present him as the legendary origin of the name of the Medes, and because his tale connects the Jason and Medea cycle with the legend of Theseus.

The myth

In the oldest version, Medus is the son of Medea and Jason. Hesiod tells how Jason, after completing the trials set by King Pelias, returned to Iolcus and took Medea as his wife in that kingdom. Their son, Medeus, was raised by the centaur Chiron in the mountains. The story ends by claiming that all this was part of a plan of Zeus, without saying what the god's will was.

Another tradition, preserved by Pseudo-Apollodorus, places his origin in Athens. After Jason abandoned her for Glauce, daughter of Creon of Corinth, Medea fled and married King Aegeus, and their son was Medus. Both were expelled from Athens once Medea was discovered plotting against Theseus. In this version Medus subdued many barbarian peoples, gave the name Media to the lands he conquered, and died in battle against the Indians.

Helanicus of Mytilene gives the boy the name Polyxenus instead. Pausanias explains that Medea reached a land called Aria with her son, and its inhabitants took the name Medes in her honour rather than his. Diodorus Siculus also records that Medus inherited his father's kingdom and that his people named themselves after him out of admiration for his courage.

In another episode, Medea returns to Colchis, where her uncle Perses had seized the throne from her father Aeetes. Medus arrives there after a storm, is captured and is about to be sacrificed by Medea, who has mistaken him for a Corinthian prince. He then kills Perses with a sword she gives him, regaining his grandfather's kingdom.

Legacy

Medus gives his name to the land of Media and to the Medes, a people who played an important role in the history of the ancient Near East. His figure appears in Greek literary tradition as an example of the link between the Medea myth and the origins of peoples.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Pseudo-Apollodorus, Helanicus of Mytilene, Diodorus Siculus and Pausanias record differing versions of his origin and deeds.

Trivia

Alternative name. Helanicus of Mytilene calls him Polyxenus and considers him the son of Jason and Medea.
Origin of the name Media. Pausanias argues that the Medes took their name from the land of Aria and from Medea, not from her son.
