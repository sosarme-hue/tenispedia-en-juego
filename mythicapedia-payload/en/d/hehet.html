Egyptian goddess of infinite space

Hehet

Hehet, sometimes called Hauhet, is an Egyptian goddess who embodies the boundless, the vastness of space and eternity spread across the four cardinal points. She belongs to the Ogdoad of Hermopolis, the group of eight primordial deities, alongside her husband Heh.

The myth

Hehet is linked to stagnant water searching for its way, an image of the boundlessness that preceded creation. According to Egyptian tradition, she and Heh form one of the four pairs of the Ogdoad, whose members represent the chaos that came before the order of the world.

Her appearance in the texts is late. While Heh is already mentioned in the Coffin Texts of the Middle Kingdom, Hehet is only attested from the New Kingdom, when she appears in the Amduat in the 18th Dynasty as goddess of the twelfth hour of the night, and later in the Book of the Night in the 19th Dynasty.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Amduat and the Book of the Night, New Kingdom funerary texts.
