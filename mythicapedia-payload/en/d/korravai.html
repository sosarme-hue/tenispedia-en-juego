Tamil goddess of war, victory and fertility

Korravai

Korravai, also spelled Kotravai, is a goddess of South India and Sri Lanka linked to war, victory, fertility and agriculture. She belongs to the primordial Dravidian folk religion and is one of the earliest goddesses recorded in Tamil literature.

The myth

Her name comes from the Tamil word korram, meaning "victory, success, bravery". Ancient texts present her as a warrior goddess and also as a mother goddess. Tradition names her as the true mother of the god Kartikeya, and also as one of the children of Shiva's consort in his form as Parvati.

Sangam literature mentions her in several poems, and the Silappadikaram, from around the 2nd century, places her as the goddess of the Pālai region. Some texts say that devoted warriors, in a frenzy, offered her their own heads. She is shown with several arms holding weapons, sometimes riding a lion and, in Tamil Nadu, accompanied by the blackbuck.

Worship and religion

Farmers offer her the first harvest, and animal and plant sacrifices along with ritual dances form part of her worship. Her cult remains alive in rural communities of South India.

Legacy

Ritual ululation, known as Korava Idal or Kulavai Idal, served as a war cry and call to victory and is still performed at celebrations such as weddings and festivals in Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

Ancient sources: She appears in the grammar Tolkappiyam and in Sangam poems such as those of the Pattuppattu and Paripāṭal, as well as in the Silappadikaram.

Trivia

Carved in stone. She appears with a lion and a blackbuck in a rock-relief panel at the Varaha Mandapam in Mahabalipuram.
A victory cry. The ululation once used in battle now accompanies weddings and celebrations.
