daughter of Icarius, heroine of Greek myth

Erigone

Erigone is a young Athenian woman in Greek mythology, daughter of the farmer Icarius. Her story is bound up with Dionysus and with one of the most painful disasters the tradition attributes to the god of wine.

The myth

According to the myth, Icarius showed great kindness to Dionysus, who repaid him by teaching him how to grow the vine and make wine. Icarius shared the drink with his shepherds, who, growing drunk, believed he had poisoned them and killed him.

His daughter Erigone, accompanied by her dog Maera, found her father's body. Overcome with grief, she hanged herself over the grave. Dionysus, enraged, punished Athens by causing all the city's maidens to take their own lives in the same way.

In some versions, Erigone was placed among the stars as the constellation Virgo, by Dionysus or by Zeus, who pitied her misfortune. Ovid adds that Dionysus approached her disguised as a cluster of false grapes, deceived her and seduced her. When Erigone learned of the deception, she gave her son, Staphylus, a name meaning 'bunch of grapes'.

Ancient sources: Ovid recounts her story in the Metamorphoses, where the deception with false grapes appears.
