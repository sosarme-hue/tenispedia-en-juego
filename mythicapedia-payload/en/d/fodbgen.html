legendary High King of Ireland of the Fir Bolg

Fodbgen

Fodbgen is a legendary High King of Ireland belonging to the Fir Bolg people, according to Irish mythology. His name means "the despoiler," and he is remembered mainly for a curious legend about trees.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Fodbgen (in modern Irish, Foidhbhgen) was a son of Sengann of the Fir Bolg. To win the throne of Ireland he overthrew his cousin Rinnal, son of Genann. The stories credit him with a striking detail: before his time, trees had no knots, and the Old Irish word for knot, odb, is related to his older form of the name, Odbgen.

His reign lasted four years. He was overthrown by Eochaid mac Eirc, Rinnal's grandson, who thus won back for his line the throne that had been lost. His story fits into the succession of kings that Irish texts present as rulers of the island.

Ancient sources: Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Annals of the Four Masters and Seathrún Céitinn's Foras Feasa ar Érinn.

Trivia

The meaning of the name. Fodbgen is usually translated as "the despoiler." Its modern Irish spelling is Foidhbhgen.
Trees without knots. According to tradition, before Fodbgen's reign trees had no knots. The Old Irish word for knot, odb, appears in the form Odbgen of his name.
