Greek mythical lion that plagued Boeotia

Lion of Cithaeron

The Lion of Cithaeron was a beast that preyed on the herds of Boeotia and was killed by Heracles during one of his early exploits. Unlike the Nemean Lion, it had no special powers.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, the lion lived on Mount Cithaeron and raided the cattle of Amphitryon and King Thespius of Thespiae. When Heracles was eighteen, Thespius asked him to kill it. The hunt lasted fifty days, during which the king hosted him, and Heracles slept each night with one of his daughters, believing it was always the same woman.

After defeating the beast, Heracles wore its skin and its scalp as a helmet, the outfit by which he would later be recognised. Another tradition, recorded by Pausanias, credits the kill to Alcathous of Elis: King Megareus promised his daughter and his throne to whoever slew the lion, which had killed many people.

Worship and religion

After his victory, Alcathous built a temple to Artemis Agrotera, the Huntress, and to Apollo Agraeus, the Hunter. No cult dedicated to the lion itself is recorded.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in the Bibliotheca; Pausanias; and the Suda, which calls it the Thespian lion and the Ravine lion.

Trivia

A lion named after its place. The Suda calls it the Thespian lion and the Ravine lion, because it lived in a place called charadra, or ravine.
A helmet of lion skin. Heracles wore the animal's scalp as a helmet, a detail that appears in Apollodorus' account.
