Titan goddess of Greek mythology, mother of Apollo and Artemis

Leto

Leto is a goddess of Greek mythology, daughter of the Titans Coeus and Phoebe, and mother of Apollo and Artemis by Zeus. She matters chiefly for the story of her wandering and the birth on the island of Delos, a central episode in the cult of Apollo.

The myth

According to tradition, Leto was the daughter of the Titans Coeus and Phoebe and the sister of Asteria. Zeus loved her and, from their union, Apollo and Artemis were born. Hera, jealous, pursued her and ordered that no land should shelter her during labour. Leto wandered through the Greek world until she reached Delos, a drifting island that agreed to give her refuge in exchange for a great temple and honours for the future god.

In the Homeric Hymns, the labour lasts nine days and nine nights. Hera keeps Eileithyia, the goddess of childbirth, away until the other deities bribe her with a necklace and bring her to the island. Artemis is then born first, followed by Apollo, who days later kills the serpent Python to avenge his mother's suffering. Other accounts tell how Tityos attacked Leto and was struck down by her children, and how Niobe, boasting of having more children than her, lost all of them to Apollo and Artemis.

The sources offer differing versions of her offspring. Some later traditions link her with Hecate or Selene, though these genealogies are usually explained by the identification of Artemis with those goddesses.

Worship and religion

Leto was worshipped together with her children, above all on Delos, where an important sanctuary stood. She was also regarded as a protector of motherhood. In Lycia, in Asia Minor, she had a principal sanctuary, the Letoon, which united the cities of the region.

Legacy

In Roman mythology her name was Latinised as Latona. Her children were collectively called the Letoids. In classical art and literature she appears as the loving mother who suffers Hera's persecution.

Ancient sources: Hesiod presents her as one of Zeus's wives and describes her as the gentlest of the goddesses of Olympus; the Homeric Hymn to Apollo recounts her wandering and the birth on Delos.
