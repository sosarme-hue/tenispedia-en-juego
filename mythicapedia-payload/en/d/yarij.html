Ugaritic moon god

Yarikh

Yarikh is the moon god of the Ugaritic texts, one of the principal deities of his pantheon and the husband of the goddess Nikkal. His figure shows how the moon was conceived in the ancient Near East: as a male deity with a family and agricultural role.

The myth

In the Ugaritic narrative known as The Marriage of Nikkal and Yarikh, Yarikh wished to marry Nikkal, but first he had to win over her father, the summer god Jirjibi. To do so he offered a generous dowry: a thousand pieces of silver, ten thousand of gold and necklaces of lapis lazuli. After the wedding, the Kathirat, goddesses of marriage skills and childbirth, oversaw the birth of their son.

He was credited with the night dew, which, according to tradition, moistened the desert and made orchards of fruit trees bloom. His epithets, "illuminator of the heavens" and "lord of the sickle", refer to his light and to the shape of the crescent moon. One passage calls him "the most pleasant of the gods", and some texts suggest he spent the day in the underworld.

Worship and religion

His worship centred on Ugarit, where he was among the главные deities. In ritual texts, two bulls were sacrificed for him during full-moon celebrations, and he also received rams in offering lists, sometimes alongside Nikkal. His primary cult centre was Larugadu, near Ebla. After the collapse at the end of the Bronze Age, references become scarce, and there is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Legacy

His name survives in the Semitic word for "moon" and "month", related to the Ugaritic term. It is presumed that the city of Jericho took its name from him, though this remains a hypothesis. The sources record no wider presence in later culture.

Ancient sources: The Ugaritic texts found at Ugarit, including the narrative of his marriage to Nikkal, the deity lists and the ritual texts.

Trivia

A name that is also a month. In Ugaritic, yarikh means both the celestial body and "month", a word close to the terms for the moon in other Semitic languages.
A male lunar god. Like most lunar deities of the ancient Near East, Yarikh was male, unlike the Greek moon goddess Selene.
