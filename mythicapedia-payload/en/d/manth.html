Etruscan god of the underworld

Manth

Manth is an epithet of the Etruscan god Śuri, a chthonic fire deity and lord of the underworld. His name is tied to the Manes, the spirits of the dead.

The myth

According to tradition, Manth ruled the underworld together with his consort Catha, also called Mania. Catha was a goddess of the dead, of spirits and of chaos, and was said to be the mother of ghosts, the undead and other night spirits, as well as the Lares and the Manes. Together they formed a divine couple of the afterlife.

Worship and religion

An archaic dedication to the god, written as manθ, was found in a sanctuary at Pontecagnano in southern Italy, suggesting that his worship reached beyond Etruria. The sources do not describe rites or festivals.

Legacy

According to one hypothesis, his name is linked to Mantua, the birthplace of Virgil. Mania, the Greek goddess of madness, derives from the same Proto-Indo-European root *men-, 'to think'.

Ancient sources: Servius, the commentator on Virgil, records the name of the god in the Po Valley.
