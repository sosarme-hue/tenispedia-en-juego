Gaulish god, protector of the Lingones

Cicolluis

Cicolluis was a Gaulish god, identified with the Roman Mars through the interpretatio romana and revered as the protector of the Lingones, a people of eastern Gaul. His cult is documented almost only in Lingon territory, where he is usually paired with the earth-goddess Litavis.

The myth

No ancient narratives about him survive. Almost everything known comes from Latin votive inscriptions, found mostly in modern Côte-d'Or. His name, built from cico- (flesh, breast) and -oll- (great), is interpreted in several ways, such as 'the muscular one' or 'the great protector'.

In most dedications he appears with Litavis, whose name derives from a Celtic word for earth. John Koch regards her as an earth-goddess and him as her consort. On one altar from Mâlain, the consort's native name is replaced by that of Bellona, the Roman war-goddess.

Worship and religion

His main sanctuary was at Mâlain, near the edge of Lingon territory, while no temple has been found in the capital, Langres. Some scholars link this absence to the Roman custom of placing temples of Mars outside the city. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Trivia

Irish counterpart. Christian-Joseph Guyonvarc'h linked his name with Cichol, a Fomorian demon of Irish tradition. Patrice Lajoye considers the connection very weak.
Altar at Xanten. The Lingones set up an altar to this god outside their territory, on the Rhine at Xanten.
