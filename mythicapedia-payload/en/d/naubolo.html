mythical king of Phocis in Greek myth

Naubolus

Naubolus is an elderly figure in Greek myth, linked to Phocis and to the story of Oechalia. He matters because his lineage connects to Iole, the woman for whose sake Heracles sacked the city.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Naubolus was an old man who had a daughter, Antioche, married to Eurytus, king of Oechalia. Other authors call the wife Antiope and make her a daughter of Pylon. In this version Naubolus is grandfather of Iole, and for her sake Heracles, son of Amphitryon, destroyed the city of fine walls.

The stories also place him in the family tree of the Argonauts. Clytius and Iphitus, guardians of Oechalia, were sons of the cruel Eurytus and therefore grandsons of Naubolus. Hyginus, a late writer, changes this line: he makes Iphitus a direct son of Naubolus, from Phocis, though he adds that Iphitus might instead have been a son of Hypasus and a native of the Peloponnese.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, a late writer, varies the genealogy of Iphitus and makes him a direct son of Naubolus; the other references belong to Greek tradition about Oechalia and the Argonauts.

Trivia

Two possible fathers for Iphitus. Hyginus records that Iphitus may have been a son of Naubolus, born in Phocis, or a son of Hypasus, a native of the Peloponnese.
Argonauts of Oechalia. Clytius and Iphitus, sons of Eurytus, appear among the Argonauts as guardians of Oechalia.
