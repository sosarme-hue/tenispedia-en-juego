Egyptian deity linked to the northern bald ibis

Akhty

Akhty (also read Netjer-akhty) was an ancient Egyptian deity, rarely mentioned in the sources, who was depicted as a northern bald ibis. His story is tied to the first pharaohs of the Second Dynasty and to the Egyptian idea of the human spirit.

The myth

Akhty appears only rarely in the texts, and no lengthy story about him survives. According to Egyptian belief, the akh bird represented the human spirit (akh). This idea may have been inspired by the shimmering of its feathers, which the Egyptians compared to the glitter of the stars in the night sky.

Akhty was believed to dwell on the evening horizon, guiding the setting sun and carrying the spirit of the deceased safely into the night sky.

Worship and religion

His earliest mentions appear on stone vessel inscriptions from the reign of the pharaoh Hotepsekhemwy, and he was also depicted under his successors Raneb and Nynetjer. Some Egyptologists, such as Wolfgang Helck, regard him as a dynastic and ancestral deity. In the Old Kingdom his name appears mainly in personal names and priestly titles such as hem-netjer-Akhty (“god’s servant of Akhty”), and it becomes increasingly rare in later periods.

Ancient sources: No major literary texts mentioning him survive; his earliest references come from inscriptions on stone vessels.

Trivia

A bird and the stars. The Egyptians compared the shimmer of the ibis’s feathers to the twinkling of the stars, which may have strengthened its link with the spirits of the dead.
A priestly title. During the Old Kingdom the title hem-netjer-Akhty, meaning “god’s servant of Akhty”, was in use.
