Egyptian vulture goddess of Upper Egypt

Nekhbet

Nekhbet was an Egyptian protective goddess of births and wars, linked to Upper Egypt. Her vulture form made her one of the most recognisable symbols of pharaonic kingship, alongside Wadjet's cobra.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Nekhbet was the local goddess of Nekheb, today's El Kab, and over time she became the protector of all of Upper Egypt. The stories present her as a powerful and fearsome deity who takes her place beside Wadjet above the head of Ra or the pharaoh to defend them, spitting fire at anyone she considers harmful. She also guarded the king during his birth, his coronation, jubilee festivals and battles.

Her role in creation is one of her most unusual features. In the Pyramid Texts she appears as a demiurge who establishes creation through seven words, a power she shares with Neith. She is also called Mother of the Sun and Daughter of Ra, and she is associated with the birth of the gods, which places her among the oldest divinities of the Egyptian pantheon.

In art she appeared as a white vulture with outspread wings wearing the White Crown of Upper Egypt, known as the hedyet. Sometimes she was shown as a woman wearing the same crown, holding arrows in her warlike role, or as a nurturing cow.

Worship and religion

Her main shrine was at Nekheb, and she also had a temple at Edfu. She was a national goddess and patron of Nekhen (Hierakonpolis), the capital of Upper Egypt. Her worship goes back to the predynastic period, when she was already venerated in her home town. In royal titulary she appeared alongside Wadjet as the Nebty, the 'Two Ladies', a title expressing that the pharaoh was under the protection of both goddesses.

Legacy

Her image has entered modern popular culture. She appears as a bird-like monster in the video game Final Fantasy XII, as a minor antagonist in Rick Riordan's novel The Throne of Fire, as the name of a pet vulture in the anime Tenshi ni Narumon, and as an ascending goddess in the Dungeon Crawler Carl series.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, where she appears as a demiurge who creates the world with seven words.

Trivia

A very ancient shrine. Her shrine at Nekheb is among the earliest temples in Egypt, and the city became the companion of Nekhen as the religious and political centre of Upper Egypt.
Which vulture was it?. Scholars disagree about the species shown. Alan Gardiner proposed the griffon vulture, while Arielle Kozloff argues it more closely resembles the lappet-faced vulture.
