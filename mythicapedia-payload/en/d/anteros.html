Greek god of requited love

Anteros

Anteros is, in Greek mythology, the god of requited love and the punisher of those who scorn the affection of others. He belongs to the Erotes and is the brother of Eros, whose rival and avenger he is. His figure embodies the idea that love should be returned.

The myth

According to the myth, Aphrodite complained to Themis that Eros remained a perpetual child, and the goddess advised her to give him a brother. Anteros was born, and whenever he stood beside Eros, Eros grew; when he went away, Eros shrank back. Originally he opposed Eros and fought him, a conflict also understood as the rivalry between two lovers.

The genealogies vary. Cicero made him the son of Ares and Aphrodite, the third Aphrodite, daughter of Zeus and Dione and wife of Hephaestus. Aelian, by contrast, says he was born from the mutual love of Poseidon and Nerites.

The best-known episode concerns Meles and Timagoras. The Athenian Meles spurned the love of the metic Timagoras and asked him to throw himself from the top of a rock. Timagoras obeyed, and when Meles saw him dead, he threw himself from the same rock. For this reason Anteros came to be seen as the avenger of unrequited love.

Worship and religion

The metics of Athens set up an altar to him in memory of Timagoras, and some Athenians worshipped him as the avenging spirit of that young man. In the gymnasium of Malto in Elis, a relief showed Anteros trying to snatch a palm branch from Eros.

Legacy

Plato described anteros as a counter-love, a mirror image of the lover's feelings returned by the beloved. In London, the Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain in Piccadilly Circus depicts him as a symbol of selfless charity, though it is popularly mistaken for Eros.

Ancient sources: Cicero, Aelian, Porphyry and Plato mention him, although most ancient authors give him little attention.
