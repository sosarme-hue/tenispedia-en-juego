Adrasteia

Adrasteia is a figure of Greek myth who, depending on the author, appears as a goddess, an epithet or a nymph. Tradition remembers her as the nurse of Zeus in Crete, and her name, meaning 'she from whom there is no escape', links her to fate and to Nemesis.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Apollodorus, when Cronus devoured his children, Rhea gave birth to Zeus in a cave on Mount Dicte in Crete and entrusted him to the Curetes and to the nymphs Adrasteia and Ida, daughters of Melisseus, to raise him. Apollonius says Adrasteia made the infant a small sphere as a toy. Callimachus adds that the Melian nymphs of Dicte rocked him, that Adrasteia used a golden cradle, that the goat Amalthea nursed him and that the bee Panacris brought him honey.

For the Orphics, Adrasteia, Ananke and Himarmene might be different names for one power of destiny. In their tradition, Night placed bronze cymbals in Adrasteia's hands for her to clash, a sound similar to the one the Curetes made as they danced in arms, drowning out the cries of the infant so that Cronus would not hear him.

Worship and religion

Adrasteia was worshipped alongside Nemesis on Cos, and Pausanias mentions an image of her in a temple at Cirrha dedicated to Apollo, Artemis and Leto. Aeschylus places her lands in Berecyntian territory, the home of the Mother of the gods, Cybele.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Apollonius of Rhodes, Callimachus, Aeschylus, Euripides and Pausanias, plus the epic poem Phoronis.
