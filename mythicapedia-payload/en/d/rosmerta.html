Gaulish goddess of abundance and fertility

Rosmerta

Rosmerta was a Gaulish goddess of abundance and fertility, worshipped mainly in eastern Gaul and the Rhineland. Her name, meaning 'the great provider', places her beside Mercury, her consort in most surviving dedications.

The myth

No narrative myths about Rosmerta survive: no classical author names her, and nearly all our knowledge comes from inscriptions and reliefs. These images show a goddess of plenty, holding a cornucopia, a patera, a purse or a basket of fruit, and sometimes Mercury's caduceus.

Her name is a Gaulish compound. The prefix ro- means 'very, great', and the stem smert- is usually understood as 'provision' or 'provider'. The exact sense of smert- is still debated among scholars.

Worship and religion

About 27 inscriptions from France, Germany and Luxembourg name her, plus two from Dacia, and most pair her with Mercury. Those who set up these dedications included magistrates, town councillors and seviri Augustales. At Wasserbillig she is linked to the founding of a hospital, and at Niederemmel dedications were placed in a temple. Some inscriptions in Gallia Belgica give her the epithet sacrum, and others invoke her alone, which has led scholars to suggest her cult may have been independent of Mercury. The sources do not show whether her worship survived Roman rule.

Legacy

The sources record no later presence of Rosmerta in art, literature or language. Her modern interest lies mainly in scholarly debate over her relationship with Mercury: Lambrechts saw her as little more than the god's female counterpart, while others argue she had a cult of her own.

Trivia

Only Gaulish inscription. A potsherd found at Lezoux, in Puy-de-Dôme, is the only known inscription in the Gaulish language that names her.
A wooden bucket in Gloucester. On a relief from Gloucester, Rosmerta carries a wooden bucket, an attribute that Aldhouse-Green links to the ale buckets of pre-Roman Britain.
