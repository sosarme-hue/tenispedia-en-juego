Viking hero of the Völsung line

Sinfjötli

Sinfjötli is a hero of Norse mythology, son of Sigmund and his sister Signý, and the central figure of the Völsunga saga. His story, shaped by incest and vengeance, places him among the great warriors of the Völsung dynasty.

The myth

In the Völsunga saga, Signý, daughter of King Völsung, begs her husband Siggeir not to make her return to him, but he ends up murdering her father and brothers. Only Sigmund survives. Determined that revenge will continue, Signý exchanges shapes with a völva and spends three nights with her brother without him knowing who she is. Sinfjötli is born, strong and fearless, able to ignore pain. Together, he and Sigmund kill Siggeir.

Later, in Hunaland, Borghild, Sigmund's jealous wife, poisons a cup of wine meant for Sinfjötli. His father carries the body to the fjords, where Odin, disguised as a ferryman, takes him to Valhalla.

Legacy

J.R.R. Tolkien drew on this legend for his work The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún.

Ancient sources: The Völsunga saga and the Poetic Edda (First Lay of Helgi Hundingsbane); Beowulf mentions him as Fitela, Sigmund's nephew.

Trivia

Ignoring pain. According to the saga, Sinfjötli says pain seems trifling to a Völsung, and he proves it against the cowardly sons of Siggeir.
Cursed wolf skins. After donning the wolf skins of sleeping men in the forest, Sigmund and Sinfjötli are cursed with a form of lycanthropy, according to some versions.
