eponymous hero of the Illyrians in Greek mythology

Illyrius

Illyrius is, in Greek mythology, the eponymous hero of the Illyrians, the ancestor who gave his name to this people and to the region of Illyria. The sources offer two different genealogies: one makes him the son of Cadmus and Harmonia, the other the son of the Cyclops Polyphemus and Galatea.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Cadmus and Harmonia left Thebes and reached the land of the Encheleans, who were under attack from the Illyrians. An oracle told them they would win if they handed over command to the newcomers. They did so and defeated the invaders. Cadmus then ruled the Illyrians, and had a son, Illyrius, who gave his name to the land.

Appian gives another version, in which the land's origin is credited to Illyrius, son of Polyphemus and Galatea. In this tradition the couple had three sons, Celtus, Illyrius and Galas, who left Sicily and gave their names to the Celts, the Illyrians and the Galatians. Illyrius had six sons and three daughters, from whom peoples such as the Taulantians, the Perrhaebi, the Encheleans, the Dardanians and the Dassaretae descended. Appian declines to go further, leaving such details to those who study the distant past.

Legacy

In the Greek tradition his name explains that of Illyria, the region along the Adriatic coast. Greek writers used the word "Illyrian" for peoples lying between the Liburnians and Epirus, and in the Roman period Illyricum covered an even wider area between the Adriatic and the Danube.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Bibliotheca, and Appian, in his Illyrian Wars, are the main ancient sources that mention him.

Trivia

Two genealogies. Apollodorus presents him as a son of Cadmus and Harmonia, while Appian makes him a son of Polyphemus and Galatea, with brothers who gave their names to the Celts and the Galatians.
Sons named after peoples. Several of his sons and grandsons give their names to real peoples, such as the Dardanians, the Encheleans or the Scordisci, according to the tradition recorded by Appian.
