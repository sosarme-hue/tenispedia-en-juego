Greek god of the Jhelum river

Hydaspes

Hydaspes is a river god of Greek mythology, linked to a swift river of the Pentapotamia region, identified with the modern Jhelum in Punjab. He matters because he links the world of rivers to the genealogy of the primordial gods and to the distant India of Greek accounts.

The myth

According to the ancient texts, Hydaspes was the son of Thaumas, the sea god, and Electra, an Oceanid, and the brother of Iris, messenger of the rainbow. While Iris travels on foot among the immortals, Hydaspes travels by water among the rivers, and both share the same speed on different paths.

With Astris, daughter of Helios and of the Oceanid or Nereid Ceto according to the cited version, he fathered Deriades, king of Pentapotamia. Nonnus of Panopolis, in his Dionysiaca, says Hydaspes supported the Indian peoples against the armies of Dionysos.

Plutarch preserves another story: Aphrodite punished the princess Chrysippe by making her fall in love with her own father. When Hydaspes discovered it, he had the nurse buried alive and his daughter crucified; overcome with grief, he threw himself into the Indus, which was said to have been renamed Hydaspes after him.

Legacy

The river's name entered ancient history: the Hydaspes is where Alexander the Great defeated Porus in 326 BC, according to classical sources. Its mythical associations survived in Greek geography of India.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis in his Dionysiaca, Plutarch, and references in Hesiod to Thaumas and Electra.

Trivia

A river with two names. The Jhelum, called Vitasta in Sanskrit, is the river the Greeks named Hydaspes. In classical accounts, a river of the Indus also took the name.
