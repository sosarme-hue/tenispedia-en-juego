Egyptian god of the Orion constellation

Sah

Sah is a god of ancient Egyptian religion, linked to the constellation Orion and closely associated with Osiris. His consort Sopdet, known by the Greek name Sothis, represents the star Sirius.

The myth

Little survives about Sah. According to the Pyramid Texts, he was a form of Osiris and the embodiment of Orion, while his wife represented Sirius. Both played a key role in the deceased's journey to the Duat.

Sah always rose on the horizon before his wife, and he was described as a swift god with long strides who looks backward. The pharaoh was thought to travel to Orion after death.

Worship and religion

Sah bore epithets such as "Father of the Gods", "Lord of Life" or "Lord of wine at the uag festival", though many of these he shared with Osiris. His link to kingship and the fate of the soul made him relevant in funerary beliefs.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom call him "Father of the Gods" and connect him with the pharaoh's journey to the sky.
