form of Vishnu, Hindu deity of the primordial waters

Narayana

Narayana is one of the forms and epithets of Vishnu, shown in yogic sleep on the celestial ocean. He appears in the Vedas and the Puranas and is regarded as the Supreme Being in Vaishnavism, the devotional tradition centred on Vishnu.

The myth

According to tradition, Narayana is the primordial being who existed even before Brahma and Ishana (Shiva). His name is usually explained as meaning "the one whose home is the waters": nara means water or man, and yana means vehicle, vessel or abode. The texts describe him with the dark blue hue of water-laden clouds and four arms holding a lotus, a mace, a conch and a discus.

In the Bhagavata Purana he is the Supreme Personality of Godhead who creates the fourteen worlds. Brahma, governed by rajas, creates; Vishnu, governed by sattva, sustains; and at the end of the great cycle Narayana destroys the universe as Kalagni Rudra, lord of tamas. Madhvacharya counts him among the five vyuhas of Vishnu, cosmic emanations distinct from his avatars. In the Mahabharata, Krishna and Arjuna are linked to him as Nara-Narayana, a mystical pair.

His eternal abode is Vaikuntha, a realm of bliss. In Mahayana Buddhism he appears as a guardian deva of the mandalas, and in Jain tradition he is a powerful half-brother of Balabhadra.

Worship and religion

Veneration of Narayana is part of devotion to Vishnu. Vaishnavas worship him as the Supreme Being, and Ramanuja's prayer of surrender in the Sharanagati Gadyam became a model for his Sri Vaishnava followers. Hinduism keeps this worship alive today.

Ancient sources: The Vedas (the Narayana Suktam and the Vishnu Suktam), the Narayana Upanishad, the Mahanarayana Upanishad, the Mahabharata, the Bhagavad Gita, the Bhagavata Purana and the Padma Purana.
