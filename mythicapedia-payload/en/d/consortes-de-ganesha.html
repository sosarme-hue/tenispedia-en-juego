wives and companions of the Hindu god Ganesha

Consorts of Ganesha

The consorts of Ganesha, the elephant-headed Hindu deity, vary across traditions: sometimes he is celibate, sometimes he is married to Siddhi and Riddhi, and sometimes he is paired with a shakti who embodies his creative energy. These variations show how one deity has been reinterpreted across regions and periods.

The myth

According to the Shiva Purana, Ganesha and his brother Skanda compete to marry the daughters of Prajapati, Siddhi and Buddhi. Their parents, Shiva and Parvati, send them around the universe. Skanda speeds off on a peacock, while Ganesha circles his parents seven times and argues that they contain the universe. He wins, marries both, and has two sons, Kshema and Labha.

In the Brahma-vaivarta Purana, the goddess Tulsi falls in love with Ganesha, who wishes to remain celibate. She curses him, he curses her in return, and later Tulsi repents and the curse is softened.

Worship and religion

In the sanctum of the Morgaon temple near Pune, Siddhi and Buddhi flank the image of Ganesha. The Puranas treat them as part of his nature and they receive no separate cult.

Legacy

The 1975 Indian film Jai Santoshi Maa depicts Ganesha as a householder married to Riddhi and Siddhi, and its popularity helped fuel the cult of the goddess Santoshi Ma.

Ancient sources: Ganesha Purana, Shiva Purana, Brahma-vaivarta Purana, Matsya Purana and Mudgala Purana.

Trivia

Eight attendants. In later iconography, the eight powers of the Ashtasiddhi appear as eight young women surrounding Ganesha. In a painting by Raja Ravi Varma, the fans show that they are attendants.
Buddhipriya. Buddhipriya is one of Ganesha's names. It can mean fond of intelligence or husband of Buddhi, depending on how the word priya is read.
