Greek hero, son of Helios and Rhodos

Actis

Actis is, in Greek mythology, one of the sons of Helios, the sun god, and of Rhodos, the nymph after whom the island of Rhodes was named. He is remembered mainly for founding Heliopolis in Egypt and for bringing astrology to that land.

The myth

According to the tradition, the island of Rhodes suffered a great flood, and Helios, who had fallen in love with Rhodos, dried it out. The couple had seven sons and a daughter, known collectively as the Heliadae.

Harmony among the brothers did not last. Jealous of the intelligence and skill of their fifth brother, Tenages, the other Heliadae ended up killing him. Actis fled Rhodes because of this crime and made his way to Egypt, a land that the story presents as unknown to the Greeks.

There he founded the city of Heliopolis in honour of his father and set up a school of astrology that, the tradition says, was famous for generations. His mastery of the art was credited to the inspiration he received from Helios.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus records his story, crediting Actis with founding Heliopolis and teaching astrology to the Egyptians.
