second High King of Ireland in Irish legend

Rudraige mac Dela

In Irish legend, Rudraige mac Dela was the second High King of Ireland and belonged to the Fir Bolg people. His figure is linked to the province of Ulster and to the lineage of the Ulaid.

The myth

Tradition holds that Rudraige was a son of Dela and belonged to the Fir Bolg, the people who invaded Ireland. After their arrival, the five sons of Dela divided the island among themselves. Rudraige received Ulster: he landed at Tracht Rudraige, today Dundrum Bay in County Down, and together with his brother Genann he led the part of the people known as the Fir Domnann. His wife was called Liber.

After the death of his brother Sláine, Rudraige ruled as High King for two years, until he died at Brú na Bóinne (Newgrange). He was succeeded by his brothers Gann and Genann.

Trivia

A shared name. Rudraige was also the name of a son of Partholón, who led the first settlement of Ireland after the Flood, and of a Milesian High King of the 2nd or 3rd century BC. All three are associated with Ulster.
A place bearing his name. Rudraige's landing place, Tracht Rudraige, is located in today's Dundrum Bay in County Down, Northern Ireland.
