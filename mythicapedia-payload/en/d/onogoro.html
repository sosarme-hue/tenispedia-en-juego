primordial island of Japanese Shinto

Onogoro

Onogoro is a mythical island in Shinto, the first one created by the deities Izanagi and Izanami according to the Kojiki, and the starting point for the formation of the Japanese archipelago.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Izanagi and Izanami stood on the Floating Bridge of Heaven and churned the sea with a heavenly spear. When they lifted it, drops fell and coalesced into the island, whose name means something like coalescing by itself.

They built a palace there with a great central pillar. Walking around it in opposite directions, they met and married. Their union produced the other islands of Japan and several deities, until Izanami died giving birth to the fire god.

Worship and religion

Shinto places it near Awaji, on the island of Nushima. There a shrine honouring both deities stands on a sacred hill, and a towering rock of about 30 metres, Kamitategami-Iwa, is revered as the heavenly pillar where they descended.

Legacy

Its exact location has been debated: Motoori Norinaga linked it to small islands near Awaji, such as Nushima or Tomogashima. Today the rock on Nushima is a symbol of the origin of the Japanese nation.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, which mentions it only three times.

Trivia

A very ancient rock. In 1994 a rare formation called Sayagata-shūkyoku, a fold about 100 million years old nicknamed the Wrinkle of the Earth, was discovered on Nushima.
A telling name. The name comes from three elements meaning self, coalesce and island, hence the idea that it coalesces by itself.
