myth that set off the Trojan War

The Judgement of Paris

The Judgement of Paris is an episode from Greek mythology that, according to tradition, led to the outbreak of the Trojan War. It tells how a Trojan prince was asked to choose among three goddesses, and how his choice set the conflict in motion.

The myth

According to the story, Eris, goddess of discord, was left out of the wedding of Peleus. Angered by the snub, she turned up anyway and tossed a golden apple inscribed "to the fairest one" into the feast. Hera, Athena and Aphrodite all claimed it, and Zeus, unwilling to settle the matter himself, appointed Paris of Troy, a herdsman prince, as judge.

Each goddess tried to win him over. Hera offered power and dominion over kingdoms, Athena wisdom and success in war, and Aphrodite the love of the most beautiful mortal woman in the world. Paris chose Aphrodite. That woman was Helen, wife of King Menelaus of Sparta. Paris carried her off to Troy, and Menelaus's demand for her return led the Greeks to invade the city.

Legacy

The scene was a favourite subject in painting. Lucas Cranach the Elder painted it in a work now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, and Rubens produced two famous versions, one in the National Gallery in London (1636) and one in the Prado Museum (1639). The phrase "apple of discord" derives from this story.
