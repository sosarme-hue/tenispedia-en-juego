king of Praeneste in the Aeneid

Erulus

Erulus is a king of Praeneste who appears in Virgil's Aeneid and is the son of the goddess Feronia. What sets him apart is that he received three souls and three sets of arms, which tripled his ability to defend himself.

The myth

In Virgil's account, the goddess Feronia gave him three animae at birth, and with them three sets of arms that multiplied his strength in combat. The poem tells his story through the Arcadian king Evander, founder of Pallantium, who allies himself with Aeneas and the Trojan newcomers. Evander regrets that the frailty of old age keeps him from fighting at Aeneas's side, and he recalls the warrior deeds of his youth.

No other literary source mentions Erulus. He may be Virgil's own invention, perhaps based on the mythological figure Geryon, or he may belong to an archaic tradition with no other surviving reference, since his mother's cult is attested only sparsely in literature.

Legacy

Some scholars have seen Erulus as an influence on Spenser's conception of Triamond's threefold life in The Faerie Queene.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, the only surviving literary source that mentions him.

Trivia

A threefold life. The idea of a character with three souls and three sets of arms reappears, according to some scholars, in Spenser's Triamond in The Faerie Queene.
A unique witness. Erulus is a rare case: no other known ancient author mentions him, so his presence depends almost entirely on a single poem.
