Russian bogatyr, youngest of the three legendary heroes of Kiev

Alyosha Popovich

Alyosha Popovich is one of the bogatyrs, the knight-errant heroes of Russian folklore who serve the prince of Kiev. He is the youngest of the three great heroes, alongside Dobrynya Nikitich and Ilya Muromets, and is known more for his cleverness than his strength.

The myth

In the byliny, the Russian oral epic poems of Kievan Rus', Alyosha is the son of a priest, which gives him his surname, Popovich, meaning 'priest's son'. He is described as a sharp-witted young man who defeats his enemies through trickery and mockery rather than by force alone.

His most famous exploit pits him against the dragon Tugarin Zmeyevich, a gluttonous and boorish foe who insults the prince and the princess. Alyosha disguises himself as a deaf pilgrim to approach unnoticed, and when rain soaks the dragon's wings and brings him down to earth, he strikes off his head. He then puts on Tugarin's multicolored robe to fool his squire into believing the dragon is returning victorious to Kiev.

Legacy

Alyosha appears in Viktor Vasnetsov's famous painting Bogatyrs (1898), alongside Dobrynya and Ilya. He has starred in Russian animated films such as Alyosha Popovich and Tugarin Zmey (2004), and appears as a supporting character in several Russian fantasy films. He is also one of the main characters of the animated series Bogatyrs by Melnitsa Animation Studio.

Ancient sources: The oral byliny recorded in the nineteenth century, such as the versions collected by Kirsha Danilov, and the fairy tale collected by Afanasyev.
