wife of Cecrops in Greek myth

Agraulos

Agraulos is a minor figure of Greek myth linked to Attica: wife of Cecrops and mother of Erysichthon and three daughters. Her interest lies mainly in the uncertainties over how her name was transmitted in ancient sources.

The myth

According to tradition, Agraulos was the daughter of Acteus and the wife of Cecrops. Their union produced Erysichthon and three daughters, Aglauros, Herse and Pandrosos. Ancient texts hardly narrate any episodes about her, and her role is almost always reduced to being a mother and a wife.

The name raises a problem of transmission. Hellanicus of Lesbos, the oldest source, calls her Agraulos. Apollodorus distinguishes between Agraulos for the mother and Aglauros for the daughter, while Pausanias uses Agraulos for both. Only late and exceptional versions apply the form Aglauros to the mother.

Ancient sources: Hellanicus of Lesbos, Apollodorus and Pausanias mention her as the mother of Erysichthon and of Cecrops's daughters.

Trivia

A name that shifts. Pausanias uses Agraulos for both the mother and the daughter, while the forms with Aglauros appear mainly in late versions.
The daughters and their matronymic. The three girls are called Aglaurides, daughters of Aglauros, and not Agraulides, which shows that the form Aglauros was used for the daughter.
