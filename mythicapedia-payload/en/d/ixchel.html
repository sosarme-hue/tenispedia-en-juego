Maya goddess of medicine, midwifery and water

Ixchel

Ixchel is a goddess of Maya mythology linked to medicine, midwifery, pregnancy, water and weaving. She matters because her shrines and pilgrimages, especially on the island of Cozumel, show how central fertility and women's health were in Maya religion.

The myth

Ixchel is described as the wife of the god Itzamna. The sources present her in two aspects: a beneficent patron of childbirth and healing, and a feared form who emptied the jars of wrath upon the world. She is usually shown as an old woman, often with jaguar ears, pouring water from a jar onto the earth or weaving on a backstrap loom. A serpent forms her headdress and crossed bones decorate her skirt.

According to a Verapaz myth recorded by Las Casas, Ixchel and her husband had thirteen sons, two of whom created heaven and earth and everything in them. Few other stories featuring her survive, and her mythology may once have centred on the sweatbath, where Maya mothers prepared for and recovered from birth.

Worship and religion

Ixchel had four manifestations, linked to the four directions of the universe and to the colours red, white, black and yellow. She was honoured in the month of Zip under her healing aspect. Her most celebrated sanctuary stood on Cozumel, where pilgrims travelled by canoe from the port of Pole to consult her oracle; women hoping to have children also made the journey. Today, an annual canoe crossing in her honour takes place from Playa del Carmen.

Legacy

For a long time Ixchel was confused with a moon goddess because of her link to fertility, but that identification is now considered doubtful. In modern popular culture she appears as a character in games and comics, such as the Darklord Ixchel card in Yu-Gi-Oh!, a member of the Ahau in Marvel, and a playable mage in Smite.

Ancient sources: Maya codices depict her, especially the Dresden Codex, where she is known as Goddess O; the Chilam Balam names her Ix Chel, and Diego de Landa in the sixteenth century described her as the goddess of medicine and of making children.

Trivia

Isla Mujeres. A small island north of Cozumel was named for the idols of goddesses such as Ixchel that the conquistadors found there.
Goddess of old age. Although linked to fertility, she is most often shown as an old woman, reflecting the role of elder midwives.
