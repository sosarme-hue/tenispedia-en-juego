legendary founder hero of Tibur (Tivoli)

Tiburtus

In Roman tradition, Tiburtus is the hero who gave his name to Tibur, today's Tivoli in Lazio. His figure links the legendary origins of the town with a founding story that reached Roman writers.

The myth

According to the account passed down by Gaius Julius Solinus, drawing on Cato the Elder's Origines, the city was founded by Catillus the Arcadian, son of Amphiaraus, who arrived in the area after escaping the slaughter at Thebes. Catillus and his three sons, Tiburtus, Coras and another Catillus, drove the Siculi off the Aniene plateau and founded a city they called Tibur in honour of Tiburtus.

Another version holds that Tibur was a colony of Alba Longa, so the tradition about the town's origins is not uniform.

Legacy

Tiburtus's name is tied to that of Tibur, the town from which the modern name Tivoli derives. Some scholars suggest Tibur shares a root with the river Tiber and the Latin praenomen Tiberius, though this remains a hypothesis that cannot be confirmed.

Ancient sources: Cato the Elder's Origines, now lost, known through Gaius Julius Solinus.

Trivia

A founding by family. According to the account, Catillus and his three sons acted together: two of them carried the name Catillus, and another bore the name Tiburtus, who gave the city its name.
Two possible founders. While one version names Catillus the Arcadian, another attributes the founding of Tibur to a colony from Alba Longa.
