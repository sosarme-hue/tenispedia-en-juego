serpentine monster of Mesopotamian myth

Labbu

Labbu is a serpentine, lion-like monster from Mesopotamian tradition, the central figure of an ancient epic about the struggle against a beast threatening the world. Scholars consider it a possible forerunner of Tiamat in the Enuma Elish.

The myth

The tale, known as 'The Slaying of Labbu', survives in two very fragmentary copies: one from the Old Babylonian period and one Assyrian from the Library of Ashurbanipal. According to the text, cities fall into ruin and people dwindle while a huge serpent, some fifty leagues long, terrifies the gods. The sea gave birth to it, and in the later version the god Enlil creates it to wipe out humanity, whose noise disturbs his sleep.

The gods are terrified and seek someone to kill it. In the later version the chosen god is Tishpak, while in the earlier one the role belongs to Nergal. After hesitating, he receives military advice, a storm rises, and an arrow brings the beast down. The reward would be kingship over Eshnunna. In the tradition, the myth justifies that god's rise to kingship.

Legacy

The monster's name can be read in several ways in cuneiform, which has linked it to Rahab, the biblical chaos monster. Scholars regard it as a possible precursor of Tiamat and of the Canaanite tale of Baal against Yam.

Ancient sources: Old Babylonian and Assyrian tablets from the Library of Ashurbanipal preserve the story of Labbu's slaying.
