warrior and sorceress of Irish Celtic mythology

Carman

Carman is a warrior and sorceress in Irish mythology who, according to tradition, came from Athens with her three sons to invade Ireland in the time of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Her story matters because it explains the origin of a place and a festival that carried her name.

The myth

According to the tale, Carman arrived with her sons Dub, Dother and Dian and tried to conquer the island. Using her magical powers, she destroyed all the fruit of Ireland.

Four of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Crichinbel, Lug, Bé Chuille and Aoi, challenged her. The sons were forced to leave Ireland and Carman was imprisoned. She died of longing and was buried in Wexford, among oak trees. Bres dug her grave.

Worship and religion

The Tuatha Dé Danann instituted an Óenach Carmán, or Festival of Carmán, in her honour, held in Leinster at Lughnasad on 1 August.

Legacy

The place where she was buried took her name and is still called Carman.

Ancient sources: A poem in the Metrical Dindshenchas tells her story and dates her death to 600 BCE.

In art

Trivia

Names of her sons. Her sons had names that expressed qualities: Dub means darkness, Dother evil and Dian violence.
A precise date. The Metrical Dindshenchas poem places her death in 600 BCE, a date with no historical basis.
