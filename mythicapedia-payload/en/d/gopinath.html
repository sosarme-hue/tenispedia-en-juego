Gopinath

Gopinath is one of the principal names of Krishna in Hinduism, meaning "lord of the cowherdesses". It is linked to the gopis, the milkmaids of the Braj region, who embody selfless devotion (bhakti) to the deity.

The myth

The bond between Krishna and the gopis is described in texts such as the Harivamsa, the Bhagavata Purana and the Gita Govinda. In these traditions the gopis gather around him in love and worship, and the name grows out of that setting.

The name is a Sanskrit compound. The most common reading takes it as a tatpurusha compound, "Lord (or protector) of the gopis". The Gaudiya school offers another reading, a bahuvrihi compound, "Krishna whose masters are the gopis".

Worship and religion

In many Krishna temples the god is worshipped as Gopinath. Historic shrines include the Radha Gopinath temples in Jaipur and Vrindavan, the Gopinath Dev Temple in Gujarat, and several temples in West Bengal and Bangladesh. Worship continues at these sites.

Ancient sources: The Harivamsa, the Bhagavata Purana and the Gita Govinda describe the relationship between Krishna and the gopis. The term gopinatha is already attested in the Atharva-veda with the sense of "refuge, help".
