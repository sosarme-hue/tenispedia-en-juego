Greek hero linked to Delphi

Hyamus

Hyamus is a hero of Greek mythology associated with the city of Delphi and Mount Parnassus. Ancient sources present him as a son of Lycorus, the founder of Hyampolis and the father of Celaeno, mother of the Delphus who gave his name to Delphi.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hyamus was a son of Lycorus, who was himself born of the union between Apollo and the nymph Corycia. After the Great Deluge, Hyamus became king of the people who lived around Mount Parnassus, where he founded the city of Hyampolis, also known as Hyia.

Hyamus married Melantheia, a daughter of Deucalion, and had at least two daughters, Celaeno and Melanis. Pausanias relates that Celaeno bore Delphus to Apollo, and that the city of Delphi took its name from him. A scholiast adds further details about Hyamus's reign after the flood.

Modern scholars have speculated that Hyamus's mother may have been Evadne, though no ancient source states this.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and an ancient scholium, which place Hyamus near Delphi and Parnassus.

Trivia

A name that passes to a place. Tradition credits Delphus, Hyamus's grandson through his daughter, with giving his name to the city of Delphi.
A city bearing his name. Hyamus founded the city of Hyampolis, also called Hyia, on the slopes of Mount Parnassus.
