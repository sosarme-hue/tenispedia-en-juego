Hindu goddess, consort of Rama

Sita

Sita is a Hindu goddess, the eternal consort of Rama and the female protagonist of the Ramayana. Regarded as an avatar of Lakshmi, she is seen as the model of marital virtue for Hindu women.

The myth

According to tradition, King Janaka of Mithila found the infant while ploughing a furrow, so she was regarded as a daughter of Bhumi, the earth goddess. Her name derives from the Sanskrit word sita, meaning 'furrow'. Raised at the court of Mithila alongside her sister Urmila, she was won in a svayamvara by Rama, prince of Ayodhya, who strung and broke the bow of Shiva.

After the wedding, Rama was exiled and the couple lived in the Dandaka forest with Lakshmana. There the Rakshasa king Ravana of Lanka abducted Sita, who stayed captive for a year in the Ashoka grove without yielding to him. Rama rescued her after defeating Ravana in battle.

In several versions of the epic, Rama asked Sita to prove her purity by walking through fire, and she emerged unharmed. Later, doubts among his subjects led Rama to send her, pregnant, into the forest. She sheltered at the ashram of the sage Valmiki, where she gave birth to twins, Kusha and Lava. In the end, according to the tale, the earth opened and received her back.

Worship and religion

Sita is worshipped as a goddess across Hinduism, especially in Vaishnava traditions. Vaishnavism holds that she and Rama live eternally with their devotees in Ramaloka, a spiritual realm beyond this universe. Her birthday is marked each year on Sita Navami. Local traditions locate her birthplace at Sitamarhi in Bihar, where the Sita Kund pilgrimage site draws visitors, and at Janakpur in Nepal. Diwali, the festival of lights, is also associated with the couple.

Legacy

Her story has spread through Southeast Asia, taking local forms such as Nang Sida in Thailand, Siti Dewi in Malaysia, Neang Seda in Cambodia, Nang Sanda in Laos and Putri Gandingan among the Tagalog people of the Philippines. She remains a model of wifely devotion.

Ancient sources: The Valmiki Ramayana, the Ramopakhyana of the Mahabharata, the Rigveda, which names an earth goddess Sita who blesses the crops, and the Harivamsa.

Trivia

Earth goddess. The Rigveda mentions an ancient goddess Sita, associated with fertile fields and good harvests. The Sita of the Ramayana may have taken her name from this earlier figure.
An earlier life. Some versions present her as a reincarnation of Vedavati, a woman who immolated herself to escape Ravana and vowed to return to bring about his downfall.
