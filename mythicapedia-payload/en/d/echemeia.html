Coan nymph of Greek mythology

Echemeia

Echemeia is a nymph from the island of Kos who, in Greek tradition, angered the goddess Artemis. Her story survives almost only in a account by the Roman mythographer Hyginus.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Echemeia stopped honouring Artemis, and the goddess punished her by shooting her with an arrow. Persephone, queen of the dead, witnessed the scene, snatched her away alive and brought her to the Underworld. Her husband, Merops, mourned her loss so deeply that Hera took pity on him, changed him into an eagle and then placed him among the stars as the constellation Aquila.

The story goes no further. One possible reading is that Echemeia had once belonged to the retinue of Artemis and that breaking her vows by marrying Merops explained the punishment. Her tale resembles those of Taygete and of Maera, wife of Tegeates.

Ancient sources: Her only known tale survives in the work of the Roman mythographer Hyginus, who presents her as a Coan nymph punished by Artemis.

Trivia

A constellation with a bird's name. Merops, turned into an eagle by Hera, was carried to the sky and became the constellation Aquila.
A nearly lost story. Echemeia's tale appears in only one author, which makes it one of the least documented myths of the Greek tradition.
