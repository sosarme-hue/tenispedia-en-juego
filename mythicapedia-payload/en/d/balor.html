Fomorian king with a deadly evil eye in Irish mythology

Balor

Balor is one of the great figures of Irish mythology: the leader of the Fomorians, a group of malevolent supernatural beings, whose destructive eye could only be opened with great effort. He is best known for his death at the hands of his grandson Lugh during the Second Battle of Mag Tuired.

The myth

In the medieval texts of the Mythological Cycle, Balor was the son of Dot, son of Néit, though another list calls him the son of Buarainech. He fought alongside Indech mac Dé at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired against the Tuatha Dé Danann, and in it he killed the king Nuada Airgetlám. His evil eye, with a venomous power, needed four warriors to lift its lid, and Lugh struck it with a sling-stone that passed through his head and harmed the Fomorian army. Another version has Balor surviving the loss of his eye, chased by Lugh to Mizen Head, where his severed head, set on a rock, shattered it.

In folktales recorded in the nineteenth century, Balor lived on Tory Island. Fearing a prophecy that he would die at his grandson's hand, he locked his only daughter, Ethniu, in a tower. After Cian stole a magical milk cow from Balor, he entered the tower, fathered three sons with the girl, and one survived to be raised by a smith. As an adult, he killed Balor by thrusting a red-hot iron rod into the giant's eye while Balor boasted of his deeds.

Ancient sources: The Cath Maige Tuired (Battle of Mag Tuired) and the Lebor Gabála Érenn, medieval Irish texts, along with the Book of Leinster, which preserves a list of builders of forts.

Trivia

A poisonous eye. According to the Cath Maige Tuired, the power of Balor's eye came from the fumes of a magic potion brewed by his father's druids.
A lake from his head. In a version of the Tory Island tale, Balor's head, set on a rock, gives rise to a lake.
