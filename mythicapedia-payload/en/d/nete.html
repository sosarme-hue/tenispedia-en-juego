Muse of the lyre at Delphi

Nete

Nete is one of the three Muses of the lyre worshipped at Delphi alongside her sisters Hypate and Mese, within the Greek tradition. Her name refers to the lowest string, or note, of the lyre.

The myth

According to tradition, Nete embodies the lowest string or note of Apollo's lyre within the musical scale. Some accounts link her and her sisters to the original three Muses of song, Aoide, Melete and Mneme, while others treat them as a distinct trio.

Other versions present these Muses as daughters of Apollo under the alternative names Cephisso, Apollonis and Borysthenis.

In his Quaestiones Convivales, Plutarch gives them an astronomical role: the universe was harmonically divided into three parts, the fixed stars, the planets and everything beneath the moon, and Nete watched over the last of these.

Worship and religion

Nete was worshipped at Delphi together with Hypate and Mese, in the setting of the Temple of Apollo and its oracle.

Ancient sources: Plutarch mentions her in his Quaestiones Convivales.

Trivia

A name for a note. Her name was given to the lowest of the seven notes of the lyre, so the Muse lent her name to a specific sound.
Muses with a cosmic role. Plutarch gave them a part in the division of the cosmos, unusual for Muses, who were mainly linked to music and the arts.
