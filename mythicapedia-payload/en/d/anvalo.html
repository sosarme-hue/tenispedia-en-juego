Gaulish god of Augustodunum

Anvallus

Anvallus was a Gaulish god known from several public inscriptions at Augustodunum, modern Autun. His name, analysed as 'without a sovereign', presents him as a lord superior to all others.

The myth

The surviving texts tell no stories about Anvallus. He is known almost only through his dedications, so no family, deeds or ending can be reconstructed with confidence.

Worship and religion

Two Latin altars were dedicated to him by the gutuatres, usually understood as priests, in fulfilment of vows. Both begin with the formula Aug(usto) sacr(um), 'consecrated to Augustus'. A thin bronze helmet in Greek style was found with them, left as a votive offering. A Gaulish inscription on a limestone cartouche mentions a seat dedicated to a sanctuary of the god, though its reading remains debated. The gutuatres have sometimes been seen as Romanized continuations of the druids. The altars were unearthed in 1900 on the site of Autun's railway station.

Ancient sources: Two Latin altars from Augustodunum and a Gaulish inscription on a limestone cartouche, both naming him.

Trivia

Bronze helmet. Among the objects found with the altars was a thin bronze helmet in Greek style, left as a votive offering.
A meaningful name. The name is analysed as an- (without) and ualos (sovereign), that is, 'without a sovereign', or superior to none.
