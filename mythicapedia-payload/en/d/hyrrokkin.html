giantess of Norse mythology

Hyrrokkin

Hyrrokkin is a jötunn, a giantess of Norse mythology, best known for launching the funeral ship of Baldr out to sea. Attested in medieval sources and possibly depicted on a Swedish runestone, she is one of the most striking figures of late pagan Scandinavia.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning, after Baldr's death the gods tried to launch Hringhorni, the largest of all ships, which was meant to serve as the god's funeral pyre. No one could move it. Then Hyrrokkin arrived from Jötunheimr, riding a giant wolf whose reins were vipers. Odin summoned four berserkers to hold the beast, but they could not control it without first knocking it unconscious.

With her seismic strength, the giantess rolled the ship down to the water on rollers, which caught fire and made the earth quake. Thor, angered that she had succeeded where he had failed, wanted to kill her with his hammer Mjöllnir, but the other gods persuaded him to spare her.

At the same funeral, Baldr's wife Nanna died of grief and was placed beside him on the pyre. Later, in the Skáldskaparmál, the skald Þorbjörn dísarskáld recounts that Thor did kill Hyrrokkin among other giants, so the tradition gives two different fates for her.

Legacy

Hyrrokkin appears in the 2020 video game Assassin's Creed Valhalla, in missions set in Jötunheimr, where her exploits are presented as visions of the past of the fictional precursor race, the Isu. Her name also appears in Norse poetic lists of troll women.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál in the Prose Edda, Úlfr Uggason's poem Húsdrápa, and the anonymous list of names Nafnaþulur.

Trivia

An uncertain name. The name Hyrrokkin has been translated as 'fire-withered' or 'fire-smoked', perhaps referring to a wrinkled or dark appearance.
A stone in Sweden. She may be depicted on one of the DR 284 stones of the Hunnestad Monument near Marsvinsholm, Sweden.
