Gallo-Roman goddess of the Moselle valley

Ancamna

Ancamna was a goddess worshipped mainly in the valley of the River Moselle, in the region of the Gaulish peoples who later came under Roman rule. She appears as the consort of Lenus Mars and of Mars Smertulitanus, which places her at an important point in the Gallo-Roman religion of the area.

The myth

Few stories survive about her origins or deeds, and ancient sources say very little about her. We know her mainly from inscriptions and offerings in which she appears beside her divine partner.

The scholars Jufer and Luginbühl note that, unlike other consorts of the Gaulish Mars such as Litavis or Nemetona, Ancamna does not seem to have been a warrior goddess. They therefore suggest she may have been linked to a spring, although this remains an unconfirmed hypothesis.

Worship and religion

At Trier, altars were set up in honour of Lenus Mars, Ancamna and the genii of various pagi of the Treveri, suggesting an officially organised cult in which the pair served as tribal protectors. At Möhn there was a sanctuary dedicated to Mars Smertulitanus and Ancamna, where small votive statuettes have been found.

Trivia

A hooded spirit. Among the few votive statuettes found at the Möhn sanctuary is one of a genius cucullatus, similar to those offered to the Xulsigiae at the Lenus Mars temple complex in Trier.
A non-warrior goddess. Unlike other consorts of the Gaulish Mars, Ancamna does not seem to have had a warlike role, according to the scholars Jufer and Luginbühl.
