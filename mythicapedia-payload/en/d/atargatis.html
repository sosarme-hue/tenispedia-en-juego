chief goddess of northern Syria

Atargatis

Atargatis, known to the Greeks as Derceto, was the chief goddess of northern Syria in classical antiquity. A goddess of fertility and protector of her city and people, she is usually described as the wife of Hadad.

The myth

According to the stories, Derceto threw herself into a lake near Ashkelon and turned into a fish. For this reason she was pictured with a woman's face and a fish's body, an image that Diodorus of Sicily, drawing on Ctesias, places at Ashkelon. Lucian, in the second century, describes her as fully human at Hierapolis instead.

Doves and fish were sacred to her. Doves stood for the love goddess, while fish stood for fertility and the life of the waters. Some traditions also link her to Semiramis as a daughter.

Worship and religion

Her chief sanctuary was at Hierapolis, modern Manbij, northeast of Aleppo. According to Lucian, the temple, nominally dedicated to Hera, showed her seated between two lions, holding a scepter and a spindle and wearing a mural crown. Her worship also reached Palmyra and Dura-Europos, where she was identified with Artemis Azzanathkona. A third-century Syriac text says that in Edessa men castrated themselves in her honor, until King Abgar, who had become a Christian, punished the practice by cutting off a hand.

Legacy

Michael Rostovtzeff called her the great mistress of the North Syrian lands. A fish-bodied figure on the tetradrachms of Demetrius III is identified with her, and coins from Hierapolis and Palmyra show her with lions. Nabatean reliefs from Khirbet et-Tannur in Jordan show the goddess with a pair of fish above her head.

Ancient sources: Diodorus of Sicily, drawing on Ctesias; Lucian of Samosata; and a third-century Syriac text mention her.

Trivia

A goddess with two names. The Greeks knew her as Derceto, and at Hierapolis the locals used her Syrian name while the temple was nominally dedicated to Hera.
A fish-bodied figure on a coin. A coin of King Demetrius III shows a fish-bodied figure that scholars identify with the goddess.
