phantom island of magnetic rock at the North Pole

Rupes Nigra

The Rupes Nigra, or 'Black Cliff', was a phantom island of magnetic black rock believed to lie at the North Pole. It was invoked to explain why compasses point north wherever one stands.

The myth

According to the idea that circulated among cartographers, the island was made of black rock so strongly magnetized that it drew compass needles towards it from anywhere on Earth. Its size was given as about 33 French miles.

The notion came from a lost work called Inventio Fortunata, of uncertain authorship, known today only through references by other writers. Its influence spread to maps of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries.

Legacy

Gerardus Mercator drew it on his posthumous atlas of 1595, surrounded by four small continents in a turbulent ocean, and described it in an 1577 letter to John Dee. Jules Verne, in The Adventures of Captain Hatteras (1866), placed a different island at the North Pole, Queen Island, formed by a volcano.

Ancient sources: Inventio Fortunata, a lost work of uncertain authorship, known only through references by other writers.

Trivia

Letter to John Dee. Mercator described the island in a 1577 letter to the English mathematician John Dee.
An ocean with four continents. Mercator drew four small fantastical continents around the island, set in a turbulent ocean.
