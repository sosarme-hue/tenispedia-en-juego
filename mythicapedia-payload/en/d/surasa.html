Hindu goddess, mother of the uragas and the serpents

Surasā

Surasā is a Hindu goddess, daughter of Daksha and wife of the sage Kashyapa, regarded as the mother of the uragas, a class of primordial serpentine reptiles. She is best known for an episode in the Ramayana, in which the gods ask her to test Hanuman on his way to Lanka.

The myth

In the Ramayana, Surasā is one of the twelve daughters of Daksha married to the sage Kashyapa. She bore the uragas, while her co-wife and sister Kadru gave birth to the nagas. Vasuki, Takshaka, Airavata and other sons of Surasā are said to live in Bhogavati. The Mahabharata instead tells that she was born from the wrath of Krodhavasha, another wife of Kashyapa. The Puranas offer variants: the Vishnu Purana credits her with a thousand multi-headed reptiles able to fly, while the Matsya Purana makes her the mother of all quadrupeds except cows. The Bhagavata Purana calls her the mother of rakshasas.

The best-known episode follows Hanuman as he flies over the ocean in search of Sita, kidnapped in Lanka. The gods, gandharvas and sages ask Surasā to take the form of a fearsome rakshasi and test him. She blocks his path and declares him her food. He asks her to let him go, promising to return and enter her mouth. Surasā replies that he may pass only through her mouth, by virtue of a boon. Hanuman grows, and she widens her jaws to a hundred yojanas. At that moment he shrinks to the size of a thumb, slips into her mouth and leaves before she can close it. Impressed by his wit and courage, Surasā resumes her true form and blesses him.

Legacy

The Surasā episode is one of the most cited passages of the Sundara Kanda and its later retellings. Scholars often read it as a test of Hanuman's strength, intelligence and celibacy. One interpretation sees Surasā as sattvika maya, the purest illusion, which must be tamed yet still respected.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Ramayana, especially the Sundara Kanda, and in the Mahabharata, the Matsya, Vishnu and Bhagavata Puranas, and the Devi Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A serpent's mouth. In one variant, Hanuman enters Surasā's mouth and leaves through her ear.
Three women on the road. Surasā is one of three women Hanuman meets on his way to Lanka, along with Simhika and Lankini.
