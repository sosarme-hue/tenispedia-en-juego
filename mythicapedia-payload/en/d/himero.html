Greek god of erotic desire

Himeros

Himeros is the Greek personification of erotic desire and lust, a winged young god who belongs to Aphrodite's retinue. He is counted among the Erotes, alongside Eros, Anteros and Pothos.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Eros and Himeros were present when Aphrodite rose from the sea foam, and they escorted the goddess as she joined the assembly of the gods. Later in the same poem, Himeros is said to dwell on Mount Olympus, a neighbour of the Muses and the Charites, where life is spent in feasting and joyful dancing.

According to the tradition, Aphrodite brought Himeros and Philotes, affection, into the world, and with them the impulse that leads to lovers' encounters. Their words of love and persuasion reached the minds and hearts of gods and mortals alike. Ancient texts do not specify who his parents were, though Aphrodite is regarded as the mother of loves in general.

Himeros is closely linked to Pothos, longing. Plato, in the Cratylus, distinguishes the two: himeros arises when the desired object is present and can be satisfied, while pothos arises from what is absent or out of reach and therefore brings suffering.

Legacy

Scopas is said to have carved statues of Eros, Himeros and Pothos, gods of love, desire and longing. Although these figures inspired artistic and poetic works, they had no myths or cults of their own.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the main ancient source that mentions him, and he also appears in the works of Plato and Philostratus.

Trivia

Longing for food. The word himeros can also refer to a desire for food, such as the craving a laborer feels after a long day at work.
No stories of his own. Although he inspired art and poetry, Himeros had no myths or cult of his own.
