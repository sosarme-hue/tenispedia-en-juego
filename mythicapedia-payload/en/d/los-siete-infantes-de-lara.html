Castilian epic legend of family feud and revenge

The Seven Lara Princes

A medieval Castilian legend about the seven sons of Gonzalo Gustioz, lord of Salas, and Doña Sancha, who are killed in an ambush plotted by their uncle Ruy Velázquez. It is one of the best-known revenge stories in Castilian epic tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition, the rivalry between the House of Lara and Ruy Velázquez begins at a wedding. During a jousting game with bohordos, Alvar Sánchez, a cousin of Doña Lambra, mocks the infantes, and Gonzalo González, the youngest of them, kills him with a punch. Doña Lambra, Ruy Velázquez's wife, seeks revenge first through the humiliation of the blood-stained cucumber and then through a letter sent to Almanzor ordering the death of its bearer. Gonzalo Gustioz is held prisoner in Córdoba, and from his union with a sister of Almanzor, Mudarra is born.

At the battle of Almenar, Ruy Velázquez betrays his nephews, who fall after an unequal fight. Their severed heads are sent to Córdoba, and Mudarra, now grown, returns to Castile and avenges his brothers by killing Ruy Velázquez.

Legacy

The story inspired the romancero and later literature. Ramón Menéndez Pidal considered it, alongside the Cantar de mio Cid, one of the earliest examples of Spanish epic, and its parallels with the Thidrekssaga and Germanic epic have been studied in detail.

Ancient sources: The Estoria de España compiled under Sancho IV of Castile before 1289 (Primera Crónica General), and the Crónica de 1344 (Segunda Crónica General).

Trivia

A blood-filled cucumber. The insult ordered by Doña Lambra is to throw a cucumber filled with blood at Gonzalo González. Some modern scholars read a sexual allusion in this scene.
The broken ring. Gonzalo Gustioz breaks a ring in two and leaves one half for the child he may have. Years later, when the two halves are joined, Mudarra proves his identity.
