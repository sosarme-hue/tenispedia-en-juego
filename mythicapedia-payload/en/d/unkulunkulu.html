supreme creator deity of Zulu mythology

Unkulunkulu

Unkulunkulu is, in Zulu mythology, the primordial ancestor and supreme creator of humankind. Adapted by Christian missionaries to the idea of a creator God, the figure remains central to the religious life and identity of the Zulu, Ndebele and Swati peoples.

The myth

According to the Zulu creation account, Unkulunkulu grew inside a reed in Uhlanga, the mythical swamp from which humanity emerged. When he became heavy enough, he fell to the earth. From there he emerged from the void, created the first man from grass, and later made humans, livestock and everything else.

When death came into the world, the tradition tells that Unkulunkulu sent a chameleon to announce that people would be immortal. The messenger stopped to eat beside a bush and was delayed. Disappointed by the lack of gratitude, Unkulunkulu sent a lizard with the opposite message: humans would be mortal. The lizard did not stop and arrived in time, fixing human fate forever.

Still, he is regarded as a benevolent god. As compensation for death, he gave marriage, which allows Zulu warriors to have children, along with medicines, fire for cooking and a dwelling for the dead.

Worship and religion

Before Christianity, the name was a general term for a revered ancestor, and each lineage had its own. Veneration of ancestors was the framework of his worship rather than a cult of a supreme deity.

Legacy

With the arrival of Christian missions in the nineteenth century, the name came to designate the creator God, although many missionaries first rejected it because of its link to ancestor worship. Bible translations gave him a masculine gender that the original concept did not have.

Ancient sources: The available sources do not cite ancient Zulu texts; the account was transmitted orally.
