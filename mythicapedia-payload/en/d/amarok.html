giant wolf of Inuit mythology

Amarok

The amarok is a gigantic, solitary wolf from Inuit mythology that, according to tradition, stalks and devours anyone foolish enough to hunt alone at night. It matters because it reveals the close bond between the Inuit and the wolf, which they regard as a being with its own spirit.

The myth

According to the accounts gathered in the nineteenth century by the Danish geologist Hinrich Johannes Rink, the amarok differs from ordinary wolves because it hunts alone, while they hunt in packs. It appears in several legends with supernatural traits.

In one, a physically stunted orphan boy, humiliated by his people, calls upon the lord of strength. An amarok appears, throws him to the ground with its tail, and causes several small bones to fall from his body, which it explains had prevented his growth. It tells him to return daily to train with it. After several days of wrestling, the boy becomes strong enough to overcome three large bears and wins the esteem of his village.

In another tale, a man mourning a relative hears that an amarok is nearby and goes out to hunt it with a companion. They find only its pups, and the mourner kills them. Hiding in a cave, they watch the mother return carrying a reindeer. Finding her young gone, she drags a human-like form out of a lake, and the mourner collapses dead, for the amarok, from which nothing is hidden, has taken his soul.

Legacy

The name amarok, also spelled amaroq, amagok or amaguk, remains in use in Inuit languages. In Inuinnaqtun it is the standard word for wolf, and in the Canadian Arctic islands it refers to the white wolf. It is sometimes compared to the waheela, a creature from cryptozoology.

Trivia

A wolf that hunts alone. Unlike wolves, which hunt in packs, the amarok is described as a solitary hunter that attacks anyone who goes out alone at night.
A word for wolf. In Inuinnaqtun, an Inuit language, amarok is the everyday word for wolf, not only the name of the legendary creature.
