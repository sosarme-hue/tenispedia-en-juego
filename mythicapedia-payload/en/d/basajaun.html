lord of the forest in Basque mythology

Basajaun

The basajaun is a creature of Basque mythology, the lord of the forest: a huge, hairy figure with a human shape who protects shepherds and their flocks. His female counterpart is the basandere, and his figure also appears in Aragonese folklore.

The myth

According to the stories, the basajaun lives in the most remote woods and mountains, such as those of Gorbea, Ataun or the Irati forest. He is very tall and covered in hair, and one of his feet is shaped like a tree stump, leaving circular footprints.

He watches over the flocks. When a storm or a pack of wolves approaches, he howls and whistles in the mountains to warn the shepherds, and the sheep shake their bells to signal his presence. In return, he receives a piece of bread left out for him at night.

He is not always kind. In some tales he is a terrifying man of the forest, with immense strength and speed greater than a stag's, and it is best not to meet him. In others he is the first farmer and blacksmith, keeper of secrets such as growing wheat, making the saw and welding, which humans cunningly took from him, often through the civilizing trickster Martin Txiki. Together with Tartalo and the jentilak, he forms the group of mountain giants of Basque tradition.

Worship and religion

No temples or festivals dedicated to him are described. His relationship with shepherds was expressed mainly through offerings, chiefly the piece of bread left for him every night.

Legacy

In Aragonese folklore he appears under names such as basajarau or bosnerau. Some anthropologists link him to a folk memory of contact with Neanderthals, and the Basque landscape keeps traces of him in place names such as Basajaundegi.

Trivia

A one-eyed being. In some tales the basajaun has a single eye in the middle of his forehead, a trait also attributed to Tartalo and often confused with him.
Dolmens named after giants. In Ataun, mountain dolmens are called jentiletxe, «house of the jentil», and in Azpeitia a geological formation is named Basajaundegi, «residence of the basajaun».
