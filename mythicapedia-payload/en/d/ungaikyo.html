mirror monster (yōkai) of Japanese folklore

Ungaikyō

The ungaikyō is a Japanese yōkai, a monster shaped like a mirror that appears in folklore and fiction. Edo period artist Toriyama Sekien made it known in his 1784 catalogue Gazu hyakki tsurezure bukuro.

The myth

In Chinese tradition, the shōmakyō mirror revealed the true identity of demons. In a Japanese adaptation of the Ming novel Fengshen yanyi, it exposed the consort Daji as a nine-tailed fox. Sekien took this idea and drew a mirror with a mocking face and its tongue out. Modern books describe it as a tsukumogami, an old object possessed by a yōkai. Some versions say the mirror's face reflects the yōkai inside it, which can manipulate reflections or trap spirits.

Legacy

In the 1968 Daiei film Yokai Monsters: Spook Warfare, it takes the form of a tanuki with a belly that works as a television screen. Many later children's books draw it the same way.

Trivia

A tanuki on screen. In the 1968 film the ungaikyō can inhale and then puff out its belly to project scenes from different places. Its tanuki look later spread through children's books.
A lunar ritual. According to a legend recorded by Shigeru Mizuki, on the fifteenth night of the eighth lunar month, tracing a yōkai's image on the mirror with water collected under the moon traps that yōkai inside it.
