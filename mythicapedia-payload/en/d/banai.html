Hindu goddess, second wife of Khandoba

Banai

Banai is a Hindu goddess venerated in the Deccan, mainly in Maharashtra and Karnataka, and the second wife of Khandoba, a form of Shiva. She is the protector of the Dhangar shepherd community and their herds.

The myth

According to oral tradition, Banai was a girl of celestial origin whom a Dhangar found in a golden box in the forest. Her adoptive father Yamu, chief of the shepherds, owned vast flocks; a virgin ewe nursed her, and a three-storey house appeared for her. She grew up a wealthy shepherdess and oversaw twelve Dhangar settlements.

One day Khandoba lost to his first wife Mhalsa in a game of saripat and, in a dream, fell in love with Banai. After a hunt, he married her in a non-ritual union and stayed with her for twelve years. To win her he disguised himself as an old leper and worked as a servant to Yamu, completing tasks with his magical bhandara. The tales contrast the two wives: Mhalsa stands for culture and purity, Banai for nature and desire.

Worship and religion

Banai has no independent cult, but she is worshipped as Khandoba's consort in most of his temples, especially at Jejuri. Bards sing her songs at all-night vigils called jagrans.

Legacy

Her story appears in many Dhangar folk songs and was mentioned by the Varkari poet Sheikh Muhammad in the sixteenth century. The scholar Günther-Dietz Sontheimer compared her tale with that of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala in the Mahabharata.
