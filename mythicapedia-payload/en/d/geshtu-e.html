minor god of intelligence in Sumerian and Akkadian mythology

Geshtu-E (Ilawela)

Geshtu-E is a minor god of intelligence in Sumerian and Akkadian mythology. He matters because of the role that the story of the creation of mankind in the Atra-Hasis epic gives him.

The myth

In the Atra-Hasis epic, the great gods sacrificed Geshtu-E. His blood was mixed with clay, and mankind was shaped from that mixture, so the tradition links this god's intelligence to the origin of human beings.

The story does not describe his family or his deeds in detail, and the sacrifice is the moment that defines his place in the myth. His figure appears mainly through this act rather than through an extended mythical life, as is common with minor gods in these narratives.

Ancient sources: The Atra-Hasis epic, from the ancient Babylonian tradition, is the text that recounts his sacrifice and the creation of mankind.

Trivia

Varied names. His name has been transcribed in several ways, such as Geshtu-(E), Geshtu, Gestu or We-ila. Today the name Ilawela is preferred.
