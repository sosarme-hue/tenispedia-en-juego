river god of the Aoos in Greek mythology

Eanthes

Eanthes, also called Eas, is the god of the Aoos river in Epirus. He was one of the Oceanids, the river gods born to Oceanus and Tethys, and he is best known from Ovid, who shows him comforting his brother Peneus.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Eanthes belonged to the large family of river gods, children of Oceanus and Tethys, who gave their names and life to freshwater streams. His river, the Aoos, rises in the mountains of Epirus and flows into the Adriatic Sea; today its course lies in Albania, where it is known as the Vjosa.

The episode that remembers him comes from Ovid. When Daphne, daughter of Peneus, was turned into a laurel to escape Apollo's pursuit, Eanthes went to console his brother. It is an intimate scene, more fitting for a relative than a hero, and it is the only story that survives about him.

Ancient sources: Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, in the story of Daphne and Apollo.

Trivia

Two names. He appears as Eante or Eas in Spanish and as Aeas in Latin. Both forms refer to the same river god.
A rarely named god. He barely appears in ancient texts, and nearly all his fame depends on the story of Daphne.
