Japanese folk creature of the Izumo tradition

Hare of Inaba

The White Hare of Inaba is a figure of Japanese folklore from the Izumo tradition of myths. Its story, recorded in the Kojiki, explains how the god Ōnamuchi-no-kami, later known as Ōkuninushi, won the favour of his rivals and of a princess through his kindness.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, a hare wanted to cross the sea from the Island of Oki to Cape Keta. To do so, it tricked some sharks into lining up so it could count them, claiming the contest was about whose clan was bigger, and hopped across their backs. At the end it boasted of the deception, and the last shark tore off its fur.

On their way to woo Princess Yakami of Inaba, Ōnamuchi and his eighty brothers came upon the flayed hare. The brothers advised it to wash in the sea and dry in the wind, which only made its pain worse. Ōnamuchi instead told it to bathe in fresh river water and roll in cattail pollen. The hare recovered, revealed its divine nature and promised that Ōnamuchi would marry the princess.

Another tradition, the Ise ga Naru version, tells how the goddess Amaterasu was led by a white hare to a mountain plateau, where the hare vanished.

Worship and religion

At Hakuto Jinja in Tottori, the White Hare is revered.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, dating from the early 8th century, which records the main version of the tale.
