Maya god of war and human sacrifice

Buluc-Chabtan

Buluc-Chabtan, sometimes called “God F”, was the Maya god of violent death, war and human sacrifice. His image reflects how central ritual violence and warfare were to life in Maya cities.

The myth

In Maya codices he can be recognised by a black arc around the eye that runs down the cheek. He often appears alongside Ah Puch, god of death, setting houses on fire with a torch and piercing men with his spear.

His companions included Ka-Ku-Pakat, who scouted enemy positions before battle, and Pakok-Exchun-Kak, linked to the cardinal points, unleashed fury and divine intervention in combat.

Worship and religion

He was associated with the number eleven, sometimes shown as a glyph above his head, and with the day manik of the Maya calendar. His cult is connected with the sacrifice of nobles and war captives.

Ancient sources: He appears in Maya codices, where he is identified by his facial marking and in scenes beside Ah Puch.

Trivia

An unmistakable mark. A black arc around the eye, running down to the cheek, makes him recognisable in Maya codices.
An associated number. The number eleven was often linked to him and was sometimes written as a glyph above his head.
