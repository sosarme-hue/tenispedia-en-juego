mythical king of Libya and suitor of Dido

Hiarbas (Iarbas)

Hiarbas is a mythical king of North Africa, son of the god Ammon and a Garamantian nymph. Roman authors such as Virgil and Ovid recorded him, and his figure may have been inspired by a real king of Numidia.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hiarbas led an army across the Libyan desert, but his men began to die of thirst. He begged his father Ammon for help, and the god sent him a ram. Following the animal, they reached a place where, as it struck the ground with its hooves, water sprang up. From then on the Libyans credited the ram to Ammon. Some accounts place him at the start of the genealogy of the kings of Numidia and make him ruler of Gaetulia.

In Virgil's Aeneid he is a suitor of the Carthaginian queen Dido. He scorns Carthage as a pitifully small city and offers her a marriage that would make it glorious, but she rejects him, and Hiarbas vanishes from the story. Ovid, in the Heroides, says Dido's people tried to force the marriage, and in the Fasti he describes how, after the queen's suicide, Hiarbas and the Numidians seized her land and palace.

Worship and religion

According to Virgil, Hiarbas raised hundreds of temples and altars to Jupiter in his realms and kept an eternal sacred fire burning. The myth links the ram cult of Ammon with the Libyans, and Ammon's famous oracle at the oasis of Siwa gives that worship a real setting.

Legacy

Christopher Marlowe makes him a character in his play Dido, Queen of Carthage, and Dante mentions him in the Purgatorio as an owner of lands south of Italy.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, Virgil's Aeneid, Ovid's Heroides and Fasti, and Silius Italicus's Punica.

Trivia

The first man. A Greek poetic fragment preserved in the Refutation of All Heresies says the Libyans believed the first man to rise from the earth was called Tarbas. Many scholars identify him with Hiarbas, though others prefer Garamas.
A real city. Later geographical studies identified Vescera, the capital of Gaetulia and home of Hiarbas, with modern Biskra in Algeria.
