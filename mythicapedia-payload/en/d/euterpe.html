Greek muse of music and lyric poetry

Euterpe

Euterpe is one of the nine Muses of Greek mythology, associated with music and, in late Classical times, with lyric poetry. Daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, she was invoked by poets and musicians seeking inspiration.

The myth

According to tradition, Euterpe was a daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, the Titan goddess of memory, and she shared with her sisters the task of entertaining their father and the other Olympian gods with song and dance. Her name is often rendered as "giver of delight", and ancient poets praised her as a bringer of pleasure.

She is credited with inventing the aulos, a double flute, although most mythographers give that honour to Athena or to Marsyas. Some accounts add that she also invented other wind instruments, and she is usually shown holding a flute.

Accounts of her children differ. Pindar and other sources make Rhesus, the Thracian king who appears in Homer's Iliad, her son by the river god Strymon, while Homer calls him son of Eioneus. Rhesus was killed by Diomedes during the Trojan War.

Worship and religion

The Muses, Euterpe among them, were worshipped at Mount Helicon in Boeotia, a major cult centre, and on Mount Parnassus, where the Castalian spring drew poets and artists. Near Delphi, the sacred Cassotis spring served the Pythia and the priests of Apollo's oracle. Musicians often called on her for inspiration before composing.

Legacy

Her name remains in use in modern music and culture, and she is one of the muses who lend their names to the tradition of poetic inspiration. She appears in Roman mosaics, sculptures and paintings, and in the sculpture on the grave of Frédéric Chopin at Père Lachaise Cemetery in Paris.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions her through her son Rhesus in the Iliad, and Pindar and the author of the Bibliotheca also record the account.

In art

Trivia

A double flute. She is linked to the aulos, a double flute widely used in ancient Greece at banquets, in theatre and at religious ceremonies.
A sacred spring at Delphi. The Cassotis spring near Delphi was sacred to the Muses, and its waters were reserved for the Pythia and Apollo's priests.
