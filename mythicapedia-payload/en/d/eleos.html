Greek goddess of pity and compassion

Eleos

Eleos was, in Greek mythology, the personification of pity and compassion. Her cult, documented mainly in Athens, makes her one of the most distinctive divinities of Greek religion.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Eleos was the daughter of Erebus and Nox, and her opposite was Anaideia, cruelty. In the stories, her role centres on protecting suppliants: the children of Heracles, hunted by Eurystheus, and Adrastus, after the defeat of the Seven against Thebes, sought refuge at her altar in Athens and begged for mercy for the dead.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes an altar of Eleos in the Athenian agora and notes that only the Athenians honoured her among the Greeks. The shrine was modest, without statues or rituals, since the goddess was believed to dwell in human hearts. There, every suppliant was heard, however horrific their crime, and she was honoured by the cutting of hair and the removal of garments at the altar.

Ancient sources: Hyginus calls her Misericordia, daughter of Erebus and Nox; Pausanias describes her altar in the Athenian agora; Statius's Thebaid, from the 1st century, recalls her as Clementia.

Trivia

An altar without a statue. Eleos's altar in Athens had no image of the goddess, because she was believed to live in people's hearts.
Clemency in Latin. The Latin poet Statius names her Clementia and describes her altar, which the wretched made sacred.
