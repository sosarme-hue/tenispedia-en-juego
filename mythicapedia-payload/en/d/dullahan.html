headless rider of Irish folklore

Dullahan

The Dullahan is a creature of Irish folklore, a headless rider who usually appears on a black horse or driving a funeral coach. Its appearance is linked to imminent death, which makes it one of the most feared beings in Irish popular tradition.

The myth

According to popular storytelling, the Dullahan appears as a headless horseman, often on a black horse, or as the driver of a coach that emerges from graveyards. He carries his own severed head under his arm or in his hand. His eyes dart in every direction, he can see in the dark, and an everlasting grin fills his face. He is said to use a human spine as a whip.\n\nWhen the Dullahan halts at a house, someone there is about to die. He calls out the person's name, and the victim drops dead at once. No door or lock can keep him out, since they all open as he approaches. Anyone who dares to watch him may be splashed with a basin of blood or have his eyes lashed. He is said to fear gold, and a single golden pin is enough to drive him away.\n\nAnother tradition links him to the ancient Celtic god Crom Dubh, whose worship supposedly demanded human sacrifice, and claims that this god returned to the world through the souls of the living. Some modern scholars doubt that the Dullahan was part of the original Irish oral tradition, since he is barely attested in native sources.

Legacy

He appears in modern popular culture, in works such as the anime Monster Musume, Lost in Two Worlds and the novel Reino de las sombras.
