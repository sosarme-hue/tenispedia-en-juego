Electra

Electra is one of the Pleiades, the seven daughters of the Titan Atlas and Pleione in Greek mythology. Her story is tied to the Palladium, the sacred statue that protected Troy, and to the origins of the Trojan royal house.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Electra bore Dardanus to Zeus on the island of Samothrace. Dardanus left the island and founded the city of Dardanus in the Troad, which makes Electra the ancestor of the Trojan royal line. With Zeus she also had Iasion, also called Eetion, who was struck down by a thunderbolt after an outrage against Demeter or her statue. The logographer Hellanicus makes her the mother of Harmonia, wife of Cadmus, though Harmonia is usually said to be the daughter of Ares and Aphrodite. Nonnus credits her with Emathion, who succeeded Dardanus as king of Samothrace. An Italian tradition makes her the wife of the Etruscan king Corythus.

The best-known episode concerns the Palladium. Seeking protection from Zeus, Electra took refuge beside the statue of Pallas Athena. In his anger Zeus hurled the image from heaven, and it fell near Troy, where Ilus found it and built a temple to keep it. In another version, Electra herself gave the statue to Dardanus as a safeguard for Troy. When the city fell, Electra could not bear to watch, so she hid her eyes or turned away, which is why one of the seven stars of the group is barely visible.

In another story, the Pleiades were fleeing the hunter Orion, who meant to assault them, and Zeus set them among the stars. In a late version, Electra, grieving for Troy, left her sisters and became a comet with long hair.

Worship and religion

Electra appears in the worship of the Great Mother of the gods, Rhea or Cybele. According to the tradition, at Harmonia's wedding to Cadmus she taught her the rites of this goddess, invoked with cymbals and drums. No cult of Electra herself is recorded.

Legacy

Electra gave her name to the star of the same name in the Pleiades nebula.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus, Hellanicus and Nonnus of Panopolis.

Trivia

The long-haired star. In a late version, Electra let down her hair in mourning, left her sisters and became a comet.
A son born on Samothrace. According to Apollodorus, Electra bore Dardanus to Zeus on the island of Samothrace, from where her son set out to found Dardania.
