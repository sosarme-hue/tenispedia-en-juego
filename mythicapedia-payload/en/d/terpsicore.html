Greek muse of dance

Terpsichore

Terpsichore is one of the nine Muses of Greek mythology, linked to dance, choral song and light poetry. Her name gave English the word "terpsichorean", meaning relating to dance, and her figure has inspired music and theatre for centuries.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Zeus lay with the Titan Mnemosyne for nine nights in a row at Pieria, and from their union the nine Muses were born. Terpsichore was credited with inventing the flute, mastering the lyre and founding a kind of education. In art she appears as a slender young woman with a cheerful air and a light manner, crowned with garlands of flowers and holding a lyre, often seated, accompanying the dancers' choruses with her music.

Accounts of her children vary from author to author. Apollonius of Rhodes makes her the mother of the Sirens by the river god Achelous. The Etymologicum Magnum names her as the mother of the Thracian king Biston by Ares, and the Byzantine scholar Eustathius makes her the mother of the Thracian king Rhesus by the river god Strymon. Other traditions give Apollo the fathering of Linus, the lament, and Hymenaeus, the wedding song, though the mother of each changes from version to version.

Like the other Muses, Terpsichore has no long story of her own. Her role is to accompany poetry, song and dance, and her name appears mostly in the family lists of other beings.

Legacy

Her name entered the language as the adjective "terpsichorean". Michael Praetorius published a large collection of dance tunes in 1612 under the title Terpsichore, and François Couperin included her name in his "Second Ordre" from the Pièces de clavecin. Handel wrote a prologue titled Terpsicore in 1734 for a later version of his opera Il pastor fido. The eighteenth-century French dancer and courtesan Marie-Madeleine Guimard called the private theatre in her Paris palace the Temple of Terpsichore, in 1766. A British 32-gun frigate under Captain Bowen took part in the Battle of Santa Cruz de Tenerife in 1797 under the same name. Sammy Cahn mentioned her in the lyrics of the 1959 song "Come Dance with Me", written for Frank Sinatra.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony counts her among the nine Muses, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, and Apollonius of Rhodes names her as the mother of the Sirens.

Trivia

A book of Herodotus. When later editors divided Herodotus's Histories into nine books, each was named after a Muse. The fifth book bears the name Terpsichore.
A temple for dance. The dancer Marie-Madeleine Guimard gave her private theatre the name Temple of Terpsichore.
