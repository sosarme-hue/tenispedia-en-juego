Yoruba orisha of the whirlwind, forest and herbs

Ajá

Ajá is a minor orisha in Yoruba religion, mistress of the whirlwind, the forest and the animals that live in it. She is also a healer who knows the secrets of herbs.

The myth

According to tradition, Ajá was the wife of Olokun, orisha of the ocean, and the mother of Yemayá. She is described as proud and quarrelsome, always ready to argue.

Her best-known story concerns those who go astray. Someone who wants to learn the secrets of herbs wanders into the forest until lost. Ajá then appears as a whirlwind and, if the seeker shows no fear, carries them off to her realm to teach the healing power of plants. The journey is said to last between seven days and three months, and the chosen one passes through Ọrun, the land of the dead, before returning as a powerful healer, an oníṣègùn.

Worship and religion

Her ritual presence survives mainly in the ceremonial broom, a bundle of palm or corojo rods decorated with shells and used in rites. Her cult is less widely known today than those of Inle and Osain.

Legacy

In Yoruba practice her name designates the ritual broom, and it is also applied to the broom of Babalú Ayé (Saint Lazarus).

Trivia

A name meaning whirlwind. In Yoruba, Àjìjà means whirlwind, and the name is often shortened to Ààjà.
An apparition that does not frighten. Among earth spirits, imọlẹ, Ajá is considered one of the rarest, because she reveals herself to humans without intending to harm or scare them.
