vidyārāja in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism

Ucchuṣma

Ucchuṣma is a vidyārāja, a wrathful king of wisdom, in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. Of Indian origin, his figure developed mainly in China and Japan, where he was invoked for purification and protection from harm.

The myth

In the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, long ago Ucchuṣma was consumed by such intense desire that he became a mass of raging fire. A buddha called King of Emptiness taught him to contemplate the warmth and coolness of his body, and that inner fire turned into the fire of wisdom. From then on he was called Fire-Head, attained arhatship and, before Śākyamuni, described the method of penetration through heat.

The Ucchuṣma Vajrapāla Sūtra presents him as the vajra manifestation of Śākyamuni. As the Buddha was about to enter nirvana, the Brahma King Spiral Hair-Knot refused to attend. Gods sent to persuade him were trapped by defiled energy. Śākyamuni emitted light from his heart, and Ucchuṣma emerged, turning the impure forces into ordinary soil and subduing the Brahma King. For this reason he is also called the Filth-Eliminating Vajrapāla.

Worship and religion

In China he was venerated mainly by the Chan school and, from the late Tang period, gained his own cult as a protector deity. His statue is often found in the Mahavira Hall of some temples, and he is invoked in the Shuilu Fahui ceremony. In Fujian and Taiwan he is invoked in exorcism rites. In Japan several schools venerate him, and he is regarded as a guardian of the bathroom, where an effigy of him is often placed.

Legacy

In Japan he was associated with purification from impurities, particularly sexual diseases, and was believed able to change a female fetus into a male. He is still invoked in temples of the Shingon, Tendai, Zen, Jōdo-shū and Nichiren schools.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Śūraṅgama Sūtra and the Ucchuṣma Vajrapāla Sūtra, as well as the Ucchuṣma Vidyārāja Dhāraṇi.

Trivia

Guardian of the bathroom. In Japan he is regarded as a guardian of the bathroom, and his effigy is often placed there.
A changed name. In a thirteenth-century Chinese text his Chinese name was changed because of negative connotations of the earlier one.
