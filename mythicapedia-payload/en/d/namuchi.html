asura demon of Hindu mythology

Namuchi

Namuchi is an asura, a demon of Hindu mythology and a recurring foe of the god Indra. He appears in the Rigveda, and later texts such as the Mahabharata and the Puranas retell his clashes with Indra in differing ways.

The myth

According to the myth, Namuchi led a vast army of asuras against the gods. Indra could not wound him, so the demon hid in the rays of the Sun. Cornered, he demanded a pact: he could not be killed by day or night, by staff or bow, or by anything wet or dry. Indra agreed, yet Namuchi stripped him of his strength through surā liquor.

With the help of the Ashvins and Sarasvatī, Indra caught him at twilight on the seashore and suffocated him with sea foam, neither wet nor dry, before cutting off his head. Because Namuchi was a son of the sage Kashyapa and thus a Brahmin, Indra incurred the sin of brahmahatya: the severed head pursued him across the worlds until he purified himself at the sacred confluence of Arunasangama.

Ancient sources: Rigveda (10.73), Shatapatha Brahmana, Mahabharata, Padma Purana and other Puranas.

Trivia

A meaningful name. His name derives from the Sanskrit roots na (not) and much (to release, let go), a meaning considered symbolic of his character.
A place of pilgrimage. After Indra was purified at Arunasangama, the sacred river confluence came to be regarded as a pilgrimage site, according to the story.
