Rainbow Serpent

The Rainbow Serpent is one of the most widespread sacred beings in Aboriginal Australian tradition, where it appears as the creator of the landscape and the giver of water and life. It matters because it sits at the heart of the worldview known as the Dreaming.

The myth

According to the stories, the serpent belongs to the Dreaming, an age before humans when ancestral beings shaped the world. It rises from beneath the ground and winds across the land, raising ridges, mountains and gorges as it goes. Its presence is tied to water: it lives in deep, permanent waterholes and controls the most precious resource of the desert.

Versions vary widely between peoples. Some describe it as male, others as female or without a fixed gender, and in some stories it takes the form of a carpet snake with a mane or beard. In northern accounts it appears alongside the wondjina, heavenly beings in human form. When angered, it can bring floods or droughts, so its power is seen as both fertile and dangerous. Tradition holds that it still lives in waterholes, rain clouds and the rainbow, which is said to be its movement from one pool to another.

Worship and religion

Its figure is linked to rituals of fertility, water and sacred places, and it appears often in rock paintings and bark painting. Many ritual details are restricted to initiates in the communities that venerate it, and its veneration remains alive among Aboriginal peoples.

Legacy

The name was popularised in English by the anthropologist Alfred Radcliffe-Brown, who noticed the same figure under different names across Australian cultures. Today it gives its name to an annual festival of music, arts and culture in Victoria and remains a recurring motif in contemporary Aboriginal art.

Trivia

A rainbow on the move. According to some peoples, when a rainbow appears in the sky it is the serpent travelling from one waterhole to another.
Many names. The serpent goes by different names in dozens of Aboriginal languages, such as Yurlunggur, Wanampi or Ngalyod, and each people gives it its own traits.
