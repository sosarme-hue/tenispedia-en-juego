wife of King Arcesius and mother of Laertes in Greek myth

Chalcomedusa

In Greek myth, Chalcomedusa is the wife of King Arcesius of Ithaca and the mother of Laertes, who was in turn the father of Odysseus. She matters because she links Ithacan kingship with the hero of Homer's Odyssey.

The myth

According to an ancient genealogy preserved in a frequently cited passage, Arcesius, son of Zeus and Euryodia, married Chalcomedusa, and their son was Laertes. Laertes fathered Odysseus with Anticleia, and Odysseus fathered Telemachus with Penelope, making Chalcomedusa the grandmother of Odysseus.

The surviving texts say little about her: her lineage is unknown and no deeds of her own are recorded. Her role is mainly as a link in the royal family that ruled Ithaca, the starting point of Odysseus's line. Her name, according to modern linguistic analysis, may mean 'guardian of bronze', linking her to Bronze Age metalworking.

Trivia

Double-meaning name. Her name combines chalcos, meaning both 'copper' and 'bronze', with medousa, meaning 'guardian' or 'protectress'.
