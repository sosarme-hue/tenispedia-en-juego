Elamite protector god of the city of Susa

Inshushinak

Inshushinak was the protective god of Susa and one of the major deities of the ancient Elamite empire. His name means 'lord of Susa', and his worship shows how Elamite religion blended local traditions with Mesopotamian influences.

The myth

Texts say little about specific adventures of Inshushinak; his figure is defined mostly by his roles. He was regarded as the lord of the city and the leading god of the local pantheon, and Elamite kings invoked him in their names and titles. Atta-hushu, for instance, called himself the 'shepherd of Inshushinak'.

As a judge, he appeared in legal oaths alongside Ishmekarab, and he was believed to extend his authority over the land of the dead, where he was considered its lord. Two Mesopotamian deities, Lagamal and Ishmekarab, were regarded as his assistants. The Susa Funerary Texts, written in Akkadian, may contain instructions for the dead arriving in the underworld.

Worship and religion

His worship centred on Susa, where several temples were dedicated to him. At Choga Zanbil a ziggurat was built for his veneration. Outside Susa, references are much less common. Parties to agreements committed themselves by touching his kidinnu or kitin, a symbol of legal authority that may have been represented by a statue or an emblem.

Ancient sources: The Susa Funerary Texts, the treaty with Naram-Sin and the Elamite and Mesopotamian inscriptions that mention him.

Trivia

A Sumerian name. His name comes from Sumerian and means 'lord of Susa'. It is a loanword with a shortened form that is rarely found in that language.
Snake gods. Frans Wiggermann includes him, along with Ninazu and Ishtaran, among the so-called 'Transtigridian snake gods'.
