fertility and water goddess of the Umbri and Piceni

Cupra

Cupra was an Italic deity worshipped by the Umbri and the Piceni, one of the great mother goddesses of ancient Italy, linked to water, fertility and trade. Her cult spread across the central Apennines and left its name in towns such as Cupramontana and Cupra Marittima.

The myth

Ancient sources say little about her story, and most of what we know comes from inscriptions and archaeological remains. She appears as "Mother Cupra", a protective figure whom the Romans identified with Bona Dea and whom Greek writers compared with Hera.

According to Strabo, the first-century BCE Greek geographer, Cupra was equated with Hera, and her temple in Picene territory was said to have been built by the Etruscans. Her name has been linked to Latin cupere, "to desire", or to Aphrodite, though its etymology remains open.

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on places tied to trade and communication, as well as springs, lakes and water sources. Four bronze plates from the fourth century BCE at Colfiorito bear dedications to the Matres Plestinas, and a votive pit at Cupra Marittima held a thousand miniature objects. In imperial times Hadrian restored her temple at Grottammare.

Legacy

Her name survives in the place names of several towns in the Marche, such as Cupramontana and Cupra Marittima.

Ancient sources: Silius Italicus mentions her altars, and Strabo compares her with Hera; Varro associates her with Bona Dea.

Trivia

A pit with a thousand objects. At Cupra Marittima excavators uncovered a votive pit cut into bedrock containing about a thousand objects, including incense burners and miniature vessels.
A plaque bearing Hadrian's name. A Roman plaque now in the church of San Martino records that the emperor Hadrian restored her temple in 127 CE.
