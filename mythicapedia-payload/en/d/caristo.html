eponymous hero of Carystus, on Euboea

Carystus

Carystus, in Greek Kárystos, is a hero of Greek mythology, son of the centaur Chiron and the nymph Chariclo, remembered as the eponymous founder of the Euboean town that bears his name.

The myth

Accounts present him as the son of Chiron and the nymph Chariclo, and brother of Hippe, Endeïs and Ocyrhoe. Tradition makes him the father of Zarex, though the genealogies vary from one ancient author to another.

Bacchylides names Aristaeus as a son of Carystus or of Chiron, among other possibilities. Tzetzes identifies Zarex with Carystus and says Zarex married Reo, daughter of Staphylus; for this reason some believe Anius was a son of Zarex and Reo in name, while his true father was Apollo. Another version makes Petraeus, son of Carystus, the father of Zarax. Phalaris adds that Zarax was later called Cafereus, and that Cafereus was colloquially known as Xylophagus.

Ancient sources: Bacchylides, Tzetzes and Phalaris record variant accounts of his descendants and of the lineage of Zarex and Zarax.

Trivia

A town named after him. The Euboean town of Carystus took its name from this hero, according to the ancient accounts.
A curious nickname. Cafereus, a descendant in some versions, was colloquially called Xylophagus, meaning wood-eater.
