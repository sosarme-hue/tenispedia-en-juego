Taoist god of culture and literature

Wenchang Wang

Wenchang Wang is a Taoist deity of Chinese mythology, the god of culture and literature. Scholars and students called on him for inspiration and help before imperial examinations.

The myth

According to tradition, Wenchang is most often identified with Zhang Yazi, a man from Zitong county in Sichuan, who died an honorable death in a rebellion against Emperor Fu Jian in 374. Accounts differ on his reincarnations: some credit him with seventy-three, others with seventeen.

He is portrayed as an upright, compassionate scholar. According to the Book of Emperor Zi Tong, as a child he cut flesh from his own thighs to feed his sick mother, and she was cured.

Worship and religion

People called on him before examinations, and many students still pray to him and make offerings. Offerings are chosen for their homophones: celery (qín, diligence) and garlic (suàn, calculation), while round fruits are avoided because they resemble zero. His birthday is marked on the third day of the second lunar month.

Legacy

A popular saying places him opposite Confucius: in the north, Confucius; in the south, Wenchang. He is represented by an asterism near the Big Dipper.

Ancient sources: The Book of Emperor Zi Tong and The Wenchang Emperor and the States He Stabilized, ancient texts that record his virtues and his time among mortals.

Trivia

Six stars. He is physically represented by a constellation of six stars near the Big Dipper, and each star has its own name.
A celestial companion. Wenchang is often shown with Kui Xing, a scholar who, according to legend, threw himself into the sea after being rejected for his appearance and rose to the heavens as a star.
