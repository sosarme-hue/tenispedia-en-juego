female demon of Greek and Byzantine folklore

Gello

Gello is a female demon or revenant of Greek folklore, feared for her link to the deaths of children and young women. Her figure changed over the centuries, from the ghost of a maiden to a demon that attacked women in childbirth in the Byzantine era.

The myth

According to tradition, Gello was a young woman who died unwed and returned from the dead as a ghost to harm the children of others. The myth is offered as an explanation of a proverb recorded by Zenobius, which places her on the island of Lesbos.

Sappho mentions her as early as the sixth century BC as the spirit of a dead girl who harms the living, showing that the fear of Gello is ancient. Later lexicons, such as that of Hesychius, describe her as an apparition that attacked virgins and newborns, and a scholiast identifies her with Mormo and Lamia.

Legacy

The name may derive from gallû, an ancient Mesopotamian demon, a theory advanced by Carl Frank and supported by Martin Litchfield West. The English word ghoul preserves an echo of it.

Ancient sources: Sappho, Hesychius of Alexandria, Zenobius and Tzetzes, among others.

Trivia

Protective amulets. Byzantine mothers used amulets to protect their newborns from Gello. The first-century Cyranides recommended hyena eyeballs in a purple pouch as protection.
A witch of the early modern age. In the 17th century, Leo Allatius noted that Greeks still believed witches were old crones who made pacts with the devil, an image borrowed from Western Europe.
