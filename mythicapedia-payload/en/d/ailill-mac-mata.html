legendary king of the Connachta in Irish mythology

Ailill mac Máta

Ailill mac Máta is the king of the Connachta and husband of Queen Medb in the Ulster Cycle, the body of Irish heroic tales. He ruled from Cruachan, today Rathcroghan in County Roscommon, and he is central to the great cattle raid recounted in the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

The myth

His name is a matronymic: his mother, Máta Muirisc, belonged to the Fir Ol nEchmacht of Connacht, and through her he claimed the throne. His father was Rus Ruad, king of the Laigin. According to the Táin, Medb chose him over other suitors because he alone was free of meanness, jealousy or fear. A later saga, Cath Boinde, tells another version: he was raised at Medb's court, became her lover and chief bodyguard, and won the throne in single combat against Medb's husband.

Together they had seven sons, all named Maine after a prophecy. The Táin describes the raid against the Ulaid to seize the bull Donn Cúailnge, meant to match the wealth of Medb and Ailill. During the march Ailill discovered Medb with Fergus mac Róich, but forgave her. After the battle the bull killed his rival Finnbhennach but died of his wounds. Ailill later fell by the hand of his blind brother Lugaid Dalléces, who threw a spear he had been tricked into throwing, and he was killed by Conall Cernach, at Medb's urging.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Táin Bó Cúailnge, the Cath Boinde and other tales of the Ulster Cycle.
