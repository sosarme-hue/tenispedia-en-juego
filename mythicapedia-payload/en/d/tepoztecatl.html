Aztec god of pulque and drunkenness

Tepoztécatl

Tepoztécatl is the Aztec god of pulque, drunkenness and fertility, and one of the Centzon Totochtin, the four hundred children of Patécatl and Mayáhuel. He was the patron deity of Tepoztlán, in present-day Morelos.

The myth

According to tradition, Patécatl and Mayáhuel invented pulque: she tapped the agave for its sap, and he found the roots that make it ferment. Tepoztécatl later perfected the drink on Chichinauhia mountain, which was renamed Popocatépetl after the foam the drink produces.

Some accounts place his origin in Tamoanchan, but it was the people of Tepoztlán who claimed him as their god. Bernardino de Sahagún ranks him tenth among the gods of drunkenness he lists.

Worship and religion

Worship centred on the people of Tepoztlán, and pilgrims from distant regions came to his sacred place on the mountain. He was linked to fertility rites and to the wind. The Codex Mendoza shows him holding a copper axe, the tepoztli.

Legacy

The archaeological site of Tepozteco, with a small pyramid on a platform overlooking the town, is named after him.

Ancient sources: The Codex Mendoza, and Bernardino de Sahagún's work, which lists him among the gods of drunkenness.

Trivia

A volcano named after a drink. Mount Chichinauhia was renamed Popocatépetl, after the foam that pulque produces.
A trade that endures. The copper axe he carries reflects the people of Tepoztlán, who were woodcutters, a trade they still practise.
