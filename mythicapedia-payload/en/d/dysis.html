Hour of sunset in Greek mythology

Dysis

Dysis is, in Greek mythology, the eleventh of the Horae and the keeper of the hour of sunset. Her figure shows how the Greeks divided the day into moments with their own character.

The myth

Dysis belongs to the group of twelve Horae, sisters who presided over the moments of the day. Alongside Auge, the first light, Anatole, sunrise, Mesembria, noon, and Hesperis, evening, she forms part of the series that set the rhythm of the day. Some accounts name Zeus and Themis as her parents; others make her a daughter of Helios or Chronos.

She is remembered as the nursemaid of Selene, the Moon, which links her to the passing of the night. According to tradition, she and her sisters Anatole, Mesembria and Arctus served the goddess Harmonia in her halls in heaven. Like her other sisters, Dysis watched over one of the four gates through which the Winds emerged.

Trivia

Twelve sisters. Dysis had eleven sisters among the Horae, including Auge, Anatole and Mesembria. Each one governed a different part of the day or of everyday life.
Keeper of the Winds. Like her sisters, Dysis watched over one of the four gates from which the Winds came forth, a role that tied her to the movement of the air.
