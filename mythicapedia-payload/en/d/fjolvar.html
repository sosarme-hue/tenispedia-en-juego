figure of Norse mythology, possibly a jötunn

Fjölvar

Fjölvar is a figure in Norse mythology, possibly a jötunn, with whom Odin, disguised as Hárbarðr, claims to have spent five winters fighting and chasing women on the island of Algrœn. He appears only once in the surviving sources, so his nature and role remain largely uncertain.

The myth

The only attestation of Fjölvar comes from the poem Hárbarðsljóð in the Poetic Edda. In it, Thor and Odin, the latter disguised as a ferryman called Hárbarðr, trade insults in a flyting. During his boasting, Odin says he spent five winters with Fjölvar on the island of Algrœn, meaning 'All-green', fighting and pursuing women.

In the following stanza, Odin adds that on the same island he slept with seven sisters and took great joy from each. No other surviving ancient source recounts these adventures, so the allusion has no known parallel.

Ancient sources: Appears only in the poem Hárbarðsljóð of the Poetic Edda, preserved mainly in the Codex Regius and dated in its present form to the 11th–13th centuries.

Trivia

A name with real human use. The name Fjolvar also appears as a human personal name on the Viking Age runestone Hs 6 at Järvsö, Sweden, where a Fjolvar is named as the mother of Harlau. This is a historical usage, not the mythological figure.
