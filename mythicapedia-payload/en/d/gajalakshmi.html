Gajalakshmi

Gajalakshmi is a form of the Hindu goddess Lakshmi, deity of wealth, prosperity and fertility, shown flanked by two elephants that pour water over her. She is counted among the Ashta Lakshmi, the eight aspects of the goddess, and her image combines the fertility of the rains with royal authority.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Gajalakshmi emerged from the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean, and her appearance restored the wealth and power that Indra had lost.

She is depicted with four arms, dressed in red, holding two lotuses while her other hands make the abhaya and varada gestures. The elephants beside her shower her with water from their trunks or from pots, an act linked to the rains that make crops grow.

The narratives explain these elephants as winged beings who once crossed the sky and watered the earth. After they interrupted a sage's meditation, they were cursed to stay on the ground, though they kept their bond with the clouds. Their presence beside Lakshmi reinforces her role as a giver of abundance. In India, elephants also stood for royal power, and kings kept them for ceremonies, processions and military campaigns.

Worship and religion

Gajalakshmi is especially venerated in Goa and the Konkan region, where various Konkani communities honour her as a tutelary deity under the names Gajantlakshmi, Gajalakshmi, Kelbai or Bhauka devi. Her worship remains alive and is linked to fertility.

Legacy

Her image appears in many temples in Odisha, often placed over the doorway as a central protective figure. A lintel at the Temple of Bantãy Srĕi in Cambodia shows her in pink sandstone. Early depictions appear on the railings of the Buddhist stupa at Bharhut and on a coin of Azilises, and a cave at Ellora shows her seated on a lotus. The painter Raja Ravi Varma also portrayed her in 1896.

Ancient sources: Her stories come from the Hindu Puranic tradition, with no specific ancient source named in the entry.
