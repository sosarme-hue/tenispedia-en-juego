Nereid of Greek mythology

Polynoe

Polynoe is one of the Nereids of Greek mythology, the fifty sea-nymph daughters of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and the Oceanid Doris. Her name is usually rendered as 'giver of reason' or 'richness of mind and thought', and she belongs to the wide marine lineage of the Greek tradition.

The myth

In the ancient genealogy, Polynoe belongs to the large family of Nereus and Doris, a pair of sea deities from whom the Nereids were born. Each of them is named within that group, but the stories give her no separate episodes or individual deeds.

Her interest lies mainly in what her name suggests: wisdom and prudence attributed to a sea nymph who is part of the retinue of her father, the old man of the sea. The sources preserve no detailed story about her, so she is known mainly as one of the daughters of Nereus listed in the tradition.

Ancient sources: Hesiod includes her, alongside her sisters, in the catalogue of Nereids in the Theogony.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Her name is interpreted as 'giver of reason' or 'richness of mind', a reference to thought rather than to the sea.
