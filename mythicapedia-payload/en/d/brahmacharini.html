Hindu goddess of devotion and penance

Brahmacharini

Brahmacharini is the second of the nine forms of Durga (Navadurga) and is worshipped on the second day of Navaratri. She represents Parvati performing penance to win Shiva as her husband, and her name means "one who undertakes penance".

The myth

According to the legend, the maiden Parvati resolved to marry Shiva despite her parents' objections, and undertook a penance that some versions say lasted 5,000 years. Meanwhile, the asura Tarakasura, who had a boon from Brahma making him vulnerable only to a son of Shiva, troubled the gods. To stir Shiva's desire, the gods enlisted Kamadeva, who shot an arrow of desire at him; Shiva, enraged, reduced Kamadeva to ashes with his third eye, yet Parvati did not waver.

Parvati took up the ascetic life of Shiva, dwelling in the mountains and subsisting only on bael leaves and river water. Shiva approached her in disguise and tried to dissuade her by listing his own flaws, but she remained unmoved. Later, the asura Prakandasura attacked with a million asuras; Parvati, at the final stage of her penance, could not defend herself, and Lakshmi and Saraswati, though they intervened, were outnumbered. After many days of fighting, the kamandalu beside Parvati fell, a flood swept the asuras away, and when she opened her eyes she emitted flames that consumed Prakandasura. Finally Shiva returned as a brahmachari, tested her with riddles she answered correctly, revealed his true form and agreed to marry her.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped on the second day of Navaratri. In iconography she wears white, holds a japamala in her right hand and a kamandalu in her left. In Ballia (Uttar Pradesh) there is a temple dedicated to Maa Brahmacharini Devi Durga.
