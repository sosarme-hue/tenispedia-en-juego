Hindu goddess of the Tapti river and of solar heat

Tapati

Tapati is a goddess in Hinduism, daughter of the sun god Surya and Chhaya, one of his wives. She is regarded as a mother goddess of the south, associated with heat, and she also gives her name to the Tapti river, with which she is identified.

The myth

In the Mahabharata, the sun god wanted to find a suitable husband for his beautiful daughter Tapati. The Kaurava king Samvarana, a devotee of the sun, was chosen.

One day, while hunting, Samvarana's horse died and the king wandered on foot until he saw Tapati bathing in the sunshine. He was captivated, but when he asked who she was, she vanished. When he came round from his faint, she had returned. She told him she loved him too, but asked him to seek her father's approval.

Samvarana meditated for two weeks until the sage Vasishtha appeared, understood that the king was in love with a goddess, and ascended to the sky to ask Surya for consent. The sun god agreed, and the two were married. Their son Kuru founded the Kuru dynasty and kingdom. Some Hindu texts add that Yami is Tapati's elder sister and that she has two brothers, Shani and Yama.

Worship and religion

Tapati is worshipped both as a goddess and as the personification of the river that bears her name, which Hindu texts credit with many admirable qualities. The sources do not describe specific temples or rites dedicated to her.

Legacy

Her name lives on in the Tapti river, known by that form in the region, and in the suggestion that it is related to Tabiti, queen of the Scythian gods.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, where she appears about two dozen times, and the Bhagavata Purana, which also tells her story.

Trivia

Possible link with Tabiti. Her name, meaning something like "the burning one", may be related to that of Tabiti, queen of the Scythian gods. Some scholars suggest that an early dominant fire goddess existed in ancient Proto-Indo-Iranian religion.
A question in the Mahabharata. In the Mahabharata, Arjuna asks a gandharva about the origin of the name Tapatya, and the gandharva tells him the story of the sun's daughter.
