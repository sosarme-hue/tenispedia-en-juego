Greek hero, descendant of Heracles

Archelaus

Archelaus is a hero of Greek myth, one of the Heraclids, the descendants of Heracles. His story links the lineage of Heracles to Macedonia and to the city of Egeae, from which, according to tradition, Alexander the Great came.

The myth

Archelaus, son of Temenus, was driven out by his brothers and arrived in exile in Macedonia, where King Ciseus was facing attacks from neighbouring peoples. The king promised him his kingdom and his daughter's hand if he defended it, since Archelaus, like Temenus, descended from Heracles. Archelaus defeated the enemies in a single battle and claimed the reward.

Ciseus, advised by his friends, broke his word and plotted to kill him: he had a pit dug, filled it with embers and covered it with brushwood. A servant of the king warned Archelaus, who asked to speak with the king in private. Once the witnesses had withdrawn, he seized Ciseus and threw him into the pit.

Later, following an answer from Apollo, he fled toward Macedonia guided by a goat, and founded the city of Egeae or Egae, named after the animal. Tradition holds that Alexander the Great came from this city. Some sources also confuse him with other namesakes, such as an Archelaus son of Electryon and one of the sons of Egypt.

Trivia

Another Archelaus. Besides the hero, tradition knew another Archelaus, son of Electryon, father of Alcmene, who was the mother of Heracles. This is why the name seemed naturally tied to that family.
Guided by a goat. According to the story, a goat showed Archelaus the way to Macedonia, and the city he founded took a name linked to that animal.
