Etruscan psychopomp of the underworld

Charun

Charun is one of the psychopompoi of the Etruscan underworld, tasked with escorting the dead. He should not be confused with Aita, the lord of the underworld, and his grotesque appearance differs sharply from the Greek Charon, from whom his name derives.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Charun guides souls to the underworld, often alongside Vanth, a winged figure also linked to the afterlife. He is shown with pointed ears, snakes around his arms, bluish skin and a hammer, his religious symbol, which he used to guard tombs and to drive away serpents attacking the deceased.

Although many scenes show him as an escort, on the sarcophagus of Laris Pulenas he threatens a man with his hammer. He was believed to have assistants, such as Tuchulcha, who has gorgon-like hair and wings, and the Tomb of the Charuns shows four Charuns with different sub-names.

Legacy

His modern presence is limited. He appears in Rachel Blau DuPlessis's poem «Back Matter», in John Maddox Roberts's novel The Tribune's Curse, in the GWAR song «The one who will not be named», and as Monster in My Pocket #97.

Trivia

A Charun with a name. The Tomb of the Charuns shows four Charuns, and each seems to have a sub-name, such as Chunchules or Lufe.
An echo in the Colosseum. Later, in the Colosseum, a figure resembling Charun, called Dispater, struck the loser with a hammer to make sure he was dead.
