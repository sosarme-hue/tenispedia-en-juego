one-footed aspect of the Hindu god Shiva

Ekapada

Ekapada is the one-footed aspect of the Hindu god Shiva, worshipped mainly in South India and Orissa. In some South Indian icons his torso is joined by those of Brahma and Vishnu, presenting Shiva as the Supreme Lord from whom the Trimurti arises.

The myth

Ekapada descends from the Vedic deity Aja Ekapada or Ajaikapada, a name his fierce Orissan form, Ekapada Bhairava, still bears. The Rig Veda links him closely with Ahi Budhnya, the serpent of the deep ocean, and commentators have proposed that he was a fire, solar or storm deity, his single foot understood as a lightning strike coming down in one streak.

In the Hindu epics, Aja Ekapada is one of the eleven Rudras and an epithet of Shiva, an identification that persists in most of his representations. The Mahabharata describes him, alongside Ahi Budhnya, as a guardian of gold together with Kubera.

Worship and religion

In South Indian temples the icon may be accompanied by yogis and ascetics who worship it, as well as by the vahanas of the Trimurti: Brahma's swan, Vishnu's garuda and Shiva's bull Nandi. In Orissa the fierce form is linked to motifs of blood sacrifice. The sources do not say whether his worship continues today.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda mentions Aja Ekapada in several hymns, and the Atharvaveda, the Taittiriya Brahmana and Yaska's Naighantuka also name him. The epics, such as the Mahabharata, and the Linga Purana describe him as a Rudra.
