Narakasura

Narakasura, also known as Naraka or Bhauma, is an asura king in Hindu mythology, founder of the Pragjyotisha dynasty and regarded as the ancestor of the rulers of Kamarupa, in present-day Assam. His story remains central to the history and identity of that northeastern region of India.

The myth

According to tradition, Narakasura is the son of Bhumi, the earth goddess. The sources disagree about his father: later texts name Varaha, the boar avatar of Vishnu, while other versions make him the son of the asura Jirania Aksha. His character turned dark after his friendship with the asura Bana, and it was foretold that a future avatar of Vishnu would destroy him. His mother secured from Vishnu a long life and near-absolute power. Drunk on power, he conquered the earth, drove Indra from heaven, stole the earrings of the goddess Aditi and kidnapped thousands of women.

The gods turned to Vishnu, who promised to intervene when he incarnated as Krishna. In the most widespread version, Krishna, together with his wife Satyabhama, whom some regard as an incarnation of the earth, attacked Narakasura's fortress and killed him with the Sudarshana discus. In another variant, Satyabhama kills him, since Naraka had asked to die only at his mother's hand. Before dying he asked that his death be celebrated with coloured lights, the origin of Naraka Chaturdashi, the second day of Diwali. In the story of Kamakhya, Naraka tried to build a staircase to her temple in a single night; a cock that she made crow too early tricked him, and the staircase was left unfinished.

Worship and religion

At Kukurakata in Darrang district, local people place the rooster episode, and the unfinished staircase is known as Mekhelauja Patha. A hill south of Guwahati has borne his name since time immemorial.

Legacy

He is remembered in Naraka Chaturdashi, the second day of Diwali, and Assamese tradition regards him as the ancestor of its dynasties. He is also associated with the worship of the goddess Kamakhya.

Ancient sources: He is first mentioned in the Mahabharata and the Ramayana, and his story is expanded in the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Kalika Purana, the last composed in Kamarupa.

Trivia

A rooster in the legend. In the Kamakhya story, the goddess makes a temple rooster crow early so that Naraka believes dawn has come and abandons his work.
A festival of lights. The celebration of Naraka Chaturdashi, linked to his death, is part of the Diwali festival.
