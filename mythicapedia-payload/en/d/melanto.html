maidservant of Penelope in the Odyssey

Melantho

Melantho is a maidservant in the palace of Ithaca who appears in Homer's Odyssey. The daughter of Dolios and sister of the goatherd Melanthios, she is remembered as one of the minor characters known for her sharp tongue and her disloyalty to Odysseus.

The myth

In Homer's account, Melantho was among Penelope's favourite handmaidens. Penelope had raised her as if she were her own child and gave her many small gifts. Despite this affection, Melantho sided with the suitors: she was one of the slave women who slept with them and became the mistress of Eurymachus.

When Odysseus returns home disguised as a beggar, Melantho treats him harshly. She asks why he has not gone to sleep in the smithy, where visitors usually stayed, and warns him that a better man than Arnaeus might soon kill him. Odysseus threatens to tell Telemachus what she has said, and she and the other maids flee in fear. Melantho insults him again, provoking a fierce response from Odysseus and Penelope.

After the suitors are killed, the text leaves unclear whether Melantho was among the twelve maids Telemachus forced to clean the hall and then hanged.

Legacy

Melantho appears as a character in Monteverdi's opera Il ritorno d'Ulisse in patria and has been played in film and television adaptations of the Odyssey, including the 1954 film Ulysses and the 1968 series Odissea.

Ancient sources: She appears in Homer's Odyssey, in Book XVIII and in later books that recount Odysseus's revenge.
