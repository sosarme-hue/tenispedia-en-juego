epigone and founder of Acarnania in Greek mythology

Acarnan

Acarnan is an epigone in Greek mythology, son of Alcmaeon and Callirrhoe and brother of Amphoterus. According to tradition, he founded the region of Acarnania in northwestern Greece, and his story is tied to the necklace of Harmonia.

The myth

According to the myth, Alcmaeon was murdered by Phegeus when his sons were still very young. Callirrhoe, grief-stricken, begged Zeus to let them grow up at once so they could avenge their father, and her prayer was granted: the boys became grown men.

Acarnan and Amphoterus entered Psophis, killed Phegeus and his wife, and also slew their two sons. The townspeople chased them as far as Tegea, where they were welcomed and kept safe. The brothers then carried Harmonia's necklace and robe to Delphi and dedicated them there, following the advice of Achelous. They then sailed on to Epirus, gathered settlers and founded Acarnania, which took its name from the hero.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Bibliotheca, recounts their revenge on Phegeus and the founding of Acarnania.
