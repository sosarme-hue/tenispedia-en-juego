Kuafu

Kuafu is a giant in Chinese mythology, grandson of Houtu, who set out to chase down the Sun. His story is one of the best-known tales about overreaching ambition and endless effort.

The myth

According to the tale, Kuafu decided to pursue the Sun from the east all the way to the west. To quench his burning thirst he drank the Yellow River and the Wei River, but even these were not enough. Still searching for more water, he died of dehydration.

His wooden club grew into a vast forest of peach trees known as the Deng Forest. In another version, his body turns into a mountain range. Tradition places both in present-day Lingbao. Later, his people, the Kuafu-shi, appear allied with the tribes of Chi You and the Sanmiao at the battle of Banquan, where they attacked the tribe of the Yan Emperor and drove it into the lands of the Yellow Emperor.

Legacy

In modern Chinese, "chasing the Sun" (Kuafu zhui ri) describes someone who is brave and overly optimistic, sets goals beyond their abilities, and keeps going anyway.

Trivia

A forest from a walking stick. According to the myth, the wooden club Kuafu carried grew into the Deng Forest of peach trees.
A modern saying. His chase of the Sun is used in China today to describe someone who pursues goals they cannot reach.
