epithet of Parvati, consort of Rudra in Hinduism

Rudrani

Rudrani is the consort of Rudra, a figure associated with Shiva in Hinduism, and often an epithet of the goddess Parvati (also called Uma). She belongs to the Shaiva and Shakta traditions, and she is important for her fierce aspect and her role as the active power of the god.

The myth

In the Puranic texts, Rudrani appears mostly as a title of Parvati, the wife of Shiva. The Vayu Purana first names her Rudrani and, in the verses that follow, Uma, showing how different epithets merged into a single Mahadevi.

In the Shakta tradition, Rudrani is the Shakti who helps Rudra carry out his cosmic duties, the Prakriti or primal nature and the power of action. The Devi Bhagavata Purana presents her as the Divine Mother who presides over Rudraloka, the celestial realm of Rudra. The Skanda Purana tells how she sent Kalaratri to fight a Daitya demon.

Worship and religion

Shiva and his consort are worshipped together, and Rudrani is identified with the power of action. She appears among the Saptamatrikas, the Seven Mothers, and the Padma Purana links her to the tirtha Rudrakoti.

Ancient sources: Pāṇini cites her in the Aṣṭādhyāyī, and she appears in the Shiva Purana, Vayu Purana, Linga Purana, Skanda Purana, Padma Purana and Devi Bhagavata Purana.
