king of Panchala in the Mahabharata

Drupada

Drupada is the king of Panchala in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He is the father of Draupadi and Dhrishtadyumna, and his story is defined by his broken friendship with his former classmate Drona, who would later kill him in the Kurukshetra War.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Drupada was the son of King Prishata, and his birth name was Yajnasena. As a youth he studied with the sage Bharadvaja, where he befriended Drona, the sage's son, and promised that once he became king he would share half his kingdom with him.

When Drupada inherited the throne, the impoverished Drona asked him for help. Drupada, now aware of the difference in rank, refused and called him a beggar. Drona swore revenge. Years later, serving as teacher to the Pandavas and Kauravas, he demanded as his fee that they capture Drupada, and the Pandavas, led by Arjuna, brought him bound before Drona. Drona returned only half the kingdom, and Drupada, unable to defeat him, performed a fire sacrifice to obtain a son who would kill him. From the offering emerged Dhrishtadyumna and Draupadi.

Draupadi later married the five Pandavas after her mother-in-law Kunti's decision, which Drupada accepted once the sage Vyasa and Krishna intervened. In the Kurukshetra War, Drupada fought alongside his sons-in-law and was killed by Drona on the fifteenth day. Dhrishtadyumna, in turn, killed Drona.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic of India.
