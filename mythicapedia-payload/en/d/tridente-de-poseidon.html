Trident of Poseidon

The trident is the defining weapon and emblem of Poseidon, the Greek god of the sea, and of his Roman counterpart Neptune. With it the god shakes the earth, opens springs and reshapes the waters.

The myth

According to the Bibliotheke attributed to Pseudo-Apollodorus, the Cyclopes forged the trident. Poseidon struck the Acropolis hill with it, producing a well of seawater, in a contest with Athena over Attica; when he lost, he dried the land so no one had water. Later tales say he made a horse by striking the earth, though Greek writers do not attest this. He also struck springs for Amymone, anchored the island of Delos to the sea floor, and split the rock where Ajax the Lesser clung after angering him.

Legacy

The trident appears on sixth-century BC coins of Poseidonia and on the seal of the Greek Navy. It gave its name to a American ballistic missile and features in the crest of the Navy SEALs and on the Maserati logo. In popular culture it appears in Disney's The Little Mermaid and the Pirates of the Caribbean films.

Trivia

A trident on a flag. The broken tip of the trident appears on the flag of Barbados, symbolising the end of British colonial rule.
Three kinds of water. The Roman scholar Servius explained the three prongs as representing three kinds of water: seas, streams and rivers.
