river god in Greek mythology

Spercheus

Spercheus is the god of the river that bears his name, one of the Oceanids, children of Oceanus and Tethys, in Greek mythology. He matters chiefly for his link to Achilles, whose hair his father Peleus dedicated to the river.

The myth

According to Antoninus Liberalis, the nymphs of Mount Otris were daughters of Spercheus and Dino, though it is unclear whether this Dino is the same as one of the Graeae. The same author says Dryope, daughter of King Dryops of Mount Oeta, descended from Spercheus and Polydora, a daughter of Danaus.

In the Iliad, Menesthius, one of the five leaders under Achilles, was the son of Spercheus and Polydora, daughter of Peleus, though he was publicly known as the son of Boros, Polydora's husband.

Worship and religion

Peleus dedicated his son Achilles' hair to the river so that he would return safe from the Trojan War. In the Iliad, Achilles lets his hair grow with the intention of offering it to Spercheus.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad and the work of Antoninus Liberalis.

Trivia

A son with two fathers. In the Iliad, Menesthius was publicly considered the son of Boros, but according to the same text his true father was Spercheus.
A promised lock of hair. Achilles let his hair grow to offer it to the river, a gesture tied to his safe return from the war.
