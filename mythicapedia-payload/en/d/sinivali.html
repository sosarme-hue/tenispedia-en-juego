Vedic goddess of fecundity and childbirth

Sinivali

Sinivali is a Vedic goddess associated with fecundity and childbirth, mentioned in the Rigveda, one of the oldest texts of India. Later tradition assigned her the new moon and linked her to other goddesses and to figures in Vishnu's circle.

The myth

The Vedic hymns describe her as broad-hipped, with fair arms and fingers, presiding over fecundity and an easy birth. In the Rigveda she is invoked together with Saraswati so that the embryo will attach to the womb, and she also appears in a group of goddesses invoked together.

In the Atharvaveda she is named the consort of Vishnu, who at that time was a minor god. In later texts she presides over the day when the new moon first shows a visible edge, while Raka presides over the full moon. Other accounts connect her to human lineages: in the Mahabharata she is a daughter of the sage Angiras, and in the Bhagavata Purana she is the wife of Dhatri and mother of Darsha. According to lexicographers, Sinivali is also a name of the goddess Durga.

Ancient sources: Rigveda (hymns 2.32 and 10.184), Atharvaveda, Mahabharata, Markandeya Purana and Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A name of Durga. According to some lexicographers, Sinivali is one of the names of the goddess Durga, showing how different deities came to share epithets.
Daughter of a sage. The Mahabharata presents her as a daughter of the sage Angiras, a genealogy different from that of the Vedic hymns.
