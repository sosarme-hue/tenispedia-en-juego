well of wisdom in Norse mythology

Mímir's Well

Mímir's Well, or Mímisbrunnr, is a spring of Norse mythology where wisdom and knowledge were kept. It lies beneath one of the roots of the world tree Yggdrasil, and it takes its name from the giant Mímir, its guardian.

The myth

According to the Norse sources, the spring rises beneath one of the three roots of Yggdrasil, the world tree, the one that reaches into Jötunheim, where the primordial void of Ginnungagap once existed. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, its waters hold great wisdom and intelligence, and its keeper, Mímir, is learned because he drinks from it each morning with the horn Gjallarhorn. Snorri places this well alongside two others: Urðarbrunnr, linked to the past and the Æsir, and Hvergelmir in Niflheim, from which the Élivágar rivers flow.

The best-known episode involves Odin. According to the Völuspá, the god wanted to drink from the well to gain wisdom, but he only got a drink after pledging one of his eyes, which was left in its waters. The Ynglinga saga adds that Mímir was beheaded during the war between the Vanir and the Æsir, which has led scholars to link the well with the realm of the dead. The Völuspá also alludes to a passage about Heimdall that some scholars read as the forfeit of an ear, though its meaning remains debated.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, within the Eddas, and the Ynglinga saga.

Trivia

An eye as payment. In the Völuspá, Odin leaves one of his eyes in the well in exchange for a drink from its waters.
A horn called Gjallarhorn. According to the Prose Edda, Mímir drinks from the well with the horn Gjallarhorn, which makes him so wise.
