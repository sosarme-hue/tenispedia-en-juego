sea monster of Finnish mythology

Iku-Turso

Iku-Turso is a sea monster in Finnish mythology, also known as Tursas or Turisas depending on the version. His figure blends with that of a war god and with the ancient giants of the runic songs and the Kalevala.

The myth

According to the runic songs, Iku-Turso, also called Iku-Turilas, is an ancient giant and lord of the North, described as the father of Väinämöinen. The tradition says he would not stay in the womb and freed himself simply by lifting one finger, already born in full armour with a sword and a saddled horse.

Other songs tell of a sea Tursas who impregnates Loviatar, the Maiden of the North, with waves, while she seeks relief from her pains in the water. From this union the diseases of the world are born. In another version, the one who makes her pregnant is the Ukko of the waters.

In the story of the Great Oak, a fiery Tursas from Pohjola steals the hay of three luonnottaret and burns it, and the ashes give rise to an oak so vast that it hides the sun and the moon, until a hero must fell it. In the Kalevala, Iku-Turso rises from the sea and burns a stack of hay; later Louhi summons him to stop the theft of the Sampo, but Väinämöinen seizes him by the ears and, with magic words, makes him promise never to rise from the bottom of the sea again.

Legacy

In Ostrobothnia and Kainuu, an old good luck charm was known as the 'heart of Tursas'. In the list of Tavastian gods recorded by Mikael Agricola in 1551, Turisas appears as a god of war. The Finnish folk metal band Turisas takes its name from him, and he appears as a monster in the video game Final Fantasy XI. A Finnish submarine of the Second World War was named after him, and so was the asteroid 2828 Iku-Turso.

Ancient sources: Mikael Agricola mentions him in 1551, and Finnish runic songs record him in several versions, as well as the Kalevala.
