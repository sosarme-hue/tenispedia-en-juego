female figure in Gnostic cosmology

Norea

Norea is a figure in Gnostic cosmology, daughter of Adam and Eve and sister of Seth, who plays a leading role in two texts from the Nag Hammadi library. She matters because she embodies a revealing and saving woman within a retelling of the flood.

The myth

According to the Hypostasis of the Archons, Norea is the daughter of Eve and the younger sister of Seth, both members of the pure race. The archons plan a flood, but their leader, the Demiurge, warns Noah to build an ark. Norea tries to board; Noah stops her, so she blows on the vessel and sets it ablaze.

The rulers try to rape her, but she cries out to the God of the Entirety. The angel Eleleth appears, frightens them away, and reveals that she is a child of the spirit. In Mandean literature she is instead the wife of Noah or Shem. Epiphanius of Salamis summarizes a book called Noria, in which she burns the ark three times and teaches how to recover stolen sparks through sexual emissions.

Ancient sources: The Hypostasis of the Archons, the Thought of Norea and Epiphanius of Salamis's Panarion, along with Mandean literature.

Trivia

Disputed name. Epiphanius suggested Norea was a distortion of Pyrrha, wife of Deucalion, based on a link to nura, the Syriac word for fire.
Untouched virgin. The Hypostasis calls her 'the virgin whom no power has defiled', a phrase the Gospel of Philip applies to Mary.
