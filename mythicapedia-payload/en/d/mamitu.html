Mesopotamian goddess of the underworld

Mamitu

Mamitu is a Mesopotamian goddess linked to the underworld, wife of Nergal or of gods analogous to him, such as Erra. Her cult was modest and centered mainly on the city of Kutha.

The myth

Cuneiform texts give her few stories of her own. Her myth is defined mainly by marriage: she was the wife of Nergal, and in the Epic of Erra she appears as the wife of Erra. Some scholars confuse her with Mami, the goddess of birth, though the god lists keep them apart.

Worship and religion

Her importance was minor. In Nippur she received offerings alongside Nergal in the Ekur complex, and her worship centered on Kutha, where it lasted into the first millennium BCE. She was also honored in Babylon and Mari. There is no evidence that her cult survives today.

Legacy

A possible successor is Amamit, a demon in the Mandaean Ginzā Yamina, described as a daughter of Qin and spouse of Zartai-Zartanai. Her name also serves as an epithet of Libat, the planet Venus.

Ancient sources: The Epic of Erra, a single passage of the Epic of Gilgamesh, and the god lists An = Anum and Weidner.

Trivia

Several spellings. Her name appears in cuneiform texts with several spellings, such as ma-ma or ma-mi-tum.
A name with a possible meaning. Her name might derive from the Akkadian word mammû, meaning oath or frost, which would give the sense of wintry.
