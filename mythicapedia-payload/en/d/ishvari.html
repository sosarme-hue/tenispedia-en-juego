Hindu epithet of the Goddess, feminine form of Ishvara

Ishvari

Ishvari is a Hindu epithet of Sanskrit origin that refers to the Goddess, the feminine counterpart of Ishvara. It is also used for shakti, the feminine energy associated with the Trimurti.

The myth

The sources do not record a separate story for Ishvari; the name works as a title or epithet. Its Sanskrit root combines īś, "to be valid or powerful, to be master of", with vara, "select, choicest, valuable, most excellent or eminent". With that meaning, the term applies to the Goddess when she is conceived as female, which is especially common in Shaktism.

Within that tradition, Ishvari names the feminine energy of the Trimurti, linked to Saraswati, Lakshmi and Parvati. A single epithet thus gathers several female divine figures under one idea of power and sovereignty.

Trivia

Origin of the name. The root īś means to be powerful or master, and vara adds the idea of what is most excellent or valuable.
An epithet, not a character. Ishvari works mainly as a title of the Goddess rather than as a figure with a story of its own.
