protector goddess of childbirth and children in Tamil tradition

Periyachi

Periyachi is a ferocious aspect of the goddess Parvati in Hinduism, revered especially by Tamil communities as the protector of expectant mothers and newborns. Her worship has spread to Singapore, Malaysia, the Caribbean and Réunion Island.

The myth

According to a Tamil legend, a Pandya king named Vallalarajan cruelly oppressed his subjects. It was said that if his child touched the ground, the king would die. When the queen went into labour and the king could not find a midwife, he had to turn to a stern woman named Periyachi, who delivered the baby and held it aloft so it would not touch the earth.

The king wanted to kill the newborn to save his own life, but Periyachi revealed her true form as Adi Parashakti. With her many arms she trampled the king and killed him with her trident. The queen also wished to destroy the child, whom she blamed for her husband's death, and the goddess killed her, tore open her belly and devoured her intestines, saving the baby. The child grew up under her care, became a Pandya king and built many temples in her honour, which is why she is known as the protector of babies and expectant mothers.

She is depicted with eight arms, holding weapons such as the trident, a noose or a sword, with the fallen king beneath her feet. According to tradition, she punishes those who hurt others with their words and men who exploit women. Together with Muneeswaran she forms a guardian pair who protect the earth from evil spirits.

Worship and religion

Tamil rural Hindu communities in the diaspora hold ceremonies during pregnancy, the Punsavana in the third month and the Simantonnyana in the seventh, with prayers to the goddess. After childbirth, offerings are made to her and, at the first temple visit, parents dedicate their baby before her. Worship includes the Periyachi Puja festival in the Tamil month of Aadi, offerings of meat and vegetarian dishes later shared as prasadam, and veneration as a household deity. Her best-known shrines are in Madurai, Manapparai and Singapore.
