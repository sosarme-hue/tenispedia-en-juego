green god of spring and fertility in Chinese religion

Cangdi

Cangdi is the manifestation of the supreme god linked to wood and spring, worshipped as the god of fertility. He belongs to the five emperors of Chinese tradition, and his astral body is Jupiter.

The myth

According to tradition, Cangdi is a form of the supreme god tied to the essence of wood and spring. In human form he was Tàihào, known as Fu Xi, and his animal and constellation share his name. His female consort is Bixia, goddess of fertility.

The Confucian Rites of Zhou discusses the so-called "Wufang Shangdi", the five supreme gods. According to the history quoted in the Kokuyo, the five emperors include Cangdi, also called Qingdi, alongside Huangdi, Heidi, Chidi and Baidi.

In Daoism he is said to be the grandson of the Jade Emperor. As Dongyue, the Great Deity of the Eastern Peak, he is revered as the god of Mount Tai, a place where, according to belief, the spirits of the dead gather.

Worship and religion

During the Han dynasty emperors performed the Feng Shan ceremony on Mount Tai, considered essential for receiving the mandate of heaven. It began in 219 BC under Qin Shihuang after he unified China. Over time the god of Mount Tai grew from a local deity into one governing life and death as a whole. The ritual of storming the city is still performed in Taiwan in connection with the Dongyue Emperor.

Ancient sources: The Confucian Rites of Zhou and the history quoted in the Kokuyo mention the five supreme gods.
