Souls of Pe and Nekhen

The Souls of Pe and Nekhen are the spirits of the mythical predynastic rulers of Lower and Upper Egypt, honoured as ancestors of the pharaohs. They matter because they symbolically linked the two halves of the country under the authority of Horus.

The myth

According to the Egyptian tradition, these rulers belonged to the divine sphere, and their bau (souls or life energies) kept acting after death. The Pyramid Texts mention them first, in the formula for the king's supernatural journey.

The history of their two towns explains their dual identity. Nekhen, in Upper Egypt, was the centre of Horus worship and an ancient capital. Pe, in Lower Egypt, was a mythical town not especially known for that worship, although Ra had given it to Horus after his eye was injured in the struggle for the throne. In this way the spiritual forces of both halves of the country were joined. In depictions, the souls of Pe have falcon heads and those of Nekhen have canine heads.

Worship and religion

Their characteristic gesture, the hnw or joyful salute, involved striking the chest with one fist while the other arm was raised. Priests and high officials performed it when greeting Ra's appearance on the horizon, and kings were greeted in this way within the solar cult. The sources do not describe a separate cult of their own apart from the solar one, nor a practice that survives today.

Legacy

Pharaohs cited these souls in many inscriptions to legitimise their power, and the Kushite rulers saw themselves as their descendants. In funerary art they appear in royal tombs such as that of Ramesses I and tomb QV52 of the Great Royal Wife Tyti in the Valley of the Queens.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts mention them first, and the Turin Papyrus calls these rulers the « Followers of Horus » (Shemsu-Her).

Trivia

Hierakonpolis. The Greeks called Nekhen Hierakonpolis. It was the centre of Horus worship and an ancient capital of Upper Egypt.
Buto. Pe was known in Greek as Buto and was regarded as the mythical capital of Lower Egypt.
