Gaulish god of the three-craned bull

Tarvos Trigaranus

Tarvos trigaranus, ‘the bull of three cranes’, is a Gaulish deity shown on the Pillar of the Boatmen, a Gallo-Roman monument in Lutetia (Paris). Depicted as a bull with three cranes on its back, it reflects the central place of the bull in Celtic religious imagery.

The myth

According to the relief, the bull stands beneath a tree, while on the adjoining panel the god Esus fells a tree, possibly a willow, with an axe. Tradition links the animal to Esus and the wooded place he cuts down.

The bull is often shown with a third horn on its forehead, which gives rise to the name ‘three-horned bull’. It is associated with kingship and strength, and set in opposition to the boar. It appears four times in two scenes on the Gundestrup cauldron.

Legacy

One of Saturn’s moons, discovered in 2000, bears its name: Tarvos (S/2000 S 4), part of a group of moons named after Gallic mythological figures.

Trivia

Gaulish root. The Gaulish word taruos means ‘bull’. It has relatives in Celtic languages such as Old Irish tarb and Welsh tarw.
Trier pillar. A pillar from Trier shows a man with an axe cutting a tree in which sit three birds and a bull’s head, a scene compared with the panels of the Pillar of the Boatmen.
