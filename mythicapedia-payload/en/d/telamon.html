Greek hero, king of Salamis and father of Ajax the Great

Telamon

Telamon is a hero of Greek mythology, son of King Aeacus of Aegina, who sailed with the Argonauts and joined Heracles on his campaigns. He is best known as the father of the Trojan War heroes Ajax the Great and Teucer, and his name later gave rise to a kind of architectural column.

The myth

According to tradition, Telamon was the son of King Aeacus of Aegina and Endeis, although other accounts name different parents, and an early story recorded by Pherecydes of Athens presents him as a friend rather than a brother of Peleus. After killing their half-brother Phocus, Telamon and Peleus fled Aegina for the island of Salamis, where King Cychreus welcomed Telamon, befriended him and eventually handed him his kingdom. Telamon married Cychreus’ daughter Periboea, who bore him Ajax; in other versions the daughter is called Glauce and Periboea is his second wife, the daughter of Alcathous.

Telamon and Peleus were close friends of Heracles and joined him on his expeditions against the Amazons and against Troy. In the version in which Laomedon rules Troy, Poseidon sends a sea monster and Heracles agrees to kill it in exchange for the divine horses that the king had received from Zeus, but Laomedon breaks his promise. Heracles and Telamon sack the city and kill Laomedon; Telamon takes Hesione as a war prize and has a son by her, Teucer. In Apollodorus’ Library, Telamon is the first to break through the walls of Troy, which enrages Heracles, who wanted that glory for himself. Telamon quickly builds an altar of stones in Heracles’ honour, and Heracles relents, later giving him Hesione as a wife. Hesione then ransoms her brother Podarces, who is renamed Priam.

In the Great Eoiae, Heracles, welcomed as a guest by Telamon, asks for a son while wearing the lion skin, and an eagle sent by Zeus appears; that is why the boy was named Ajax. Some accounts mention a third son, Trambelus, who fell in love with Apriate, a young woman from Lesbos, and when she rejected him he drowned her in the sea. After the Trojan War, Ajax took his own life, and Telamon banished Teucer from Salamis for failing to bring his brother home.

Legacy

In architecture, a telamon is a colossal male figure used as a column, also called an atlas or atlantid, the male version of the caryatid. The “Telamon Song” was an ancient Greek song from the 5th century BC known only through references in plays and later commentaries; it is usually thought to have been a warlike song about Ajax, though some commentators believed it was a mournful song about Telamon himself.

Ancient sources: Homer in the Iliad, Pherecydes of Athens, Apollodorus in his Library, and the Great Eoiae.

Trivia

A name with meaning. The name Telamon means “broad strap” in Ancient Greek.
From ransom to Priam. According to Apollodorus, Hesione’s brother Podarces was renamed Priam, a name linked to the Greek verb “to buy”, since his sister ransomed him with a veil.
