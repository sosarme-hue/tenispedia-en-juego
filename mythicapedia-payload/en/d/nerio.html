Roman goddess of war and valor

Nerio

Nerio, or Neriene, is a Roman war goddess and the personification of valor, the partner of Mars in ancient cult. She matters because she shows how the Romans pictured martial virtue before other deities came to overshadow her.

The myth

Ancient Roman literature seems to present Nerio as one of two wives of the war god Mars, the other being Moles. Her name is thought to derive from a Proto-Indo-European root linked to strength, vigour and valor.

Aulus Gellius, in his Attic Nights, noted that her name was a Sabine word meaning strength and fortitude.

Worship and religion

In ancient cult practices she was the companion of Mars. Romans often dedicated spoils taken from enemies to her. Over time she was sometimes identified with Bellona and occasionally with Minerva, especially Pallas Athena, before being supplanted by more clearly defined deities such as Bellona or Anna Perenna.

Ancient sources: Aulus Gellius, Attic Nights, which records her name as a Sabine word.

Trivia

A Sabine name. Aulus Gellius claimed that the goddess's name was a Sabine word meaning strength and fortitude.
Indo-European origins. Her name is thought to be related to a Greek word meaning man, both belonging to the semantic field of masculine attributes.
