Euboean hero of Greek myth

Chalcodon

Chalcodon is a hero of Greek myth tied to the island of Euboea. Son of Abas, eponym of the Abantes, he was the father of Elephenor, a warrior who took part in the Trojan War.

The myth

According to tradition, Chalcodon was the son of Abas, and in one version of his lineage he is named as a grandson of Poseidon. Accounts do not agree on his wife, who is called Melanippe in some and Alcyone in others. With her he had Elephenor and a daughter, Chalciope, who later married Aegeus as his second wife.

Chalcodon is also mentioned alongside Telamon, helping Heracles in his campaign against Elis. His death came during a Theban expedition against the Euboeans, who were trying to free themselves from an ancient tribute imposed on them. Amphitryon killed him in the fighting, and his tomb was shown near the city of Chalcis.

Trivia

A tomb near Chalcis. His tomb was shown near the city of Chalcis, on the island of Euboea.
A wife with several names. Sources name his wife as Melanippe or as Alcyone, depending on the version.
