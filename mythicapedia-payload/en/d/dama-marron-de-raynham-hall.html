English ghost of Raynham Hall

The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall

The Brown Lady of Raynham Hall is a ghost that, according to legend, haunts the mansion in Norfolk, England. It is one of the best-known hauntings in the United Kingdom, mainly because of a photograph taken in 1936.

The myth

According to legend, the ghost is Dorothy Walpole (1686-1726), sister of the politician Robert Walpole and second wife of Charles Townshend, 2nd Viscount Townshend, a man with a violent temper. After discovering her affair with Thomas Wharton, the viscount locked her in her rooms in the mansion, where she died in 1726, reportedly of smallpox. Mary Wortley Montagu claimed that the Countess of Wharton had tricked Dorothy into staying there.

Sightings began at Christmas 1835, when several guests said they saw her in an old-fashioned brown dress. In 1936, photographer Hubert Provand and his assistant Indre Shira published in Country Life magazine an image of a female figure coming down the main staircase.

Legacy

She inspired three comic book adaptations, in 1948, 1953 and 1974, published in American horror anthologies.

Trivia

A much-debated photo. Critics such as Joe Nickell argue the image is a double exposure, and magician John Booth reproduced a similar effect with an assistant covered by a sheet.
A possible Virgin Mary. Some sceptics suggest the figure resembles a statue of the Virgin Mary superimposed on the empty staircase.
