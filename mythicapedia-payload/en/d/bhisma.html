hero of the Hindu epic, warrior and patriarch of the Kuru house

Bhishma

Bhishma (Bhīṣma) is one of the central figures of the Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic. Son of King Shantanu and the river goddess Ganga, he is remembered for his vow of celibacy, for his role as adviser to the royal house of Hastinapura, and for commanding the Kauravas in the Kurukshetra War.

The myth

According to the story, Bhishma was born as Devavrata, son of Shantanu and Ganga, and his origin goes back to a curse. Eight Vasu gods, after stealing the cow Nandini from the sage Vashishtha, were condemned to be born as mortals. Seven of them would be freed soon after birth; the thief, Prabhasa, was to live longer on Earth, in exchange for becoming the most illustrious man of his time.

Ganga agreed to marry Shantanu on the condition that he never question her actions. After she drowned seven children, he could not prevent her from doing the same to the eighth, so she took him away and promised to return him when he grew up. Devavrata was taught statecraft, the Vedas, weapons and spiritual sciences by sages and gods, and received his military training from Parashurama.

His great sacrifice came when his father wished to marry the fisherwoman Satyavati. To reassure her father, Devavrata gave up the throne and swore lifelong celibacy. For this vow he received the name Bhishma, meaning one who takes a terrible vow, and the boon of choosing the moment of his death. Later he swore to serve whoever sat on the throne of Hastinapura, a promise that bound him to the line of Dhritarashtra.

In the Kurukshetra War he fought on the side of the Kauravas, though he did not want to kill the Pandavas. After ten days of combat, Arjuna, aided by Shikhandi, brought him down with arrows, and Bhishma offered no resistance. He spent fifty-one days lying on a bed of arrows, counseling both sides, and died at the time he had chosen, during the auspicious period of Uttarayana, after passing the Vishnu Sahasranama on to Yudhishthira.

Worship and religion

The Pandavas and Kauravas called him Pitamaha, meaning grandfather, and Hindu tradition honors him as an example of sacrifice and duty. His death anniversary is observed as Bhishma Ashtami, on the eighth lunar day of the bright half of the month of Magha (January-February), a date still observed today. The sources do not describe specific temples dedicated to him.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata itself, the great Sanskrit epic, is the main source for his story, where his birth, vows and the Kurukshetra War are narrated.

Trivia

A golden palm tree. His battle banner was a golden palm tree, an emblem that distinguished him on the field of Kurukshetra.
Fifty-one days of waiting. After falling, he spent fifty-one days on a bed of arrows, advising Pandavas and Kauravas before dying at the moment he himself chose.
