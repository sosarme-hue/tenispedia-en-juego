Mesopotamian god of the night watch

Hendursaga

Hendursaga is a Mesopotamian god, a night watchman of the streets, associated with the goddess Nanshe and, in god lists, with the Akkadian god Ishum.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hendursaga was a divine watchman who walked the streets at night. His name may mean "torch or staff bearer who goes in front", although its origin is uncertain. A hymn tells how Nanshe granted him his functions and his insignia, a staff or perhaps a torch.

His wife varied by period: in the third millennium BCE he was linked to Dumuziabzu, and in a later tradition to Ninmug. A Sumerian hymn assigns him three groups of seven attendants, including the so-called "warriors", associated with animals such as the fox, the wolf and the shark.

Worship and religion

He was chiefly associated with the area of Lagash, and may have had a chapel in Ur. According to a hymn, lighting a torch and calling on his name guaranteed safe passage through the streets at night. An incantation asked him for protection from demons.

Ancient sources: A Sumerian hymn dedicated to him, the god lists of Weidner and Nippur, the Epic of Erra and the incantation Marduk's address to demons.

Trivia

An uncertain name. Its etymology is unclear, although it has been proposed to mean something like "torch or staff bearer who goes in front".
A unique epithet. He was called "lamp of the people", an epithet applied to him alone.
