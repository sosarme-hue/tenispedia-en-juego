Þrívaldi

Þrívaldi is a jötunn, or giant, of Norse mythology whose name means "thrice mighty". He is known mainly because the god Thor killed him, an event that poets used as one of their poetic labels for the god.

The myth

According to the Norse tradition, Þrívaldi was a giant who met his end at the hands of Thor. The surviving sources do not describe the encounter in detail, so most of what we know about him comes from brief references.

Snorri Sturluson, in the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, records the event within a list of poetic names. There, "killer of Þrívaldi" is a kenning, an indirect way of naming Thor. Snorri quotes verses by Bragi Boddason, who describes the god as a blade that split the giant's nine heads, and by Vetrliði Sumarliðason, who praises Thor for battering him.

Legacy

His name also appears in the þulur, a list of names of giants and other beings from Norse mythology, which suggests his figure was kept alive as part of the poetic repertoire.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson mentions him in the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, and two skaldic poets he quotes, Bragi Boddason and Vetrliði Sumarliðason, name him in their verses.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Þrívaldi means "thrice mighty" in Old Norse, a name that stresses the giant's strength.
The nine heads. Bragi Boddason's verses speak of Þrívaldi's nine heads, a detail the poet uses to highlight Thor's feat.
