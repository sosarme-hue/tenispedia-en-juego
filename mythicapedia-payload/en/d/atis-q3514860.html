demigod of Greek myth in Ovid's telling

Athis

Athis is a demigod of outstanding beauty, son of a Ganges nymph, who appears in Ovid's Metamorphoses. His death at the hands of Perseus, during a brawl beside an altar, is one of the most tense episodes of Book V.

The myth

In Book V of Ovid's Metamorphoses, Athis is a young demigod of remarkable beauty, coming from India and born to Limnaee, a nymph of the Ganges. He was a very brave and skilled archer, and he followed Phineus.

During a quarrel between Perseus and Phineus, the Greek hero killed Athis with a log still smoldering in the middle of the altar, just as he was about to shoot his bow. The Assyrian Lycabas, who Ovid says was his closest friend or, most probably, his lover, wept for his companion and tried to avenge him by shooting an arrow at Perseus from Athis's bow. Perseus dodged the arrow, and Lycabas fell dead too.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Metamorphoses, Book V, where he appears as a demigod son of a Ganges nymph.

In art

Trivia

A possible Virgilian model. The classical scholar Alison Keith suggests that Athis's death was modelled on the killing of Ebysus by Corynaeus in Virgil's Aeneid, where a burning firebrand taken from an altar is also thrown.
