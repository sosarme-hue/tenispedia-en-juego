Etruscan hero, mythical founder of Tarquinia

Tarchon

Tarchon is a hero of Etruscan mythology, brother of Tyrrhenus and mythical co-founder of Tarquinia, one of the cities of the Etruscan dodecapolis. He matters because tradition presents him as one of the architects of the Etruscan people's organisation.

The myth

According to the tradition, Tarchon was the brother of Tyrrhenus, who placed him in charge of the city of Tarquinia. He also appears in Virgil's Aeneid as king of the Tyrrhenians and an ally of Aeneas in the war against Mezentius, Turnus and the other Latin tribes. There he kills the young Italic warrior Venulus.

The later Byzantine writer John the Lydian, in the sixth century, distinguishes two legendary figures with this name: Tarchon the Elder and Tarchon the Younger. The Elder is said to have received the Etrusca Disciplina from the prophet Tages and to have learned augury from Tyrrhenus. The Younger fought alongside Aeneas after his arrival in Italy, so on this reading he lived shortly after the Trojan War.

The legends place the founding of these cities in a mythical past. Archaeology does not contradict this rough chronology: Tarquinia and the other cities of the dodecapolis were founded around 1000 BC, at the end of the Bronze Age.

Legacy

His name has been linked to the Tarquins, a Roman family, and to two of the legendary Seven Kings of Rome. The Hittitologist Oliver Gurney suggested a connection with Tarhunt, the Luwian storm god, a idea other researchers such as Carlo De Simone have dismissed.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid names him as the Etruscan leader and ally of Aeneas; John the Lydian, in the sixth century, mentions him when discussing the Etrusca Disciplina.

Trivia

A name with possible roots. Some scholars link his name to the Roman Tarquins. Gurney connected it to the Luwian god Tarhunt, a proposal not accepted by everyone.
Two Tarchons. John the Lydian distinguished an Elder Tarchon, linked to the Etrusca Disciplina, from a Younger Tarchon, who fought alongside Aeneas.
