king of Laconia in Greek myth

Dion

Dion was a king of Laconia in ancient Greece, husband of Amphithea and father of three daughters: Orphe, Lyco and Carya. His story revolves around his daughters' gift of prophecy, Dionysus's love for Carya and the punishment the three sisters suffered.

The myth

According to the myth, Apollo was kindly received by Dion and Amphithea, and in gratitude he gave their three daughters the gift of prophecy, on one condition: they must not betray the gods or pry into forbidden things.

Dion built a temple to Dionysus, who also visited his house and fell in love with Carya. When Orphe and Lyco tried to stop their sister from joining the god, breaking Apollo's prohibition, Dionysus turned them into rocks and Carya into a walnut tree.

Worship and religion

Dion built a temple to Dionysus. The Lacedaemonians, informed by Artemis, dedicated a sanctuary to the goddess under the epithet Caryatis, linked to Carya's story. The sources do not say whether this cult survived.

Trivia

A shrine named after a nut. The Lacedaemonians dedicated a temple to Artemis under the epithet Caryatis, linked to Carya, the daughter turned into a walnut tree.
Three sisters, three fates. The two sisters who defied Apollo were turned to stone, while Carya, who joined Dionysus, was transformed into a tree.
