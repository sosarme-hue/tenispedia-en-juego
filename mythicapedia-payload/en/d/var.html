Norse goddess of oaths

Vár

Vár is a goddess among the Ásynjur, the female Æsir of Norse mythology, associated with oaths and agreements between women and men. Her name may mean "pledge" or "beloved," and the word várar for such contracts comes from it; she is said to punish those who break them.

The myth

In the Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson lists Vár ninth among the sixteen Ásynjur in chapter 35 of Gylfaginning. She listens to the oaths and private agreements that women and men make with each other, and she punishes those who break them.

In the Poetic Edda poem Þrymskviða, the giant Þrymr asks for Vár's blessing at a wedding that is really a trap: his "bride" is Thor in disguise as Freyja. After she is consecrated with the stolen hammer Mjöllnir, the giant invokes Vár's blessing.

Vár also appears in a list of twenty-seven Ásynjur in the Skáldskaparmál and in a kenning in the poem Haustlöng by the skald Þjóðólfr of Hvinir, where she stands for the goddess Skaði as "bow-string Vár."

Legacy

Her name survives in a runic inscription from around 1300 in Bergen, Norway, recording a mercantile transaction followed by a verse from an annoyed scribe that mentions Vár, meaning "wise."

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Þrymskviða), Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), and the poem Haustlöng by Þjóðólfr of Hvinir.

Trivia

A name with two meanings. Scholars suggest Vár may mean "pledge" or "beloved," and the word várar referred to the oaths she protected.
A wedding with a trick. In Þrymskviða, the giant calls on Vár at a wedding where the bride is really Thor dressed as Freyja.
