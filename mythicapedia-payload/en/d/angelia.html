daughter of Hermes, personification of messages

Angelia

Angelia is, in Greek mythology, the personification of the message, and she appears as a daughter of Hermes, the messenger of the gods. Her name, derived from the Greek word meaning "message, news or proclamation", explains her role.

The myth

Angelia appears in only one passage, a poem by Pindar, where she is named as a daughter of Hermes. The text places her in a scene of announcement: she is to carry to Callimachus the bright honour won at Olympia, a triumph that Zeus granted to his lineage. Iphion hears the news, and so the message reaches its recipient.

As her name suggests, Angelia embodies the act of announcing itself rather than a personality with traits of her own. No other stories about her survive, so her figure is understood mainly as an extension of Hermes and of the idea of communication between gods and mortals.

Ancient sources: Pindar (5th century BC), in a poem that names her as a daughter of Hermes.

Trivia

A name that describes her role. The Greek noun angelia means message, news, mission or proclamation, so her name is already a definition of her role.
A single ancient mention. Angelia appears in only one surviving source, making her one of the least documented figures associated with Hermes.
