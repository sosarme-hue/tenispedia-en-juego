jotunn queen in Norse mythology

Glöð

Glöð, Old Norse for "glowing embers", is a queen of the jötnar who appears in the saga Þorsteins saga Víkingssonar. Wife of Logi, the personification of fire, she is the mother of two daughters, Eisa and Eimyrja.

The myth

Tradition presents her as a daughter of Grímr of Grímsgarðr, a jötunn who dwells in Jötunheimr, and of Alvör, whose family ties link her to Álfheimr, the realm of the light elves. This mix of a giant father and an elven mother leads scholars to regard Glöð as more mythological than historical.

Married to Logi, she had two beautiful daughters, Eisa and Eimyrja, whose names also evoke fire and embers. In the Prose Edda, Logi appears as a fire giant, and by extension his consort is sometimes identified as a goddess, as are her daughters.

Ancient sources: She appears in the legendary saga Þorsteins saga Víkingssonar, and her husband Logi is mentioned in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A name of fire. Her name means "glowing embers", and her daughters bear names related to fire and embers.
A frequent confusion. She is sometimes wrongly identified as the wife of Loki, when her husband is Logi, a different figure.
