celestial realm of the devas in Hinduism

Svarga

Svarga, also spelled Swarga, is the celestial abode of the devas in Hindu cosmology: a set of heavenly worlds on and above Mount Meru, where the righteous enjoy pleasures before being reborn on earth. It is one of the seven higher lokas and is often translated as "heaven", though it differs from the Abrahamic concept.

The myth

According to the tradition, Svarga was built by Tvashtar, the architect of the devas, and is ruled by Indra with his consort Indrani. His palace is called Vaijayanta, his capital is Amaravati, and its entrance is guarded by the elephant Airavata. The realm also holds the cow Kamadhenu, the wish-granting tree Parijata and the Kalpavriksha, planted by Indra in the Nandana gardens after emerging from the churning of the ocean of milk.

The central conflict of Svarga is the endless war between devas and asuras. In many stories an asura king, such as Hiranyakashipu, seizes the realm, and Vishnu intervenes, sometimes as his avatar Narasimha, to restore order. Svarga is not eternal: during each pralaya it is destroyed along with the first realms. The souls of those who performed pious deeds dwell there for a time, without yet attaining moksha, before returning to earth according to their karma.

In the Mahabharata, the prince Arjuna is escorted to Svarga by Matali, Indra's charioteer, and sees its gardens and the apsaras.

Worship and religion

In contemporary Hinduism Svarga is often relegated to a lower heaven, placed beneath Vaikuntha and Kailasha, the abodes of Vishnu and Shiva. In Buddhism it is one of the worlds of rebirth, and some Buddhists aspire to be reborn there as a step toward final salvation.

Legacy

Svarga appears in the religious and philosophical literature of India and in Buddhist tradition. Its name is still used as a synonym for a celestial paradise in Hindu culture.

Ancient sources: The Vedas, especially the Atharvaveda, describe it as the abode of ancestors and of those who make sacrifices; the Mundaka Upanishad, the Vedanta Sutra, the Bhagavata Purana, the Ramayana and the Mahabharata also mention it.

Trivia

Trishanku. In the Ramayana, King Trishanku is expelled from Svarga by Indra and is left suspended in the air, held up by the sage Vishvamitra.
Third heaven. Because of its position, Svarga is called Tridiva, the third highest heaven.
