Catalan folkloric figure of New Year's Eve

Man of the noses

The Man of the Noses (in Catalan, home dels nassos) is a figure of Catalan folklore, also known in Aragon, La Rioja, Navarre and Álava, who has as many noses as days remain in the year. His legend, tied to 31 December, was mainly told to amuse children.

The myth

According to tradition, he loses one nose each day, so he can only be seen on the last day of the year, when just one remains. Adults told children he had 365 noses spread over his body, since they would not all fit on his face, and that he had just been seen passing a nearby street so they would run to look for him. In some places it was said he could be spotted reflected in the gutters or that he visited the church to drink from the holy water font.

The folklorist Joan Amades thought the character might be a degenerated figure symbolising the passing of the year. He lived at the base of a tree and dressed in leaves from twelve kinds of trees in four colours. According to Amades, he may have been inspired by Janus, the two-faced Roman god.

Legacy

In Barcelona, at midnight on 31 December, people searched for him in the Plaça del Palau, where he was covered with sheets in front of the crowd. In Arnedo, since 1991, the Peña Lubumbas moved the legend to 1 January with a character who arrives in an old bus. In some towns a giant-head figure representing him still appears in street parades.

Trivia

A children's prank. Adults often claimed he had just passed a nearby street, so children would run off to look for him.
A change of date. In Arnedo, the Peña Lubumbas moved the legend to 1 January, when the character can finally have all 365 noses.
