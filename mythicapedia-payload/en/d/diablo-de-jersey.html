legendary creature of South Jersey folklore

Jersey Devil

The Jersey Devil is a legendary creature, sometimes classed as a cryptid, said to haunt the Pine Barrens of southern New Jersey in the United States. It is one of the best-known figures of East Coast American folklore and gave its name to an NHL hockey team.

The myth

The most widespread legend traces the creature's origin to Mother Leeds, a woman who supposedly had twelve children and, pregnant with a thirteenth, cursed the baby in frustration, declaring that it should be the devil. According to the tale, the child was born normally but soon grew wings, a tail and claws, then flew off into the pines, where it became a creature with hooves, a goat's head and bat wings.

Other versions change the place and the characters. In one, set at Esteville near Mays Landing around 1855, the mother rejects her baby and wishes a stork would bring a devil instead; the winged child returns from time to time and she shoos it away. Some variants make the mother a witch and the father the devil himself.

Reports of sightings multiplied over the centuries, with famous episodes such as that of 1909, when newspapers published hundreds of encounters in one week and panic spread through the Delaware Valley.

Legacy

The creature's name gave rise to the Jersey Devils hockey team of the NHL, and the legend has become a cultural icon of the state, which uses it as a nickname for many organisations and groups.

Trivia

A shot with no effect. According to legend, Commodore Stephen Decatur fired a cannonball at a flying creature he saw at a cannonball factory, without any effect.
A historian's alternative theory. Historian Brian Regal argues that the legend may have grown out of colonial-era political and religious disputes involving the Leeds family rather than a single real person.
