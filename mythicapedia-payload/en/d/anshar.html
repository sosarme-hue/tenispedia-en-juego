Anshar

Anshar is a primordial god of Mesopotamian mythology whose name means "the whole heaven". Together with his sister and spouse Kishar, "the whole earth", he represents the totality of heaven and earth, and he is regarded as the father of the god Anu.

The myth

According to tradition, Anshar belongs to the second generation of gods. He is usually seen as a son of Lahmu and Lahamu, although the texts do not state this explicitly.

In the Enuma Elish, the great Babylonian epic, Anshar rules as king of the gods. When Tiamat, the primordial mother, plans to avenge the death of Apsu, Anshar first asks Ea to attack her. When Ea fails, he sends Anu to negotiate with her, without success. Ea eventually persuades him that only Marduk, Ea's son, can defeat her. After Marduk's victory, he takes the throne of the gods, and Anshar gives him his new names. Anshar then tasks his messenger Kakka with informing Lahmu and Lahamu of this decision.

Worship and religion

Anshar was more an abstract figure than a worshipped deity: the texts present him as a primordial principle, and he had little cult of his own. There is one exception: a small shrine dedicated to Anshar is attested in Uruk in the Neo-Babylonian and early Achaemenid periods, although it is uncertain when his cult was introduced.

Legacy

From the reign of Sargon II, the Assyrians identified Anshar with Ashur, the supreme god of their pantheon, to establish Ashur's seniority over Marduk. In an Assyrian version of the Enuma Elish, Ashur replaces Marduk as the protagonist. The logogram AN.ŠÁR, originally the writing of Anshar's name, became the conventional writing of Ashur's name under Sennacherib.

Ancient sources: The Enuma Elish and god lists such as An = Anum, where he appears as the father of Anu.

Trivia

An astronomical pairing. In a late astronomical commentary, Kishar appears as the spouse of the star Gudanna, described as the "Enlil of Shuruppak".
Among the defeated. Anshar appears in lists of defeated primordial figures alongside Asag, Enmesharra, Qingu and others.
