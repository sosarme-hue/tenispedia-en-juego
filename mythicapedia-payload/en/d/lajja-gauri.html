Hindu goddess of abundance and fertility

Lajja Gauri

Lajja Gauri is a Hindu goddess associated with abundance, fertility and sexuality. Her name, Lajja, means modesty, and her image, with a lotus in place of her head, is one of the most unusual in India.

The myth

The sources offer no extensive narrative about her; her weight lies mainly in iconography. She is shown squatting with her legs open, as in childbirth, and her raised arms hold lotus stems.

Many identify her with Gauri, a form of Parvati, consort of Shiva. Over time she was linked to Shiva, given Shakti's lion as her vahana, and associated with Nandi the bull and the lingam.

Worship and religion

Her worship is prevalent in the Deccan and in tribal areas of central India. Childless women, couples and newlyweds visit temples such as the Bala Brahma in Telangana to seek fertility, offering ghee, powders and flowers and circling the sanctum. Maithili people invoke her at weddings.

Legacy

British archaeologists of the 19th century were shocked by her nude figures; today they are read as symbols of creation and modesty.

Ancient sources: Her earliest images appear on Indus Valley seals, and later depictions date to the 1st-3rd centuries; Vedic texts do not describe her.

Trivia

Kohbar paintings. Kohbar Mithila paintings include this goddess and are kept in the nuptial chamber of newlyweds.
Images at Badami. A sculpture of her, found in Bijapur, is kept in the archaeological museum of Badami, known for its cave temples.
