centaur seer of Greek mythology

Asbolus

Asbolus is a centaur from Greek mythology who read omens in the flight of birds. His story is tied to the battle between the centaurs and the Lapiths and to the death of several centaurs at the hands of Heracles.

The myth

According to the myth, Asbolus foresaw the centaurs' battle against the Lapiths at Pirithous's wedding and tried, without success, to keep them from attending. Later, when Heracles visited the centaur Pholus, Pholus opened a jar of wine that belonged to all the centaurs.

Asbolus saw this and called the others, who, as the wedding had shown, were not used to drinking. A bloodbath followed in which Pholus, Chiron and Nessus died at Heracles's hands. It is said that Asbolus himself was also struck down by the hero's arrows.

Legacy

The minor planet 8405 Asbolus, an object in the outer Solar System, bears his name. It belongs to the class of centaurs, whose orbits lie between Jupiter and Neptune.

Ancient sources: Hesiod calls him an oionistes, an augur who read omens in the flight of birds, and Ovid mentions him in the Metamorphoses when recounting the centaurs' battle.

Trivia

An asteroid named after him. The asteroid 8405 Asbolus keeps the centaur's name in the sky. It belongs to a group of bodies that orbit between Jupiter and Neptune.
A name meaning soot. The Greek name Asbolos means soot or carbon dust.
