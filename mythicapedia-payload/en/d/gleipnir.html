magic ribbon of Norse mythology

Gleipnir

Gleipnir is the magical ribbon with which the Norse gods bound the wolf Fenrir, a cord as thin as silk that no creature could break. It matters because it is the only bond able to hold the beast until Ragnarök.

The myth

According to the myth, the Æsir had already tried twice to bind Fenrir with heavy iron chains, called Leyding and Dromi, but the wolf tore them apart without effort. They then asked the dwarves to forge something that could never be broken. The dwarves made it from six impossible ingredients: the sound of a cat's steps, the beard of a woman, the roots of mountains, the sinews of a bear, the breath of a fish and the spittle of a bird. The tradition adds that these things vanished from the world to become part of the ribbon. The result looked light and silken, yet it was stronger than any iron chain.

Fenrir distrusted the gods. He agreed to take part in a test, presented as a game of strength, in which one of them had to place a hand in his mouth as a pledge. Only Tyr dared. When the wolf realized it was a trick and could not break free, he snapped his jaws shut and bit off the god's hand. The gods left him bound, and the tradition says Gleipnir will hold until Ragnarök, when Fenrir will break loose, kill Odin and in turn be slain by Vidar.

Ancient sources: It appears in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in chapter 34 of Gylfaginning.

Trivia

Vanished ingredients. The sources say the six ingredients of the ribbon ceased to exist in the world so that they could become part of it.
A disputed name. Its name is read as the entangler or the deceiver, and it has also been rendered as wolf lock.
