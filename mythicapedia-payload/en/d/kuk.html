Egyptian god of primordial darkness

Kek

Kek, also written Kuk or Keku, is the god who personifies primordial darkness in ancient Egyptian mythology. He belongs to the Ogdoad of Hermopolis, the group of eight deities involved in the creation of the world.

The myth

In the Ogdoad cosmogony of Hermopolis, before creation there was a primeval abyss of waters wrapped in darkness. Kek embodied that darkness and was paired with his female counterpart, Kauket, sometimes spelled Kuket or Keket. Together they stood for the male and female powers of the darkness that covered the abyss.

Over time his role extended to night and day. As a god of the night, he was also linked with light, and was called the raiser up of the light, since he was responsible for the hours of darkness that heralded the sunrise.

Legacy

The Egyptologist Heinrich Karl Brugsch compared Kek and Kauket with Erebus, the Greek personification of darkness. In modern times Kek returned to public attention through the internet. Users of message boards such as 4chan noticed his resemblance to Pepe the Frog, a comic frog that had become a meme, and the two were combined in images tied to the acronym KEK, a variant of LOL. After the 2016 United States presidential election, supporters of Donald Trump built a mock religion around him, The Cult of Kek, complete with a language, anthem, passport, currency and an imaginary country, Kekistan, said to cover Azerbaijan, Armenia, northern Iran and eastern Turkey.

Trivia

Comparison with Erebus. The Egyptologist Heinrich Karl Brugsch linked this divine pair with Erebus, the Greek god who personified darkness and gloom.
An imaginary country. Followers of the mock religion invented Kekistan, a fictional country with its own language, anthem, passport and currency.
