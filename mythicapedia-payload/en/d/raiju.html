lightning beast of Japanese folklore

Raijū

The raijū is a Japanese creature linked to lightning and thunder, companion of the Shinto god Raijin and classed as a yōkai. It became especially famous during the Edo period and remains part of modern pop culture.

The myth

According to tradition, the raijū is a beast made of lightning that can take many forms, from cats and badgers to whales, though it is most often pictured as a blue-white wolf or dog. It is generally calm and harmless, but during storms it grows agitated and leaps through trees, fields and even buildings. Trees marked by lightning were said to bear the scratches of its claws.

It is also said to hide in human navels. Raijin would then shoot lightning arrows to wake it, harming the person who sheltered it, so people slept face down during bad weather. Other tales say it only hides in the navels of those sleeping outdoors.

Edo-period essays record many episodes, such as a man in Akita who caught, boiled and ate one, or villagers who hunted down raijū that damaged their crops in a practice called "thunder hunting". One modern theory holds that the creature may be an attempt to explain phenomena such as ball lightning.

Legacy

The raijū appears in anime and manga, inspiring creatures such as Raikou, Raichu and Electrike in Pokémon, and giving its name to a shot in Captain Tsubasa. It also inspired the thunder god Raiden in Mortal Kombat and one of the kaiju in Pacific Rim.

Ancient sources: It appears in Edo-period essays and collections of supernatural tales, such as the Ehon Hyaku Monogatari and the Kasshi Yawa, and is linked to older Chinese sources such as the Shan Hai Jing.

Trivia

Thunder hunting. In the Kantō region, villagers hunted raijū that damaged their rice paddies, a practice they called "Kaminari-gari".
Corn as a remedy. According to Tani Bunchō, people left disoriented near a lightning strike could recover by eating powdered maize.
