Mexica god of the night and protector of children's sleep

Yohualtecuhtli

Yohualtecuhtli, whose Nahuatl name means "lord of the night", was the Mexica god of nocturnal darkness and the protector of children's sleep. He belongs to the Mesoamerican pantheon and is linked to his aspect Yacahuitztli, associated with sunset.

The myth

According to the sources, Yohualtecuhtli was a lord of the night hours, as colonial writers describe him. Yacahuitztli appears as one of his aspects, tied to dusk. Both deities seem to descend from common ancestors, suggesting a shared and ancient origin within the Mesoamerican pantheon.

Classical sources also link him to death, birth, cremation and the closing of time periods. In the tradition of Creation he is credited with a throne that was supposedly one of the three stones of the original heart. That heart, considered the centre of Maya houses and a reflection of the universe, is related to the stars below Orion's Belt, with which both deities are identified in that region, placed in the fifth layer of the sky.

Trivia

A heart of stars. According to this tradition, the stars below Orion's Belt formed a heart that served as an image of the model of the universe.
Two names, one figure. Yacahuitztli appears as one of the aspects of Yohualtecuhtli, associated with sunset.
