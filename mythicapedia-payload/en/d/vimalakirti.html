lay bodhisattva of Mahayana Buddhism

Vimalakirti

Vimalakirti is a lay bodhisattva and the central figure of the Vimalakirti Sutra, embodying the Mahayana ideal of the upasaka, or lay practitioner. He matters because, though a layman, his understanding of emptiness surpasses that of the Buddha's own disciples.

The myth

According to the sutra, Vimalakirti lived in the city of Vaishali, in what is now the Indian state of Bihar, as a wealthy patron of the Buddha Shakyamuni. His name means, literally, stainless fame.

The story shows him respectful toward everyone, the Buddha included, yet never intimidated by anyone. He describes himself as one of the Great Sorcerers (mahasiddhas) and capable of miracles to teach fellow practitioners. His main role is that of a master of skillful means, and his episodes, full of debates and paradoxes, show that wisdom does not depend on the rank of the one who teaches it. Scholars read his deeds as allegories.

Worship and religion

He is not commonly venerated on altars or in tantric rituals. He is honored mainly as a model of practice. Tradition places his home in Vaishali, a site the Chinese monk Xuanzang visited in the 7th century, when he was shown the house where Vimalakirti was said to have lived.

Legacy

In China, the sutra and its figure had a strong literary influence and were especially important for Chan (Zen) Buddhism. In Chan monasteries, the term fangzhang, used for the abbot, refers to the size of Vimalakirti's stone room, ten square feet. His image appears in the 8th-century wall paintings of Dunhuang and in reliefs at the Longmen Grottoes.

Ancient sources: The Vimalakirti Sutra (Vimalakirti Nirdesa Sutra), a Mahayana text that bears his name.

Trivia

Ten square feet. The term fangzhang, used in Chan monasteries for the abbot, means one square zhang, about ten square feet, a reference to Vimalakirti's stone room.
A pointed-out house. In the 7th century, the Chinese monk Xuanzang recorded that in Vaishali he was shown the house where Vimalakirti was said to have lived.
