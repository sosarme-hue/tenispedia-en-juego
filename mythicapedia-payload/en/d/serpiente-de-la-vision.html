sacred creature of Maya mythology

Vision Serpent

The Vision Serpent is a central creature of Pre-Columbian Maya mythology and the most important of the sacred serpents of that culture. It acted as a bridge between the world of the living and the spirit realm of gods and ancestors.

The myth

According to Maya tradition, serpents were the vehicles by which the sun and stars crossed the sky, and the shedding of their skin made them a symbol of rebirth. It was usually shown bearded, with a rounded snout, sometimes with two heads, and a god or ancestor emerged from its jaws.

During bloodletting rituals, participants had visions of a giant serpent that served as a gateway to the spirit realm. The smoke from the blood-soaked papers burned at the end of the rite made its appearance possible.

Worship and religion

It was placed at the centre of the world, atop the World Tree, and appeared in ceremonies, pottery, jewelry and architecture. Kings invoked their ancestors in coronation rites. According to J.E.S. Thompson, around 1930 the Q'eqchi' Maya of Belize still practised a similar ritual to initiate new shamans.

Trivia

One of the earliest dated stelae. The Hauberg Stela, dated to 199 AD in the Maya Lowlands, is one of the first dated monuments showing the serpent's link to bloodletting.
Och-Kan, lord of Kalak'mul. One of these serpents was named Och-Kan and was associated with Kalak'mul. The same name reappeared in the shaman initiation ritual observed in Belize.
