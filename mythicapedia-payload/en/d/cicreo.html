hero and legendary king of Salamis

Cychreus

Cychreus is a hero of Greek mythology, a legendary king of the island of Salamis and son of Poseidon. He is linked to a serpent and to the naval battle of Salamis, which made him an important figure in local and Athenian tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, Cychreus was the son of Poseidon and the nymph Salamis, daughter of the Oceanid Asopus. The myth says he killed a serpent that was plaguing the island, and the inhabitants proclaimed him king. Another version holds that he had raised that serpent himself, called Cychreides, and that it was Eurylochus, not Cychreus, who drove it away after the damage it caused on Salamis.

His court gave refuge to Telamon, who had murdered his brother Phocus and had been expelled from Aegina by Aeacus. Telamon married Glauce, daughter of Cychreus, and when Cychreus died without descendants he inherited the throne of Salamis. Another tradition names Cychreus's daughter Chariclo. Pherecydes of Leros, however, makes Telamon the son of Acteus and Glauce, which would make him Cychreus's grandson.

Worship and religion

Pausanias mentions a sanctuary dedicated to Cychreus on the island, built in gratitude for the help he gave the Greeks at the Battle of Salamis, since he was believed to have appeared as a serpent among the ships. Athenian tradition also recalls that Solon and Themistocles invoked him to reinforce claims over the island, though the sources give no detailed rites.

Legacy

The lexicographer Stephanus of Byzantium notes that Cychreus was known as the “serpent” for his irascible character, a nickname that reflects his image in tradition. His story was woven into the genealogy of Telamon and his son Ajax the Great, the hero of the Trojan War, through the line that passes via Glauce.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Pherecydes of Leros and Stephanus of Byzantium mention him, and Herodotus records the serpent's appearance at the battle of Salamis.

Trivia

Serpent nickname. Stephanus of Byzantium reports that Cychreus was known as “serpent” because of his irascible temper.
The serpent's name. The serpent was called Cychreides, according to a version in which Cychreus had raised it himself.
