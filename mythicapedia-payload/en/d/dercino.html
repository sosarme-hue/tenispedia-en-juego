son of Poseidon in Greek mythology

Dercynus

Dercynus is a son of Poseidon in Greek mythology and brother of Alebion. According to tradition, he fought Heracles in Liguria, in north-western Italy, while the hero was returning with the cattle of Geryon.

The myth

According to the myth, Heracles had taken the cattle of Geryon as his tenth labour and was crossing Liguria on his way back to Mycenae from Iberia. There the two brothers, Dercynus and Alebion, confronted him at the head of a large army.

The battle was fierce, and Heracles and his men found themselves in a difficult position. The hero prayed to his father Zeus, and with the aegis of the god he won the fight, though both brothers were killed. Some sources give the brother other names, such as Ligys, and call Dercynus Bergion or Beergios.

Legacy

The kneeling position Heracles adopted when he prayed to Zeus gave its name to the constellation of Hercules, called Engonasin in Greek, meaning "on his knees" or "the Kneeler".

Ancient sources: Aeschylus mentions him in his lost play Promētheus Lyomenos, and the story is also alluded to by Hyginus, Dionysius and Strabo.

Trivia

Other names. In some sources the brother is called Ligys, and Dercynus appears as Bergion or Beergios.
A constellation. The name Engonasin comes from the Greek phrase «en gónasin», meaning «on the knees».
