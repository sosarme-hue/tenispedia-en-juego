lunar king of Hindu myth

Nahusha

Nahusha is a king of the Lunar dynasty (Chandravamsha) who appears in the Puranas and the Mahabharata. His story mixes power, arrogance and punishment, and shows how easily a ruler can lose his dharma when authority turns into pride.

The myth

According to tradition, Nahusha was the son of Āyu, the eldest son of Pururavas, and of Prabha, daughter of the asura Rahu. Āyu had no children, so he asked the sage Dattatreya for a son. The child was born, but the asura Hunda tried to kill him, fearing a curse Ashokasundari had laid on him. Hunda's minions left the baby at the hermitage of the sage Vashistha, who raised him and named him Nahusha, "the fearless one". As a young man Nahusha killed Hunda, returned to his parents and married Ashokasundari. According to the Padma Purana, he was the father of Yayati, who succeeded him on the throne, while his eldest son Yati became an ascetic.

While Indra was away fighting Vritra, Nahusha ruled Svarga. He grew arrogant and wanted Shachi, Indra's wife, for himself. He made the seven Vedic sages (Saptarishi) carry his palanquin to her mansion, urging them on with the word sarpa (move on, move on). The sage Agastya, outraged, turned him into a serpent, and Nahusha fell from the sky.

In the Mahabharata, during the Pandavas' exile, the serpent Nahusha seizes Bhima. A boon from Agastya meant that anyone stronger than him lost their strength on touching him, so Bhima could not overpower him. Yudhishthira comes looking for his brother, and the two discuss dharma. Nahusha admits his own mistakes, is freed from the curse and goes to heaven, and Bhima regains his strength.

Legacy

Nahusha also appears in Sister Nivedita's story "The Worth of Kine", in which fishermen catch the sage Chyavana in their net along with the fish and bring him before King Nahusha, who offers a cow in exchange for the sage.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda, the Mahabharata, the Harivamsha, the Vayu Purana and the Padma Purana.

Trivia

A boon against strength. According to the Mahabharata, the sage Agastya granted Nahusha a boon that drained the strength of anyone stronger than him who touched him.
A cow for a sage. In Nivedita's story, King Nahusha pays the fishermen with a cow in exchange for the sage Chyavana.
