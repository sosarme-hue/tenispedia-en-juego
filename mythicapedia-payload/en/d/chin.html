Maya god linked to relations between men

Chin

Chin is a male deity of Maya mythology, named alongside Cu, Cavil and Maran. Colonial sources connect him with sexual relations between men, a practice they describe as part of the customs of some Maya peoples.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded in the sources, Chin had sexual relations with other male deities and with humans.

Bishop Bartolomé de las Casas, describing the Maya of Verapaz in the sixteenth century, writes that Chin had relations with another demon and so introduced such practices. The same chronicler notes that relations between unmarried young men and boys were regulated by customary law and also occurred among adolescents receiving instruction in the temples.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples, festivals or offerings dedicated specifically to Chin. They do record that among the Itza of Petén, Spanish reports of the seventeenth century documented institutionalized pederastic prostitution, including transvestism.

Ancient sources: Bishop Bartolomé de las Casas, a sixteenth-century chronicler, is the main colonial reference on Chin and the customs associated with his name.

Trivia

A cave with Classic scenes. The Naj Tunich cave contains Classic Period scenes showing a naked, sexually excited male creature embracing a nude Maya nobleman, possibly as part of an initiation.
Companions in name. Chin is named alongside Cu, Cavil and Maran. Cavil is glossed as 'idol' and likely refers to K'awiil.
