Greek personification of Vice

Kakia

Kakia, also written Kakía or Kakiá, is the Greek personification of Vice, set against Virtue (Arete). She is best known for trying to seduce Heracles at the crossroads, which makes her a classic symbol of temptation.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Xenophon, when Heracles leaves childhood and enters adolescence, he sits in a quiet place to decide which of two paths to take. Two women appear before him: Virtue, sober and dressed in white, who walks calmly, and Kakia, plump, heavily made up and coquettish, who runs towards him and promises an easy life full of pleasures, without labour or hardship.

When the young man asks her name, she replies that her friends call her Happiness, but her enemies call her Wickedness. In a different tradition, the Gnostic one, she is the child of the first angel and Authadia, with siblings such as Zelos, Erinys or Epithymia.

Ancient sources: Xenophon recounts the crossroads episode of Heracles, and the Greek word for "evil" appears in the New Testament (Matthew 6:34).
