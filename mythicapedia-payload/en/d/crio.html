Greek Titan, father of Perses

Crius

Crius is one of the Titans of Greek mythology, son of Uranus and Gaia, and his name means "ram". He matters mainly as the father of Perses and the grandfather of Hecate, although in the myths his role is nearly anonymous.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Crius was one of the Titans, children of Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth). He took as his wife Eurybia, daughter of Pontus, and with her he fathered Astraeus, Pallas and Perses, the last of whom was renowned for his wisdom. Through Perses, Crius became the grandfather of Hecate, a figure who clearly fascinated Hesiod himself.

The stories present him as a barely individualized figure. According to M. L. West's reading, Hesiod added him to complete the number of Titans, alongside other figures drawn from older traditions. Not even his female patronymic, Crieida, was preserved. In some accounts his descendants belong more to the race of giants than to second-generation Titans. Astraeus, with Eos, fathered the stars, the winds, Eosphorus, Hesperus and Astraea, and his grandchildren include Boreas, Zephyrus and Notus.

Crius took part in the Titanomachy, the ten-year war between the Olympian gods and the Titans, though without any specific role. After the Titans were defeated, he was banished with his brothers to Tartarus, the deepest part of the underworld, and from then on he vanishes from the story.

In the Homeric Hymns he is called Megamedes, "great lord", at least as the father of Pallas. Pausanias mentions other people named Crius without clarifying whether they are the same figure: a river near Pelene bears the name in his honour, and according to him the serpent Python was his daughter, while another Crius ruled in Boeotia as a man of authority.

Legacy

His name, meaning "ram", led the poet Nonnus to identify him with the constellation Aries. The scholar Cornutus, for his part, linked the name to the authority of the shepherd over his flocks, an allegorical reading that shows how ancient interpreters tried to explain his name.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony and the Homeric Hymns; also Nonnus, Cornutus and Pausanias.

Trivia

A name that is an animal. Crius means "ram" in Greek. Nonnus linked him with the constellation Aries, although the myth does not place him in the sky.
A serpent among his children. Pausanias says that the serpent Python was the daughter of a Crius, linking him to another strand of the myth.
