epithet of the goddess Athena in Arcadia

Athena Alea

Alea was an epithet of the Greek goddess Athena, closely tied to Arcadia, where she was worshipped at Alea, Mantineia and Tegea. She may have started as an independent local goddess later assimilated to Athena.

The myth

According to tradition, the oldest temple of Athena Alea, at Tegea, was built by Aleos, son of Aphidas, from whom the goddess probably took her epithet. This sanctuary burned in 394 BCE and was rebuilt to a design by the architect Scopas, in the Doric order and surrounded by a triple row of columns. Its statue, made by Endoios, was entirely of ivory; Augustus later moved it to Rome to adorn his forum.

The temple offered asylum, and records list many people who escaped persecution by taking refuge there. A statue of the goddess also stood on the road from Sparta to Therapne.

Worship and religion

Her priestess at Tegea had to be a virgin, serving only until she reached puberty. A local festival, the Aleaia, was held in honour of Alea. According to Pausanias, the custom of calling the goddess Alea persisted at Tegea.

Ancient sources: Herodotus mentions the Temple of Alea Athena, and Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, describes the festival of the Aleaia.
