heroine of Greek mythology, mother of Byzas

Ceroessa

Ceroessa is a heroine of Greek mythology tied to the founding myth of Byzantium. Daughter of Io and Zeus, she is the mother of Byzas, the man credited with founding the city, and her name, meaning "the one of the horn," refers to the Golden Horn.

The myth

According to the myth, Io, turned into a heifer and pursued by a gadfly sent by Hera, was passing through Thrace when she gave birth to a girl on the banks of the Golden Horn, beside the altar of the nymph Semystra. The nymph raised the girl, who grew up more beautiful than the other maidens of the place. The historian Hesychius of Miletus places her birth in Thrace, while Nonnus says she was born in Egypt, like her brother Epaphus.

Ceroessa had a relationship with Poseidon, and their son Byzas, whom later tradition makes the founder of Byzantium, named the Golden Horn after his mother. According to the story, Byzas founded the city on the site of today's Sarayburnu. Ceroessa also bore Strombos, who fought against his brother and the Byzantines.

Ancient sources: Hesychius of Miletus and Nonnus of Panopolis; the Etymologicum Magnum calls her Creusa, by mistake.

Trivia

A name with a horn. Her name means "the one of the horn," and the myth links her to the Golden Horn, the arm of the sea where, according to tradition, she was born.
A mistaken name. The Etymologicum Magnum calls her Creusa, and the text itself notes this as a misnomer for her name.
