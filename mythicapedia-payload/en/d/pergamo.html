legendary hero of Greek myth, son of Neoptolemus and Andromache

Pergamus

Pergamus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Neoptolemus and Andromache. He matters mainly because of the legend that linked his name to the city of Pergamon in Asia Minor.

The myth

According to the tradition, Pergamus was the son of Neoptolemus, son of Achilles, and of Andromache, widow of Hector. After the Trojan War, Neoptolemus took Andromache to Epirus. After his death, some sources say she returned to Asia Minor with her youngest son.

A later version, probably created in the Kingdom of Pergamon, says the young man killed the king of Teuthrania, founded the city and ruled there. This story is usually seen as an invention linking the Attalid dynasty to Alexander the Great.

Worship and religion

A small heroon in Pergamon was dedicated to Pergamos Ktistes, probably built in the 3rd century BC.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the city of Pergamon and, in the Bible, appears as the seat of one of the seven churches of Asia.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad names his parents and the citadel of Troy called Pergamus; the story of the son comes later.

Trivia

A name with several uses. Pergamus also names the citadel of Troy in Homer's Iliad, a use separate from the hero.
A biblical church. The King James Version uses the form Pergamos for one of the seven churches of Asia, now called Pergamon.
