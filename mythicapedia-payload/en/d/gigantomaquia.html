battle of the Olympian gods against the giants in Greek mythology

Gigantomachy

The Gigantomachy is the great battle between the Olympian gods and the giants, earth-born beings who, according to Greek tradition, tried to storm the heavens. It is one of the most frequently depicted episodes in Greek art, and it explains the origin of the volcanoes where the defeated giants were said to lie buried.

The myth

According to Hesiod, the giants were born from Gaia (Earth) when the blood fell from Uranus after Cronus castrated him. Other accounts make them the children of Gaia and Tartarus. They were immensely strong, with thick hair and beards and, depending on the version, serpent or scaly feet. Their birthplace is placed at Phlegra, the «burning land», or at Palene, and from there they attacked the sky, hurling boulders and tree trunks.

The gods learned from a prophecy that they could not defeat the giants without the help of a mortal. Gaia then sought a herb that would make the giants invulnerable to men, but Zeus destroyed the substance before anyone could use it and summoned Heracles with the help of Athena. In the battle, Zeus struck down the fleeing giants with thunderbolts, and Heracles finished the dying with his arrows. The defeated giants were buried beneath volcanic islands, which in the tradition explains earthquakes and eruptions.

After the victory, the furious Gaia joined with Tartarus and gave birth to Typhon. The gods established their seat at Mecone, the old name of Sicyon, where they first divided their honours.

Legacy

The Gigantomachy was one of the most frequently depicted subjects in Greek art, and the frieze of the Pergamon Altar is one of its best-known examples. In later tradition the giants were confused with the Titans and with Typhon, so the story merged with other struggles against the Olympians.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony), Homer (Odyssey), Pindar, Apollodorus and Hyginus, among others.

Trivia

A borrowed ankle bone. According to one account, Chiron exhumed the body of the swift giant Damastes and removed the astragalus from his foot, which he implanted in the heel of Achilles.
Mecone. According to tradition, the gods gathered at Mecone, the old name of Sicyon, after the war, and it was there that Prometheus first tried to outwit Zeus.
