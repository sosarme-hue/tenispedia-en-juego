chief of the Eleans in Greek mythology

Amarynceus

Amarynceus was a chief of the Eleans, the people of Elis, in Greek mythology. He matters mainly as the father of Diores, one of the Elean leaders who fought in the Trojan War.

The myth

The sources disagree about his parentage. Some name his father as Alector (also called Acetor), others as a certain Onesimachus, a name of doubtful reliability. His mother was said to be Diogeneia, daughter of Phorbas and granddaughter of Lapithes. Pausanias gives a different account, making his father Pyttius, a Thessalian immigrant who is otherwise unknown.

Amarynceus married Mnesimache, daughter of King Dexamenus of Olenus, and their son Diores, known in Homer's patronymic form as Amarynceides, fought at Troy. Another son, Hippostratus, was said to have tried to seduce Periboea. Hyginus, in a corrupt passage, has Amarynceus himself join the war from Mycenae with nineteen ships.

According to Pausanias, Amarynceus defended the city against Heracles, and Augeas, in gratitude, shared his throne with him. After his death, his sons held funeral games in his honour, in which the young Nestor took part.

Ancient sources: Homer refers to Diores by the patronymic Amarynceides in the Iliad, while Hyginus and Pausanias preserve other episodes of his story.
