Chinese god of wealth

Zhao Gongming

Zhao Gongming is the Chinese god of wealth and the central figure of the Five Roads system. He is the most widely worshipped form of Caishen and is recognised by his armoured, martial appearance, unlike the civil gods of fortune.

The myth

According to tradition, his figure took shape over centuries. In the earliest versions he was a demon-marshal who caused plagues; the Soushen Ji, from the Six Dynasties period, counts him among three generals sent by the Heavenly Emperor to retrieve human souls.

In the Ming novel Fengshen Yanyi he is a Taoist hermit who dies in battle and is deified by Jiang Ziya as Lord of the Dark Altar, with four attendants who attract and guard riches.

Worship and religion

He is venerated in temples across China, Hong Kong and Taiwan, and his birthday, the fifteenth day of the third lunar month, draws pilgrims to Mount Tiantai. The cult remains alive today.

Legacy

His image is part of Chinese New Year celebrations, and in Changli a street keeps the name of his temple, Cai Shen Temple Street.

Ancient sources: The Soushen Ji, a compendium from the Six Dynasties period, and the Ming novel Fengshen Yanyi.

Trivia

A sun turned to stone. According to a late tradition, a sun fell onto Mount Tiantai and became a stone, the Sun Stone, from which his essence later emerged.
A street named for his temple. In Changli, Cai Shen Temple Street grew up around a temple dedicated to the god of wealth and still keeps his cult in memory.
