Zeus's eagle, childhood companion of the god

Aetos

Aetos is the sacred bird of Zeus, king of the gods and lord of thunder in Greek mythology. According to tradition, he was an earth-born youth who was turned into an eagle and became the emblem of the god.

The myth

According to the myth, Aetos was a beautiful youth born from the earth on Crete. While Zeus was hidden on the island to escape his father Cronus, who devoured his children, Aetos befriended the young god and was the first to swear loyalty to him as the new king.

Years later, after Zeus had overthrown Cronus, Hera transformed Aetos into an eagle out of fear that Zeus loved him. The bird then served the god: it helped Zeus during the Titanomachy and the Gigantomachy by placing lightning bolts in his hands, and it was credited with abducting a youth, as Zeus did with Ganymede. In another version, the eagle also tore at Prometheus's liver each day until Heracles shot it during his eleventh labour.

Legacy

Aetos gave its name to the eagle as the emblem of Zeus, a symbol of power and kingship linked to the god's throne and sceptre.
