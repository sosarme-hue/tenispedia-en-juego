Revanta

Revanta, also called Raivata, is a minor deity of Hinduism and a son of the sun-god Surya according to the Rig-Veda. He is mainly honoured as the lord of the guhyakas and as a protector of horses, warriors and hunters.

The myth

According to the Rig-Veda, Revanta is the youngest son of Surya and his wife Saranyu. The Puranas tell his birth differently: the Vishnu Purana and the Markandeya Purana say that Saranyu, unable to bear her husband's blazing heat, fled to the forests as a mare and left her shadow, Chhaya, in her place. When Surya discovered the deception, he searched for her and united with her in the form of a horse, and the twin Ashvins and Revanta were born. Other texts, such as the Kurma and Matsya Puranas, name Ratri as his mother instead.

The Markandeya Purana adds that Surya made him chief of the guhyakas and charged him with protecting mortals from the perils of forests, great fires and robbers. In a tale from the Devi Bhagavata Purana, he rides towards Vishnu's abode, where Lakshmi is so captivated by his horse that she ignores her husband, and is cursed to become a mare.

Worship and religion

His worship was especially widespread in medieval eastern India, particularly in Bihar and Bengal, and archaeological finds point to a cult dating back to the 6th century CE. He was venerated alongside Surya, following sun-worship rituals, and was linked to the Saura sect. Homage was paid to him in the Hindu month of Ashvin, especially by warriors.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rig-Veda and several Puranas, including the Markandeya, Vishnu, Kalika and Devi Bhagavata Puranas.

Trivia

Seven-headed horse. In the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Revanta rides Uchaishravas, a horse with seven heads.
Long boots. Unlike most Hindu deities, except Surya, he is often shown wearing long boots reaching the calves.
