Mexica god of trade and merchants

Yacatecuhtli

Yacatecuhtli is, in Aztec mythology, the patron god of commerce and of merchants and travelers, especially those who journeyed long distances to trade goods. He belongs to the group of elder gods and is recognised by his symbol, the “bundle of sticks,” and by a prominent nose.

The myth

Tradition presents him as the protector of those who set out to trade. His name means “lord of the nose,” from the Nahuatl yacatl, nose, and tecuhtli, lord. The nose carries a symbolic meaning: since it always runs ahead of the body, it leads the traveler forward. The name can therefore also be read figuratively as “lord of the vanguard,” because a vanguard goes ahead of the main body.

The bundle of sticks ties in with the daily life of the merchants. They carried a cane as they moved from village to village selling their wares, and at nightfall they gathered the canes into a neat bundle. That gesture linked ordinary work with the god’s protection against wild beasts and robbers on the road.

Worship and religion

The Aztecs regarded Yacatecuhtli as a god whose favour had to be secured, and they offered him slaves as sacrifices to satisfy him. Merchants also observed a rite in his honour: they tied their sticks into a bundle and sprinkled it with blood from their ears, in the belief that this guaranteed success in future business ventures. The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to him, and do not say whether his cult survives today.

Trivia

Lord of the nose. His name combines the Nahuatl words yacatl, nose, and tecuhtli, lord. The nose, which always goes in front of the body, symbolised the guidance of the traveler.
The bundle of sticks. Merchants tied their travelling canes into a bundle at the end of the day, and this gesture became the god’s symbol.
