Welsh god of youth and son of Modron

Mabon ap Modron

Mabon is a figure of Welsh mythology, the divine son of Modron and Gwyn ap Nudd, linked to youth, hunting and fishing. He is also a member of King Arthur's war band in medieval Welsh literature.

The myth

According to Welsh tradition, Mabon was stolen from his mother's arms when he was only three nights old and held prisoner in Gloucester. His story appears mainly in the medieval tale Culhwch and Olwen, where Arthur's men question the world's oldest animals until they reach the salmon of Llyn Llyw, which carries them downstream to the prison. There Cei and Bedwyr rescue him while the rest of the warriors attack the front.

Later he is needed to fulfil one of the impossible tasks set by the giant Ysbaddaden: hunting the boar Twrch Trwyth, since only he can handle the hound Drudwyn, the only dog able to track the beast.

Worship and religion

His cult was centred in north-western Britain, along the line of Hadrian's Wall, where Romano-Celtic inscriptions mention him. He was equated with Apollo because of his healing powers.

Legacy

His figure has been linked to continental Arthurian characters such as Mabonagrain and Mabuz, and he is also identified with Pryderi and with the Arthurian figure Mabon fab Mellt.

Ancient sources: He appears in the poem Pa Gur, from around the tenth century, and in the medieval tales Culhwch and Olwen and The Dream of Rhonabwy.

Trivia

Name with meaning. Mabon derives from Maponos, meaning "Great Son", and Modron from Mātronā, meaning "Great Mother".
