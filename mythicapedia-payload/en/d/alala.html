Greek goddess of the war cry

Alala

Alala was the personification of the war cry in Greek mythology and, according to Pindar, the daughter of Polemos, war itself. Her name comes from the onomatopoeic Greek word alalḗ, and she survives more as an allegory than as a figure of stories.

The myth

Alala has no narrative of her own: no myth recounts her origin or deeds. She is known mainly through her parentage, which Pindar gives as daughter of Polemos. He is the only ancient author to address her as a deity, calling her the prelude to spears, to whom men offer a holy sacrifice of death on behalf of their city.

Worship and religion

Alala had no temples or festivals of her own. Her role was tied to military practice: Greek soldiers raised the cry as they charged, aiming to spread panic through the enemy ranks. Athenians were said to have adopted it to imitate the hoot of the owl, the bird of Athena. Ares bore the epithet Alalaxios, leader of the war chorus. Her cult did not survive.

Legacy

The word lived on as a military shout. In August 1917 Gabriele D'Annunzio devised the Italian cry 'Eja, eja, alalà', blending the Greek call with a Sardinian shout to replace what he considered the barbaric 'Hip! Hip! Hurrah!'. Aviators used it during the First World War, and the Fascist movement later adopted it. In 1926 the young Polish poet Artur Maria Swinarski titled a collection of poems with the word.

Ancient sources: Pindar is the only ancient author who names her as a deity.

Trivia

A war cry with an owl's call. Athenians were said to have adopted the cry to imitate the hoot of the owl, the bird of their goddess Athena.
