Etruscan god of the underworld, fire and oracles

Śuri

Śuri was an Etruscan chthonic and solar deity, lord of the underworld, of volcanic fire and of divination. His cult spread to other Italic peoples and was eventually absorbed into Roman religion under the name Soranus.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Śuri was the son of the supreme sky god Tinia and the earth goddess Semla, and the brother of Fufluns. He was credited with power over the sun, lightning, healing, plague and divination. His partner was the goddess Cavatha, linked to the moon and the underworld.

Texts and images present him in different forms: a crowned young man with a spear or bow, a bearded man in a wolf-skin cap, or a winged monstrous figure. His sacred animals were the wolf and the goat. Some sources also associate him with Manth, an ancient infernal god, and with Charun as guide of the dead.

Worship and religion

His best-known sanctuary was at Pyrgi, the sacred harbour of Caere, where he was worshipped alongside Cavatha between the late 6th and 4th centuries BC. Lead offerings, astragali used for casting lots and bronze ram heads have been found there. On Mount Soracte, the Faliscans honoured him as Soranus, served by the Hirpi Sorani, priests who walked over glowing embers unharmed.

Ancient sources: The Etruscans mention him on the Pyrgi Tablets and the Liver of Piacenza. Virgil names him Apollo Soractis in the Aeneid.
