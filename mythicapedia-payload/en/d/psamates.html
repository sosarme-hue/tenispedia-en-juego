daughter of King Crotopus and lover of Apollo

Psamathe

Psamathe is, in Greek mythology, a daughter of King Crotopus of Argos who became the lover of Apollo. Her story is tied to the birth of Linus and to a plague that struck the city.

The myth

According to the mythographer Conon, Psamathe became pregnant by Apollo and abandoned the newborn Linus out of fear of her father. A shepherd raised the foundling, but one day the shepherd's dogs tore the child apart. Meanwhile, Crotopus ordered his daughter killed. Apollo avenged her death by sending a plague upon Argos.

When consulted, Apollo demanded that Psamathe and Linus be honored with due rites and festivities. The Argives complied, but the plague persisted, and by oracular decree the king was forced to leave and found the city of Tripodiscium near Megara, where he would end his days.

In an alternative version recorded by Pausanias, Psamathe exposed the child, which was torn apart by the king's sheepdogs, and Apollo sent Poena, the personification of punishment, against the city. She snatched children from their mothers until the hero Coroebus slew her. Pausanias adds that Apollo then struck Argos with a plague; Coroebus went to Delphi to ask that the punishment fall only on him, and following the oracle he carried a tripod until it fell, and there he founded a town.

Ancient sources: Conon and Pausanias in the Greek tradition; a version also appears in the work of Statius.
