Hindu deity in monkey form, devoted companion of Rama

Hanuman

Hanuman is a Hindu deity with the form of a monkey (vanara), a devoted companion of the god Rama and a central figure of the Ramayana. He is venerated as a model of strength, learning and devotion, and remains one of the most popular deities in India today.

The myth

According to tradition, Hanuman was born to Anjana, a woman who had been turned into a monkey by a curse that would only be lifted if she gave birth to an incarnation of Shiva. His legal father was Kesari, chief of the vanaras. The Valmiki Ramayana also presents him as the direct son of Vayu, the wind god, which is why he is called Vayuputra or Pavanputra.

The best-known story of his childhood tells how, hungry, he mistook the sun for a fruit and leaped towards it. Indra struck him with his thunderbolt and broke his jaw, and his name is usually explained as meaning he who has a disfigured jaw, although its origin remains uncertain. Angered, Vayu withdrew the air from the world until the gods pacified him.

The texts differ on his lineage. In some Shaiva traditions he is an incarnation of Shiva, while in most Vaishnava traditions he is the son and incarnation of Vayu. As a child he was taught the sutras by the sun god Surya, and tradition credits him with mastery of grammar, the four Vedas and the six shastras, yet he is famously humble about his learning.

In the Ramayana, Rama, exiled to the forest with Sita and Lakshmana, sets out to find Sita, abducted by the demon Ravana. Hanuman enters Rama's service, leaps across the sea to Sri Lanka, finds Sita and later fights beside Rama against Ravana. Tradition remembers him as a lifelong celibate and as a chiranjivi, an immortal destined to remain on earth until the end of the current age.

Worship and religion

Hanuman is a secondary figure in temples dedicated to Rama and also has shrines of his own, which are often full of monkeys. Across India he is shown as a monkey with a red face, standing upright like a human being. His worship and theological importance developed mainly during the second millennium CE, about a thousand years after the Ramayana was composed, and in recent times veneration through images and temple worship has grown considerably. He is considered a model of devotion (bhakti) and an example of self-control, faith and commitment to a cause. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

Figures of the Bhakti movement, such as Samarth Ramdas, portrayed him as an emblem of nationalism and resistance to oppression. Later literature has presented him as the patron of martial arts, meditation and scholarly pursuits. Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, from the sixteenth century, made him the ideal devotee, and his hymn the Hanuman Chalisa is recited throughout the country.

Ancient sources: The Valmiki Ramayana, the Mahabharata and several Puranas recount his stories; hymn 10.86 of the Rig Veda mentions a divine monkey, regarded as the earliest reference.

Trivia

A broken jaw. In the childhood story, Indra struck him with his thunderbolt after he mistook the sun for a fruit, and his name is said to come from the wound to his jaw.
Prodigious memory. Tradition says he memorized the sutras taught to him by the sun god in just 60 hours.
