Lydian giant of Greek mythology

Damasen

Damasen is a giant, son of Gaia (Earth), who appears in a Lydian story preserved by the late antique poet Nonnus of Panopolis. His tale is almost unknown outside Nonnus's work, which makes him a marginal figure in the Greek repertoire.

The myth

According to Nonnus, Damasen was born from Gaia, who bore him without a father. From birth he was armed: Eris, the goddess of discord, nursed him, lances took the place of the breast and armour of swaddling clothes, and Eileithyia gave him a shield. He grew so huge that his head seemed to reach the sky.

The central episode pits him against a poisonous serpent. When the hero Tylus is fatally bitten, his sister Moria asks Damasen to avenge him. The giant uproots a tree and fights the snake, which coils around him and spits venom into his face. Damasen throws it off and crushes it with the trunk. Then a female serpent fetches the flower of Zeus, a painkilling herb, and lays it on the dead snake's lips, bringing it back to life. Moria obtains the same flower and revives Tylus.

Nonnus joins Tylus, Moria and Damasen in one story, but only the giant seems to be his own invention. Manes, another figure mentioned elsewhere, may be an anagram of his name.

Legacy

In Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson series, in The House of Hades, Percy and Annabeth meet Damasen in Tartarus, and the giant cures Percy of gorgon blood.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis, in his Dionysiaca, the only ancient source that mentions him.

Trivia

Born armed. According to Nonnus, Damasen was born with a spear and received a shield from Eileithyia, goddess of childbirth.
A giant in Tartarus. Rick Riordan brought him into the Percy Jackson series, where he appears in Tartarus.
