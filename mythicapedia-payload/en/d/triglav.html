chief god of the Pomeranian Slavs

Triglav

Triglav, whose name means «three-headed one», was the chief god of the Pomeranian Slavs and probably of some Polabian Slavs. He matters because his cult is documented in written sources shortly before it was suppressed.

The myth

The surviving texts do not record myths, genealogies or deeds of Triglav, so his mythical story cannot be reconstructed with confidence. The only thing that follows with certainty is his name, which points to three heads, although the sources do not explain how he was depicted.

What we know about him concerns mainly his place in the religion of the peoples who worshipped him: he was the most important deity of his regional pantheon, and his cult lasted until it was suppressed. The information is therefore more historical than narrative, describing a god recognised across several cities rather than a tale.

Worship and religion

His cult was practised in Szczecin, Wolin and probably Brenna, today Brandenburg. Several biographies of St Otto of Bamberg, written in the years immediately before 1127, attest to this veneration and to the suppression the bishop carried out that year. Because these texts come from the bishop's circle, they describe the cult from the outside. The sources do not detail temples, priests or festivals, and there is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Latin biographies of St Otto of Bamberg, from the years before 1127, which mention his cult and its suppression.
