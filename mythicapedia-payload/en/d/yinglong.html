winged dragon and rain deity of Chinese mythology

Yinglong

Yinglong is a winged dragon and rain deity in ancient Chinese mythology, linked to myths of the early sovereigns and to the struggle against great floods. His name means "responsive dragon", and he is one of the oldest dragon figures in China.

The myth

According to the stories, Yinglong served the Yellow Emperor, Huang Di, and appears in his battles against Chiyou, the war god, and the giant Kua Fu. Some versions say Yinglong killed both of them.

In the flood myth, Yinglong helped King Yu control the waters: sometimes by drawing lines on the ground to show where drainage channels should be dug, other times by using his tail to carve out long channels that drained the Yellow River. He is also linked to rain and drought, and to the pair Fuxi and Nüwa, whom texts show riding a chariot pulled by two winged dragons.

Legacy

Yinglong appears in many classical Chinese texts, such as the Huainanzi and the Guangya, which describe him as one of the principal dragons, distinguished by having wings. His image has persisted in the depiction of winged Chinese dragons.

Ancient sources: The Chuci (Songs of Chu), the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the Huainanzi and the Guangya.

Trivia

A name with a double reading. The name combines ying, meaning to respond, with lóng, dragon. Although ying can also be read as "should", the correct meaning is "responsive dragon".
A dragon that gives birth to animals. The Huainanzi says Yinglong was the progenitor of the quadrupeds, and that other dragons gave rise to birds and other winged beings.
