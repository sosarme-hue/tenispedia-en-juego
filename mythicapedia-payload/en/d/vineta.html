legendary city of the Baltic Sea said to lie beneath the waves

Vineta

Vineta is a legendary city on the southern Baltic coast, sometimes identified with a major medieval Slavic trading center, which according to legend sank beneath the sea as punishment for its residents' excessive way of life. It is one of the best-known sea myths of northern Europe and has inspired many works.

The myth

There are several versions of the legend, but they share the same pattern: the Vinetans lead an excessive, voluptuous or blasphemous life, and a flood sinks the city to the bottom of the Baltic. In some versions the city, or part of it, reappears on certain days or can be seen from a boat, making the warning more tangible for the listener.

The story seems to grow out of traditions about a medieval emporium called Jumne, Jomsborg or Julin, with which Vineta is sometimes identified. Old chronicles describe its wealth and large population, but not its sinking, which belongs to popular storytelling.

Legacy

The myth inspired poems by Wilhelm Müller, Heinrich Heine and Ferdinand Freiligrath, an opera by Jan Nepomuk Škroup, and novels such as E. Werner's Vineta and Günter Grass's The Flounder. It also gives its name to a street and a subway station in Berlin, an ocean planet in the video game Signalis, and a track in Wipeout Pure.

Ancient sources: Medieval chronicles by Adam of Bremen and Helmold of Bosau mention the emporium of Jumne, and the Nordic Knytlinga saga recounts the siege of Jomsborg.
