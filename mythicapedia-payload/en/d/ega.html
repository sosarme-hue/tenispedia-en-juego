nymph or goat who nursed Zeus in Crete

Ega

Ega (also Ege or Aix) is, in Greek mythology, a nymph or, in other versions, a goat that nursed the infant Zeus while he was hidden in a cave on Crete. Her story links to the origin of the aegis and to a star in the constellation Auriga.

The myth

Accounts differ on Ega's family. Some name her as a daughter of Olenus or of Helios, sister of Helice and, for certain authors, wife of Pan and mother of Aegipan. In one Cretan tradition, Rhea hid the young Zeus on Crete and a goat, Amalthea, nursed him. In other tellings, Ega herself raised the child, after the Titans hid her in a cave on the island because of her dreadful appearance.

When Zeus prepared for war against the Titans, he was advised to protect himself with a goatskin and the head of a Gorgon. After his victory, he covered the goat's remaining bones with a hide and set them among the stars.

Legacy

Her name survives in Capella, the brightest star in the constellation Auriga, whose Latin name means "little she-goat". The kids of the myth appear in the same constellation, close to that star.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Astronomy and Eratosthenes mention her, alongside Aratus, Strabo and Antoninus Liberalis, and Homer's Iliad names cities that some say took their names from these nymphs.

Trivia

Two myths in one. Some critics think the story combines two separate submyths: an astronomical one tied to the star Capella, and another about the goat that raised Zeus on Crete.
A name with wind in it. Her name is linked both to aix, "goat", and to aix, "gale", which fed the idea of storms associated with the star.
