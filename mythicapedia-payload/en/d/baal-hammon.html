chief god of Carthage

Baal Hammon

Baal Hammon was the chief god of Carthage, the Phoenician colony in North Africa. As a weather god, he was believed to be responsible for the fertility of vegetation and was revered as king of the gods, and the Greeks and Romans equated him with Cronus and Saturn.

The myth

Baal Hammon, meaning "Lord Hammon", was depicted as a bearded older man with curling ram's horns. The origin of the name Hammon is uncertain. It has been linked to Amun, the god of ancient Libya whose oracle stood at the Siwa Oasis, though that connection is not secure. His cult partner was Tanit.

Scholars have identified him in various ways, with the Semitic god El or with Dagon. His symbols were the crescent moon and the solar disc, and some writers have tied him to the sun while others associate him with the moon.

Worship and religion

His worship flourished in Carthage and other Phoenician colonies, and his supremacy among the Carthaginian gods is believed to date to the fifth century BC, after relations with Tyre broke off around the Battle of Himera. In North Africa he was also venerated as Baal Qarnaim, "Lord of the Two Horns", in an open-air sanctuary on Jebel Boukornine, in Tunisia. Greek and Roman sources describe child sacrifices made in his name, a point scholars still debate, since the remains found in the Carthage tophet can be read in different ways.

Legacy

In Roman times he was identified with Saturn, and cultural exchange between Rome and Carthage may have influenced the festival of Saturnalia. Today his name survives in some Tunisian first names, in a street in modern Carthage, and in the Andalusian town of Carmona, which is believed to derive its name from Kar-Hammon, "city of Hammon".

Ancient sources: Philo of Byblos's successor Philo of Alexandria calls him Amunus, and Greek and Roman authors record the child sacrifices offered to him.

Trivia

Hill of the two horns. The sanctuary at Jebel Boukornine, near Carthage, takes its name from the hill, which worshippers linked to the god's horns.
A biblical vineyard. The Song of Songs mentions a place called Baal Hamon, with a productive vineyard. Some have proposed it shares the name, though this is uncertain.
