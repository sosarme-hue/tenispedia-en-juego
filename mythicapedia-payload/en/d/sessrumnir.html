hall of the goddess Freyja in Norse mythology

Sessrúmnir

Sessrúmnir is the hall of the goddess Freyja in Norse mythology, located in the field of Fólkvangr, where she receives half of the warriors who fall in battle. Its Old Norse name, meaning roughly «seat-room» or «roomy seats», makes it one of the best-known dwellings of the Scandinavian warrior afterlife.

The myth

According to medieval accounts, Fólkvangr is a field where Freyja takes half of those slain in battle, while Odin brings the other half to Valhalla. Sessrúmnir is the hall she keeps there. In the Prose Edda, written in the 13th century by Snorri Sturluson, it is described as large and beautiful. It comes up when the god High explains to Gangleri, who is King Gylfi in disguise, who Freyja is and where she lives.

Later legends add that Valhalla is for warriors, while Freyja's hall welcomes bards and craftsmen, and that lovers separated by death meet there too. Snorri also uses the name as one of the poetic titles of the goddess, calling her the possessor of the fallen and of Sessrúmnir. In the same work it appears a third time, in a list of ship names, which has led scholars to wonder whether hall and ship are the same element or a confusion between two traditions.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson (13th century), in the books Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál, names it as Freyja's hall and as the name of a ship.

Trivia

A name that is also a ship. The Prose Edda also lists Sessrúmnir among ship names. This has opened a debate about whether it refers to a hall, a ship, or both.
Stone ships and a modern hypothesis. In 2012, Joseph S. Hopkins and Haukur Þorgeirsson proposed linking Fólkvangr and Sessrúmnir to the many stone ships found across Scandinavia, and connecting Freyja to the «Isis» of the Suebi mentioned by Tacitus.
