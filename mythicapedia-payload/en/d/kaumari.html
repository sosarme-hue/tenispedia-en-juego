Hindu goddess, Shakti of Kartikeya

Kaumari

Kaumari is one of the Matrikas, the mother goddesses of Hinduism, and according to tradition she is the Shakti, or energy, of Kartikeya, the god of war. Her figure links warfare with the protection of children.

The myth

According to tradition, Kaumari emerges from Kartikeya, just as Brahmani emerges from Brahma or Vaishnavi from Vishnu. The Matrikas help Durga in her struggle against demons, whom they defeat.

The Mahabharata describes them in detail in some of its chapters, and the Rigveda mentions a group of seven mothers linked to the preparation of soma.

Worship and religion

Originally they were worshipped to ward off illness and the deaths of infants, in a cult tied to Kartikeya. From the ninth century onward they became common figures in temples. Their worship remains active today in South India and Nepal, where an eighth mother is sometimes added.

Legacy

The Gupta kings raised the Matrikas from popular village goddesses to court goddesses, and their images were carved on royal monuments to strengthen the loyalty of the soldiers.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata describes the Matrikas in some of its chapters, the Rigveda mentions seven mothers linked to soma, and the Devi Mahatmya describes the eight Ashtamatrikas.

Trivia

One more mother. In Nepal an eighth Matrika is sometimes added to represent the eight cardinal directions. In Bhaktapur, in the Kathmandu Valley, a ninth is added to represent the centre.
From village to court. The Gupta kings Skandagupta and Kumaragupta I took Kartikeya as their model and elevated the Matrikas, Skanda's foster mothers, to court goddesses.
