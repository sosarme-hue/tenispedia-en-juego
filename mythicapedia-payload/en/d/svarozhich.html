Slavic god of fire, son of Svarog

Svarozhich

Svarozhich is a Slavic god of fire, son of Svarog. He is one of the few Pan-Slavic gods, meaning he was shared by the Slavic peoples as a whole, and he is most often identified with Radegast or a regional variant of him, and less often with Dazhbog.

The myth

Little is known about Svarozhich, and no extensive stories of his deeds have survived. What is passed down is mainly his parentage: he is the son of Svarog, and his name derives from his father's through a suffix. For this reason his figure seems to be understood in relation to Svarog, as a deity tied to his father rather than the protagonist of stories of his own.

The name is formed with the suffix -its, -ich. The most widespread view treats it as a patronymic, so that Svarozhits literally means "son of Svarog", much as Polish pan ("master") gives panicz ("son of the master"). Other scholars argue that the suffix is diminutive, so the name would mean "little Svarog" or "young Svarog", just as the Serbo-Croatian Djurdjić is not "son of Djurdjo" but "little Djurdjo". Aleksander Brückner cited as a parallel Lithuanian prayers in which the form dievaite appears in place of dieve.

As a god of fire, his figure belongs to a sphere the Slavic peoples shared, although the sources do not detail how that power was represented in practice. The identification with Radegast is the most accepted, while the one with Dazhbog appears less often, so his figure is linked to two different gods depending on the region.

Trivia

Son or little Svarog. The name's suffix allows two readings: the patronymic "son of Svarog" and the diminutive "little Svarog". The second is supported by parallels such as the Serbo-Croatian name Djurdjić.
Cousins of English words. His father's name, Svarog, is linked to the Slavic word svar, "quarrel", and to Old English andswaru, the source of "swear". Links to Sanskrit svárgas, "heaven", have also been proposed.
