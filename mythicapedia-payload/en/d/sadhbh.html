deer-woman and mother of Oisín in Irish mythology

Sadhbh

Sadhbh is a figure from Irish mythology, mother of Oisín by Fionn mac Cumhail. Her story, told in the Celtic tradition, follows a woman enchanted into the form of a deer.

The myth

According to the tradition, Sadhbh was a daughter of Bodb Derg, king of the Síd of Munster. The dark druid Fear Doirche enchanted her into the shape of a doe after she rejected his love, and she remained so for three years. A servant of the druid advised her to enter the dún of the Fianna, where his power would end. She reached Almhuin, the home of Fionn mac Cumhail, who found her while hunting, and they married.

While Fionn fought the Vikings, a false image of her husband lured her outside the dún. There the druid struck her with a hazel wand and turned her back into a deer. Fionn searched for seven years without success. At the end, the Fianna found a wild boy in the forest with Sadhbh's features: it was Oisín, their son.

Trivia

Transformed hounds. Fionn's hounds, Bran and Sceolan, were also humans transformed into animals, which is why they did not harm Sadhbh when they found her.
Hazel wand. The druid used a wand of hazel to turn Sadhbh back into a deer, a detail the story stresses when describing the blow.
