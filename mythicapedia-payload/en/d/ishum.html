minor fire god and night watchman of Akkadian mythology

Ishum

Ishum is a minor god of Akkadian origin, linked to fire and above all to the night watch over streets and houses. He is best known as a sensible counsellor in the Epic of Erra, where he tries to halt the destruction of Babylon.

The myth

According to the texts, Ishum served as sukkal, or minister, to Nergal after the Old Babylonian period, though in literary texts he appears mainly as a nocturnal herald who patrols the streets to protect homes from outside.

In the Epic of Erra and Ishum, Ishum tries to dissuade his master Erra and the Sebitti from attacking the people of Babylonia. Erra dismisses him, arguing that human respect must be regained, and launches a destructive campaign. The bloodshed that follows is narrated from Ishum's perspective, as he describes chaos beyond measure.

Worship and religion

Ishum received moderate but widespread worship. His name appears in theophoric names from the Early Dynastic period onward, and he was popular on cylinder seals. His importance grew in the Neo-Assyrian period, perhaps because his protective role suited times of war and instability. He had a shrine in Nippur and was worshipped in Tarbiṣu, in Assyria.

Ancient sources: The Epic of Erra and Ishum, known from copies from Nineveh dating to the seventh century BCE, and god lists such as An = Anum.

Trivia

A god of the streets. Many of his epithets refer to his role as night watchman, such as herald of the street or herald of the night.
Brother of the sun. According to some sources, he was the son of Shamash, the sun god.
