chaos serpent of ancient Egyptian mythology

Apophis

Apophis, also known as Apep, is the ancient Egyptian serpent of chaos, darkness and fire. As the greatest enemy of the sun god Ra and of Maat, the cosmic order, his daily struggle with the solar barque explained to the Egyptians how night gave way to day.

The myth

According to the Egyptian stories, Apophis was a gigantic serpent whose task was to stop Ra's solar barque from reaching the new day. He tried to do this in two ways: by attacking the boat directly, or by slithering through the water to create sandbanks where the vessel would run aground. His sole aim was to break Maat.

Accounts of his origin differ. Some texts place him after Ra, born from his umbilical cord. A later tradition, recorded by Geraldine Pinch, has him spring from the spit of the goddess Neith while she was still in the primeval waters. Others held that he had existed since the beginning of time in the waters of Nu, the primordial chaos.

Myths place him sometimes in a western mountain called Manu, where the sun set, and at other times in the tenth region of the night, just before dawn. His roar was said to make the underworld tremble, and his movements were believed to cause earthquakes. New Kingdom tales elaborated his battles with Ra, in which Set defended the barque, and in one version Ra defeats the serpent in the form of a cat. He was never destroyed for good, only damaged or subdued, because without this struggle the solar cycle would fail.

Worship and religion

Egyptian priests regarded Ra's victory each night as the result of their prayers and of rituals in the temples. An annual Ritual of Overthrowing Apophis involved building an effigy believed to hold all the evil of Egypt, which was burned to protect the population for another year. Priestly texts described in detail how to defeat him: spitting on him, defiling him with the left foot, striking him with a lance and a knife, fettering him and setting him on fire. Wax models or small drawings were spat on, mutilated and burnt while spells were recited. Because it was feared that an image of Apophis might empower the demon, every depiction included another deity to subdue him. The dead were also buried with spells against him.

Legacy

The name Apophis has been given to a potentially hazardous asteroid discovered in 2004, named after the Egyptian chaos serpent.

Ancient sources: The Books of Overthrowing Apep, a priestly guide to fighting the serpent, and the Coffin Texts, which attribute to Apophis a magical gaze that overwhelmed Ra. Some spells in the Book of the Dead also target him.

Trivia

An asteroid with his name. A potentially hazardous asteroid discovered in 2004 was named after this chaos serpent.
Hyksos kings with his name. Several Hyksos pharaohs of the Fifteenth Dynasty bore his name, such as Apophis I and Apophis II.
