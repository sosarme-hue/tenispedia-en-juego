Celtic god of skill, craft and oaths

Lugh

Lugh is one of the most prominent figures in Irish mythology, a member of the Tuatha Dé Danann and a probable deity of the ancient Celts. He is remembered as a warrior, king, craftsman and saviour, and is linked with mastery of many arts, with oaths and with rightful kingship.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Lugh is the son of Cian of the Tuatha Dé Danann and Ethniu, daughter of the Fomorian tyrant Balor. His birth is wrapped in prophecy: Balor, fearing death at the hands of a grandson, locked his daughter in a tower, yet Cian still fathered a child who was cast into the sea and rescued to be raised by foster kin.

In the Battle of Mag Tuired, Lugh kills his grandfather Balor. His epithets, Lámfada ("long arm") and Samildánach ("equally skilled in many arts"), reflect his fame. He is credited with an unstoppable fiery spear, a sling stone and a hound named Failinis, and with inventing fidchell, ball games and horse racing. According to the texts, he is also the father of Cú Chulainn.

Worship and religion

Lugh is linked to the harvest festival of Lughnasadh, which bears his name and was celebrated at the start of autumn. His worship is poorly documented in direct sources, and no organised religious practice in his honour survives today.

Legacy

His name lives on in the Irish harvest festival of Lughnasadh and in the Welsh Lleu Llaw Gyffes, his counterpart in Welsh tradition. Some scholars link him with the Roman god Mercury, whom the Romans identified with him through the interpretatio romana.

Ancient sources: He appears in medieval Irish texts such as Cath Maige Tuired, the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Táin Bó Cúailnge and Baile in Scáil.

Trivia

A sun with arms. The texts compare Lugh's face to the sun, and Balor is astonished that the sun rises in the west, as the druids explain it is the radiance of Lugh's face.
A disputed name. The meaning of his name is still debated: some link it to a word for oath, others to a root meaning light, though the latter etymology is now largely rejected.
