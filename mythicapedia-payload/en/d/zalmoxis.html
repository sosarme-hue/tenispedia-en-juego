chief god of the Getae, a Thracian people

Zalmoxis

Zalmoxis was the principal divinity of the Getae, a Thracian people of the lower Danube, as recorded by Herodotus. He is remembered mainly for the doctrine of the immortality of the soul attributed to him, which Greek writers linked with Pythagoras and Orphism.

The myth

According to the tradition Herodotus recorded, the Getae believed that whoever died joined Zalmoxis, a divine being whom some also called Gebeleizis. Every four years they sent a messenger, chosen by lot, who was tossed into the air on three spears: if he died impaled, the god was thought to grant their requests; if he survived, he was branded wicked and another was sent.

Herodotus also reports what Greeks of the Hellespont and the Black Sea said: that Zalmoxis had been a slave of Pythagoras on Samos. Freed and wealthy, he returned home and taught the Getae about the immortality of the soul. He then had an underground chamber made, vanished for three years, and reappeared in the fourth, which convinced his people of his teachings. Herodotus was doubtful and believed Zalmoxis had lived long before Pythagoras.

Worship and religion

After Zalmoxis's death his cult grew into a henotheistic religion. Under Burebista, his traditional birth year, dated to 713 BC, was taken as the first year of the Dacian calendar. His figure has been linked to possible shamanic practices, although shamanism among the Getae is not certain, and scholars debate whether he was a sky god, a god of the dead or a mystery god.

Legacy

Plato mentions him as an expert in incantation and as a great physician who healed both body and soul. Aristotle compared him with Atlas, and some ancient writers identified him with Cronus, the Thracian Dionysus or Zeus.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, in Book IV of his Histories, is the main source; Plato, Aristotle, Strabo and Diodorus Siculus also refer to him.

Trivia

Three spears. According to Herodotus, the messenger sent to Zalmoxis was flung into the air and caught on three spears held by other men.
