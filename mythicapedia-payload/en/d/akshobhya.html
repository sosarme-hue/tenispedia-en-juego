Buddha of mirror wisdom, Vajrayana Buddhism

Akshobhya

Akshobhya (Sanskrit for "the Immovable One") is one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas of Vajrayana Buddhism. He represents consciousness as an aspect of reality and is linked to the mirror, a symbol of a mind that sees things as they truly are.

The myth

According to tradition, Akshobhya was once a monk who, in the world of Abhirati ("The Joyous"), made a vow to the Buddha who ruled that land: he would harbour no anger, malice or selfishness toward any sentient being until he attained enlightenment. After long effort and keeping his vows, he became the Buddha Akshobhya, lord of the Eastern Pure Land.

The texts place him in the Akṣobhyatathāgatasyavyūha Sūtra, translated in the second century CE and among the oldest Mahayana scriptures. In Vajrayana Buddhism he belongs to the mandala as head of the Vajra family, named after the diamond or the hard, flashing thunderbolt. His consort is Lochana, he is usually shown with two elephants, and he is depicted in blue, with a bell, staff and sword among his attributes.

Worship and religion

Before the rise of Bhaishajyaguru he was the object of a minor cult in Japan as a healing Buddha, and today he is venerated within Shingon Buddhism alongside other deities. In the Chinese tradition of the Śūraṅgama sutra he appears as host of the Vajra Division of the East, which controls the demon armies of that direction.

Legacy

His blue colour is associated with water and with the sky reflected in it, and the mirror becomes a metaphor for a clear mind that reflects without attachment. Some traditions merge him with Acala, also called "the Immovable", and many wrathful tantric figures are painted blue as an emblem of anger transformed into wisdom.

Ancient sources: The Akṣobhyatathāgatasyavyūha Sūtra, translated in the second century CE, and the Śūraṅgama sutra, highly influential in Chinese tradition.

Trivia

The mirror of wisdom. His wisdom is described as a mirror that reflects a rose or a bloodstained dagger exactly as they are, without being affected by either.
A healing Buddha. Before Bhaishajyaguru rose to prominence, he was venerated in Japan as a Buddha who healed illness.
A shared name. He is sometimes confused with or merged into Acala, whose name also means "immovable" in Sanskrit.
