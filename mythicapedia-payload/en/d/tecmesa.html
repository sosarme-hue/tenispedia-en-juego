Phrygian princess, captive and companion of Ajax

Tecmessa

Tecmessa is, in Greek mythology, a Phrygian princess taken captive by Ajax during the Trojan War, who became his companion and the mother of their son Eurysaces. She matters chiefly for her role in Sophocles' tragedy Ajax, where she tries to dissuade the hero from taking his own life.

The myth

According to the tradition, during one of the Greek raids on the cities of the northwestern coast of Asia Minor, Ajax, son of Telamon and king of Salamis, killed the king of the city, called Teleutas in the sources (or Teuthras in other accounts), and took his daughter captive. Tecmessa became his concubine. She was not his legally married wife, but the relationship is described as one of respect and fidelity. They had a son, Eurysaces.

In Sophocles' tragedy, Athena drives Ajax mad, and he attacks a herd of cattle, mistaking them for his enemies, Odysseus and the Atreides. Once his wits return, ashamed, he resolves to end his life. Tecmessa tries to hold him back by reminding him that she and the boy would be left unprotected, but he does not relent. When he leaves the tent, she discovers that he has gone and finds him dead, fallen on the sword once given to him by Hector. She covers the body with her robe and, together with their son, offers locks of her hair. Afterwards the story hardly mentions her again. His half-brother Teucer takes Eurysaces to Salamis, but Tecmessa's final fate goes unrecorded.

Legacy

Her role in Sophocles' tragedy Ajax, written around the middle of the fifth century BC, is what has kept her present in later literature. On painted pottery, a red-figure lekythos from about 460 BC shows her spreading a cloak over Ajax's body, one of the surviving images of the episode.

Ancient sources: Homer alludes to Ajax's prize but does not name the concubine; Sophocles' tragedy Ajax, from the mid-fifth century BC, is the main ancient source for her.

Trivia

Unnamed in Homer. The Homeric poems mention that Ajax took a captive as his prize, but they never name her or show her as a character.
A story about the son. According to Servius, Telamon exiled Teucer not because of Ajax's suicide but because he believed Teucer had not brought Tecmessa and Eurysaces, who in fact reached Salamis later on a different ship.
