Cethlenn

Cethlenn is a female figure in Irish mythology, the wife of the Fomorian giant Balor and the mother of Ethniu. She is remembered as a prophetess who foretold her husband's defeat and as the woman who wounded the god Dagda during the Second Battle of Mag Tuired.

The myth

In Irish tradition, Cethlenn was the wife of Balor, lord of the Fomorians, and the mother of Ethniu. She was known as a prophetess and warned Balor that he would be defeated by the Tuatha Dé Danann at the Second Battle of Mag Tuired.

In that battle, according to the Lebor Gabála Érenn, Cethlenn hurled a javelin at the Dagda and gave him a mortal wound, though he took 120 years to die of it. In the later Oidheadh Chloinne Tuireann, she identifies Lugh as her grandson and declares that his arrival will end Fomorian rule, a prophecy fulfilled when Lugh kills Balor. She is nicknamed "of the Crooked Teeth".

Legacy

According to local tradition in Enniskillen, County Fermanagh, the town takes its name from Inis Cethlenn, "Cethlenn's island", where the goddess is said to have died after swimming there wounded. This explanation appears in the seventeenth-century Annals of Clonmacnoise and was repeated later, though it has no firm historical basis.

Ancient sources: The Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Oidheadh Chloinne Tuireann, the Annals of the Four Masters and O'Flaherty's Ogygia, which record her role at Mag Tuired.

Trivia

A name with many forms. Her name appears as Ceithlinn, Céithlionn or Kethlenda depending on the source and period.
An island bearing her name. The island of Innis Cethleen, on the River Erne, is still enclosed by a stone wall built in the nineteenth century.
