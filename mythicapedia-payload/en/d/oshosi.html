Yoruba hunter deity

Oshosi

Oshosi (also Ochosi) is a hunter orisha of the Yoruba religion of West Africa, also worshipped in Brazil and Cuba. He is considered a protector of those who face trouble with the law and, according to tradition, can help prevent a person from being imprisoned.

The myth

According to Yoruba tradition, Oshosi is the finest of hunters, and his arrows never miss. Yet there was a time when he could never reach his prey, because the dense forest kept him away. In despair he went to Orula, who advised him to perform ebbo, a ritual offering.

Oggún had the opposite problem: he cleared paths quickly but never managed to kill his game. The two were rivals, since Eshu had sown discord between them. While both performed their ebbo in the forest, Oshosi accidentally spilled his offering on Oggún, and a heated argument followed, which ended in conversation. When a deer passed by, Oshosi shot it through the neck with an arrow, and Oggún opened a path to the animal in an instant. From then on they agreed they needed each other, and according to the story, apart they were nothing, so they sealed their pact at Orula's house.

Worship and religion

He is honoured on Tuesdays with honey, liquor or rum, tobacco smoke, toasted maize and smoked fish or utia, and by lighting a candle without kneeling. Sacrificial animals include doves, goats, roosters and deer. His receptacle is shared with Oggún. His worship remains alive in Nigeria, Benin and Togo, and in Cuban Santería and Brazilian Candomblé.

Legacy

In Cuban Santería he is identified with Saint Norbert and, in Santiago de Cuba, with Saint James the Great; in Brazil he is linked with Saint Sebastian and Saint George. He forms a ritual trilogy with Elegguá and Oggún.

Trivia

Colours. He is associated with blue, yellow and coral. In Candomblé, depending on the nation, his colour may be blue or green.
Arrows and saints. Saint Sebastian, depicted covered in arrows, is associated with Oshosi in Santería imagery.
