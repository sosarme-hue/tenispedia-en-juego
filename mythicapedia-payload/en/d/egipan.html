goat-Pan creature of Greek mythology

Aegipan

Aegipan is a figure of Greek mythology, sometimes identified with Pan and sometimes treated as a separate being. His story is of late origin and varies considerably from one author to another.

The myth

Depending on the version, Aegipan was the son of Zeus and Aega, the wife of Pan, though some sources make Apollo his father, or Hermes, or simply a goat as his mother. Hyginus gives Zeus and Aega as his parents and says he was placed among the stars, where he became the constellation Capricorn.

The best-known episode belongs to Zeus's struggle with Typhon. After Typhon took the god's sinews, he hid them in the Corycian cave under the guard of the dragoness Delphyne. Hermes and Aegipan stole them and restored them to Zeus, who was then able to defeat Typhon. Oppian credits Aegipan with a final trick, luring Typhon to the shore with the promise of a fish feast.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Eratosthenes, Apollodorus, Oppian, Plutarch and Pliny the Elder mention him with variations in his origin and deeds.

Trivia

Capricorn. One version says Zeus placed Aegipan among the stars, and his image is linked to the constellation Capricorn.
The Aegipanes of Libya. Pliny the Elder used the term Aegipanes for a people of satyr-like wild men said to live in Libya.
