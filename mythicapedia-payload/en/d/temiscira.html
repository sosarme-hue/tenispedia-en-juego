mythical capital of the Amazons on the Black Sea

Themiscyra

Themiscyra was an ancient Greek town on the southern coast of the Black Sea, near the mouth of the Thermodon river, in what is now Turkey. In Greek mythology it was the capital of the Amazons, which makes it one of the best-known places in the myths about these warrior women.

The myth

According to Herodotus and other ancient authors, Themiscyra was the capital of the Amazons, independent women famed for their skill in combat. Aeschylus, in his Prometheus Bound, placed their original home near Lake Maeotis, the modern Sea of Azov, and said they later moved to Themiscyra. Strabo added that they once shared the city with the Gargareans before separating and settling in the north, at the foot of the Caucasus.

In the myth, Heracles came to Themiscyra on his ninth labour to obtain the girdle of Queen Hippolyta. Hippolyta at first agreed to hand it over, but Hera, disguised as an Amazon, spread rumours that Heracles meant to betray her, and a fierce battle followed in which the queen died. Pausanias tells that Theseus also visited the city, and that the Amazon Antiope fell in love with him and gave the city up. Another version has Theseus leading a later expedition of his own.

Apollonius of Rhodes described the Amazons as scattered across three tribes near the Thermodon. When the Argonauts sailed past Themiscyra on their way to Colchis, Zeus sent Boreas, the north wind, to help them pull away from a shore where the Amazons were arming for battle.

Legacy

In the Wonder Woman comics published by DC Comics, Themyscira is a hidden island nation protected by a supernatural barrier, and the name has also been carried into the character's screen adaptations. The ancient city is usually identified with the modern town of Terme in Turkey.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, Pausanias, Aeschylus, Strabo, Apollonius of Rhodes and Diodorus Siculus all mention Themiscyra in their works.

Trivia

Famous honey. In ancient times, the bees of Themiscyra were famous for their honey.
Bees against an army. According to Appian, when Lucullus undermined the walls, the inhabitants released bears, other wild beasts and swarms of bees against his workmen.
