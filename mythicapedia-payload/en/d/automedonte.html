Achilles' charioteer, hero of Greek mythology

Automedon

Automedon is, in Greek mythology, the charioteer of Achilles and his companion during the Trojan War. The son of Diores, his name has become a byword for a loyal and skilful driver, able to control his master's horses even in the most difficult moments of battle.

The myth

In Homer's Iliad, Automedon rides into battle once Patroclus puts on Achilles' armour, commanding the horses Balius and Xanthos. After Patroclus dies, Automedon is driven to the rear of the battle, where he tries to console the grieving horses. Zeus finally intervenes, and Automedon returns to the fighting, though he cannot aid the Achaeans until Alcimedon agrees to act as his driver.

Back in combat, he repels an attempt on his life by Hector, Aeneas, Chromios and Aretos; he kills Aretos and takes his armour. Later, after Achilles' death, he passes into the service of Neoptolemus, Achilles' son. According to the Fabulae, he came from the island of Scyros and brought ten ships to the Trojan War, while Virgil's Aeneid places him in the Greek assault on Priam's palace.

Legacy

His name came to mean a loyal and skilful charioteer. He appears in ancient pottery on the François Vase, dated to around 565 BC, in a scene of the chariot race held in honour of Patroclus, where he reaches the finish line in second place. Centuries later, the painter Henri Regnault portrayed him in 1868 with Achilles' horses.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad describes him as Achilles' charioteer; Virgil's Aeneid mentions him in the assault on Troy, and the Fabulae of Hyginus record his origin on Scyros.

Trivia

A race on pottery. On the François Vase, dated to around 565 BC, Automedon appears in a version of the chariot race held at the funeral games of Patroclus, finishing second.
An island origin. According to the Fabulae of Hyginus, Automedon came from the island of Scyros and brought ten ships to the Trojan War.
