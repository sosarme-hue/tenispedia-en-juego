Hindu goddess associated with the Mahavidyas

Bhairavi

Bhairavi is a goddess of Hindu Tantric tradition and one of the ten Mahavidyas, forms or avatars of the Mother Goddess. Her name means "terrifying" or "awe-inspiring", and she is one of the most intense feminine figures in Tantra.

The myth

According to tradition, Bhairavi is one of the ten Mahavidyas, manifestations of the Mother Goddess. Her name means "terrifying" or "awe-inspiring", and her form is described in the dhyana shloka of the Devi Mahatmya: she wears red, a garland of severed heads, has three eyes and a crescent moon on her head. Other depictions show her as a beautiful woman, also with three eyes and dressed in red, holding rosaries, manuscripts, a trident, a sword, a noose or a bow, with bloodstains on her breasts.

According to the Puranas and Tantras, she is the consort of Bhairava. She is regarded as the supreme leader of the 64 yoginis, female deities who support her, and tradition holds that a Bhairavi, meaning a female adept of Tantra, succeeded in her practice with their help. The term also denotes a woman initiated into Kundalini and Tantra.

Despite her fearsome appearance, she is called Shubhankari, the doer of auspicious deeds, because she protects her devotees like a good mother. For the irreligious and cruel, however, tradition says she favours violence and punishment.

Worship and religion

Within Tantra she is also venerated through the Bhairavi yantra, a geometric diagram used in ritual practice.

Legacy

Her image appears in 17th-century manuscript paintings, such as the one showing Bhairavi with Shiva as Kapalika ascetics seated in a charnel ground, now held at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya describes her form in its dhyana shloka, and the Puranas and Tantras mention her as the consort of Bhairava.

Trivia

Meaning of Shubhankari. Her epithet Shubhankari means "the doer of auspicious deeds". It reflects her role as a benevolent mother to those who honour her.
The 64 yoginis. The yoginis are 64 in number and, in Tantric tradition, work together with the 52 Bhairavs and 56 Kalve under Bhairavi's leadership.
