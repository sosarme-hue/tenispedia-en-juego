Polybotes

Polybotes is one of the Giants of Greek mythology, a son of Uranus and Gaia, who fought the Olympian gods during the Gigantomachy. His story is tied to the island of Kos and to the islet of Nisyros, which according to myth was formed on top of his body.

The myth

According to tradition, the Giants were born from the blood that fell to the earth when Uranus was castrated by his son Cronus, so Polybotes was a child of Gaia. His name, usually taken to mean something like "much food", appears in the sources as that of one of the most formidable enemies the gods faced in that cosmic war.

Apollodorus tells how, during the Gigantomachy, Polybotes fled across the sea, chased by Poseidon, until he reached the island of Kos. There the god broke off a piece of the island and hurled it at his enemy, burying him beneath it. That fragment of land became the islet of Nisyros. The geographer Strabo records the same story and adds that some believed the giant actually lay beneath Kos itself.

Another version holds that Polybotes, first struck by Zeus, escaped by swimming. Poseidon then threw a trident at him, which missed and became the island of Nisyros or Porphyris.

Legacy

Pausanias describes a sculpture in Athens showing Poseidon on horseback hurling his spear at Polybotes. Greek vase painting of the sixth and fifth centuries BC also shows him many times, on amphorae, cups and other vessels where he is locked in combat with the god, sometimes with the island balanced on the god's shoulder. His story thus links mythology with the geography of Kos and Nisyros.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Strabo, Pausanias, and the poet Alcman, whose fragment may allude to the island.

Trivia

An island made by a throw. According to the myth, the islet of Nisyros was born from the piece of Kos that Poseidon tore off and hurled at the giant.
Two weapons, two versions. In one version of the story, Poseidon throws a trident that misses and turns into the island, instead of a fragment of land torn from Kos.
