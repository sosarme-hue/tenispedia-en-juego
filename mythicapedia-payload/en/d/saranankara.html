third Buddha of the Sāramaṇḍa kalpa

Saraṇaṅkara

Saraṇaṅkara is, in some traditions, the third of the Buddhas who preceded Gotama, the historical Buddha. The Buddhavamsa of the Pali canon mentions him briefly as a Buddha of the Sāramaṇḍa kalpa, the aeon that also saw Taṇhaṅkara, Medhaṅkara and Dīpaṃkara.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, Saraṇaṅkara was born in Vipula to King Sumaṅgala and Queen Yasavadi. Once grown, he succeeded his father and ruled the country peacefully for 7,000 years.

While he reigned, he saw four sights created by the devas. When his son was born, he left the palace and became an ascetic. He practised austerity for a month and reached enlightenment under the bodhi tree, Dolichandrone spathacea. After liberating many beings, he attained parinirvana at the age of 90,000 years.

Ancient sources: The Buddhavamsa of the Pali canon, which mentions him briefly among the Buddhas of the Sāramaṇḍa kalpa.

Trivia

A long reign. According to the account, Saraṇaṅkara ruled for 7,000 years before giving up the throne to become an ascetic.
A tree of awakening. Tradition places his enlightenment under a bodhi tree of the species Dolichandrone spathacea.
