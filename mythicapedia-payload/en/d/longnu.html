Dragon Girl, acolyte of Guanyin in Chinese Buddhism

Longnü

Longnü, the "Dragon Girl", is a young naga, daughter of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea, who in Chinese Buddhism serves Guanyin (Avalokiteśvara) alongside Sudhana. She matters because she embodies the idea that enlightenment does not depend on gender.

The myth

In the Lotus Sutra, Longnü is eight years old, wise, and reaches enlightenment instantly. When Śāriputra doubts that a woman can become a buddha, she offers a pearl to the Buddha, transforms into a male bodhisattva, and attains full enlightenment.

In a sixteenth-century Ming novel, she is the daughter of the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea. After freeing a carp caught by a fisherman, she gives a pearl to Guanyin and stays on as her disciple.

Worship and religion

Little is known about rites dedicated to her. Her presence is mainly fixed in the iconography of Guanyin, where she appears beside Sudhana, shaped by tantric sutras that show her offering a precious pearl.

Legacy

In Chan Buddhism her story was used to illustrate the power of sudden awakening. Her figure may have been inspired by Yunü, the Jade Maiden, and Jintong, the Golden Youth, who attend the Jade Emperor.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra (Saddharma Puṇḍarīka), where she appears as the daughter of the naga king Sāgara, and a sixteenth-century Ming novel that links her with Sudhana as acolytes of Guanyin.

Trivia

A pearl as offering. According to the sutra, the pearl she offers symbolizes her life and ego, and the Buddha accepts it as a sign of her devotion.
A snake in a bottle. In an eighteenth-to-nineteenth-century sacred scroll, Sudhana frees a snake that had been trapped in a bottle for eighteen years.
