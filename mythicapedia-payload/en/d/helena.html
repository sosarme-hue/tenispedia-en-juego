Helen

Helen is one of the most famous figures of Greek mythology, known as the most beautiful woman in the world. Her abduction or seduction by Paris, prince of Troy, triggered the Trojan War, and her figure moved from an ancient cult divinity to a central character of Homeric epic.

The myth

According to tradition, Helen was the daughter of Zeus, although the sources disagree about her mother. The best-known version makes her the daughter of Leda, wife of King Tyndareus of Sparta: Zeus, in the form of a swan, lay with Leda, and Helen hatched from an egg. Hesiod makes her the daughter of Zeus and an unnamed Oceanid, and other authors link her to Nemesis. Her siblings are Castor, Pollux and Clytemnestra.

As a girl she was abducted by Theseus and Pirithous, who left her with his mother Aethra at Aphidna until her brothers rescued her. When she came of age, numerous suitors arrived at Sparta. To avoid a war among them, Tyndareus followed Odysseus's advice: the suitors swore to support whichever husband was chosen if anyone ever took her away. Helen married Menelaus, and they had Hermione.

The judgement of Paris changed her fate. Eris threw a golden apple meant for the most beautiful, and Zeus charged Paris with choosing among Hera, Athena and Aphrodite. The goddess of love promised him Helen, and Paris took her to Troy. According to Homer, Helen fell in love with Paris and went with him willingly, though other versions present her as abducted. In Troy her fame and her ambiguity make her a complex character. After Paris died, in Homer's account she returned to Menelaus; other traditions say she spent the war in Egypt or ascended to Olympus.

Worship and religion

Helen was worshipped as a goddess before she became a literary character. She received cult especially in Sparta and on Rhodes, and at Therapne she shared a shrine with Menelaus. She was also worshipped in Attica.

Legacy

Her figure has inspired painters and writers who portrayed her as the ideal of human beauty. In medieval and Renaissance art her abduction was a recurring subject. The line "Was this the face that launched a thousand ships?" from Christopher Marlowe's Doctor Faustus has made her a symbol of beauty capable of causing war.

Ancient sources: Homer places her in the Iliad and the Odyssey, and Hesiod, Euripides, Virgil and Ovid also mention her.

Trivia

An egg hung from the ceiling. In the sanctuary of the Leucippides in Sparta, an egg hung from the ceiling by ribbons, believed to be the one from which Helen had been born.
A name with several explanations. The origin of her name remains a problem for scholars, who have linked it to the torch, the moon or the sun.
