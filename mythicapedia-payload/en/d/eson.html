king of Iolcus and father of Jason in Greek myth

Aeson

Aeson is a figure of Greek myth, king of Iolcus in Thessaly and father of the hero Jason. He matters because his story is bound to the quest for the Golden Fleece: his imprisonment and dispossession by his half-brother Pelias set in motion the voyage of the Argonauts. According to the myth, Aeson was the son of Cretheus and Tyro, and had two brothers, Pheres and Amythaon. His mother also bore two sons to the sea god Poseidon, Neleus and Pelias. The sources disagree about his wives: one version names Alcimede as the mother of Jason and Promachus, while others name Polymede or Polymele, daughter of Autolycus, or Amphinome. Pelias, hungry for power, banished Neleus and Pheres and locked Aeson in the dungeons of Iolcus. Aeson sent Jason to be educated by the centaur Chiron. Later an oracle warned Pelias to beware of a man wearing one sandal. Jason lost a sandal crossing the river Anauros on his way to Iolcus, and when he claimed the throne, Pelias sent him after the Golden Fleece, keeping him far from the city. While Jason was away, Pelias meant to kill Aeson. In one version, Aeson took his own life by drinking bull's blood; his wife hanged herself, and Pelias murdered their young son Promachus. Another tradition has Aeson surviving until Jason returned with Medea. Medea cut his throat, boiled his body in a cauldron, and he emerged as a young man. She then persuaded Pelias's daughters to do the same for their father, but she refused to revive him, and Pelias died.
