Slavic god of darkness

Chernobog

Chernobog, or «Black God», is a Slavic deity about whom very little is known. He appears as the brother and counterpart of Belobog, the White God, and is associated with darkness, evil and, later, with the Christian devil.

The myth

Stories about Chernobog are scarce, and almost all come from Christian authors. His name is attested only among the western Slavic tribes, which suggests he was neither a very important nor a very ancient deity. According to post-Christian remnants of creation myths, a divine dualism credited the world's creation to the brother gods Belobog and Chernobog, who supposedly raised the earth from the primordial sea, embodying the original opposition between light and darkness, goodness and evil.

Some researchers argue, however, that this sharp bipolarity may partly reflect Christian influence, which would have turned Chernobog into an image of the devil. The name is usually explained as «dark» plus «god», though the Slovene historian Anton Tomaž Linhart linked it to roots meaning «to practise witchcraft» and «to hate», making the god a patron of magic and war.

Worship and religion

Sources barely describe his worship. Helmold recounts that at feasts the Slavs drank toasts both to the good god and to the evil one, whom he identified with the devil. There is no evidence of temples or festivals of his own, so it is unclear how far he had an independent cult.

Legacy

His image entered the arts mainly through the Russian composer Modest Mussorgsky, who used it in 1867 for Night on Bald Mountain; in the 1872 revision the scene was retitled The Glorification of Chernobog. Neil Gaiman portrays him in American Gods as an old man who slaughters livestock with a club, and Disney turned him into a huge black devil in Fantasia. He also appears in Kingdom Hearts, The Witcher and Smite.

Ancient sources: Helmold of Bosau's Chronica Slavorum, written around 1168-1169, is the earliest known mention; later writers such as Albert Krantz, in 1519, repeated it.

Trivia

A changed title. In the 1872 revision of Mussorgsky's work, the scene originally presided over by Satan was reassigned to the black god and retitled The Glorification of Chernobog.
A double toast. According to Helmold, the Slavs toasted both the good god and the evil one, whom he identified with the devil.
