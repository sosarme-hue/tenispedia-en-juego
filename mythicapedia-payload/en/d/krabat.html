figure of Sorbian folklore

Krabat

Krabat is a figure of Sorbian folklore from the Lusatia region of Germany, sometimes called the "Wendish Faust". Over the 19th century he changed from an evil sorcerer into a folk hero and beneficial trickster, and his story has inspired novels and films.

The myth

According to the traditional tale, Krabat learned his magic in the village of Čorny Chołmc (Schwarzkollm), now a district of the city of Hoyerswerda, from an evil sorcerer with terrible powers. The story fits tale type 325 in the Aarne-Thompson-Uther classification, "The Sorcerer's Apprentice". Over time the character changed sides: he stopped being a feared magician and became someone who helped his people, teaching them to drain swamps, build mills and farm the land.

After his death, supernatural powers were attributed to him and legends about his deeds multiplied. The earliest known mentions appear in 1839 minutes of a Lusatian learned society, though its writings were lost. Modern research suggests he may have been inspired by Janko Šajatović, a Croatian cavalry officer who served in Saxony in the 17th century.

Legacy

The legend has given rise to several novels. Čorny młyn (The Black Mill, 1968) by Jurij Brězan became the basis for the film Die Schwarze Mühle. Otfried Preußler's novel Krabat (1971) inspired an East German television film in 1975, the Czech film Čarodějův učeň (1977) by Karel Zeman, and the German film Krabat (2008). The German gothic rock band ASP also released an album inspired by it.

Trivia

A Sorbian "Faust". He is often called the "Wendish Faust", comparing him to the German figure who learns magic from a sorcerer.
Possible historical origin. Some studies identify the figure with Janko Šajatović (1624-1704), a Croatian soldier who came to Germany in 1658.
