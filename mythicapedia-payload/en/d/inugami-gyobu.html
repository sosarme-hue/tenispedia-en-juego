leader of the Matsuyama tanuki, Japanese

Inugami Gyōbu

Inugami Gyōbu is the leader of the bake-danuki, shapeshifting tanuki, in legends from Matsuyama, in the former Iyo Province, now Ehime Prefecture. He is a central figure in the story of the O-Ie Sōdō revolt and the 808 tanuki.

The myth

According to tradition, the tanuki of Matsuyama went back to the reign of Emperor Tenji and numbered 808 in all. Inugami Gyōbu lived in an old cave at Kumayama and protected Matsuyama Castle. During the O-Ie Sōdō revolt he sided with the rebels, and the samurai Inō Budayū, armed with a divine rod, defeated him and sealed him away with his family.

Other versions change the story: in some, Gyōbu is bound by a pact with the young rebel Gotō Kogenta; in others, he allies with the just cause and asks Budayū for help, and Budayū uses a wooden mallet.

Worship and religion

The cave where he was sealed still remains in Kutaninagumi, Matsuyama, and is known as Yamaguchi Reishin.

Legacy

He is one of the three great tanuki tales, alongside the Shojoji no Tanuki-bayashi and the Bunbuku Chagama, and spread through kōdan storytelling.

Ancient sources: The historical record Iyo Nagusa describes the O-Ie Sōdō revolt, and the Budayū story spread mainly through Edo-period kōdan.

Trivia

A mallet, not a rod. In some versions, Budayū does not use the divine rod but a wooden mallet given to him by the yōkai Sanmoto Gorōzaemon.
A cave that still stands. The cave where he was sealed still exists in Kutaninagumi, Matsuyama, and is known as Yamaguchi Reishin.
