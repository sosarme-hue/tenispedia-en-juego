Auson

Auson is a hero of Greek mythology, regarded as the eponym of Ausonia, an ancient name for part of Italy, and of the Ausones. He matters because ancient accounts use his figure to explain how several Italian territories and islands received their names.

The myth

In some Greek stories Auson was a son of Odysseus and Calypso, while other writers make his mother Circe. The sources disagree: Lycophron suggests he was a son of Italus and a woman named Leutaria, or of Calypso and Atlas, whereas Tzetzes lists him among the children of Odysseus and Circe, alongside Telegonus, Ardeas, Latinus and Casiphone.

Tradition makes him the first king of the Ausones and says Ausonia, one of Italy's older names, took its name from him. Dionysius of Halicarnassus notes that the western sea along the peninsula was also called Ausonian after its inhabitants, until the Tyrrhenians gained control of it. In the myth, his son Liparus was driven out of Italy by rebellious brothers and founded the islands later called Lipari, while Liparus's daughter Cyane married Aeolus, keeper of the winds.

Ancient sources: Lycophron and Tzetzes name him as a son of Odysseus and Calypso or Circe, and Dionysius of Halicarnassus describes the Ausonian sea.

Trivia

Ancient names of Italy. According to ancient poets, Italy was successively called Artesa, Saturnia, Ausonia, Tyrrhenia and finally Italia.
Liparus and the Aeolian Islands. The Lipari islands were named after Liparus, son of Auson, who fled Italy after a revolt by his brothers.
