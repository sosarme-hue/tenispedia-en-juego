Greek sea goddess

Eurybia

Eurybia is a primordial sea deity of Greek mythology, daughter of Gaia and Pontus and one of the Pontides. She matters mainly as an ancestor: her union with Crius produced several gods of the generation before the Olympians.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Eurybia is the youngest of the children of Gaia and Pontus, and the poet gives her a striking trait: she has a heart of flint within her breast. She belongs to the lineage of sea powers born of Earth and Sea, alongside her siblings.

The same poem recounts her marriage to Crius, with whom she bore Astraeus, Pallas and Perses, the last of whom Hesiod singles out for his wisdom. These children are significant figures in the divine genealogy, though the account says little about their deeds.

The Library of Apollodorus, which largely follows Hesiod's genealogies, agrees with the Theogony on Eurybia. Her presence remains slight: she appears only in genealogical contexts, and some authors leave her out when listing the offspring of Pontus and Gaia.

Legacy

Eurybia barely appears outside the ancient genealogical lists, so her legacy in later art or culture is minimal.

Ancient sources: She appears in Hesiod's Theogony and in the Library of Apollodorus, both in genealogical contexts.

Trivia

A heart of steel. Hesiod is the only source that describes Eurybia as having a heart of steel, an unusual image for a sea deity.
The youngest of the Pontides. In the Theogony she is presented as the youngest among her siblings, which reinforces her secondary role in the lineage.
