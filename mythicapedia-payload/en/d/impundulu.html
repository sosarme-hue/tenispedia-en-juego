lightning bird, creature of South African folklore

Impundulu

The impundulu, or lightning bird, is a creature in the folklore of South African peoples such as the Zulu, Xhosa and Pondo. It is regarded as a vampiric being tied to witchcraft.

The myth

According to traditions of South African peoples such as the Zulu, Xhosa and Pondo, the impundulu, or lightning bird, takes the form of a black and white bird the size of a person, which summons thunder and lightning with its wings and talons. It is a vampiric creature tied to witchcraft: it serves as the familiar of a witch or witch doctor and attacks their enemies. Its appetite for blood is insatiable, and at times it takes the shape of a handsome young man who seduces women.

It is said to pass from mother to daughter within a witch's family and to outlive its masters, which is why it is considered immortal. Bullets, stabbing, poison and water cannot harm it; only fire can destroy it. Belief holds that its fat can be obtained by catching the bird at the moment the lightning strikes the ground.

Legacy

It serves as the main antagonist of Marlon James's Dark Star trilogy, appearing in the novels Black Leopard, Red Wolf and Moon Witch, Spider King.

Trivia

A bird linked to lightning. Among some African tribes the lightning bird is believed to be the hammerkop, a small wading bird.
A real case from 2005. In 2005 a South African man was convicted of culpable homicide after killing a two-year-old child he believed to be an impundulu.
