Russian hero of the Novgorod cycle

Vasily Buslayev

A hero of the Novgorod cycle of the byliny, Russian oral epic poems, who embodies youthful and boundless prowess. He is one of the best-known characters of Russian folklore.

The myth

According to tradition, Vasily is the protagonist of three tales in the Novgorod cycle. From a young age he shows no restraint: he does as he pleases regardless of consequences, antagonizes most of Novgorod and gathers a band of like-minded daredevils. Only his mother has any real influence over him.

At a feast, drunk and quarrelsome, he bets that he and his companions can beat up every man in Novgorod. When the fight begins, it becomes clear the wager will be carried out, and only his mother's intervention saves the city. Later, weighed down by his sins, he makes a pilgrimage to Jerusalem to repent, but the journey does not change him: he defies every prohibition and dies absurdly on his return, once more trying to prove his prowess.

Legacy

Sergei Eisenstein gave him a supporting role in the film Alexander Nevsky (1938). Gennadi Vasilev directed Vasiliy Buslaev (1983), based on a poem by Sergei Narovchatov. James Joyce makes a coded allusion to him in Finnegans Wake.

Trivia

A coded allusion in Joyce. James Joyce mentions this bogatyr in Finnegans Wake under a coded name: «Wassaily Booslaeugh of Riesengeborg».
A role on screen. He appears as a supporting character in Sergei Eisenstein's film Alexander Nevsky (1938).
