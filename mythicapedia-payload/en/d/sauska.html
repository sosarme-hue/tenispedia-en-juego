Hurrian goddess of love, war and magic

Šauška

Šauška was the highest-ranking goddess in the Hurrian pantheon, linked to love, war and the power of incantations, and by extension to healing. Her cult spread from Anatolia to northern Syria and Mesopotamia, and she was eventually admitted into the Hittite state pantheon.

The myth

In Hurrian myths, known largely through Hittite translations, Šauška appears either as an ally of her brother Teshub or as a heroine in her own right. According to these narratives, she fought the sea monster Ḫedammu, the diorite giant Ullikummi, the sea god Kiaše and the mountain god Pišaišapḫi. She also protected Hašarri, a personified olive tree, from various threats. Her two handmaidens, Ninatta and Kulitta, usually accompanied her.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in many centres, including Nineveh, Alalakh, Ugarit and Nuzi, and in southern Mesopotamia she was identified with Ishtar. In Anatolia she could be invoked as a healing deity. Ritual texts mention offerings to her male and female attributes, reflecting her ambiguous character.

Ancient sources: Hurrian texts translated into Hittite, preserved in the Bogazköy Archive, along with offering lists and ritual descriptions from the region.

Trivia

An ambiguous character. She appears in Yazılıkaya reliefs both among the gods and among the goddesses, and one of her forms is shown as male, beardless and armed with a golden axe.
A name with meaning. Her Hurrian name is usually translated as The Great One or The Magnificent One, and the name of the underworld goddess Allani is thought to derive from the same root.
