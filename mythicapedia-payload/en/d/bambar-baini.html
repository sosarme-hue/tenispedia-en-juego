regional Hindu goddess, local form of the Mother Goddess

Bambar Baini

Bambar Baini is a regional form of the Mother Goddess (Devi), closely identified with Amba, worshipped on a hilltop in Laundi, in Chhatarpur district (Madhya Pradesh, India). It is one of the most important pilgrimage sites in the Bundelkhand region.

The myth

According to local tradition, the goddess appeared in a dream to a priest and told him where she was on the top of the hill. The next morning, the priest and other senior townsfolk climbed up and found her inscription in a small hole in a large rock covering a cave. The hill was then full of wild animals such as lions and tigers, so locals believe she was named Babbar Vahini, «the one who rides the lion», which over time became Bambar Baini. The townspeople decided to build the temple on the rock itself.

She is depicted as a small, young goddess, richly dressed in a sari and jewelry. The original carving in the hole is hard to photograph because of the poor light.

Worship and religion

The temple, on the hilltop and reached after climbing about 450 steps, draws devotees all year round. The Navaratri fairs in April and October attract thousands of visitors, coinciding with the annual Hindu fasting days. A picturesque pond can be seen below the hill, in front of the temple.

Trivia

A name that changed. According to tradition, the name Babbar Vahini gradually changed over time until it became Bambar Baini.
A staircase of 450 steps. Pilgrims must climb about 450 steps to reach the temple at the top of the hill.
