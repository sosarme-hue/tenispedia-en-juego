demon of scribal errors in medieval Christianity

Titivillus

Titivillus is a demon of medieval Christian lore, blamed for the mistakes scribes made while copying manuscripts. His legend offered a wry explanation for why texts reached later generations so full of errors.

The myth

According to medieval tradition, Titivillus worked on behalf of Belphegor, Lucifer or Satan, gathering into a sack the syllables, words and verses that clergy skipped, mispronounced or mumbled during services and prayer. These faults were recorded in a book that could be used against the monks at the Last Judgement or presented before God.

The image appears already in a sermon by Jacques de Vitry in the 1220s, where a demon carries syllables stolen from God. Around 1285, John of Wales gave him his name in his Tractatus de Penitentia. Some scholars connect the name with the Roman goddess Tutilina, whom Augustine mentions in The City of God as a guardian of stored grain, as well as with a chain of copying errors. Over time he came to be called the patron demon of scribes, since he offered an easy excuse for the mistakes that crept from one manuscript to the next.

In late medieval English pageants, such as the Iudicium that closes the Towneley Cycle, he became a comic, satirical figure mocking human vanity. In an anonymous 15th-century devotional treatise he introduces himself speaking in garbled words and swallowing syllables.

Worship and religion

He had no cult of his own. His presence is mainly iconographic: he is shown with a sack or basket, sometimes carrying books. In the Monastery of Las Huelgas in Burgos, a panel of around 1485 shows a devil with a bundle of books on his back, which the scholar Joaquín Yarza Luaces identifies with him.

Legacy

He is no longer a living object of faith, but he survives in popular culture. He appears among the demons of the role-playing game Dungeons & Dragons since 1983, is the subject of Michael Ayrton's book Tittivulus: or The Verbiage Collector, and gives his name to the cat of a dictionary company in Eley Williams's novel The Liar's Dictionary.

Ancient sources: Jacques de Vitry, in a sermon from the 1220s, describes the demon who gathers omitted syllables from the choir, and Augustine's The City of God mentions the goddess Tutilina.

Trivia

An error in the dictionary. Marc Drogin noted that for half a century editions of the Oxford English Dictionary carried an incorrect page reference in a footnote on the earliest mention of Titivillus.
A cat with a demonic name. In The Liar's Dictionary, Titivillus is the name of the cat of a dictionary-producing company that discovers it has a problem with fake words.
