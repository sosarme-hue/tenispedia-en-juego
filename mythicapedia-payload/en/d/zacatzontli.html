Aztec god of the daytime road

Zacatzontli

Zacatzontli is a god of Aztec mythology, protector of the road during the day and of those who travel in daylight. He belongs to the divinities who watched over everyday journeys.

The myth

According to tradition, Zacatzontli was one of several brothers, among them Tlacotzontli and Yacatecuhtli. His task was to guard travellers during the daytime hours.

He is usually shown with an eagle as his guide, a staff in his left hand and a bag full of quetzals in his right. Unlike many deities, he wears no headdress, only a single feather.

As a protector of merchants, he is equated with the Maya god Ek Chuáj. His name may mean 'Lord of the Road' or 'His Road, the Lord', although the former seems more likely.

Trivia

An eagle as guide. Zacatzontli is accompanied by an eagle that serves as his guide on the road.
