dwarf smith of Norse mythology

Sindri

Sindri is a dwarf smith in Norse mythology, brother of Brokkr, who appears as Eitri in some sources. He is credited with forging several of the gods' most famous treasures, and his name is also linked to a golden hall where the souls of the virtuous will dwell after Ragnarök.

The myth

According to the myth, Loki cut the hair of Sif, wife of Thor, and to make amends he asked the sons of Ivaldi for golden hair, along with Odin's spear Gungnir and Freyr's ship Skidbladnir. On the way, Loki wagered his head that the brothers Brokkr and Sindri could not match those treasures.

While Sindri worked at the forge, Loki, disguised as a fly, stung Brokkr to distract him. From the fire came a golden boar, the ring Draupnir and a hammer with a rather short handle, Thor's Mjölnir.

The gods sided with the dwarves, above all because the hammer offered protection against the frost giants. When Brokkr came to claim Loki's head, Loki argued that the bet included his head but not his neck. Brokkr tried to sew shut Loki's lips with a knife that would not cut well, until his brother's breath sharpened it. According to the tale, Loki later tore free of the threads and went on deceiving others with words.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, the Poetic Edda (where he appears as Eitri) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning and the Skáldskaparmál.

Trivia

A fly in the forge. Loki turned into a fly to pester the brothers at work. Each time the sting landed on a different part of Brokkr, from his hand to his eyelids.
Two names for one dwarf. In the Poetic Edda the dwarf is called Eitri, while in the Prose Edda he is Sindri. The story of the brothers and their gifts remains the same in both versions.
