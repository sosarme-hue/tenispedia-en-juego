Clymene

Clymene is a princess in Greek mythology, daughter of King Minyas of Orchomenus. She appears in many heroic genealogies, and ancient authors give her different husbands and children.

The myth

According to tradition, Clymene was the daughter of Minyas, king of Orchomenus, and Eurianassa. The accounts do not agree on her husbands. In one version she married Phylacus, son of Deion, and became the mother of Iphiclus, a youth so swift-footed that he raced the winds and ran over ears of grain without bending them. Pausanias, citing the Nostoi, presents her as the wife of Cephalus and also the mother of Iphiclus, adding that she was his second wife, after Procris. Others link her to Helios and make her the mother of Phaethon, the version that became most common among Hellenistic and Roman poets, who pictured her as an Oceanid. Hyginus connects her with Aeson, father of Jason, and with Alcimede. According to another tradition, she was the wife of Iasus of Arcadia and the mother of the huntress Atalanta. Odysseus sees her among the queens in the underworld.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Odyssey, where Odysseus meets her in the underworld, and is mentioned by Pausanias, Hyginus and Stesichorus.

Trivia

Swift feet. Her son Iphiclus was so fast that, according to the account, he ran over ears of grain without bending them.
Several husbands. Ancient authors give her up to four different husbands, among them Phylacus, Cephalus, Helios and Aeson.
