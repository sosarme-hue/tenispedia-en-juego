magical cat of Russian folklore

Kot Bayun

Kot Bayun is a legendary cat from Russian folklore, famed as a great feline living high in the treetops of the Siberian forests. His name is tied to lulling and sleep, and he is one of the most recognisable animal creatures in Slavic tales.

The myth

According to popular tales, Kot Bayun is a large, powerful cat who lives among the tallest trees of the Siberian forests. His claws are so strong that they can destroy any armour.

Tradition gives him a soft singing voice: when he hums a tune, travellers in the forest feel a drowsiness that overcomes them. His name comes from the Russian verb baiukat, to lull to sleep, linking him to the idea of putting someone to rest.

In many stories he appears as the cat of Baba Yaga, the fearsome witch of Russian tales, and he is said to hold power over wolves and bears. The best-known tale is "Naum the Godfather", in which Tsar Dmitry orders that Kot Bayun's father be brought to the palace.
