stepmother of Tyro in Greek myth

Sidero

Sidero is a figure of Greek myth, the second wife of King Salmoneus and the cruel stepmother of Tyro. Her story is tied to the Aeolid family line and to Sophoclean tragedy, and it ends with her violent death at a sanctuary of Hera.

The myth

In the tradition recorded by Diodorus, Sidero was the second wife of King Salmoneus of Elis, and she treated Tyro, his daughter from his first marriage to Alcidice, as a harsh stepmother would.

Apollodorus tells a different ending. When Tyro's twin sons by Poseidon, Pelias and Neleus, reached adulthood, they set out to avenge their mother. Sidero fled to a sanctuary of Hera, but Pelias killed her on the altar, and the goddess then brought about his ruin.

Hesiod gives another version, in which Salmoneus, his wife and the rest of the family, except Tyro, were destroyed by Zeus' thunderbolt, so Sidero plays no part. Later writers probably merged the epic and tragic traditions into one story.

Ancient sources: Diodorus, Apollodorus and Hesiod mention her or allude to her story, and Sophocles names her in his lost tragedies titled Tyro.

Trivia

A name meaning iron. Her name comes from a Greek word meaning "of iron", and Sophocles links it to the harshness of her character.
A mask of suffering. Pollux describes a theatrical mask used in Sophocles' play, with bruised cheeks meant to show the blows Sidero dealt to Tyro.
