Greek personification of good conduct and well-being

Eupraxia

Eupraxia is, in Greek mythology, the personification of good conduct, good reputation and well-being. She appears in a single passage by Aeschylus, within a proverb that places her among a family of abstract personifications.

The myth

Ancient texts preserve very little about her. The only known testimony is from Aeschylus, who quotes a saying in which Eupraxia is linked to Soter, Salvation. In the version most often cited, she is the daughter of Peitharchia, Obedience, and of Soter.

Another reading of the same passage reverses the family tree: Peitharchia would be her mother and Eupraxia the wife of Soter. That difference shows the genealogy was never fixed, and that the saying worked as a moral lesson rather than a story about her origins.

The meaning of the proverb is simple: one who calls on the gods should not do so carelessly, because obedience leads to success, and success to salvation.

Ancient sources: Aeschylus, who mentions her only once, in a proverb about obedience, success and salvation.
