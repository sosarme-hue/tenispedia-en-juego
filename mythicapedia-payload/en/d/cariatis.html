epithet of the goddess Artemis in Laconia

Caryatis

Caryatis is an epithet of Artemis linked to the nut tree and to the Laconian town of Caryae. It matters because it preserves the memory of an older nut-tree goddess later absorbed into the Olympian Artemis.

The myth

According to Lactantius, a group of girls dancing for Artemis, sensing disaster, hanged themselves from the branches of an oak. The oak was thus called karia, and the epithet caryatis came to be applied to the goddess and her sanctuary.

Servius tells another story: Carya, a maiden daughter of a Laconian king, was loved by Dionysus and turned into a nut tree, and from then on she was worshipped as Artemis Caryatis.

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on the Laconian town of Caryae, where Pausanias describes an annual dance by women, the caryatis, performed at a festival called the Caryateia. Scholars often see Carya as an older goddess of the nut tree, later assimilated into Artemis.

Legacy

The dancing girls gave their name to the caryatids, architectural supports shaped like women, such as those of the Erechtheum on the Athenian Acropolis.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes the dance and festival in his Description of Greece; Lactantius and Servius preserve the accounts of Carya.

Trivia

The Caryatids of the Erechtheum. The caryatids that support the porch of the Erechtheum on the Athenian Acropolis are female marble figures that serve as columns.
Two versions of the metamorphosis. In one account, Carya becomes a nut tree after her union with Dionysus; in another, to prevent her rape.
