plain of the gods in Norse mythology

Iðavöllr

Iðavöllr is, in Norse mythology, the plain where the gods gathered to raise Ásgard, their home, and where they will meet again after Ragnarök. Its name, of uncertain meaning, is usually rendered as "plain of deeds" or "green plain".

The myth

According to the Völuspá, the first poem of the Poetic Edda, the seeress who narrates the poem recalls that, in the earliest days of the world, the mighty gods met at Iðavöllr. There they raised shrines and high temples, set up forges, worked ore and fashioned tongs and tools. This is how the stage was set for Ásgard, the home of the Æsir gods.

The poem returns to this place at the end, after the events of Ragnarök. In the final battle most of the gods have been killed, and only a few survivors come back to the plain. Gathered there, they speak of the terrible serpent that girdles the earth, recall the mighty past and call to mind the ancient runes of the Ruler of the Gods. They then build a new city on Iðavöllr, beginning with Gimlé, in what the tale presents as the start of a renewed world.

The texts do not describe in detail what the place looked like. Its role is mainly that of a meeting ground, first at the creation of the world and later at its renewal after the old gods have passed.

Ancient sources: It appears twice in the Völuspá of the Poetic Edda, in stanzas 7 and 60.

Trivia

A possible echo in Tacitus. If the name is emended to Ið[is]avǫllr, it would match Idisiaviso, the site on the Weser river where, in 16 CE, the forces of Arminius and Germanicus fought, according to Tacitus' Annals book II. It is a philological hypothesis, not a certainty.
Asgard as Troy. Snorri Sturluson claims that Asgard was actually Troy. For that reason, according to a modern interpretation, Iðavöllr might be Mount Ida, which lies next to the city.
