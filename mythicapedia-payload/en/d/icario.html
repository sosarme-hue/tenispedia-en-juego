Spartan king and father of Penelope

Icarius

Icarius was a member of the Spartan royal house, a champion runner, and the father of Penelope, wife of Odysseus. His story links Sparta with Ithaca through the Odyssey and through several ancient genealogies.

The myth

Accounts of Icarius' parentage differ. According to the Bibliotheca, his parents were Perieres and Gorgophone, but elsewhere he is named a son of Oebalus and the naiad Bateia, and brother of Tyndareus and Hippocoon.

Hippocoon drove his brothers out of Lacedaemon. Icarius and Tyndareus fled to Thestius at Pleuron and helped him win lands beyond the river Achelous. When Heracles killed Hippocoon and his sons, Tyndareus went back to Sparta while Icarius stayed in Acarnania.

Icarius was a champion runner who allowed no suitor to marry his daughter unless he beat him in a race. Odysseus won, and took Penelope away, but Icarius followed them and begged his daughter to stay. Penelope said nothing and covered her face with a veil, a gesture Icarius read as her wish to leave with her husband. He let them go and set up a statue of Aidos, Modesty, on that spot.

Ancient sources: The Odyssey mentions Penelope and her sister Iphthime, and Strabo cites the lost Alcmeonis, which speaks of Icarius' sons in Acarnania.

Trivia

A statue to Modesty. According to the story, Icarius set up a statue of Aidos, Modesty, on the spot where he parted from his daughter.
A father with many names. The sources give up to three different wives for Icarius, and the lists of his children change from one author to another.
