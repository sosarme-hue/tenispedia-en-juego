daughter of Orius and mother of the Hamadryads

Hamadryas

In Greek mythology, Hamadryas is the daughter of Orius, a mountain god of Othrys or the Pindus, and the mother of the Hamadryads, nymphs tied to trees. Her name means "together with the tree" or "together with the oak."

The myth

According to the myth, Hamadryas bore eight daughters with her brother Oxylus, and they became known as the Hamadryads. Each was linked to a tree: Aigeiros to the poplar, Ampelos to the vine, Balanos to the acorn, Carya to the walnut, Kraneia to the cornel, Morea to the mulberry, Ptelea to the elm, and Syke to the fig. The Hamadryads were understood as tree nymphs, so each daughter was bound to a particular species, and the story explains why several trees bear the names of these sisters.

Legacy

Several trees carry names taken from these daughters, and Hamadryas' own name reflects the idea of a mother bound to the life of the forest.

Trivia

A name with trees inside. Hamadryas comes from the Greek words hama, meaning together with, and drys, which can mean holm oak or tree in general.
