Gilling

Gilling is a jötunn, a giant of Norse mythology, and the father of Suttungr, whose revenge for his death leads to the mead of poetry ending up in the giants' hands, a story the Prose Edda tells in detail. His name is linked to an Old Norse verb meaning 'to scream'.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, the dwarfs Fjalar and Galar first killed Gilling by trickery. They persuaded him to row out to the middle of a lake during an outing, then overturned his boat. While they clung to the wreckage and reached the shore, the giant, too clumsy to manage this, drowned.

His wife, hearing the news, wept loudly and could not be comforted. Galar, weary of her howling, ended her life by dropping a millstone on her head. Suttungr, his son, cunning and greedy, swore to avenge his father.

He tied the dwarfs to a rock that the tide was about to cover. To save themselves, they promised him the mead of poetry, and Suttungr agreed. A skaldic verse by Eyvindr skáldaspillir, from the 10th century, calls the mead 'Gilling's compensation'.

The tale turns a murder and a drowning into the origin of a famous prize, the mead of poetry, which the Prose Edda recounts in detail.

Legacy

Gilling is also a surname, though not a very common one.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál), which narrates his death, and a 10th-century skaldic verse by Eyvindr skáldaspillir, which mentions him.

Trivia

A name that screams. His name has been translated as 'screamer' and relates to the Old Norse verb gjalla, 'to scream, yell'.
Gilling's compensation. In Eyvindr skáldaspillir's verse, the mead of poetry is called 'Gilling's compensation'.
