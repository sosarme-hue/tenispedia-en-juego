demon queen of the leyaks in Balinese mythology

Rangda

Rangda is the demon queen of the leyaks, evil spirits in traditional Balinese mythology. Her battle against Barong, leader of the forces of good, is one of the most important figures in Balinese culture.

The myth

According to tradition, Rangda leads an army of evil witches against Barong. Their struggle is staged in the Barong dance, which portrays the eternal battle between good and evil. She is described as a mostly nude old woman, with long unkempt hair, pendulous breasts, claws, a fanged mask, bulging eyes and a long protruding tongue.

Many versions link her to Calon Arang, a feared widow in the Java of King Airlangga. Her daughter, Ratna Manggali, could not find a husband because people feared her mother. Angered, Calon Arang kidnapped a girl to sacrifice her to Durga, and a flood and disease followed. Empu Bahula married Ratna, and Empu Bharada recovered the witch's book of spells. Calon Arang was defeated without Durga's help. Another interpretation identifies her with Queen Mahendradatta, exiled by King Udayana and vengeful after being widowed.

Worship and religion

Her clashes with Barong are performed in traditional Balinese dances, which are also very popular tourist attractions.

Legacy

Her image appears in statues at Balinese temples and at Ngurah Rai International Airport, where a modern statue of Rangda greets travellers.

Trivia

A name with meaning. The name Rangda means widow in Old Javanese, a clue to her origin in the story of Calon Arang.
A welcome at the airport. A modern statue of Rangda greets travellers at Ngurah Rai International Airport in Bali.
