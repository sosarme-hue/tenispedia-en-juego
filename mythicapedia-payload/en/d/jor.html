Earth goddess in Norse mythology

Jörð

Jörð is the personification of the Earth in Norse mythology. The texts count her among the goddesses known as ásynjur, although they also describe her as a giantess, and she is the mother of the thunder god Thor.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the Prose Edda, Jörð is the daughter of Annar and Nótt, the goddess of night, and her lineage links her to the giants. Gylfaginning includes her among the ásynjur, and the poems present her as the mother of Thor, whom Skáldskaparmál calls the son of Odin and Jörð. The Poetic Edda, in Völuspá and Lokasenna, records the same parentage under the names Hlóðyn and Jörð.

Her relationship with Odin is more ambiguous. Tradition makes her his partner, although some scholars think it was an extramarital union and perhaps not a willing one. In skaldic poetry she appears as a rival of Frigg, and the kennings also call her Sif's mother-in-law. Some nineteenth-century scholars thought that Meili, Thor's brother, was her son too.

Legacy

In Richard Wagner's Das Rheingold the goddess appears under the name Erda. The word jörð is also the common term for "earth" in Old Norse and in modern Scandinavian languages such as Icelandic, Danish, Swedish and Norwegian.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Völuspá and Lokasenna), the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, which names her Iuritha.

Trivia

Erda in Wagner. Richard Wagner brought her back in Das Rheingold under the name Erda, a figure who knows the fate of the gods.
A different manuscript. In manuscript U of Gylfaginning, Jörð is the wife of Dellingr and mother of Dagr, while in the other three manuscripts Nótt holds those roles.
