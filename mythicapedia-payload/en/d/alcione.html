Alcyone

Alcyone is one of the Pleiades, the daughters of the Titan Atlas in Greek myth, and her descendants tie her figure to Boeotia, Thebes and Euboea. She is best remembered as the mother of Glaucus, who was transformed into a marine god, and her name gave English the word halcyon through a mistaken etymology.

The myth

Alcyone was one of the Pleiades, daughters of Atlas and Pleione, though some sources make her the daughter of Aethra instead. She caught the attention of Poseidon, who fathered several children with her: Hyrieus, Hyperenor and a daughter, Aethusa, whom Apollo also desired. From this line descend figures of Boeotia who came before Cadmus arrived, among them Amphion and Zethus in Thebes, Elephenor in Euboea and Iasus in Orchomenus.

Other authors give her different children, such as Hyperes, Anthas or Epopeus. In this version Alcyone takes the place of Canace, daughter of Aeolus. In another account, she is the wife of the mortal Anthedon and the mother of Glaucus, a fisherman who later became one of the sea gods. Her story thus links local genealogies with myths of the sea that grew together over time.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to a folk etymology connecting it with the sea, and from that confusion comes the English word halcyon, used for the kingfisher genus. Her link to the sea through Glaucus explains why her story is associated with navigation and the waters.

Trivia

A mistake that made a word. Her original name was written without an initial aspirate, but a false etymology linked it to the sea and it was spelled with an H. That mistaken form led to the English word halcyon.
From fisherman to sea god. Glaucus, son of Alcyone and a mortal, became a marine god in some versions, linking the mother to the world of the sea.
