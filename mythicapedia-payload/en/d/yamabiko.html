mountain spirit of Japanese folklore

Yamabiko

The yamabiko is a mountain god, spirit and yōkai in Japanese folklore, credited with the echo heard in mountains and canyons. Its name literally means "mountain boy".

The myth

It is described as a small, elusive creature living deep in the mountains, rarely seen but often heard. It is usually pictured with gray fur, a peach-colored belly, floppy ears, a broad grin and outstretched arms, as if caught mid-shrug.

It is sometimes imagined as a cross between a dog and a monkey. Depending on the region, its voice was linked to a bird, to a tree spirit (kodama) or to a rock that returns people's words. There is no single version: in Tottori it is associated with a bird, while in Kusuyama, Kōchi, a sudden dreadful voice from the mountain was called yamahiko.

Trivia

A yōkai that was barely classified. It was not officially classified until the Edo period. Before that, the strange noises from the mountains were attributed to natural causes, such as birds.
A rock that answers. In Nagano Prefecture there is a small rocky mountain called the "yamabiko rock", which returns the words of anyone who speaks to it.
