epithet of the Hindu mother goddess of the universe

Jagadamba

Jagadamba, meaning "mother of the world", is an epithet Hinduism uses to address several goddesses, above all Durga, Lakshmi and Parvati. It appears especially in devotional literature and in worship of these goddesses.

The myth

Jagadamba is not a character with a story of its own but a title applied to different forms of the mother goddess. Her figure is therefore understood through the stories of Durga, Lakshmi and Parvati.

In the Ramcharitmanas, Tulsidas's Awadhi version of the Ramayana, the title is spoken by Ravana. After the envoy Angada visits him, Ravana admits he abducted Sita, whom he calls Jagadamba, mother of the world and wife of Hari (Rama), and advises that she be returned to her husband with honour.

Worship and religion

The epithet is linked to temples dedicated to these goddesses across India, such as the Devi Jagadambi Temple in Khajuraho, the Mahalakshmi Ambabai Temple in Kolhapur, the Jagadamba Devi Temple in Kasaragod and the Mahalakshmi Jagdamba temple in Koradi, Nagpur. These shrines remain active places of devotion today.
