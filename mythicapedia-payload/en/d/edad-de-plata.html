second age of humankind in Greek mythology

Silver Age

The Silver Age is, in Greek mythology, the second of the Ages of Man described by Hesiod: a humanity less noble than the Golden Age, which disobeyed the gods and was destroyed by Zeus. The term has since been applied to later cultural periods.

The myth

According to Hesiod, after the Golden Age, the Olympian gods, led by Zeus, created a second race of men, silver because it was less noble than the one before. These humans lived for one hundred years as children under the care of their mothers, and their adult life was brief and marked by strife among themselves. They also refused to honour the immortal gods, so Zeus destroyed them for their impiety. After death, they became blessed spirits of the underworld.

Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, places this age after Saturn was driven into Tartarus, when Jupiter introduced the seasons. Men then learned to farm the land and build houses.

Legacy

The name has been applied to later cultural periods: a Silver Age of Latin literature, another of Spanish letters and sciences, of Russian poetry, of comic books and of mountaineering. In each case the phrase suggests a period of somewhat lesser quality than an earlier golden one.

Ancient sources: Hesiod in Works and Days and Ovid in the Metamorphoses describe this age within their accounts of the ages of humanity.

Trivia

Christian chronology. Saint Jerome placed the Silver Age between 1674 and 1628 BC, in a chronology that tried to connect the myths with history.
Reused metals. The terms Bronze Age and Iron Age, used today for prehistory, borrow the names of metals from these mythical ages.
