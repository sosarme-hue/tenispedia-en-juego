eldest son of Ravana, warrior of the Ramayana

Indrajit

Indrajit, also called Meghanada, is the eldest son of Ravana and crown prince of Lanka in Hindu tradition. He is one of the most celebrated warriors of the Ramayana, and his name means "conqueror of Indra".

The myth

According to the story, he was born with a cry that sounded like thunder, which gave him the name Meghanada, "the one who roars like a cloud". Ravana wanted his son born under favourable stars, but Shani disobeyed and settled in the twelfth house of his horoscope, foretelling a death at the hands of Lakshmana.

After defeating the devas, he captured Indra and kept him prisoner in Lanka until Brahma intervened. As a reward, Brahma granted him a fire ritual that would bring a celestial chariot and invulnerability in battle, but warned that whoever destroyed that sacrifice would kill him. Indrajit fought from the sky, hidden among the clouds, and with weapons such as the Nagapasha and the Brahmastra he defeated Rama and Lakshmana twice. The text says he killed 670 million vanaras in a single day. When Vibhishana alerted Rama to the secret sacrifice, Lakshmana burst into the shrine, and the mortally wounded Indrajit fell to his enemy.

Worship and religion

The central ritual the sources describe is a fire sacrifice to the goddess Pratyangira, performed in a secret temple inside a cave. No cult dedicated to him as a figure is described.

Legacy

He is the protagonist of the Bengali epic poem Meghnad Badh Kavya. In later versions of the Ramayana he is said to have married Sulochana, daughter of the serpent king Shesha Naga.

Ancient sources: The Valmiki Ramayana, which recounts the interrupted sacrifice and Lakshmana's intervention.

Trivia

Many names. Besides Indrajit and Meghanada, the sources give him the epithets Shakrajit, Ravani, Vasavajit, Varidanada and Ghananada.
A record toll. According to the text, no other warrior in the Ramayana achieved a comparable death toll in a single day.
Blame on Shani. Ravana blamed Shani for occupying the house of death in his son's horoscope, which the story treats as the origin of his tragic fate.
