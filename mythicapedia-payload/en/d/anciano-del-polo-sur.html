Taoist god of longevity, linked to the star Canopus

Old Man of the South Pole

The Old Man of the South Pole is the Taoist deification of Canopus, the brightest star in the constellation Carina. A symbol of happiness and long life across the Far East, he is one of the Seven Gods of Fortune in Japan, where he is known as Jurōjin.

The myth

According to legend, the Old Man of the South Pole was once a sickly boy named Zhao Yen, who had been predicted to die at nineteen. He was advised to take a jar of wine and some dried meat to a certain field, where he would find two men playing checkers under a tree. He was to offer them food and drink, but avoid answering their questions.

Zhao Yen followed the advice. To thank him, the two men agreed to swap the years of life assigned to him, giving him ninety-one instead of nineteen. He was later told that one of them was the star of the North Pole, which fixes the date of birth, and the other the star of the South Pole, which fixes the date of death.

In the novel Investiture of the Gods, he is the eldest disciple of Yuanshi Tianzun and descends to help King Wu against the deity Yin. In Journey to the West, his white deer escapes to the underworld and becomes a demon, until Sun Wukong defeats it and the Old Man takes it back.

Worship and religion

In Chinese popular religion he is often shown in paintings and figurines, frequently beside a deer. Some Chinese salvationist religions, such as Yiguandao, give him a role in bringing the Tao to beings of the Deva realm. His veneration remains part of Chinese family and festive life, although the sources do not describe specific temples or festivals.

Legacy

His image of a long-bearded old man is used as a symbol of good fortune. In Japan, Jurōjin is one of the deities of fortune. He appears in the novel Investiture of the Gods and in Journey to the West, and popular tradition links him to the legend of the white snake spirit Bai Suzhen, who went to his cave to steal medicinal herbs. The poet Du Fu also mentioned him in a poem.

Ancient sources: He appears in the novels Investiture of the Gods and Journey to the West, later works of Chinese tradition, as well as in a poem by Du Fu, the Tang dynasty poet.

Trivia

The colour red. Because red symbolises happiness and longevity in China, Canopus is also called the Star of Old Age in China and neighbouring Korea, Japan and Vietnam, although it is barely visible in northern China.
A fugitive deer. His white deer escaped to the underworld and became a demon before Sun Wukong defeated it.
