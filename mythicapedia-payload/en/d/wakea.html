Hawaiian sky father god, ancestor of the aliʻi

Wākea

Wākea is the Sky Father of Hawaiian religion, husband of Papahānaumoku, the Earth Mother. Together they are regarded as the parent couple of Hawaii's ruling chiefs, since Wākea is the ancestor of the aliʻi nobility.

The myth

According to tradition, Wākea was the eldest son of Kahiko, the Ancient One, who lived in Olalowaia. His union with Papahānaumoku gave rise to the islands of Hawaiʻi, Maui, Kauaʻi and Oʻahu, along with others such as Molokaʻi, Lānaʻi, Niʻihau, Lehua and Kaʻula, created at different moments.

Wākea had a daughter, Hoʻohokukalani, with whom he had a child, Haloa-naka, born stillborn. That child was planted and became the first kalo, or taro, a staple food. Later Haloa was born and became the first kanaka, or Hawaiian person. Departed souls were believed to travel to his home: the pure remained in his realm, while the others were sent to Lua-o-Milu.

Worship and religion

Wākea had a first high priest, Komoawa, and the traditional ʻaikapu, a sacred eating arrangement that separated men and women, is attributed to him.
