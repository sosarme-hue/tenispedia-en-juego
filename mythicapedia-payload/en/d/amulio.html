king of Alba Longa in Roman mythology

Amulius

Amulius is, in Roman mythology, the king of Alba Longa who seized the throne from his brother Numitor and ordered the deaths of his infant twin grandnephews, Romulus and Remus. He matters because his story frames the legend of Rome's founding, since the twins, raised after being abandoned, eventually overthrew and killed him.

The myth

According to tradition, Amulius was the son of Procas and the younger brother of Numitor. Taking advantage of Numitor's claim to the throne, he deposed him, killed his son and took power in Alba Longa. To prevent any heirs who could reclaim the crown, he forced Rhea Silvia, Numitor's daughter, to become a Vestal Virgin, whose vow of chastity was meant to stop her from bearing children.

Rhea Silvia nonetheless became pregnant, claiming she had been raped by Mars, although some accounts name Amulius himself, disguised in armour, as the attacker. She gave birth to twins. The king had her imprisoned and the boys thrown into the Tiber. The waters left them on dry land, where a she-wolf nursed them, and the shepherd Faustulus and his wife later raised them. As adults, Romulus and Remus learned their origins, joined their grandfather Numitor, killed Amulius and restored Numitor to the throne. They then founded Rome, where a quarrel between the brothers ended with Remus's death at Romulus's hands.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus, a Greek historian of the first century BC, gives the most detailed account of the king's schemes, while Plutarch, quoting Fabius Pictor, relates the story of the foundation.

Trivia

A king with a date. Dionysius of Halicarnassus places Amulius's reign at 41 years, ending around 752 BC.
An attack in disguise. In one version, Amulius himself, wearing armour to hide his identity, is the one who rapes Rhea Silvia.
