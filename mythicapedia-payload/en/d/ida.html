nymph of Greek mythology, nurse of Zeus

Ida

Ida is a nymph of Greek mythology, one of the nurses who raised the infant Zeus on Crete. Her name is linked to Mount Ida on the island, so she is also tied to the Cretan tradition of the god's birth.

The myth

According to the tradition, Rhea, alarmed by what Cronus did to his children, gave birth to Zeus in a cave on Mount Dicte in Crete and entrusted his upbringing to the Curetes and to the nymphs Adrasteia and Ida, daughters of Melisseus. They fed the child with the milk of the goat Amalthea.

The sources disagree about her parentage. According to Hyginus, Ida and Adrasteia, along with Amalthea, were daughters of Oceanus, though others said they were daughters of Melisseus. Diodorus Siculus tells that Zeus, grateful, turned Ida and Adrasteia into the constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor. Pausanias places her on the altar of Athena Alea at Tegea, among the nymphs attending Rhea in caring for the infant.

Legacy

Her name is connected to the Cretan mountain of the same name, a sacred place in the tradition of Zeus's birth.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus, Pausanias and Diodorus Siculus record her role as a nurse of Zeus.
