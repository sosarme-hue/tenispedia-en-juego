legendary archer of Chinese mythology

Hou Yi

Hou Yi is a legendary archer of Chinese mythology, sometimes called the Lord Archer and presented at times as a god of archery and at others as a xian sent from heaven to help humankind. He is best known for shooting down the surplus suns and for being married to Chang'e, one of the lunar deities.

The myth

According to the myth, there were once ten suns that crossed the sky in turn. One day they all came out together to play with each other and scorched the earth. Emperor Yao, or in some versions the Jade Emperor, ordered Hou Yi to rein them in. He first tried reasoning with them and, when that failed, pretended to shoot at them; when they kept defying him, he brought them down one by one. Each fallen sun turned into a three-legged crow. Only one was left, and Xihe, mother of the suns, together with Yao, asked that it be spared for the good of mankind.

He also hunted down or imprisoned beasts such as Yayu, Zaochi and Dafeng, which were harming people. The gods had given him the pill of immortality. One day, while he was out hunting, his apprentice Feng Meng tried to steal it, but his wife Chang'e swallowed it first and rose up to the Moon.

In another version, the people proclaimed him a hero-king after his feat, but he became a tyrant. Chang'e, fearing he would live forever and keep oppressing people, drank an elixir obtained from Xiwangmu and floated away. Hou Yi tried to shoot her down with an arrow, but missed.

Legacy

He appears in the poem Heavenly Questions from the anthology Chu Ci, which asks why he killed the river god Hebo and took his wife Luoshen, and in reliefs at the Wu Liang Shrines. Chang'e, linked to the Moon, is honored during the Mid-Autumn Festival.

Ancient sources: The poem Heavenly Questions in the Chu Ci anthology mentions him, and the Bamboo Annals record him as a tribal leader who attacked the Xia dynasty.

Trivia

Three-legged crows. According to the stories, each sun he shot down turned into a three-legged crow.
An archer in the chronicles. The Bamboo Annals describe him as a tribal leader who attacked the Xia dynasty, and some recent research suggests the myth of the ten suns arose from a miswriting.
