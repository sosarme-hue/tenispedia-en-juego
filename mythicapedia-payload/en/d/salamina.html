nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Asopus

Salamis

Salamis is a nymph of Greek mythology, a naiad and daughter of the river god Asopus, whose name was given to the island of the same name in the Saronic Gulf. Her story explains how the island got its name and who became its first king.

The myth

According to the myth, Salamis was the daughter of the river god Asopus, who had settled in Phlius, and of Metope, herself the daughter of another river god, Ladon. Asopus and Metope had many daughters, and Salamis was one of them; her sisters included Corcyra, Aegina and Thebe, names that were later also attached to islands and cities.

One day Poseidon fell in love with her and carried her off to an island in the Saronic Gulf, opposite Eleusis and stretching towards Megaris. There Salamis gave birth to Cychreus, also called Cencreus, her son by the sea god. When he grew up, Cychreus became king of the island and named it after his mother.

In a minority version, Salamis became the mother of Saracon, also called Saracontes, the child of Zeus. In this way the tradition ties the island's name to a nymph and to the gods who took part in her story.

Trivia

Many sisters. Salamis had numerous sisters, among them Corcyra, Aegina and Thebe. According to some versions, Poseidon carried off several of them from their father's house.
An island named after a nymph. The island in the Saronic Gulf bears Salamis's name because her son Cychreus named it after her when he became king.
