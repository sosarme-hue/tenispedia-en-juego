Vedic god of the sun, roads and cattle

Pushan

Pushan is a Hindu Vedic deity linked originally to the sun and later one of the twelve Adityas, the sons of Aditi. He matters because he is one of the few benevolent gods of the Vedas, a guardian of travelers and a guide of souls to the other world.

The myth

In the Rig-veda, Pushan watches over herds, helps recover lost cattle and protects travelers from bandits and wild beasts. His chariot is pulled by goats, and at times he is described as driving the Sun across the sky. As a knower of paths, he knows the route between earth and heaven and leads the souls of the dead to the other world. He is also associated with marriages, journeys and the feeding of cattle.

According to the Taittiriya Samhita, Rudra was left out of a sacrifice and, in anger, shot an arrow that broke Pushan's teeth as he tried to eat part of the offering. In the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Puranas the story grows: Shiva, angry that his father-in-law Daksha had not invited him, kicks Pushan in the mouth, or his creation Virabhadra knocks out his teeth. That is why Pushan is toothless and lives on a gruel of grain.

Worship and religion

Vedic rituals invoked him to ask for protection of cattle and safe roads. The sources do not describe temples, festivals or a living cult dedicated to him.

Legacy

Some modern scholars link his name to the Greek god Pan, an idea first proposed by the German scholar Hermann Collitz in 1924.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rig-veda, the Taittiriya Samhita, the Shatapatha Brahmana, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Puranas.

Trivia

A toothless god. In later versions, Pushan lost his teeth in a dispute over a sacrifice with Rudra or Shiva. The Brahmanas say he eats a grain gruel because of it, and he is called karambhād.
A name for the Sun. According to the Balarama-ayana and the Kadambari, the common noun pushan is a name of the Sun as a celestial body, in line with his solar origin.
