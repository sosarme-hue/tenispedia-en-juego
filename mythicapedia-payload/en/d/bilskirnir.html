Thor's hall in Norse mythology

Bilskirnir

Bilskirnir is the hall of Thor in Norse mythology, the home where the god lives with his wife Sif and their children. Located in Asgard, it stands out for its enormous size, and Snorri Sturluson used it to form one of the poetic names of Thor.

The myth

According to the Norse sources, Bilskirnir was Thor's residence, where he lived with Sif and their children. The hall stood in Þrúðheimr, also called Þrúðvangar, within Asgard, like the other dwellings of the gods.

The Grímnismál describes it as the greatest of all buildings and says it had 540 rooms. Its construction answered a specific need: it had to be tall enough to accommodate Thor's great height. Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning repeats this description and even reuses verses from the Grímnismál to portray the hall.

Among the poetic names of Thor, Snorri also records the kenning 'owner of Bilskirnir', which shows how closely the hall was tied to the god's identity. There is no agreement on the meaning of the name: it has been suggested that it means 'suddenly lit' or 'eternal'.

Legacy

The name inspired that of an Asgard starship commanded by Supreme Commander Thor in the television series Stargate SG-1, where it appears as the Beliskner. It is also the name of a black metal band from Hesse, Germany.

Ancient sources: The Grímnismál and Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning in the Prose Edda describe the hall and its rooms.

Trivia

A starship. In the series Stargate SG-1, an Asgard ship takes its name from Thor's hall and appears as the Beliskner.
A name with several readings. Its Old Norse name has been translated as 'lightning-crack', though scholars are not certain of its meaning.
