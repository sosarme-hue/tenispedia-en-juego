wife of King Phoroneus of Argos

Cerdo

In Greek mythology, Cerdo (Κερδοῦς, Kerdous) was the wife of Phoroneus, a hero and king of Argos. She is mainly remembered for the cult she received in that city and for the many names her sources give her.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cerdo was the consort of King Phoroneus of Argos. The sources do not agree on who she was. Pausanias names her as his wife, while Hyginus gives Cinna as Phoroneus's wife, and the scholia on Euripides speak of a first marriage to Peito and a second to Europa. Other accounts name Teledice, Perimede or Peitho. Cerdo is also said to have had Apis and Niobe as children.

Worship and religion

Pausanias reports that a monument and a tomb were built in her honour in the agora of Argos, next to a temple of Asclepius. The tomb suggests she received worship in the city, although the sources give no details of the rites.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes her tomb in his account of Greece, while Hyginus and the scholia on Euripides preserve other versions of her marriage to Phoroneus.

Trivia

A tomb in the marketplace. Her tomb stood in the agora of Argos, the centre of public life, near a temple dedicated to Asclepius.
A wife with many names. Sources call her by different names, such as Cinna, Teledice, Perimede or Peitho, showing that the tradition never settled on a single version.
