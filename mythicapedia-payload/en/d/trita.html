minor deity of the Vedic pantheon

Trita

Trita ("the Third") is a minor deity of the Rigveda, mentioned about forty-one times. He appears alongside Indra, the Maruts and Vayu, and is linked with the waters.

The myth

According to the texts, Trita is one of the Āptya, that is, one of the children of the waters, or Apas. The Shatapatha Brahmana tells that they were born from Agni's anger at the waters: the fire god, after the priests who had chosen him died one after another, hid himself in the waters, and the gods forcibly pulled him out. When he spat on the waters, Trita, Dvita and Ekata came into being.

Trita followed Indra as a Brahman follows a king's retinue. The same text says that when Indra slew Visvarūpa, the three-headed son of Tvashtri, it was Trita who struck him down, and the guilt fell on Trita, while Indra was free from it because he is a god.

In another story, Trita falls into a well and begs the gods for help. According to Sayana's commentary, his companions Ekata and Dvita pushed him in and trapped him there. Inside, he composed a hymn and miraculously prepared the Soma for the sacrifice. Another hymn asks Dawn to send away bad dreams: Trita eats them and wears them as ornaments on his body.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in the Rigveda, where he appears about forty times, and the Shatapatha Brahmana recounts his origin; the Mahabharata alludes to his episode in the well.

Trivia

Devoured dreams. A Rigveda hymn asks Dawn to drive away bad dreams. Trita eats them and wears them on his body as ornaments.
The well. According to Sayana's commentary, Trita was trapped in a well by his two companions and composed a hymn to the gods there.
