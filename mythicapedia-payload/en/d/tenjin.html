Japanese kami and patron of scholarship

Tenjin

Tenjin is the Shinto kami of Japan who patronizes scholars, learning and education. He is the deified form of Sugawara no Michizane (845-903), a poet, politician and scholar of the Heian period, whose spirit was first worshipped to placate natural disasters.

The myth

In the late ninth century Sugawara no Michizane rose high in government, but at the start of the tenth he fell victim to the schemes of a rival from the Fujiwara clan. He was demoted and exiled to Kyushu, where he died in 903.

After his death, a run of misfortunes struck the capital. The death of Crown Prince Yasuakira in 923 was widely blamed on Michizane's vengeful spirit (goryō), and in 930 a storm of lightning, fires and floods killed several leading Fujiwara. The court concluded that he was responsible, restored his offices, burned the order of exile and ordered him worshipped as Tenjin, meaning sky deity.

Worship and religion

His cult, Tenjin Shinkō, spread through oracular pronouncements and merged the older thunder and weather deity Tenjin raijin with the poet's spirit. The Kitano shrine became the first of the official shrines, supported directly by the government. For centuries he was venerated mainly to avoid his curses, but in the Edo period scholars and educators came to see him as a patron of learning. Today his worship centres on exams: many students and their parents pray at his shrines before entrance tests and return to give thanks if they pass. His memorial day, 25 February, is marked with ceremonies at temples across Japan.

Legacy

Michizane loved ume (plum) trees. According to legend, a plum tree he missed in the capital flew to Dazaifu to be with him, and it is still displayed at his shrine there. Many Tenjin shrines are planted with plum trees, which bloom in February, the same time exam results are announced. His animal is the bull: legend says the ox pulling his funeral cart refused to go further, and a shrine was built on that spot.

Trivia

The flying plum tree. Legend says a plum tree from the capital flew to Dazaifu to join the exiled poet. The tree is still on show at Dazaifu Tenman-gū.
The stubborn bull. According to legend, during Michizane's funeral procession the bull pulling the cart refused to go further. A shrine was built on that spot, and the bull is linked with Tenjin.
