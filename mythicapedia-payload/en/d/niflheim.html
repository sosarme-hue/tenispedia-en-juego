Norse realm of ice and mist

Niflheim

Niflheim, or "Home of Mist", is a primordial realm of ice, cold and darkness in Norse mythology, wrapped in perpetual fog. It matters because, together with the fiery realm of Muspelheim, it forms the setting in which, according to tradition, creation began.

The myth

According to Norse accounts, Niflheim was one of the first worlds to emerge from the primordial void of Ginnungagap. Its extreme cold, with frozen rivers called Élivágar and the well Hvergelmir, from which all rivers flow, contrasted with the heat of Muspelheim. When the icy waters of Niflheim met the flames of Muspelheim in the abyss, a creating steam formed, from which the first giant, Ymir, arose.

At the bottom of this realm lives the dragon Níðhöggr, who gnaws without rest at the roots of the world tree Yggdrasil. After Ragnarök, the texts say, it will torment the souls left in the world.

Beneath Niflheim lies Helheim, the realm of the dead who did not die a heroic or notable death. In Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning, the goddess Hel, daughter of Loki, was cast into Niflheim by Odin and rules over the dead there, although in some manuscript versions the place is called Niflhel, the lowest level of Hel.

Legacy

The name survives in modern Icelandic, where nifl means mist, and it is related to words in other Germanic languages and to the Greek nephélē, "cloud".

Ancient sources: It appears in Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning and briefly in the Hrafnagaldr Óðins; the Poetic Edda mentions Hel and Níðhöggr but not Niflheim.

Trivia

A name meaning mist. Niflheim means "home of mist". The word nifl survives in Icelandic with the sense of mist.
