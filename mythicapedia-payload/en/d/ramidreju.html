Cantabrian mythological creature

Ramidreju

The ramidreju is a fantastic creature of Cantabrian tradition, a long, weasel-like animal said to live in the mountains and forests of northern Spain. It is best known for the remarkable properties attributed to its bile.

The myth

According to the accounts, the ramidreju is born once every hundred years from a weasel or a marten. Its long, slender body is snake-like, its fur is striped in black and green, and it has a hog's snout, yellow eyes and boar-like tusks, which it uses to dig very deep tunnels. The Cantabrian writer Manuel Llano made it known through a story that he said he had heard from a neighbour in Viaña, in Cabuérniga.

Tradition holds that its bile can cure every illness and reveal hidden treasure, so the anjanas of the Cieza valley drink it. Another version stresses the value of its fur, which heals any sickness, and the creature's strong craving for gold.
