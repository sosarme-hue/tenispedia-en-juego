Vedic god of storms, hunting and medicine

Rudra

Rudra is a deity of the Rigveda, the oldest text of India, associated with wind, storms, hunting and healing. His figure, both fearsome and capable of curing, lies at the root of Shiva, one of the great gods of Hinduism.

The myth

In the Vedic hymns Rudra appears as a powerful and frightening god, praised as the "mightiest of the mighty". He is shown armed with a bow and swift arrows, and his arrows are said to be lightning flashing across heaven and earth. People believed he sent illnesses, but also that he cured them: the hymns credit him with healing remedies and call him the best physician of physicians, which gave him the alternative name Vaidyanatha, "Lord of Remedies".

The hymns beg him for mercy, asking that he spare children from sickness and keep villages free of disease. Some texts call him Lord of the Universe and place him in the heart of the gods, while the Taittiriya Aranyaka identifies him with the supreme self of the Vedas. In the Rigveda he is called father of the Maruts, the storm gods, although other passages make him a son of the Earth alongside Brihaspati.

Worship and religion

In later tradition, a hymn dedicated to Rudra, known as the Shri Rudram or Namakam, was recited during the agnicayana ritual, the building of the fire altar, and became a standard part of Rudra liturgy. His worship has been especially important in the Shaivite tradition, where it remains alive today.

Legacy

His name gave rise to that of Shiva, who was first a euphemistic epithet for Rudra. The rudraksha, the "eye of Rudra", is the seed of the Elaeocarpus ganitrus tree and gives its name to prayer beads made from it.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda, which devotes three full hymns to him, and in the Yajurveda, which contains the Shri Rudram hymn.

Trivia

Eleven. In later texts, the word rudra can simply mean the number eleven.
Rudra's beads. Prayer beads made from rudraksha seeds take their name from the god.
