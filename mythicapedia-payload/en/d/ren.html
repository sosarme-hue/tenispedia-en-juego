component of the spirit in Egyptian mythology

Ren

In Egyptian belief, Ren was the name of a person and one of the components of the human spirit. The Egyptians held that as long as the name was spoken, the deceased did not fully vanish.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, the human being was made up of several spiritual components: the Ib or heart, the Ka or vital force, the Ba or soul-force, the Akh or spiritual force, the Ren or name and identity, and the Sheut or shadow. The Ren was given at birth, but it could change over a lifetime, as a person received new titles or gained recognition for their deeds.

Because the name was what allowed a person to endure, pharaohs and other powerful figures had it inscribed again and again on monuments, tombs and documents. For the same reason, damnatio memoriae, the erasure of a name, was a very severe punishment: the case of Akhenaten is the most cited example, since after his death his name was removed wherever it appeared. A part of the Book of Breathings, drawn from the Book of the Dead, gives instructions for ensuring the name survived.

Ancient sources: The Egyptian Book of the Dead, from which part of the Book of Breathings derives.

Trivia

A sign with no clear meaning. The hieroglyph for Ren combines a mouth sign, representing the R, and a wavy line, representing the N. It is not known for certain whether its value was purely phonetic or whether it also carried a symbolic meaning.
