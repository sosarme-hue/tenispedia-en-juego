Yoruba goddess of rivers and love

Òṣun

Òṣun is one of the most popular deities of the Yoruba religion, goddess of the river that bears her name and of fresh waters, associated with love, fertility, beauty and wealth. Her worship crossed the Atlantic with the African diaspora, and in Cuba she is syncretized with Our Lady of Charity of El Cobre.

The myth

According to the Ifa literary corpus, Òṣun was the only female irunmole sent by Olodumare to help Shango create the world. The other spirits ignored her. In one version their efforts failed because they acted without male spiritual leadership; in another, the male spirits tried to make the world without female influence, and that exclusion caused the failure. The story ends with Shango forcing everyone to respect her as they respected him. After her death, Olodumare granted her the powers of an orisha.

Another story, preserved in Cuban tradition, tells that the orishas excluded women from their meetings. Offended, Òṣun made women infertile, and the gods' projects failed. Olodumare explained that nothing would prosper without her power over fertility, so she agreed to take part after much pleading. Women conceived again and their works succeeded.

Worship and religion

She is venerated at rivers, waterfalls and springs. Her principal sanctuary is the Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove in Nigeria, where a two-week festival is held every August. In Cuba and Brazil her followers wear beads in gold tones and bronze bracelets, Saturday is her day, and offerings include sweets, honey and shrimp dishes. Her worship remains alive today.

Legacy

In Brazil she is known as the Lady of Gold, a title linked to copper, once the most valuable metal of its time, and she is invoked for love and money matters. Her syncretism with Our Lady of Charity of El Cobre is one of the best-known examples of Afro-Cuban religion.

Ancient sources: The Ifa literary corpus, the oral and written tradition of the Yoruba that preserves her narratives and sacred verses.

Trivia

Osogbo festival. The annual festival in the Osogbo sacred grove draws devotees for two weeks and is one of the major events of Yoruba worship.
Cuban syncretism. In Cuba, the river goddess is identified with the island's patroness, Our Lady of Charity of El Cobre.
