wife of Metapontus in Greek myth

Theano

Theano is the wife of King Metapontus and queen of Icaria, a small island in the Aegean Sea. Her story, tied to the twin sons of Poseidon and Melanippe, makes her one of the darker figures in tales of deception and adoption.

The myth

Theano, married to Metapontus, could not give him children, and the king threatened to drive her out unless she produced an heir. To keep her place, she had servants find a baby and presented it as her own. The servants found twin boys, sons of Poseidon and Melanippe, abandoned by their maternal grandfather; she named them Aeolus and Boeotus.

Soon after, she bore twin sons of her own, but Metapontus continued to favour the adopted boys, who grew handsome and strong. Theano wanted the kingdom to pass to her own blood. While the king was making a sacrifice to Artemis Metapontina, she told her sons the truth and ordered them to kill their brothers during a hunt. Aeolus and Boeotus, aided by Poseidon, overpowered their half-brothers. When Theano saw the bodies, she took her own life with a hunting knife. Poseidon later revealed the truth to the twins, who were reunited with Melanippe; she married Metapontus and became the new queen of Icaria.

Ancient sources: Euripides staged the story in his lost tragedy Captive Melanippe, and Hyginus recounts it in his Fabulae.

Trivia

A lost tragedy. Euripides wrote about this story in Captive Melanippe, a play that has not survived. It is known only through fragments and other ancient versions.
