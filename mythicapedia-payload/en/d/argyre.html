legendary island of silver in Greek geography

Argyre

Argyre is a legendary island of silver that Greek and Roman geography and mythology placed in the Indian Ocean, alongside Chryse, the island of gold. Its name comes from the Greek argyros, "silver", and the legend survived for centuries thanks to Latin authors.

The myth

According to Pliny the Elder, the islands of Chryse and Argyre lay beyond the mouth of the Indus River and were rich in metals, although he doubted that their soil was really gold and silver. Solinus and Isidore of Seville repeated the report, and the legend survived in Europe throughout the Middle Ages.

As geographers placed the Indian Ocean more accurately, the islands drifted eastward, reaching the vicinity of Japan on Martin Behaim's 1492 globe. Some have proposed identifying them with the Burmese coast of Rakhine or the Malay Peninsula, and others with Salakanagara on Java, whose name means "island of silver" in Sanskrit.

Legacy

In 1877 the astronomer Giovanni Schiaparelli gave these names to light patches on Mars. The Mariner 9 probe showed in 1972 that they were not islands, but the names survive in Chryse Planitia and Argyre Planitia.

Ancient sources: Pliny the Elder's Natural History (Book 6), along with Pomponius Mela, Solinus and Isidore of Seville's Etymologies.

Trivia

An island that kept moving. As maps of the Indian Ocean improved, the imagined location of Argyre shifted further and further east, to the edge of the known world.
A name on Mars. The light patches Schiaparelli named in 1877 are not islands but depressions covered in wind-blown dust.
