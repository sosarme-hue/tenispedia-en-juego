minor Roman god of hearths and brick ovens

Lateranus

Lateranus is an ancient minor Roman god, guardian of hearths and Genius of brick ovens. He is barely known, mainly from a satirical passage by the Christian writer Arnobius, which makes him one of the least documented figures in Roman religion.

The myth

Arnobius presents him as a god who moves through people's kitchens. According to that satirical passage, he checks which woods feed the fire, keeps clay vessels from shattering in the flames, and tastes the food and sauces like a taster.

His name is linked to the Latin stem later-, meaning «brick», as in opus latericium, a type of brickwork. Arnobius uses him to mock the idea that only hearths of baked clay could have genii, and he suggests the god owes his name to hearths built of unbaked brick.

Worship and religion

Wilhelm Heinrich Roscher places him among the indigitamenta, lists of deities kept by the Roman priestly college so that the correct divine name was invoked in public prayers. No temples or festivals of his own are known.

Ancient sources: Arnobius, a Christian writer, is the only ancient author who describes him in detail; no other ancient writer mentions him.

Trivia

A name made of brick. His name comes from later-, «brick», the same root as opus latericium, a Roman brickwork technique.
A satirical source. Arnobius, a Christian writer, mentions him in a satirical passage that is the main surviving description of him.
