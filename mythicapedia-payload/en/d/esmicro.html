father of Branchus in Greek mythology

Smicrus

Smicrus is a figure of Greek mythology connected to the oracles of Apollo at Delphi and Didyma. He is the father of Branchus, a beloved of the god who founded a line of famous seers.

The myth

According to the myth, Smicrus was either the son of Democlus, a man from Delphi, or of an unnamed man from the island of Ceos. An oracle told Democlus to sail for Miletus, and he took his thirteen-year-old son along. After lunch, he was in such a hurry to leave that he forgot Smicrus on shore.

In despair, the boy wandered until a goatherd's son led him to his father, Eritharses, also called Patronus. Eritharses took him in with affection and set him to tend goats alongside his own sons. One day the boys caught a swan and dressed it in a garment, then argued over who would give it to Eritharses. Tired of fighting, they removed the cloth and found that the swan was the sea goddess Leucothea, who, delighted by their rivalry, asked the Milesians to honour her with athletic contests for boys. Smicrus later married and had a son, Branchus, whose birth was foretold by his mother's dream. Branchus won the favour of Apollo, founded an oracle near Didyma, and became the ancestor of the Branchidae, a clan of great prophets.

Worship and religion

He is credited with establishing athletic contests for boys in honour of Leucothea. The Branchid priests of Didyma enjoyed great prestige among the Ionians and Aeolians of western Anatolia, and Apollo's oracle at Didyma was linked to the god's solar connections.

Ancient sources: His story survives through Conon's lost Narrations and a lost work by Varro, both preserved in quotations by Photius of Constantinople.

Trivia

A name with a false etymology. The name Branchus was falsely linked to a Greek word meaning windpipe, based on his mother's dream.
Two origins for the same lineage. According to Conon, the Delphic link may have been an attempt to claim credit for founding the Didyma oracle, something the older tradition did not include.
