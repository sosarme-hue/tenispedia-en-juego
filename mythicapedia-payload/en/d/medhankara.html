second Buddha of the Sāramaṇḍa kalpa

Medhaṅkara

Medhaṅkara is the second of the Buddhas of the past that certain Buddhist traditions place before the historical Gautama, in the Sāramaṇḍa kalpa. The Pali canon mentions him briefly as one of the Buddhas born in that age.

The myth

According to the legend, Medhaṅkara was born in Yaghara to King Sudeva and Queen Yasodharā. He ruled for 8,000 years, until seeing the Four Sights led him to leave the palace. As soon as his son was born, he withdrew to the forest to practise asceticism for fifteen days and attained enlightenment beneath a bodhi tree, Butea monosperma.

A future incarnation of Gautama met him and became his disciple, asking for a wish, which Medhaṅkara did not grant. After death, that incarnation was reborn as a deva in the desire realm. Medhaṅkara lived 90,000 years, liberated many beings and attained parinibbāna together with his disciples.

Ancient sources: The Buddhavamsa, a Pali text listing past Buddhas, mentions him briefly.
