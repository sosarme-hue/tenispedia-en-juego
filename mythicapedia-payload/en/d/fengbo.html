Taoist god of the wind

Fengbo

Fengbo, also known as Fengshi, is the Taoist god of the wind in Chinese tradition. In ancient times he was the object of state rituals, and in Ming mythology he appears as an old man who directs the winds as he pleases.

The myth

Early depictions gave him the body of a deer, a bird's head, horns, a snake's tail and leopard markings. Over time his role passed to Feng Po Po. In Ming mythology he is an old man with a white beard who holds a fan and a goatskin bag full of winds.

In his dragon form, Fei Lian, he is a monster who, according to one legend, stirred up storms in the south until Shen Yi shot an arrow into his knee and defeated him. Another version makes him a minister of the tyrant Chou, killed along with his son while serving that king.

Worship and religion

According to the Records of the Grand Historian, he was the object of state rituals from early times, with temples and festivals held in his honor.

Ancient sources: The Records of the Grand Historian (Sima Qian) mentions him as an object of state ritual, and the Han Feizi recalls him sweeping the path when the Yellow Emperor gathers the demons at Mount Tai.

Trivia

A bag of winds. In Ming mythology, Fengbo keeps the winds in a goatskin bag and releases them as he chooses.
A god with two forms. Fengbo appears as an animal-like being in ancient depictions and as the dragon Fei Lian in other traditions.
