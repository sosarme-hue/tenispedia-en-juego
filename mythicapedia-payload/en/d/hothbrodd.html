legendary Norse hero and king

Hothbrodd

Hothbrodd is a legendary hero and king of Norse tradition, son of King Granmar and rival of Helgi Hundingsbane. His story survives in several versions, reflecting the feuds between royal lines that run through the Northern heroic legends.

The myth

In the Poetic Edda, Hothbrodd is the son of King Granmar, and his brother is Guðmundr; some accounts also name Starkaðr. He is engaged to Sigrún, a valkyrie and daughter of King Högni, but she rejects the match and urges Helgi Hundingsbane to attack him.

Helgi gathers a large army, crosses the sea and defeats Hothbrodd in a bloody battle, aided by valkyries. In the Volsunga saga, Helgi kills Hothbrodd beneath his banner at Wolfstone, and Sigrún celebrates his death. According to the tradition, all of Granmar's sons fall in the fighting.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Helgakviða Hundingsbana I and II poems of the Poetic Edda, in the Völsunga saga, in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, where he is given a son called Hother, and in Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla.
