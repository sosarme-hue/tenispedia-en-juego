winged dog-like creature of Armenian mythology

Aralez

The aralez are winged, dog-like creatures from Armenian mythology that were said to descend upon dead heroes and lick their wounds to bring them back to life. They are tied to the legend of the king Ara the Handsome.

The myth

The best-known story links them to Ara the Handsome. According to the tale, after he was killed in battle, the Assyrian queen Semiramis (Shamiram) called on the aralez to lick his wounds and revive him. By popular interpretation, this is where their name came from, linked to the verb 'to lick'.

The aralez were said to live on Mount Ararat, a sacred landmark in Armenia. According to the Armenian history attributed to Faustus of Byzantium, after Mushegh Mamikonian was killed, his relatives placed his corpse on a tower, hoping the aralez would revive him.

Worship and religion

Placing the dead on a tower suggests that belief in the aralez was still current in fourth- and fifth-century Armenia, after the country was Christianized. Some tower-like structures in Armenia and Anatolia have been suggested as linked to this belief.

Ancient sources: The Armenian history attributed to Faustus of Byzantium, written in the fourth or fifth century, records the episode of Mushegh Mamikonian.

Trivia

Towers and resurrection. The episode of Mushegh Mamikonian shows his relatives placing his body on a tower, hoping the aralez would bring him back to life.
A disputed name. The explanation of the name from 'Ara' and the verb 'to lick' is a folk etymology. The origin of the word remains disputed.
