Slavic god of vegetation and springtime

Yarilo

Yarilo is a Slavic god of vegetation, fertility and springtime, whose name is linked to the idea of a youthful life-force. He is known from fragmentary evidence, and his existence as a clearly defined deity is considered partly uncertain.

The myth

Tradition places him at the center of nature's renewal after winter, the growth of plants and the fertility of the land. His name comes from the Slavic root jar or yar, meaning spring or summer, but also strength, fury or youthful vitality. The pre-Christian Slavs regarded this energy as sacred, and the god who embodied it was called Jarovit, or affectionately Jarilo.

His name varies by region and language: Yaryla, Iarilo, Larilo or Gerovit, and in some traditions Juraj or Jurij. This variety suggests the figure was passed on orally and locally, with no single, fixed story surviving intact.

Worship and religion

In pre-Christian Slavic religion, the youthful force Yarilo embodied was considered sacred, and the god who personified it held a place in the religious life of his people. Concrete data on temples, rites or festivals in his honour are scarce.
