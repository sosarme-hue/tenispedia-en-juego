nightmare spirit of Slavic mythology

Nocnitsa

The Nocnitsa, or "Night Witch", is a nightmare spirit from Slavic mythology that torments people at night, especially children.

The myth

According to folklore, the Nocnitsa settles on the chest of a sleeper and slowly draws away their life energy, which is why many regard her as a kind of vampire. She tends to return again and again. Some traditions say she visits people who sleep on their backs with their hands folded over their chest, a posture known as "sleeping with the dead". She is described as a shadowy figure with a screeching voice and the smell of moss and earth from the forest she came from.

Worship and religion

There was no formal cult, but there were protective customs. In some regions mothers placed a knife in the cradle or drew a circle around it with a knife, perhaps because of the belief that supernatural beings could not touch iron. People also believed that a stone with a hole in its centre offered protection against her.

Legacy

She appears in Neil Gaiman's novel Coraline, where she takes the form of the protagonist's mother in an alternate world; in the horror film Slumber (2017); in John Passarella's novel Night Terror; and in the series The Iron Druid Chronicles.

Trivia

A protective stone. Tradition held that a stone with a hole in its centre protected against the Nocnitsa. In Coraline, the heroine carries such a stone, which shields her from the false mother.
A Bulgarian name. In Bulgaria she is known as Gorska Makua, a name different from those used in the eastern and western Slavic regions.
