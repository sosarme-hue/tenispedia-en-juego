Greek personification of sloth

Aergia

Aergia is, in Greek mythology, the personification of sloth, idleness and laziness. Her name translates the Latin concepts socordia and ignavia, so she is considered both a Greek and a Roman figure.

The myth

Aergia appears only rarely in ancient sources. According to Hyginus, she was a child of Aether and Earth, counted among the primordial forces alongside abstractions such as Grief, Wrath and Deceit.

Statius places her in the Underworld, in a deep rocky cave where the halls of Sleep stand. There she guards the entrance alongside Quiet, Oblivion and Ease, her face always drowsy.

Another tradition, recorded by J.F.M. Nöel, says Hephaestus turned her into a tortoise for praising her too much, an allusion to Chelone, the nymph famous for her laziness.

Legacy

As a personified abstraction, her natural counterpart was Horme, the spirit of effort. She was depicted with a drowsy face, draped in cobwebs and beside broken spindles, a symbol of her aversion to work.

Ancient sources: Hyginus lists her among the children of Aether and Earth, and Statius describes her as a guardian in the home of Sleep.

Trivia

A goddess without a cult. Aergia appears very rarely in ancient sources, and there is no record of temples or rites in her honor.
A translated name. Her Greek name translates two Latin words, which is why she is considered both Greek and Roman.
