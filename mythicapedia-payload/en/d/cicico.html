mythical king of the Doliones in Greek mythology

Cyzicus

Cyzicus was, in Greek mythology, the king of the Doliones, a people who lived on an island in Phrygia, in Asia Minor. He is remembered mainly for the episode in which the Argonauts, his guests, killed him by mistake during a night battle.

The myth

According to tradition, Cyzicus was the son of Aeneus and Aenete, daughter of Eusorus. Sources differ on his parentage: in another version Eusorus is named as his father. The name Aeneus recalls the Thessalian town of Enez, and some accounts make him a son of Apollo and Stilbe, daughter of the river Peneus.

The Doliones shared their island with the Terrigenes, monsters who left them alone thanks to the protection of Poseidon. Cyzicus was married to Cleite, daughter of Merops of Percote, though other authors name his wife Larissa.

When the Argo reached the island, Cyzicus welcomed the Argonauts generously. The Terrigenes attacked and were defeated by Heracles and the heroes. After the Argonauts sailed away, a storm drove them back to the coast by night. Neither side recognised the other: the Doliones took them for Pelasgian enemies, and in the battle that followed Cyzicus was killed. At dawn the Argonauts understood their terrible mistake and mourned the king for three days. Cleite hanged herself on hearing of her husband's death.

Worship and religion

Some later accounts say the Argonauts raised a burial mound in his honour and held funeral games, and that the people of Cyzicus poured libations on the mound every year. The sources do not describe a formal cult beyond this custom.

Legacy

The city of Cyzicus, on the shore of the Propontis, took its name from the king. The night battle is best known from the account of Valerius Flaccus, who adds that Cybele and Pan brought about the clash in revenge for the king's killing of one of the goddess's lions.

Ancient sources: The Argonauts' killing of Cyzicus is part of the tradition of the voyage of the Argo, recorded by writers such as Valerius Flaccus.

Trivia

A city named after him. The city of Cyzicus, on the shore of the Propontis, was named after the king according to the tradition recorded in the texts.
A lion and a goddess. According to Valerius Flaccus, Cyzicus had killed a lion belonging to Cybele, and the goddess arranged the battle in revenge.
