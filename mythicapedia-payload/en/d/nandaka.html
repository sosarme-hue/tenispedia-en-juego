sword of Vishnu in Hindu mythology

Nandaka

Nandaka is the sword of the Hindu god Vishnu, the weapon that lets him carry out his task of defeating evil. Its symbolism, tied to knowledge that destroys ignorance, makes it especially meaningful within Vaishnavism.

The myth

According to the Agni Purana, the creator god Brahma was performing a sacrifice on Mount Meru when the hundred-armed asura Loha interfered. Vishnu then appeared from the sacrificial fire, seized the sword Nandaka from the asura, drew it, and used it to slay him. The blade is described as blue, with a gem-studded hilt.

Before dying, the asura wielded his mace and drove several heavenly beings from the mountain. When his severed body fell to earth, its parts turned to iron through contact with Nandaka, and Vishnu blessed them to be used for making weapons on earth.

Worship and religion

Within Sri Vaishnavism, the saints Annamacharya and Peyalvar are considered avatars of Nandaka.

Legacy

Nandaka appears in Vishnu's iconography, mainly in images with more than four arms, though it is rarely depicted. It appears in anthropomorphic form as an ayudhapurusha at the Gupta temple of Deogarh and at Mahabalipuram.

Ancient sources: The Agni Purana narrates its origin, the Vishnu Sahasranama mentions it twice, the Ramayana places it in Rama's hands, and the Vishnu Purana links it to knowledge.

Trivia

Celestial iron. According to the myth, iron weapons on earth come from the body of the asura Loha, which turned to metal on contact with Vishnu's sword.
Sword of knowledge. The Krishna Upanishad equates the sword with Shiva, who takes the form of a flaming sword of knowledge that destroys ignorance.
