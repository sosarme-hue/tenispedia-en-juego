Chione

Chione is a heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of Daedalion, loved on the same day by Apollo and Hermes, who fathered her sons Philammon and Autolycus. Her punishment for defying Artemis and her father's leap, turned into a bird, make her a classic story about hubris.

The myth

Chione, daughter of Daedalion, was a young woman of extraordinary beauty who attracted many suitors from the age of fourteen. Apollo, returning from Delphi, and Hermes, coming down from Mount Cyllene, saw her on the same day and fell in love. Hermes put her into a magical sleep and raped her; Apollo came at nightfall disguised as an old woman. Twins were born: Autolycus, a thief and trickster, son of Hermes, and Philammon, a singer and lyre player, son of Apollo.

Chione boasted that her beauty surpassed that of Artemis, and the goddess punished her with an arrow that pierced her tongue. Her father tried to throw himself onto the funeral pyre three times and, after a fourth failed attempt, ran to Mount Parnassus and jumped off. Apollo turned him into a hawk, or the gavilán called the dedalion, before he touched the ground.

The versions differ: according to Hesiod and Pherecydes her mother was called Philonis, and in Homer's Odyssey Autolycus is not the son of Hermes.

Worship and religion

At Thorikos, in Attica, Philonis received offerings during the month of Mounichion. Because the names of the girl and her father carry meteorological or astronomical meanings, it has been suggested that these stories were linked to seasonal weather cults at Thorikos. No living cult survives.

Legacy

The scholar Graham Anderson suggested that the German fairy tale of Snow White has ancient roots in the myth of Chione: the heroine's beauty, a sleep induced by supernatural means, and an approach disguised as an old woman.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Metamorphoses (Book XI) gives the fullest account, though Hesiod, Pherecydes, Homer in the Odyssey and Hyginus in his Fabulae also mention her.

Trivia

A name meaning snow. Chione comes from the Greek word for snow, chiōn, related to kheîma (snow) and kheimōn (winter).
A name for prostitutes. In classical antiquity, Chione was a generic name for prostitutes, which may reflect her role in the myth.
