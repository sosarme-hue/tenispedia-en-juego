son of Dian Cecht, hero of Irish mythology

Miach

Miach is a son of Dian Cecht, the god of healing, and one of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology. His story revolves around healing and the jealousy it provoked in his father.

The myth

According to the myth, Miach restored the use of the arm of Nuada, king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, by replacing the silver arm his father had made with one of flesh and blood. Dian Cecht, jealous that his son could do what he himself could not, attacked him with his sword and struck his head four times. Miach healed the first three wounds, but the fourth split his brain and killed him.

From his grave grew 365 healing herbs, which his sister Airmid arranged, though their father scattered them. Even so, in later tellings he is seen again healing the Tuatha Dé alongside his father and sister.

Ancient sources: His story appears in the medieval tale The Fate of the Children of Tuireann, where he is also described alongside his brother Oirmiach.

Trivia

A name with history. Miach is often translated as bushel, an agreed amount of grain. Irish law texts also use the word for a quantity of malted grain.
Possible divine twins. Some scholars consider Miach and Oirmiach remnants of the Celtic Divine Twins. Their alliterative names are a common trait of twin figures.
