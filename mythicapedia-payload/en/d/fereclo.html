Trojan carpenter of Greek mythology

Phereclus

Phereclus is a Trojan figure of Greek mythology, son of Tecton and a skilled shipbuilder. He matters because he built the ship on which Paris sailed to Troy with Helen, tying him to the origins of the war.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phereclus was the son of Tecton and was renowned for his carpentry. He built the boat that Paris used to carry Helen off to Troy after travelling to Sparta. His skill was not limited to ships: he was said to make other instruments of war, which made him a valuable target for the Greeks.

In Homer's Iliad, book five, Meriones pursues him and kills him with a spear that drives into his right buttock. His death is told in vivid detail, as one of the Trojan losses in the battle.

Legacy

His name lives on in the asteroid 2357 Phereclos, a Jovian asteroid named after him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, book five, recounts his death at the hands of Meriones.

Trivia

An asteroid with his name. The asteroid 2357 Phereclos, a Jovian asteroid, keeps his name in the sky.
An unusual wound. Meriones hits him with a spear that pierces his right buttock, a detail Homer describes with grim precision.
