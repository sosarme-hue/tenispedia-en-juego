legendary king of Sparta

Soos

Soos was, according to tradition, a legendary king of Sparta, son of Procles and father of Eurypon. He matters mainly because modern historians think he was invented in the 4th century BC to balance the king lists of the city's two royal houses.

The myth

According to tradition, Soos was the son of King Procles, who was himself a descendant of Heracles. He was the father of Eurypon, the eponymous ancestor of the Eurypontids, one of Sparta's two royal families. During his reign, the Spartans supposedly subjected the helots and advanced into Arcadia.

Plutarch tells an anecdote from that campaign. The people of Clitor surrounded the Spartans and cut off their access to water. Soos made a deal with them: he would return the conquered lands if he and all his men were allowed to drink. He then promised the kingdom to any soldier who refused to drink. Everyone drank except Soos himself, who only splashed water on his face, and so, according to the story, he broke his agreement with the Clitorians.

The ancient sources do not entirely agree about him. Plato mentions him in the Cratylus, but only as a nobleman, not a king. Ephorus already counts him as a monarch, placing Lycurgus as the sixth Eurypontid from Procles, which is only possible if Soos is included. Pausanias describes him as Procles' son and Eurypon's father. Herodotus, by contrast, does not include him in his lists of kings.

Ancient sources: Plato in the Cratylus, Ephorus, Pausanias and Plutarch, who record his figure and the Arcadian anecdote.

Trivia

An invented king. Modern historians believe Soos never existed as a historical king and was added to the list in the 4th century BC.
The king who did not drink. In the Clitor anecdote, Soos only splashed water on his face, so he did not keep his promise to return the lands.
