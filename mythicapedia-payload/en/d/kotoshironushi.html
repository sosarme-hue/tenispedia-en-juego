Shinto god of fishing, son of Ōkuninushi

Kotoshironushi

Kotoshironushi is a Shinto kami, the son of Ōkuninushi, the earthly deity of Izumo province. His story explains how control of Izumo passed to the heavenly gods, and he is linked with fishing and with the god Ebisu.

The myth

In the Kojiki, Kotoshironushi is the son of Ōkuninushi, the earthly ruler of Izumo. When the heavenly gods sent Takemikazuchi to conquer Izumo, Ōkuninushi left the choice of resisting to his two sons.

Kotoshironushi was out fishing when the envoy arrived. He accepted the rule of the heavenly gods, handed over his spear and withdrew from Izumo. His brother Takeminakata fought Takemikazuchi and was defeated.

Legend also casts him as an adviser to Empress Jingū during her invasion of Korea, and as one of the eight deities charged with guarding the imperial court. His daughter Himetataraisuzu-hime became a consort of Emperor Jimmu.

Worship and religion

Kotoshironushi is the principal deity of Asuka shrine. He is associated with Ebisu, the god of fishing and good fortune, who is still widely honored in many regions as a protector of fishermen and merchants.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki names him as Ōkuninushi's son and recounts his withdrawal before the heavenly gods.
