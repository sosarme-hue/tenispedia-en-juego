Mesopotamian god of agriculture and irrigation

Ennugi

Ennugi is a Mesopotamian god linked to agriculture, especially irrigation, and to the underworld. He belonged to the court of Enlil and was worshiped mainly in Nippur.

The myth

In Mesopotamian stories Ennugi appears only briefly. He is mentioned in the Atra-Hasis and, in the standard edition of the Epic of Gilgamesh, he is among the gods who swear an oath to bring about the great flood. In the Šurpu incantation series he is called upon to bind the demon Asakku, a role that, according to Wilfred G. Lambert, may reflect a tradition in which Ennugi, not Ninurta, defeated it.

Tradition places him as the guzalû of Enlil, a title rendered as "throne-bearer," "chamberlain" or "herald." An incantation says he created grubs, and a hymn to Nuska states that Nuska gave him orders.

Worship and religion

He was worshiped in Nippur, where Shulgi built a temple in his honor, probably the Erabriri, and another, the Erabšaša, whose location is unknown. Epithets such as "lord of dike and ditch" and "lord of the field and ploughmen" stress his agricultural character. The sources do not say whether his cult survived.

Ancient sources: Atra-Hasis, the Epic of Gilgamesh, the Šurpu incantation series, the Lipit-Ishtar and the Plow text, and god lists such as An = Anum.

Trivia

A name meaning no return. A late explanatory list interprets his name as "lord of the netherworld, lord of no return." Some scholars link him with Ennugigi, although they probably were not the same deity.
A temple named after a shackle. His temple Erabriri bore a ceremonial name that can be translated as "house of the shackle which holds in check." A temple of Mandanu in Babylon had the same name.
