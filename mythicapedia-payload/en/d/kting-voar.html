reputed bovid of Cambodia and Vietnam, creature of doubtful existence

Kting voar

The kting voar, also called the snake-eating cow, is a reputed bovid said to live in Cambodia and Vietnam, described as a cow-like animal with twisted horns and a mottled coat. Its existence as a real species is highly doubtful, and sceptics regard it as a mythical animal.

The myth

In Cambodian folklore, the kting voar is an animal with a particular connection to snakes, although accounts vary from one telling to another. Its local name means "gaur with liana horns", referring to a wild Asian cattle species.

In the West it was mistranslated as "jungle sheep", which led people to assume it was related to sheep and goats. Adding to the confusion, the Vietnamese name linh dương was also used for it, though that is in fact a local name for the Chinese serau.

Trivia

Cow horns sold as fakes. In Cambodian markets, cow horns are often sold as imitations of kting voar horns.
DNA analysis. All supposed kting voar specimens tested for DNA turned out to be artificially shaped cattle horns.
