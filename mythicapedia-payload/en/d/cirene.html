Thessalian nymph of Greek myth

Cyrene

Cyrene is a Thessalian nymph of Greek myth, a huntress who tamed a lion and was carried off by Apollo to North Africa. She matters chiefly because she gave her name to the Greek city of Cyrene in Libya and because she was the mother of Aristaeus.

The myth

According to the stories, Cyrene was a daughter of Hypseus, king of the Lapiths, and of the naiad Chlidanope, although other authors made her the daughter of the river Peneus. From a young age she preferred hunting and the open country to household work. She tended her father's flocks in the forests of Pindus and, according to Callimachus, Artemis chose her as a companion and gave her two hunting dogs, with which she won a prize at the funeral games of Pelias.

One day she wrestled with a lion that was attacking the flocks and killed it with her own hands. Apollo, who witnessed the feat, fell in love with her and carried her off in his golden car to Libya, where he made her queen of a fertile land and founded the city that bears her name. Their children included Aristaeus, god of beekeeping and herding, and Idmon, the Argonaut seer, while some accounts also name Autuchus.

After the births, Apollo turned her into a nymph so that she could live a long life and keep hunting alongside Artemis. Later, when Aristaeus lost his bees after the death of Eurydice, his mother advised him to seek the sea prophet Proteus, who told him how to appease the dead woman's spirit.

Legacy

The city of Cyrene, on the coast of modern Libya, and the region of Cyrenaica keep her name. She also appears in Aristophanes' Thesmophoriazusae, where a character, seeing the poet Agathon dressed as a woman, says he sees no man there at all, only Cyrene.

Ancient sources: Pindar in his Pythian Ode nine, Callimachus, Ovid, Hyginus and the scholia on Apollonius of Rhodes.

Trivia

Artemis's two hounds. According to Callimachus, the goddess gave her two hunting dogs that helped her win a prize at the funeral games of Pelias.
A nymph by Apollo's decision. After she gave birth, Apollo transformed her into a nymph so she could live long and keep hunting with Artemis.
