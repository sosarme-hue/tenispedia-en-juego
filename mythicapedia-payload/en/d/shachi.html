queen of the devas in Hinduism, consort of Indra

Shachi

Shachi, also known as Indrani, Paulomi or Aindri, is the queen of the devas in Hinduism and the wife of Indra, king of the gods. Daughter of the asura Puloman, she is an important goddess in Shaktism, and her name is linked to the idea of divine power.

The myth

According to the epic and Puranic accounts, Shachi was so beautiful, proud and alluring that many suitors sought to marry her. The best-known episode is that of Nahusha, a mortal king of the Lunar dynasty whom the devas made ruler of heaven while Indra was performing penance for slaying Vritra. Nahusha tried to seduce her, but Shachi, sheltered by Indra's teacher Brihaspati, devised a scheme with the help of the goddess Upashruti: she told him he must come to her in a palanquin carried by sages. When the impatient and arrogant Nahusha kicked the sage Agastya, the sage cursed him to fall from heaven and become a snake. Indra regained his throne and his wife.

The Vedas give her a smaller role. The Rigveda invokes her several times, describing her as beautiful and jealous of her rivals, and in one hymn she boasts of having subdued her husband. The Brahmanas present her as Indra's beloved. The Puranas credit her with three sons, Jayanta, Rishabha and Midhusha, and a daughter, Jayanti, who married Shukra.

Worship and religion

She is rarely worshipped as an independent deity. Most often she is honoured alongside Indra throughout India. She is one of the Sapta Matrikas, the seven divine mothers, and holds an important place in Shaktism. She is also mentioned in Jain and Buddhist texts.

Legacy

Her name, shachi, has been linked to speech and the power of eloquence, according to Monier-Williams, and to the root shak, meaning power or strength. The scholar David Kinsley saw in her a foreshadowing of the concept of Shakti, the personification of divine power. Other scholars translate shachi as divine grace.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Rigveda and the Brahmanas (Shatapatha and Taittiriya), and later in epics and Puranas such as the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Kanda Purana.

Trivia

The parijata tree. The Puranas state that Shachi owned the parijata tree, one of the jewels that emerged from the churning of the ocean.
Quarrel over Indra's ape. A Rigvedic hymn recounts a quarrel between Indrani and Indra over the pranks of Vrishakapi, Indra's pet ape.
