Egyptian prince in Greek mythology, son of King Aegyptus

Aegius

Aegius is a prince in Greek mythology, a son of King Aegyptus, whose story is closely tied to that of his brother Lynceus and to the Danaids, the daughters of Danaus. His tale belongs to the great family rivalry between the descendants of Aegyptus and those of Danaus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Aegius was one of the many sons of King Aegyptus. The sources do not agree on his mother: in one account she was a Phoenician woman, making him full brother to Agaptolemus, Cercetes, Eurydamas, Argius, Archelaus and Menemachus; in others his mother was Eurryroe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre. His fate matched that of his brothers: they were slain on their wedding night by their wives, who obeyed the command of their father, King Danaus of Libya. Only Lynceus was spared. Aegius later married Mnestra, daughter of Danaus and an Ethiopian woman.
