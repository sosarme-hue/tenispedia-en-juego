gigantic yōkai of Japanese folklore

Daidarabotchi

Daidarabotchi is a gigantic yōkai of Japanese folklore, said to pose as a mountain range while asleep. Its colossal size makes it one of the most striking giants in Japan's supernatural imagination.

The myth

Tradition holds that its footprints created countless lakes and ponds. In one legend, it weighed Mount Fuji against Mount Tsukuba to see which was heavier, and accidentally split Tsukuba's peak when it had finished.

The 8th-century Hitachi no Kuni Fudoki, a record of imperial customs in Hitachi Province, places it on a hill west of a post office in Hiratsu Ogushi, where it fed on giant beach clams and piled their shells atop a hill. In Ibaraki Prefecture it was remembered as a benevolent giant who moved mountains to help people. The Izumo no Kuni Fudoki also mentions Ōmitsunu, grandson of Susanoo, who pulled land from Silla with ropes through Kuni-biki to enlarge his realm. It was further said to dwell on Mount Togakushi, where a pond on its south side bears its name.

Legacy

In Mito, a 15-metre statue stands in Ogushi Kaizuka Park to commemorate the yōkai and its myths.

Ancient sources: The 8th-century Hitachi no Kuni Fudoki and the Izumo no Kuni Fudoki, records of Japanese provinces.

Trivia

Lakes made by footprints. His enormous size led to the belief that countless lakes and ponds were formed by his footprints.
Statue in Mito. The city of Mito erected a 15-metre statue of him in Ogushi Kaizuka Park.
