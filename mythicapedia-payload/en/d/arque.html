Greek muse of origins and beginnings

Arche

Arche is, in Greek religion, one of the muses linked to origins and beginnings; her name means simply "beginning" in Greek. She belongs to the earlier group of four muses, which came before the better-known set of nine.

The myth

According to a late tradition, the first muses were four in number, Arche among them, alongside Thelxiope, Aede and Melete. Cicero places them as daughters of the second Jupiter, before the nine muses who were later established as daughters of Mnemosyne.

The ancient texts give her no deeds of her own: her role is simply her place in that first generation of muses. Over time, the group of four slipped into the background behind the nine muses of the arts, sciences and literature.

Legacy

One of Jupiter's moons bears her name, Arce, in her honour. It is one of the few traces of this muse in later culture.

Ancient sources: Cicero, in De natura deorum (III, 21), lists the first muses, Arche among them.

Trivia

A moon named after her. One of Jupiter's moons is called Arce, taken from this muse.
A name meaning beginning. Her Greek name, Arkhê, means "beginning", which explains her link to origins.
