Deiopea

Deiopea is the name of two female figures in Greek and Roman mythology: a Nereid, daughter of the Old Man of the Sea, and one of Juno's nymphs. Her best-known appearance comes in Virgil's Aeneid, where she serves Juno's plan against the Trojans.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Deiopea is a Nereid, daughter of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and of the Oceanid Doris. She was part of the train of Cyrene alongside her sisters Ephyre, Opis and Arethusa.

In the Roman world another Deiopea appears, one of the fourteen nymphs who attend Juno. Virgil, in the Aeneid, describes her as having an excellent body (praestanti corpore). To take revenge on the Trojan refugees, Juno promised her in marriage to Aeolus, king of the winds, in exchange for his help in wrecking their ships with storms.

Legacy

Her name lives on in astronomy: the asteroid 184 Dejopeja was named after her, keeping her memory alive in modern science.

Ancient sources: Virgil mentions her in the Aeneid, where she appears as one of Juno's nymphs.

Trivia

An asteroid named after her. The asteroid 184 Dejopeja was named in her honour, so a mythological nymph now has a place in the astronomical sky.
An admired body. Virgil describes her with the Latin phrase praestanti corpore, meaning of excellent body or outstanding figure.
