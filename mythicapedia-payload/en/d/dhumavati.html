Hindu goddess, the fifth Mahavidya

Dhumavati

Dhumavati, whose name means "the smoky one", is one of the ten Mahavidyas, a group of Hindu Tantric goddesses. She embodies the fearsome side of Mahadevi, the supreme goddess, and is linked to the inauspicious, though she is also credited with boons and wisdom.

The myth

According to tradition, Dhumavati appears at the cosmic dissolution (pralaya) and is "the Void" that exists before creation and after dissolution. She is usually portrayed as an old, dishevelled widow who rides a horseless chariot bearing a crow, often in cremation grounds.

In the Shaktisamgama-Tantra, she rises from the sad smoke of Sati's burning body after Sati dies at Daksha's yagna. Another account in the Pranatosini-Tantra tells how the hungry Sati devoured Shiva, and when he was released she cursed her to become a widow. An oral tale says the warrior goddess Durga created her to fight the demons Shumbha and Nishumbha, and her name comes from the stinging smoke she uses against her foes.

Worship and religion

She has very few dedicated temples, and her Tantric worship is practised mostly in private, in secluded places such as cremation grounds and forests. In her Varanasi temple she is revered as a local protective deity, including by married couples. Traditionally her devotees are unpaired people such as bachelors, widows and renouncers, as well as Tantrikas seeking power or victory over enemies.
