Mesopotamian goddess, wife of Enki and mother of Marduk

Damgalnuna (Damkina)

Damgalnuna, also known as Damkina, was a Mesopotamian goddess regarded as the wife of Enki (Ea) and, in later tradition, the mother of Marduk. Her character is poorly defined in the sources, though she was linked to ritual purification and to intercession with her husband on behalf of worshippers.

The myth

The sources present her as the consort of Enki and mother of several deities, including Nanshe, Asalluhi, Marduk and Enbilulu. In the myth Enki and Ninhursag she is equated with the goddess of the title, though the two were usually regarded as separate.

Her most notable story is Damkina's Bond, which tells of a conflict between the pantheons of Babylon, led by Marduk, and Nippur, led by Enlil. Nippur's forces lose, and the dispute is settled when Damgalnuna, alerted by a messenger, releases an object called a "bond", whose exact nature remains uncertain.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Eridu, but she was also worshipped at Malgium, where she shared a temple with Enki, and at Nippur, where Shulgi built a sanctuary in her honour. She was venerated at Sippar and Kalhu too, and in Hurrian religion she was adopted as Ea's wife, appearing in the treaty between Šattiwaza and Šuppiluliuma I and in the Yazılıkaya sanctuary. Her cult is documented in every period of Mesopotamian history, though its reach gradually declined.

Legacy

In Mesopotamian astronomy she was identified with the Wagon of Heaven, which corresponds to Ursa Minor.

Ancient sources: The Enūma Eliš and the composition Damkina's Bond, known from two copies, one Neo-Assyrian and one Neo-Babylonian or later.

Trivia

Devotional seals. On Old Babylonian cylinder seals from Sippar appears the formula "servant of Enki and Damgalnuna", showing the joint devotion to the divine couple.
Variant names. Her name appears in many spellings, such as Damkianna, Damnuna or Damgalana, and her Hurrian name was Tapkina.
