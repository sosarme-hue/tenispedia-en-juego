princess of the Bebryces in Greek and Roman myth

Pyrene

Pyrene is a princess of the Bebryces, a people who lived on both sides of the Pyrenees. Her tragic fate gave the mountain range its name, and her story is one of the few legends that place Heracles on the Iberian Peninsula.

The myth

According to the myth, the hero Heracles arrived at the court of King Bebryx during one of his Twelve Labours, on his way to seize the cattle of Geryon. After drinking heavily, he forced himself on the princess and continued his journey. Months later, Pyrene gave birth to a serpent. Terrified of her father's anger, she fled into the forests of the mountains, where wild beasts tore her apart.

When Heracles returned and found her remains, his grief was so great that the mountain shook, and it took the name of the unfortunate maiden. He then buried her remains deep in the earth.

Legacy

The name of the Pyrenees is her most lasting mark. In antiquity it was linked to the Greek word for fire, although this is now seen as folk etymology. The name is probably Celtic and means mountains.

Ancient sources: The Roman poet Silius Italicus, writing in the first century AD, preserves her myth. Diodorus Siculus, Parthenius and Pliny the Elder also recount variants, and Pliny considered such stories the product of imagination.

Trivia

Two names for her father. The sources call her father Bebryx or Bebrycius, depending on the version. Both forms appear in ancient texts.
Another Celtic princess. Diodorus Siculus tells of a princess of Celtice who rejected all suitors but bore a son to Heracles, Galates, who later became king.
