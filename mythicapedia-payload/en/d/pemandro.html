hero of Greek mythology, founder of Tanagra

Poemander

Poemander is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Chaeresilaus and Stratonice, credited with founding Tanagra in Boeotia. His story, tied to the Trojan War, shows how an accidental killing could demand a ritual purification.

The myth

According to the tradition, Poemander refused to support the Achaeans in the Trojan War, and they besieged him at Stephon. He escaped at night and began fortifying Poemandria, his town. An architect named Polycritus mocked the fortifications, and Poemander, enraged, threw a stone at him. The stone missed and killed his own son Leucippus.

According to the law, the killing forced him to leave Boeotia, already occupied by the Achaeans. His other son, Ephippus, persuaded Achilles, Tlepolemus and Peneleos to escort him to Chalcis, where Elephenor purified him. Grateful, Poemander ordered sacred precincts built in honour of his companions. Some accounts say Achilles had earlier carried off Stratonice, Poemander's mother.

Ancient sources: Rhianus, a Hellenistic poet, gives a different version, with five children and Stratonice as Poemander's wife. Ancient accounts also differ on the identity of the naiad wife and on who died in the accident.

Trivia

Poemandria. The city founded by Poemander was called Poemandria, while Tanagra took its name from his wife, a naiad.
A different death. In one variant of the story, the son killed by the stone is Ephippus rather than Leucippus, and he dies in a way similar to Toxeus.
