Japanese kami of wisdom

Omoikane

Omoikane is a kami of wisdom and intelligence in Japan's Shinto tradition. His name means "having the wisdom and thoughtfulness of many people", and he is called upon to ponder and offer good counsel among the heavenly deities.

The myth

According to tradition, he is the son of the creator deity Takamimusubi and the older brother of Takuhatachijihime, who married Ame-no-Oshihomimi. In the Kojiki he appears among the kami who descend to earth in the Tenson kōrin myth. His best-known episode comes when Amaterasu hides in a cave: he is entrusted with finding a way to bring her out.

The Kujiki tells a different story: Omoikane descends to Shinano Province, present-day Nagano, and becomes the ancestor of local lineages. According to this text, he is the father of Ame-no-Uwaharu and Ame-no-Shitaharu. His name varies across sources, appearing as Tokoyo-no-Omoikane in the Kojiki and as Omoikane in the Nihon Shoki.

Worship and religion

Carpenters pray to him while raising the pillars of a building, and many people in Japan ask him for success in school and exams, a custom that continues today. He is venerated at shrines such as Achi in Kurashiki, Chichibu in Saitama, and Shizu in Naka, Ibaraki.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, Nihon Shoki and Kujiki (Sendai Kuji Hongi), Japanese chronicles that mention him under different names and genealogies.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His name translates as "the wisdom and thoughtfulness of many people", an idea that sums up his role as a collective counselor.
A lineage in Shinano. The Kujiki places him in Shinano Province, today Nagano, as the ancestor of local families, a detail not found in the Kojiki.
