legendary Danish king of Norse and Anglo-Saxon tradition

Hrólfr Kraki

Hrólfr Kraki is a semi-legendary king of Denmark who appears in both Scandinavian and Anglo-Saxon literature. He belongs to the Vendel period, and he is the nephew of Hroðgar and the protagonist of some of the most famous stories about the Scyldings.

The myth

In Scandinavian tradition, Hrólfr was the son of Halga, one of Hroðgar's brothers, and Yrsa, born from Halga's encounter with a young woman in Halland or Lolland. When Halga learned that Yrsa was his daughter, he was overcome with shame and took his own life. Hrólfr later succeeded to the throne after a brief reign by a king the Danes had been forced to accept as a dog, and he became famous for his generosity and strength.

According to the stories, his sister Skulda married Hartwar, a German earl, who took advantage of Hrólfr's trust and killed him with his retinue in Zealand. Only Wigg survived, pretending loyalty until he ran the German through with a sword. In Saxo Grammaticus's version, Hrólfr escapes from Uppsala with his mother after outwitting the Swedish king Adils, who stoops to pick up gold scattered along the road.

In the poem Beowulf he appears as Hroðulf, Hroðgar's nephew, and there are hints that he would seize the throne from the sons of queen Wealhþeow. Later Scandinavian sources also tell of a contest with Bödvar Bjarki and of his death in the burning of his hall, set by his brother-in-law Hjörvard.

Legacy

His figure inspired the Hrólf Kraki's saga and the Skjöldunga saga, and he has been studied extensively in medieval literature. In scholarship, his name is linked to the relationship between the Beowulf legend and the Norse sagas.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Anglo-Saxon poems Beowulf and Widsith, in the Chronicon Lethrense, the Annales Lundenses, Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum and the Hrólfs saga kraka.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His name in Proto-Norse, Hrōþiwulfaz, would mean "famous wolf".
A curious nickname. According to Saxo, the nickname Krage referred to a tall carved trunk used as a ladder.
