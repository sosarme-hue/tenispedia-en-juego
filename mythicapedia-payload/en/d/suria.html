Hindu god of the Sun

Surya

Surya is the Sun and the solar deity of Hinduism, one of the oldest gods of the Vedic pantheon. Although other deities now hold more prominence, his figure remains present in temples, festivals and healing practices.

The myth

In the Vedas, Surya appears as the source of light that dispels darkness and gives life to everything. The Rig Veda describes him with golden arms, hands and hair, and links him to the dawn goddess Ushas, sometimes as her son and sometimes as her husband. His origin changes from hymn to hymn: he is born of the Adityas, of Aditi, of Indra, of Soma or of other gods. Yaska places him among the Adityas, the sons of Aditi, and later texts name Kashyapa as his father.

His family is large. With Saranyu he had the Ashvins, Yama and Manu, who in tradition is the ancestor of humanity. With Chhaya he fathered Shani. Other accounts make him the father of Sugriva, king of the monkeys in the Ramayana, and of Karna, the hero of the Mahabharata, born of his union with Kunti before her marriage. He is typically shown in a chariot pulled by seven horses, which stand for the seven colours of visible light and the seven days of the week.

In the Ramayana, Rama receives the Adityahridayam hymn before facing Ravana. In the Shatapatha Brahmana, Surya forms a triad with Agni and Indra after sacrifices that made them more powerful than other deities.

Worship and religion

Surya was widely venerated in the Vedic age and had important temples, such as those at Multan (now in Pakistan), Martanda in Kashmir, Modhera in Gujarat and Konark in Odisha. From around the 13th century his worship declined, and many temples fell into ruin or were reused for other deities. Today his cult survives mainly in popular religious life, where devotees ask for healing from skin diseases, eye ailments and infertility. It remains alive in festivals such as Makar Sankranti, Chhath Puja and Ratha Saptami, and some Shiva temples in southern India still keep a small altar to him.

Legacy

The name Ravi, also used for the Sun, designates the planet in Indian astrology, and Surya is the lord of Simha (Leo) among the twelve signs of the Hindu zodiac. Sunday, Ravivara, takes its name from him in the Hindu calendar. His wheel, the chakra, is sometimes interpreted as the Dharmachakra. The 108 names of Surya, recorded in the Brahma Purana, are still recited as devotional practice.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in the Rig Veda, the Atharva Veda, the Brahmanas, the Shatapatha Brahmana, the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Brahma Purana.

Trivia

Seven horses. The seven horses of Surya's chariot are linked to the seven colours of the rainbow and the seven days of the week.
A compound name. The name of Konark Temple is explained as a compound of kona and arka, meaning roughly Arka in the corner.
