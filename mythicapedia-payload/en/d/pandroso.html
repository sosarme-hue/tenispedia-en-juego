Pandrosus

Pandrosus is one of the three daughters of Cecrops, the first king of Athens, and the only one of the Kekropidai who obeyed Athena. Her story belongs to Greek myth and is tied to the birth of Erichthonius and to Athenian worship on the Acropolis.

The myth

According to tradition, Pandrosus was a daughter of Cecrops and Agraulos, and sister to Aglauros and Herse. Some accounts say that, by Hermes, she was the mother of Ceryx, who gave his name to the Ceryces of Eleusis. Another version, found among the Atthidographers, makes her a daughter of Actaeus rather than Cecrops, since these writers often blended the legendary figures of Attica.

The central episode begins when Athena hides the infant Erichthonius in a basket and entrusts it to the sisters with a strict warning never to open it. Pandrosus kept the prohibition, while Aglauros and Herse, driven by curiosity, opened it and saw the child with a serpent's lower body. Driven mad by Athena, they threw themselves off the Acropolis. In other versions all three sisters are blamed, or only Aglauros.

Worship and religion

Pandrosus was honoured in Athens alongside her sisters during the Arrephoria, as goddesses who favoured agriculture, and she was linked to abundance of water. Under an old Attic law, whenever a cow was sacrificed to Athena, a ewe had to be sacrificed to Pandrosus as well. Her sanctuary, the Pandroseion, stood beside the Erechtheion, with Athena's sacred olive tree and an altar to Zeus Herkeios.
