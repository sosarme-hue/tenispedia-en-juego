Greek hero, son of Theseus

Acamas

Acamas is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Theseus and Phaedra and brother of Demophon. He is remembered for his role in the Trojan War and for his restless love of distant places, a trait that shapes many of his stories.

The myth

According to tradition, Acamas and his brother grew up in exile on Euboea, under the care of Elephenor, a relative by marriage, after Theseus lost the throne of Athens. Before the Trojan War, he and Diomedes were sent to demand the return of Helen, though Homer gives that embassy to Menelaus and Odysseus.

At Troy, Acamas caught the eye of Laodice, daughter of Priam, and fathered her son Munitus. The boy was raised by Aethra, Acamas' grandmother, who was living there as one of Helen's slaves. Munitus later died from a snakebite while hunting in Thrace.

In the war he fought for the Greeks and, according to Virgil, hid inside the Trojan Horse. Afterwards he rescued Aethra from her long captivity. His best-known death is set on Cyprus, where he fell from his horse onto his own sword, though other versions give that death to Demophon.

Legacy

Polygnotus painted him in the Lesche at Delphi, and a statue of him stood in the city. His name lives on in several places: the promontory of Acamas in Cyprus, the town of Acamantion in Phrygia, and the Attic tribe of the Acamantis.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid names him among the warriors inside the Trojan Horse, and the lost Iliou persis probably did too; the Iliad does not mention him.

Trivia

A grandson of Priam. Munitus, son of Acamas and Laodice, was a grandson of King Priam of Troy, making him a member of the Trojan royal family.
Names on the map. A promontory in Cyprus, a town in Phrygia and an Athenian tribe all bear his name, a sign of how widely his figure was remembered in antiquity.
