Cretan girl raised as a boy, according to Ovid

Iphis

Iphis is a girl from Crete, raised as a boy and transformed into a man by the goddess Isis, according to Ovid. Her story is the only Greco-Roman myth of desire between women.

The myth

According to Ovid, in Phaistos lived Ligdus and his pregnant wife Telethusa. Unable to afford a dowry for a daughter, Ligdus declared that a girl must be put to death. Isis appeared to Telethusa at night and urged her to keep the child, whatever its sex. A daughter was born and raised as a boy, under the gender-neutral name Iphis.

At thirteen, Iphis was due to marry Ianthe, whom she had grown up with and loved without hope. The day before the wedding, mother and daughter prayed at Isis's temple, and Iphis emerged transformed into a man. The next day, Iphis married Ianthe.

Legacy

In the seventeenth century, publisher Humphrey Moseley claimed to own a manuscript of a Shakespeare play based on this story, but scholars doubt the claim and the play is lost. Scholars still debate whether Ovid disapproves of or sympathizes with female same-sex desire.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Metamorphoses (Book IX), where her story is told.

Trivia

A unique case. Scholars regard the story of Iphis and Ianthe as the only Greco-Roman myth that deals with female same-sex desire.
A possible variant. Her story resembles that of Leucippus, also from Phaistos, and may be a variant of it.
