son of Priam in Greek mythology

Isus

Isus is one of the sons of King Priam of Troy in Greek mythology. He appears briefly in Homer's Iliad, within the long catalogue of the king's children, and he matters mainly as an example of the large royal family of Troy.

The myth

According to Homer's Iliad, Isus was one of the fifty sons King Priam had with an unidentified woman. Little else is recorded about him, apart from the fact that he was a half-brother of Antiphus, son of Priam and Hecuba.

In the Homeric account, Agamemnon kills him during the Trojan War. His death is one of the many Trojan losses listed as the poem moves through the battlefield. Isus has no deeds of his own and no episodes built around him, which makes him one of the most understated figures of the Trojan cycle.

Ancient sources: Homer, in the Iliad, mentions him as one of Priam's sons killed by Agamemnon.
