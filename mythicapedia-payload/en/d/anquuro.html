Phrygian prince, son of Midas

Anchurus

Anchurus, also called Aegisteus, is a Phrygian prince and the son of King Midas. His sacrifice in closing a chasm that opened near the town of Celaenae makes him a heroic example of self-giving in Greek tradition about Phrygia.

The myth

According to the story, during Midas's reign a huge chasm opened in the earth near the town of Celaenae in Phrygia, sent by the wrath of Idaean Zeus. Water surged up and dragged houses and their inhabitants into the depths.

The king consulted an oracle, which told him to throw into the abyss the most precious thing he owned. Midas threw in great quantities of gold, silver and women's jewellery, but the earth did not close.

Anchurus realised that human life was the most precious thing of all. He embraced his father, said goodbye to his wife Timothea, and spurred his horse at full speed into the split ground. As he plunged down while still seated on the animal, the earth closed at once.
