Egyptian lioness goddess of war

Pakhet

Pakhet is an Egyptian lioness goddess of war who appeared in the pantheon during the Middle Kingdom. Her name means 'she who scratches', and she was linked to the sun, the desert and the flooding of the Nile.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Pakhet could master fire and take other animal forms, such as a serpent. She was seen as a protector of the sun and an ally of Osiris, and people believed she could help bring on the Nile's flood.

As a huntress, she was imagined wandering the desert alone at night in search of prey, which is why texts call her the Night Huntress with Sharp Claws. She was associated with desert storms and wadis, and one of her titles, She Who Opens the Ways of the Stormy Rains, probably refers to flash floods in narrow valleys. She was shown with a lioness head, wearing the solar disk between two lyre-shaped horns and two feathers, often killing snakes with her claws.

Worship and religion

In the Middle Kingdom she had a sacred site near Beni Hassan and was an important local goddess. Her cult gained special importance under the 18th and 19th dynasties, when she was associated with the royal court. Her most famous shrine was an underground cavern near al-Minya, built by Hatshepsut, where great numbers of mummified cats have been found, probably brought from far away to be buried during rituals.

Legacy

The Greeks, who ruled Egypt for about three hundred years, identified her with Artemis, and the cave became known as Speos Artemidos, the Cave of Artemis. That name is still used today, even though Artemis was never an Egyptian goddess.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts give her the title 'Night Huntress with Sharp Claws', and Spell 470 of the Coffin Texts presents her as the lioness who sees and catches by night.

Trivia

Speos Artemidos. The Greek name of the cave, 'Cave of Artemis', is still used even though Artemis was not an Egyptian goddess.
Mummified cats. The catacombs have yielded great numbers of mummified cats, many brought from far away to be buried in rituals.
