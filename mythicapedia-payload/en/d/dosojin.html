guardian kami of roads and village boundaries, Japan

Dōsojin

Dōsojin are a class of protective Shinto kami widely venerated in eastern Japan, especially in the Kantō region. They guard village boundaries, crossroads and travellers, and are often represented as a married couple.

The myth

Tradition links the Dōsojin with kami that appear in the Kojiki. According to the story, Izanagi, returning from Yomi, the land of the dead, washed himself and threw away his trousers, and from them was born Chimata no Kami.

In another episode, Izanagi, pursued by a demoness of Yomi, threw her a stick, and from it arose Sae no Kami, the deity who keeps the spirits of the dead from entering the world of the living. Over time he merged with the Dōsojin, and because the elongated rocks that represent him, he also came to be linked with fertility, children and marital happiness.

Worship and religion

Every January 15 the Dosojin Matsuri is held in Nozawaonsen, Nagano. This fire festival asks for good fortune and happy marriages. Men aged 25 and 42, considered unlucky ages, take part in burning a large straw structure. Some villages still offer flowers, sake and coins to these figures, and the festival, more than 300 years old, is an intangible cultural property of Japan.

Legacy

Dōsojin appear in Ichiko Ima's supernatural manga Hyakki Yakoushou, where the protagonist and his cousin are compared to one of them.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki mentions Chimata no Kami and Sae no Kami, deities that tradition later associated with the Dōsojin, though it does not use that name.

Trivia

Batō Kannon with a horse's head. One Dōsojin form, Batō Kannon, wears a horse's head atop its own. It also protects horses, which were vital for transport, farming and war.
The six Jizō. Jizō statues are often grouped in sixes, called Roku Jizō, reflecting his vow to exist in all six states of karmic rebirth. They appear in the folktale Kasa Jizō.
