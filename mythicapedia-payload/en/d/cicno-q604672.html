son of Apollo, turned into a swan

Cycnus

Cycnus is a semidivine figure in Greek mythology, a son of Apollo and Hyrie whose story explains the origin of a swan lake in Aetolia. His story is known mainly through Antoninus Liberalis and Ovid, who tell it with differences.

The myth

According to tradition, Cycnus lived between Pleuron and Calydon and spent most of his time hunting. He was handsome but arrogant, and he scorned the young men who fell in love with him. All of them drifted away except Phylius, who stayed at his side.

To be rid of him, Cycnus set three impossible tasks. Phylius overcame a lion by getting it drunk on wine, caught two vultures by feigning death, and, with Heracles' help, brought a bull to Zeus's altar. When Cycnus learned that Phylius no longer obeyed him, he felt disgraced and threw himself into a lake called Conope. His mother did the same, and Apollo turned them both into swans. In Ovid's version the ending differs: Phylius refuses to hand over the bull, Cycnus leaps from a cliff and turns into a swan as he falls, and his mother, believing him dead, dissolves in tears and becomes a lake.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis records his story in detail, and Ovid includes it in the Metamorphoses.
