middle world of Japanese mythology

Ashihara no Nakatsukuni

Ashihara no Nakatsukuni is, in Japanese mythology, the middle world between Takamagahara, the heavens, and Yomi, the netherworld. Over time the name also came to mean the country of Japan itself, which makes it a key term for understanding how the Japanese land was conceived.

The myth

According to tradition, this middle world was created by the god Susanoo-no-Mikoto after the gods drove him out of Takamagahara for his violent deeds. The territory thus appears as the realm where human beings live, lying between the celestial home of the gods and the land of the dead.

The name appears in both the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, within the land-ceding myth involving Ōnamuchi. The texts use the longer form Toyoashihara no Nakatsukuni interchangeably with it.

The meaning of the name rests on the worldview of the ancient peoples: naka means 'middle', and the 'middle land' is the real world lying between heaven and the netherworld. Some scholars suggest the name suited a damp land covered in reeds, although historians still disagree about the exact part of Japan it originally referred to.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, within the land-ceding myth involving Ōnamuchi.

Trivia

A name with two forms. The term can be used interchangeably with the longer form Toyoashihara no Nakatsukuni, built on the same elements of the middle land.
A land of reeds. One hypothesis holds that the name referred to a damp land covered with reeds (葦), although historians disagree about its location.
