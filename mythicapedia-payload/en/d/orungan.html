Yoruba god of the winds and father of the Ifá priesthood

Orungan

Orungan is the Yoruba deity of the winds, son of Aganju and Yemaja. He is regarded as the father of the Ifá priesthood and the orisha of the babalawos.

The myth

According to some stories, Orungan violated his mother, Yemaja. When he tried again, she fell and burst open, and from her body came forth the fifteen orishas.

Tradition also holds that he was the first man dedicated to the worship of Ifá. He received the divination tablet and its tools from Elegguá. He is the inseparable companion of Iwi, the ethereal double, and is assisted by the orisha of the staves, Amoman.

Worship and religion

Orungan is the orisha of the babalawos, the priests of Ifá. He is believed to dwell in people's skin and to own their youth, and he is represented adorned with a golden heart, a sun and two volcanic stones. His ekeles are made of seven red beads, seven dark transparent blue beads, and a golden bead between every seventh bead. His worship remains alive in the Yoruba religion and its Afro-American traditions.

Trivia

Fifteen orishas. According to the story, fifteen orishas emerged from Yemaja after she fell, an episode that explains their origin within the pantheon.
Beads of his necklace. His ekeles combine red, blue and golden beads in a fixed sequence that serves as a mark of the orisha's identity.
