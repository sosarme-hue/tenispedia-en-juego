Egyptian god of the virility of the dead

Babi

Babi is an ancient Egyptian deity, the deification of the hamadryas baboon. Regarded as the firstborn son of Osiris and lord of the dead's virility, he is linked to the judgement of souls in the Duat.

The myth

According to tradition, Babi was an aggressive, bloodthirsty being who fed on entrails and devoured the souls of the sinful after they were weighed against Maat, beside a lake of fire. His enormous sexual appetite made him a guardian of the dead's virility: in some depictions his phallus served as the mast of the ferry that carried the righteous to Aaru.

The Papyrus Jumilhac tells of his clash with Thoth. Babi, shown as a red dog with yellow eyes, accused Thoth of theft before the Ennead and the sun god Ra, but the tribunal rejected the charge for lack of witnesses. When he kept spreading rumours, Thoth anointed his sleeping phallus with his writing reed and cast a spell on him; during intercourse the organ swelled and trapped him. Ra declared him guilty and Thoth executed him on a sacrificial block. The text seems to justify the sacrifice of dogs in Thoth's honour. The exact crime is unclear: it could have been rape, adultery or simple sexual excess.

The Papyrus Geneva places him in Horus' childhood. Isis warns her son to keep away from Babi, who roams the land with seventy-seven dogs. After a bite, Isis tells Horus to treat the wound with a plant called Sryw; the biting dog eats it and dies, symbolising the removal of the venom.

Worship and religion

The sources record no independent cult of Babi. His clearest ritual link is the sacrifice of dogs in honour of Thoth, which the myth seems to justify.

Ancient sources: Papyrus Jumilhac, Papyrus Geneva, Temple of Edfu, Mythological Manual of the Delta and Egyptian funerary texts.

Trivia

A yellow-eyed dog. In the Papyrus Jumilhac Babi appears as a red dog with yellow eyes, an animal form that contrasts with his identity as a baboon.
Seventy-seven dogs. The Papyrus Geneva describes Babi roaming the land accompanied by seventy-seven dogs.
