loa of love and beauty in Haitian Vodou

Erzulie

Erzulie is a loa, or spirit, of Haitian Vodou, associated with love, beauty, dance and luxury. She is in fact a family of spirits linked to water, femininity and feminine bodies, and she remains a central figure in Haiti's Afro-Caribbean religion.

The myth

According to Vodou tradition, Erzulie is the feminine deity par excellence. She wears three wedding rings, one for each of her husbands: Damballa, Agwe and Ogoun. Her best-known form is Erzulie Freda, a coquettish lover of beauty who blesses her followers with material riches. Those she possesses, her 'horses', often weep tears of longing, because she is believed never to attain the deepest desire of her heart.

Erzulie Dantor, of the Petro nation, is her sterner counterpart. She is the mother of Ti Jean Petro and is often shown as a dark-skinned woman protectively holding a child. Where Freda distributes wealth, Dantor grants spiritual knowledge and takes revenge on those who wrong women and children.

Worship and religion

During ceremonies, possessed women fall into trance and perform courtship and seduction rituals toward the men present. Offerings include jewelry, perfume and sweet cakes. Her major pilgrimage site is Saut-d'Eau in Haiti, where a supposed Marian apparition appeared in 1847 atop a palm tree. Catholics identified the figure as the Virgin of Mount Carmel, while Vodou followers believe it was Erzulie. Every year, from 14 to 16 July, a large pilgrimage gathers by the Le Saut waterfall.

Legacy

Her name appears in music from jazz to rap, including Cecil Taylor's 1988 album Erzulie Maketh Scent and the Broadway musical Once on This Island, where she is the goddess of love. She also appears in Terry Pratchett's novel Witches Abroad as Erzulie Gogol, a swamp witch.

Trivia

A Black Madonna. Erzulie Dantor is linked to the Black Madonna of Częstochowa, whose copies may have reached Haiti with Polish soldiers who fought in the Haitian Revolution from 1802.
A seductress in rock. In Steely Dan's song Two Against Nature, the narrator describes Madame Erzulie as a succubus who bangs you silly but leaves a nasty bite.
