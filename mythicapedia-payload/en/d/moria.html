naiad nymph of Greek mythology

Moria

Moria is a naiad nymph of Greek mythology, sister of Tylus, who dwelt by the river Hermus. Her story appears in Nonnus of Panopolis's Dionysiaca and revolves around a herb able to restore life.

The myth

According to Nonnus of Panopolis, Tylus accidentally touched a serpent that coiled around his body and suffocated him; he was not the creature's first victim. Moria could only watch helplessly until Damasen, a giant son of Gaia, arrived. She begged him for help, and he killed the serpent by uprooting a tree trunk and striking it with it.

Then the female serpent, the monster's mate, appeared and used a magical herb called 'Zeus' flower' to bring the dead serpent back to life. Moria then used the same herb to revive her brother.

Some modern scholars have suggested the tale may have roots in Lydian mythology. A comparable episode is that of Polyidus, who used an herb to resurrect Glaucus, son of Minos.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis's epic poem the Dionysiaca.

Trivia

Meaning of her name. Her name, Moria, means 'sacred olive-tree' in Ancient Greek.
Parallel with Polyidus. Her episode is compared with that of Polyidus, who revived Glaucus, son of Minos, with an herb.
