river god of the Cydnus in Cilicia

Cydnus

The Cydnus is both a river and a river god of Cilicia, a region in what is now southern Turkey. Along with the Pyramus and the Saros, it was one of the region's three great rivers.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, the Cydnus was a son of Oceanus and Tethys, though some accounts name Anchiale as his mother. His son was Comaetho.

The best-known story links him to Alexander the Great: Arrian reports that the conqueror fell seriously ill after bathing in the river's icy waters.

Worship and religion

His image often appeared on the reverse of coins minted at Tarsus. The sources offer no details about specific rituals.

Legacy

The river lives on in its name, now Tarsus Çayı. In the Middle Ages Arabs knew it as Nahr al-Baradan, and the lagoon at its mouth, the Rhegma, served as the harbour of Tarsus.

Ancient sources: Arrian mentions it in the Anabasis (2.4.7), and Stephanus of Byzantium cites Athenodorus of Tarsus.

Trivia

A diverted course. The river originally flowed through the middle of Tarsus. After a flood, Justinian I changed its course so that it went around the city.
Waterfalls near the city. North of Tarsus, the river forms quite impressive waterfalls.
