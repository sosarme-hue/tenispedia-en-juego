Roman god of fertility and flocks

Lupercus

Lupercus is an ancient god of Roman mythology, invoked to protect fertility and herds. His name comes from lupus, “wolf”, and his cult was tied to the Lupercal, a sacred cave on the Palatine, and to the festival of the Lupercalia.

The myth

According to tradition, Lupercus was linked to the wolf. At first he was identified with the sacred wolf of Mars. Later he came to be seen as an epithet of Faunus, known as Faunus Lupercus, and finally he was assimilated to the Greek god Pan.

The Lupercal, the cave at the foot of the northwest slope of the Palatine, is where the legend places the discovery of the twins: according to the story, Faustulus found Romulus and Remus there. Through this connection, the place and the god became tied to the legendary origins of Rome.

Worship and religion

Lupercus had his sanctuary in the Lupercal. His priests, the Luperci, were divided into two brotherhoods, the Luperci Quintiles and the Luperci Fabiani, which tradition linked to the companions of Romulus and Remus. On 15 February the Lupercalia were celebrated. First a goat and a dog were sacrificed; then two young Luperci, naked and wearing the hides of the victims, their faces smeared with the animals' blood, ran around the Palatine striking whomever they met with whips made of hide, especially women. The women sought the lashes because they believed this made them fertile.

Trivia

A cave with two stories. The Lupercal was the sanctuary of Lupercus and also the place where, according to legend, Faustulus found the twins Romulus and Remus.
Three names, one god. Over time, Lupercus was identified with the sacred wolf of Mars, with Faunus and with Pan.
