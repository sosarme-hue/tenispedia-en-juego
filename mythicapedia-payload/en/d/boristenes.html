Greek river god of the Dnieper

Borysthenes

Borysthenes is the river god of the Greek river that is identified today with the Dnieper, which flows into the Black Sea. The name also referred to an ancient town near its mouth, showing how the divinity of the river and its territory were closely linked in Greek geography.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Borysthenes was the son of Oceanus and Tethys, that is, of the great primordial waters. He fathered a nymph, Borysthenis, and a son, Thoas, who became king of the Taurians.

The sources give few details of his own deeds. His role is mainly genealogical: that of a deified river linking the world of rivers with the sea.

Legacy

The name survived in literature and history. Edward Gibbon mentions it often in his History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire. The emperor Hadrian wrote an epitaph for his favourite horse, also named Borysthenes, and that epitaph survives to this day.

Ancient sources: Herodotus describes both the river and the town in book 4 of his Histories.

Trivia

A horse with an imperial epitaph. The emperor Hadrian wrote an epitaph for his horse, which he named Borysthenes. The text survives, a rare example of a written tribute to an animal.
A river with two names. The name Borysthenes was also used for a town near the river mouth, Olbia, so the same word could designate both a river and a settlement.
A route to the Black Sea. The river served as a route to the Black Sea for peoples such as the Goths, as recorded in historical texts.
