mother of Garuda and Aruna in Hindu mythology

Vinata

Vinata is a figure of Hindu mythology, daughter of Daksha and mother of Aruna and Garuda. Her story, told in the Mahabharata, explains the origin of the nagas and Garuda's bond with Vishnu.

The myth

According to tradition, Vinata was a daughter of Daksha and the younger sister of Kadru. Both became wives of Kashyapa, who granted each a wish: Kadru asked for a thousand valiant naga sons, and Vinata for only two sons, but more powerful than her sister's.

Kadru's eggs hatched after five hundred years, while Vinata's remained closed. Impatient, she broke one open in secret, and from it emerged Aruna, half-formed. Angered, the young being condemned her to be Kadru's slave, but told her the other egg would hatch and that her son would set her free. He then rose into the sky and became the charioteer of Surya.

Her enslavement followed the churning of the Ocean of Milk. Vinata and Kadru wagered over the colour of the tail of Uccaihshravas, the horse Indra had obtained: Kadru said black, Vinata said white. Kadru's naga sons clung to the tail and made it look black, so Vinata lost the bet and became a slave.

When Garuda hatched, he learned that his mother's freedom required the amrita, the elixir of immortality, which he had to steal from Devaloka. Following his mother's advice, he devoured nishadas along the way but not a single brahmana. His feat earned him a boon from Vishnu, who made him his mount. He brought the nectar to the nagas as promised, but Indra stole it; even so, Vinata was freed.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata tells her story, and the Brahmanda Purana links it to the Solar Dynasty.

Trivia

Vishnu's mount. Garuda earned from Vishnu the privilege of becoming his mount after defeating several celestial deities.
Charioteer of the sun. Aruna, Garuda's brother, became the charioteer of Surya after emerging from the egg Vinata broke.
