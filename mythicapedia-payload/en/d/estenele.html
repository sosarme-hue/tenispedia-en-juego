Danaid of Greek mythology

Sthenele

Sthenele is one of the fifty Danaids of Greek mythology, daughters of Danaus and linked to the city of Argos. Her story is tied to the killing of the husbands on the wedding night, and to the memory of these women in the name of the ship Argo.

The myth

According to tradition, Sthenele was a daughter of Danaus and Memphis II. Together with her forty-nine sisters she was married to the sons of Aegyptus, but all the sisters, except Hypermnestra, killed their husbands on the wedding night, in revenge for the murder of their brothers. Only Lynceus, Hypermnestra's husband, was spared.

Sthenele lived with her sisters in Argos, where they played an active and prominent role in public life, taking part in decisions such as the campaign against Tiryns or the conquest of Epidaurus. Some sources say she escaped to nearby Hermione, where she received the support of the Dorians.

A namesake, daughter of Acastus and wife of Menoetius, appears in other accounts as the mother of Patroclus.

Legacy

The ship Argo, sailed by Jason and his companions, takes its name from the city of Argos, in recognition of these women.

Ancient sources: Pausanias records, in the part of his work devoted to the Argolid, the story of the killing of Danaus and his daughters.

Trivia

A ship named after a city. Jason's ship, the Argo, takes its name from the city of Argos. According to tradition, the name honours the Danaids who lived there.
Women with a public voice. In Argos these women were not confined to the household: they took part in the city's public decisions, which was unusual in Greece at the time.
