nymph of Greek mythology, lover of Apollo

Stilbe

Stilbe is a naiad of Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Peneus and the naiad Creusa, and a lover of Apollo. She matters because she is the mother of the ancestral figures of the Lapiths and the Centaurs.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus, the only ancient author who names her in detail, Stilbe was the daughter of the river god Peneus, who gave his name to the Peneus river in Thessaly, and of the naiad Creusa, daughter of Oceanus. Apollo lay with her, and their twin sons Lapithus and Centaurus became the ancestors of the Lapiths and the Centaurs. According to the myth, these two peoples ended up at war in the Centauromachy, which broke out during the wedding of Pirithous and Hippodamia.

Versions vary in the details. A scholium also makes Stilbe and Apollo the parents of Aineus, father of Cyzicus, and Diodorus says Triopas was the son of Lapithus and grandson of Stilbe, although other authors made him the son of Canace and Poseidon. Stilbe was also the mother of the nymph Chariclo by a river god, and Chariclo married the centaur Chiron, linking this nymph to the world of the Centaurs.

Beyond these references, she barely appears in ancient texts, so her figure is understood mainly through her descendants. For that reason her name appears mostly in genealogical lists and in commentaries on other stories.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus, in his Historical Library, names her as the mother of Lapithus and Centaurus; a scholium also mentions her.

Trivia

Mother of Chariclo. According to the sources, Stilbe was the mother of the nymph Chariclo, who married the centaur Chiron.
Two accounts of Triopas's father. According to Diodorus, Triopas was the son of Lapithus and grandson of Stilbe, while other authors considered him the son of Canace and Poseidon.
