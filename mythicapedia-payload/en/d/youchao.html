Chinese culture hero who invented wooden houses

Youchao

Youchao is a culture hero of Chinese mythology, credited with inventing wooden houses. He belongs to the Youchao clan, one of the Four Clans that marked the period of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors, alongside Fu Xi, Suiren and Shennong.

The myth

The name Youchao means roughly 'Clan of Nesters'. The Old Chinese prefix you denoted a clan, but once it was forgotten, Han and Tang texts such as the Han Feizi treated the name as if it belonged to a single person. The Song scholar Luo Mi clarified in the Lushi that it was a clan. It is also known as Da Chao, probably a variant name, recorded in the Taiping Yulan.

According to the Han Feizi, the Youchao taught people to build wooden structures to protect themselves from animals. Zhuangzi, in his eponymous book, says they gathered nuts by day and rested in trees at night to escape predators.

The Western Zhou Lost Book of Zhou describes their downfall: a corrupt minister, given too much power by the ruler, rebelled after the ruler took it back, and the Youchao perished in the conflict. These are stories from an ancient tradition, and the clan is best understood as a legendary people rather than a specific individual.

Ancient sources: The Han Feizi, Zhuangzi and the Lost Book of Zhou, ancient texts that record the clan's teachings and its downfall.

Trivia

Trees as shelter. Zhuangzi describes the Youchao gathering nuts during the day and resting in trees at night to escape predators.
An origin at Chao Lake. Scholar Ning Yegao, citing recent archaeological findings, proposes that the Chao Lake basin was the ancestral home of the clan.
