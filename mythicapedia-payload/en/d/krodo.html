purported Saxon god of Germanic myth

Krodo

Krodo is a purported Germanic god of the Saxons, known mainly from the Saxon Chronicle of 1492. Modern historians regard him as a fabrication, so his existence is considered doubtful.

The myth

According to the chronicle, Conrad Bothe credited Julius Caesar with building several fortresses crowned by statues of Roman deities. One of them depicted Saturn, whom the local population called Krodo, and stood where Harzburg Castle was later built.

When Charlemagne occupied the region in 780, during the Saxon Wars, he supposedly ordered the statue destroyed as part of the Christianization of the Saxons. The image shows a man in a linen garment standing on a fish, holding a bucket of roses in his right hand and a wheel in his left, symbols that may refer to the four elements.

Legacy

In Bad Harzburg several streets and institutions bear his name, and the town uses his figure as a tourist mascot. A replica of the statue was erected at Harzburg Castle in 2007. German folklore also preserved tales of the pagan idol Götze Crodo in the area near Meerane.

Trivia

An altar with his name. The collegiate church of Goslar kept the so-called Krodo Altar, probably dating from the 11th century, which may have been moved from Harzburg Castle.
A tourist mascot. The town of Bad Harzburg uses the figure of Krodo as a mascot to promote itself.
