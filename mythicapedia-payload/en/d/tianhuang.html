first legendary sovereign of China, the "Heavenly Sovereign"

Tianhuang

Tianhuang, the "Heavenly Sovereign", is the first legendary ruler of China after the era of Pangu and the first of the Three Sovereigns. He matters because he opens the line of mythical Chinese rulers.

The myth

According to the legends, Tianhuang emerged once heaven and earth had taken shape. He is described as having twelve heads, though other sources say thirteen. The texts portray him as a detached ruler: he barely intervened, yet people's customs improved. He reigned under the influence of the wood element, lived eighteen thousand years and had twelve children who helped him govern the world.

His greatest achievement was ending chaos, dividing the land among the many tribes and choosing the best people to rule them. He was succeeded by Renhuang. Accounts differ: one tradition says his remains lie beneath Kunlun, in the region of Zhuzhou, while another places him alongside Tianhuang and Dihuang as one of three sovereigns, in which Renhuang had nine brothers and divided the world into nine provinces.

Ancient sources: Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji), with Sima Zhen's supplement; the Yiwen Leiju, a Tang dynasty encyclopedia that gathers several traditions; and the Song dynasty Lushi.

Trivia

A name with meaning. The Song dynasty Lushi records his family name as Wang, meaning "to observe" or "the night of the full moon", and his given name as Huo, meaning "to hunt or catch".
Twelve or thirteen heads. The sources disagree on the number of heads: the Shiji and its supplement say twelve, while other traditions cited by the Yiwen Leiju give thirteen.
