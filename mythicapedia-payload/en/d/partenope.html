Greek mythological siren

Parthenope

Parthenope is one of the sirens of Greek mythology, and one of the few who has a personal name. Tradition links her to the origins of Naples, which according to ancient accounts took its early name from her.

The myth

According to Latin texts, Parthenope was the daughter of the river god Achelous and a Muse, although the sources disagree: some name Melpomene, others Terpsichore, and others Esterope. Together with her sisters Leucosia and Ligeia, she lured sailors with her song. When Odysseus managed to resist them, the sirens threw themselves into the sea and drowned.

The waves carried Parthenope's body to the shore, where she received a burial with great honours. That place grew into a village and, eventually, into the city of Naples, once called Parthenope. A later Roman version tells that the centaur Vesuvius fell in love with her; Jupiter turned the centaur into the volcano Vesuvius and her into the city.

Worship and religion

According to Timaeus of Sicily, sacrifices were offered to her at Neapolis and torch races were held in her honour. Strabo mentions a monument in her memory and a gymnastic contest held on the orders of an oracle.

Legacy

Parthenope has inspired many operas, from the eighteenth-century works of Vinci, Handel and Vicente Martin y Soler to Ennio Morricone's later version. In popular music she gives her name to songs by Clementino and Liberato, and in 2024 Paolo Sorrentino released a film titled after her.

Ancient sources: Virgil, in his Georgics, says he was nurtured by Parthenope, and Strabo and Timaeus of Sicily record the honours paid to her at Neapolis.

Trivia

2024 film. Paolo Sorrentino, a filmmaker born in Naples, directed a film called Parthenope whose main character shares the siren's name.
Nea Polis. According to Strabo, settlers from Cumae, Chalcidians and others from Pithecusae and Athens founded a new city there, Nea Polis, which gave rise to the name Naples.
