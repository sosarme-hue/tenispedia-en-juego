Zoroastrian yazata of obedience and conscience

Sraosha

Sraosha (Soroush in modern Persian) is a yazata, a divinity worthy of worship, in Persian Zoroastrianism. His Avestan name means "obedience" or "observance", and he is the being who teaches humankind conscience and the religion of Ahura Mazda.

The myth

According to the Avestan texts, Sraosha already appears in the Gathas, the oldest hymns of Zoroastrianism, which tradition attributes to Zoroaster himself. There he is associated with the Amesha Spentas, the six "Bounteous Immortals" through whom Ahura Mazda brought creation into being. His main role is to spread conscience and the beauty of life, and to teach humanity the religion of Ahura Mazda.

In Yasna 57 he is described as the strongest, swiftest and most awe-inspiring of youths, and as the first in creation to worship Ahura Mazda. He is said to win his battles against evil, using sacred formulas as his weapons. In Yasht 11, devoted entirely to him, he keeps a constant vigil over humankind and fights the druj, the forces of deceit. Later tradition makes him one of three guardians of the Chinvat Bridge, which every soul of the dead must cross. He is the only one of them who accompanies the soul on the crossing, and he is closely linked with Ashi, "Recompense".

Worship and religion

Sraosha has a central place in Zoroastrian liturgy. Yasna 56 and 57 form part of the principal act of worship, though they describe the worshipper's relationship with him rather than addressing him directly. The officiating priest receives the epithet Soroushvarez, and the seventeenth day of each month in the Zoroastrian calendar is dedicated to Soroush.

Legacy

His name survives in modern Persian as Soroush, a form also used for the angel Gabriel in Islam, where he is called Jibril.

Ancient sources: The Gathas, Yasna, Yashts and Vendidad of the Avesta, and the Middle Persian commentaries of the 9th to 12th centuries.

Trivia

Angel of Conscience. He is frequently called the "Angel of Conscience" or "Voice of Conscience", a title that reflects his role as teacher of Daena.
Calendar day. The seventeenth day of each month in the Zoroastrian calendar bears his name.
