Sumerian goddess of the earth

Uras

Uras (also Urash) is the Mesopotamian goddess of the earth, regarded as the personification of arable ground. She is best known as the consort of Anu, the sky god, and is counted among the primeval deities of the pantheon.

The myth

According to tradition, Uras appears mostly as the wife of Anu, although some accounts make her his ancestor or even an alternative name for the sky god himself. Texts present her as the mother of many deities, among them the medicine goddesses Ninisina and Ninkarrak, and also Amurru, Bau, Ishtaran and Ishkur, depending on the source. Other traditions disagree: Enki was usually said to be the son of Nammu, and Geshtinanna appears in other compositions with Duttur as her mother.

The hymns attributed to Enheduanna already name her among the couple who fathered the gods. Her link to the hero Lugalbanda is less certain, suggested only in a single passage of the poem Lugalbanda in the Mountain Cave, while other texts stress that his parents were absent.

Worship and religion

Worship of Uras is poorly documented. Offerings to her and Anu as a pair appear in Ur III records, and some Old Babylonian letters mention her. She may have had a temple at Nippur, where she was honoured as Urash-Nibru, "Urash of the city of Nippur". There is no evidence that her cult survives.

Legacy

Wilfred G. Lambert suggested that the element uraš in the name of the god Ninurta could derive from her name. Outside Mesopotamian scholarship, the sources record no significant later presence.

Ancient sources: Hymns attributed to Enheduanna, the god list An = Anum, and Sumerian hymns to Enki, Geshtinanna and Shulgi, among others.

Trivia

A god with the same name. Urash was also the name of a male agricultural god worshipped at Dilbat. In some texts it is unclear which of the two is meant.
A double name. A god list from late Assyrian copies gives the compound name Ki-Urash, joining the earth into a single primordial being.
