Irish god, prince of the Daoine Sidhe

Aed

Aed (or Aodh) is a prince of the Daoine Sidhe and a god of Irish mythology. Different traditions make him a son of Lir or of the Dagda, showing how the stories tie him to Ireland's great divine lineages.

The myth

According to some inscriptions, Aed was the eldest son of Lir, High King of the Tuatha Dé Danann, and Aoibh, daughter of Bodb Dearg. Aoibh died giving birth to four children, two pairs of twins, and Aed was in the first pair with Fionnuala. Aoife, Lir's second wife, grew jealous of them and plotted to kill them, but her servants refused. She then cursed them to live as swans for nine hundred years.

In the Dindsenchas, however, Aed is the son of the Dagda and brother of Aengus and Cermait. In this account, a warrior named Corrgend of Cruach killed him on Benn Bain Baith for seducing his wife Tethra. The Dagda cursed Corrgend, who could not free himself from Aed's body until he found a suitable stone for the grave, and forced him to dig the burial at Ailech of Imchell.

Ancient sources: Medieval Irish texts such as the Dindsenchas, which record the names and places of the tradition.

Trivia

A name of fire. His name comes from the Old Irish word for fire, which derives from a Proto-Indo-European verb meaning to burn or to kindle.
Swift horses. In the Dindsenchas he is given the epithet of the wind-swift horses and called Aed Luirgnech, meaning big-shins.
