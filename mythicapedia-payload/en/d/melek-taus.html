Peacock Angel, central figure of Yazidism

Melek Taus

Melek Taus is the central figure of Yazidism, a Kurdish religion with roots in Mesopotamian and Iranian traditions. Yazidis regard him as a benevolent angel, leader of the archangels and an emanation of God, who is responsible for everything that happens in the world.

The myth

According to Yazidi tradition, before creating this world God made seven divine beings and placed Melek Taus at their head. After passing a test he was entrusted with the care of the earth, and some accounts say he created the cosmos from the cosmic egg. Other popular stories tell that after weeping for seven thousand years, his tears filled seven jars that put out the fires of Hell.

When he descended to earth, the seven colours of the rainbow turned into a seven-coloured bird, the peacock, which flew across the world to bless it and finally rested at Lalish. This is why the rainbow is linked with his blessing. The linguist Jamal Nebez proposed that the name Taus comes from Greek, though this idea remains controversial.

Worship and religion

His main sanctuary is Lalish, whose shrines are decorated with countless peacock images. The first Wednesday of Nisan marks his descent to earth and the Yazidi New Year, Sersal, when 365 or 366 lamps are lit, one for each day of the year. The peacock is a sacred bird that Yazidis do not hunt, eat or ill-treat. His veneration remains alive among Yazidi communities.

Legacy

He appears in Alan Moore's graphic novel Top 10, in Hugo Pratt's comic The Golden House of Samarkand, and in S. M. Stirling's novel The Peshawar Lancers. The Swedish band Therion dedicated a song to him on the double album Lemuria Sirius B (2003), and a Colombian black metal band takes his name.

Ancient sources: The Yazidi sacred hymns (qewl) and the book Mishefa Resh, which identifies him with Ezrayil or Ezazil.

Trivia

A bird protected by faith. Yazidis are forbidden to hunt, eat, curse or ill-treat the peacock, and its image adorns the Lalish sanctuary and many homes.
New Year lamps. On the eve of Sersal, 365 or 366 lamps are lit, as many as the days of the year, as a symbol of Melek Taus descending to earth.
