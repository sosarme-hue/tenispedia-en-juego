Chinese deity who protected against pirates

Chun Kwan

Chun Kwan, also called Chun Kwan Tai Tai, is a Chinese deity who rose to divine status after Ng, a local man, freed Guangdong from pirate raids. His story shows how a regional hero came to be worshipped after death.

The myth

According to tradition, during the reign of Emperor Lizong of the Southern Song dynasty, the Guangdong coast suffered constant pirate raids that the army could barely hold back. Ng led a force that destroyed the pirate bands and restored peace to the people.

After his death, his spirit kept doing good in Lung Kong. The emperor gave him the title Chun Kwan Tai Tai and ordered temples built in his honour. He is also credited with kind deeds on Tsing Yi Island in Hong Kong, where an inscription at the Chun Kwan Temple commemorates them.

Worship and religion

In Thailand he is known as Chao Pho Ton Sai, the tutelary spirit of the banyan tree. His shrines are few and little known. One lies on the fourth floor of a restaurant car park in Soi Texas, in Bangkok's Chinatown, which is usually closed to the public and opens only during festivals.

Legacy

His presence endures in the temples dedicated to him in Hong Kong and Thailand.

Trivia

Banyan tree. In Thailand his name, Chao Pho Ton Sai, means 'lord of the banyan tree'.
Shrine in a car park. One of his Thai temples is on the fourth floor of a Bangkok car park and is rarely open to the public.
