Ulster hero of Irish mythology

Celtchar

Celtchar is an Ulster hero from the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, a tall, fearsome warrior known mainly for the three tasks he must perform to atone for a killing.

The myth

Son of Uthechar or Uthidir, he lives at Dún Lethglaise in Downpatrick and is married to Findmór of Dún Sobairche. His spear, the Lúin Cheltchair, thirsts so much for blood that it must be dipped in a cauldron of poison to be controlled.

In the Táin Bó Cúailnge he helps Conchobar mac Nessa rescue eight captive Ulster women. After killing Blaí Briugu, who had slept with his wife, he must rid Ulster of three menaces. The first is Conganchnes mac Dedad, whose skin is like horn; only hot spits driven into the soles of his feet can kill him, and his daughter Niam reveals this. The second is Luch Donn, a ferocious dog that Celtchar overcomes by offering it a softened alder log and pulling its heart out through its throat. The third is his own dog, Dóelchú, which had escaped and preyed on the cattle. When he kills it with his spear, a drop of its poisonous blood runs down the shaft into his body, and he dies.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Táin Bó Cúailnge and the Scéla Mucce Maic Dathó, medieval Irish tales of the Ulster Cycle.

Trivia

A dog named after a mouse. Luch Donn means «brown mouse», an ironic name for such a ferocious dog.
A poisoned spear. The spear Lúin Cheltchair had to be dipped in a cauldron of poison to tame its thirst for blood.
