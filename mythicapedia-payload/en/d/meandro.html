Greek river god of the Meander

Meander

Meander is a river god in Greek mythology, the patron of the river that bears his name in Caria, in southern Asia Minor. He matters mainly as the father of figures tied to the legendary history of the Aegean coast.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Meander was one of the sons of Oceanus and the Titan Tethys. He had several sons and daughters, among them Kalamos; the daughters include Samia, who gave her name to the island of Samos, Cyanee, wife of the hero Miletus, and Callirhoe, wife of Car, eponym of the Carians.

A story told by Pseudo-Plutarch gives a different account. Meander went to war against the Pessinuntines and vowed to the Mother of the Gods to sacrifice the first person to come out and congratulate him if he won. On his return, the first to greet him were his mother, his son and his sister. Unable to bear keeping the vow, he threw himself into the river, which, according to the tale, took its name from him. In this version his parents are Cercaphus and Anaxibia, not Oceanus and Tethys.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, which lists him among the children of Oceanus and Tethys, and the story attributed to Pseudo-Plutarch about his vow and his death.

Trivia

Origin of the river's name. According to the story attributed to Pseudo-Plutarch, the Meander river received its name from the god who threw himself into its waters after keeping his vow.
Two versions of his lineage. Hesiod makes him a son of Oceanus and Tethys, while in the story attributed to Pseudo-Plutarch his parents are Cercaphus and Anaxibia.
