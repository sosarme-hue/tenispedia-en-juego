queen of the Amazons in Greek myth

Orithyia

Orithyia is a queen of the Amazons in Greek myth, daughter of Marpesia and a key figure in the story of Hercules' quest for the girdle of the Amazon queen, his ninth labor. Her story reflects the fearsome military reputation of a people admired and feared across the Greek world.

The myth

According to the tradition, Orithyia succeeded her mother Marpesia as queen of the Amazons, ruling alongside her sisters Antiope and Hippolyte. While she was fighting the Phrygian kings Otreus and Migdon and Priam of Troy by the Sangarius river, Heracles and his men, Theseus among them, invaded Amazon territory and seized Antiope, who ended up as Theseus's wife.

Orithyia swore revenge and asked the Scythian king Sagillus for help; he sent his son Panasagoras. The Amazons invaded Attica, occupied Athens and besieged the Acropolis, but Panasagoras abandoned the decisive battle and they suffered an almost fatal defeat. Orithyia fled to Megara, where she died of grief and helplessness. Having vowed to remain a virgin, she left no offspring, and Penthesilea took charge of the Amazons.

Legacy

Giovanni Boccaccio devoted a chapter to her in his work Famous Women.

Ancient sources: Justinus, in his Epitome of Pompeius Trogus' History of the World, records her role in Hercules' expedition.

Trivia

A vow of virginity. According to the sources, Orithyia had sworn to remain a virgin all her life, so she left no offspring.
An alliance with the Scythians. The Amazons were considered descendants of the Scythians, and the two peoples maintained a close alliance.
