cloud and storm spirit of northwestern Spain

Nuberu

The nuberu is a figure of northwestern Spanish folklore linked to clouds, storms and rain, and usually regarded as malevolent. Its appearance varies by region: an elderly, one-eyed man dressed in pelts, or a crowd of small, mischievous spirits.

The myth

According to Asturian tradition, a nuberu fell to earth from a cloud and asked for shelter, until a peasant from Taja took him in for a night. Grateful, he watered the peasant's fields. Years later, the peasant, presumed dead during the Crusades, was carried home by the nuberu, who flew him back just in time to stop his wife's wedding. She recognised him by a mark on his chest. In some versions, this nuberu is called Xuan Cabritu.

In Cantabria they are small, mischievous beings, often in groups, who cause downpours and damage. In Galicia they appear as hairy little men with twisted tails, or as giants who forge lightning in a smithy.

Worship and religion

To drive them away, villagers light candles and ring bells during stormy nights, a custom described as common practice. Cantabrian fishermen blame them for the sudden galernas and fear them at sea.

Trivia

Xuan Cabritu. In Asturias, the nuberu is also known as Xuan Cabritu. Some versions place his home in distant places such as Egypt, though there is no evidence of Phoenician traders in the area.
A missing eye. Many descriptions show him with one eye, a trait that some scholars compare with Odin.
