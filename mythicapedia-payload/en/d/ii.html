jötunn (giant) of Norse mythology

Iði

Iði is a jötunn, a giant of Norse mythology, son of Alvaldi and brother of Þjazi and Gangr. His name is usually read as 'the active one' or 'the hard-working one', linked to an Old Norse word for work.

The myth

In the Skáldskaparmál, part of the Eddas, Iði is named as the son of the jötunn Alvaldi, a giant described as very rich in gold, and as the brother of two other giants, Þjazi and Gangr. That is essentially what the surviving sources tell us: a place in a family of giants, with no episodes recounted about him.

Jötnar were, in Norse tradition, the giants who dwelt at the edges of the ordered world and often clashed with the Æsir gods. No deeds are attributed to him, so his role in myth is simply membership in this giant lineage.

Ancient sources: Skáldskaparmál, part of the Norse Eddas.

Trivia

A name with meaning. The name Iði is usually translated as 'the active one' or 'the hard-working one'. It is related to modern Scandinavian words such as Norwegian idig and Swedish idog, meaning 'hard-working'.
A family of giants. Iði appears alongside his brothers Þjazi and Gangr, showing that the sources group giants into family lineages.
