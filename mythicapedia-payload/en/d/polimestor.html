king of the Bistonians in Thrace

Polymestor

Polymestor was a king of the Bistonians, a people of Thrace, in Greek mythology. His betrayal of Polydorus, son of Priam and Hecuba, made him a key figure in Hecuba's revenge, the theme of Euripides' tragedy.

The myth

Polymestor was king of the Bistonians, a people of the Thracian Chersonese. Priam, fearing for his youngest son's safety during the siege, sent Polydorus to his court with a treasure so that the boy could survive if Troy fell. According to the myth, when the city fell, Polymestor murdered the young prince to keep the riches and threw his body into the sea.

Hecuba, now a slave of the Greeks, learned of the betrayal, either in a dream or by finding the corpse on the shore, depending on the version. She asked Agamemnon to help her take revenge, and he agreed. Pretending to reveal a hidden treasure, the queen lured the Thracian king and his two sons into her tent, where the Trojan women killed the boys and Hecuba gouged out his eyes.

In Euripides' tragedy Hecuba, the blinded Polymestor prophesies the deaths of Hecuba, Cassandra and Agamemnon. Ovid tells that the grieving queen was transformed into a black bitch. Hyginus gives another version: Priam entrusted Polydorus to his daughter Ilione, wife of Polymestor, and the king, tempted by the Greeks, killed his own son Deipylus thinking he was killing the Trojan; the real Polydorus, learning this, blinded and stabbed the traitor.

Ancient sources: Euripides, in the tragedy Hecuba; Ovid, in the Metamorphoses; and Hyginus, in his Fabulae.

Trivia

A black bitch. According to Ovid, Hecuba was transformed into a black bitch and ran off howling, so her attackers, confused, withdrew.
A shared name. Polymestor was also the name of a Greek king of Arcadia, distinct from the king of the Bistonians.
