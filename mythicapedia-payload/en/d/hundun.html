faceless primordial chaos being of Chinese myth

Hundun

Hundun is a legendary faceless being in Chinese mythology, both a figure of primordial chaos that preceded the separation of heaven and earth. It matters because its image of a closed, round world without distinguishable features anchors Chinese cosmogony, comparable to the world egg.

The myth

In the ancient accounts, Hundun appears as a ruler or being without features, the closed centre of the world before any form existed. In the Zuozhuan commentary, however, he is a worthless son of the Yellow Emperor, dull and wicked, whom Shun banishes together with four other villains, and whom all the people under heaven call Chaos.

In the Zhuangzi he stars in a parable: the emperor of the South Sea, Shu (Brief), and the emperor of the North Sea, Hu (Sudden), repay the hospitality of the central emperor, Hundun. Since he lacks the seven openings that humans have, they decide to bore them, and on the seventh day Hundun dies.

Legacy

The term hundun has entered everyday language meaning confusion or disorder, and its linguistic kinship with wonton, the dumpling soup, is a connection scholars debate.

Ancient sources: The Zuozhuan, Zhuangzi, Daodejing, Mengzi, Shijing and Chu Ci, which mention different uses of the term.

Trivia

Soup namesake. The term hundun is linguistically related to wonton, the dumpling soup, though scholars debate this connection.
Seven openings. In the Zhuangzi parable, Hundun dies when his visitors bore the seven openings of his face.
