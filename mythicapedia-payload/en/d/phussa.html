twenty-first Buddha before Gautama

Phussa

Phussa is, according to the Pali canon of Theravada Buddhism, the twenty-first of the Buddhas who preceded Gautama. His figure illustrates the Buddhist idea of a succession of Buddhas across kalpas, each with his own teaching.

The myth

According to the Buddhavamsa, Phussa was born in Kāsika, son of King Jayasena and Queen Sirimā. He married Princess Kisāgotamī and reigned for 9,000 years, living in three palaces. After his son Anupama was born, he gave up worldly life upon seeing four signs and departed on an elephant, accompanied by ten million followers who also became ascetics.

After six months of asceticism, he practised alone and attained enlightenment beneath the Maha Bodhi tree. Earlier, a merchant's daughter named Sirivaddhā had offered him milk-rice. He lived for 90,000 years and died at the Senārāma monastery. In his time, Gautama was a king named Vijitāvī, who after hearing his teachings gave away his possessions to become an ascetic; Phussa predicted that he would become a Buddha named Gotama after 92 kalpas.

Ancient sources: Buddhavamsa of the Theravada Pali canon and its commentary.

Trivia

Legendary stature. The sources give him a height of 58 cubits, about 87 feet, and he is often compared with the sun and the moon.
A king who would become a Buddha. King Vijitāvī, whom Phussa foretold would become the Buddha Gotama, represents Gautama's path before his final existence in the tradition.
