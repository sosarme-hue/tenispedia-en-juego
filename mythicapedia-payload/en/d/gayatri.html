Hindu goddess, personification of the Gayatri Mantra

Gayatri

Gayatri is the personified form of the Gayatri Mantra, a hymn from the Vedic texts, which is why she is also called Vedamata, 'mother of the Vedas'. Her importance grew alongside that of the mantra, and in part she came to be merged with Sarasvati.

The myth

Originally, Gayatri was the name of a metre of the Rig Veda consisting of twenty-four syllables. Most scholars take the goddess to be the feminine form of Gayatra, another name of the Vedic solar god Savitr, and so she is linked to him and to the goddess Savitri.

According to the scholar Dominik Haas, the goddess took shape in the post-Vedic period. She first appears as a female counterpart of Savitr in the Jaiminiya Upanishad Brahmana, though there she is more a quality than a deity. Then, in the Gopatha Brahmana, Savitr and Savitri become the parents of the Vedic student, and she is declared mother of the Vedas. The Mahanarayana Upanishad explicitly links her with the mantra and with Sarasvati. Later, the Mahabharata goes so far as to say the Vedas came out of her mouth. The Mahabharata also tells the story of Savitri and Satyavan, in which the two goddesses are fused, although in the Harshacharita they still appear as friends.

Worship and religion

The oldest bronze images of Gayatri come from Himachal Pradesh, where she was revered as the consort of Sadasiva. In iconography she appears with five faces and ten arms, and in later depictions she sits on a swan. Today she remains present in Hindu religious practice through the mantra, which is recited during the Vedic initiation (upanayana).

Legacy

The Gayatri Mantra is one of the best-known prayers in Hinduism, and its recitation remains alive among the faithful. The goddess is associated with knowledge and learning, and in modern iconography she appears holding a book beside a swan.

Ancient sources: The Mahanarayana Upanishad, the Mahabharata, the Gopatha Brahmana and the Savitri Upanishad mention her as mother of the Vedas or identify her with the mantra.

Trivia

Twenty-four syllables. The mantra has twenty-four syllables, which gave the Vedic metre its name. The texts assign each one a rishi, a metre and a deity.
Five heads. The Mahanarayana Upanishad describes her with five heads, which the Taittiriya Sandhya Bhashyam says represent five of the Vedangas.
