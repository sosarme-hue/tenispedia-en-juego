one of the fifty Danaids of Greek mythology

Scaea

Scaea is one of the fifty Danaids, the daughters of King Danaus and Queen Europe in Greek mythology. Her story belongs to the tale of Danaus's daughters, who were punished in the afterlife after killing their husbands on their wedding night.

The myth

According to the tradition, Danaus ordered his daughters to kill their husbands on their wedding night. Scaea, like nearly all her sisters except Hypermnestra, obeyed and killed her first husband, Daiphron, son of Aegyptus. She later married Archander, with whom she had a son, Metanastes.

For this crime, the Danaids were punished in the underworld by having to carry water to fill a bottomless tub. The water always leaked out, so they had to keep trying forever.

In some accounts, Scaea remarried Archander, while her sister Automate took Architeles, Archander's brother, as her husband. Both were sons of Achaeus, who came from Phthiotis to settle in Argos.
