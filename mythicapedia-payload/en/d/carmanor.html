Cretan priest who purified Apollo

Carmanor

Carmanor is a Cretan priest in Greek mythology, best known for purifying Apollo after he killed the Delphic dragon Python. In his house at Tarrha, in Crete, Apollo lay with the nymph Acacallis.

The myth

According to the myth, Apollo and Artemis, after killing Python at Delphi, came to Crete to be purified by Carmanor, because they were frightened at Aegialea and a disease struck its people. In Carmanor's house at Tarrha, Apollo mated with the nymph Acacallis, who bore Phylacides and Philander.

Carmanor had two children: Eubuleus, whose daughter Carme bore Britomartis to Zeus, and Chrysothemis, who won the first recorded contest, a hymn sung to Apollo at the Pythian games at Delphi.

Worship and religion

Some scholars suggest Eubuleus was worshipped alongside Persephone in mystery cults, and both of Carmanor's children are associated with agriculture and the harvest.

Ancient sources: The second-century geographer Pausanias recounts Apollo's purification, his union with Acacallis, and Chrysothemis's victory at Delphi.

Trivia

Britomartis. According to Cretan tradition, Britomartis was born to Zeus and Carme, the daughter of Eubuleus. She was a huntress much loved by Artemis, until Minos began to pursue her.
The first contest. Chrysothemis won the oldest singing contest, the first in which prizes were awarded, with a hymn to Apollo.
