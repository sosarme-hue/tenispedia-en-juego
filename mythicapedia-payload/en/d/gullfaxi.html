magical horse of Norse mythology

Gullfaxi

Gullfaxi is a magical horse in Norse mythology whose name means “golden mane”. It was famous for its speed on land, in the air and on water, and its story links the gods Odin and Thor with the giant Hrungnir. It belongs to a group of tales in which gods and giants compete over valuable animals and objects.

The myth

According to the myth, Gullfaxi belonged to the giant Hrungnir, and was as fast on land as in the air and on water, though not quite as fast as Sleipnir, Odin’s horse. One day the giant challenged the gods and dared Odin to a race between their horses. Odin won by a wide margin, and the humiliation angered Hrungnir, who ended up confronting Thor.

In that fight Thor managed to knock the giant down, but Hrungnir’s huge stone leg fell over him. According to the tale, the giant lay across the unconscious Thor and was strangling him. It was Magni, son of Thor and a giantess, who helped lift that leg and free him. As a reward, Thor gave him Gullfaxi.

Odin was not pleased: he said Thor had been wrong to give such a fine horse to the son of a giantess rather than to his own father.

Legacy

Gullfaxi resurfaces in modern Icelandic folklore: the tale The Horse Gullfaxi and the Sword Gunnfoder, collected by Jón Árnason, was translated into German and then into English, and included in The Crimson Fairy Book (1903), compiled by Andrew Lang.

Ancient sources: The Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, where Thor gives the horse to Magni.

Trivia

A horse with the same name. The name Gullfaxi reappears in an Icelandic folk tale collected by Jón Árnason, which has no direct connection to the giant’s horse.
