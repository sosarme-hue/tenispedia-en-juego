Bali (Mahabali)

Bali, also called Mahabali, is a daitya king of the asuras in Hindu mythology, known for his generosity and devotion to Vishnu. His story appears in ancient texts and gives rise to one of the most popular festivals in Kerala.

The myth

According to tradition, Bali was born into the asura line, the grandson of the sage Prahlada, a devotee of Vishnu who taught him duty and devotion. As king, his reign was prosperous, but his expansion eventually seized heaven, and the devas turned to Vishnu for help.

During a horse sacrifice, Vishnu appeared as the dwarf Brahmin Vamana and asked for three steps of land. Bali agreed, despite warnings from his guru Shukra. The first step covered the earth and the second the heavens. When asked where to place the third, Bali offered his own head. He was sent to Patala, but Vishnu, moved by his devotion, allowed him to visit his subjects once a year.

Worship and religion

In Kerala, Bali is remembered as a kind-hearted king. His yearly return is celebrated during the Onam festival, with decorations, lamps, feasts and gift-giving. In other states such as Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra, it is marked as Balipratipada, around the third day of Deepavali. The tradition remains alive.

Legacy

Jain texts portray him as an antagonist, and Jain temple inscriptions compare the minister Vastupala to him for his generosity. Buddhism also includes him in several sutras, such as the Lotus Sutra, where he appears among the audience listening to the Buddha's teachings.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rig Veda, linked to the goddess Diti, and in the Ramayana, the Mahabharata, the Shatapatha Brahmana, the Puranas and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Immortal. Some versions say Vishnu granted him the status of Chiranjivi, one of seven immortals.
Festival of lights. His yearly return is celebrated near Deepavali, with lit lamps and gift-giving.
