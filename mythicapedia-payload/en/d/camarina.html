Oceanid nymph, eponym of the Sicilian city of Kamarina

Camarina

Camarina is an Oceanid in Greek mythology, a daughter of the Titan of the Sea, Oceanus, and the eponymous figure of the small Sicilian city of Kamarina, near Syracuse. She matters because she is one of the few deities said to have given her name to a city.

The myth

The myths give her very little space. According to tradition, Camarina is a daughter of Oceanus and therefore one of the Oceanids. Her mother is not clearly identified in the ancient accounts: some versions name Tethys, Oceanus's sister-wife, but this is not always stated.

Hesiod, in the Theogony, says that Oceanus and Tethys fathered three thousand Oceanids, but he names only a few of them, and Camarina is not among them. Her most notable appearance is in an ode by Pindar dedicated to Psaumis, a wealthy and generous neighbour from Sicily. In it, the nymph is addressed as a daughter of Oceanus, and Psaumis is said to have enlarged her populous city.

Legacy

The name of the city of Kamarina is linked to this nymph, whom tradition presents as its protector and the origin of its name.

Ancient sources: Pindar, in an ode dedicated to Psaumis, and Hesiod, in the Theogony, which mentions the Oceanids without naming her.

Trivia

Three thousand daughters. Hesiod states that Oceanus and Tethys had three thousand Oceanids, though the Theogony names only a few of them.
An ode for a patron. Pindar wrote an ode for Psaumis, a wealthy and generous Sicilian neighbour, in which he addresses the nymph.
