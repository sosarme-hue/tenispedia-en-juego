queen consort of Krishna in Hindu mythology

Rohini

Rohini is one of the queen consorts of Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, in Hindu mythology. Her identity is sometimes merged with that of Jambavati, and her name appears in epic and Puranic texts.

The myth

According to tradition, Krishna ruled over Dvaraka in the Dvapara Yuga and had eight principal wives, the Ashtabharya, along with thousands of others. Rohini appears in the Mahabharata, the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Harivamsa. Some lists count her among the eight principal queens, while others identify her with Jambavati, a title meaning "daughter of Jambavan". The commentator Ratnagrabha accepts this identification, but Sridhara treats her as a separate figure. The Vishnu Purana credits her with sons such as Diptimat and Tamrapaksha.

In other accounts, Rohini leads the sixteen thousand non-principal wives, who were rescued by Krishna after he defeated the demon Narakasura. According to the Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata, when Krishna died and his clan came to an end, four of his wives, Rohini among them, threw themselves onto his funeral pyre.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Harivamsa mention her as a queen of Krishna.

Trivia

One wife, two identities. Some sources treat Rohini as the birth name of the queen Jambavati, while others consider them two different women.
A tragic end. The Mausala Parva says that Rohini threw herself onto Krishna's funeral pyre together with three other wives.
