Greek god of the south wind

Notus

Notus is, in Greek mythology, the god of the south wind and one of the Anemoi, the wind gods. He was linked to the storms of late summer and early autumn, and was seen as a bringer of rain and dry heat.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Notus was a son of Eos, the goddess of the dawn, and of Astraeus, and a brother of Boreas, Zephyrus and Eurus. Unlike his two more famous brothers, he has very little mythology of his own and mostly appears alongside Eurus.

In the story of the Great Deluge, Zeus locked up Boreas and the other cloud-bearing gales and let Notus loose, and he poured rain over the whole earth until it drowned nearly everyone. In the Odyssey, Aeolus gave Odysseus a bag holding all the winds except Zephyrus. His crewmates opened it thinking it held treasure, and Notus escaped and blew the ships back to Aeolia. Later, he and Eurus kept Odysseus stranded for a month on Thrinacia, the island of the sun god Helios.

In the Dionysiaca he lives with his father and pours water from a jug when Demeter visits. Lucian of Samosata shows him with Zephyrus in his Dialogues of the Sea Gods, where he admits, rather disappointed, that he has seen nothing worth reporting during the abduction of Europa.

Legacy

In the Tower of the Winds in Athens, a Roman-era octagonal clock tower, he appears as a beardless young man emptying a jar of water, a symbol of rain. The Romans identified him with Auster, whose name means south and is the root of Australia, literally "south land". The sirocco, a hot wind blowing from the south, is also associated with this figure.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions him among the winds in the Theogony, and the Odyssey, the Iliad, the Dionysiaca and Lucian's Dialogues of the Sea Gods include episodes in which he appears.

In art

Trivia

The Tower of the Winds. In an octagonal tower in Athens, built in the Roman era, Notus is carved as a young man pouring water from a jar. It is one of the best-known depictions of this god.
A name that reaches Oceania. Auster, the Roman name linked to Notus, means south and is the root of the word Australia, which means "south land".
