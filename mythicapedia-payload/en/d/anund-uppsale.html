legendary Swedish king of the Viking Age

Anund Uppsale

Anund Uppsale is a Swedish king from Norse tradition who ruled alongside his brother Björn. His nickname comes from his time at Old Uppsala, an important pagan religious centre, and his story appears in the Hervarar saga and in Rimbert's chronicle.

The myth

The Hervarar saga says that Anund and Björn succeeded their cousin Erik on the throne and shared the realm: Anund lived at Uppsala and Björn at Haugi, 'the Barrow'. Anund's son Erik succeeded them.

Rimbert, in the Vita Ansgari, written around 870, tells that Anund was driven out of Sweden and took refuge in Denmark. To regain his throne, he promised the Danes the plunder of Birka. With 21 Danish longships and 11 of his own, he appeared before the city and demanded one hundred marks of silver. The Danes, feeling tricked, wanted to attack it; Anund dissuaded them by casting lots, and the warriors ended up plundering a Slavic town. Anund returned the ransom and reconciled with his people.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar saga and Rimbert's Vita Ansgari, with mentions by Adam of Bremen.

Trivia

Lots with runes. To dissuade the Danes from attacking Birka, Anund had them draw lots, possibly using runes, about the city's future.
An ancestor of Härjedalen. A 14th-century Norwegian document tells that Herjulf Horn-breaker, who fled to Sweden, was well received by Anund, and that his descendants settled Härjedalen.
