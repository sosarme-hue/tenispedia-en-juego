Titan of Greek mythology, benefactor and rebel against Zeus

Prometheus

Prometheus is a Titan in Greek mythology, son of Iapetus and Clymene, best known for stealing fire from the gods and giving it to humankind. His figure is ambivalent: a benefactor of humanity who is also the victim of an everlasting punishment, which has made him a symbol of creative defiance and of the risks of challenging established power.

The myth

According to the tradition, Prometheus was clever and cunning, a Titan who did not fight in the war against the Olympians but advised his kin not to take part. In Hesiod's Theogony, he tricked Zeus at Mecone: he wrapped the meat inside the ox's stomach and the bones in glistening fat, and when Zeus chose the fat, he was left with the bones, which gave rise to the custom of burning that part in sacrifices. Angered, Zeus hid fire from humans, but Prometheus stole it back, carrying it in a hollow fennel stalk. Other versions say he took it from the forge of Hephaestus or from the chariot of Helios.

Zeus's revenge came in the form of Pandora, the first woman, shaped by Hephaestus and adorned by Athena. Prometheus had warned his brother Epimetheus never to accept gifts from Zeus, yet Epimetheus received her and married her. Pandora opened a jar that released evils into the world, and only hope remained inside.

Prometheus was punished by being chained to a rock in the Caucasus, where an eagle devoured his liver each day, and the liver regrew overnight. According to Hesiod, the hero Heracles later freed him with Zeus's permission. Aeschylus, in Prometheus Bound, presents him as the "friend of humankind", and in that play Prometheus lists the skills he gave humans: astronomy, number, writing, the taming of animals, navigation, medicine, divination and metalworking. Some traditions also credit him with creating humanity from clay, and it is said that his son Deucalion, advised by him, survived the flood in an ark.

Worship and religion

The worship of Prometheus as a deity was not widespread. He had a notable religious presence mainly in Athens, where he was linked with Athena and Hephaestus, the deities of crafts and technology, and where he held a semi-divine status.

Legacy

The adjective "Promethean" comes from his name and describes acts of creative defiance. The word philanthropy takes its etymological origin from the phrase that defines him as the friend of humankind. In the Romantic era he became a model of the lone genius whose efforts to improve human life may end in tragedy; Mary Shelley gave The Modern Prometheus as the alternate title of Frankenstein. His figure also stands for the pursuit of scientific knowledge and the risk of "playing God".

Ancient sources: His oldest account appears in Hesiod's Theogony, and his tragic figure is developed in Aeschylus's Prometheus Bound.

Trivia

Origin of philanthropy. The word philanthropy comes from philánthrōpon, the epithet with which Aeschylus describes Prometheus as the friend of humankind.
Two rocks at Panopeus. According to tradition, two coloured rocks at Panopeus formed from the leftover clay with which Prometheus created humanity, and they give off a smell similar to human flesh.
