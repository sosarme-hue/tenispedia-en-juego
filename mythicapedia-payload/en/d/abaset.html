Egyptian hedgehog goddess

Abaset

Abaset is an ancient Egyptian goddess with a hedgehog form, venerated in El-Bawiti, capital of the Bahariya Oasis, during the 26th Dynasty. She is a little-documented deity, known mostly from her funerary depictions.

The myth

No narrative about Abaset survives. What we know comes from images: she appears three times on the tomb walls of Qarat Qasr Salim, twice in the Tomb of Bannentiu, in human form, wearing a tripartite wig with a vulture cap and a hedgehog on top, a feature specific to her. The Tomb of Bannentiu also holds an inscription with her name, one of only two known attestations, and she appears at no other site.

Worship and religion

She was venerated in El-Bawiti, capital of the Bahariya Oasis, during the 26th Dynasty. The sources do not describe temples, festivals or offerings. Her presence in tombs suggests a link to the funerary sphere, though this is not documented with certainty.
