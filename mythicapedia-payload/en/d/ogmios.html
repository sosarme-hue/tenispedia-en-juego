Gaulish god of eloquence

Ogmios

Ogmios was, according to Lucian of Samosata, the Gaulish god of eloquence and the spoken word, and tradition credits him with inventing a script of notches. He is linked to the Irish Ogma and the Welsh Gwyddyon, although the exact nature of that connection remains unclear.

The myth

The main account of Ogmios comes from Heracles, a short piece by Lucian, the second-century Syrian writer who wrote in Greek. According to Lucian, he saw in Gaul an image of the god portrayed as an aged, dark-skinned Heracles, with a chain of gold running from his tongue to a group of cheerful followers.

A Celt fluent in Greek explained to him that the Gauls associated Ogmios with eloquence, which reaches its peak in old age. In later Irish medieval tradition, the god Ogma was said to have invented the Ogham alphabet, carved in stone or wood, which bears his name.

Worship and religion

Outside Lucian's text there is very little evidence of his worship. No image has been found that resembles the one he describes, and only two curse tablets from Brigantium, in present-day Austria, invoke his name. Most scholars accept that the god existed, though a minority are sceptical.

Legacy

Lucian was widely read during the Renaissance, and the "Gallic Hercules", as Ogmios was known, inspired works by Albrecht Dürer and Hans Holbein the Younger.

Ancient sources: Lucian of Samosata, in his work Heracles, is the only substantial ancient source that mentions him; two Byzantine lexicons depend on it.

Trivia

A god with chains. In the image Lucian describes, Ogmios's followers are bound by gold chains attached to the god's tongue.
A name with many forms. Depending on the Celtic region, the god was known as Ogmios in Gaul, Occma among the Scottish Celts and Ogmia among the Britons.
