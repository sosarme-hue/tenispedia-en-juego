son of Athamas in Greek myth

Leucon

Leucon is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Athamas and Themisto. He matters for his Boeotian lineage and for the three Leuconids, who looked after the young Dionysus.

The myth

Leucon was the son of Athamas and his third wife, Themisto, daughter of Hypseus. He had two brothers, Erythrios and Ptoos, and he is said to have died of an illness. In some versions he had a son, Erythras, who founded the Boeotian city of Erythrae; Erythras and Erythrios are spelling variants of the same name.

According to the Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, Leucon had three daughters, the Leuconids: Pisidice, Euippe and Hyperippe. After Leucothea, that is Ino once she had become a goddess, disappeared, the infant Dionysus was placed in their care. The girls carried him toward the temple of Athena Skiras, passing through Lilaea and Panopeus, until they reached Orchomenus. By then Dionysus had reached puberty. In Orchomenus, Pisidice married Copreus, a grandson of Orchomenus, and Euippe married Andreus.

Ancient sources: The Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, records his descendants and the episode of the Leuconids and Dionysus.

Trivia

Two names, one lineage. Erythras and Erythrios appear as spelling variants of the same figure, which is common in Greek mythological names transmitted by different authors.
Dionysus's nursemaids. According to this tradition, the three Leuconids cared for the young Dionysus after Ino disappeared and brought him to Orchomenus.
