Thracian king, son of Boreas

Haemus

Haemus was, in Greek myth, a king of Thrace and the son of Boreas, the north wind. His story explains the origin of a great mountain range and the ancient name of a peninsula.

The myth

According to the tale, Haemus was a vain and haughty king who compared himself and his wife, Queen Rhodope, to Zeus and Hera. For this arrogance, the gods turned the couple into mountains: him into Haemus Mons, today known as the Balkan Mountains, and her into the Rhodope Mountains.

Tradition also holds that the Balkan Peninsula was known in ancient Greek as the «Peninsula of Haemus». Another classic etymology links the name to the battle between Zeus and the dragon Typhon.

Legacy

His name survives in the Balkan Mountains and in the old name of the Balkan Peninsula, which still has some currency in modern Greek.

Trivia

A name that endures. The name of Mount Haemus gave way to that of the Balkan Mountains, while the old name still ties to the whole peninsula.
Two etymologies. The name Haemus is explained by the story of his transformation, but also by Zeus's fight against the dragon Typhon.
