fairy queen in Shakespeare

Titania

Titania is the queen of the fairies in William Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream (c. 1595–1596). Her name, borrowed from Ovid, became the usual name for fairy queens in later literature. In traditional folklore the fairy queen had no name of her own.

The myth

In the play, Titania and her husband Oberon are powerful spirits who guarantee the fertility and health of the human and natural worlds. Their quarrel over custody of an Indian changeling boy throws the whole forest into disorder.

Puck casts a spell that makes Titania fall in love with Nick Bottom, a weaver whom Puck has given a donkey's head. Under the spell she loses her pride and strength and becomes fawning. When Oberon tires of the farce, he reverses the spell and the couple reconcile.

In the end, the couple bless the weddings of the play's lovers. Paul A. Olson reads the episode as a reversal of the Circe myth from Greek mythology: here the enchantress is the one who ends up in love with an animal.

Legacy

Goethe included her in Faust, and Carl Maria von Weber revived her in his opera Oberon (1825–1826). One of Uranus's moons, Titania, takes its name from her. She has also appeared in more recent works, such as Disney's cartoon Gargoyles, the 1999 film with Michelle Pfeiffer, and the video game Warframe.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Metamorphoses, where Titania is an appellation given to the daughters of a Titan, is the likely source of the name Shakespeare used.

Trivia

A moon of Uranus. Titania is the largest of Uranus's moons, and all of them are named after characters from Shakespeare or Alexander Pope.
A title, not a name. In Final Fantasy XIV: Shadowbringers Titania appears as an opponent, and in that fiction the name is really a title held by the ruler of the fairies.
