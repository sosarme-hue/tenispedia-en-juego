son of Helios and king of Colchis in Greek mythology

Perses

Perses is a figure of Greek mythology, a son of Helios and the Oceanid nymph Perse, who seized the throne of Colchis from his brother Aeëtes. He appears mainly in late and Roman sources, and his story closes the episode of the Golden Fleece from the side of the royal family.

The myth

According to the tradition, Perses was the brother of Aeëtes, king of Colchis and keeper of the Golden Fleece, and of Circe and, in many accounts, Pasiphaë. Aeëtes had received an oracle warning that great peril would come to him if the fleece ever left his land. When Jason and Medea stole it with the sorceress's help, Perses took the chance to seize the throne.

According to the Bibliotheca, Medea returned to Colchis without revealing who she was and learned that her father had been deposed by his brother. She killed Perses and restored the kingdom to Aeëtes. Another version adds that an oracle had foretold Perses would die at the hands of his own kin, and so it happened through his niece.

Another tale, preserved in Roman sources, tells of Medus, Medea's son, who arrived in Colchis and was imprisoned under a false identity during a famine. Medea, posing as a priestess of Artemis, unknowingly betrayed her son's identity to Perses. She then gave Medus a sword, and he slew the usurper. Valerius Flaccus makes him a brother of Aeëtes only on his mother's side, with an unknown father.

Diodorus Siculus offers a different version: a Perses who ruled in Taurica at the same time Aeëtes ruled Colchis, and who, like him, was notoriously cruel and lawless. Perses fathered a daughter, Hecate, by an unknown mother; she was a priestess and sorceress who surpassed her father in boldness. Aeëtes later married this Hecate and had Medea and Circe by her.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus in his Bibliotheca historica, the Bibliotheca mythologica, the Fabulae of Hyginus and the Argonautica of Valerius Flaccus, all of them late or Roman sources.

Trivia

A mortal Hecate. Diodorus describes a Hecate, daughter of Perses, as a mortal priestess and sorceress, distinct from the Titan Hecate, goddess of witchcraft. She shares the name and some traits with her.
A name with two possible origins. His name may be connected to the Greek verb perthō, meaning to sack or destroy, or to the word for Persian.
