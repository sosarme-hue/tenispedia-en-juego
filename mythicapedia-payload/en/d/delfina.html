dragoness of Delphi in Greek mythology

Delphyne

Delphyne is, in some Greek accounts, the serpent or dragoness that guarded the sanctuary of Delphi and that Apollo killed to establish his oracle there. Her name seems linked to Delphi, and ancient sources portray her inconsistently, sometimes as female and sometimes as a male serpent identified with Python.

The myth

The Homeric Hymn to Apollo, the oldest known account, describes an unnamed she-dragon, a fierce monster who wrought havoc on men and their flocks. According to this story, Apollo, while building his oracular temple, met her near a sweet-flowing spring and struck her down with arrows. She lay writhing and gasping in the wood before she died.

Tradition links her to Typhon. In the Hymn, Hera gave the monstrous Typhon to the dragoness to raise. Apollodorus makes her Typhon's companion and involves her in his battle with Zeus: Typhon hid the god's severed sinews in the Corycian cave in Cilicia and set Delphyne to guard them. Hermes and Aegipan stole them back, and Zeus regained his strength to defeat Typhon later.

Apollonius of Rhodes has Orpheus sing, during the voyage of the Argonauts, of the young Apollo's killing of Delphyne beneath Parnassus. Nonnus says Apollo had to tame her before going to live on Olympus. As in other cases, the authors disagree about her parentage and sex.

Worship and religion

According to the Suda, Delphi was named after Delphyne. An account recorded by the chronicler John of Antioch says the Pythian Games were held in honour of the serpent, though others attributed the cult to a heroine named Delphyne. The sources do not describe a specific cult devoted to her, and her link to the oracle is understood mainly through Apollo.

Legacy

The Greek word delphys means 'womb', and it has been proposed that the name of the dragoness was formed from Delphi rather than the other way round.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Apollo (6th century BC), Apollonius of Rhodes, Apollodorus, Nonnus, Dionysius Periegetes, Plutarch and the Suda.

Trivia

A name with a double origin. According to the Suda, Delphi was named after Delphyne, although her own name seems to have been formed from Delphi.
Female or male, depending on the author. While the Homeric Hymn and Plutarch describe her as a she-dragon, Apollonius of Rhodes presents a male dragon, which he identifies with Python.
