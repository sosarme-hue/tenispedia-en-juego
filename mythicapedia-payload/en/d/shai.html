Egyptian god of fate

Shai

Shai was the deification of fate in Egyptian mythology. He was believed to determine how long each person would live, and his power was invoked even by pharaohs such as Akhenaten and Ramesses II. His figure shows how the Egyptians saw destiny as a force present from birth to the final judgement.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Shai was born together with each person and stayed at their side throughout life. His name means "that which is ordained", reflecting his role in setting the length of each life. At the end, the person faced judgement before Osiris in the underworld Duat.

As a concept, Shai had no fixed gender. He was usually considered male, but sometimes female, and then called Shait, simply the feminine form of the name. He was occasionally described as the husband of Meskhenet, goddess of birth, or, in later times, of Renenutet, who assigned the Ren and had come to be seen as a goddess of fortune.

In Ptolemaic Egypt, Shai was identified with the Greek god Agathodaemon, a deity linked to fortune-telling. Since Agathodaemon was imagined as a serpent, and the Egyptian word shai also meant "pig", Shai could appear in the Hellenic period as a pig with a serpent's head.

Legacy

Egyptologists call this hybrid figure the "Shai animal", a term still used in modern scholarship. Akhenaten described Shai as an attribute of Aten, while Ramesses II styled himself "lord of Shai", that is, "lord of fate".

Trivia
