Greek mythological princess, daughter of Boreas

Cleopatra

Cleopatra is a princess of Greek mythology, daughter of Boreas, the north wind, and of Orithyia, a daughter of the Athenian king Erechtheus. Her story, recorded mainly by Apollodorus and Diodorus Siculus, centres on her marriage to King Phineus, the fate of her sons, and her link to the voyage of the Argonauts.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cleopatra, whose name means roughly «glory of her father», was the daughter of Boreas and Orithyia. Her siblings included the Boreads, Calais and Zetes, and Chione. She married Phineus, king of Thrace, and had two sons with him, though their names vary by author: Apollodorus calls them Plexippus and Pandion, while other traditions name Gerymbas and Aspondus, Polydector and Polydorus, or Parthenius and Crambis.

Phineus later married Idaea, a daughter of Dardanus, who falsely accused her stepsons of trying to seduce her. Phineus believed her and punished the young men cruelly. Apollodorus says he blinded them; Diodorus describes them being kept in a pit and whipped without rest.

When the Argonauts reached Thrace, the boys took them for gods and begged for help. The Boreads, their uncles, were the first to rush to their aid, cutting their bonds and killing their enemies. According to Diodorus, Heracles also freed Cleopatra, who had been held captive in a prison. Apollodorus instead credits the Argonauts with punishing Phineus.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Library, and Diodorus Siculus, in his Historical Library, recount her marriage to Phineus and the arrival of the Argonauts.

Trivia

A shared name. Greek mythology has several women named Cleopatra, including a wife of Meleager who is also called Alcyone, which makes them easy to confuse.
Sons with different names. Ancient sources do not agree on the names of Cleopatra's sons with Phineus, and offer up to four different pairs of names.
