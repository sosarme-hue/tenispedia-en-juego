Mexica water god, patron of fishermen and archers

Atlahua

Atlahua is a Mexica water god whose name means «lord of the waters» in Nahuatl, and the patron of fishermen and archers. Little information about him survives, so much of what is known comes from a hymn recorded by Sahagún.

The myth

The sources say little about his story, so his traits are reconstructed mainly from his links with other deities. According to Ángel María Garibay's interpretation, Atlahua was the blue Tlaloc, the one of the south, and was related to Amimitl, another form of Tlaloc tied to a different direction of the universe. His name is connected to ahtlatl or atlatl, the spear-thrower, which would explain his association with arrows and fishing.

The hymn known as Atlahua Icuic, preserved in the appendix to Book II of the General History of the Things of New Spain, works as a dialogue between the god and his devotees. It includes the line «Opochtli is my god, the lord of the waters», which suggests the voice of a young Mexica singing in his honour.

Worship and religion

He was said to have had at least four temples, the tallest supposedly over 200 feet (61 metres). The Mexica prayed to him when people died in water. One account says that during the conquest of Tenochtitlan the lake was covered with heads and corpses, and he was invoked. The sources do not say whether his cult survives today.

Legacy

His image was reproduced in the Rig Veda Americanus (1890), a book of American Indian literature, based on Sahagún's work.

Ancient sources: The Florentine Codex by Bernardino de Sahagún, in Book II, where the hymn Atlahua Icuic is recorded.

Trivia

Link to the atlatl. His name seems to derive from the possessive form of ahtlatl, the spear-thrower, which is why he is linked to arrows.
Many spellings. He appears under several spellings, such as Atlahoa or Atlavâ, because early written Nahuatl was orthographically unstable.
