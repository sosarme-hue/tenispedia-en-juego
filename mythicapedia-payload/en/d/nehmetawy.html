ancient Egyptian goddess of protection and return

Nehmetawy

Nehmetawy is a little-known goddess of ancient Egypt, whose name means she who embraces those in need. Depending on the place of worship she was the wife of Nehebkau or of Thoth, and she is usually shown wearing a sistrum-shaped headdress with a child in her lap.

The myth

According to the Tebtunis Mythological Manual, the goddess Unut speared Geb, the earth god, for uniting with Nehmetawy. Nehmetawy belongs to the tale of a goddess who flees and must be persuaded by a male god to return to Egypt. After taking refuge in Naunet, the primordial waters, she was escorted back to the temple of Khemenu by Nephthys and Thoth.

Worship and religion

The goddess's return formed part of a festival dedicated to Thoth. In the Temple of the Ogdoad at Khemenu, eight sistra were depicted, linking her to the eight primordial deities. Two Demotic ostraca from the Ptolemaic period describe a festival at Herakleopolis of music, drunkenness and sexual activity in honour of Nehmetawy and Ay.

Ancient sources: The Tebtunis Mythological Manual and two Demotic Ptolemaic ostraca from Herakleopolis.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Her Egyptian name can be translated as she who embraces those in need, a trait that sums up her role as a welcoming goddess.
A festival like the one at Bubastis. The Herakleopolis ostraca allude to the great festival of Bastet at Bubastis, which shared its festive character.
