Great Earl of Hell in goetic demonology

Furfur

Furfur is a Great Earl of Hell in goetic demonology, depicted as a winged deer with a flaming tail. He belongs to European demonological lore and is associated with love, storms and hidden secrets.

The myth

In demonological texts, Furfur is a powerful ruler of Hell with twenty-nine legions of demons, though some versions give twenty-six. He is shown as a deer or a winged deer with human arms, and sometimes as an angel.

According to these texts, he can cause love between a man and a woman and unleash storms, thunder, lightning and gusts of wind. He also teaches secrets about the hidden and the divine. He is deceitful: he lies, unless he is forced into a magic triangle, where he answers with certainty in a harsh voice.

Legacy

His name has inspired interpretations: the Latin furfur means 'bran', and some link it to furcifer, 'scoundrel'. He appears in popular culture in series such as Welcome to Demon School! Iruma-kun, Blue Exorcist and Good Omens, and in the video game Voices of the Void. At the University of Missouri-Columbia he is blamed for the 1892 campus fire.

Trivia

A name with two readings. The name is linked to the Latin furfur, 'bran', although some connect it to furcifer, an insult meaning 'scoundrel'.
A seal that sounds like cloth. One possible reading of his seal is 'Frufrú', an onomatopoeia for the rustle of certain fabrics.
A university scapegoat. Students at the University of Missouri-Columbia blame him for mischief, small fires and bad luck on campus.
