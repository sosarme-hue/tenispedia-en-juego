Greek hero, ancestor of the Achaeans

Achaeus

Achaeus is, in Greek tradition, a hero regarded as the eponymous ancestor of the Achaeans. Son of Xuthus and Creusa, his name gave rise to the name of his people and of the region of Achaia.

The myth

According to tradition, Achaeus was a son of Xuthus and Creusa, brother of Ion and grandson of Hellen. The Achaeans saw him as the author of their race and took their own name from him, as well as that of Achaia, formerly called Aegialus, in the northern Peloponnese.

After his uncle Aeolus died in Thessaly, Achaeus went there and became master of Phthiotis, which also took the name Achaia. His only known children, Archander and Architeles, married two of the Danaids. Both brothers later set out to fight against Laomedon, king of Troy.

Hellenistic and Roman versions offer other accounts. Marius Servius Honoratus calls him a son of Jupiter and Phthia, daughter of Phoroneus, which places him in the Inachid line rather than the line of Hellen, as the Greek sources have it. Dionysius of Halicarnassus, by contrast, made him a son of Poseidon and once again the eponym of Achaia.

Legacy

His name survives in that of the Achaeans and in Achaia, the region of the northern Peloponnese that was once called Aegialus. Tradition also links his name to a district of Phthiotis that bore the same name, Achaia.

Ancient sources: Pausanias names him as the father of Archander and Architeles; Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Marius Servius Honoratus give alternative genealogies.

Trivia

Two Achaias. There were two regions called Achaia, one in the northern Peloponnese and another in Phthiotis, in Thessaly, both linked to the name of Achaeus.
Conflicting genealogies. While Greek sources place him in the line of Hellen, Servius includes him in the Inachid line, showing how lineages varied from author to author.
