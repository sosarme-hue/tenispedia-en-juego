Greek goddess of universal remedy

Panacea

Panacea is the Greek goddess of universal remedy, credited with healing through herbs and potions. She belongs to the family circle of Asclepius, god of medicine, and her name gave English the word 'panacea'.

The myth

According to tradition, Panacea was the daughter of Asclepius and Epione, although the sources do not fully agree: one text makes her the daughter of Asclepius and Lampetia, daughter of Helios. The Suda names five daughters of Asclepius and Epione: Hygieia, Aegle, Iaso, Aceso and Panacea. Each embodied a facet of the healing art.

Her brothers include Podaleirius and Machaon, kings of Tricca who took part in the Trojan War; in some versions Machaon was killed by Penthesilea, queen of the Amazons. Telesphoros devoted his life to serving Asclepius, and some sources name Aratus, Panacea's half-brother and a hero of Sicyon. The accounts are not always consistent: at times Panacea and her sisters appear as Asclepius' wife. She may have been an independent goddess before being absorbed into his myth.

She was traditionally said to have healed the sick with a poultice or potion.

Worship and religion

At Oropus, between Attica and the land of Tanagra, a sanctuary of Amphiaraus was famed for its cures. Part of its altar was dedicated to Aphrodite, Panacea, Iaso, Hygieia and Athena Paeonia, 'the healer'. Her name also appears in the Hippocratic Oath, alongside Hygieia, among the deities physicians invoke. No living cult is documented.

Legacy

The word 'panacea' now denotes, in medicine, a substance supposed to cure all diseases, and figuratively a solution that resolves a complex problem completely. A river in Thrace or Moesia took its name from the goddess, and the Zlatna Panega river in modern Bulgaria preserves that Greek root.

Ancient sources: The Suda, the Hippocratic Oath, Aristophanes and texts describing the sanctuary at Oropus.

Trivia

A river named after her. A river in Thrace or Moesia took its name from the goddess, and the Zlatna Panega in modern Bulgaria still carries that Greek root.
A root for the centaur. Chiron is said to have first found a medicinal root on a snowy hill of Mount Pelion and named it centaury after his own kind.
