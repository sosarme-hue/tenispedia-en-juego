Oceanid of Greek mythology

Ocyrhoe

Ocyrhoe is an Oceanid of Greek mythology, daughter of Oceanus and Tethys. She is best remembered through the story of her son Phasis, who gave his name to a river.

The myth

According to tradition, Ocyrhoe was one of the playmates of Persephone. Latin poets, however, portray her as a lover of Helios, the god of the Sun. From that union Phasis was born.

The myth tells that Phasis killed his mother after catching her in adultery. Tormented by the Erinyes, who appeared to haunt him, he threw himself into the river Arcturus. That river was then renamed Phasis after him. The ancient account places the waterway in Scythia, beside a city of the same name, and explains that the cold region it flowed through had once been called Arcturus.

Ancient sources: Latin poets make her a lover of Helios; the story of Phasis and the renaming of the river comes from an ancient source that the record does not identify.

Trivia

A name meaning swift current. Her Greek name is usually rendered as "swift current", a fitting label for an Oceanid, daughter of the great river that encircles the earth.
