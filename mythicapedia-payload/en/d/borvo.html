Gaulish god of thermal and healing springs

Borvo

Borvo was an ancient Celtic god of thermal and healing springs, worshipped across Roman Gaul and reaching the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula. He matters because his cult left a lasting mark on the place names of European spa towns.

The myth

Ancient texts preserve almost no stories about Borvo, so most of what we know comes from his role and his images. His name, from a Celtic root meaning 'to boil', marks him as the spirit of the hot water that wells up from the earth, a 'boiling' or 'warm' spring.

His usual partner was the goddess Damona, whose name is linked to the Celtic word for cattle and who, like him, presided over curative waters. Where the god bears the form Bormanus, as at Die and Aix-en-Diois, his consort is Bormana.

Worship and religion

His worship is known almost entirely from Latin votive dedications cut on altars and set up at Gallo-Roman spa sites. The main centres lay in central and eastern Gaul, above all Bourbonne-les-Bains, in the territory of the Lingones, and Bourbon-Lancy. Two reliefs show him: one from Entrains with a goblet, a money bag and a plate of fruit, symbols of plenty; another from Vichy, seated naked on a rock holding a cup over which liquid bubbles.

Legacy

His legacy lives on mainly in geography. Many place names of spa towns in France and Germany derive from his name, such as Bourbon, Bourbonne, La Bourboule and Worms, as do the names of several small French rivers. The name Bormanicus also appears on two altars from Caldas de Vizela, in the northwest of the Iberian Peninsula.

Ancient sources: Latin votive inscriptions from the Gallo-Roman period, mostly from Bourbonne-les-Bains and Bourbon-Lancy, plus two altars from Caldas de Vizela.

Trivia

Aquae Bormonis. The Roman place name Aquae Bormonis, recorded on the Tabula Peutingeriana, is formed from the god's name. It is disputed whether it refers to Bourbon-Lancy or Bourbon-l'Archambault.
A god of the Boii. Borvo was the tutelary god of the Boii, and the many place names carrying his name show how widely his cult spread through France and Germany.
