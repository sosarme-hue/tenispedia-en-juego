Sun Goddess of Arinna, chief goddess of Hittite mythology

Arinnitti

Arinnitti, the Sun Goddess of Arinna, was the chief goddess of Hittite mythology, alongside her husband, the weather god Tarhunna. She protected the kingdom, and her main cult centred on the sacred city of Arinna, one of the empire's three holy cities.

The myth

According to Hittite tradition, the goddess was of Hattian origin, where she was worshipped as Eštan. The Hittites also knew her as Ištanu or Urunzimu. Together with Tarhunna she formed the pair that held the highest place in the pantheon. Their daughter was Mezulla, whose daughter was Zintuhi, and their other children included the Weather god of Nerik, the Weather god of Zippalanda and Telipinu, the god of grain. Her messenger was the eagle.

In the myths she plays a minor role. A Hattian fragment describes the building of her house in Lihzina, and another refers to her apple tree. In some legends she forms a triad with her son Sarruma, shown in an open-air sanctuary north of Hattusa.

Ancient texts also equate her with the Egyptian Ra in the treaty between Hattusili III and Ramesses II, and in the New Kingdom she was linked with the Hurro-Syrian goddess Hebat.

Worship and religion

Her most important temple stood in Arinna, and another was on the citadel of Hattusa. She was shown as a solar disc and also as a seated woman with a halo. Several queens donated solar discs in the city of Tarhurpa, and King Ulmi-Teššup of Tarhuntassa offered her a disc of gold, silver and copper each year, together with a bull and three sheep. The deer was sacred to her. The king worshipped her with daily prayers at sunset.

Ancient sources: Hittite texts: the prayers of Arnuwanda I and Queen Puduhepa, and the Egyptian-Hittite treaty between Hattusili III and Ramesses II.

Trivia

Annual offering. King Ulmi-Teššup of Tarhuntassa donated a solar disc of gold, silver and copper each year, together with a bull and three sheep.
Mother of the land. One of her Hattian epithets, Wurunšemu, may mean Mother of the land.
