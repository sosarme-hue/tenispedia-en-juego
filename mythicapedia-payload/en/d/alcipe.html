Athenian princess, daughter of Ares

Alcippe

Alcippe is a princess of Athens in Greek myth, daughter of Ares and the mortal Aglauros, of the Cecropid line. Her story is tied to the first murder trial in Greek tradition.

The myth

According to the myth, Halirrhothius, son of Poseidon, attacked her on the beach of Athens. Ares came upon the scene and killed him. Poseidon demanded justice, and a court of gods gathered on a hill of Athens acquitted Ares, after Alcippe's testimony confirmed her father's account.

According to Panyasis, however, Ares was sentenced to serve mortals, probably as a penalty for the killing. Some accounts add that Dedalus, the famed architect and first sculptor, was born to Alcippe and Eupalamus, son of Metion.

Legacy

The hill where the trial took place took its name, Areopagus or 'hill of Ares', in memory of the god. It lies next to the Acropolis of Athens.

Ancient sources: The Library of Greek mythology, Pausanias and Panyasis, who record Alcippe's story and Ares' trial.

Trivia

A name that endures. The hill where the trial was held still bears the name Areopagus, derived from the god Ares.
Daedalus, according to some accounts. Some versions make Alcippe the mother of Dedalus, the architect and first sculptor.
