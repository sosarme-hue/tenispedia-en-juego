Thespian princess in Greek mythology

Procris

Procris is a Thespian princess in Greek mythology, the eldest of the fifty daughters of King Thespius. Her story is linked to the legend of Heracles and the Cithaeronian lion and explains the origin of two of her sons.

The myth

According to the tradition, King Thespius was plagued by the lion of Mount Cithaeron and asked Heracles to kill it. The hero spent fifty days hunting the beast and, in the meantime, was the king's guest, who entertained him lavishly and made him drunk.

The king had a purpose: he wanted all his fifty daughters to have children by Heracles. During those nights, the hero unknowingly slept with each of them, since he believed his bed-fellow was always the same. Procris, the eldest, was one of them, and from this union twin sons, Antileon and Hippeus, were born.

The accounts differ. In one version, Heracles spent a week with Procris and her sisters, lying with seven each night. In another, he bedded all of them in a single night except one, who refused him. Feeling insulted, the hero condemned her to remain a virgin all her life and to serve him as his priestess.

Trivia

Virgin priestess. In some accounts, the daughter who rejected Heracles was condemned to lifelong virginity and to serve the hero as his priestess.
Fifty daughters. King Thespius had fifty daughters and wished for all of them to have offspring by the hero.
