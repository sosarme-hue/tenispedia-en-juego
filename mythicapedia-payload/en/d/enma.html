king of hell and judge of the dead in Japanese Buddhism

Enma

Enma is the Japanese name of Yama, the ruler of the underworld who judges the dead and presides over the cycle of rebirth. His figure derives from the Hindu Yama, but in China, Korea and Japan he acquired functions of his own.

The myth

In the Pali canon, the Buddha explains that whoever has mistreated parents, ascetics or elders is brought before Yama at death. Yama asks whether the person ever reflected on old age, sickness and death; since the offender never did, he is sent to a brutal hell until the consequences of his deeds are exhausted.

Buddhaghosa describes him as a being of mixed condition, sometimes enjoying heavenly pleasures and at other times suffering for his karma, though he considers his rule just. In China, Yanluo Wang began as a heavenly post that sometimes went to an honest mortal. From the Tang dynasty, under Taoist influence, he became the fifth of ten infernal kings. In Japan he is called Enma, and in Korea, Yeom-ra.

Worship and religion

In Vietnam, the ten kings of hell are venerated together as a council that governs the realm of the dead. In Tibetan Buddhism he is feared as the driving force of the cycle of death and rebirth, yet also honored as a guardian of spiritual practice.

Legacy

In Japan his name has entered anime and manga: Bleach's Commander Yamamoto takes his name from Yama, Enma is the father of Koenma in Yu Yu Hakusho, and in Super Paper Mario Queen Hadesia recalls King Enma with more Western traits.

Ancient sources: The Buddhist Pali canon, with Buddhaghosa's commentaries on the Majjhima Nikaya, and the Hindu Vedas, where Yama originates.

Trivia

A name in Bleach. Bleach's commander general, Genryusai Shigekuni Yamamoto, takes his name from Yama and plays a similar role.
A human judge. Chinese traditions hold that honest people, such as Bao Zheng, took the post of Yanluo Wang after death or at night.
