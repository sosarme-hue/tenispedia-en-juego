Aztec feathered serpent god

Quetzalcoatl

Quetzalcoatl, the Feathered Serpent, is one of the most important deities of Mesoamerica and especially of the Aztec pantheon. He is associated with life, light, fertility, knowledge and the winds, and his figure is also linked to the planet Venus.

The myth

According to Nahua cosmogony, Quetzalcoatl is one of four sons of the primordial couple Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl, and embodies the masculine and feminine essences of creation. Son of Mixcoatl and Chimalma, he is brother to Tezcatlipoca, Huitzilopochtli and Xolotl. Accounts differ on his role: for some he was a creator of humanity, while for others he was a solar god who accompanies the Sun from dawn to dusk alongside his brothers.

Over time the myth grew richer. Toltec tradition turned him into Ce Acatl Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl, a legendary ruler of Tollan, a city many scholars identify with Tula. Some stories present him as a rival of Tezcatlipoca, although the codices show that both share the same attributes and were regarded as the Supreme Being. Tezcatlipoca, the smoking black mirror, represented the destructive side of this divine duality.

Quetzalcoatl was also linked to Venus: as the morning star he was the precious twin, while Xolotl was the evening star. The Mexica expected his return to bring rain, fertility and harvests, a prophecy that would be fulfilled at his second, bodily coming.

Worship and religion

The supreme priests of the Toltec religion bore the title Quetzalcoatl. Cholula was the main centre of his worship, where the largest pyramid in the world was dedicated to him. The earliest documented veneration of a feathered serpent appears at Teotihuacan, and the cult spread across Mesoamerica, reaching the Maya under names such as Kukulkan or Gucumatz. Some religions of neo-Toltec origin speak in their traditions of his rebirth, so his veneration survives in part today.

Legacy

Because of his bearded image, some peoples identified the Spanish conqueror Hernan Cortes with Quetzalcoatl, an idea that grew stronger during the colonial period. His name, built from quetzal and serpent, is one of the best-known symbols of ancient Mexico.

Ancient sources: The Huehuetlahtolli, or ancient words, passed down orally and recorded by Spanish chroniclers, and the testimony of Motolinia's informants in his Teogonia e Historia de los Mexicanos.

In art

Trivia

Wind jewel. Quetzalcoatl wears a spirally voluted breastplate called the ehecacozcatl, a cut conch shell linked to whirlwinds and other natural forces. Similar shells have been found in burials across Mesoamerica.
Two stars, two brothers. As the morning star, Venus was Quetzalcoatl, and as the evening star it was Xolotl. The two brothers thus shared the same planet, seen at two different times of day.
