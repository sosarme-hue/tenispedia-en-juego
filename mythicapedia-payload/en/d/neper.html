Egyptian god of grain

Neper

Neper is an Egyptian god of grain, associated with the barley and emmer wheat that sustained the harvests of the Nile valley. Very ancient, he was later absorbed into the cult of Osiris.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Neper was the son of the goddess Renenutet and was married to Tait. Though not a major deity, he was shown as a human figure whose body was made of grains of wheat, or holding two ears of wheat in his hand or on his head. He often appeared as a child nursed by Renenutet, a goddess linked to nourishment and to people’s true names.

His name is usually rendered as “lord of the mouth,” alluding to grain as sustenance. His female counterpart was Nepit, also a goddess of grain. Because he depended on the silt brought by the Nile flood, he was subordinate to Hapy, who was proclaimed “Lord of Neper.”

Over time, once the myth of Osiris and Isis took shape, Neper came to be seen as an aspect of Osiris, god of agriculture and the dead. His story was tied to the yearly harvest and to the seasonal disappearance of life from the fields, and he gained the title of one who lives after dying.

Worship and religion

He was revered from predynastic times. A text describes Pharaoh Amenemhat I of the Twelfth Dynasty as responsible for ripening the grain, and calls him “beloved of Neper.”

Ancient sources: The Coffin Texts recognize him as a god who lives after having died.

Trivia

A name meaning “mouth”. His name is interpreted as “lord of the mouth,” an allusion to grain as the sustenance of the people.
A pharaoh called his beloved. Pharaoh Amenemhat I appears in a text as “beloved of Neper,” showing that his cult reached the royal court.
