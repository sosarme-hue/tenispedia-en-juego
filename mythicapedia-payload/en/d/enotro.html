Oenotrus

Oenotrus is a hero of Greek myth, son of Lycaon and eponym of the Oenotrians, who lived in southern Italy. His story explains the origin of the name Oenotria.

The myth

According to the tale, Oenotrus was the youngest of Lycaon's sons, king of Arcadia. Dissatisfied with the plot of land he received in his father's division of the realm, he prepared a fleet and crossed the Ionian Gulf with his brother Peucetius. Peucetius landed on the promontory of Iapygia and gave his name to the Peucetians, while Oenotrus sailed further west, to a coast then called Ausonia. When the Tyrrhenians came to rule the sea, the region took the name Oenotria. Pausanias presents this as the first Greek expedition to found a colony beyond Greece, long before the Trojan War. Dionysius of Halicarnassus places him within a longer genealogy, seventeen generations before Troy.

Legacy

The name of the Oenotrians and of the region of Oenotria, applied to southern Italy, keeps his memory alive.

Ancient sources: Pausanias (Description of Greece) and Dionysius of Halicarnassus (Roman Antiquities) mention him.

Trivia

A headland with a name of its own. According to the tale, Peucetius landed on the promontory of Iapygia, and the people of the area were called Peucetians after him.
Ausonia and Oenotria. The coast Oenotrus reached was called Ausonia after its inhabitants, the Ausonians, until the Tyrrhenians came to rule the sea and it took the name Oenotria.
