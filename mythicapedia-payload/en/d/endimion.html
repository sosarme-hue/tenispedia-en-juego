Aeolian shepherd or king loved by Selene, cursed with eternal sleep

Endymion

Endymion is a figure of Greek myth linked to Elis in the Peloponnese and to Mount Latmus in Caria. He is best known as the lover of Selene, goddess of the Moon, and for the endless sleep that Zeus granted him.

The myth

According to tradition, Endymion was the son of Aethlius and Calyce, although other authors name Zeus as his father. Hesiod, the earliest source, says Endymion led a group of Aeolians from Thessaly and founded Elis, where he became king after displacing Clymenus.

The best-known story is his love affair with Selene. The goddess was captivated by his beauty as he slept; in some versions she asked Zeus to grant him eternal youth, while in others Zeus punished him for daring to pursue Hera. Either way, Zeus offered him a gift and Endymion chose to sleep forever, young and immortal. Selene visited him each night and bore him fifty daughters, the Menai, whom some scholars link to the months of the Olympiad.

Other versions differ. A writer from Chios tells of Hypnos, the god of sleep, who loved Endymion and watched over his slumber with eyes wide open. Endymion's sons included Epeius, who won a race for the throne, and Paeon, who was exiled. As for his death, the Eleans showed his tomb at Olympia, while the people of Heracleia placed it on Mount Latmus.

Worship and religion

At Heracleia in Caria there was a shrine to Endymion on Mount Latmus, and at Olympia his tomb was shown in the stadium. He is regarded as one of the precursors of the Olympic Games, through the race his sons ran.

Legacy

His story inspired John Keats's poem Endymion, and a lunar crater bears his name. The Endymion character in Sailor Moon is also inspired by him. The myth produced many Roman sarcophagi of Late Antiquity, and the Latin saying meaning to sleep the sleep of Endymion described a very deep sleep.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Apollonius of Rhodes, Pausanias, Pliny the Elder and Licymnius of Chios are among the authors who mention him, each with different variants.

Trivia

An Olympic precursor. According to Pausanias, Endymion settled his succession with a race between his sons at Olympia. Epeius won, and his subjects were then called Epeans.
A proverbial sleep. The Latins spoke of sleeping the sleep of Endymion to mean a very deep sleep, and the myth was popular on Roman sarcophagi.
