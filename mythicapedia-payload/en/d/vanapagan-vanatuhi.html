devil and ogre of Estonian folklore

Vanapagan (Vanatühi)

Vanapagan is a devil and ogre in Estonian folklore, sometimes regarded as a lord of the underworld. He stars in internationally known tales of the stupid ogre and is usually outwitted by his servant, being more foolish than malevolent.

The myth

According to Estonian tradition, Vanapagan, whose name means Old Devil, also called Vanatühi (Old Empty One), is a giant farmer more stupid than wicked. He appears in Estonian versions of the stupid ogre tales found across much of Europe and classified in the Aarne-Thompson index. In these stories his servant Kaval Ants, Crafty Hans, usually gets the better of him.

In one of these tales Vanapagan steals the musical instruments of Pikne, the god of lightning, a plot that has parallels in other traditions. He is also the worst enemy of Suur Tõll, a giant known in the folklore of the island of Saaremaa. According to the story, Tõll is beheaded in a battle against Vanatühi's forces, and Vanatühi sometimes wears a hat of fingernails that makes him invisible.

Trivia

Fingernail hat. Vanatühi sometimes wears a hat made of fingernails that makes him invisible.
A servant cleverer than his master. His servant Kaval Ants, Crafty Hans, is usually the one who outwits him in the tales.
