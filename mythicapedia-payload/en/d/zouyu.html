righteous creature of ancient China

Zouyu

The zouyu is a legendary creature of old Chinese literature, a righteous beast that appears only under a benevolent and sincere ruler.

The myth

Texts describe it as fierce-looking like a tiger, yet gentle and strictly vegetarian. The Shuowen Jiezi pictures it as a white tiger with black spots. Its earliest mention is in the Book of Songs, though J. J. L. Duyvendak considered that interpretation very doubtful.

In 1404, under the Yongle Emperor, Prince Zhu Su sent him from Kaifeng a captured animal taken to be a rare white tiger. Chroniclers hailed it as a good omen, alongside a clear Yellow River and a giraffe brought from Bengal on Zheng He's fleet.

Legacy

Some have proposed the zouyu was a giant panda, an idea Duyvendak raised with doubt, or a snow leopard, according to Riordan and Shi. In Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald (2018) it appears as an elephant-sized cat with a ruffled tail, like Chinese guardian lions.

Ancient sources: It is mentioned in the Book of Songs (Shijing), and the Shuowen Jiezi describes it as a white tiger with black spots.

Trivia

A white tiger at court. In 1404 a captured animal was sent to Emperor Yongle and taken for a rare white tiger, a sign of good rule.
A giraffe as an omen. The giraffe brought from Bengal on Zheng He's fleet was also counted among the good omens of the era.
