Athenian prince in Greek mythology

Erysichthon of Attica

Erysichthon was an Athenian prince in Greek mythology, son of King Cecrops I of Athens. He is remembered mainly for his link with Delos, the cult of Eileithyia, goddess of childbirth, and the place where his tomb was said to lie at Prasiae.

The myth

According to tradition, Erysichthon was the son of Cecrops and Agraulus, daughter of Actaeus, and he had three sisters: Aglaurus, Herse and Pandrosus. The accounts agree that he died childless, though they differ on some details of his family.

Pausanias notes that Erysichthon never ruled, since he died during his father's lifetime. After Cecrops died, the throne passed to Cranaus, another native king, during whose reign the flood of Deucalion's time was said to have occurred.

The best-known episode is set on Delos. Erysichthon was returning from the island after a sacred embassy, carrying a statue of Eileithyia, when he died during the voyage. According to the story, he was buried at Prasiae on the east coast of Attica, and his tomb could still be seen in Pausanias's time.

Worship and religion

Athens preserved several ancient wooden images of Eileithyia, which the Athenians covered down to the feet. According to the tradition, the oldest of them was the one Erysichthon had brought from Delos. The Cretans, however, claimed a different origin for the goddess, which shows that her cult had different versions from city to city.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records his tomb at Prasiae and the image of Eileithyia he brought from Delos.

Trivia

A wooden image. The ancient images of Eileithyia in Athens were made of wood, and some were covered down to the feet.
A tomb by the sea. His tomb was placed at Prasiae, on the east coast of Attica, where his ship had come into port.
