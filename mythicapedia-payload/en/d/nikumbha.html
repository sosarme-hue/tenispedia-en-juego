rakshasa and danava demon in Hindu mythology

Nikumbha

Nikumbha is the name of two beings in Hindu mythology: a rakshasa, or demon, and a danava, also demonic in nature. Both appear as enemies in stories linked to the tales of Hanuman and Krishna.

The myth

According to tradition, the rakshasa Nikumbha is the son of Kumbhakarna and Vajramala, and the god Kubera entrusted him with watching over the pisacas, evil spirits. The Nilamata Purana calls him "the noble and strong lord of the pisacas."

When his brother Kumbha falls in battle, Nikumbha charges in furious, carrying an iron club. Hanuman strikes his chest with a fist; Nikumbha is unmoved and lifts Hanuman off the ground, but Hanuman frees himself, throws him down and, after pounding his chest, tears off his head. Nikumbha dies at Hanuman's hands.

The danava Nikumbha appears in a story about Krishna. While the Yadavas, led by Krishna and his brother Balarama, hold revelries during a pilgrimage to a festival, Nikumbha abducts a maiden from their company, Bhanumati. Krishna's son Pradyumna rescues her, and Krishna himself slays the danava.
