Penelope (mother of Pan)

Penelope is a figure of Greek mythology, best known as the wife of Odysseus, whom some traditions also present as the mother of the god Pan. Her link to Pan shows how one name could be tied to very different stories depending on the author.

The myth

According to various Greek authors, Pan was the son of Hermes and a Penelope. This Penelope appears to be the same person as Odysseus' wife in the Odyssey, although it has been suggested that she was originally a nymph, a separate figure from the hero's wife.

The accounts of his mother do not agree. Pindar and Hecataeus of Miletus state that Penelope conceived Pan by Apollo, while the historian Duris of Samos claims that his birth resulted from her sleeping with all of her suitors. These versions contrast with one another and reflect the variety of stories told about the shepherd god.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Hecataeus of Miletus and the historian Duris of Samos, who record different versions of his mother.
