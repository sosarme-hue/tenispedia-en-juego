Mesopotamian god, consort of Tiamat

Kingu

Kingu (also spelled Qingu) is a Mesopotamian god, the second consort of the primordial goddess Tiamat and an adversary of Marduk in the Babylonian epic Enuma Elish. His defeat and the creation of humanity from his blood make him a key figure in that creation story.

The myth

In the Enuma Elish, Tiamat sought to avenge the death of her husband Apsu and gathered an army of monsters. To lead it she chose Kingu, to whom she gave the tablet of destinies, a symbol of supreme power, and raised him to a rank close to that of Anu. The text never explains how Tiamat obtained the tablet, nor where Kingu came from; he is defined only as her spouse.

When Marduk defeats Tiamat, he takes back the tablet and presents it to Anu. The assembly of the gods then concludes that Kingu had instigated the rebellion, and he is put to death. Ea uses his blood to create human beings, whose purpose is to serve the gods, who depend on sacrifice for their sustenance.

His role is secondary to those of Tiamat and Marduk, and he appears mostly through his defeat. Some scholars believe he may originally have been the antagonist of an independent myth, now lost, which the compilers of the poem folded into the story of Tiamat.

Ancient sources: The Babylonian epic Enuma Elish, which presents him as Tiamat's consort and as Marduk's defeated enemy.

Trivia

A Sumerian name. His name is usually linked to Sumerian terms such as kíĝ, meaning "work", and kingal, meaning "leader", which fits his role as a commander.
A name given to Marduk. In the Enuma Elish, Marduk receives fifty names after his victory, and among them is Irqingu, a variant of Kingu's name.
