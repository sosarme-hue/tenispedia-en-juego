the Otherworld of Welsh mythology

Annwn

Annwn (also Annwfn) is the Otherworld in Welsh mythology, a realm of delight and eternal youth where disease is unknown and food is always plentiful. Its name and location, either underground or on an island, connect it to wider Celtic beliefs about the underworld.

The myth

According to the tales, Annwn was ruled by Arawn. In the First Branch of the Mabinogi, Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, offends Arawn by setting his hounds on a stag that Arawn's dogs had already brought down. To make amends, the two swap places for a year: Pwyll defeats Arawn's enemy Hafgan and refrains from sleeping with Arawn's wife, earning Arawn's lasting friendship. Pwyll is afterwards known as Penn Annwn, "Head" or ruler of Annwn.

In Arthurian literature, the medieval poem Preiddeu Annwfn, found in the Book of Taliesin, describes a voyage led by Arthur through several kingdoms of Annwn, either to rescue the prisoner Gweir or to retrieve the cauldron of its lord. Later, the kingship passed to Gwyn ap Nudd, a hunter who leads the Wild Hunt with the Cŵn Annwn, the Hounds of Annwn, in pursuit of mortal souls.

Legacy

J. R. R. Tolkien took the word annún, meaning "west", for his Sindarin Elvish, inspired by Welsh. Lloyd Alexander used the name "Annuvin" in his Chronicles of Prydain, where Arawn is a dark lord. A German folk duo and a Seanan McGuire urban fantasy series also use the name.

Ancient sources: The Mabinogi, especially the First and Fourth Branches; the poem Preiddeu Annwfn in the Book of Taliesin; and the poem Cad Goddeu.

Trivia

Magic cauldron. In Preiddeu Annwfn, the cauldron of the lord of Annwn is finished with pearl and will not boil the food of a coward.
Hounds of the Otherworld. The Cŵn Annwn, the Hounds of Annwn, accompany Gwyn ap Nudd on his hunt for mortal souls.
