ancient Egyptian god of ointments, wine and blood

Shezmu

Shezmu is an ancient Egyptian deity of contradictory character, both benefactor and violent. He was worshipped from the early Old Kingdom, and his figure joins oils, perfumes and wine with ritual slaughter.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Shezmu was lord of ointments, perfume, the wine press and wine. He was linked to festivities: Old Kingdom texts mention a celebration in which young men trod grapes with their feet and then danced and sang for him.

In his darker aspect, Shezmu was lord of blood and great slaughterer of the gods. In the Pyramid Texts, several prayers ask him to dismember and cook certain deities to feed a dead king, who needed divine powers to survive the perilous journey to the stars. His violence also made him a protector of Ra during the sun god's nightly voyage, when he threatened and fought demons.

Over time, his negative traits were gradually overshadowed by positive ones. From the New Kingdom he appears mainly as a maker of fine oils for the gods, a role that became his chief one in the Greco-Roman period. Some traditions also present him as an aspect of Nefertum.

Worship and religion

His main cult center was in the Fayum, and shrines were later erected at Edfu and Dendera.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts and Coffin Texts mention his role as protector of Ra and his violent nature.

Trivia

Wine as blood. The Egyptians offered red wine as "the blood of the gods", and its dark color may explain Shesmu's link with blood.
Lion head. When depicted, he appeared as a man with a lion's head holding a butcher's knife.
