Chinese goddess of fertility and child protection

Lady Kam Fa (Golden Flower)

Lady Kam Fa, also known as Goddess Golden Flower (金花聖母), is a popular deity of southern China, especially in Guangdong, Guangxi and Hong Kong. Devotees call on her for fertility, safe childbirth and the protection of children.

The myth

According to the most widespread version, Golden Flower was a young shamaness from Guangzhou who never married and could communicate with spirits. She drowned young, yet her body stayed uncorrupted and fragrant for days; afterwards, a wooden statue bearing her likeness floated up from the lake. Since prayers for children seemed especially effective, she came to be honored as a goddess who grants offspring.

Other traditions present her as a gifted midwife who helped a high official's wife through a dangerous labor, or as a general's daughter, skilled in martial arts and letters, who withdrew from the world and attained immortality.

Worship and religion

Her cult is strongest among Cantonese communities in Hong Kong, South China and Southeast Asia. Families bring incense, flowers, food and paper clothing, and mothers visit with their babies at one month and on the first birthday. Her great festival, Kam Fa Dan, features lion dances and Cantonese opera, and on Peng Chau Island the lions undergo a 'dotting the eyes' ritual to wake them. The cult remains very much alive.

Ancient sources: The Guangdong Xinyu (廣東新語) by Qu Dajun (屈大均) records one of her legends.

Trivia

Eyes that awaken. On Peng Chau, the lions receive the 'eye-dotting' (點睛) ceremony to wake them, presided over by local officials.
Peace in four places. Before the festival, officiants burn paper offerings (money, clothing and food) in four spots on the temple grounds and the island to pray for peace with the underworld spirits.
