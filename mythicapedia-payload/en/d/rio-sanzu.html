river of the afterlife in Japanese Buddhism

Sanzu River

The Sanzu River, or River of the Three Crossings, is the stream that souls must cross before reaching the afterlife in Japanese Buddhist belief. The path a soul takes depends on the weight of its misdeeds, and it is often compared to the Greek Styx.

The myth

According to popular belief, the soul must cross the river on the seventh day after death. There are three ways across: a bridge decorated with seven precious stones, for those who did good deeds; a ford, for those whose good and bad karma are roughly balanced; and deep waters infested with fearsome snakes, where those who committed great wrongs must wade.

On the far bank, beneath a great tree, live a female demon named Datsue-ba, who strips the dead of their clothing, and a male demon named Keneō, who hangs those garments on a branch to weigh their sins. A toll of six coins is placed in the coffin to pay for the crossing.

Worship and religion

The belief is reflected in Japanese funerals, where six coins are placed in the casket as the toll for crossing the river. It is popularly placed on Mount Osore, a remote and desolate area of northern Japan.
