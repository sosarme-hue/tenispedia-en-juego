Titan and god of the river that encircled the world, Greek

Oceanus

Oceanus was, in Greek mythology, both a vast river encircling the world and the god who personified it. His name gave rise to the word "ocean", and over time his image shifted from the great outer river of the earth to the distant, unknown waters of the Atlantic.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Oceanus was the eldest of the Titans, son of Uranus and Gaia, and the origin of all rivers. Married to his sister Tethys, he fathered the three thousand Oceanids, nymphs of fresh waters, and the river gods. Homer, by contrast, seems to present him as a more primordial figure, the father of the gods, although the Homeric evidence is ambiguous. Plato tried to reconcile both versions by making Uranus and Gaia the parents of Oceanus and Tethys. Other sources make him father of the lakes, the clouds and the Harpies, and name Hermione, Europa or Parthenope as his wife depending on the author.

In the story of the succession of the gods, Oceanus took no part in the revolt against Uranus or in the Titanomachy. While the other Titans fought, he stayed at the edges of the world, and in the Theogony he sent his daughter Styx with her children to help Zeus. Aeschylus shows him visiting the chained Prometheus to advise submission to Zeus, and Pherecydes tells how, when Heracles crossed his waters in Helios's cup, Oceanus raised waves but backed down when threatened with arrows.

Legacy

The name Oceanus gave rise to the word "ocean". Ancient cartographers continued to depict the encircling stream as on Achilles' shield, and Hellenistic and Roman mosaics show him with a human torso, horns, crab claws and a serpent or fish tail. Helios's golden cup, in which Heracles sailed, was a popular subject on Attic pottery.

Ancient sources: Hesiod presents him as a Titan and father of the rivers, Homer mentions him as the origin of the gods, and Aeschylus shows him in Prometheus Bound.

Trivia

A name with history. The etymology of Oceanus remains unresolved. Some scholars propose a Semitic origin and others an Indo-European connection.
The great river. For the Greeks, Oceanus was not a sea but a great river encircling the limits of the world.
