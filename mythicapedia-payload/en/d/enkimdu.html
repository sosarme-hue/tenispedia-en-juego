Sumerian god of farming and dykes

Enkimdu

Enkimdu is a minor god of ancient Mesopotamia, associated with agriculture and irrigation. Known as lord of dykes and ditches, he is best remembered for the poem in which he competes with the shepherd Dumuzi for the favour of the goddess Inanna.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian tradition, Enkimdu looked after the dykes and some canals, the basis of farming in a land that depended on water. His role in the stories is modest. He appears in the myth Enkimdu and Dumuzi, where the goddess Inanna must choose between him and the shepherd Dumuzi, and her brother Utu urges her to pick the shepherd.

In the poem Enkimdu offers Inanna gifts from his own domain, but Dumuzi answers each offer with one of his own. In the end the two argue until they become friends. In other texts Enkimdu is called the farmer of Enlil, and when king Ur-Nammu dies he stops carrying out his duties.

Legacy

A simulation engine developed by the MASS project of the Oriental Institute of the University of Chicago, which studies farm production in ancient Near Eastern settlements, bears his name.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Sumerian poem Enkimdu and Dumuzi, in hymns and laments, and in god lists such as An = Anum.

Trivia

A computer project. A simulation engine from the University of Chicago is named after him, in connection with farming in ancient settlements.
