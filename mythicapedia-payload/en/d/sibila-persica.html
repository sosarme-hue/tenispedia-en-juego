Persian Sibyl

The Persian Sibyl, also known as the Babylonian, Chaldaean, Hebrew or Egyptian Sibyl, was a prophetic priestess who presided over the oracle of Apollo. She was said to have foretold the exploits of Alexander the Great.

The myth

According to Nicanor, who wrote a life of Alexander, she predicted the deeds of the Macedonian conqueror. She had at least three names: Sambethe, Helrea and Sabbe, and Sambethe was said to belong to the family of Noah. Pausanias, pausing at Delphi to list four sibyls, mentions a Hebrew sibyl named Sabbe, daughter of Berosus and Erymanthe. Some considered her Babylonian, others Egyptian.

Legacy

The medieval Byzantine encyclopedia Suda credited the Hebrew Sibyl with the Sibylline Oracles, a collection of texts from the 2nd to 4th centuries gathered in the 6th century. Guercino painted a Persian Sibyl, which now hangs in the Capitoline Museum in Rome.

Ancient sources: Nicanor, who wrote a life of Alexander, and Pausanias, who mentions her when listing the sibyls at Delphi.

Trivia

The Sibylline Oracles. The Suda attributes the Sibylline Oracles to the Hebrew Sibyl. They should not be confused with the Roman Sibylline Books.
Many names. She is credited with at least three different names, and her origin changes from source to source: Babylonian, Hebrew or Egyptian.
