Shinto goddess of food

Uke Mochi

Uke Mochi, also known as Ōgetsu-hime or Ukemochi, is a goddess of food and fertility in Japanese Shinto mythology. According to the stories, her death gave rise to the crops and useful animals that feed people.

The myth

In the tradition, the goddess provided food from her own body: rice-based dishes, fish and game came out of her mouth, nose and other openings to feed her guests. In one version, the moon god Tsukuyomi is sent by his sister Amaterasu, the sun goddess, to visit her. Ukemochi prepares a feast for him, but Tsukuyomi is disgusted by the way the food was produced, believes she has dishonoured him, and kills her.

In the older account preserved in the Kojiki, the killer is Susanoo, the storm god, who had asked her for food after being banished from heaven. Thinking she had poisoned the meal by making it that way, he kills her. From her corpse grow the foods: horses and cattle from her head, silkworms from her eyebrows, millet from her forehead, rice from her eyes, azuki beans from her nose, wheat from her genitals and soybeans from her rectum. The primordial creator Kamimusubi gathers the seeds and has them planted for the benefit of mortals.

In the Tsukuyomi version, her death explains why the sun and the moon are never seen together, and why day always follows night.

Worship and religion

Uke Mochi is linked with Inari and Toyouke-hime, and she is identified with Wakaukanome, a deity enshrined at Hirose Taisha. The sources do not describe festivals or rites specific to her, so only these connections with other deities and shrines are recorded.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki gives the version in which the goddess is killed by Susanoo; the variant with Tsukuyomi appears in other accounts of the tradition.

Trivia

A goddess with two genders. In different contexts she is referred to as both male and female. In other representations she takes the form of a fox.
A daughter who sows the crops. In a legend passed down in Iwami district, Shimane Prefecture, her daughter Otogosa-hime descends on a red goose to sow the seeds of the crops. According to the story, she could get food from any part of her body.
