sea monster of Japanese folklore

Akugyo

The akugyo ("evil fish") is a sea monster, sometimes classed as an aquatic yōkai, from Japanese folklore. It is associated mainly with the waters near former Kibi Province, today Okayama Prefecture, and was feared for its huge size and its appetite for ships.

The myth

According to tradition, an akugyo can capsize whole ships and devour sailors who fall into the water. Some breathe fire; the variety that looks like a colossal mermaid has two white, oni-like horns. Fishermen dread it because a boat can become trapped between its fins.

A popular account places a roughly eleven-metre specimen off Echigo in 1805, killed by the lord of Kaga with 1,500 men and 450 cannons. Another story tells of the drummer Izutsuya Kanroku, stuck on its back, who played so loudly that the sound shook his boat free.

Legacy

It appears in the 1963 Toei animated film Wanpaku Ōji no Orochi Taiji, where Susanoo battles an akugyo.

Ancient sources: Its possible connection to the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki is only a hypothesis, since those texts do not describe it under this name.

Trivia

A drum against the monster. According to the story, the drummer Kanroku survived by beating his drum with all his strength until his boat broke free from the akugyo's back.
Oni-like horns. The mermaid-like variety has two white horns resembling those of an oni, which set it apart from other akugyo.
