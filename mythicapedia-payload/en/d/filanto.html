king of the Dryopes in Greek mythology

Phylas

Phylas is, in Greek mythology, king of the Dryopes and ruler of Ephyra. His story is tied to the deeds of Heracles, who killed him after he tried to attack the temple at Delphi, and his name is linked to the offspring the hero had with his daughters.

The myth

According to the story, Phylas ruled Ephyra and decided to lead his army against the temple at Delphi, not realizing that this act would bring him into conflict with Heracles, son of Zeus. During the fighting, the king himself met his death at the hero's hands.

All of his soldiers were taken prisoner. Upon reaching the city's oracle, they were offered to the protecting deity of the site, Apollo, a fate that closes the campaign against the sanctuary.

Phylas had two daughters, Meda and Astyoche. Heracles lay with both: Antiochus was born of Meda, and Tlepolemus of Astyoche, so the king's line is joined, through the mothers, to the hero's descendants.

Trivia

Heracles' descendants. According to the story, Heracles had children with both daughters of Phylas: Antiochus, born to Meda, and Tlepolemus, born to Astyoche.
