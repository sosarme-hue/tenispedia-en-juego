Chinese god of the sea and doors

Qianliyan

Qianliyan is a god of Chinese mythology, often shown as a door god. He appears alongside his partner Shunfeng'er as a guardian of the temples of the sea goddess Mazu, and his name refers to a gaze that can reach across great distances. Stories portray him watching over sailors at night and in fog.

The myth

According to tradition, Qianliyan and Shunfeng'er appeared off Meizhou Island during a storm. Mazu defeated them with her magic silk scarf, which blew sand into their eyes and ears. After their surrender, she healed them and they swore loyalty to her.

Other accounts change the pair's origin. In one, they were Song generals who competed for Mazu's hand at Peach Blossom Mountain. In another, they were the ruthless brothers Gao Ming and Gao Jue, who haunted the mountain as demons. In a third, they were guards of Di Xin, the last Shang king, and the sage Jiang Ziya defeated them with the blood of a black dog.

Worship and religion

Qianliyan mostly appears as a door god in Mazuist temples and as a guardian beside Mazu on her altars and charms. Some villages and sailors worship him separately for protection from danger. During the annual pilgrimages from Dajia to Beigang, masked men on stilts portray him and Shunfeng'er.

Legacy

In art he typically appears as a green-skinned demon shielding his eyes from the sun. In Chinese, his name, "thousand-mile eyes", is used to mean clairvoyance.

Ancient sources: He first appears in the sixteenth-century novel Journey to the West, and in a Southern Song carving in the Shimen caves.
