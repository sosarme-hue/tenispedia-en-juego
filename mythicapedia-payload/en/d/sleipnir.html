Odin's eight-legged horse

Sleipnir

Sleipnir is the grey, eight-legged horse ridden by Odin in Norse mythology. Born to Loki and the stallion Svaðilfari, he is regarded as the finest of all horses and is one of the best-known beings of Scandinavian tradition.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the gods of Asgard needed a wall to keep out their enemies. A builder offered to raise one within three seasons in exchange for the goddess Freyja, the Sun and the Moon. The gods agreed on conditions, and the builder asked to use his stallion, Svaðilfari, which Loki made possible.

When the wall was nearly finished, Loki, under pressure from the gods, lured the stallion away with a mare that emerged from the forest. Svaðilfari ran after her, and the builder could not finish on time. His true nature as a giant was revealed, and Thor killed him with a blow of Mjölnir.

Loki then gave birth to a grey foal with eight legs, Sleipnir, and gave him to Odin, promising that no horse would match his speed and that he could carry Odin over sea, land and air, even to Hel. According to the tales, Sleipnir never failed his master. Odin rides him, for instance, in the myth of Baldr, when he journeys to Hel. The sources also say that runes were carved on his teeth, and that he symbolized the eight winds. A few texts give him six legs, an uncommon variant.

Legacy

Sleipnir appears in a riddle in the Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks. In modern Icelandic folklore he is credited with creating Ásbyrgi, a horseshoe-shaped canyon. He is depicted on 8th-century Gotlandic image stones and appears in artworks, literature and video games. The name has also been given to ships.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda, Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks and Völsunga saga.
