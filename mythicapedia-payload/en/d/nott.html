Norse goddess of the night

Nótt

Nótt is the personification of night in Norse mythology, a dark figure who crosses the sky every day. She matters because she is the mother of key beings in the order of the world, among them day and the earth.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, Nótt is the daughter of the giant Narfi, a dweller of Jötunheimr, and she is described as black and swarthy. She married three times. With Naglfari she had Auðr; with Annar, Jörð, the personified earth; and with the god Delling, Dagr, the day, who inherited the brightness and fairness of his father's people.

The gods set Nótt and Dagr in the sky, each with a chariot and a horse, so that they would circle the earth every twenty-four hours. Nótt rides ahead of her son, and the foam dropping from the bit of her horse, Hrímfaxi, falls on the valleys as dew. In the Poetic Edda, the Alvíssmál lists the names of night in each world: humans call her night, the gods darkness, the giants unlight, and the elves joy-of-sleep. One manuscript of the Prose Edda, however, changes the family: there Jörð is Delling's wife and Dagr's mother, and Nótt takes that role instead.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Vafþrúðnismál, Alvíssmál and Sigrdrífumál) and the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál).

Trivia

Foam that becomes dew. According to the Prose Edda, the foam falling from the bit of Hrímfaxi, Nótt's horse, is what covers the valleys with dew in the mornings.
Nine names for night. In the Alvíssmál, the dwarf Alvíss lists what night is called in each of the nine worlds, depending on who is speaking.
