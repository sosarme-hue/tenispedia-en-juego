clay giant of Norse mythology

Mökkurkálfi

Mökkurkálfi, whose name is usually translated as "mist-calf", is a creature of clay in Norse mythology who accompanies the jötunn Hrungnir. He appears in the story of the duel between that giant and the god Thor.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Hrungnir rides his horse Gullfaxi after losing a race to Sleipnir, Odin's horse, and arrives in Ásgard. There he gets drunk and threatens the gods, promising to take Valhalla, bury Asgard and keep Freyja and Sif for himself. The gods call Thor, and the two agree to a duel.

For the fight, Hrungnir is escorted by Mökkurkálfi, a creature of clay with the heart of a mare. At the sight of Thor, the clay giant is said to be terrified and wets himself, while Hrungnir, whose heart, head and shield are made of stone, stands unguarded. Thor kills him with his hammer Mjölnir, but is then trapped under one of the giant's legs, and only Thor's young son Magni can lift it.

The earlier poem Haustlöng does not mention Mökkurkálfi, so it is unclear whether the character was part of the original story.

Ancient sources: Attested in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál, 13th century); not mentioned in Þjóðólfr of Hvinir's poem Haustlöng (10th century).

Trivia

Name. His name is usually translated as "mist-calf", an image that points to his origin in clay.
An initiation. The scholar Georges Dumézil read the episode as the initiation of Thor's servant Þjálfi into killing a clay monster.
