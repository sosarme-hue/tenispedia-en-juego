Trojan warrior in Greek mythology

Pedasus

Pedasus is a Trojan warrior in Greek mythology, son of Bucolion and twin brother of Aesepus. He appears in the Iliad among the casualties of the Trojan War, and his name is also borne by one of Achilles' horses.

The myth

According to the Greek tradition, Pedasus was the son of Bucolion and the naiad Abarbarea, and he had a twin brother, Aesepus. Since Bucolion was a son of Laomedon, Pedasus was a grandson of that king, who was also the father of Priam, so he belonged to the royal house of Troy.

He fought in the Trojan War on the Trojan side. In book VI of the Iliad, Pedasus and his brother Aesepus, along with other warriors, are killed by Euryalus. He is a minor figure in the conflict, remembered mainly for that death in battle.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions Pedasus in book VI.

Trivia

A twin. Pedasus had a twin brother, Aesepus, with whom he died in the same episode of the Iliad.
A horse with his name. One of Achilles' horses was also named Pedasus, and according to the tradition it had been brought from Thebes.
