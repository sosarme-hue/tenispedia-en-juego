mountain spirit of Japanese folklore

Yamawaro

The yamawaro is a mountain spirit of Japanese folklore, most present in western Japan and especially in Kyushu. It is pictured as a childlike creature covered in hair who works with woodcutters, plays pranks and makes strange sounds in the forest.

The myth

According to local tradition, the yamawaro lives deep in the mountains. Edo-period texts describe it as a child of about ten, with dark hair, a body covered in fine fur and long legs that let it walk upright. Other accounts give it a single eye above the nose, dog-like pointed ears and long reddish hair falling over its eyes. It feeds on crabs and on the roots of yam and of a hybrid mulberry.

In Kumamoto it is said to hate carpenters' ink lines, so anyone who uses them in the mountains will not be visited. It sometimes helps woodcutters, but it expects the reward to match what was promised; if it receives something different, it grows angry. It is also said to enter homes and baths, leaving them greasy and foul-smelling, and to play pranks on cattle and horses.

Legacy

It appears in Edo-period painting, where it is shown with branch-like arms and one eye. In popular culture it inspired the Pokémon Dusknoir and it is a boss in the video game Touhou 18.

Ancient sources: Its descriptions come from Edo-period texts, including a book on supernatural beings of Kyushu.

Trivia

Work in the mountains. Yamawaro are said to help woodcutters, but only if the reward matches what was promised.
Carpenter's ink. In Kumamoto, they are thought to avoid places where carpenters use ink lines.
