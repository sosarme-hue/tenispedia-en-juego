pre-Islamic Arabian idol named in the Qur'an

Ya'uq

Ya'uq, also written Jawc or Jawk, is one of the idols that the Qur'an says were worshipped in the days of Noah. Its name appears in a verse that places it among the gods of pre-Islamic Arabia, which makes it an important clue to the religious beliefs of the region before Islam.

The myth

The story we have is very brief. The Qur'an (71:23) records a warning that the people of Noah must not abandon their gods, naming Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr. The text does not tell detailed stories about any of them, so the myth is mainly a list of deities linked to the age of the patriarch.

Muslim commentators added details about their appearance. According to them, Ya'uq took the form of a horse, while Wadd was shaped like a man, Suwa' like a woman, Yaghuth like a lion and Nasr like an eagle. These descriptions come from later interpretation rather than from the Qur'anic text itself. Some critics also point to an anachronism, since the names match the Arabia of Muhammad rather than the time of Noah.

Worship and religion

According to a tradition cited by the commentators, Ya'uq was venerated by the tribe of Hamadan. A South Arabian inscription mentions a synagogue dedicated to Rahmanan named Ya'uq, which shows that the name also had religious use beyond the northern Arabian world. The sources offer no information about later worship, and no living practice survives.

Legacy

Its presence is mainly textual. The name was preserved in the Qur'an and remains part of Islamic exegetical tradition, where it serves as an example of the idols that Islam rejected. Beyond that setting, the sources record no significant artistic or cultural legacy.

Ancient sources: The Qur'an (71:23), which names it among the idols of Noah's people, together with later commentaries by Muslim exegetes.

Trivia

A horse form. Muslim commentators described Ya'uq in the shape of a horse, while other idols on the same list were imagined as a lion, an eagle or human figures.
A synagogue bearing its name. A South Arabian inscription mentions a synagogue dedicated to Rahmanan named Ya'uq, written mkrbn yʿwq.
