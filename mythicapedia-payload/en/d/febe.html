Titaness of Greek mythology, goddess of pure light and prophecy

Phoebe

Phoebe is one of the original Titans of Greek mythology, daughter of Uranus and Gaia, and grandmother of Apollo and Artemis. Her name, meaning 'bright', was given to several lunar goddesses, although she herself plays only a minor role in the stories.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Phoebe married her brother Coeus, and they had two daughters: Leto, mother of Apollo and Artemis, and Asteria, who was later carried off by Perses. Hesiod calls her 'golden-crowned', an epithet that reflects her luminous nature.

Her best-known episode appears in Aeschylus' play The Eumenides, where the Delphic priestess explains that Phoebe received the Oracle at Delphi from her sister Themis, who had in turn received it from their mother Gaia. Phoebe then passed it on to her grandson Apollo as a birthday gift. Modern scholars such as D. S. Robertson suspect that Aeschylus invented this succession to fit the oracle into a pattern of divine generations, since the historical reconstruction of the sanctuary does not support it.

Tradition also records her role in the Gigantomachy, the battle of the Olympian gods against the Giants. She appears beside her daughter Asteria on the Pergamon Altar, wielding a flaming torch.

Worship and religion

Phoebe had very little presence in worship. No temples or festivals dedicated to her are known, and Greek and Roman art rarely depicted her, so she has no distinct iconography. Her religious importance was indirect, through her link to the Delphic oracle and her descendants.

Legacy

Her name became an epithet of Artemis as a moon goddess and of Apollo as a sun god, and Roman poets used it as a synonym for Diana and Luna. One of Saturn's moons is named after her, in honour of the sister of Cronus, the Greek counterpart of Saturn. Phoebe is also a popular feminine given name in the English-speaking world.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony names her as the mother of Leto and Asteria; Aeschylus' The Eumenides recounts the transfer of the Delphic oracle.

Trivia

Epithet of Apollo. The adjective Phoibos, from which her name derives, was applied to Apollo as a sun god, and from it came the Greek word for prophesying.
A moon of Saturn. Phoebe is the name of one of Saturn's moons, honouring the Titaness sister of Cronus.
