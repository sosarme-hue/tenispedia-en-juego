daughter of Alcmaeon in Greek mythology

Tisiphone

Tisiphone is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of the Argive hero Alcmaeon and the seer Manto. Her story of enslavement and reunion with her father survives mainly through a lost tragedy by Euripides and the account of the writer Apollodorus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Alcmaeon, driven mad after murdering his mother Eriphyle, fathered Tisiphone and her brother Amphilochus with Manto, probably born at Delphi. Alcmaeon brought the infants to Corinth and entrusted them to King Creon to raise.

As Tisiphone grew into an exceptionally beautiful young woman, Creon's wife grew uneasy and feared the king might set her aside to marry the girl. To prevent this, she sold Tisiphone into slavery, unaware that the buyer was her own father, who did not recognise her and kept her as a maid. Alcmaeon later went to Corinth and recovered his son too. The queen eventually revealed the girl's identity, Creon fled into exile, and Amphilochus learnt that Alcmaeon, not Creon, was his true father.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, a second-century author, is the most coherent source for the story, which he attributes to Euripides' tragedy Alcmaeon in Corinth, first staged posthumously around 405 BC and now lost.

Trivia

A lost play. Much of what is known about the character comes from Alcmaeon in Corinth, a tragedy by Euripides of which only fragments survive.
Possibly twins. Apollodorus indicates that Tisiphone and Amphilochus might have been twins, although this is not certain.
