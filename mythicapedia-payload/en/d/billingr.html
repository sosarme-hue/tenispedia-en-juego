father of the maiden desired by Odin in Norse mythology

Billingr

Billingr (also Billing) is a figure in Norse mythology, the father of a young woman whom Odin tried to seduce. He matters mainly for the episode in the Hávamál, where Odin uses the story as an example of female deceit.

The myth

According to stanzas 96 to 102 of the Hávamál, part of the Poetic Edda, a maiden, whose name is not given, asked Odin to visit her after nightfall, when it would be safest, and promised to give herself to him.

On his way back, Odin found the path blocked by warriors with swords and burning torches, so he had to turn back. At daybreak he discovered that the maiden had gone and had left a female dog tied to the bed in her place. Odin tells the story in the first person as a warning about the fickleness and deceit of women, and he laments the folly of longing for what cannot be had.

Billingr's nature is uncertain. He may have been a giant or a dwarf, and John Lindow sets out arguments for both possibilities. Billing appears as a dwarf name in the Hauksbók manuscript, and a poetic kenning names him as the father of the cup of poetry. If he were a dwarf, his daughter would be one of very few female dwarves in the myths, while Odin's relations with giantesses are well attested.

Ancient sources: The episode appears in stanzas 96 to 102 of the Hávamál in the Poetic Edda, and the name Billing appears in the Hauksbók manuscript version of the Völuspá.

Trivia

A poetic kenning. A poetic kenning calls poetry the 'cup of the son of Billing', which works whether Billing is a dwarf or a giant.
