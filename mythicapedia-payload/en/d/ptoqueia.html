Greek personification of beggary

Ptocheia

Ptocheia (Greek Ptocheía) was, in Greek mythology, the personification of beggary. Ancient texts barely mention her, which makes her a marginal figure in the Greek imagination.

The myth

The only ancient witness that names her is a comic passage by Aristophanes, where she is presented as the sister of Poverty (Penia). Tradition places her alongside other personified abstractions such as Helplessness (Amechania) and Want (Aporia).

These figures formed a set of opposites: Wealth (Ploutos) and Prosperity (Euthenia) stood for the reverse of poverty and begging. Rather than stories of deeds or detailed genealogies, her presence is almost a wordplay, and the poet mentions her in passing to joke about poverty.

Ancient sources: Aristophanes mentions her in a comic passage, as a sister of Poverty.
