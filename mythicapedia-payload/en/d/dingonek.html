African cryptid with an armoured, reptilian look

Dingonek

The dingonek is an African cryptid, a scaly creature with a dart-tipped tail and walrus-like tusks, described in accounts from the early twentieth century. It matters because it is one of the most cited monsters in African cryptozoology and shows how a hunter's story became a modern legend.

The myth

The story comes from the big-game hunter John Alfred Jordan, who claimed he saw the creature in 1907 near the Maggori River in Kenya. According to his account, the animal had scales, claws, a long tail and a large head with huge fangs.

Jordan hid, and fearing it would spot him, he fired a .303 round behind its ear. The beast did not fall; it leapt into the water, and Jordan fled in terror into the bush. For two days he searched the shores for tracks without success. Edgar Beecher Bronson recorded the story in his 1910 memoir In Closed Territory.

Legacy

The creature appears in the Bengali novel Chander Pahar by Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay. In 1918 the Canadian magazine Maclean's published an article presenting it as a newly discovered species.

Trivia

South African rock painting. At Brakfontein Ridge in South Africa there is a rock painting of an unknown creature that some link to the dingonek, including its walrus-like tusks.
A lake monster. In 1913 Charles William Hobley linked the account to a rare, unclassified animal said to live in rivers and in Lake Victoria.
