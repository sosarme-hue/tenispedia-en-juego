Celtic god of Godmanchester

Abandinus

Abandinus is a Celtic god, or male spirit, worshipped at Godmanchester in Cambridgeshire, Britain, during the Romano-Celtic period. He is known from a single inscription, which makes him a local deity with very little surviving evidence.

The myth

Almost no stories survive about Abandinus. His name is the main clue to his nature, and its meaning remains uncertain. One suggestion links it to Indo-European roots meaning 'sing or rejoice' or 'bind', which would give a sense like 'the god who sings to something' or 'who binds something to something else'.

Another reading treats it as a derivative of *abon-, the Celtic word for 'river', seen in the names of British waterways. On this view Abandinus would mean roughly 'Andinus of the river', fitting a deity connected with a spring or stream near the Roman fort.

Worship and religion

Abandinus was worshipped at the Roman fort of Godmanchester, possibly beside a spring or stream. The evidence is a small bronze feather, most likely a votive offering, dedicated by a man named Vatiacus, who gave it 'out of his own funds'. No other shrines or festivals are known.

Trivia

A bronze feather. The only direct evidence of his worship is a bronze object shaped like a feather, inscribed with a dedication to the god.
A river name. His name may come from the Celtic word for river, which appears in place names across Britain.
