Greek goddess of wisdom and prudence

Metis

Metis is the Greek goddess of wisdom, counsel and cunning, a pre-Olympian deity and the first wife of Zeus. She matters because she was the mother of Athena and because her story explains how the goddess of wisdom came into being.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Metis was one of the Oceanids, the three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, and one of the oldest. She gave Zeus an emetic potion that made his father Cronus vomit first the stone he had swallowed and then Zeus's siblings, Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades and Poseidon. After the Titanomachy, Zeus took her as his wife, and she was both an indispensable aid and a threat to him.

Gaia and Uranus prophesied that Metis would bear a daughter wiser than her mother and then a son who would overthrow Zeus. To prevent this, Zeus tricked her and swallowed her when she took the form of a small creature, in some versions a fly. Metis was already pregnant with Athena, and according to the tradition she continued to advise Zeus from within.

When Athena grew up, Metis made her armour, a shield and a spear. The young goddess beat her weapons together until Zeus suffered a splitting headache, and he had Hephaestus split his skull open. Athena emerged full-grown and armed beside the river Triton. According to Apollodorus, Zeus had forced himself on Metis, who changed shape many times to escape him, and another version makes the Cyclops Brontes the father of Athena.

Legacy

Her name has been applied as a concept in literary criticism, notably by Jean-Pierre Vernant and Marcel Detienne, who studied metis as a cunning intelligence associated with heroes. Metis Island in Antarctica is named after her, as are the asteroid 9 Metis and a moon of Jupiter.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony presents her as an Oceanid and the wife of Zeus; Apollodorus recounts her metamorphoses and her escape. Plato names her as the mother of Poros, Resourcefulness.

Trivia

A Greek word. The term metis described an intelligence combining wisdom and cunning, a quality highly admired in heroes such as Odysseus.
A Homeric title. In the Homeric poems Zeus is given the epithet Mētíeta, 'good counsellor', an echo of the goddess who was his wife.
