prophetic bird of Russian folklore

Gamayun

The Gamayun is a prophetic bird of Russian folklore, a symbol of wisdom and knowledge that lives on an island in the mythical East, close to paradise. Its songs carry divine messages, but they can also lure those who hear them.

The myth

According to Russian popular tradition, the Gamayun is a large, dark bird with a woman's face that lives on an island in the mythical East, near paradise. Its songs reveal prophecies and divine messages, but only a select group of people can receive them, and whoever listens may be bound to it for the rest of their life, much as with the Sirin. The stories describe it without legs or wings, always in flight and kept aloft by its tail.

Medieval Russian literature set it apart from other mythical birds: from the phoenix, which regenerates and has no human features; from the Alkonost, which calms storms at sea; and from the Sirin, which has a female face and an enchanting voice. It was identified mainly by its appearance and its voice.

In books from the 17th to the 19th centuries it appears as an omen of misfortune: its fall foreshadows the death of statesmen.

Legacy

During the Silver Age of Russian poetry, at the start of the 20th century, it became a source of tragic prophecies. Aleksandr Blok wrote the poem Gamayun, the Prophetic Bird about it. Its image also appears on the coat of arms of Smolensk.

Ancient sources: Medieval Russian literature distinguishes it from other mythical birds, and books from the 17th to 19th centuries describe it in detail.

Trivia

Possible Persian origin. Some scholars link its name to the Huma bird of Persian mythology. Oleg Trubachyov derived it from the Avestan hu-māiia.
On the coat of arms of Smolensk. The Russian city of Smolensk includes the Gamayun on its coat of arms.
