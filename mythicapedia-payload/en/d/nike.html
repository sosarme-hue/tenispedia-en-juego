Greek goddess of victory

Nike

Nike is the goddess and personification of victory in Greek mythology, usually shown winged and often beside Zeus or Athena. She presided over battles as well as athletic, musical and theatrical contests, and her image has endured to the present day.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Nike was the daughter of the Titan Pallas and Styx, and the sister of Zelus (Rivalry), Kratos (Strength) and Bia (Force). When Zeus called the gods to choose sides in the Titanomachy, Styx and her children were the first to join him, and he granted them the honour of living always at his side.

In a Homeric hymn, however, Nike is called the daughter of Ares, and a later version makes her born from the union of Styx and her own father Acheron. According to an Arcadian legend recorded by Dionysius of Halicarnassus, she was raised alongside Athena as her foster-sister.

In the Dionysiaca of Nonnus of Panopolis, a late work, Nike serves as an attendant of Athena. She reproaches Zeus for his hesitation before Typhon, lists the gods who fled the fight and urges him to gather his thunderbolts. During the battle she shields Zeus, and afterwards she drives his chariot. The Theogony, by contrast, tells that struggle differently, and Nike does not appear in it.

Worship and religion

Evidence for her cult is scarce and concentrated in several places in Greece and Magna Graecia. Sacrifices were offered to her alongside Zeus, Athena and Poseidon, and at Olympia she had an altar between those of Zeus. In Athens her worship was closely tied to that of Athena Nike on the Acropolis. Playwrights often closed their plays with an appeal to her in theatrical competitions.

Legacy

Her winged figure entered Western art from the Renaissance, and the Winged Victory of Samothrace in the Louvre is among her most famous depictions. She often appears beside Justice on government and court buildings in Europe and America. Since the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics her figure has adorned the Olympic medals, and the sportswear company Nike took its name from her.

Ancient sources: Hesiod in the Theogony, a Homeric hymn, Nonnus of Panopolis in the Dionysiaca, and the poets Pindar and Bacchylides, who celebrate her in their odes.

Trivia

Victory at Marathon. Tradition holds that a soldier ran from Marathon to Athens to announce the victory with the cry "Nike!" and then collapsed.
A goddess with a brand name. The sportswear company Nike takes its name from the goddess, whose figure has appeared on Olympic medals since 1928.
