hero of Welsh mythology, cousin of King Arthur

Culhwch

Culhwch is the protagonist of Culhwch and Olwen, one of the earliest medieval Welsh tales, and a cousin of King Arthur. His story, centred on a quest for an impossible bride, is one of the oldest to link Arthur with a world of extraordinary feats.

The myth

According to the tale, Culhwch was born to Goleuddydd, wife of Cilydd son of Celyddon, when the queen was frightened by a herd of swine and gave birth in the pigs' run. A swineherd found him there and brought him to his father, who recognised him as a child of noble lineage. After Goleuddydd's death, the king's new wife tried to pair the young man with her stepdaughter; he rejected her, and she cursed him so that he could marry only Olwen, daughter of the giant Ysbaddaden.

Although he had never seen Olwen, Culhwch fell in love with her. His father warned him he would never find her without Arthur's help, so the young man set off for Arthur's court. Arthur agreed to assist him and sent warriors along. After learning of Ysbaddaden's cruelty, who had murdered twenty-three of his brother's children, the group gained entry to the giant's castle. Ysbaddaden agreed to give his daughter in exchange for Culhwch completing a series of impossible tasks, the anoethau, including hunting the boar Twrch Trwyth and rescuing Mabon ap Modron. Once they were completed, Goreu, Culhwch's half-brother, avenged his murdered brothers by beheading the giant, and Olwen was free to marry her beloved.

Legacy

Culhwch briefly appears in the Marwnad Cynddylan, a Welsh poem probably from the seventh century, where he is compared to Cynddylan, a ruler of Pengwern.

Ancient sources: His story belongs to the medieval Welsh tales appended to Lady Charlotte Guest's edition of the Mabinogion; he is also mentioned in the Marwnad Cynddylan.

Trivia

Name and pigs. The tale explains his name as 'sow run', although philologists think this is a folk etymology.
