Buddhist goddess of transcendent wisdom, mother of the Buddhas

Prajñāpāramitā Devī

Prajñāpāramitā Devī is the female personification of prajñāpāramitā, the perfection of transcendent wisdom, which in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism leads to Buddhahood. Originating in India, she is a central figure of Buddhist tantra and is known as the "Mother of Buddhas".

The myth

According to Buddhist texts, perfect wisdom neither comes into being nor passes out of being, and so it is seen as the source from which all Buddhas arise. The Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra presents her as a mother who gives birth to the Buddhas without birth, and through them turns the wheel of true teaching.

Tradition portrays her as a caring mother: just as sons rally around a sick mother, the Buddhas honour her, since they owe their omniscience to her wisdom. The hymn attributed to Rāhulabhadra calls her the grandmother of all beings and says that, depending on how one regards her, she either liberates or binds. She can appear terrifying to fools and friendly to the wise.

Worship and religion

In India, prajñāpāramitā texts were themselves worshipped, placed on altars with flowers, lamps and incense, and considered superior to the veneration of stupas or relics. Later the goddess was worshipped in personified form, and Buddhist art from the 9th to 12th centuries, especially under the Pala Empire, shows her, as well as in Java, Cambodia, Tibet and the Himalayas.

Legacy

She is one of the most frequently depicted figures of wisdom in Buddhist art across Asia. She is recognised by her golden or white colour and by having either two or four arms.

Ancient sources: The Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra, the Heart Sutra, the Sādhanamālā and Rāhulabhadra's hymn quoted in the Dà zhìdù lùn.

Trivia

Mother of the Buddhas. The texts call her the "Mother of All Buddhas" and state that the Buddhas owe their existence to her.
Earliest images. The earliest known images of the goddess are at the Ellora Caves and date from the 7th century.
