legendary king and figure of Norse mythology

Hraudung

Hraudung is the name of several legendary figures in Norse mythology. According to the prose introduction to the Grímnismál, he was a human king, father of the giant Geirröd and grandfather of Agnar.

The myth

The sources offer different versions of him. In the prose introduction to the Grímnismál, from the Poetic Edda, he appears as a human king who fathered Geirröd and Agnar, which places him as an ancestor of both. Geirröd is a jötunn, a giant, so the genealogy links Hraudung to the line of giants.

In the Prose Edda, within the Skáldskaparmál section, his name turns up in lists of giants and sea kings. He is first listed as a jötunn and later as a sea king, so the texts do not fully agree on his nature. None of these sources recounts his deeds in any detail.

Ancient sources: Grímnismál (prose introduction) from the Poetic Edda and Skáldskaparmál from the Prose Edda.

Trivia

Two natures. The Prose Edda lists him first as a jötunn and then as a sea king, so the same name is used for two different kinds of being.
One name, several figures. The name Hraudung is applied to more than one legendary figure in Norse sources, so it is not always clear which one a given passage means.
