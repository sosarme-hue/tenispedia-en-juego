female jinn-like figure of Moroccan folklore

Aisha Kandisha

Aisha Kandisha is a female figure of Moroccan folklore, a jinn-like being whose beauty lures young men to their doom. Her legend is especially strong in northern Morocco, where it serves as a warning to children and sometimes lingers into adulthood.

The myth

She is typically pictured as a beautiful young woman with the legs of a hoofed animal, such as a goat or a camel. She lives beside a body of water, the sea at Tangier, the Martil river at Tetouan or the Sebou among the Beni Ahsen, and mainly preys on young men, enticing them with her looks or posing as their wives before driving them mad or killing them.

Among the Beni Ahsen she is said to fear steel knives and needles and to have a husband named Hammu Qayyu; farther south, in Doukkala, he is called Kharaja. For the Buffi Sufi order she is just one of several female jinn named Aicha, each with her own character.

The legend is thought to be based on a Moroccan countess from El Jadida who, after her husband's death, seduced Portuguese soldiers during the invasion, and local fighters then killed them.

Worship and religion

The Hamadsha and Zār societies hold rites in which members seek voluntary spirit possession by her and by other djinn.

Legacy

She appears in Moroccan books, films and songs, including the Gnawa tune Lalla Aicha and the French horror film Kandisha.

Trivia

Steel against spirits. Among the Beni Ahsen it is believed that steel knives and needles drive her away, a belief that does not apply to other beings in the tradition.
A possible historical root. Her figure is thought to stem from the memory of a real woman from El Jadida who resisted the Portuguese.
