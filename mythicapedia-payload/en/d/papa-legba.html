lwa of Vodou, guardian of crossroads and mediator between worlds

Papa Legba

Papa Legba is a lwa of West African Vodun and its diasporic derivatives, who serves as the intermediary between God and humanity and as the guardian of the paths between the world of the living and the world of the spirits. Rooted in Yoruba traditions and the kingdom of Dahomey, he remains one of the most widely recognized figures of Vodou.

The myth

In Vodou tradition, Legba stands at a spiritual crossroads, where he decides whether to grant or deny permission to speak with the spirits who dwell in Guinée, the world of spirits, a name that recalls the African homeland to which enslaved people hoped their souls might return after death. He is believed to speak every human language.

He is always the first and the last to be invoked, since no communication between mortals and the loa is possible without his permission. For this reason, every Vodou ceremony opens with a song addressed to him, meant to neutralise his power and win his favour.

He is usually shown as an old man with a crutch or cane, a broad-brimmed straw hat and a pipe, and the dog is sacred to him. In Benin and Nigeria, by contrast, he is imagined as young and virile, with an exaggerated phallus and horns.

Worship and religion

His cult remains alive in the diasporic Vodou religions, where ceremonies always open with songs to him. Offerings include candy, tobacco and strong black coffee. As a spirit close to children, he is often given the task of entertaining them while adults perform the more serious rituals.

Legacy

Papa Legba appears in the blues, where researcher Robert Palmer noted that he is identified with the Devil or the Black Man, and he is linked to Robert Johnson's song "Crossroads". Alejo Carpentier mentions him in The Kingdom of This World (1949), and William Gibson places him at the gateway to cyberspace in Count Zero. Talking Heads wrote a song about him, and the character also appears in television series such as American Horror Story.

Trivia

A Terry Pratchett character. In Terry Pratchett's novel Witches Abroad (1991), a black cockerel named Legba appears in a voodoo-themed subplot set in a pseudo-New Orleans.
A road god in a horror series. In American Horror Story the character guards the afterlife and has been criticised for resembling Baron Samedi more than the traditional Papa Legba.
