protective goddess of Jainism, yakshini of Parshvanatha

Padmavati

Padmavati is the protective goddess, or śāsana devī, of the Jain tirthankara Parshvanatha, the twenty-third. She is one of the most revered deities in Jainism, invoked as a powerful intercessor alongside Ambika and Chakreshvari.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, in an earlier life Padmavati was a naga, a serpent, who with her mate was saved by Parshvanatha when both were about to be burned alive inside a log by the ascetic Kamatha. After death they were reborn as Dharanendra and Padmavati, serpent deities.

The legend says that when the ascetic Meghamali harassed Parshvanatha, Dharanendra and Padmavati came to his defence. Padmavati protected him and afterwards became a powerful yakshi, a tantric deity who surpassed the snake goddess Vairotya.

Worship and religion

Padmavati is worshipped by Jains alongside the tirthankaras. Her tantric rites include yantra-vidhi, pitha-sthapana and mantra-puja, and Friday is the most popular day to honour her. Her worship continues today, with temples dedicated to her at Humcha, Karkala and Warangal.

Legacy

Jain tantric literature preserves many texts devoted to her, such as the Bhairava-Padmavati-Kalpa, composed by Mallisena in the twelfth century. Her image, with a serpent hood over her head and seated on a lotus, appears in numerous sculptures and bronzes.

Ancient sources: Jain tantric and devotional texts, such as the Bhairava-Padmavati-Kalpa and the Adbhuta-Padmavati-Kalpa, mention her as an object of worship.

Trivia

Symbol in the crown. She is often depicted with a small image of Parshvanatha placed in her crown.
Her mount. Her mount, or vahana, is the rooster.
