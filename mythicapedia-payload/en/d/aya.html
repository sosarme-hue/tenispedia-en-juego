Mesopotamian goddess of dawn, wife of Shamash

Aya

Aya is a Mesopotamian goddess associated with dawn and the rising sun, wife of the sun god Shamash. Her figure, rooted in Akkadian and Babylonian tradition, partly descends from the Sumerian goddess Sherida, and shows how the Sumerian and Akkadian pantheons merged.

The myth

In Akkadian and Babylonian tradition, Aya was the goddess of dawn; her name means simply "dawn" in Akkadian. From the Akkadian period she was linked with the rising sun, with love and with youth, and she was often called kallatu, "the bride".

As the wife of Shamash, she was regarded as daughter-in-law of Suen and Ningal and sister-in-law of Ishtar. Her main role was that of intercessor: she was believed to plead with her husband on behalf of worshipers. In Sippar and Larsa, statues of the two gods were placed together on a bed in a "sacred marriage" rite (hasadu) to renew their vows. Her origins go back to Sherida, a Sumerian deity who became the consort of Utu.

Worship and religion

Aya was already worshiped in the Early Dynastic period. She received cult in the temples of Sippar and Larsa, where she shared a sanctuary with Shamash, and she appears in the E-babbar temple at Girsu. The community of the nadītu women of Sippar was also associated with her cult.

Ancient sources: She appears in Mesopotamian god lists such as An = Anum, which records several of her names, and in texts from Ugarit and Mari.
