Mermaid of Warsaw

The Syrenka, or Mermaid of Warsaw, is the legendary figure that symbolises the Polish capital, appearing on its coat of arms and in many statues. She is a freshwater creature of Slavic folklore, related to the sea mermaid and linked to the River Vistula.

The myth

According to the legend told by the city and its tour guides, the mermaid decided to stay after stopping on a riverbank near the Old Town. Fishermen noticed something stirring the water, tangling their nets and freeing their fish. They planned to trap it, but when they heard her singing they fell in love with her. A rich merchant captured and imprisoned her, until the fishermen, drawn by her cries, rescued her. Ever since, armed with a sword and a shield, she has protected the city and its people.

Other versions exist: one links her to the Little Mermaid of Copenhagen, her sister, from whom she parted in the Baltic; another says she helped a prince lost while hunting, who founded the city in her honour.

Legacy

Her image appears on Warsaw's coat of arms, documented from 1390 as a creature with shifting features; the first depiction of a mermaid dates from 1622. She appears in statues such as the one in the Old Town, by Konstanty Hegel, whose original was moved to the Museum of Warsaw in 2008 and replaced by a copy. Another statue, from 1939 in Powiśle by Ludwika Nitschowa, survived the Second World War almost undamaged. She also appears in the Sejm, on the Palace of Culture and Science and in a mural by Pablo Picasso on Obrońców Street, and her coat of arms is displayed in Berlin.

In art

Trivia

A statue that was never built. The original plan for the Powiśle statue was a 20-metre glass sculpture on a pillar in the middle of the Vistula channel. For financial reasons it was abandoned in favour of a more modest version surrounded by fish and seagulls.
A monument that survived the war. The Powiśle statue was not on the German list of objects to be dismantled, and it was one of the few that came through the Second World War without major damage.
