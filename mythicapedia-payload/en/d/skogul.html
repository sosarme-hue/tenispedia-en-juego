valkyrie of Norse mythology

Skögul

Skögul is one of the valkyries of Norse mythology, the warrior maidens who chose the fallen in battle and brought them to Valhalla. Her name appears mostly as part of kennings, the metaphors of skaldic poetry.

The myth

Sources list her among the valkyries in the Völuspá and the Grímnismál, where she sometimes seems to be a separate being from Geirskögul, while in other passages both names refer to the same figure.

In the Hákonarmál, preserved in the Heimskringla, Odin sends Skögul and Göndul to choose which king should come to his halls. After a bloody battle, King Haakon dies, and Skögul tells him he will reach Valhalla, where he will join the einherjar and prepare to fight the wolf Fenrir.

Her name also appears in kennings: "board of Skögul" for shield and "fire of Skögul" for sword. A runic inscription from Bergen mentions her, suggesting she was an important figure, although beyond these sources little is known about her.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, Grímnismál and Skáldskaparmál of the Poetic Edda, and the Hákonarmál within Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla.

Trivia

Her name in metaphors. In skaldic poetry, "board of Skögul" referred to the shield, an example of how a valkyrie's name could become a poetic image.
A name that might mean tower. According to Hilda Ellis Davidson, if Skögul means "high-towering", the name might refer to the gigantic size of these beings.
