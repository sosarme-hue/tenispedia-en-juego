Hindu goddess of the Tamil Shaiva tradition

Jyoti

Jyoti is a goddess of the Tamil Shaiva tradition, sometimes seen as the embodiment of the feminine principle and closely linked to Murugan. She matters above all for her connection to the vel, the sacred spear of the god.

The myth

Two myths tell of her birth. In the first, she emerges from Shiva's halo and is a physical manifestation of her father's grace. In the second, she is born from a spark on Parvati's forehead, just as Murugan is born from six sparks on Shiva's forehead.

According to this story, the goddess fashions a weapon from her light and presents it to her son as a vel, and with it Murugan destroys the asura Surapadman. She is also believed to be the flame held by her father Nataraja, a form of Shiva.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped mainly in her vel form in many Murugan temples, where she is believed to be present in a formless state (arupa). The name Saravanabhavai comes from her association with her brother. In the Shodasam, attributed to the sage Agastya, she appears with her parents in the ajna chakra, located on the forehead.

Ancient sources: The Shodasam, a devotional composition attributed to the sage Agastya, describes her as born from the pranava pillar of Om.

Trivia

Two births. Accounts of her origin do not agree: in one she comes from Shiva's halo, in another from a spark on Parvati's forehead.
Regional identifications. In North India she is identified with the goddess Jwalaimukhi, and in some regions with Rayaki, linked to the Vedic term raka.
