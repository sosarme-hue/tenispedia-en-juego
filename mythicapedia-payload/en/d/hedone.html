Greek goddess of pleasure

Hedone

Hedone is the personification of pleasure in Greek mythology. According to the tradition described in the reference texts, she is the daughter of Eros, love, and Psyche, the soul. Her name, meaning ‘pleasure’, gave English the word ‘hedonism’, and the concept became central to ancient philosophy.

The myth

The myths about Hedone are sparse. Tradition presents her as the daughter of Eros and Psyche and links her mainly to sensual pleasure and enjoyment. No specific episodes from her life are recorded, so she is known mostly through her name and her place in the family line.

Among her mythological opposites are Pain (Algos) and Fatigue (Ponos). Her Roman equivalent is Voluptas, who does appear in Roman art, whereas few images of Hedone survive.

Worship and religion

There is no evidence that Hedone was ever worshipped as a deity.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the word ‘hedonism’, the doctrine that treats the pursuit of pleasure and the absence of pain as the goal of life. Ancient philosophers debated the concept. Aristotle counted pleasure as one of the two components of the emotions, alongside pain, and held that pleasure reveals a person’s character. Epicurus applied it mainly to physical pleasures and set it apart from terpsis, a pleasure that is always virtuous. The Stoics viewed it with suspicion, since they saw it as contrary to nature and reason. Martin Heidegger later read Aristotle’s hēdonē as a movement of the soul from which tranquility arises.

Ancient sources: The reference texts do not cite ancient sources that name her as a deity; the concept appears in the writings of Aristotle and Epicurus.

Trivia

From the name to ‘hedonism’. The Greek word for pleasure is the root of ‘hedonism’ and ‘hedonist’, terms that today describe the pursuit of pleasure as a way of life.
An emotion with two sides. Aristotle placed pleasure and pain as the two components of the emotions, and held that well-directed pleasure reflects a virtuous character.
