Japanese god of fire and blacksmiths

Kagutsuchi

Kagutsuchi is the god of fire in Japanese mythology, son of Izanagi and Izanami. His birth caused the death of his mother and, according to the tradition, marks both the end of the creation of the world and the beginning of death.

The myth

In the myth, Kagutsuchi was born while Izanami was giving birth to the last deities of creation. The burns he caused her at birth led to her death. Izanagi, overcome with grief and rage, drew his sword, Ame no Ohabari, and killed his son.

Deities were born from the blood that dripped from the blade. Those from the tip, falling onto rocks, were Iwasaku, Nesaku and Iwatsutsunoo; from the edge came Mikahayahi and Takemikazuchi, and from the hilt came Kuraokami and Kuramitsuha. More gods arose from the corpse, each from a different part of the body. The sources differ on the details: the Kojiki describes beheading and dismemberment, while the Nihon Shoki says Izanagi cut him into three pieces. In one popular version, his eight body parts became volcanoes. The Engishiki adds that, dying, Izanami bore the water goddess Mizuhanome to calm the fire god should he turn violent.

Worship and religion

Kagutsuchi is revered as the god of fire and of blacksmiths. Offerings are made to him at the Akiba Shrine in Hamatsu (Shizuoka Prefecture), the Atago Shrine in Kyoto, and the Odaki Shrine in Yasugi (Shimane Prefecture).

Legacy

A group of American B-29 bombers in the Second World War was nicknamed the Kagutsuchi Group and took part in the bombings of Tokyo. In popular culture he appears as the creature Child in the anime Mai HiME, as the central figure of the game Shin Megami Tensei III: Nocturne, and as the antagonist of Persona 4 Arena Ultimax. He is also a Yu-Gi-Oh! trading card, and in Rurouni Kenshin the Sword of Shishio is named after him for producing fire through friction.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Japan's two chief mythological chronicles, tell of his birth and of the dismemberment of his body in differing versions, and the Engishiki records the episode of Mizuhanome.

Trivia

A wartime nickname. A group of B-29 bombers in the Second World War was nicknamed the Kagutsuchi Group and took part in the bombings of Tokyo.
Fire by friction. The Sword of Shishio in Rurouni Kenshin takes its name from Kagutsuchi because it produces fire when dragged across the ground, thanks to the oil that coats it.
