Armenian god of fire

Mher

Mher is the Armenian god of fire, son of the supreme god Aramazd and identified with the Greek Hephaestus. His figure shows how ancient Armenian religion absorbed and reshaped traditions from its neighbours, such as that of the Iranian god Mithra.

The myth

According to Armenian tradition, Mher is the son of Aramazd and the brother of Anahit and Nane. His link to fire led to his identification with Hephaestus, the Greek god of smiths and craftsmen, but detailed accounts of his deeds are not preserved in the available sources.

His name derives from Mithra, the Iranian god of light in Zoroastrian tradition, also pronounced Mihr or Mehr. The ancient Armenian word mehean, meaning «pagan temple», is connected with one of these variants.

Worship and religion

Mher's worship centred on Derjan, a district of Upper Armenia now in eastern Turkey, where the village of Bagayarich had a temple dedicated to him. After the fall of Urartu, Armenians identified the Urartian war god Ḫaldi with Mher, perhaps because both were linked to fire and war. Mher belonged to a triad with Aramazd and Anahit. When Christianity became the state religion in 301 AD, the church adopted many pagan rites: the fire festival of Trndez, with pagan roots, is still celebrated in February, the month once dedicated to Mher.

Legacy

His legacy survives mainly in language and names: the seventh month of the Armenian calendar, called Mehekan, and the word mehean. It also appears in personal names such as Mihran, Mihrdat and Mehruzhan.

Trivia

A month named after him. The seventh month of the Armenian calendar was dedicated to either Mher or Mithra and was called Mehekan.
A fire festival that survives. The Christian fire festival of Trndez, with pagan roots, is still celebrated in February, the month once dedicated to Mher.
