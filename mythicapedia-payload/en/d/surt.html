fire giant of Norse mythology

Surtr

Surtr, also written Surt, is a jötunn and the guardian of Muspelheim, the fiery realm that existed before time began. He becomes a central figure at Ragnarök, when he sets the world ablaze in battle against the gods.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Surtr rules the fire giants of the south and guards the frontier of Muspelheim, one of the two primordial realms alongside Niflheim. His sword is said to shine more brightly than the sun.

In the Völuspá of the Poetic Edda, a völva foretells that Surtr will come from the south at Ragnarök with flames and a bright sword, and will fight Freyr, described as the slayer of Beli. Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda expands the story: Surtr leads the sons of Muspell across the collapsing Bifröst bridge, and hurls fire over the earth after Heimdallr and Loki have killed each other. Only Líf and Lífthrasir survive, hidden in the wood of Hoddmímir.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Surtsey, a volcanic island that emerged off Iceland's south coast in 1963, meaning 'Surtr's Island'. Richard Wagner included him in his opera Götterdämmerung. He appears as a demon in the Megami Tensei and Persona series, as an ally in God of War: Ragnarök, and as an enemy in Assassin's Creed Valhalla.

Ancient sources: Völuspá, Vafþrúðnismál, Fáfnismál and Fjölsvinnsmál in the Poetic Edda, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

Surtsey island. The volcanic island Surtsey rose in 1963 off Iceland's south coast and takes its name from the giant.
Luminous sword. Snorri describes his sword as shining more brightly than the sun, making it central to the final battle.
