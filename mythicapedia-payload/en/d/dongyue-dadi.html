Chinese god of sacred Mount Tai

Dongyue Dadi

Dongyue Dadi, the Great Emperor of the Eastern Peak, is the divine guardian of Mount Tai, one of China's five sacred mountains. He is regarded as judge of the dead and ruler of the underworld, and his figure moved from mountain worship into Daoism and Buddhism.

The myth

Tradition held that Mount Tai was a gathering place for the spirits of the dead, so its god came to be seen as the sovereign of the underworld, who fixed the lifespan and status of humans. Some accounts make him a grandson of the Jade Emperor.

In the novel Investiture of the Gods, he appears as Huang Feihu, a Shang general who defected to Xiqi and died in battle against Zhang Kui during the Zhou campaign. Daoist sources describe him as a descendant of Fuxi or Pangu, granted the title Taihua Zhenren. Another version makes him the son of Jinlun Wang Shaohai and Milun Xianü.

Worship and religion

Under the Han dynasty, emperors performed the Feng Shan ceremony on the mountain, considered essential to receive the mandate of heaven. His cult lost prominence in the Ming dynasty to that of Bixia Yuanjun. Today his birthday falls on the 28th day of the third lunar month, and in Taiwan the ritual of storming the city is performed to rescue a dead person's soul from the underworld.

Legacy

As judge of the underworld he shaped the Buddhist concept of Diyu, where he is called Taishan Fujun and placed among the ten kings of hell.
