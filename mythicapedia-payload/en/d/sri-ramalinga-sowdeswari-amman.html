Hindu goddess of the Devanga community

Sri Ramalinga Sowdeswari Amman

Sri Ramalinga Sowdeswari Amman is a popular Hindu folk goddess, venerated as a regional form of Mahadevi and as the kuladevata (family deity) of the Devanga people, a weaving community of southern India. She matters because she links a warrior goddess to the identity and history of this community.

The myth

According to the Devanga Purana, the sage Devala is the progenitor of the Devanga community. An incarnation of Manu, he is said to have emerged from Shiva's third eye to make clothing and teach weaving. Returning with threads obtained from Vishnu, he was attacked at midnight by five rakshasas. Devala tried to fight them with Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra and failed, so he prayed to Shakti, who appeared riding a lion, killed the rakshasas and dyed his threads with their multicoloured blood. The goddess was given the name Sowdeshwari, meaning 'goddess of brightness'.

In a tradition linked to the Ramayana, Rama wished to worship Shiva with a large lingam after the war in Lanka. When Hanuman was delayed, Sita made a small one from sand, and the lingam Hanuman brought was given to Devala, who named it Ramalingam. From then on he worshipped Ramalingam together with Sowdeshwari, hence the compound name.

Worship and religion

The rituals blend North and South Indian traditions. Devotees build a small cart-like structure of jaggery, roof it with sugarcane and decorate it with betel leaves, and offer a special turmeric called pandaram as prasada. The alagu seva ritual, performed only by the Devanga, commemorates a story in which King Devadas wounded himself with swords until the goddess appeared, and the veerakumars strike their bare chests with holy swords. The Devanga regard every amavasya as a holy day, stopping weaving to pray.

Ancient sources: The reference texts cite the Devanga Purana and the Ramayana as sources for its stories.

Trivia

Sword ritual. In alagu seva, the veerakumars strike their bare chests with holy swords in a ritual dance, and the pandaram turmeric is applied to the wounds to prevent infection.
Many names. She is invoked under several names, such as Banashankari, Renugadevi, Chandi or Chowdamba, and is worshipped in three forms: Shakti, Chamunda and Jyothi.
