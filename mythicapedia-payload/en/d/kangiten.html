Japanese Buddhist god of bliss, counterpart of Ganesha

Kangiten

Kangiten, also called Shōten or Binayaka, is a Japanese Buddhist deva and the Buddhist counterpart of the Hindu god Ganesha. He is venerated mainly in the Shingon and Tendai schools and is widely popular as a deity who grants wishes and brings good fortune.

The myth

In early Buddhist texts, Vinayaka appears as a malignant being, a creator of obstacles and leader of a class of demons who hindered practice. According to tradition, esoteric Buddhism transformed him: once soothed with mantras and offerings, he becomes the practitioner's ally and a guardian who removes impediments.

Later, a distinction arose between the vinayakas and their lord, seen as a manifestation of Avalokiteshvara or Vairochana. In Japan, the deity was first depicted as a single figure, and by the late Heian period the embracing couple, both with elephant heads, became popular.

Worship and religion

He is considered extremely effective, granting whatever is asked of him, and is said to watch over those with a karmic bond to him from conception. Because he is still bound by passions, he is also seen as demanding and quick to punish those who offend him. Unlike Ganesha, his images are kept hidden in temples, rituals are performed by qualified monks out of public view, and lay devotees are discouraged from venerating him at home. Devotees also call him Tenson.

Legacy

The embracing couple, unique to East Asia, has puzzled scholars, who have found no clear origin for it. It is compared with the yab-yum iconography of Nepal and Tibet, though it is markedly different from those images.

Ancient sources: Buddhist texts such as the Mahavairochana Tantra and the Mahamayuri Vidyarajni Sutra, with a note by the monk Yijing, and the Hindu Mahabharata and Manava-Grihyasutra.

Trivia

Many names. Kangiten is known by names such as Shōten, Binayaka, or Tenson, used depending on context and devotion.
Dual form. His embracing two-bodied form, with elephant heads, is known as Sōshin Kangiten.
