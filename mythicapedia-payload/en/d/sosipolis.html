child deity of the Eleans, savior of the city

Sosipolis

Sosipolis, whose Greek name means 'savior of the city', was a child deity and local spirit of the Eleans in Elis. He was the son of the goddess Eileithyia and was worshipped mainly at Olympia, where he had a sanctuary.

The myth

According to the legend recorded by Pausanias, the Arcadians invaded Elis and the Eleans prepared to fight. A woman then appeared among them with a baby at her breast, saying that a dream had told her to offer the child as a champion to the army. The commanders placed the naked boy before the ranks and, as the attack began, he turned into a serpent. The Arcadians fled in terror and the Eleans won. For this reason they called him Sosipolis and built a sanctuary on the spot where the marvel happened.

Worship and religion

At Olympia his sanctuary lay within that of Eileithyia. The outer part was public, while the inner part, dedicated to Sosipolis, could be entered only by the priestess. The Eleans chose an old woman each year, who had to live in chastity, bring water for the god's bath and set before him barley cakes kneaded with honey, covering her head and face with a white veil. He was invoked in oaths of great importance. There is no evidence in the sources that his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, is the main source that relates his legend and describes his cult at Olympia.

Trivia

A boy turned into a serpent. In the legend, the child who gives the deity his name turns into a serpent in the middle of battle, and this prodigy is what routs the Arcadian army.
Depiction in Elis. In his other shrine, next to that of Tyche in Elis, he was painted as a boy in a star-spangled robe holding the horn of Amaltheia.
