Samian Sibyl

The Samian Sibyl was a priestess who presided over an oracle of Apollo near the temple of Hera on the Greek island of Samos. She belongs to the tradition of the sibyls, prophetesses whose name gave English the word “sibyl.”

The myth

Ancient sources give her different names: Eratosthenes calls her Phemonoe or Phyto, a name linked to wandering, especially of the mind. The Suda states that the Erythraean Sibyl was also known as Samian, and Pausanias says that the Erythraean Sibyl spent most of her life on Samos.

According to the Neoplatonist philosopher Porphyry, her home may have been the cave associated with Pythagoras.

Worship and religion

She presided over the oracle of Apollo near the temple of Hera; the sources do not describe her rituals in detail.

Legacy

In later Christian tradition she was credited with prophesying the birth of Jesus in the stable. Her name gave rise to the word “sibyl” through Latin.

Ancient sources: Eratosthenes, Pausanias and the Suda, which record her name and her life on Samos.

Trivia
