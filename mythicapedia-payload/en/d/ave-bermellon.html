sacred bird of the south in Chinese mythology, symbol of summer and fire

Vermilion Bird

The Vermilion Bird, known in Japan as Suzaku, is one of the Four Symbols of the Chinese constellations, linked to the south, the element of fire and summer. It matters because it shaped the sky of Chinese tradition and, later, the layout of Japan's ancient capitals.

The myth

In Chinese tradition the Vermilion Bird is a red creature resembling a pheasant, its plumage in many shades of vermilion and its body perpetually wrapped in flames. It is described as noble and elegant, and as particular about what it eats and where it perches.

Its presence in the sky is vast: it spans seven of the twenty-eight Chinese constellations, about a quarter of the star map. According to the star lore, Gemini forms its left wing, the right wing lies over Hydra and Crater, the head and body belong to Hydra, and the tail to Corvus.

In the older beliefs, which predate the Qin dynasty, it was thought to carry the souls of the dead up to heaven. Later generations credited it with granting immortality, and Taoism absorbed it as a protective creature under the name Lingguang Shenjun.

Worship and religion

Under the Han dynasty its symbolic meaning grew, linked to the Five Phases theory that tied it to fire and the south. In Japan, ancient capitals such as Fujiwara-kyō, Heijō-kyō and Heian-kyō were laid out on these beliefs, each quarter of the city dedicated to one of the four symbols. A great gate called suzakumon stood on the southern side, and from it ran an avenue serving as the north-south axis. The Suzaku Gate of Heijō Palace in Nara still survives. Over time its worship declined and was partly replaced by the Four Heavenly Kings of Buddhism, though its symbolic use lasted.

Legacy

In Japan the name Suzaku gave names to gates and avenues, and the avenue dedicated to it remains an important thoroughfare in Kyoto, now called Senbon. In modern popular culture it appears in Final Fantasy XIV, in the Beyblade series as the bit-beast Dranzer, and in the manga Kagurabachi. In haiku its names designate summer.

Ancient sources: The oldest texts that record it are the inscriptions from the tomb of the Marquis Yi of Zeng, from the Warring States period, where it appears among the Four Symbols of the constellations.

Trivia

A lacquer box. An object from the tomb of the Marquis Yi of Zeng records the names of the twenty-eight constellations and the four symbols, including the Vermilion Bird.
A phoenix, but not quite. Although often confused with the fenghuang, the Chinese phoenix, they are distinct creatures: the fenghuang rules the birds and is associated with the empress.
