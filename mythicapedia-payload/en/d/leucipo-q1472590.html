young man of Phaistos in Greek myth

Leucippus

Leucippus is a young man from Phaistos, on Crete, who according to myth was born a girl and became a boy through the will of the goddess Leto. His story is one of the best-known Greek accounts of a change of sex.

The myth

According to the myth, Leucippus was born in Phaistos to Lamprus and Galatea. Since her husband wanted a son, seers advised Galatea to give the baby a masculine name and tell him it was a boy. As puberty approached, hiding the child's sex became impossible, and the mother prayed at the sanctuary of Leto. The goddess took pity on them and turned the girl into a boy.

Worship and religion

The people of Phaistos surnamed Leto Phytia and held the Ecdysia, an initiation feast in which young men publicly stripped off their feminine peploi, commemorating Leucippus's transformation. Brides and bridal couples of the city would lie beside a statue of him before their weddings.

Legacy

Some modern scholars cite him as a transgender male figure in Greek mythology.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis included his story in his Metamorphoses; his case parallels that of Iphis, told by Ovid.

Trivia

A festival named after him. The Ecdysia of Phaistos took its name from the Greek verb meaning to undress, referring to the garment Leucippus could remove after his transformation.
A wedding ritual. Brides and bridal couples of Phaistos would lie beside his image or statue before their weddings.
