Slavic god of the earth, waters and the underworld

Veles

Veles, also called Volos or Weles, is a deity of Slavic mythology linked to the earth, the waters, cattle and the realm of the dead. He matters for his possibly very ancient origins and for his role as guardian of the boundary between the living and the dead.

The myth

Tradition describes him above all as a god of cattle, and from this role his ties to wealth and to the underworld are said to follow. That underworld was imagined as a pasture where souls grazed. As guardian of the line between the living and the dead, he is also associated with forests, hunting and wild animals. Comparisons with other traditions attribute to him powers over magic, knowledge, divination and poetry.

Veles was also a guarantor of oaths. Anyone who broke one was punished by turning yellow, most likely a metaphor for some form of jaundice. His traits partly resemble those of Varuna, Velnias, Hermes or Hades, though they are not identical.

His rivalry with the thunder god Perun has long been regarded as one of the central myths of Slavic religion. Around 1974, Ivanov and Toporov proposed that Perun defeated Veles, whom they identified with a dragon or serpent, but the idea was criticized soon after for lack of evidence and is no longer part of scholarly consensus. Toporov later revised his view and linked Veles with Varuna rather than with the demon Vṛtra.

Trivia

A yellow oath. According to tradition, breaking an oath sworn in Veles's name made the offender turn yellow, an image probably describing a condition such as jaundice.
A disputed dragon. The identification of Veles with a dragon or serpent comes from a 1974 hypothesis that was criticized soon after and is now set aside by scholarly consensus.
