Mesopotamian god of the underworld and vegetation

Ninazu

Ninazu was a Mesopotamian god linked to the underworld, to snakes and to the cycles of vegetation, who later also took on the traits of a warrior god. His Sumerian name means “lord healer,” and he was honoured as the patron of the cities of Enegi and Eshnunna.

The myth

According to the stories, Ninazu was closely bound to the underworld goddess Ereshkigal. Some traditions made him her son, others her husband, and others simply a member of the same class of underworld deities. Another genealogy named him a son of Enlil and Ninlil, a version that began in Eshnunna, and some texts also linked him with a “Great Lord,” possibly identified with Gugalanna.

In the myth “How Grain Came to Sumer,” Ninazu and his brother Ninmada give grain and flax to humanity. His best-known child is Ningishzida, whom he fathered with his wife Ningirida. He was also regarded as “king of the snakes,” and he was invoked in incantations against snakebite.

Worship and religion

His main cult centre was Enegi, a city between Ur and Uruk, where his temple was called Egidda, the “sealed house.” He was also worshipped in Eshnunna, in the temple Esikil, the “pure house,” until the local god Tishpak gradually replaced him. His cult declined after the Old Babylonian period, although in Ur he kept a number of worshippers even in the Achaemenid era.

Legacy

Mesopotamian astronomers associated Ninazu with the planet Mars, which they called “the Elam star.” The sources also describe him as armed with two maces, although no artistic representations of him have been identified with certainty.

Ancient sources: Sumerian and Akkadian texts such as “How Grain Came to Sumer,” “Enlil and Ninlil,” the Anzû myth, the Gula hymns, and the god lists An = Anum and the Ugarit trilingual list.

Trivia

King of the snakes. He was called “king of the snakes” and was invoked in incantations against snakebite, many of them written in Elamite and Hurrian.
A star in his name. In astronomical texts, the planet Mars was associated with Ninazu and was known as “the Elam star.”
