Japanese goddess of grass and fields

Kaya-no-hime

Kaya-no-hime is a goddess in Japanese mythology, protector of grasslands, fields and herbs. Her name gave the kaya shrub its name.

The myth

According to tradition, she was born to Izanagi and Izanami during the creation of the deities. She married the mountain god Ōyamatsumi, with whom she had eight children, grouped in four pairs.

Some versions of her origin story also make her the mother of Konohanasakuya-hime.

Worship and religion

In the past, people prayed to her before cutting wood or reeds for building, since the plants she favored supplied materials for houses and furniture. Today she is still honored at Tarumaeyama Shrine in Hokkaido, alongside Ōyamatsumi and Kukunochi, and at Kayatsu Shrine in Aichi, the only place in Japan where tsukemono, pickled vegetables, are offered to her.

Legacy

Her name remains tied to the kaya shrub, from which she takes it.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki mentions her, though the sources consulted do not detail the passage.

Trivia

A unique offering. At Kayatsu Shrine in Aichi Prefecture, she is offered tsukemono, pickled vegetables, a practice not recorded at any other site in Japan.
Protector of materials. People prayed to her before cutting wood or reeds, since those plants provided the materials for building houses and making furniture.
