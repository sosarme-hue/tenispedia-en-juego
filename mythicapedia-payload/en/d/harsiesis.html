Egyptian god, form of Horus, son of Isis

Harsiesis

Harsiesis, 'Horus son of Isis', is a form of Horus in Egyptian mythology, a falcon god especially venerated in the Nile Delta. He was regarded as the avenger of his father Osiris against his uncle Seth.

The myth

According to the myth, Harsiesis was born posthumously to Osiris and raised by Isis in the Jemmis marshes of the Delta, where his mother hid him from every danger. The goddess Wadjet, protector of Lower Egypt, watched over the place in the form of a serpent.

As an adolescent, with a fiery temper and little gratitude, he cut off Isis's head out of carelessness, because he pitied Seth. Thoth gave the goddess a replacement head, that of a cow.

Worship and religion

In the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom, the king is equated with the son of Osiris and Hathor, and Harsiesis is said to have performed the Opening of the Mouth ritual on the deceased pharaoh, so that he would keep his faculties in the afterlife.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom (spell 466a), which mention him when equating the king with his figure.

Trivia

A cow's head. After he cut off Isis's head, Thoth gave her a cow's head as a replacement.
Mistaken for Harpocrates. He is often shown as a child with a finger to his mouth, which makes him easy to confuse with Harpocrates, though the two are told apart by their myths.
