young man of Crissa, hero of Greek mythology

Alcyoneus

Alcyoneus is a handsome young man from Crissa, a Greek city near Delphi, who was nearly sacrificed to a monster and was saved by Eurybarus. His story is a classic example of the myth in which a hero defeats a beast.

The myth

According to the tradition, the monster Sybaris terrorised Delphi from Mount Cirphis, preying daily on young people and animals. Apollo told the people that the threat would end if they sacrificed a youth at her cave. Alcyoneus, the only son of Diomus and Meganeira, was chosen by lot and led off in procession, crowned with garlands.

On the way he met Eurybarus, coming down from Curetis in Aetolia, who fell in love with him at first sight. Learning of the prophecy, Eurybarus offered to take his place: he took the garlands, put them on himself and entered the cavern. He dragged the monster out of her den and hurled her from the crags. Sybaris cracked her head on the rocks and was never heard of again.

Legacy

The name Alcyoneus shares a root with alkuōn, the Greek word for a mournful-singing seabird, usually the common kingfisher. From that root come the kingfisher genus Halcyon and the English phrase "halcyon days", meaning calm, peaceful times.

Ancient sources: His story survives in the writings of the second-century author Antoninus Liberalis, who may be drawing on an older work by Nicander of Colophon.

Trivia

An Olympic parallel. Euthymus, an Olympic victor from Locri in the early fifth century BC, saved a woman from Temesa from a ghostly monster and then married her.
A female monster. In this myth the ogre is female and the victim male, a rare variant of the dragon-slayer tale.
Menestratus and Cleostratus. In Thespiae, Menestratus took the place of his lover Cleostratus before a dragon, wearing a breastplate covered in fish hooks.
