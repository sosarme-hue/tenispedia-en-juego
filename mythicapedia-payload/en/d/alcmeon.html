Greek hero, son of Amphiaraus and a matricide pursued by the Erinyes

Alcmaeon

Alcmaeon is a hero of Greek mythology, son of the seer Amphiaraus and Eriphyle, and one of the Epigoni who took Thebes. His story, marked by matricide and madness, provided material for many ancient tragic and epic poets.

The myth

According to tradition, Eriphyle accepted the necklace of Harmonia, given to her by Polynices, in exchange for persuading her husband Amphiaraus to join the expedition against Thebes. Amphiaraus, knowing he would die, asked his sons to kill their mother once they grew up. When the Epigoni prepared a second campaign, an oracle promised victory if they chose Alcmaeon as leader. He at first refused, but Eriphyle, who had also received Harmonia's robe, urged him to join. In the battle he killed Laodamas, son of Eteocles.

After the fall of Thebes he learned the truth and killed his mother on the advice of an oracle of Apollo. For this he was pursued by the Erinyes and driven mad. Purified by King Phegeus in Psophis, he married the king's daughter and gave her the necklace and robe of Harmonia. A famine in the land led him to consult the oracle again, which told him to seek a land that did not exist when he committed the crime: a newly formed delta at the mouth of the Achelous river. There he married Callirrhoe, daughter of the river god, who asked him for the necklace and robe. Alcmaeon returned to Psophis on a pretext, and Phegeus and his sons killed him. His sons by Callirrhoe avenged him, in a bloody manner according to the myth.

Worship and religion

After his death he was worshipped as a hero. According to Pindar, he had an altar in Thebes, and his tomb was shown at Psophis, surrounded by sacred cypresses. At Oropus, where Amphiaraus and Amphilochus were honoured, he did not receive the same honours for being a matricide.

Legacy

He appeared in a statue at Delphi and on the Chest of Cypselus. He gave his name to lost tragedies by Euripides, Sophocles and other authors.

Ancient sources: Pindar, in his eighth Pythian Ode, names him as the first to pass through the gates of Thebes; Apollodorus and Pausanias recount his wanderings, and Thucydides records the episode of the Achelous delta.
