Maya god of the forest and the hunt

Yum Kaax

Yum Kaax is a god in the Yucatec Maya tradition, lord of wild vegetation and guardian of the animals that hunters pursue. His name means 'lord of the forest', and he is notable mainly because for a long time he was wrongly identified as an agricultural deity or as the maize god.

The myth

According to tradition, Yum Kaax embodies the power of wild nature and watches over its animals. He is regarded as the owner of all game, so hunters call on him before venturing into the forest.

In the stories, the god can appear to a hunter in an instant and possesses songs that ensure a successful hunt. His gifts also include arrows that return magically to the archer after being shot. Maya sorcerers claimed to have learned from him many secrets for improving the hunt.

Worship and religion

Traditional farmers invoke him because, as lord of wild vegetation, he can protect their maize fields from the same wild nature he represents. They offer him the first fruits of their fields, which were cleared from the forest. Hunters also ask him for success, and some performed rituals such as lying beneath a garment stretched over four stakes in order to find deer more easily. Before setting out, offerings of balché or chocozacan were made to him, and to locate prey a copal candle was lit and its flame followed.

Trivia

A mistaken identity. For years he was described as the maize god, a label applied to god E of the Maya codices, but this identification is considered false.
The deer rite. According to the advice recorded, to claim a private hunting ground one had to kill a deer, drag its head across the area and bury it with the liver.
