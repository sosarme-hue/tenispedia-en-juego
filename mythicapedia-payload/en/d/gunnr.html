Valkyrie in Norse mythology

Gunnr

Gunnr, also spelled Guðr, is one of the Valkyries of Norse mythology, the maidens who were said to decide the outcome of battles and carry off the slain. Her name means "battle", and it is among the earliest attested names of these figures.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, the Valkyries served Odin, and Gunnr was one of those who, together with Róta and the young norn Skuld, always rode out to choose the warriors who would fall in combat and to decide the outcome of fights.

Snorri's Prose Edda names her in this role, while the Völuspá includes her in a list of Valkyries alongside Hildr and Göndul. Gunnr also appears in the Darraðarljóð. Yet no individual traits or episodes of her own are recorded; she appears almost always as part of a group.

Legacy

Gunnr was a feminine given name among the Norse peoples, and its modern forms Gun and Gunn are still used as first names in Scandinavia.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Völuspá, Snorri's Prose Edda, the Darraðarljóð, and the Rök Stone, where her name forms part of a kenning for wolf.

Trivia

A name for the wolf. On the Rök Stone, the earliest known mention of her name appears in a kenning, a poetic circumlocution that calls the wolf "the horse of Gunnr".
