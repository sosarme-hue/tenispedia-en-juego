Shinto god of hot springs, healing and sake

Sukunabikona

Sukunabikona is a tiny Shinto deity linked to hot springs, healing, magic and the brewing of sake. He matters because he explains the sacred character of several springs and shrines, and because he appears beside Ōkuninushi in the making of the land.

The myth

According to tradition, Sukunabikona was a son of Kami-musubi, one of the creator deities, yet he was so small that he is described as a dwarf. Ōkuninushi found him at Miho Bay in a little boat made from a plant pod. Since the child would not give his name, a toad suggested asking Kuebiko, the god of agriculture, who recognised him as Kami-musubi's son. The goddess confirmed it, and the two joined forces to finish the land of Izumo.

In another episode, Sukunabikona fell ill, and Ōkuninushi bathed him in the waters of Dōgo. After a nap he woke cured and danced on a rock, leaving footprints that can still be seen. He is also credited with remedies and protective spells. In the end, the tale says, he left the world by climbing a grain of millet, which flung him into Tokoyo no Kuni.

Worship and religion

He is venerated at shrines such as Oarai Isosaki-jinja, at Mount Mitake and on Awashima, where Empress Jingū is said to have promised a larger shrine in return for protection. Sacred stones linked to him are kept in Noto Province, and the Dōgo baths keep his healing waters in memory.

Legacy

The asteroid 10725 Sukunabikona is named after him, and the archaeal parasite Sukunaarchaeum was provisionally named for the deity.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki records a banquet toast by Empress Jingū that credits Sukuna with the creation of sake.

Trivia

Footprints in stone. Tradition holds that the footprints he left dancing after his cure are still visible on a rock at Dōgo.
Possible origin of the hina doll. Jingū is said to have carved a doll in his likeness, which may be the origin of the hina doll.
