Greek god of fruit and seasons

Carpus

Carpus is a minor figure in Greek mythology, linked to fruit and the seasons of the year. He appears either as one of the Horae or as a young son of Zephyrus, the west wind.

The myth

Ancient sources present Carpus in two ways. As a Hora, he belongs to the goddesses who personify the seasons. Pausanias names him alongside Thallo as the two Horae worshipped in Athens, while other accounts list three Horae: Auxo, Carpo and Thallo.

As a male figure he appears mainly in late sources. According to Servius, he was the son of Zephyrus and a nymph Hora. He fell in love with the youth Calamus, son of the river god Meander. While the two competed in a swimming race, the wind drove Carpus under and he drowned. Grief-stricken, Calamus asked Zeus to let him die too, and both were transformed: Calamus into a reed that whispers in the wind, and Carpus into the fruit of the earth, which dies and is reborn each year. Some scholars suggest his mother was Chloris, the wife of Zephyrus.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes a temple in Athens where two Horae, Carpo and Thallo, were worshipped, alongside an altar to Dionysus Ortos. The sources give no details of specific rites, nor whether his cult survived.

Legacy

His name comes from the Proto-Indo-European root kerp-, which gives English words such as harvest, carpet, excerpt and scarce, though some reached English through Latin.

Ancient sources: Servius calls him the son of Zephyrus, and Nonnus, in his fifth-century epic Dionysiaca, tells the story of Calamus; Pausanias mentions his cult in Athens.

Trivia

An apple on the Sosias vase. In Greek art he appears with other Horae and fertility deities, holding summer fruit. On the Sosias vase, around 500 BC, he holds a red fruit.
A sigh in the wind. According to the story, the rustling of reeds in the wind was interpreted as Calamus lamenting Carpus.
