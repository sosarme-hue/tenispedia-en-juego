bodhisattva of space in Chinese, Japanese and Korean Buddhism

Ākāśagarbha

Ākāśagarbha, whose name means "space womb" or "store of space", is a bodhisattva revered in Chinese, Japanese and Korean Buddhism, linked to the great element of space (ākāśa). He is counted among the eight great bodhisattvas and is believed to have the power to purify transgressions.

The myth

According to tradition, his wisdom is as boundless as space, which is why his name is often rendered as "boundless space treasury" or "void store". Some accounts describe him as the twin brother of the bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha, with whom he appears briefly in the sutra devoted to Kṣitigarbha's original vows.

The best-known story comes from Japan. Kūkai, founder of the Shingon school, is said to have chanted his mantra as a young acolyte under a famous monk. During this practice he had a vision in which Ākāśagarbha told him to travel to Tang China to study the Mahāvairocana Abhisaṃbodhi Sūtra. There he trained under Huiguo and, on his return, founded the esoteric tradition of Buddhism in Heian Japan.

Worship and religion

His most widespread practice is the Gumonjihō rite, associated with improving one's memory of the teachings. His mantra, known as the "morning star mantra", is chanted mainly in the Shingon school. Tradition holds that the Five Great Ākāśagarbhas, manifestations of the Five Wisdom Buddhas, bring benefits such as good health, and they are placed in the traditional mandala.

Legacy

His veneration remains alive in Japan's Shingon school, where his mantra is part of the daily practice of monks and lay believers.

Ancient sources: Numerous Chinese Mahāyāna sutras in the Taishō canon make him a central figure, including the Ākāśagarbha Bodhisattva Sūtra (T.405) and several dhāraṇī texts.
