Punga

Punga is a supernatural being in Māori mythology, regarded as the ancestor of sharks, lizards, rays and every deformed or ugly creature. His name, related to the word for an anchor stone, links him to the sea.

The myth

According to tradition, Punga is a son of Tangaroa, god of the sea. When Tāwhirimātea, god of storms, waged war on his brothers after they separated Rangi and Papa, Punga's two sons, Ikatere and Tū-te-wehiwehi, had to flee. Ikatere escaped into the sea and became the ancestor of certain fish, while Tū-te-wehiwehi took refuge in the forest and became the ancestor of lizards.

Accounts of his parentage vary. Some versions make him a son of Rangi-potiki and Papatūānuku and a twin of Here. In a version of the epic of Tāwhaki that White attributes to the Ngāti Hau tribe, he is a brother of Karihi and Hemā, while in many other versions he is a cousin of Karihi and Tāwhaki.

Trivia

Anchor stone. Across tropical Polynesia, related words refer to a coral stone that was also used as an anchor.
Hema and Punga in Hawaii. In some Hawaiian stories, Hema and Punga are sons of Aikanaka and Hinahanaiakamalama.
