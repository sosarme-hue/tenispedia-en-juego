divine bird of Hindu mythology, mount of Vishnu

Garuda

Garuda is a divine winged being in Hindu mythology, best known as the mount of the god Vishnu, and also found in Buddhism and Jainism. Shown as an eagle-like bird with a human body, he has become a symbol of royal power and state authority in India, Indonesia and Thailand.

The myth

According to tradition, Garuda was born from an egg, the son of the sage Kashyapa and his wife Vinata. His brother Aruna, charioteer of the sun god Surya, hatched from another egg. The story begins with a rivalry: Kadru, Kashyapa's other wife, had a thousand serpent sons, the nagas, while Vinata impatiently broke one of her eggs. Aruna, still unfinished, cursed her to slavery until her other son freed her.

To rescue his mother, Garuda had to obtain amrita, the nectar of immortality. He defeated the gods, Indra included, and seized the vessel. However, through a trick by Indra, the nectar was stolen on its way to the nagas, and Garuda devoured the serpents. Vishnu, impressed by his courage, asked him to serve as his mount, and the two have been shown together ever since. In the Mahabharata, his wings are so vast that they can block out the sun, and his strength rivals that of Indra.

Worship and religion

Garuda is central to Vaishnavism, the tradition centred on Vishnu, but he also appears in Shaivism, in texts such as the Garuda Tantra and in some Shiva temples. His image is found in shrines dedicated to Vishnu, and early examples survive in the 6th-century Badami cave temples. His iconography varies between texts: he may have two or four arms, and one text describes his body in bands of colour, from golden yellow at the feet to black above the neck.

Legacy

Garuda appears on early Indian coins as a symbol of kingship and dharma, and he is part of the official emblems of India, Indonesia and Thailand. The Indonesian national airline is called Garuda Indonesia, and the Indian Air Force named its special operations unit the Garud Commando Force. He also appears in popular culture, from Digimon and Saint Seiya to Final Fantasy and Yu-Gi-Oh!.

Ancient sources: Rig Veda, Shatapatha Brahmana, Mahabharata and the Puranas, among other Sanskrit texts.

Trivia

National emblem. Indonesia and Thailand both have Garuda in their coats of arms, and in India his figure appears on the insignia of an Army guards brigade.
Garuda and the Phoenix. In Malaysia Garuda is considered the local version of the Phoenix, a bird that could be reborn from its ashes.
A vegetarian bird. In Japan, where he is known as Karura, the story goes that Garuda devoured snakes until a Buddhist prince taught him vegetarianism, and he then brought back the vipers he had eaten.
