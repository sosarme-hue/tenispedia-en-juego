Lachesis

Lachesis is one of the three Moirai, the goddesses of fate in Greek mythology, and according to tradition the one who measures the thread of each human life. Her name means roughly «she who allots by lot», and in Rome she was identified with Decima.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Lachesis and her sisters Clotho and Atropos were daughters of Nyx, Night, though later in the Theogony they appear as daughters of Zeus and Themis. In Plato's Republic she is the daughter of Ananke, Necessity, and she presides over the souls about to choose their next life, assigning them lots and laying out the human and animal lives among which they may choose.

Clotho spins the thread of life, Lachesis measures it with her rod and, in some accounts, settles a person's destiny during that measuring. Tradition says the three appear within days of a child's birth to fix its fate. Whoever she touches with an ill-omened hand dies young, and her thread grows more frail as the years pass. Lachesis also has a role in the birth of Asclepius, whom she attended as midwife in Apollo's temple. Seneca reports the saying that the Fates lead those who are willing and drag those who are not, as if everything happened by necessity.

Legacy

The asteroid 120 Lachesis, in the main belt, takes her name, as does a genus of pit vipers sometimes called bushmasters, which includes the largest venomous snake in the Western Hemisphere.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony), Plato (Republic), Cicero (De natura deorum) and Seneca (Letters to Lucilius) mention her and her sisters.

Trivia

Midwife of Asclepius. According to some accounts, Lachesis assisted at the birth of Asclepius in Apollo's temple, while Apollo eased the pains of the mother, called Aegle or Coronis.
Rhodes as her lot. She is said to have been sent by Zeus to mediate an agreement with Helios, and the island of Rhodes was assigned to her as her lot.
