Scottish folklore creature

Water bull

The water bull (tarbh-uisge) is a creature of Scottish folklore, related to the Manx tarroo ushtey and the Irish tarbh-uisce. It lives in moorland lochs and can change shape.

The myth

According to tradition, the water bull lives in moorland lochs and is seen mostly at night. It can take human form and, although sometimes it is a black, malevolent beast, at other times it is friendly and even helpful. It is said to breed with ordinary cows, and its calves have tiny ears, as if cut in half.

In a tale from Islay, an old woman later identified as a witch advised keeping such a calf apart and raising it on the milk of three cows. Years later, a young herdswoman by a loch was courted by a handsome man with seaweed in his hair, a sign that he was a water horse. As she fled towards the farm, the witch shouted for the herdsman to free the water bull; the two creatures fought until they fell into the sea. The water horse never returned, and the next day the bull's remains were found.

Trivia

Knife-eared calves. The folklorist John Gregorson Campbell described the calves of these unions as knife-eared, with ears that looked sliced off.
Silver bullets. The water bull was said to be killed only by silver. In one story, a farmer loaded his musket with silver sixpence coins to hunt it.
