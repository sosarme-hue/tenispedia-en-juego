river god of Sicily

Crinisus

Crinisus is the god of the Sicilian river that bears his name, linked to the founding of Segesta by Trojan survivors. Mostly preserved in Roman texts, his story makes him the father of a founder hero.

The myth

According to the most widespread version, the river god Crinisus, in the form of a dog, lay with a Trojan woman, and their son Acestes founded Segesta and named it after his mother.

Lycophron, in the Alexandra, tells how King Laomedon exposed three daughters of Phoenodamas to sea monsters. They reached Sicily, where one of them became the bride of the river, who had taken the shape of a dog, and their son founded three cities. Virgil changed the names of the god and the hero, while Dionysius of Halicarnassus gives a different account, in which the father is a young nobleman who marries the mother after reaching the island.

Worship and religion

Aelian reports that the people of Segesta honoured Crinisus in human form, alongside other local deities.

Legacy

Coins of Segesta, minted from 475 BCE, show a dog and a woman's head, images traditionally linked to Crinisus and the city, though scholars still debate the connection. Karl Galinsky suggests the woman may be Artemis.

Ancient sources: Lycophron's Alexandra, Virgil's Aeneid, Servius's commentary, Aelian's Varia Historia and Dionysius of Halicarnassus's Roman Antiquities.

Trivia

Dog coins. From 475 BCE, Segesta's coins showed a dog on one side and a woman's head on the other. They have been linked to the myth, but their exact meaning remains unclear.
A god in dog form. In the earliest version, the god takes the form of a dog to unite with the Trojan woman, a detail that gives its name to the scene on the coins.
