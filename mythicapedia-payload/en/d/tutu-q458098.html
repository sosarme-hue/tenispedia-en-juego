Mesopotamian god, patron of Borsippa

Tutu

Tutu is a god of ancient Mesopotamia, patron of Borsippa, a city near Babylon. The meaning and origin of his name remain uncertain.

The myth

Few narratives belong to Tutu alone; his figure is understood mainly through his merging with other gods. In the Enuma Elish, Tutu is one of the names given to Marduk, linked to Babylon's role in ritually restoring damaged divine statues.

God lists identify him with Marduk and with other deities such as Asalluhi or Šazu. According to Wilfred G. Lambert, during the first millennium BCE his name came to refer to Nabu instead, though Francesco Pomponio points to only a single neo-Assyrian text that links him to Nabu.

Worship and religion

Before he was merged with Marduk, he was worshipped at the Ezida temple in Borsippa. Hammurabi recognised him as the city's tutelary god, and Nebuchadnezzar II invoked him in his inscriptions to legitimise his rule and the renewal of the temple.

Legacy

He was associated with Jupiter and, possibly, with Mercury, through his links to Marduk and Nabu.

Ancient sources: The building inscriptions of Nebuchadnezzar II, the Enuma Elish, an incantation from the Muššu'u series and the so-called Bird Call Text.

Trivia

A name in a ruler. Tutu appears in the name of Puzur Tutu, a governor from the Ur III period.
Renewing statues. Babylon was a centre for restoring and ritually reviving damaged divine statues, a role linked to the name Tutu.
