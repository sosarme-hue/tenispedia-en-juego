hero of the Fianna in Irish mythology

Oscar

Oscar is a hero of the Fianna, the warrior band of Fionn mac Cumhail in the Fenian Cycle of Irish mythology. Son of Oisín and Niamh, he is remembered above all for his death at the Battle of Gabhra.

The myth

In the Fenian Cycle, Oscar was the son of Oisín, himself a son of Fionn mac Cumhail, and of Niamh. He had a brother, Finn, and a sister, Plúr na mBan; his bride was called Malvina. Possibly added late to the cycle, he nonetheless became popular and served his grandfather as one of the Fianna's warriors.

In the tale Bruidhean Chaorthainn (The Fairy Palace of the Quicken Trees), Oscar cuts off the head of Sinsar, King of the World, in the battle at the ford of the River Shannon.

His death is told in Cath Gabhra (The Battle of Gabhra). There the increasingly corrupt Fianna face the army of the High King of Ireland, Cairbre Lifechair. Oscar delivers the fatal blow to Cairbre, but as his last act Cairbre mortally wounds him with his steel chains. Oscar's death makes Fionn weep for the only time in his life and marks the end of the Fianna's strength.

Ancient sources: The medieval Fenian Cycle tales Bruidhean Chaorthainn and Cath Gabhra.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Oscar is linked to the old word oscara, usually translated as deer friend or god friend.
A single tear. According to the tales, Fionn weeps only once in his life, when Oscar dies.
