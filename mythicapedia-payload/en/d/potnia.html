Greek title meaning "mistress" or "lady" for goddesses

Potnia

Potnia is an ancient Greek word meaning "mistress" or "lady" that served as an honorific title for several goddesses. It goes back to the Mycenaean period, when it referred to a great goddess of nature, birth and death.

The myth

Potnia is not a goddess with stories of her own, but a title applied to several female divinities. In Mycenaean tablets written in Linear B, dated to about 1450-1300 BC, she appears alone or with epithets that name her domain, such as Potnia of the horses or Potnia of the labyrinth at Knossos. In the classical period the title was given to Demeter, Artemis, Athena, Persephone and the earth goddess Gaia.

According to tradition, the Mycenaean Potnia was a goddess of nature responsible for the yearly birth of a divine child, and her male companion was Wanax, a title usually applied to Poseidon.

Worship and religion

In Mycenaean worship, Potnia had an important shrine near Pylos, and her traits survived in the Eleusinian cult, where it was said that the mighty Potnia had borne a strong son. In Arcadia a nameless goddess called Despoina was venerated, later identified with Persephone.

Legacy

The word survived in classical Greek and gave rise to despoina, "mistress of the house". Some scholars propose that the name of Demeter comes from a fusion with this root, and Karl Kerenyi linked Persephone with the mysterious "mistress of the labyrinth".

Ancient sources: The Linear B tablets from Pylos and Knossos are its earliest witnesses, and Homer calls Artemis potnia theron, mistress of the animals, in the Iliad.
