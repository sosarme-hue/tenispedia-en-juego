Irish heroine of Arthurian legend

Isolde of Ireland

Isolde of Ireland is a heroine of Arthurian legend, the lover of the knight Tristan and the wife of King Mark of Cornwall. Her tragic love story, of Celtic origin, is one of the great medieval romances.

The myth

According to tradition, Isolde is the daughter of the Irish king Anguish. She heals Tristan of a wound, and later he returns to Ireland to ask for her hand on behalf of King Mark. On the voyage back, the two accidentally drink a love potion and fall in love.

Their affair ends when Mark banishes Tristan. In the prose version, the lovers meet again, but the king catches them beneath a tree and stabs his nephew in the back; at Isolde's request, Tristan embraces him so tightly that he dies. Other versions, such as those by Béroul and Thomas of Britain, differ in the details.

Legacy

Richard Wagner drew on this character for his opera Tristan und Isolde.

Ancient sources: The medieval poems of Béroul, Thomas of Britain and Gottfried von Strassburg, as well as the prose Tristan.

Trivia

A Dublin place name. A popular legend holds that Isolde may have lived in Chapelizod, a suburb of Dublin, from which the place takes its name. Her remains are also believed to lie there.
A name with meaning. Her name is not of Irish origin. According to the sources, it derives from Adsiltia, which in Anglo-Saxon means 'the one who is gazed upon'.
