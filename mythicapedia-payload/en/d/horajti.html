Egyptian sun deity of the horizon

Khor-akhti

Khor-akhti, "Horus of the Two Horizons", was the Egyptian personification of the sun on the horizon. He is attested from the First Dynasty and was later merged with the sun god Ra as Ra-Horakhty.

The myth

According to tradition, Khor-akhti was in charge of the Fields of Rushes in the Duat, which later came under Osiris. He was regarded as a son of Serket and the husband of Iusaas. As a form of Horus, he was linked to the falcon, to war and to hunting, which gave the pharaoh a model of majesty and power.

The Pyramid Texts contain a "Hymn of Ascension" describing the pharaoh, identified with Horus, arriving in the celestial realm of Aaru. Khor-akhti was called "Lord of Dendera", although he eventually lost his primacy there to Hathor.

Worship and religion

He was venerated at Letopolis, Abu Simbel, El Derr and Amada. As Ra-Horakhty he was honoured alongside Ra at Heliopolis, Karnak and Deir el-Bahari. He was depicted as a falcon wearing the solar disk and a cobra on the forehead, called the uraeus.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts include the "Hymn of Ascension", which speaks of Horus, although it does not name Khor-akhti directly.

Trivia
