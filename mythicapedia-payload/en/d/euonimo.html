eponymous hero of Greek mythology

Euonymus

Euonymus is an eponymous hero of Greek mythology, described as a son of Gaia by either Uranus or the river Cephissus depending on the version. His name, Eúōnymos, means 'well-named' and works as a euphemistic epithet, and it gave its name to the Attic deme of Euonymeia.

The myth

According to tradition, Euonymus was a son of Gaia, the Earth, though accounts differ on his father: some make him a son of Uranus, others of the river Cephissus. Surviving sources do not credit him with great deeds; his importance lies mainly in his lineage and in the places that bear his name.

His daughter Aulis gave her name to the city of Aulis in Boeotia, linking the memory of the family to the geography of central Greece.

Legacy

His name survives in place names: the Attic deme of Euonymeia bears his name, and the Boeotian city of Aulis takes the name of his daughter.

Trivia

A euphemistic name. Euonymus means 'well-named' in ancient Greek. It is a euphemistic epithet, a way of naming someone with kind words.
A city named after a daughter. The Boeotian city of Aulis took its name from Aulis, daughter of Euonymus, according to tradition.
