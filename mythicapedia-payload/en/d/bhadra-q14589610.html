Krishna's queen, princess of Kaikeya

Bhadra

Bhadra is one of the eight principal queen-consorts (Ashtabharya) of Krishna, according to the Bhagavata Purana, which counts her as his eighth wife and sometimes as his seventh. A princess of Kaikeya, she is a figure of Hindu tradition tied to Krishna.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, Bhadra was the daughter of King Dhrishtaketu and a cousin of Krishna. Her five brothers gave her in marriage to him, although in another account she chooses him herself in a svayamvara.

She and Krishna had ten sons. When the god died and his line came to an end, she and other queens threw themselves onto his funeral pyre in an act of sati. The Bhagavata says all of them did so; the Mahabharata names only four, Bhadra among them.

Legacy

In Telugu literature, Dr K. V. Krishna Kumari wrote the book Bhadra Kalyanam (The Marriage of Bhadra), which she dedicated to Satya Sai Baba on his 80th birthday. In it, Bhadra is identified with Mahalakshmi, wife of Vishnu, and her marriage to Krishna is presented as a union of beauty, devotion and love.

Ancient sources: Bhagavata Purana, Mahabharata (Mausala Parva), Vishnu Purana and Harivamsa, which do not name her among the eight queens.

Trivia

A queen without a name. The Vishnu Purana and the Harivamsa do not mention Bhadra in the list of eight queens, referring to her only as the daughter of Dhrishtaketu or the princess of Kekeya.
