Norse god of the day and dawn

Dellingr

Dellingr is a god of Norse mythology, the father of Dagr, the personified day. According to the Prose Edda, he was the third husband of Nótt, the night, although some manuscripts make him the husband of Jörð, the earth. Scholars read him as the personification of dawn or twilight, and his name survives in an English surname and place name.

The myth

According to the Norse sources, Dellingr is the father of Dagr, the day. In the Poetic Edda, the poem Vafþrúðnismál records a contest between Odin, disguised as Gagnráðr, and the giant Vafþrúðnir, in which the giant states that Dellingr is the father of the day, while night was born of Nörvi.

In the Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson describes him as the third husband of Nótt. The couple had Dagr, who inherited his father's bright and beautiful looks. Odin placed mother and son in the sky so that they could ride across it with their horses and chariots every twenty-four hours. Some manuscripts, however, make Dellingr the husband of Jörð and the father of Dagr with her.

His name also appears in the Hervarar saga, in riddles that repeat the phrase "Delling's doors". In Hávamál, a dwarf named Þjóðrœrir sings before those doors, which some interpret as a warning that the sun is coming and creatures of the night must return home.

Legacy

The English surname Dallinger has been linked to Dellingr. The place name Dalbury, south of Derbyshire, is thought to derive from Dellingeberie, a form that also comes from his name.

Ancient sources: Poetic Edda (Vafþrúðnismál, Hávamál, Fjölsvinnsmál, Hrafnagaldr Óðins), Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and the Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks.

Trivia

A riddle about a leek. In the riddle that Odin, in disguise, poses in the Hervarar saga, the phrase "Delling's door" is part of the clues. The answer is the leek, whose head is fixed in the ground while its leaves seek the sun.
A surname and a village. The English surname Dallinger and the place name Dalbury, in Derbyshire, have been linked to the name Delling, showing that his name survived in the English language.
