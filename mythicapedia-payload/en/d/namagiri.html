Hindu goddess, local form of Lakshmi at Namakkal

Namagiri

Namagiri, also called Namagiri Mahalakshmi, is the local form of the goddess Lakshmi, consort of Narasimha at the Namakkal temple in Tamil Nadu.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Narasimha, an avatar of Vishnu, killed the asura king Hiranyakashipu. Lakshmi, Vishnu's consort, grew angry that Narasimha had not forgiven the asura and withdrew into penance.

Hanuman was carrying a shaligrama stone and asked the disguised Lakshmi to hold it until he returned. She could not bear its weight and set it down, where it grew into a hill. Narasimha appeared and made his abode in the statue there. Lakshmi then returned to Vishnu's chest, and the story says her place can still be seen in the main image.

Worship and religion

The fifteen-day Panguni Uthiram festival, held in March or April, carries the images of the presiding deities through the streets on a chariot, and the sacred marriage of the deity is performed during it. The temple remains active, drawing devotees and visitors.

Legacy

The mathematician Srinivasa Ramanujan credited the formulas that came to him in visions to Namagiri, his family goddess. The saint-composer Purandaradasa, regarded as the grandfather of Carnatic music, wrote a Kannada song here in praise of the deity.

Trivia

Formulas in visions. Ramanujan said the goddess proposed mathematical formulas to him in dreams and visions, which he then had to verify.
A song from the temple. Purandaradasa composed his famous song "Simha Rupanada Sri Hari, Namagirishane" here.
