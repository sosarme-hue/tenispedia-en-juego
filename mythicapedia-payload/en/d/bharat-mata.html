Mother India, national personification of India as a mother goddess

Bharat Mata

Bharat Mata, or Mother India, is the national personification of the country as a mother goddess with Hindu iconography. Born in Bengali literature and art in the late nineteenth century, she became a central symbol of Indian nationalism and of the independence movement.

The myth

According to the modern tradition that shaped her, Bharat Mata is not an ancient deity of sacred texts but a nineteenth-century literary and artistic creation. The word Bharat appears in ancient Hindu literature to designate the Bharatha people, and its use for a wider territory dates to the first century BCE. The idea of a mother of all Indians appears in the satirical work Unabimsa Purana, by Bhudev Mukhopadhyay, first published anonymously in 1866.

In 1873 Kiran Chandra Banerjee staged the play Bharat Mata, set during the Great Bengal famine of 1770. A couple prays to the goddess, who emboldens them to rebel against the British Raj. Bankim Chandra Chatterjee later popularised her in his novel Anandamath (1882), which also introduced the slogan Vande Mataram. Her image blends features of the goddesses Durga and Kali.

Worship and religion

From the 1930s her image entered religious practice. The first Bharat Mata temple, in Varanasi, was inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi in 1936; it has no idols, only a marble relief map of India. Other temples include one in Haridwar, founded in 1983, and one in Kolkata, inaugurated in 2015. Some Muslims and Sikhs have objected to her depiction as a divine human figure, given their belief in one God.

Legacy

Her image spread through Indian art, posters, magazines and calendars. The phrase "Bharat Mata ki Jai" (Victory to Mother India) often accompanies her picture and serves as a patriotic cry. She appears alongside the tricolour flag, which replaced the saffron flag when she came to symbolise Indian nationalism.
