protective sea creature of Japanese folklore

Shachihoko

The shachihoko is a creature of Japanese folklore with the head of a tiger or dragon and the body of a carp covered in dark scales. It became famous as a guardian of temples and castles against fire.

The myth

According to the tradition, the shachihoko lives in the cold northern ocean. Its broad fins and tail always point toward the sky, and its dorsal fins bristle with numerous sharp spines. It is said to swallow vast quantities of water and hold it in its belly, and to summon clouds and control the rain.

The animal's origins are linked to the chiwen, a creature from a Chinese tale of the Han dynasty that appears on a relief brick of the Eastern Han, where it crowns the roof ridge to shield the building from fire. In Japan it is known as shibi and grew popular during the Nara and Heian periods. Other scholars suggest it derives from the makara, the sea monster of Vedic and Hindu mythology, which in Southeast Asia decorates temple lintels and arches.

Worship and religion

Rather than an object of devotion, the shachihoko was a protective figure in architecture. Temples and castles placed it on the ridges of their roofs, often in male and female pairs at each end, and it was believed its connection with water could quench fires. Its size varied according to the wealth of the builder. From the Muromachi period it was also associated with the authority of the feudal lord.

Legacy

Today it survives mainly as ornamental roof decoration. Its odd posture gave rise to the expression shachihoko-baru, meaning to be stiffly dignified. The famous golden kin shachi of Nagoya Castle are plated in 18-karat gold. In popular culture, the mascot of the 2026 Asian Games, Honohon, represents a shachihoko, and it appears as a playable character, Kinshachi, in Kemono Friends 3. The Rainmaker weapon in the Splatoon series is directly based on it.

Trivia

A word that also means orca. The character 鯱 is a kokuji, a kanji created in Japan, and when read shachi it can mean orca.
Gold in Nagoya. The male kin shachi at Nagoya stands 2.62 metres tall and weighs 1,272 kilograms, and its gold plating is 0.15 millimetres thick.
