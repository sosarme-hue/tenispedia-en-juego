Norse mythological dwarf

Gandalf

Gandalf is a dwarf of Norse mythology who appears in the list of dwarves in the Völuspá, from the Poetic Edda. He is best known today because J. R. R. Tolkien took the name for his wizard in fiction.

The myth

In the Norse tradition, Gandalf is one of the dwarves named in the Völuspá and also appears in the Prose Edda. His name combines Old Norse gandr, a magical entity often linked to wands and monsters, and álfr, meaning elf. It is read as a protective spirit who wields a magical wand, perhaps a wand of power. The sources give him no deeds of his own; he survives mainly as a name in a catalogue of beings.

Legacy

Tolkien gave the name to his wizard Gandalf, one of the best-known characters in fantasy literature. In early drafts, the name belonged to the leader of the dwarf company, who would later become Thorin Oakenshield.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá of the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda.

Trivia

A name also used for a king. In the Heimskringla, the name also appears as that of a Norse king, showing it was used for both beings and rulers.
From dwarf to wizard. Tolkien first used the name for a dwarf and only later gave it to his wizard, who became the most famous character to bear it.
