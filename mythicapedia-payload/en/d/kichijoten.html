Japanese goddess of fortune and prosperity

Kichijōten

Kichijōten, also called Kisshōten, is the Japanese form of Śrīmahādevī, a devi of Mahayana Buddhism who is especially honored in East Asian Buddhism as a protector deity. She is the goddess of fortune, prosperity, happiness, fertility and beauty, and in ancient and medieval Japan her worship was tied to the luck of children.

The myth

Her presence in Buddhism comes from the Golden Light Sutra, where she introduces her mantra and vows to protect the monks who uphold and teach it. Buddhist tradition links her origin to the Hindu goddess Lakshmi, which is why her Chinese alternative name Gongdetian means 'Meritorious Devi'.

In iconography she usually holds a cintamani jewel in her left hand and forms a mudra with her right, sometimes wearing a headdress and accompanied by a phoenix (fenghuang or hōō). In China she is regarded as the sister of Bishamonten, one of the Four Heavenly Kings, while in Japan she sometimes takes a place among the Seven Gods of Fortune, replacing Jurōjin or Fukurokuju.

Worship and religion

Her image is enshrined in the main halls of most Chinese Buddhist monasteries alongside other deities, and her dharani is among the Ten Small Mantras recited at morning services. In ancient and medieval Japan she was worshipped for luck and prosperity, especially on behalf of children, and was also considered the guardian goddess of geishas.

Legacy

She appears in the Japanese group of the Seven Gods of Fortune, where in some late eighteenth-century illustrated editions she replaces Fukurokuju.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Golden Light Sutra, a Mahayana Buddhist text.
