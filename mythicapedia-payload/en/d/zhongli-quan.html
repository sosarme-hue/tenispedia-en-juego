Chinese Taoist deity, one of the Eight Immortals

Zhongli Quan

Zhongli Quan is one of the Eight Immortals of the Chinese Taoist pantheon, a former Han dynasty general who became a deity after attaining immortality. He is regarded as the patron of soldiers and of abundance, and his feather fan, said to raise the dead and turn stones into gold, makes him one of the most cherished figures of the group.

The myth

According to tradition, Zhongli Quan was born in Yantai, the son of a second-rank prince, and bright beams of light filled the room at his birth. He rose to become a general of the Han dynasty and was sent to fight the Tibetans. After a defeat he became a fugitive and wandered through wild, mountainous country.

In a dense forest he met a foreign priest who guided him to a village where, he was told, the master Donghua lived. Later an old man dressed in white deer skins and carrying a plum-wood staff asked why he had not found lodging. Zhongli sensed that this was no ordinary man, and learned from him the mysteries of immortality and the secret of extracting the elixir. When he turned to say goodbye, the old man had vanished.

After reaching the state of Lu, birthplace of Confucius, he retired to the mountains and discovered the elixir while meditating. He fell ill and went to the Hengzhou mountains to die, but when his disciples opened the tomb, it was empty. Another account says his ascent came when a wall of his hermitage collapsed, revealing a jade vessel that carried him to the shimmering cloud of the immortals.

Worship and religion

Taoists celebrate his birthday on the fifteenth day of the fourth month of the Chinese calendar, and he is regularly venerated as the patron of silversmiths.

Legacy

He is often shown bare-chested and drinking wine, holding a crucible for alchemy and a peach of longevity. He appears in the television series Jackie Chan Adventures, where he is revealed to have sealed away Xiao Fung, the Wind Demon.

Trivia

First words. According to some stories, his first words were that his feet had wandered through the purple palace and that his name was recorded in the capital of the Jade Emperor.
Zhongli on television. In Jackie Chan Adventures he was first shown as the one who sealed away Dai Gui, the Earth Demon, until it was revealed that he had actually sealed the Wind Demon.
