Hindu goddess, wife of Shukra

Jayanti

Jayanti is a goddess in Hindu mythology, daughter of Indra and wife of Shukra, the guru of the asuras and the god of the planet Venus. Her story is an episode in the rivalry between gods and demons.

The myth

According to the Matsya Purana, the asuras had been nearly wiped out by the devas, so Shukra went to Mount Kailash to perform austerities and regain his power. Fearing the asuras would regroup, Indra sent his daughter Jayanti to serve the sage and do whatever would benefit the gods. She attended him faithfully for a thousand years, until Shiva granted Shukra what he sought. Grateful, the sage married her, and they spent ten years together, hidden from the world behind a magical veil. Their union produced Devayani, whom the Devi Bhagavata Purana assigns instead to another wife, Urjjasvati.

Worship and religion

In Cambodia, where she is known as Kimira Devi, she is the protector of Fridays and is honoured during the Cambodian New Year when the first day falls on a Friday, as she is believed to descend from heaven to watch over the land for a year.

Legacy

Her story has blended with Buddhism and Cambodian folk culture, where she takes on local traits, such as a buffalo as her mount.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Matsya Purana, the Brahmanda Purana, the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the Padma Purana and the Vayu Purana, and the Mahabharata mentions her daughter Devayani.

Trivia

Two mothers for Devayani. The Mahabharata considers Jayanti the mother of Devayani, but the Devi Bhagavata Purana assigns her to Urjjasvati, another wife of Shukra.
A buffalo mount. In the Cambodian version she appears with only two arms, holding a harp and a khanda, and her mount is a buffalo.
