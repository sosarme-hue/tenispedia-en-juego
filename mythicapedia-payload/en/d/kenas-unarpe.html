Ainu evil goddess, trickster and blood-sucker

Kenas-unarpe

Kenas-unarpe is an evil goddess (wen kamuy) of the Ainu people, known as a trickster and blood-sucker. She is described as a tangle-haired mountain hag or as a monstrous bird.

The myth

According to an origin myth, the creator god Kotan-kar-kamuy tried to invent fire-making with poplar tools; the failed ones turned into evil deities, and from the wooden mortar came Kenas-unarpe. Another version says she was born from the mire where discarded axes rotted. She uses bears, or animals turned into bears, to attack horses and people, and she disguises herself as Hasinaw-uk-kamuy, the goddess of the hunt, to fool hunters. She is also accused of drinking the blood of the wounded and of hunters sleeping in the wilderness.

Worship and religion

She was invoked during difficult childbirths through a sly ritual that called on an evil god under the protection of a good one; the spirit had to be bribed with a swig of blood.

Trivia

Squirrel turned into a bear. In a tale from the Saru region, the creature believed to be a bear turned out to be a dead squirrel, showing that Kenas-unarpe could transform one animal into another.
Owl form. According to some traditions, she takes the form of a horned, owl-like bird, which some informants identified with the short-eared owl.
