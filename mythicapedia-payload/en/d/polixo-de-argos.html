queen of Rhodes, wife of Tlepolemus

Polyxo of Argos

Polyxo is a figure of Greek mythology, the wife of the hero Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, and queen of the island of Rhodes. Her story matters because it explains the Rhodian cult of Helen, whom she puts to death in an act of revenge.

The myth

Polyxo was born in Argos and married Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, with whom she had a son. After his exile for the killing of Licymnius, the couple settled on Rhodes. When Tlepolemus joined the Trojan War, where he had once been a suitor of Helen, he was killed by Sarpedon. Left a widow with an orphaned son, Polyxo held funeral games in his honour, in which children competed and the winners were crowned with poplar leaves.

Years later, according to Pausanias, Helen fled to Rhodes after Menelaus died, seeking shelter with her old friend Polyxo. Polyxo, eager to avenge her husband, caught her while she bathed and sent handmaidens dressed as Erinyes, who seized Helen and hanged her from a tree. In Polyaenus' version, Polyxo and the Rhodians attacked Menelaus' ships with fire and stones and killed a servant dressed as Helen by mistake.

Worship and religion

The Rhodians had a sanctuary to Helen of the Tree (Helene Dendritis), in memory of her hanging. The myth of Polyxo was probably invented to explain this cult.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Polyaenus and Tzetzes record different versions of her story; Tzetzes calls her Philozoe.
