rooster of Norse mythology

Víðópnir

Víðópnir is a golden, bright rooster that, according to the Eddic poem Fjölsvinnsmál, perches at the top of Mímameiðr, a tree with a vast crown often identified with Yggdrasil, the world tree of Norse cosmology. The name appears in no other medieval written source, so the figure is known mainly from this single passage.

The myth

In the poem Fjölsvinnsmál, the young Svipdagr asks the wise Fjölsviðr about the bird sitting on the highest branch of Mímameiðr. The tree bears abundant fruit and its crown spreads over all known lands, and the rooster that crowns it stands out for its golden brightness.

Fjölsviðr answers by revealing its name: Víðópnir. The story adds no further deeds or episodes about the bird, so its role is limited to occupying a prominent place at the top of the world tree.

Some later descriptions vary the image. In certain modern accounts it appears as a falcon perched between the eyes of the cosmic eagle Hræsvelgr, also at the crown, but that image does not come directly from the poem's passage.

Ancient sources: It appears in the poem Fjölsvinnsmál, included in the Poetic Edda, and in no other medieval written source under that name.
