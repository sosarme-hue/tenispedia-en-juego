legendary creature of West African folklore

Ninki Nanka

The Ninki Nanka is a monster from West African traditions, linked above all to the Gambia River and the Mandinka people. It was feared as a dangerous spirit whose mere sight could bring death.

The myth

According to local beliefs, the Ninki Nanka lives in swamps, rivers, dense forests and caves, and it kept people away from the fertile lands of the lower Gambia. Its appearance varies with the people describing it: some imagine a dragon with a crocodile's head and a donkey's body, others a blend of hippopotamus and giraffe, and others a large serpent with a feathered crest or wings capable of breathing fire.

Among its most repeated traits are its deadly stare, compared to that of Medusa, and scales that shimmer in different colours as camouflage. Some tales say its powers come from a gem in its head. Seeing its body was said to foretell a serious illness, while seeing its eyes or crown meant instant death. For this reason, people were advised to carry mirrors to deflect its gaze.

The story spread from one people to another, and some link it to droughts or famines, although there is no direct historical record of such disasters being caused by the creature.

Legacy

It appears in the song "Ninki Nanka" on the album Casamance au clair de lune (1984) by the Senegalese group Touré Kunda. It also features in the fantasy novels Skin of the Sea (2021) by Natasha Bowen and Moon Witch, Spider King (2022) by Marlon James, and in an episode of the second season of Destination Truth.

Ancient sources: No ancient written texts preserve these tales; the tradition is oral. There are colonial-era testimonies, such as a 1906 commissioner's report and a description by Lady Southorn.
