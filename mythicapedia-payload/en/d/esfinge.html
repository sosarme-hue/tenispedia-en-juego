Sphinx

The sphinx is a creature of Greek mythology with the head of a woman, the body of a lion and wings, who terrorised the land around Thebes and killed anyone who failed to solve her riddle. Her image came from Pharaonic Egypt, where sphinxes guarded temples and royal tombs, and in Greece she became a symbol of danger and mystery that remains familiar today.

The myth

According to Hesiod, who is the first to mention her in his Theogony, the sphinx was the daughter of Orthus and of a mother he does not name, who might be the Chimera, Echidna or Ceto. Other authors gave different parentage: Laso of Hermione, Hyginus and Apollodorus make her the daughter of Echidna and Typhon, while others name Typhon and the Chimera. Hesiod considers her a sister of the Nemean lion. Some writers rationalised the myth and presented her as a mortal woman, daughter of Laius or wife of Cadmus.

In the most widespread version, the sphinx settled on Mount Phicium, west of Thebes, and from there she ravaged the countryside, destroying crops and strangling those who could not answer her riddles. Hesiod calls her the «ruin of the Cadmeans». Most authors blame Hera for sending her, though the scholium to the Phoenician Women links her to Laius's crime against Chrysippus; other sources point to Dionysus, Ares or Hades. According to Hyginus, she made Creon an offer that Apollodorus found hard to credit.

Oedipus, a man from Corinth, arrived in Thebes, solved the riddle and killed the sphinx. The exact riddle was not fixed until a late date, and the story grew more elaborate over time. According to Palaephatus, the sphinx was an Amazon wife of Cadmus who rebelled against him.

Worship and religion

In Egypt the sphinx had a religious and political role. The Great Sphinx of Giza, according to most Egyptologists, bears the face of the pharaoh Khafre and dates to around 2600-2500 BC. An inscription of about 1400 BC names three aspects of the sun deity Khepri, Ra and Atum. Many pharaohs had their own faces carved on guardian statues to show their link with the lioness goddess Sekhmet. At Thebes, nine hundred ram-headed sphinxes were built for Amun, meant to hold back evil forces. In Greece, sphinxes flanked temple entrances as guardians. No living cult is associated with them.

Legacy

During the Renaissance the sphinx was revived in European decorative art, spreading through engravings and arabesque designs in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. In France she was popular at the Fontainebleau School and in the late Baroque. Neoclassical and Romantic gardens brought her back, especially in her Greek form linked to Oedipus. In the Decadent movement of the 1890s the motif became tied to the femme fatale. Oscar Wilde wrote the poem «The Sphinx», and James Thomson used a similar beast in «The City of Dreadful Night».

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions her in his Theogony. Apollodorus, Hyginus, Pausanias, Palaephatus, Herodotus, Statius and Euripides also write about her.

Trivia

Emblem of Chios. The ancient city-state of Chios used the sphinx as its emblem on seals and on the obverse of its coins.
A sphinx at Dendera. In March 2023 a limestone sphinx was found at the Dendera temple complex. It has a slight grin and dimples and is thought to be made in the image of the Roman emperor Claudius.
