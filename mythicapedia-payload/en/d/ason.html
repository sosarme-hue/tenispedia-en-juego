father of Niobe in a little-known Greek version

Assaon

Assaon is, in some versions of Greek mythology, the father of Niobe. His story appears in a little-known tale, perhaps of Lydian origin, preserved mainly by Parthenius of Nicaea.

The myth

In the most common version, Niobe is the daughter of Tantalus and the wife of Amphion, queen of Thebes, with twelve or fourteen children. She boasted that she was a better mother than Leto, who had only Artemis and Apollo, and the gods avenged the insult by killing all of her children.

In a very different version, recorded by Parthenius and some scholiasts, Niobe is the wife of the Assyrian Philottus, and Assaon, her father, is consumed by desire for his own daughter, who rejects him every time. Leto punishes Philottus first, who is killed by a bear while hunting. Assaon then invites the twenty Niobids to a banquet and sets them all on fire. Learning of this, Niobe throws herself from a rock or turns to stone or ice, and Assaon, left with his guilt, takes his own life.

Ancient sources: Parthenius of Nicaea, in the Sorrows of Love, and some scholiasts.

Trivia

Lydian origin. Parthenius attributes his version to Xanthus of Lydia, Neanthes of Cyzicus and Simmias of Rhodes, though he follows Xanthus most closely.
Two lines of development. Scholars think the Niobe tales come from a Theban line and an Anatolian one, and that Assaon emerged from the latter.
