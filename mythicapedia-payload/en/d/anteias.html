son of Odysseus and Circe, founder of Antium

Anteias

Anteias is, in some versions of Rome's foundation myth, one of the three sons of Odysseus and Circe, and the supposed founder of the city of Antium (Anteia), which took its name from him. His story echoes the myth of Romulus and Remus.

The myth

According to the version recorded by the historian Xenagoras, Anteias was one of the three sons that Odysseus had with the sorceress Circe. His brothers, Rhomos and Ardeas, were said to have founded two major Roman cities, while Anteias was credited with founding Antium.

Modern scholars read this account as a reflection of the period when Antium, once the capital of the Volsci, was absorbed into Roman identity after the Roman-Volscian wars of the 4th century BCE. Rome sent colonists to the city, and the myth placed it on an equal footing with Rome and Ardea.

Ancient sources: The main ancient source that mentions him is the historian Xenagoras; the available sources cite no other texts.
