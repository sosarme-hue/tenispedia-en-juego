sea deity of Greek mythology

Aegaeon

Aegaeon is an ancient sea god of Greek mythology, belonging to the generation of the Titans, though some traditions place him among the giants. He is often seen as the personification of the Aegean Sea, which is said to have taken its name from him.

The myth

In Homer's Iliad, Aegaeon is only the name mortals use for Briareus, the strongest of the hundred-handed sons of Uranus and Gaia, while the gods call him Briareus. In Hesiod's Theogony, Briareus and his brothers dwell in the depths of Oceanus, which links him with the sea. Ion imagines him as a son of Thalassa, the female personification of the sea.

In the lost Titanomachy, Aegaeon is a son of Gaia and Pontus, the primordial sea. As a dweller of the deep, he fought alongside the Titans against Zeus and the Cronids, and the Pillars of Heracles were said to belong to him. Other versions describe him as a giant whose footsteps caused earthquakes. Ancient authors disagree, and some even claimed that Aegaeon, Briareus and Gyges were the same figure.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, Hesiod's Theogony, the work of Ion, and the lost epic Titanomachy.

Trivia

Pillars of Heracles. According to one tradition, the famous Pillars of Heracles belonged to Aegaeon, placing him at the edge of the known world.
A seismic giant. Some accounts describe him as a giant of immense size whose steps could cause earthquakes.
