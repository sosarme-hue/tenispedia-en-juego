Hindu goddess of shadows, consort of Surya

Chhaya

Chhaya is the Hindu goddess of shadows and the consort of Surya, the sun god. She is considered the shadow or reflection of Saranyu (Sanjna), Surya's first wife, and her story explains the origin of several beings linked to the sky and to justice.

The myth

According to tradition, Saranyu, daughter of the divine craftsman Vishwakarma, gives birth to Surya's twins but, unable to bear his brilliance and heat, leaves him and flees in the form of a mare. To take her place she creates Chhaya, a woman who looks just like her and cares for the house and the children. Surya mistakes Chhaya for his wife and has offspring with her.

The versions differ. In the Rigveda the substitute is called Savarna and is mortal, with no children by Surya. In the Harivamsa, Chhaya is an illusion created by Sanjna, and she bears Savarni Manu and, by implication, Shani. When Chhaya favours her own children and neglects Sanjna's, Yama threatens her by raising his foot. She curses him, and Surya softens the curse. Once the deception comes to light, Surya finds Sanjna and brings her back.

The Puranas add further children, such as Tapti and Vishti. In some versions Surya abandons Chhaya when he learns the truth, while in a later tradition she is forgiven and lives with the family.

Worship and religion

The Markandeya and Vishnudharmottara Puranas direct that images of Surya be shown with Chhaya and his other wives beside him. Shani, son of Chhaya in these accounts, is feared as a graha, a celestial body of astrological influence. The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to Chhaya herself, and it is unclear how far she is venerated today in her own right.

Legacy

Shani, regarded as Chhaya's son, is identified with the planet Saturn in Hindu astrology. Tapti, another of her children in some texts, is the personification of the river that bears her name.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda mentions her earliest form, and she later appears in the Harivamsa and the Markandeya, Vishnu, Matsya and Bhagavata Puranas.

Trivia

A mortal substitute. In the Rigveda, Saranyu's substitute is called Savarna and is mortal, unlike the Puranic versions in which she is a mere shadow.
A raised foot. In the Harivamsa, Yama threatens Chhaya by raising his foot, and she curses him so that parts of his legs fall away and are eaten by worms.
