Aurvandill

Aurvandill is a giant in Norse mythology, husband of Gróa, whose frozen toe Thor flung into the sky to become a star. His name, shared with other Germanic traditions, links him to the light of dawn and the morning star.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda from the thirteenth century, Thor waded north across the river Élivágar carrying Aurvandill in a basket on his back, having brought him out of Jötunheim. One of the giant's toes poked out of the basket, froze and broke off. Thor picked it up, threw it into the heavens, and it became the star known as Aurvandill's Toe.

The episode belongs to a larger story. Thor had a whetstone lodged in his head, and Gróa, a wise woman, sang charms to loosen it. To reward her, Thor told her of the journey and promised that Aurvandill would soon come home. Gróa was so delighted that she forgot her incantation, and the stone never came out. Georges Dumézil read the passage as part of a tale in which the jotnar abduct Aurvandill and Thor frees him. The identity of the star remains uncertain, with Sirius, Venus and Rigel all proposed.

Legacy

In Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum, written in the twelfth century, the father of Amleth is called Horwendillus, a Latinized form of the same name. Scholars also compare Aurvandill with the Old English Ēarendel, a figure linked to the morning star, and the star identified in modern times with Rigel.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál) and Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A whetstone in the head. According to Snorri, the whetstone lodged in Thor's head is still there, which is why it is forbidden to throw a whetstone across the floor, since that stirs it in Thor's skull.
