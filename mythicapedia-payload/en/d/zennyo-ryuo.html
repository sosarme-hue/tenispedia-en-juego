rain-god dragon of Japanese mythology

Zennyo Ryūō

Zennyo Ryūō is a rain-god dragon in Japanese tradition, tied to Buddhism and to rituals for summoning rain. He is best known for appearing during a rainmaking contest held in Kyoto in 824.

The myth

According to Japanese Buddhist tradition, the priest Kūkai made Zennyo Ryūō appear in 824 during a famous rainmaking contest at the Kyoto Imperial Palace, in the Shinsen'en garden. His rival, the priest Shubin, competed by reciting rain sutras during a drought.

Other accounts place the dragon in a cave on Mount Murō in Nara, where he was sometimes called Zentatsu. A record from 937 says that in 778 five monks prayed there for the future Emperor Kanmu, and that a Dragon King of the site vowed to protect the country, earning a court rank. The Kojidan tells how the dragon left a pond in Nara after a palace lady drowned and settled in the cave, where the monk Nittai saw him rise from the ground after entering it.

Some texts show him as a woman with a dragon's tail, or as a lovely lady who asked a monk for a mudra to become a Buddha and revealed a five-coloured claw.

Worship and religion

A small Shinto shrine dedicated to Zennyo Ryūō stands on an island in the Shinsen'en, reached by crossing the Hōsei Bridge. A shrine was also built near the Murō cave where he was last seen, and rain prayers and sutra readings are still offered there, with the belief that a black cloud forms over the cave and rain follows.

Legacy

He appears often in Japanese art, usually as a small dragon and sometimes as a human figure with a dragon's tail.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Konjaku Monogatarishū, the Genkō Shakusho and Kūkai's Goyuigō, as well as in medieval texts such as the Kojidan.

Trivia

A name with several meanings. His name can be written with the character 女 (woman) or with 如, which in Buddhism translates the Sanskrit term tathāgata.
A five-coloured claw. In one story, the lady who reveals herself as Zennyo shows a finger that is a claw more than ten shaku long, giving off a five-coloured light.
