one-eyed giant of Cantabrian mythology

Ojáncanu

The ojáncanu is a cyclops-like giant of Cantabrian mythology, a figure of cruelty and brutality in the mountains of Cantabria. It is considered the most popular being in the folklore of the region.

The myth

According to tradition, the ojáncanu lives in deep caves in the most remote places, their entrances hidden by brush and huge boulders. It has a single eye in its forehead, a voice as deep as thunder, ten digits on each hand and foot, and two rows of teeth. Its weak spot is a single white hair in its mane and beard: whoever blinds its eye and pulls out that hair kills it.

Its misdeeds are constant: it uproots trees, blocks springs, steals sheep, kidnaps young shepherdesses, destroys bridges and drags boulders onto the pastures. It feared flying toads and owls; if a flying toad touched it, it died, unless someone used a green hazel leaf smeared with fox blood. Newborns were protected with holy water. Some versions also tell of kindly ojáncanos, one born every hundred years, who warned people when the wicked ones were coming.

Legacy

It reached children's culture: the TVE programme Los lunnis de leyenda dedicates a song to it.

Trivia

A plant remedy. According to tradition, a green hazel leaf smeared with fox blood was the only way to survive if a flying toad touched the ojáncanu.
Kindly giants. Some versions speak of good ojáncanos, born one every hundred years, who could even be stroked by humans.
Enemy of the anjanas. The anjanas, good Cantabrian fairies, are the only beings the ojáncanu fears.
