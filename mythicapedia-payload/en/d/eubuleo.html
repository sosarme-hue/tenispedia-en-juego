Greek god of the Eleusinian Mysteries

Eubuleus

Eubuleus is a deity of the Eleusinian Mysteries whose name means good counsel. He is known mainly from devotional inscriptions and from a few artworks that show him holding a torch.

The myth

The stories disagree. In the Orphic tradition he was a son of Disaules and brother of Triptolemus, and he advised Demeter when she searched for her daughter Persephone, abducted by Hades. In the Eleusinian version he was a swineherd who was swallowed by the earth with his pigs at the moment Hades seized the goddess. Diodorus Siculus makes him a son of Demeter and father of Karme, while other sources call him a son of Zeus.

Worship and religion

During the Athenian Thesmophoria, live piglets were thrown into pits dedicated to Demeter and Kore, recalling that episode. At Eleusis he appears in the First Fruits Decree alongside Demeter, Triptolemus, Theos and Thea, and he is invoked in the Orphic tablets.

Legacy

A sculpted head found in the Eleusinian Ploutonion has sometimes been attributed to him, although it may represent Triptolemus. He is also identified with the torchbearer on the Lacrateides relief and, according to some scholars, on the Regina Vasorum vase.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus, the Orphic gold tablets, the scholia to Lucian and the Eleusinian First Fruits Decree. The Homeric Hymn to Demeter does not mention him.

Trivia

Pigs at the Thesmophoria. The Athenians threw live piglets into sacred pits, and attendants later retrieved the remains, mixed them with seeds and placed them on altars.
Grandfather of Britomartis. Diodorus Siculus makes him the father of Karme and therefore grandfather of the Cretan goddess Britomartis.
