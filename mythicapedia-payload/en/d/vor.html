Norse goddess of wisdom and truth

Vör

Vör is a goddess of Norse mythology associated with wisdom and the ability to uncover what is hidden. She barely appears in the sources, so much of what is said about her remains speculative.

The myth

Ancient sources preserve very little about Vör. Snorri Sturluson, in the thirteenth-century Prose Edda, includes her among the ásynjur, the goddesses, in chapter 35 of Gylfaginning, where she is listed tenth. According to Snorri, she is so wise and inquiring that nothing can be hidden from her. He also cites a saying that a woman becomes aware (vor) of something when she finds it out, which links the goddess's name to the idea of attentiveness. Her name appears again in a list of ásynjur in Skáldskaparmál and twice in kennings of skaldic poetry.

Scholars remain divided. Rudolf Simek doubts that Vör was really a goddess and questions whether Snorri's etymology, connecting her name to vörr, 'careful', is correct. Simek groups her with Sága, Hlín, Sjöfn, Snotra and Vár, vaguely defined figures who may have been protective goddesses of the household, somewhat like matrons. He also suggests that the second element of the valkyrie name Geiravör might match the goddess's name, meaning 'spear-goddess', or simply be a common suffix in personal names. Andy Orchard judges Snorri's etymology scarcely profound and suspects he had little other material about her.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson (13th century), which mentions her in Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál, and skaldic poetry, which preserves two kennings with her name.

Trivia

A saying with her name. Snorri records a saying that a woman becomes aware (vor) of something when she finds it out, a wordplay on the goddess's name.
A possible echo in a valkyrie. The name of the valkyrie Geiravör ends like Vör's, and Simek suggests it may share the same element, though it may also be a common suffix.
