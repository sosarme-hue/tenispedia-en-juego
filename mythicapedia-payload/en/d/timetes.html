fifteenth mythical king of Athens

Thymoetes

Thymoetes was, according to Greek tradition, the fifteenth mythical king of Athens and the last descendant of Theseus. His willingness to give up the throne in a single combat against the Boeotian king Xanthus explains how Melanthus came to power, as he was the man who fought in his place.

The myth

Thymoetes was the son of Oxyntes and the brother of Aphidas. He reigned in Athens as the fifteenth king of the mythical line that traced back to Theseus, of whom he was the last descendant. During a border dispute with the Boeotians, the Athenian king and the Boeotian king Xanthus faced the prospect of a long and costly war.

To avoid it, it was agreed that a single combat between the two kings would decide the outcome of the conflict. Thymoetes, fearful of fighting, announced that he would hand over his throne to whoever fought in his place. This promise opened the way for Melanthus, who volunteered, accepted the challenge and defeated Xanthus. Keeping his word, Melanthus became king of Athens, and the story thus places the transfer of power between a cautious ruler and a volunteer who was not the king himself.
