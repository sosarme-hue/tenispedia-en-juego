Norse god of the Æsir

Hœnir

Hœnir is one of the Æsir in Norse mythology and appears as a companion of Odin in the creation of the first human beings. He is an enigmatic god, rarely described but present in key episodes such as the war with the Vanir and Ragnarök.

The myth

According to the tradition, after the war between the Æsir and the Vanir a peace was sealed with an exchange of hostages. Hœnir was sent to the Vanir along with Mímir. There he was received as a chief because of his imposing appearance, yet he was indecisive and left all his choices to Mímir; when Mímir was absent, he only answered with vague grunts. The Ynglinga saga calls him Hone and records this episode.

In the Völuspá, Hœnir accompanies Odin and Lóðurr in creating Ask and Embla, the first human couple, and he is the one who gives them their senses. In Snorri's Prose Edda, Vili and Vé take that role instead, and it is thought that Hœnir may have been another name for Vili.

In other tales he travels with Odin and Loki. In Skáldskaparmál they stop to cook a meal, and the giant Þjazi, disguised as an eagle, asks to share it. In Reginsmál he accompanies the two to the waterfall where the dwarf Andvari lived, whose gold Loki would later steal. According to the Völuspá, Hœnir is one of the few gods who survive Ragnarök.

Legacy

Some scholars, such as Viktor Rydberg, connect his name and epithets such as langifótr ('Long-legs') or aurkonungr ('mud-king') with the stork, a bird that in European folklore delivers babies. This association is supported by the medieval Faroese ballad Lokka táttur, in which Hœnir protects a farmer's boy by summoning seven swans.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), the Ynglinga saga, the poem Haustlöng, Reginsmál and, from the medieval period, the ballad Lokka táttur.

Trivia

A god linked to the stork. Some scholars propose that Hœnir was connected with the stork, based on his epithets and his role in the ballad Lokka táttur. This idea is a modern interpretation rather than something stated in the ancient texts.
Seven swans. In the Faroese ballad Lokka táttur, Hœnir protects a boy by summoning seven swans, a detail that reinforces his link with birds.
