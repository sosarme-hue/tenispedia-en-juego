Tumburu

Tumburu is the foremost of the gandharvas, the celestial musicians of Hindu mythology. He performs in the courts of Indra and Kubera, sings the praises of Vishnu, and is regarded as the king of songs.

The myth

According to tradition, Tumburu is the son of the sage Kashyapa and Pradha, and the finest of the gandharvas as a singer. The Bhagavata Purana presents him as a student of Narada, who, jealous of his fame, learned music from an owl named Ganabandhu and challenged him. When Narada reached Tumburu's house, he found the ragas wounded by his own dreadful singing, and he left in humiliation.

In the Ramayana, Kubera curses him for failing to bring the apsara Rambha on time, and Tumburu is reborn as the rakshasa Viradha, whom Rama defeats in the forest. In the Mahabharata he helps the Pandavas, gives Yudhishthira a hundred horses and grants Arjuna his gandharva weapon.

Legacy

The tambura instrument is believed to be either a cognate of his name or named after Tumburu. In South India he is often shown with a horse's face, playing the veena and holding wooden cymbals.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, the Bhagavata Purana, the Adbhuta Ramayana and the Kathasaritsagara.

Trivia

A rakshasa with his identity. In the Ramayana, Viradha is Tumburu reborn through a curse. Rama and Lakshmana defeat him, and in death he regains his form as a gandharva.
A curse over a delay. Kubera cursed him for failing to bring the apsara Rambha on time. According to the Kathasaritsagara, another of his curses separated King Pururavas from Urvashi.
