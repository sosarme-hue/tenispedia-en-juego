Vedic goddess of death and decay, later a Hindu guardian of the southwest

Nirṛti

Nirṛti is a Hindu deity linked to death, decay and sorrow. She began as a goddess in the Vedic world and later became a male god, one of the dikpala or guardians of the directions, ruling the southwest.

The myth

In the earliest Vedic hymns, Nirṛti appears as a dreaded power from whom protection is sought; the Rigveda even asks for her departure from the sacrificial site. She is described as dark, dressed in dark clothes, and associated with a realm of non-existence where there is no light, food or children, threatening those who neglect their ritual duties. The Atharvaveda gives her golden locks, and a brahmana text says she binds mortals with cords, as death does.

Later traditions tell different stories. According to the Mahabharata, she is the wife of Adharma and mother of Bhaya (fear), Mahabhaya (terror) and Mrityu (death). According to the Markandeya Purana, she is the daughter of Adharma and Himsa (violence), and mother of Naraka and Bhaya. Other accounts make her the wife of Arita, or a childless goddess who adopts two of Brahma's creations. Some texts identify her with Jyeshtha or Alakshmi, and she is said to have emerged from the churning of the ocean.

Legacy

In later Sanskrit, the adjective nairṛta refers to what belongs to the goddess or to the rakshasas, and also to the southwest. Varahamihira linked her to the lunar mansion Mula, which he called Nairṛta.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda mentions her first, followed by the Atharvaveda, the brahmana texts and the Mahabharata.
