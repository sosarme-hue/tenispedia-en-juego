Roman god of the north wind

Aquilo

Aquilo is the Roman god of the cold, stormy north wind. He is one of the four principal winds associated with the cardinal directions and is identified with the Greek Boreas.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Aquilo is the son of Aeolus and Aurora, and he counts among the four winds that blow from the cardinal points, alongside Favonius, Vulturnus and Auster. In his Metamorphoses, Ovid shows him shut inside the caves of Aeolus with the winds that scatter gathered clouds, while the south wind Notus is released. He is usually shown as an old man with wild, white hair.

Pliny links him with other northern winds, such as Circius and Aparctias, and modern scholars equate him with the Greek Boreas, though each tradition keeps its own image of the wind.

Legacy

The alternative name Septentrio, derived from septem triones (seven oxen), refers to the seven prominent stars of the Big Dipper in the northern sky. From this comes the poetic use of Septentrio as a synonym for the north.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions him in his Metamorphoses, and Pliny links him with other northern winds.

Trivia

Origin of Septentrio. The name Septentrio comes from septem triones, meaning seven oxen, a reference to the seven stars of the Big Dipper.
