eighth sign of the Chinese zodiac

Goat

The Goat is the eighth of the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac, a cycle that repeats every twelve years in the lunisolar Chinese calendar. It is regarded as a gentle, kind and well-liked sign, and its year is associated with prosperity. As a zodiac creature, its significance lies in astrology and in the way Chinese culture reads the character of those born under it.

The myth

The lore around this sign is not told as a story of gods or heroes, but as a set of traits attributed to those born in its year. According to astrological tradition, people born under the Goat share characteristics with others of the same sign, and these traits recur in every twelve-year cycle.

Combined with the Wood element, the Goat is described as peace-loving, helpful and trusting, devoted to those close to it. At the same time, it is said to cling to the familiar and resist change, and to show strength when circumstances turn hard, even though its usual manner is sweet.

As for dates, the Year of the Goat does not match the Gregorian calendar: it begins with the new moon falling between late January and mid or late February. It is associated with the eighth Earthly Branch, 未 (wèi).

Finally, on compatibility, tradition holds that the Goat forms harmonious pairs with the Pig, the Rabbit and the Horse, and can also get along well with the Tiger, Monkey, Dragon, Rooster and another Goat. Its opposite in the wheel of signs is the Ox.

Legacy

The ambiguity of the name has left its mark on languages. The Chinese yáng refers to both goats and sheep; to tell them apart, shānyáng (goat) and miányáng (sheep) are used. In Vietnamese the sign is mùi, clearly a goat; in Japanese it is hitsuji, a sheep; in Korea, Mongolia and the Philippines it is rendered as ram or sheep. Within China, the sign is said to be thought of as a goat in the south and as a sheep in the north. The character yáng also forms part of 祥 (xiáng, good fortune) and 善 (shàn, kindness), reinforcing its link with luck.

Trivia

An auspicious animal. The Chinese generally regard sheep and goats as lucky animals, so their year is linked with promise and prosperity.
A shared character. The character yáng appears in 祥, meaning auspiciousness, and in 善, meaning kindness. According to one source, the two were interchangeable in ancient Chinese.
