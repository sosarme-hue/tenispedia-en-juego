Greek daimon of surfeit and disdain

Corus

Corus (Greek Κόρος, Koros) is a daimon of Greek mythology, the personification of surfeit and disdain. He embodies the excess that leads people to scorn others and forget their limits.

The myth

According to tradition, Corus was the son of Hybris, Arrogance, who was in turn the daughter of Dyssebeia, Impiety. This lineage places him among the spirits who embodied the attitudes the Greeks regarded as most dangerous: overweening pride and disrespect toward the gods.

The name also appears in the story of the hunter Actaeon, where one of his hunting dogs was called Corus. Like other daimones of his family, Corus has no major stories of his own; his significance lies in the symbolic role he plays within the genealogy of hubris.
