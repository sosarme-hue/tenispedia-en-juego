Greek-named deity worshipped in Syria by Arab tribes

Theandrios

Theandrios, also called Theandrates, whose name means 'God-Man', is a deity with a Greek name who was venerated in towns and villages around Mount Hermon by North Arabian tribes of pre-Islamic Arabia.

The myth

The surviving texts do not record a detailed story about him. It is believed that the Greek name was imposed on an earlier local god, so his figure seems to have emerged from the meeting between regional religion and Hellenistic culture.

What we know comes mainly from his worship and inscriptions, not from any continuous narrative.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped in settlements around Mount Hermon. A dedication to a male god found at Beit Rime, in Syria, and an inscription from Attil, in Suwayda, are the main evidence. No concrete details about temples or rites survive, and there is no record that his worship continues today.

Legacy

Some scholars have considered him the Arabian version of other 'God-man' figures, such as Dionysus, Heracles, Mithras, Krishna or Jesus. This is an interpretive comparison, not a documented inheritance.

Ancient sources: He does not appear in ancient literary texts; he is known from dedications and inscriptions found at Beit Rime and Attil (Suwayda).

Trivia

Revealing name. Theandrios means 'God-Man' in Greek, a name that points to a divine nature joined with a human one.
Illustrious company. He has been compared with Dionysus, Heracles, Mithras, Krishna and Jesus, figures often said to share a similar character.
