Hindu goddess of scorpions in southern Karnataka

Chelamma

Chelamma is a Hindu goddess of southern Karnataka in India, worshipped in Kolar. She is linked to protection from scorpion stings, and her shrine is associated with the goddess Parvati.

The myth

The sources offer few narratives about her origins. The best-known legend concerns a donation box, or hundi, carved into the ground at her shrine, where worshippers have been leaving offerings for about 1,000 years without anyone ever opening it.

According to tradition, the box holds gold coins and precious gems from ancient times. Her name includes the suffix amma, common to many South Indian goddesses.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped alongside Kolaramma in Kolar. Believers pray at her shrine, dedicated to Parvati, for protection from scorpion stings, and leave offerings called kanike in the hundi. The sources do not describe how her cult is practised today.

Trivia

A box never opened. According to legend, the hundi has received offerings for centuries, and no one has ever opened it.
The suffix amma. Her name includes amma, a suffix very common among goddesses of South India.
