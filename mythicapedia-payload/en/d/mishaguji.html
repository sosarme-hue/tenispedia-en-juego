collective deities and spirits (kami) of the Suwa region, Japan

Mishaguji

Mishaguji is a collective term for the kami worshipped in the Suwa region of Nagano Prefecture, especially linked to the rites of the Upper Shrine of Suwa Taisha. They matter because they preserve an old local tradition whose exact origin and nature remain unresolved.

The myth

Medieval documents produced by the Moriya describe the Mishaguji as lesser deities forming the retinue of Suwa Myōjin, identified with Takeminakata, the god of Suwa Shrine. In the early modern period, Suwa people understood them as the divine children (mikogami) of Takeminakata, and these descendants were credited with forming and developing the land of Shinano.

The name's origin is uncertain. Several etymologies have been proposed, including links to sacred stones (ishigami, "stone deity"), to measuring poles (shakujin), or to the word sakuchi, "to open up the land".

Worship and religion

In the winter and spring rites of the Upper Shrine, the Kan-no-Osa or Jinchō, a high-ranking priest of the Moriya clan, summoned the Mishaguji into vessels (yorishiro), such as people or objects, to inhabit them temporarily, and then ritually dismissed them. Today they are still venerated in numerous small shrines across the region.

Ancient sources: Medieval Moriya documents, which portray them as the retinue of Suwa Myōjin.
