King of Corinth in Greek myth

Polybus

Polybus is the king of Corinth who raised Oedipus as his own son, the central figure of the Theban legend. He matters because his household is where Oedipus believes he has escaped the prophecy that doomed him.

The myth

According to tradition, Polybus ruled Corinth with a gentle hand and loved his wife, although their marriage remained childless for many years. The sources do not agree on the queen's name: she is sometimes Merope, sometimes Periboea, and in one account Medusa, daughter of Orsilochus.

When the infant Oedipus, abandoned by Laius and Jocasta, the rulers of Thebes, was found and brought to the court, Polybus and his wife gladly adopted him. The queen named him Oedipus because of the swelling in his feet, caused by injuries to his ankles. Years later, Periboea gave birth to a daughter, Alcinoe.

As a young man, Oedipus stood out for his courage and strength, and his companions mocked him, saying he could not be the son of a man as mild as Polybus. Getting no answer from his adoptive mother, he consulted the oracle at Delphi, which warned that he would kill his father and marry his mother. Horrified, and believing Polybus and Merope to be his parents, he resolved never to return to Corinth.

On the road through Phocis he met a chariot in a narrow pass. Its driver, Laius, treated him with disdain, and Oedipus killed him in a quarrel without knowing he was his true father. Years later, as king of Thebes after defeating the Sphinx, he faced a plague that led him to investigate the crime. A messenger announced that Polybus had died of natural causes, and Oedipus took this to mean the prophecy was false, since he had not killed his father. Only at the end of the story does he learn the truth.

Ancient sources: He appears in Sophocles' Oedipus Rex, where a messenger brings Jocasta the news of Polybus' death.

Trivia

Three names for one queen. Ancient sources give Polybus' wife different names: Merope, Periboea and, in some versions, Medusa.
A name from the feet. According to the account, Oedipus was named for the swelling of his feet, caused by injuries to his ankles.
