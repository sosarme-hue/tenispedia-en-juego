Surapadma

Surapadma is an asura from Hindu mythology, son of the sage Kashyapa and the shakti Maya. He is the great enemy of the devas, and the adversary defeated by Kartikeya, son of Shiva.

The myth

According to the Kanda Puranam, the Tamil version of the Skanda Purana, Surapadma and his brothers Simhamukha and Tarakasura performed tapas to Shiva, who granted them weapons and a boon: they could only be killed by a son of Shiva, which made them nearly immortal.

Surapadma married Padmakomalai, with whom he had several sons, the eldest being Banukopan. He ruled from his capital at Viramakendiram, oppressed other celestial beings and spread tyranny across the three worlds. He also desired Indrani, wife of Indra, king of the devas.

The devas asked Shiva for help, and from him emerged divine sparks that developed into Kartikeya. Surapadma invaded Devaloka with a huge army, and Indra fled to earth. Kartikeya first sent Viravakuteva to urge him to stop, but the asura ignored the message and war was declared. In the final battle at Tiruchendur, Surapadma took a gigantic form, but when Kartikeya threw his spear, the vel, he fled to the sea and became a huge mango tree spreading across the three worlds. The vel split the tree in two: one half became a peacock and the other a rooster. Kartikeya took the peacock as his vahana and the rooster as his pennon.

Worship and religion

The killing of Surapadma by Kartikeya is commemorated in the festival called Surasamharam. According to tradition, his death marks the onset of the Kali Yuga.
