popular Hindu god of southern India, guardian of plantations

Muneeswarar

Muneeswarar is a popular Hindu deity of southern India, especially Tamil Nadu, revered as a rural guardian of plantations and estates and as a divine attendant of the goddess Mariamman. His name combines muni (sage or saint) and ishvara (a title of Shiva), and many devotees regard him as a form of Shiva.

The myth

Accounts of his origin vary and do not appear in classic sacred texts such as the Vedas or Puranas. According to an oral tradition, he arose when Shiva was enraged at being excluded from the Daksha Yajna, a ritual conducted by Daksha, Shiva's father-in-law; several deities were born from the god's anger, Muneeswarar among them. Versions differ on whether he was born from Shiva's face, his sweat, or simply created by him.

Another legend presents him as a great devotee of Shiva who lived long ago. Through his devotion, Shiva granted him the title ishvara and appointed him a guardian. He is described as a mature man with a large moustache, carrying a trident, machete, whip or spear, and smeared with holy ash. He is said to have several forms, numbering from three up to 1008.

Worship and religion

He is worshipped in two ways: the orthodox Vedic-Agamic style, with elaborate rules, vegetarian offerings and Brahmin priests in Agamic temples, and the non-Vedic style, less rigid, with offerings of animals, alcohol or tobacco and trance practices. Historically he was honoured through trees, stones and tridents, and today also through statues. His worship remains alive in India and among diaspora communities in Malaysia and Singapore.
