king of the Burgundians in Germanic legend

Gunther

Gunther, known in Old Norse as Gunnar, is the king of Burgundy who rules from Worms, on the Rhine, in the Germanic legend of the Nibelungenlied. His story matters because it brings together the love, betrayal and revenge that shape the whole epic.

The myth

According to the tradition, Gunther is the son of Gjúki and Grimhild and rules Burgundy alongside his brothers Gislahar, Gutthorm and Hagen, the grim warrior, as well as his sister Kriemhild. To win Brunhild as his wife he turns to Siegfried, whose help lets him overcome the trials the queen demands.

The story takes a tragic turn when Kriemhild marries Siegfried and, after he is killed at Hagen's hand, she seeks revenge. Gunther is caught in the chain of violence that ends in a great slaughter, and he dies in the final climax.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Nibelungenlied, and his Norse version, Gunnar, is recorded in the Poetic Edda and the Völsunga saga.

Trivia

A name in different languages. The character appears as Gunther in the Nibelungenlied and as Gunnar in the Norse versions, reflecting the difference between the Germanic and Scandinavian traditions.
A court on the Rhine. His castle stood in Worms, a city on the banks of the Rhine, which in the legend serves as the centre of the Burgundian court.
