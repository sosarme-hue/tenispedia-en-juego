prophet of Apollo at Miletus

Branchus

Branchus is a young shepherd and prophet in Greek mythology, son of Smicrus and beloved of the god Apollo. His story explains the founding of Apollo's cult at Didyma and the priestly clan of the Branchidae, making him a key figure of the Milesian oracle.

The myth

According to the tradition, Branchus was born to Smicrus and a distinguished woman of Miletus. During her labour, his mother dreamed that the sun entered her mouth, passed through her belly and came out through her genitals. The seers took this as an excellent omen, and the boy was named Branchus, since the sun had passed through her throat.

As a young man, Branchus was the most handsome of men. One day in the woods he kissed Apollo, who returned his affection. The god then gave him a crown and a magic staff, breathed prophecy into him and made him a prophet and priest. After Branchus vanished suddenly, an altar was raised on the spot of the kiss. Callimachus tells another version: Apollo, riding a dolphin from Delos, saw Branchus tending flocks in the sacred woods and disguised himself as a goatherd to seduce him, though he ended up milking a billy goat. To persuade the young man to leave herding, he promised to protect his flocks, and after they became lovers he taught him the mantic arts.

Worship and religion

The Milesians built temples to Branchus and Apollo, named Philesia after the kiss, where the god was worshipped as Apollo Philesius. Temples dedicated to Branchus alone were called Branchiadae. His oracles were said to rank second only to Apollo's at Delphi. The Branchidae, who claimed descent from him, formed an important clan of prophets.
