female spirit of Caucasian folklore

Al Basty

Al Basty is a female spirit from Turkic and Caucasian tradition, linked to guilt, fever and women after childbirth. Her name, rooted in Turkic languages, suggests pressure or pressing down on the sleeper.

The myth

According to tradition, Al Basty visits those who carry guilty souls or come from families that committed bloody crimes never punished. Her victims wake with a high fever. She is also said to steal horses, which are found sweating and exhausted at dawn.

Her figure is often confused with other female spirits that enter men's erotic dreams, but in Turkic legends she has no sexual character. Because she haunts women after childbirth, traditional care lasting forty days was practised. She is known as the 'red mother' because of her torments.

Trivia

Link to Lilith. A variant of the Al Basty myth appears in Sumerian tradition, as one of the names given to Lilith.
Armenian version. In Armenian folklore she appears as Āl, a childbirth demon with snake-like hair, brass nails and iron teeth who hides in damp places.
