Lithuanian goddess of the morning star

Aušrinė

Aušrinė is the goddess of the morning star, that is, Venus, in Lithuanian mythology. She is associated with beauty, love, youth and new beginnings, and her figure merged with that of the Virgin Mary after Lithuania's Christianization.

The myth

According to the traditional tale, each morning Aušrinė and her servant Tarnaitis, possibly identified with Mercury, prepare the way for Saulė, the Sun, while in the evening Vakarinė, the evening star, prepares his bed. Accounts of the Sun's family vary: sometimes he is said to be the mother of Aušrinė, Vakarinė and other planets.

A popular myth tells how Mėnulis, the Moon, fell in love with Aušrinė and betrayed his wife Saulė, and was punished by Perkūnas, the thunder god. In other stories Saulė is jealous of Aušrinė's beauty, yet she keeps serving the Sun each morning.

In the tale Saulė ir Vėjų Motina, analysed in detail by Algirdas Julien Greimas, a young man named Joseph becomes fascinated by a light in the sky and sets out to find a "second sun". He discovers it is a maiden living on an island, who appears in three forms: as a star, as a woman on land and as a mare in the sea. Greimas reads the tale as a double origin myth explaining the birth of Tarnaitis and Aušrinė's ascent into the sky.

Worship and religion

Her cult may derive from an ancient Indo-European dawn goddess and later blended with Christian imagery.

Legacy

Her name entered Lithuanian folk astronomy as a term for the morning star, in forms such as Aušrinė žvaigždė or Aušrinukė. In Latvian folk riddles her name is the answer to a riddle about dew.

Ancient sources: The 16th-century Polish historian Jan Łasicki mentions her as Ausca, a goddess of the sun's rays.

Trivia

Name of the morning star. According to Jonas Vaiškūnas, her name gives rise to several terms in Lithuanian folk astronomy for the morning star.
