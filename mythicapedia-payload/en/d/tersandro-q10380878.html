Greek hero, son of Sisyphus

Thersander

Thersander is a figure of Greek myth, a son of Sisyphus whose name is linked to the origins of two towns in Boeotia. He is mentioned mainly by Pausanias, who lists him among his father's four sons.

The myth

According to the tradition, Thersander was one of Sisyphus's four sons, alongside Glaucus, Ornithion and Almus. His mother is given as Merope. Few sources mention him at all, so his story works as a small link in a larger family tree.

Accounts of his descendants vary. Some say he fathered two sons, Haliartus and Coronus, who gave their names to the towns of Haliartus and Coronea. Others add Proetus, whose daughter Mera died a virgin. The episode shows how Greek myths often tied the names of places to founding ancestors. Versions differ on this point, so the list should be read as one tradition among several.

Ancient sources: Pausanias is the ancient author who names him as a son of Sisyphus, alongside his brothers.

Trivia

Two Boeotian towns. Some traditions credit the sons of Thersander with giving their names to Haliartus and Coronea, two towns in Boeotia.
A daughter who died a virgin. Some versions add Proetus, whose daughter Mera died a virgin, to the descendants of Thersander.
