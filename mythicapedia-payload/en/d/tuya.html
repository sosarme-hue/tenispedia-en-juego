Delphic naiad, mother of Delphus

Thyia

Thyia is a naiad of a spring on Mount Parnassus in Phocis, linked to the cults of Dionysus and Apollo at Delphi. Her name derives from the verb meaning 'to sacrifice', and in tradition she is the mother of Delphus, founder of the city of Delphi.

The myth

In the Delphic tradition, Thyia was a daughter of the river god Cephissus, though other accounts make her a daughter of the autochthon Castalius, after whom the Castalian Spring was named. She was said to have been loved by Apollo, and to have borne him Delphus, the eponymous founder of the town beside the oracular shrine.

Tradition also credits her as the first to sacrifice to Dionysus and to celebrate orgies in his honour. Her shrine was the gathering place of the Thyiades, and Attic women who climbed Parnassus each year for the Dionysiac rites took the name Thyades or Thyiades. Other accounts link her with Melaena, an alternative mother of Delphus, and with the Corycian nymphs.

Worship and religion

Thyia had a shrine at Delphi, where the Thyiades gathered. According to Herodotus, the Delphians later set up an altar to the winds on the site of this precinct during the Persian Wars.

Legacy

The name of Thyia was applied to the white cedar genus Thuja by Linnaeus in 1753.

Ancient sources: Herodotus (Histories 7.178) and Pausanias (Description of Greece 10.6) mention her, and the Catalogue of Women attributed to Hesiod names her as a daughter of Deucalion.

Trivia

A genus of trees. Linnaeus took the name Thyia for the genus Thuja, which includes the white cedars.
The Thyiades. Attic women who celebrated Dionysiac orgies on Parnassus took their name from her.
