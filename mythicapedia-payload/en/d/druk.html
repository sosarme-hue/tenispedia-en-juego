thunder dragon of Tibetan and Bhutanese mythology

Druk

The druk is the thunder dragon of Tibetan and Bhutanese mythology, and one of the most recognisable national symbols of Bhutan. Its image and name appear on the flag, in the national anthem and in the title of the country's leaders, which makes it central to understanding Bhutanese identity.

The myth

According to the tradition of the Drukpa school, which arose in Tibet and later spread to Bhutan, the druk is linked to a specific episode. When its founder, Tsangpa Gyare, 1st Gyalwang Drukpa, began to build Ralung Monastery, a violent storm broke out. The thunder, also called the «Cloud-Voice», was seen as the roar of the dragon, and the founder took it as an omen.

He then named the monastery Drug-Ralung, adding the phrase «thunder dragon» to its name. His disciples became known as drugpa, «those of the thunder».

Worship and religion

The druk became an emblem of the school. At the beginning of the twentieth century, the Grand Lama of Bhutan wore a hat decorated with thunder dragons to signify the origins of the order. As the school grew, it founded monasteries in what is now Bhutan, and the region came to be known as Dug Yul, Land of Thunder, among both Tibetans and Bhutanese.

Legacy

The druk appears on the flag of Bhutan, holding jewels to represent wealth. In Dzongkha the country is called Druk Yul, «Land of Druk», and its leaders are called Druk Gyalpo, «Thunder Dragon Kings». During the 2007 mock election, the four mock parties were all named the Druk party of a given colour. The national anthem, Druk tsendhen, translates as «Kingdom of Druk».

Trivia

A dragon on the flag. The druk appears on the flag of Bhutan, holding jewels that represent the wealth of the country.
Dragon Kings. The leaders of Bhutan are called Druk Gyalpo, meaning Thunder Dragon Kings.
