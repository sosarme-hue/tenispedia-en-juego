Māori god of war

Tūmatauenga

Tūmatauenga is the Māori god of war and of human activities such as hunting, cultivation, fishing and cooking. Son of Rangi and Papa, he explains why people fight and draw their food from the natural world.

The myth

According to Māori tradition, the children of Rangi, the sky, and Papa, the earth, lived pressed together in darkness. Tired of it, they planned to separate their parents. Tū proposed killing them, but Tāne preferred to pull them apart, and so light entered the world.

Tāwhirimātea, angered, declared war on his brothers. Tū was the only one who stood against him, and after defeating his brothers he took on many names. He then snared birds, caught fish and dug up plants, all children of his brothers, and so people could hunt, fish and farm. Tāwhirimātea was never fully defeated, and his storms still lash humankind.

Worship and religion

Warriors were invoked in rituals before battle, and the body of the first warrior to fall was offered to him. In times of war, local deities such as Kahukura, Maru or Uenuku were also called upon.

Legacy

The New Zealand Army uses a Māori name inspired by him, under which all soldiers belong to one iwi under his protection. The band Alien Weaponry refers to him in its songs.

Trivia

Ovens of Tūmatauenga. The marae, the Māori ceremonial space, is considered the fiery ovens of Tūmatauenga. Areas where battles take place are his domain.
Kaitangata. Kaitangata was a mortal who was taught how to fish. The name also referred to groups of men tasked with fighting and gathering food.
