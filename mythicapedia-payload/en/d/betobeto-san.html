yōkai of the footsteps that follow night walkers

Betobeto-san

Betobeto-san is a yōkai from Japanese folklore said to follow people walking at night. It is considered harmless, and simply letting it pass is enough to be rid of its company.

The myth

Tradition describes Betobeto-san as an invisible presence that quietly accompanies anyone walking down a dark road. It reveals itself only through the sound of footsteps behind the walker, though no one is there.

In Uda District, Nara, people say it is better to meet it on a dark night road. In Shizuoka, the encounter supposedly happens when coming down from a small mountain. Although it is not blamed for any harm, anyone who hears the steps should step aside and say "Go ahead, Betobeto-san." In Shizuoka the advice is to say "Coming" or "Please go ahead," and the creature will then leave those it is following.

Legacy

Manga artist Shigeru Mizuki said he had encountered something seemingly akin to this yōkai. A station on the JR West Sakai Line at Yonago Airport is nicknamed "Betobeto-san Station" by locals.

Trivia

A station named after it. The Yonago Airport station on the JR West Sakai Line is known locally as "Betobeto-san Station."
Relatives of the footsteps. Bishakutsu, from the former Sakai district of Fukui, is regarded as a monster of the same kind, and the recent Teke Teke is almost the same as a footsteps monster.
