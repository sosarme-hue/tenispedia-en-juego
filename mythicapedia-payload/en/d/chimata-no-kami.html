Japanese kami of crossroads and boundaries

Chimata-no-kami

Chimata-no-kami is a kami in Japanese mythology linked to crossroads, paths and boundaries. Also called Tsukitatsufunado, it was believed to guard against plagues and malevolent spirits.

The myth

After fleeing Yomi, the land of the dead, Izanagi reached the border known as Yomotsu Hirasaka and blocked the path with a huge boulder. Near Awagihara, by the Tachibana River in Hyūga, he removed his clothes and accessories and purified himself in the water through misogi to cleanse himself of kegare.

Several gods were born from the things he discarded. The first was Tsukitatsufunado, born from the staff he had thrown. Together with Yachimatahiko and Yachimatahime, this kami was believed to keep beings from Yomi away from the world of the living.

Worship and religion

As guardians of forks, roads and crossroads, these kami were invoked for protection against plagues, epidemics and evil deities and spirits.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki recounts Izanagi's flight from Yomi and the birth of the gods from the objects he discarded.

Trivia

Born from a staff. Among the gods born from the objects Izanagi discarded, Tsukitatsufunado arose from the staff he threw.
A protective trio. Tsukitatsufunado shares its protective role with Yachimatahiko and Yachimatahime, with whom it was associated.
