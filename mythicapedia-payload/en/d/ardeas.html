Greek hero, son of Odysseus and Circe

Ardeas

Ardeas is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Odysseus and Circe, credited with founding Ardea, a city in Latium. He barely appears in ancient texts, so he survives mainly as the legendary origin of a place.

The myth

According to the Greek historian Xenagoras, Ardeas, also called Ardias or Ardeias, was the son of Odysseus and Circe, the sorceress who in the Odyssey keeps the hero on her island for a time. Tradition places him as the founder of Ardea, a town in the region of Latium on the Italian peninsula, where he is said to have brought the line of the hero of Ithaca after his long wanderings.

No other known source attributes deeds, adventures or a specific ending to him. His myth is reduced almost entirely to his parentage and the founding of the city, which is the only trace his name leaves in tradition.

Ancient sources: The Greek historian Xenagoras, who names him as son of Odysseus and Circe and founder of Ardea.
