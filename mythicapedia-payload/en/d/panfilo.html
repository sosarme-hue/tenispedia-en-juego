Dorian hero of Greek mythology

Pamphylus

Pamphylus is a Dorian hero of Greek mythology, son of Aigimios and brother of Dymas. He was regarded as the mythical ancestor of one of the three Dorian tribes, which makes him a key figure in the story of the Dorian migration into the Peloponnese.

The myth

According to tradition, Pamphylus ruled the Dorians from the region at the foot of Mount Pindus, in northern Greece. Originally he was considered a son of Doros, the ancestor after whom the Dorians are named, but by at least the sixth century BCE he had become a son of Aigimios, who was himself Doros's son. When Aigimios died, Pamphylus and his brother submitted to Hyllos, the son of Herakles whom Aigimios had adopted, and Hyllos became king of the Dorians.

Later, Pamphylus joined the Herakleidai, the descendants of Herakles, in their invasion of the Peloponnese. He and Dymas were killed in battle during that campaign. Apart from the manner of their deaths, the myth records no deeds or episodes from his life. Some accounts add that he married Orsobia, daughter of Deiphontes.

Trivia

Three brothers, three tribes. Tradition linked Pamphylus and his brothers to the three tribes into which the Dorians were divided, each with its own eponymous hero.
A genealogy that changed. Pamphylus was first a son of Doros, but later became a son of Aigimios. A modern hypothesis suggests this change may have reconciled two versions of the Dorians' origins.
