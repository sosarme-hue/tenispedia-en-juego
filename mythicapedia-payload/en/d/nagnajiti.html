wife of Krishna, princess of Kosala

Nagnajiti

Nagnajiti, also called Satya, is one of the eight principal wives of the Hindu god Krishna. Daughter of King Nagnajit of Kosala, she is regarded in Vaishnava tradition as an incarnation of Niladevi, an aspect of Lakshmi.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, King Nagnajit set a condition for his daughter's marriage: any suitor had to subdue seven fierce bulls. No prince managed it. Krishna travelled to Kosala with a large retinue, and when he faced the bulls he expanded into seven forms and tied them with a noose, winning Satya's hand.

The wedding was celebrated with great splendour, and the king gave a lavish dowry. On the way to Dvaraka, the defeated princes attacked the procession, but Krishna's army, with Yadava warriors and his friend Arjuna, drove them off. Tradition says Nagnajiti bore ten sons. After Krishna's death, the Bhagavata Purana relates that she threw herself onto his funeral pyre along with other chief queens.

Ancient sources: The Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Harivamsa mention her, and the Mahabharata refers to a wife of Krishna named Satya.
