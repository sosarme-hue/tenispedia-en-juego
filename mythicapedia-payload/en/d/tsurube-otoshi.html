Japanese yōkai that drops buckets from trees

Tsurube-otoshi

The tsurube-otoshi is a yōkai from the Tōkai and Kansai regions said to drop from treetops to attack and devour people. Its name refers to the buckets it seems to lower.

The myth

The stories describe it in varied ways. In Sogabe, Kyoto, it drops from a kaya tree, lets out a sniggering laugh and rises back up. Elsewhere it takes the form of a severed head that devours someone and then vanishes for a few days. In Kuroe, Wakayama, an old tree glints at its base like a koban coin; whoever reaches for it is hauled up in a bucket to the crown and, after being devoured, dies as they hit the ground.

In the Edo-period collection Kokon Hyakumonogatari Hyōban it appears as the spirit of a great tree turned into falling flames. Its author, Yamaoka Genrin, explains it through the Chinese theory of the five phases: the tree's fire emerges on rainy days, which belong to water.

Legacy

Toriyama Sekien included it in his catalogue Gazu Hyakki Yagyō under the name tsurubebi, a will-o'-the-wisp hanging from trees. From the Shōwa period onward, yōkai literature usually treats the two as separate beings, though some writers believe they were originally the same.

Ancient sources: Kokon Hyakumonogatari Hyōban (Edo period) by Yamaoka Genrin, and Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō.

Trivia

A false coin. In the Kuroe legend, the glint that lures the victim looks like a koban coin, which is why people reach out to touch it.
Sacred trees. In Ashidani, Shimane, a sacred tree has a small shrine; written accounts say that people who tried to cut nearby trees were badly hurt by falling flames.
