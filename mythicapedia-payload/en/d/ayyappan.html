Hindu deity of truth and righteousness, son of Shiva and Mohini

Ayyappan

Ayyappan is a Hindu deity of truth and righteousness, widely worshipped in Kerala and across southern India. He is regarded as the son of Shiva and Mohini, the female form of Vishnu, and so his figure bridges the Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions.

The myth

According to Malayalam lore, Ayyappan is a warrior prince of the Pandalam kingdom. A childless king found a baby boy on the banks of the Pamba river and raised him as his heir under the name Manikantha. When the boy turned twelve, the queen, who wanted the throne for her own son, feigned illness and demanded tiger's milk as a cure. Manikantha set off into the forest, defeated the demoness Mahishi, who wished to marry him, and returned to the palace riding a tiger. The king recognised his divine nature and ordered a shrine built, marked by an arrow the young man shot himself.

Other versions say he renounced the kingdom to live as an ascetic yogi on a forested mountain, or that after enemies of the king destroyed the shrine of Sastha at Sabarimala, he defeated them, restored the statue and vanished into it. Later legends link him with Durga, who slew the buffalo demon Mahisha, and with a story of two divine beings cursed to be reborn as demons. According to Eliza Kent, these tales seem artificially assembled into a collage. Ruth Vanita suggests Ayyappan emerged from the fusion of a tribal Dravidian god with the Puranic episode of Shiva and Mohini.

Worship and religion

His most famous shrine is Sabarimala in the Western Ghats, visited by millions of pilgrims each year, making it one of the largest pilgrimages in the world. Devotees prepare for weeks by living simply and remaining celibate, then climb the hill barefoot carrying an irumudi, a bag of offerings on the head. The mantra Swamiye Sharanam Ayyappa, meaning "Grant us refuge, Lord Ayyappa!", is chanted across Kerala as a greeting and prayer.

Ancient sources: The Puranic texts Vishnu Purana and Srimad Bhagavatham refer to him as Dharmasastha and Sastha. The Sabarimala tradition developed largely in Malayalam literature and oral tradition in Kerala.

Trivia

Massive pilgrimage. Sabarimala receives around 50 million devotees each year, making it the second largest pilgrimage in the world.
Many names. He is also called Manikandan, Dharmasastha and Hariharasuthan, meaning son of Harihara, a fusion of Hari (Vishnu) and Hara (Shiva).
