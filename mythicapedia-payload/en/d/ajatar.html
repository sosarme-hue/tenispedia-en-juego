evil female spirit of Finnish folklore

Ajatar

Ajatar is an evil female spirit of Finnish folklore, often described as a serpent or dragon and linked to disease and pestilence. Her name also came to mean a demon in some Finnish Bible translations.

The myth

According to tradition, Ajatar, also known as "the Devil of the Woods", is a spirit that appears as a serpent or dragon. She is said to be the mother of the devil and to nurse serpents. Anyone who looks at her may fall ill, since she spreads plague and disease.

In the tales linked to the Kalevala and popular poetry, she is connected to other beings such as Hiisi and Lempo. In Aleksis Kivi's play Kullervo (1860), she is a ferocious figure who urges the protagonist to kill his master's family, and she says she lives in the mountains with Lempo and gnomes in her service. Folk accounts portray her as a forest spirit who pursues and leads people astray.

Legacy

Ajatar appears in fiction inspired by the Kalevala and in modern fantasy, often as a dragon or serpentine creature. She lends her name to the Finnish black metal band Ajattara and to musical works such as the song Ajatar by Winter Gardens.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources do not name her as a central figure in documented folk songs; she appears in Kalevala-inspired material and in some Finnish Bible translations, where the term refers to demons in general.

Trivia

A demonic name in the Bible. In a 1776 Finnish translation of Leviticus, a form of Ajatar's name is used to refer to demons in general, not to one specific entity.
An invented spelling. The form "Ajatar" was proposed by Matthias Castrén to standardize a name that appears in several spellings in the texts.
