Prometheus's eagle in Greek myth

Aethon

Aethon is the eagle that, on Zeus's orders, tormented Prometheus in Greek myth. He matters because he embodies the punishment of the Titan who gave fire to humankind, and his end comes at the hands of Heracles.

The myth

According to the myth, Prometheus shaped human beings from water and earth and gave them fire, hidden in a fennel stalk and without Zeus knowing. When the god found out, he ordered Hephaestus to nail Prometheus to Caucasus, a mountain in Scythia, where he remained chained for many years.

There a broad-winged eagle devoured his immortal liver. Because the organ regrew during the night, the torment was repeated every day. The sources do not agree on the bird's origin: one version says it was born of Typhon and Echidna; another makes it a daughter of Earth and Tartarus; and a third claims Hephaestus built it and Zeus breathed life into it. Other accounts say it devoured his heart rather than his liver.

The ending comes with Heracles, son of Alcmene, who killed the eagle and freed Prometheus. The release happened with Zeus's consent, since the god wanted to enhance his son's fame, although he still held a grudge against the Titan for defying his will. Other accounts place the episode thirty thousand years later.

Trivia

Liver or heart. The most widespread version says the eagle devoured Prometheus's liver, but some accounts claim it was his heart.
Thirty thousand years. According to some accounts, Heracles killed the eagle thirty thousand years after the punishment began.
