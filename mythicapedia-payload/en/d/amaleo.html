eldest son of Niobe in Greek myth

Amaleus

Amaleus is the eldest of the Niobids, the children of Amphion and Niobe, the rulers of Thebes. He has little personality in the stories, but his tale stands out for the attempt on his life made by his aunt Aëdon.

The myth

According to the tradition, Amaleus was the eldest of the Niobids and was very close to his cousin Itylus, the son of Zethus and Aëdon. The two boys shared a room, and Aëdon, resentful of Niobe for having so many children while she had only one, plotted to kill Amaleus. She told Itylus to sleep at the back of the room, but the boy forgot, and in the dark Aëdon killed her own son without recognising him.

In another version, Aëdon did kill Amaleus, but out of fear of Niobe's reaction she took the life of her own son as well. The best-known Niobid story, however, is about Niobe's pride: she boasted of being a better mother than Leto, and Leto's children, Artemis and Apollo, avenged their mother by shooting them all with their arrows.

Ancient sources: The Iliad records Niobe's boast against Leto but does not mention Amaleus; a scholium on Euripides names a son of Amphion called Homoloeus, though it is uncertain that this is the same figure.

Trivia

Possible origin of the name. The name might be connected to the Greek word amalós, meaning 'soft'.
A revenge that goes wrong. Aëdon meant to kill her nephew, but in the dark she struck down her own son, who had slept in the spot where she expected Amaleus to be.
