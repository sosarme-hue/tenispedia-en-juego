king of Arcadia in Greek mythology

Nyctimus

Nyctimus is a legendary king of Arcadia in Greek mythology, son and successor of the impious Lycaon. His story centres on his brothers' cannibal banquet for Zeus and the god's miraculous restoration of him.

The myth

According to the tradition, Nyctimus was the youngest of Lycaon's fifty sons, although some authors make him the eldest. When Lycaon served Zeus a banquet of human flesh, the god overturned the table and struck the family with thunderbolts, but Gaia seized his right hand and spared Nyctimus.

In another account, Nyctimus is the victim: his brothers, testing the suspicions raised by a disguised visitor, killed him, cooked him and served him to Zeus. The god punished the household, burned the palace, restored Nyctimus to life and gave him the throne of Arcadia. His reign was short, as floods in the age of Deucalion followed, which some blamed on his brothers' impiety. He is said to have had a son, Periphetes, ancestor of Psophis.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Apollodorus' Library, Nonnus in his Dionysiaca and Plutarch mention Nyctimus, and Asius of Samos places him in the genealogy of Callisto.

Trivia

A cannibal banquet. Nonnus recalls that Lycaon served Zeus a dish made from his own grandson Nyctimus, cut into pieces, and that the god ate it without knowing what it was.
Inheriting as the youngest. James Frazer suggested that ancient Greeks may have followed a custom of ultimogeniture, in which the youngest son inherited, and that Nyctimus's succession reflects it.
