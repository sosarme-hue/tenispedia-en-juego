Egyptian goddess of weaving and textiles

Tait

Tait, also written Tayt, Tayet or Taytet, was the ancient Egyptian goddess of weaving, textiles and the craft of weavers. Known as the "Lady of Linen", her link to mummy bandages made her a protective figure for the dead.

The myth

Funerary texts place her around the dead body: in invocations from the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom and the Coffin Texts, she takes part in the rites in which the corpse is wrapped in bandages to preserve it from decay.

She was also said to weave the king's garments for the rituals of his office and to restore his limbs as she sewed his clothing. In the Pyramid Texts she appears as a motherly figure wrapping the dead king, and in one spell she guards his head and helps him win the favour of other gods. Some sources make her the wife of Neper, while others suggest she may have been the consort of Hedjhotep, the male god of weavers.

Worship and religion

She was especially venerated at Sais. Linen cloth, prized for its quality and regarded as divine, clothed cult statues in the daily temple ritual, and priests were given white linen garments, the Osiris tunic, a symbol of power, purity and protection. Her iconography shows her offering textiles on a tray.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Pyramid Texts, the Coffin Texts and the Book of Amduat, and in the Book of Caverns, where she greets Ra and Osiris.

Trivia

Cloth from the land of Tait. According to funerary texts, mummy bandages came from the "land of Tait", a town named after the goddess, of which she was the protective deity.
