king of Argos in Greek mythology

Cisus

Cisus is a king of Argos in Greek mythology, son of Temenus and successor to his son-in-law Deiphontes. His reign, marked by the loss of royal power, makes him an example of a monarch who reigned in name only.

The myth

According to the tradition, Temenus left his kingdom to his son-in-law Deiphontes, even though he had sons of his own. Temenus's sons devised schemes that led to Deiphontes' death. Cisus, the eldest of them, succeeded to the throne and took possession of the kingdom.

In time, however, the Argives demanded freedom of speech and laws of their own making. These reforms reduced the king's power so greatly that, by the end of his reign, Cisus was king only in name. He was succeeded by Lacidaus.
