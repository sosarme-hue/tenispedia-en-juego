nymph of the sacred spring at Delphi

Castalia

Castalia is a nymph of Greek myth whose name was given to the sacred spring at Delphi, linked to Apollo and the Muses. Her story explains the origin of one of the most famous springs of the ancient world.

The myth

According to tradition, Castalia fled the amorous advances of Apollo and threw herself into a spring, which took her name ever after. The story varies: some place it at Delphi, at the foot of Parnassus, others on Mount Helicon, as Lactantius Placidus records in his commentary on Statius's Thebaid.

The spring already existed when Apollo came to Delphi in search of Python. Some held that its water was a gift to the nymph from the river Cephisus, a version Alcaeus included in his hymn to Apollo. Paniasis, by contrast, made Castalia a daughter of the river god Achelous.

Worship and religion

The spring's water was used to purify the temples at Delphi, and those who drank from it or listened to its quiet sound were believed to receive the inspiration of poetry. Apollo consecrated her to the Muses. On certain days the Lilaeans threw cakes and other offerings into the spring of the Cephisus, believing they would reappear at Castalia.

Legacy

Her name lived on in literature. The first collection by the Mexican poet Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz, published in Madrid in 1692, was titled Inundación castálida, a nod to poetry inspired by her waters. Hermann Hesse took the name for the futuristic province in The Glass Bead Game (1943), home to an order of intellectuals. In Spain, Editorial Castalia, founded in 1949, and Editorial Gredos, founded in 1950 with a name inspired by the Sierra de Gredos, echo the same name.

Ancient sources: Paniasis, Alcaeus's hymn to Apollo, and Lactantius Placidus's commentary on Statius's Thebaid mention Castalia and her spring.

Trivia

A nymph with two fathers. Ancient sources disagree about her origin: some make her a daughter of Achelous, while others say the water was a gift from the river Cephisus.
A flood of poetry. Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz titled her first collection Inundación castálida, referring to the poetic inspiration said to flow from Castalia's waters.
