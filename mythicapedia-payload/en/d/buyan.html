mysterious island in Slavic folklore

Buyan

Buyan is a mysterious island in East Slavic folklore that, according to tradition, rises and sinks in the ocean as if moved by the tide. It is one of the best-known settings of Russian fairy tales and a place steeped in magic.

The myth

According to the legends, three brothers live on Buyan, the North, East and West winds, and strange events take place there. One of the most famous tales places Koschei the Deathless on the island, hiding his soul inside an egg kept in a marvellous oak tree. Other legends make Buyan the source of all weather: the god Perun is said to generate it there and send it out into the world.

The island is also the home of Gagana, a bird with an iron beak and copper claws, described as the mother of all birds. She nests in the world tree and guards the magic stone Alatyr, which has healing powers, alongside the giant serpent Garafena. Gagana is said to be able to conjure and work miracles, and to help anyone who asks her the right question.

Legacy

Buyan became widely known beyond oral storytelling thanks to Alexander Pushkin, who sets it in his Tale of Tsar Saltan. Some scholars read it as a kind of proto-Indo-European "other world", while others suggest the name actually refers to the real island of Rügen in the Baltic.

Ancient sources: It appears in Russian byliny and skazki, and in the medieval Dove Book, which describes the Alatyr stone and its guardians.

Trivia

Koschei and his egg. In the tales, Koschei the Deathless's soul is not in his body but hidden in an egg kept in an oak on Buyan.
A real island?. Some scholars believe the name Buyan refers to the Baltic island of Rügen, while others regard it as purely mythical.
