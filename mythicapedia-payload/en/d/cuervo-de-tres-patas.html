sun bird of East Asian mythology

Three-legged crow

The three-legged crow is a creature of Chinese, Korean and Japanese mythology, usually linked to the sun. Its image, much older than many of its stories, has lasted in art, national emblems and sport across the region.

The myth

In China, the sun bird is called sanzuniao ("three-legged bird") and appears in the Shanhaijing, a classical text of mythical geography. The most popular version is Yangwu or Jinwu, the "golden crow", which is usually painted red even though it is described as a crow. According to tradition, there were once ten sun crows that perched in the Fusang tree; each day one of them crossed the sky in a carriage driven by Xihe, mother of the suns. When all ten rose at once and scorched the world, the celestial archer Hou Yi shot down all but one.

In Korea, the samjok-o was regarded as a symbol of the sun and of power during the Goguryeo kingdom, even above the dragon and the bonghwang. It was believed to live in the sun while a toad lived in the moon. In Japan, the Yatagarasu is a crow sent from heaven to guide Emperor Jimmu on his journey from the Kumano region to Yamato. It is often shown with three legs, although the Kojiki does not describe it that way.

Legacy

The samjok-o appears in the television drama Jumong and in the emblem of Jeonbuk Hyundai Motors FC. The Yatagarasu features in the emblems of the Japan Football Association and its national team, and the winner of the Emperor's Cup wears it the following season. In Korea it was considered as a possible replacement for the bonghwang on the state seal.

Ancient sources: The Shanhaijing mentions the Chinese sun bird and the Kojiki records the Japanese Yatagarasu, though without three legs.

Trivia

A red crow. Although described as a crow, the Chinese golden crow is usually painted red rather than black.
A possible misreading. The Kojiki does not say the Yatagarasu has three legs; it is thought the idea arose in the Heian period by identifying it with the Chinese golden crow.
