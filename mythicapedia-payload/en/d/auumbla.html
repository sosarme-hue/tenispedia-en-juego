primeval cow of Norse mythology

Auðumbla

Auðumbla is the primeval cow of Norse mythology, also known as the Great Cosmic Cow. According to the tradition, she nourished the giant Ymir with her milk and, by licking salty ice, freed the god Búri, grandfather of Odin.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, Auðumbla came into being at the beginning of time from the melted ice of Niflheim, in the void of Ginnungagap, just as the giant Ymir did. She fed on the salty ice and rime she licked, while the ice melted in contact with the hot air of Muspelheim. Four rivers of milk flowed from her udders, and Ymir drank from them. Her name, which is generally understood as meaning something like hornless cow rich in milk, is not entirely settled, since it can also suggest other meanings.

One day, while licking the ice, she slowly uncovered beneath its surface the figure of a man. She kept licking for three days: on the first her hair came free, on the second his head, and on the third his whole body. This being was Búri, who later fathered Bor, the father of Odin, Vili and Vé. For some scholars, the cow links the two opposing sides of the myths: she nourishes the giants, descended from Ymir, and brings forth the gods known as the Æsir.

After this episode, Auðumbla does not appear again in the Eddas. Her name returns only in a list of ways to refer to cows, where she is called the noblest of them.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson, composed in the 13th century, in the Gylfaginning section, where her main episode is told, and again in the Nafnaþulur list.

Trivia

Four rivers of milk. According to Snorri, four rivers of milk flowed from Auðumbla's udders, and the giant Ymir fed on them.
A cow with a name of her own. In the Nafnaþulur list, Auðumbla is the only cow mentioned by name, which sets her apart from the rest of the cattle.
