Greek personification of injustice

Adicia (Adikia)

Adicia (Adikia) is, in Greek mythology, the personification of injustice and wrongdoing. She is best known for her opposition to Dike, justice, with whom she appears in ancient depictions.

The myth

Ancient stories say very little about Adicia, and nearly everything we know comes from images and later allusions. Her name comes from the Greek word adikia, meaning injustice, offence or harm, which explains her role as the embodiment of everything opposed to a just order.

The best-known scene shows her defeat at the hands of Dike. Pausanias, the author who describes her in most detail, says she appeared on the chest of Cypselus: a beautiful woman, Dike, punished an ugly one by choking her with one hand and beating her with a staff in the other. Two archaic vase paintings show a similar scene. In the version of the chest preserved in the reference texts, Adicia is a hideous, barbaric woman covered in tattoos, dragged along by Dike.

Her parentage is also unsettled. She is often presented as a daughter of Eris, Discord, although other versions link her to Nyx, Night, and no ancient account settles the question with certainty. She probably appeared in the Orphic Rhapsodies, a theogony attributed to Orpheus that has been lost, where she seems to have been the opposite of Dike.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes her on the chest of Cypselus, and she was probably mentioned in the Orphic Rhapsodies, now lost.
