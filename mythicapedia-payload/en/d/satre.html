Etruscan god identified with Saturn

Satre

Satre is an Etruscan god usually identified with the Roman god Saturn. He appears on the Liver of Piacenza, a bronze model used in haruspicy to interpret the will of the gods.

The myth

According to the surviving evidence, Satre occupies the dark, negative northwest region of the liver and is described as a frightening, dangerous god who hurls his lightning from a dwelling deep within the earth.

He may also appear in the Liber Linteus, the Linen Book preserved as mummy wrappings in Ptolemaic Egypt, although this identification is uncertain. No image in Etruscan art has been identified as Satre, so he remains something of a riddle.

Worship and religion

His role was tied to divination by examining animal entrails, a practice carried out by the haruspices.

Legacy

He is linked to Saturn because of his similar position on the liver, as described by Martianus Capella. It is unclear whether Satre derives from Saturnus, whether Saturnus comes from Etruscan, or whether the two gods are unrelated.

Ancient sources: The Liver of Piacenza and, possibly, the Liber Linteus, an Etruscan text preserved in Egyptian mummy wrappings.
