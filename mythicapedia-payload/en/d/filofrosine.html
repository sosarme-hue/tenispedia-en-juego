Greek daimona of kindness and welcome

Philophrosyne

Philophrosyne is, in Greek mythology, the personification of kindness, optimism and welcome. She belongs to a group of auspicious abstractions counted among the descendants of Zeus.

The myth

According to tradition, Philophrosyne was a daughter of Hephaestus and Aglaia, and had three sisters: Eucleia ("glory"), Euthenia ("prosperity") and Eupheme ("acclamation"). The genealogy comes mainly from the fifth-century AD Neoplatonist philosopher Proclus, who attributes this lineage to the theologians. Some sources link her with the goddesses Eumeleia? No, with Philia ("friendship") and Euphrosyne ("joy"), although they may be the same deity.

The scholar Martin Litchfield West included this genealogy in his reconstruction of the Orphic Rhapsodies, calling it "a new idea" among the descendants of Zeus who are personified abstractions of an auspicious character.

Ancient sources: Proclus, the fifth-century AD Neoplatonist philosopher, records this genealogy, which also appears in Martin Litchfield West's reconstruction of the Orphic Rhapsodies.
