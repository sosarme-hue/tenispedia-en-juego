Inuit goddess of hunting and medicine

Pinga

Pinga is a goddess of hunting and medicine in Inuit religion, closely associated with the sky. She matters because she oversees the relationship between hunters and the animals that sustain them.

The myth

Pinga, whose name means roughly "the one up on high", is a goddess strongly linked to the sky. In Caribou Inuit tradition, she has some authority over caribou herds and becomes angry when people kill more animals than they can eat.

She also acts as a psychopomp: she receives the souls of the newly dead and prepares them for reincarnation.

Worship and religion

Caribou Inuit communities took care not to overhunt, since doing so could anger her. Angakkuit, or shamans, might have seen or communicated with Pinga, and sometimes she was thought to send a spirit to speak with them. Some groups viewed Pinga and Sila as the same or similar, while others distinguished between the two.

Trivia

Guardian of the caribou. In Caribou Inuit communities, the goddess was associated with control over the herds, giving hunting a dimension of respect for the animals.
A goddess with two names. Some Inuit groups considered Pinga and Sila to be the same deity, while others saw them as separate beings.
