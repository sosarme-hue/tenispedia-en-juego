Norn and valkyrie of Norse mythology

Skuld

Skuld is one of the three Norns of Norse mythology, alongside Urðr and Verðandi, and is described as one of the figures who decide the fates of people. The sources also place her at times as a valkyrie, which links her to battle and to the choice of the slain.

The myth

According to the Eddas, Skuld belongs to a trio of Norns who weave the threads of fate beneath the ash tree Yggdrasil. The sources do not give a detailed account of her origin or lineage; they present her as a figure already established in the order of the world, together with her sisters Urðr and Verðandi.

Her name is linked in Old Norse to words meaning debt, obligation or necessity. For this reason she is often understood as the Norn associated with what is to come, what ought to happen or what will be needed. Some scholars note, however, that the texts do not sharply divide time among the Norns: all three seem to be tied to fate together, and past, present and future appear intertwined.

In the Poetic Edda, Skuld is mentioned in the poem Völuspá. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the book Gylfaginning, the youngest Norn is also described as a valkyrie. Along with Gudr and Róta, she rides forever to take the slain and decide the outcome of battles. In at least two Old Norse poems she is named as a valkyrie, showing that her role in myth went beyond weaving fate.

Ancient sources: Völuspá and other poems of the Poetic Edda, Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) and the Nafnaþulur addition.

Trivia

A name meaning debt. In Old Norse her name is associated with debt, lack or guilt. It shares its origin with modal verbs such as should in English and skulle in Swedish.
Also a valkyrie. Snorri Sturluson describes her in the Prose Edda as one of the valkyries who choose the warriors who have fallen.
