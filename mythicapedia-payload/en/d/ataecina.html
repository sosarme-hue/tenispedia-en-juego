Iberian goddess of spring and healing

Ataegina

Ataegina is a goddess of the Iberian Peninsula, worshipped by the Lusitanians, Carpetani and Celtiberians. Linked to rebirth, spring, the moon and healing, she matters for blending indigenous, Celtic and Roman traditions.

The myth

No extended narrative about Ataegina survives; her figure is reconstructed mainly from inscriptions.

She was a chthonic deity tied to the underworld and the night, but also to spring, fertility and the moon. Her sacred animal was the goat, and she was often shown with a cypress branch. The Romans identified her with Proserpina and the Carthaginians with Tanit.

Worship and religion

Her cult spread mainly across Lusitania and Baetica, with sanctuaries at Alcuéscar and Malpartida de Cáceres. Worshippers invoked her for healing and sometimes, through a devotio, to curse someone; inscriptions call her servatrix, guardian of health.

Legacy

In 2005 a trans-Neptunian body found beyond Pluto was proposed to be named after her, since Proserpina was Pluto's wife. The International Astronomical Union rejected the name and chose Haumea instead.

Ancient sources: Votive inscriptions from Hispania and Lusitania, such as the one from Augusta Emerita; Pliny the Elder places Turobriga in Celtic Beturia.
