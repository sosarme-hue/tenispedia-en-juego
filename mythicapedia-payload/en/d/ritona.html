Celtic goddess of fords and river crossings

Ritona

Ritona was a Celtic goddess worshipped in Roman Gaul, above all among the Treveri of the Moselle. Her name means she of the ford, so she is generally regarded as a protector of fords and river crossings.

The myth

No account of her origin, family or deeds has survived. She is known only from Latin dedicatory inscriptions, so any story told about her would be a modern reconstruction.

Scholars link her to the Gaulish idea that river crossings were sacred places that called for religious care. Some also read her as a mother goddess of the Treveri, though others point out that this label fits many deities of the Mediterranean world.

Worship and religion

Her main cult lay at Augusta Treverorum (Trier), in the Altbach valley sanctuary, where she had a temple with an exedra and an adjoining kitchen, probably used to prepare sacrificial banquets. Several dedications tie her to the public life of the colony, made in honour of the Divine House and the divine powers of the emperors. She is also attested at Contiomagus (Pachten), at a river crossing, and at Uzès. There is no evidence that her cult survived.

Ancient sources: No ancient literary texts mentioning her have survived; her name appears in six Latin dedicatory inscriptions, most from the territory of the Treveri.

Trivia

A name related to Welsh. The root ritu- survives in Welsh rhyd, Cornish rid and Breton rit, all meaning ford.
Two forms of the name. She appears as Ritona and as Pritona. On one dedication from Trier both forms stand together: Ritona Pritona.
