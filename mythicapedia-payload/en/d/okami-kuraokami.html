Shinto dragon deity of rain and snow

Okami (Kuraokami)

Okami, also called Kuraokami, is a legendary dragon and Shinto deity of rain and snow. He appears in the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, and his figure reflects the central role of water in Japanese religion and daily life.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded in the Kojiki, the siblings Izanagi and Izanami gave birth to the islands and gods of Japan. Izanami died from burns while giving birth to the fire deity Kagutsuchi, and in his fury Izanagi killed his son with a sword.

Several deities arose from the blood of the fire god. In the Kojiki, Kuraokami was born from blood that dripped from the sword's hilt onto Izanagi's fingers. In the Nihon Shoki, Kagutsuchi's body, cut into three pieces, gave rise to three gods, among them Kuraokami.

In the genealogies, Okami is the father of the goddess Hikawa-hime, who married a grandson of Susanoo and became an ancestor of Ōkuninushi. His name combines kura, meaning dark, with okami, an old term for a dragon guardian of water.

Worship and religion

Shinto shrines dedicated to water kami such as Okami and Takaokami are visited mainly during droughts to pray for rain. Niukawakami Jinja in Nara is a center of prayers to Kuraokami, and there are several Okami shrines across Japan, as well as many Kibune Jinja dedicated to the water-god Takaokami. His worship remains alive.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (c. 680) and the Nihon Shoki (c. 720) recount his origin, and the Man'yōshū mentions him as a rain dragon living on a hill.
