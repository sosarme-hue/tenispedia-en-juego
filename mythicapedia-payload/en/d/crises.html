Trojan priest of Apollo in Greek mythology

Chryses

Chryses is, in Greek mythology, a priest of Apollo whose daughter, Chryseis, provokes the god's anger and sets off the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles. His story belongs to the Trojan War and is one of the threads that lead into the Iliad.

The myth

According to a tradition recorded by Eustathius of Thessalonica, Chryses was the brother of Briseus, father of Briseis, and both were sons of a man named Ardys, about whom nothing else is known. Other accounts make him a son of King Minos of Crete, showing that genealogies varied from one author to another. His city, Chryse, lay in the Troad near Troy, and Chryses served Apollo at the god's sanctuary there.

During the Trojan War, before the events narrated in the Iliad, Agamemnon seized Chryseis as a prize of war. Chryses, heartbroken, offered a ransom to win her back, but the king refused. The priest then prayed to Apollo, who sent a plague upon the Greek camp. To end it, Agamemnon had to return the girl, on the advice of the seer Calchas. As compensation, he demanded that Achilles hand over his own captive, Briseis, which enraged the hero and led him to refuse to keep fighting alongside the Greeks. The episode is remarkable not for the abduction, which was commonplace at the time, but for the refusal to return the girl despite her father's plea, which was what provoked the divine wrath.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, where he appears as a priest of Apollo whose ransom offer is rejected by Agamemnon.

Trivia

The meaning of his name. His Greek name, Χρύσης (Khrýsēs), means “golden.”
Two names for the same young woman. In some sources, Chryses' daughter also appears under the name Astynome.
