eighteenth Jain tirthankara

Aranatha

Aranatha is the eighteenth tirthankara of Jainism, one of the teachers who, in this tradition, have reached liberation and show the way to it. He is still honoured across India, above all by Jain communities.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, Aranatha was the eighteenth tirthankara of the present half cycle of time, known as avasarpini. He was born at Hastinapur to King Sudarshana and Queen Devi (Mitra), of the Ikshvaku dynasty. Texts also describe him as the eighth chakravartin and the thirteenth kamadeva. Like every chakravartin, he conquered the lands and wished to write his name on the mountain slopes, but on finding the names of his predecessors already there, he saw how small his ambitions were.

He then gave up his throne and became an ascetic. After a life of penance, he attained moksha on Mount Shikharji, at an age over 84,000 years, and became a siddha, a liberated soul who has destroyed all his karmas. His height is given as 30 dhanusha. He is shown without ornaments, clothes or weapons, a symbol of pure knowledge and complete control of the senses.

Worship and religion

He is recognised by the fish, the emblem that makes his images identifiable. He is venerated across the subcontinent at important temples, such as the Digambar Jain Mandir in Hastinapur. On Mount Shikharji, a shrine preserves his footprints, which pilgrims worship. In the south, the Chaturmukha basadi at Karkala in Karnataka, completed in 1586, houses life-size images of Aranatha facing the four cardinal directions. His worship remains alive among Jains.

Ancient sources: The Svayambhūstotra by Samantabhadra devotes twenty verses to him. He also appears in the Yashastilaka by Somadeva Suri and the Vividha Tirtha Kalpa by Jinaprabha Suri.

Trivia

An inscription at Mathura. An old stupa at Mathura bears an inscription dated 157 CE that mentions an image of Aranatha. Somadeva Suri and Jinaprabha Suri instead attribute the stupa to Suparshvanatha.
No ornaments. His images wear no jewellery, clothing or weapons, and the fish is what distinguishes them.
