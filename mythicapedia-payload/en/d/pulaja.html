sage of Hindu mythology

Pulaha

Pulaha is one of the seven great sages, or Saptarshi, of Hindu mythology, regarded as a son of Brahma and a progenitor of humankind. He appears in epic and Puranic texts, and his hermitage was a place of retreat and pilgrimage.

The myth

According to tradition, Pulaha was born from the mind of Brahma, and some accounts place him among the sons who sprang from Brahma's head. The Mahabharata names him as one of the Seven Sages and also as one of the Prajapatis, the progenitors of creation.

The Bhagavata Purana adds details about his family life. He married Kshama, with whom he had three sons, and, at his father's request, he also married Gati, a daughter of Kardama and Devahuti, with whom he had three more sons. In the closing episode, King Bharata gives up his kingdom and retreats to Pulaha's hermitage.

Worship and religion

His hermitage, known as the Pulaha-ashrama, became a place of pilgrimage. There the shalagram-shila stones were gathered, and Hindus worship them as gods.

Ancient sources: He first appears in the Mahabharata and is also mentioned in the Atharva Veda, the Laws of Manu and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Pulaha is traced to the sense 'destroyer of pula', where pula can mean breadth or extent, bristling hair, or a particular gait of horses.
Sacred stones. His hermitage was the source of the shalagram-shila stones, which Hindus worship as gods.
