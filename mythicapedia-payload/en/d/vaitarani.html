infernal river of Hindu and Buddhist mythology

Vaitarani

The Vaitarani is a river separating the world of the living from the realm of Yama, god of death, in Hindu traditions, and it has a Buddhist counterpart in the hells. It matters because it is the first obstacle the soul must cross after death, and how easily depends on one's conduct in life.

The myth

According to Puranic texts such as the Garuda Purana, the Vaitarani stretches for more than a hundred yojanas and runs between the Earth and Naraka, the hellish realm of Yama. Its waters, full of blood, pus and filth, teem with vultures and fish, while the righteous see it filled with something like nectar.

Virtuous souls cross riding a sacred cow, unharmed, whereas sinners must struggle across amid torment and attacks by infernal creatures. To ease the passage, a cow is offered to the dying person. The Mahabharata says the river can destroy every sin, and the Ramayana tells how Ravana crossed it on his flying vimana.

Worship and religion

Funeral rites include offerings and symbolic gifts, such as the gifting of the Vaitarani cow, to help the soul's passage. The Vaitarani Vrata is observed on the eleventh day of the dark fortnight of Margashirsha, with worship and the donation of a cow.

Legacy

Its name means "that which carries across". It is symbolically linked to a river in Maharashtra, though this link is devotional rather than geographical. It is compared with the Greek Styx and with the Japanese Buddhist Sanzu river.

Ancient sources: Garuda Purana, Agni Purana, Devi Bhagavata Purana, Mahabharata, Ramayana and the Sikh Guru Granth Sahib.

Trivia

River of ash. In East Asia the Buddhist river is known as the Ash River Hell, a name adapted from the Sanskrit.
Cow for the afterlife. The cow offered to the dying is called the Vaitarani cow and, according to the Agni Purana, a formula is spoken when giving it.
