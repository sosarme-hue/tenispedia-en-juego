wife of Lycaon and mother of Callisto in Greek myth

Nonacris

In Greek myth, Nonacris is the wife of King Lycaon of Arcadia and the mother of Callisto. According to tradition, the Arcadian town of Nonacris took its name from her, which explains the origin of the place name.

The myth

In Greek myth, Nonacris was the wife of King Lycaon of Arcadia and the mother of Callisto. Tradition held that the town of Nonacris had taken its name from her.

Other accounts say that Lycaon's wife was instead the nymph Cyllene. From this town, Hermes and Evander received the epithets Nonacriates and Nonacrius, meaning Arcadian in a general sense.

Ancient writers placed the town near Pheneus, at the foot of the Aroanian mountains. Pausanias describes that in his day only barely visible ruins remained.

Legacy

The sources consulted record no later traces of her figure beyond the place name and the epithets she lent to other figures.

Ancient sources: Herodotus and Pausanias mention the town of Nonacris, from whose name the myth derives; the sources do not say which ancient text names her as Lycaon's wife.

Trivia

An oath beside a spring. Herodotus tells how Cleomenes planned to gather the Arcadian leaders near Nonacris to swear oaths on the waters of the Styx, believed to be the water gods swore by. In reality it was a spring flowing from a rock above a walled pool.
