Mesopotamian mythical object of divine authority

Tablet of Destinies

The Tablet of Destinies is an object in Mesopotamian mythology: a clay tablet inscribed in cuneiform that conferred rule over the universe on whoever held it. It matters because it presents divine sovereignty as a legal document that could be stolen and recovered.

The myth

According to the tradition, the tablet was a permanent legal document, also impressed with cylinder seals, which conferred on Enlil his supreme authority as ruler of the universe. Only he could transform present circumstances back into their original state, redefining the course of fate.

In the Sumerian poem Ninurta and the Turtle the tablet is held by Enki, who keeps it in the Abzu, the primeval sea. There the bird Imdugud steals it, and Ninurta recovers it and returns it to Enlil. The Akkadian poem Anzû tells of a similar theft, committed by the bird Anzû.

In the Babylonian epic Enuma Elish, Tiamat gives the tablet to Kingu when she takes him as her consort and places him in command of her army. After defeating Kingu, Marduk seizes it, strengthening his dominance among the gods.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Sumerian poem Ninurta and the Turtle, the Akkadian Anzû and the Babylonian epic Enuma Elish. It is also referenced in a Neo-Babylonian copy of an inscription of Sennacherib held in the British Museum.
