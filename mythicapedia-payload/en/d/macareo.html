son of Aeolus in Greek mythology

Macareus

Macareus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Aeolus. His story is marked by incest with his sister Canace and by his descendants' link to Apollo.

The myth

According to the myth, Macareus was one of the sons of Aeolus, although the accounts disagree about which Aeolus was his father: the lord of the winds or the king of Tyrrhenia. In the latter version, his mother was Amphithea.

Macareus fell in love with his sister Canace and they had a child together, Amphissa, since they did not know that incest was forbidden to mortals. When Aeolus discovered this, he ordered the baby to be thrown to the dogs and sent Canace a sword, with which she took her own life. Macareus escaped punishment and took refuge at the sanctuary of Delphi, where he served as a priest of Apollo. Amphissa became the lover of the god and bore him a son, Amphisus. Another of his daughters, Euboea, was also loved by Apollo and became the mother of Agreus. In another version, Macareus killed himself with the same sword as his sister. Ovid, meanwhile, says that Phoebus seduced Issa, another daughter of Macareus, disguised as a shepherd.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions Macareus and his daughter Issa in his accounts of Apollo's love affairs.
