son of Rama and Sita in the Ramayana

Kusha

Kusha is one of the twin sons of Rama and Sita in the Hindu epic Ramayana, the elder of the two alongside his brother Lava. After Rama's death he ruled the southern region of Kosala and founded a new capital there.

The myth

According to tradition, Rama told his brother Lakshmana to leave the pregnant Sita at the hermitage of the sage Valmiki, because the public doubted her after her captivity in Lanka. There she gave birth to the twins Kusha and Lava, who were educated and trained in warfare by the sage.

Accounts differ on his birth: the Kathasaritsagara says that Valmiki, noticing the boy missing while Sita bathed, created Kusha from kusha grass, fearing the news would distress her.

At a horse sacrifice held by Rama, the brothers recited the Ramayana before the assembly. Rama recognised them as his sons, reconciled with Sita and named them his heirs before his death. In some versions they captured the sacrificial horse and defeated Rama's brothers. After Rama died, Kusha ruled Kosala and married Kumudvati, daughter of the naga chief Kumuda, with whom he had a son, Atithi.

Ancient sources: Valmiki, the composer of the Ramayana, and the Raghuvamsha, which records his lineage, mention him; the Kathasaritsagara gives a different account of his birth.
