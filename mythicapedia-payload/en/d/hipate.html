minor Muse of Greek mythology

Hypate

Hypate is a minor Muse of Greek mythology, associated with the lyre and one of its strings. She was worshipped at Delphi alongside her sisters Mese and Nete.

The myth

Hypate was one of three Muses who personified the strings of the lyre, the instrument favoured by Apollo. Her name means 'the highest' and refers to the tallest string.

In Plutarch's Quaestiones Convivales, these Muses also received a cosmic role: Hypate governed the highest region, that of the fixed stars, Mese the planets, and Nete everything below the moon. Plutarch still recognised Urania as the chief heavenly Muse. Some traditions identified them with the Apollonides, daughters of Apollo, named Cephisso, Apollonis and Borysthenis.

Worship and religion

These three Muses were worshipped at Delphi, home of the Temple of Apollo and its Oracle.

Legacy

Her name entered ancient Greek music theory, where it designates one of the notes of the tetrachord, as did those of her sisters.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, in his Quaestiones Convivales, which gives her a role in the order of the cosmos.

Trivia

A namesake city. The ancient city of Hypate, today Ypati in Greece, is unrelated to this Muse. Its name means 'the height of Oeta'.
Music and the cosmos. The lyre served as a model for explaining the universe: each string corresponded to a part of the cosmos.
