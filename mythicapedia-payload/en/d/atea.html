Polynesian goddess of light

Atea

Atea is a Polynesian deity who gives light in the Marquesas Islands and is killed by her son Tane in the Tuamotu Islands.

The myth

In Marquesan tradition, Atea is the giver of light. In one version he is a brother of Tane, both sons of Toho; in another, he creates himself and brings forth Ono. Together they break the boundless darkness of Po, the underworld where Tanaoa, lord of darkness, and Mutu-hei, silence, had lived for eternity.

Atea and Ono defeat Tanaoa and Mutu-hei and confine the gods of night within set limits. From the struggle comes Atanua, the dawn, whom Atea marries; their children are the lesser gods and humankind. Unverified sources make one of their sons Tu-mea, the first man.

In the Tuamotu Islands the story differs: Tane, Atea's second son, kills him. After the death of his firstborn Tahu, the two gods switch sexes, and Atea tries to kidnap Tane, who flees to earth and, according to the myth, becomes the first cannibal.

Trivia

A primordial cannibal. According to the Tuamotu story, Tane becomes so hungry on earth that he eats a man, thus becoming the first cannibal.
A divine change of sex. In the Tuamotu myth, the gods switch sexes after the death of Tahu, Atea's first son.
