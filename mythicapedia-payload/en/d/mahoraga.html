Mahoraga

The Mahoraga are a race of serpentine deities in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism, related to the nagas and regarded as the exalted ones among the Uragas. They matter chiefly as protectors of the Dharma in Buddhist tradition.

The myth

According to the accounts, the Mahoraga are primordial beings described as huge subterranean serpents who lie on their sides and turn the earth, which causes earthquakes. They are also associated with instrumental music, like the kinnara.

Artists depict them in varied ways: sometimes with a humanoid body and serpent skin, and other times with a serpent head on a muscular divine body. While the nagas are linked to the cobra, the Mahoraga are associated with pythons and other large snakes. In Buddhism they form one of the eight classes of deities said to protect the Dharma. The Śariputraparipṛcchā Sūtra states that one who practices generosity, upholds the Dharma, adapts to hardship and is inclined toward anger may be reborn as a shikigami.

Legacy

In Gege Akutami's manga series Jujutsu Kaisen, the character Megumi Fushiguro harbors a monster called Mahoraga. The original Japanese name is Makora, derived from Mahāla, one of the Twelve Heavenly Generals of Buddhism.

Ancient sources: The Buddhist Śariputraparipṛcchā Sūtra (T. 1465), which describes rebirth as a shikigami for those who practice generosity and uphold the Dharma.

Trivia

A translation error. In most English translations of Jujutsu Kaisen, the name Makora is mistakenly rendered as Mahoraga, and that form is the best known among Western fans.
