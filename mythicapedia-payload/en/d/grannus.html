Celtic god of healing and sacred waters

Grannus

Grannus was a Celtic healing god linked to springs, wells and curative waters, most often identified by the Romans with Apollo. His usual companion was the goddess Sirona, and his cult spread across Gaul, the Germanies, Raetia and the Danube provinces.

The myth

Ancient texts hardly narrate stories about Grannus, so his figure is known mainly from inscriptions and brief mentions. His main trait is healing, and the sources attribute no episodes or genealogy of his own.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped above all at springs and wells, with large sanctuaries at Grand in the Vosges and Faimingen in Raetia. At Limoges the community celebrated the Ten Nights of Grannus, a festival requiring running water over ten days and nights. Most of his dedicants were officials and soldiers. No living cult survives.

Legacy

His name lives on in place-names such as Aachen, linked to the medieval Aquae Granni, and Grignols in the Dordogne.

Ancient sources: Cassius Dio, who mentions him in the account of Caracalla's illness; also numerous Latin dedications and a Greek inscription from Ephesus.

Trivia

Grannus and Mars. At Chassenon he appears as Mars Grannus Victor, the only inscription linking him to Mars, alongside the local goddess Cobrandia.
Caracalla and the god. According to Cassius Dio, the emperor Caracalla sought help from Apollo Grannus during his illness, alongside Asclepius and Serapis, without relief.
