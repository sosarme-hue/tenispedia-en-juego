Roman god of the underworld

Pluto

Pluto is the Roman god of the underworld, ruler of the dead and brother of Jupiter and Neptune. His name, taken from the Greek Plouton, means the rich one, so he was also linked to the wealth of the earth, such as crops and metals.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Saturn and Ops had six children: Pluto, Neptune, Jupiter, Ceres, Vesta and Juno. After Jupiter drove his father from power, the three brothers divided the world by lot: the sky went to Jupiter, the sea to Neptune and the underworld to Pluto. His guardian is Cerberus, a three-headed hound, and his court includes the Furies and the Fates.

The best-known myth tells of the abduction of Proserpina, daughter of Ceres. Pluto carried her off in his chariot while she gathered flowers on Mount Etna, in Sicily. Ceres obtained permission from Jupiter for the girl to spend half the year with her mother and half with her husband, a traditional explanation of the seasons. According to the tradition, Pluto was implacable yet just, since all mortals end up in his realm regardless of rank.

In the story of Orpheus, the god appears as the holder of a object of quest: the poet went down to his domain to win back Eurydice and moved him with his music. In other tales, Theseus and Pirithous tried to carry off Proserpina and were caught by the Furies, until Hercules freed them.

Worship and religion

The Greeks distinguished between Hades, a gloomy god with few temples, and Pluto, a more benign figure associated with the Eleusinian Mysteries. There he was venerated alongside Persephone as a couple who welcomed initiates into the afterlife. In Rome he was called Dis Pater and Orcus. Black cattle were sacrificed to him, and he was connected with the purification rites called Februa, which gave February its name.

Legacy

The name Pluto became the most common name for the ruler of the underworld in later Western literature and art, with forms such as Pluton in French and German and Plutone in Italian. Orpheus's encounter with the god became one of the most significant mythological themes of the classical tradition and was repeated for centuries in literature and art.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Apollodorus, Lucretius, Cicero, Varro, Ennius and Plato mention Pluto, and the Orphic Hymns include a hymn addressed to him.

Trivia

A name that means wealth. Pluto comes from the Greek Plouton, meaning the rich one, and in antiquity he was sometimes confused with Ploutos, the god of wealth.
A god who is never satisfied. Because he receives everything into his bosom and is never sated, he was called Vedius, the evil god, and Jupiter Stygius.
