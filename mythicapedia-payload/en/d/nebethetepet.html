ancient Egyptian goddess of offerings

Nebethetepet

Nebethetepet is a goddess of ancient Egypt whose name means “Lady of the Offerings” or “Satisfied Lady.” She was regarded as the female counterpart of Atum in Heliopolis, although her overall significance was rather modest.

The myth

According to the Heliopolitan tradition, Nebethetepet was the female companion of Atum, the primordial god from whom creation emerged. She was associated with Atum's hand, that is, with the female principle of creation that worked through him.

In some sources her name also serves as a title for Hathor, which shows how easily her figure blended with that of other goddesses. Beyond that role, she barely appears in the surviving accounts.

Worship and religion

She was venerated in Heliopolis, one of Egypt's great religious centres, alongside Atum. Her name, which refers to offerings and satisfaction, suggests she may have been invoked in the context of cult practices and the offerings made to the gods. Details about her rites are scarce.
