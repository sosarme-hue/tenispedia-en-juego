Egyptian god of the Western Desert oases

Igai

Igai was a minor Egyptian deity associated with the oases of the Western Desert, known by the title "Lord of the Oasis". Today he is best known in part because a fossil dinosaur bears his name.

The myth

The sources offer few stories about Igai, so no detailed account of his deeds or family survives. He is shown in human form, with two was sceptres on his head, signs that spell out his name.

Worship and religion

His cult is attested from the Third Dynasty onwards, and he was an important god in Dakhleh Oasis during the Old Kingdom. The Middle Kingdom was the golden age of his worship, and most references date from that period. The main urban centre of his cult in the New Valley was the 19th Nome of Upper Egypt. Few artefacts document his veneration. Outside the oases, his name appears in a graffito at the funerary temple of Niuserre at Abusir.

Legacy

The dinosaur Igai semkhu, whose name means "Forgotten Lord of the Oasis", was discovered in the Egyptian desert, in the area now known as the Kharga Oasis, and dates to the Campanian age of the Late Cretaceous.

Trivia

A dinosaur named after him. The dinosaur Igai semkhu was found in the Egyptian desert and is named after this deity.
A name written on his head. He is depicted with two was sceptres on his head, which together spell out his name.
