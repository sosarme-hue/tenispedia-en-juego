Egyptian creator god and patron of Thebes

Amun

Amun is one of the chief gods of ancient Egypt, linked to creation, kingship and hiddenness, whose cult reached its peak when he merged with the sun god Ra as Amun-Ra, the national god of the New Kingdom. His importance spread beyond Egypt, and he came to be identified with Zeus and Jupiter.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, the name Amun means something like "the Hidden One" or "the Invisible", and he appears in the Pyramid Texts, where he is addressed as a great god whose name is unknown. Already in the Old Kingdom he appears with his wife Amunet as a member of the Ogdoad, the group of eight deities in the Hermopolitan creation myth.

When Thebes became the most important city in the country after expelling the Hyksos, its local god became a national god. The pharaohs credited their victories to Amun, and his fusion with the sun god Ra gave rise to Amun-Ra, a self-created creator and patron of the poor and troubled. He was shown with blue skin and, in art, as a man with a ram's head, an animal linked to virility, which also connected him with fertility and with Min.

In the Theban tradition, Amun is the father of the triad formed with Mut, his wife, and the moon god Khonsu, their son. During the Amarna period, the pharaoh Akhenaten tried to impose the cult of Aten, but after his death the worship of Amun was restored. Later, the high priests of Amun came to rule Egypt in practice during the Third Intermediate Period.

Worship and religion

The main centre of Amun's cult was the Precinct of Amun-Ra at Karnak in Thebes, begun in the reign of Senusret I and expanded over centuries, with the Great Hypostyle Hall and the great entrance pylon. His oracle in the Siwa Oasis, in the Libyan Desert, remained the only oracle of Amun throughout. His temple priests held enormous political and economic power.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the term "ammonia" and the phrase "horns of Ammon", and he was identified with Zeus Ammon in Greece and Jupiter Ammon in Rome. Greeks and Romans knew his oracle and temple in the Siwa Oasis.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Old Kingdom Pyramid Texts and in New Kingdom hymns and stelae, such as the Hymn to Amun-Ra.

Trivia
