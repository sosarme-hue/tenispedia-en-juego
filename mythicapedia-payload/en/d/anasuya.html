Anasuya

Anasuya, whose name means free from jealousy, is the wife of the sage Atri in Hindu mythology. Venerated as Sati Anasuya, the chaste wife, she is the mother of Dattatreya, Durvasa and Chandra, and her story combines devotion, austerity and miracles.

The myth

According to the tradition, Anasuya was the daughter of the sage Kardama and Devahuti, and the sister of the sage Kapila, who was also her teacher. Married to Atri, she lived with him in a small hermitage in the forests of Chitrakuta, where her austerities gave her miraculous powers. When Rama and Sita visited during their exile, she gave Sita an ointment that would keep her beauty forever.

Another episode tells how the sun stopped rising because of a faithful wife's vow, and Anasuya persuaded her to restore light to the world. Her most famous trial came when the wives of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, jealous of her virtue, asked their husbands to test her. Disguised as ascetics, the gods asked for food while naked; she turned them into infants with enchanted water and nursed them without losing her chastity. Repentant, the gods asked to be born as her sons: Chandra, Dattatreya and Durvasa.

Worship and religion

At the Anasuya Devi Temple in Uttarakhand, beside the Amrit Ganga river, a tributary of the Alaknanda, she is worshipped as a goddess. The Sati Anasuya Ashram in Chitrakoot, 16 km from the town, remains a place of pilgrimage where tradition places her family's life, surrounded by thick forests.

Legacy

Her story inspired two Telugu films: Sati Anasuya (1957), directed by Kadaru Nagabhushanam, and a 1971 version directed by B. A. Subba Rao.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana, the Bhagavata Purana, the Markandeya Purana, the Shiva Purana and the Brahma Purana.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Anasuya combines the negative prefix an with asuya, meaning jealousy, hence her sense of being free from envy.
Iron beans. According to one legend, Narada claimed Anasuya was so chaste she could cook iron beans. The three goddesses failed at the task and united in envy of her.
