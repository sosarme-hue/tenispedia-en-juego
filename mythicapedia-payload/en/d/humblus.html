legendary king of Denmark

Humblus

Humblus is a legendary king of Denmark, one of the earliest in the prehistory of the country according to the Scandinavian tradition recorded by Saxo Grammaticus. His story is notable for the oath taken over stones by which kings were chosen.

The myth

According to Saxo Grammaticus in his Gesta Danorum, Humblus was a son of Danus, patriarch of all the Danes, and a brother of Lotherus. Both were sons of the matriarch Grytha, who was highly revered among the Teutons.

Tradition held that in ancient times a king was chosen by swearing an oath over stones planted in the ground. Humblus was elected this way, but Lotherus, driven by ambition, went to war to seize power. Defeated, Humblus had to sell the crown to his brother in exchange for his life, the only way out his brother offered him.

Ancient sources: Saxo Grammaticus, in his Gesta Danorum, is the main source. The Chronicon Lethrense and Olaus Magnus's History of Sweden also mention the motif, and some scholars think Humblus may be the same king as Humli in the Hervarar saga.

Trivia

Kings on stones. Choosing a king by swearing over stones also appears in other Scandinavian sources, such as the Chronicon Lethrense and Olaus Magnus's Swedish history.
