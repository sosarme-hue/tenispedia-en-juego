fierce protector in Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism

Ekajaṭī

Ekajaṭī is one of the most powerful and fierce protectors of Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism and one of the three principal protectors of the Nyingma school. Also known as Mahācaṇḍatārā, Blue Tārā or Black Tārā, she is counted among the 21 Taras.

The myth

According to Tibetan legend, the tantric master Padmasambhava pierced her right eye so that she could subdue Tibetan demons more effectively. Since then she has guarded secret mantras and tantric teachings, helps practitioners decipher the symbolic codes of the dakinis, and judges when certain doctrines may be revealed.

Sri Singha is said to have entrusted her with the Heart Essence teachings of Dzogchen. With the great master Longchenpa she was unusually personal: she appeared to him during a ritual, corrected his pronunciation and told him to imitate her when he chanted the mantra.

Worship and religion

She is regarded as the principal guardian of Dzogchen, one of the most closely guarded teachings in Tibetan Buddhism. Her practice centres on protective rites, and the Troma Tantra, included in the Vima Nyingtig, is devoted to them. Düsum Khyenpa, the first Karmapa, meditated on her from early childhood.

Ancient sources: The Troma Tantra, also called Ekajaṭĭ Khros Ma'i rGyud, gathers rites devoted to her and is part of the Vima Nyingtig.

Trivia

A pierced eye. According to Tibetan legend, Padmasambhava pierced her right eye so she could subdue Tibetan demons more effectively.
A song in the dakini language. When Longchenpa recited the mantra, she scolded him and sang it herself in a strange, harmonious melody in the language of the dakinis.
Iron wolves. From her left hand she emanates a hundred ferocious iron wolves.
