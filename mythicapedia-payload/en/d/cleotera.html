daughter of Pandareus, handmaiden of the Erinyes in Greek myth

Cleothera

Cleothera is one of the daughters of Pandareus and Harmothoë, sister of Aedon and Merope. In the Greek tradition her story tells of a young woman raised by Aphrodite who ends up serving the Erinyes.

The myth

According to the myth, Cleothera's parents, Pandareus and Harmothoë, were natives of western Asia Minor or Crete. After Pandareus tried to steal a sacred golden dog that guarded Zeus's temple in Crete, the family fled to Athens and then to Sicily, where the king of the gods eventually punished them with death.

Orphaned, Cleothera and her sister Merope were raised by Aphrodite, while their eldest sister Aedon married Zethus, king of Thebes. The Olympian goddesses showered the girls with gifts: Hera gave wisdom and beauty, Artemis tall stature, and Athena the womanly arts. When they came of age, Aphrodite went up to heaven to ask Zeus for good marriages, and in her absence the sisters were seized by the Erinyes, with the help of the winds or the Harpies. In another version, the Harpies carried off all three sisters, Aedon included, and the Erinyes made them their servants forever.

Ancient sources: The traveller Pausanias, in the 2nd century AD, describes an ancient painting of the myth by Polygnotus, which labels the maidens as Cameiro and Clytie.

Trivia

Other names in the painting. Polygnotus painted the myth with the maidens named Cameiro and Clytie. They are shown crowned with flowers and playing dice.
Sisters with different fates. In some versions Aedon was not carried off with her sisters, while in others she suffered the same fate.
