Greek hero, son of Alector

Iphis

Iphis is a hero of Greek myth, son of Alector, one of the kings of Argos. He matters for his role in the war of the Seven against Thebes and for the family line that links Argos with Thebes and with stories such as that of Phaethon.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Iphis played a quiet but decisive part in the expedition of the Seven against Thebes. Polynices, who was preparing the campaign, advised him how to win over Amphiaraus, who was reluctant to take part. The key was to give Eriphyle the necklace of Harmonia, Amphiaraus's wife, since with that gift Eriphyle would persuade her husband.

Iphis was the father of Eteocles and of Evadne, who married Capaneus. In the end he left the kingdom to his grandson Sthenelus, son of his son-in-law Capaneus. Accounts differ: other tellings say his daughter Clymene married Philacus and had Phaethon with Helios, and that his daughter Laodice was the mother of Capaneus by Hipponous.

Trivia

Mother of Phaethon. According to some accounts, Clymene, daughter of Iphis, was the mother of Phaethon by Helios, and also had Iphiclus with her husband Philacus.
