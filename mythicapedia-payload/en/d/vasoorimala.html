Hindu goddess of contagious diseases

Vasoorimala

Vasoorimala is a Hindu goddess worshipped in Kerala and in the Kodagu region of Karnataka, believed to protect against contagious diseases such as smallpox, chickenpox and measles. She is venerated as a minor deity in temples dedicated to Bhadrakali or Shiva.

The myth

According to the Markandeya Purana, the asura Darikan (also spelled Darukan) was defeated in battle by the goddess Bhadrakali, who emerged from the third eye of Shiva. Kerala folklore, recorded by Kottarathil Sankunni in the Aithihyamala, adds Manodari, Darikan's wife. Facing her husband's imminent defeat, she performed intense penance on Kailasa. Pleased, Shiva gave her the sweat from his body and told her that if she sprinkled it on people, they would give her everything she needed.

When Manodari saw Bhadrakali returning with her husband's head, she sprinkled the water on the goddess's body, and smallpox pustules appeared on it. Bhadrakali pierced Manodari's eyes, named her Vasoorimala and made her her companion. Another version says the goddess arose from Shiva's consciousness when the god contracted smallpox.

Worship and religion

Vasoorimala is worshipped as a subordinate deity in temples such as Kodungallur Bhagavathy, Valiyakulangara Devi and the Vasoorimala Devi Temple in Mavelikara. In north Kerala she is honoured through theyyam, a ritual dance in which the performer embodies the goddess. Tradition says that during a smallpox epidemic people began worshipping the disease in theyyam form to be freed from it. Today this theyyam is performed to seek healing from illness, so her worship remains alive.

Ancient sources: No ancient text names her directly; the story of Darikan and Bhadrakali comes from the Markandeya Purana, while her Keralan version appears in the Aithihyamala, a modern work by Kottarathil Sankunni.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Vasoorimala literally means chain of smallpox pustules, since vasoori is the Malayalam word for smallpox.
A theyyam for healing. The theyyam dedicated to this goddess has become a rite for asking for the healing of illnesses.
