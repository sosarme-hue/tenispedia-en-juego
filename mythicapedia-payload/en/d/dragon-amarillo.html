Yellow Dragon

The Yellow Dragon is the zoomorphic incarnation of the Yellow Emperor, the centre of the universe in Chinese religion and mythology. It represents the element earth and completes the group of the Four Symbols.

The myth

According to the tradition, the Yellow Emperor was conceived by Fubao, who became pregnant after seeing a yellow ray of light turning around the Northern Dipper, the principal symbol of God in Chinese theology. Twenty-four months later he was born, and he was associated with yellow, the colour of the earth in which he had incarnated.

The Yellow Dragon appeared to Fuxi without horns, emerging from the River Luo, and taught him the elements of writing. According to the legends, it also filled a hole in the sky that the monster Gonggong had made. At the end of his life, the Yellow Emperor was said to have taken the form of this dragon in order to ascend to Heaven.

Legacy

The Chinese consider the Yellow Emperor their ancestor, and so they sometimes call themselves children of the dragon. The legend helped establish the Chinese dragon as a symbol of imperial power. The Chinese national flag used from 1889 to 1912 is known as the Yellow Dragon Flag.

Trivia

Yellow Dragon Flag. The Chinese national flag used between 1889 and 1912 is also called the Yellow Dragon Flag, in reference to this figure.
A Japanese counterpart. The Yellow Dragon does not appear in native Japanese mythology, but some compare it to the Ōryū, which shares some similarities with it.
