Mesopotamian god of the underworld

Birtum

Birtum is a Mesopotamian god associated with the underworld and the husband of the goddess Nungal, mistress of prisons. His name means «fetter» or «shackle» in Akkadian, so he was probably a deification of such objects, although the word is grammatically feminine and the deity was regarded as male.

The myth

In a late version of the Anzû myth, Enlil asks his assistant Nuska to summon Birtum. The fragment explaining why Enlil needs him is missing, but in the following section Birtum congratulates Ninurta on Enlil's behalf and urges him to return the Tablet of Destinies after defeating Anzû. Ninurta rebukes him and says he will keep it for himself.

His sukkal, or attendant deity, was Eturammi, whose name means «do not slacken». Some traditions make him a twin of Šarrabu, although the latter is sometimes paired with Šâbu instead.

Worship and religion

Wilfred G. Lambert proposed that Birtum was originally worshipped alongside Nungal in a city now unknown, which declined in the third millennium BCE, and whose tutelary deities were later moved to Nippur. A similar transfer is documented for Nisaba, whose cult moved from Eresh to Nippur.

Ancient sources: He appears in the god list An = Anum and in a late version of the Anzû myth.

Trivia

A name of chains. His name means «fetter» in Akkadian, and he probably began as a deification of a restraining object.
An attendant who never slackens. His attendant Eturammi has a name meaning «do not slacken», like other attendants who personify their masters' commands.
