king who protected Zoroaster, from the Avestan tradition

Vishtaspa

Vishtaspa is a king in Zoroastrian tradition, the patron and protector of the prophet Zoroaster and one of the first to spread his message. He belongs to the ancient Iranian world, and his importance lies in the tradition that he was the first ruler to embrace the new faith.

The myth

According to the Avestan texts, Vishtaspa ruled from Bactria. The Gathas describe him as an ally of Zoroaster, a follower of the path of Vohu Manah and committed to spreading the prophet's message. He is called a kavi, a term for a seer or poet-priest, and his family was thought to have passed on the gift of prophecy.

Later books from the ninth to the eleventh centuries expand his story. They tell that when Zoroaster reached his court, the prophet debated with rival priests for three days and triumphed. His enemies then slandered him to Vishtaspa, who imprisoned him, but from prison Zoroaster healed one of the king's favourite horses, which earned him the monarch's support. Battles against King Arejataspa also appear, in which Vishtaspa wins with the help of his brother Zairivairi. In the Sistan cycle, by contrast, he is a cruel ruler who sends his eldest son to certain death.

Ancient sources: The Gathas and the Yashts of the Avesta, and later Pahlavi texts such as the Denkard and the Ayadgar i Zareran.
