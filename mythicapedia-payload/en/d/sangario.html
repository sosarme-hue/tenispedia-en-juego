Phrygian river-god of Greek mythology

Sangarius

Sangarius is the divine personification of the river that flowed through Phrygia, in northwestern Asia Minor. A son of Oceanus and Tethys, he is linked in myth to the royal house of Troy and to the story of Attis.

The myth

In Greek tradition, Sangarius was one of the many river-gods born to Oceanus and Tethys. He was married to Metope, a naiad, though some accounts name Evagora as his wife instead. From this union, according to some authors, came Hecuba, who became the second wife of King Priam of Troy.

Accounts of his offspring differ. Other sources say that Eunoe, his daughter, became the mother of Hecabe by King Dymas of Phrygia. Sangarius was also the father of Nana, who conceived Attis after picking up an almond tree that sprang from the severed genitals of Agdistis. With Cybele he fathered Nicaea, whom Dionysus violated. Ocyrrhoe, mother of the Trojan defender Hipomedon, and Sagaritis, who loved Attis, complete his children.

The tradition also names Alpheus, his son, who taught Athena to play the flute. When he insulted the goddess, Zeus killed him with a thunderbolt. According to myth, the river took its name from Sangas, a man who mocked Rhea and was turned by her into flowing water.

Ancient sources: Pausanias recounts the birth of Attis from Nana, the river's daughter; other ancient texts link Sangarius to Priam and Hecuba.
