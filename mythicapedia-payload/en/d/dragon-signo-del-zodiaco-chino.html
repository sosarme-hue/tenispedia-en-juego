Dragon (Chinese zodiac)

The Dragon is the fifth sign of the twelve-year Chinese zodiac cycle and the only mythical creature among its animals. Rooted in Chinese culture, it stands for strength, good fortune and harmony, and its image has spread worldwide through martial arts.

The myth

The Chinese zodiac assigns twelve animals to a cycle that repeats every twelve years, and the Dragon takes fifth place. It is linked to the Earthly Branch 辰 (chén), a symbol that some scholars have proposed was once associated with scorpions or with the star Antares.

According to tradition, people born in a Dragon year inherit its strength, health and harmony. Each Dragon year also carries one of five elements (wood, fire, earth, metal or water), creating five variants with distinct personality traits.

Worship and religion

In China, dragon figures were placed above doors and on rooftops to drive away demons and evil spirits. The Dragon year is the one with the most births in Chinese culture, since the sign is believed to bring good fortune. In countries that use the zodiac or have large Chinese communities, birth rates usually spike in these years, and the rush to plan births has strained hospitals; toward the end of these years, it has been linked to a slight rise in infant mortality.

Legacy

The dragon is one of the most widespread emblems of martial arts, especially in the West, where many teachers have adopted its symbolism for self-defence. Its place in other calendars varies: Buddhist calendars in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar and Sri Lanka replace it with the nāga, and the Gurung zodiac uses the eagle instead.

Trivia

The most wanted year. Births rise noticeably in Dragon years in countries with Chinese influence, because children born then are thought to have better fortune.
A different animal by calendar. In medieval Persian translations the dragon became a sea serpent, and today it is often read as a whale. In the Old Turkic calendar it was replaced by a fish or a crocodile.
A failed attempt at change. During China's Cultural Revolution, an attempt was made to replace the dragon with the giant panda, but the movement was short-lived.
