legendary bogatyr of Russian folklore

Bova Korolevich

Bova Korolevich is a bogatyr, or hero, of Russian folklore, also depicted in popular prints known as lubok. He most probably originated as a Russian retelling of a French romance about the exploits of Bevis of Hampton.

The myth

The tales present him as a hero of the Russian chivalric tradition, whose adventures reworked those of Bevis of Hampton. For some time it was believed that the story reached Russia through Italian sources, such as I Reali di Francia by Andrea da Barberino and the poem Buovo d'Antona, both based on French material.

The oldest known text is a translation into Old Ruthenian made in the 1540s in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, from a Serbo-Croatian version. From the second half of the sixteenth century, the story took shape as a romance in Russia.

Legacy

His popular prints, the lubki, spread him among the Russian public, and his figure became linked to the tradition of the bogatyrs.

Ancient sources: The earliest known written source is the Old Ruthenian translation of the 1540s; no older texts from the Russian tradition mention him.

Trivia

An Italian route. For some time it was thought that the story reached Russia through Italian sources, although the most likely origin is the French romance of Bevis of Hampton.
Arrival from the Balkans. The version translated into Old Ruthenian in the Grand Duchy of Lithuania was written in Serbo-Croatian, showing how the tale circulated in southeastern Europe.
