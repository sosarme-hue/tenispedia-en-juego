Zoroastrian divinity of immortality

Ameretat

Ameretat is the Zoroastrian divinity of immortality, one of the Amesha Spenta, the holy principles of Zoroaster's faith. She is a female figure linked to plant life and water.

The myth

According to the Bundahishn, when Angra Mainyu withered the primordial plant, Ameretat crushed it into pulp and mixed it with water. Tishtrya then spread that water over the world as rain, and many other plants grew.

In the Gathas she appears alongside Haurvatat, "Wholeness" and health. Both are tied to sustenance and, in the afterlife, to the reward of the righteous.

Worship and religion

Her name marks the seventh day of each month and the fifth month of the year, which fall under her protection. Out of respect for her and Haurvatat, meals were traditionally eaten in silence.

Legacy

Mordad, the fifth month of the Iranian civil calendar, keeps her name. In Islam, Harut and Marut have been linked to Haurvatat and Ameretat, an idea proposed by Paul Lagarde.

Ancient sources: Gathas, Younger Avesta, Bundahishn and Denkard.

Trivia

Milk from white cows. According to the Denkard, Zoroaster's material self passed to his mother when she drank the milk of six white cows that had eaten grass nourished by the rain.
