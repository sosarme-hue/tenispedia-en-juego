Etruscan goddess of the underworld

Persipnei

Persipnei is the queen of the underworld in Etruscan mythology, equivalent to the Greek goddess Persephone and the Roman Proserpina. Her name reached the Etruscans from Greece.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Persipnei, also called Phersipnai and later Ferspnai, ruled the realm of the dead alongside her husband Aita, the divine ruler of the underworld. Together they governed the Etruscan underworld, which was guarded by Mantus and Mania.

She is not depicted often, but she appears regularly beside Aita in tomb paintings. In a fresco in the Tomb of Orcus she is shown with snakes in her hair, a feature that recalls Medusa. The sources offer few details about her deeds, so her figure is known mainly through her role as consort and through these images.

Trivia

Borrowed name. The Etruscans took her name from the Greeks, reflecting the influence of Greek culture on the Etruscan world.
Snakes like Medusa. In a fresco in the Tomb of Orcus she appears with snakes in her hair, a detail that brings her close to the Gorgon Medusa.
