Libyan shepherd in Greek mythology

Cephalion

Cephalion is a Libyan shepherd from Greek mythology, also called Caphaurus, whose story revolves around a stolen flock and the revenge he took. He is a minor figure, known mainly for his link to the Argonauts.

The myth

Accounts disagree about his parentage. One makes him a grandson of Apollo and Acacallis; another makes him the son of Amphithemis and the nymph Tritonis, brother of Nasamon and thus a descendant of the sun god and of Libya.

Cephalion lived in Libya with his family and tended a flock. The Argonauts Canthus and Eurybates raided it, and he killed them in revenge. In another version, tribal chiefs later killed him, buried him and seized his animals. His story ends in a cycle of theft and reprisal. Few details survive, so his figure remains sketchy in the Greek tradition.

Trivia

A fragrant name. His alternative name, Caphaurus, is linked to the word for camphor, which probably hinted at a somewhat exotic African origin.
Two names, one figure. Cephalion and Caphaurus are two forms of the same name, and both appear in accounts of the Libyan shepherd.
