cosmic boar of Norse mythology

Sæhrímnir

Sæhrímnir is the cosmic boar of Norse mythology, killed and eaten every night by the Æsir and the einherjar. Its meat feeds the fallen warriors of Valhalla, and tradition presents it as the endless food supply of Odin's hall.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Sæhrímnir is killed and cooked each day to feed the Æsir and the einherjar, the warriors who have fallen in battle and dwell in Valhalla. The cook of the gods, Andhrímnir, handles the slaughter and prepares the animal in the cauldron Eldhrímnir. By evening the beast is whole again, so it can serve as food the next day. The meat never runs out, even as more warriors arrive.

The most cited passage appears in Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning, where High answers Gangleri, King Gylfi in disguise, that Valhalla has plenty of food. In this account Odin eats nothing: he gives his food to his wolves and drinks only wine. Snorri describes Sæhrímnir as a boar. The Poetic Edda poem Grímnismál, however, mentions it in the voice of Grímnir, another disguise of Odin, without making clear what kind of animal it is. Some scholars think the difference between the two sources may come from an addition by Snorri.

Worship and religion

There is no direct evidence of a cult devoted to this creature. Some scholars have suggested that its daily sacrifice may go back to ritual practices in Germanic paganism. In the nineteenth century Jacob Grimm linked it to hero worship: gods received only the entrails and fat of a sacrifice, while deified heroes were given the flesh itself, which the einherjar eat in Valhalla. Rudolf Simek compares it to Thor's goats, which are also renewed after being eaten, and points to shamanic rites.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda, in the poem Grímnismál, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning and in the list of hog names in the Skáldskaparmál.

Trivia

Three names. The name appears in variants such as Sahrimnir in Old English, Særimner in Danish and Norwegian, and Särimner in Swedish.
A name that clashes. Modern scholars often propose a meaning like sooty sea-beast, which sits oddly with its description as a boar.
Thor's relatives. Rudolf Simek compares its daily rebirth with Thor's goats, Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr.
