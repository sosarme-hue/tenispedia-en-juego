protégé of Freyja in Norse mythology

Óttar

Óttar, also called Óttar the Simple, is a protégé and human lover of the goddess Freyja in Norse mythology. His story is told in the poem Hyndluljóð, part of the Poetic Edda.

The myth

According to the poem, Óttar was deeply devoted to the goddesses. He raised a shrine of stones, a hörgr, and made many offerings to Freyja, who answered his prayers and set out on a journey to help him discover his lineage. The goddess disguised Óttar as her boar Hildisvini, the Battle-Swine, and brought him before Hyndla, a seeress. Freyja forced Hyndla to reveal his ancestors and to give him a memory potion so he would retain everything he heard.

Worship and religion

The tale portrays Óttar as a devotee who honoured Freyja with a stone shrine and offerings, though the sources say little more about any organised cult.

Ancient sources: Hyndluljóð, one of the poems of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A boar with a battle name. Freyja turns Óttar into her boar Hildisvini, a name often rendered as the Battle-Swine.
A seeress forced to speak. Hyndla is a seeress whom Freyja compels to recount Óttar's ancestry and to give him a memory potion.
