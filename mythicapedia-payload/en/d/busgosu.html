forest creature of Asturian mythology

Busgosu

The Busgosu, also called Musgosu, is a forest creature of Asturias, half man and half goat, recalling Pan or Faunus. Its figure may have Celtic roots, and its role changes by region: in the eastern Cantabrian mountains it helps shepherds, while in the west it is lord of the forest.

The myth

According to tradition, the Busgosu walks on two legs, with goat's legs, horns, a hairy torso and a human face. Other depictions bring it closer to a goblin, with pointed ears and a green suit and hat.

There are two accounts. In the eastern Cantabrian region it is a benefactor who guides lost shepherds and helps repair the huts of the mountain pastures. In western Asturias it is the lord of the forest and of all that lives in it, a fierce enemy of woodcutters and hunters; its kiss brings consumption and a slow death. Those who do no harm to the woods and get lost receive its help to find the way out.

Legacy

In the Ruta del Río Profundu, in Villaviciosa, striking images of this spirit can be seen in the middle of the forest. Depending on the area it is also known as Bulligoso, Peludu, Vellosu or Calabiernu.

Trivia

A protective she-bear. Gómez-Tabanera linked the figure to the ancient myth of the human bear, related to the legend of Doriga, in which a girl lost in the forest is cared for by a she-bear.
A French origin?. In 1922 Aurelio de Llano argued that the Busgosu was not native to Asturias, but a recent import from the Bassa-yaon of French Navarre.
