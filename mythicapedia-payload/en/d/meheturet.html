Egyptian celestial cow goddess, mother of the sun

Mehet-Weret

Mehet-Weret is an ancient Egyptian goddess of the sky, birth, and fertility, linked to the primordial waters. Her name means 'Great Flood', and tradition remembers her as the celestial mother who gives birth to the sun every day.

The myth

In Egyptian creation myths, Mehet-Weret gave birth to the sun at the beginning of time. She already appears in the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom, and the Book of the Dead credits her with bringing forth the sun god Ra each day. Every morning she raises him into the sky, and according to the tradition she also protects him and takes him into the underworld at night, so that he can be reborn the next day.

Her figure is also tied to water. As a celestial goddess, she is associated with the rains that feed the Nile and with the yearly flood that fertilizes the land. For this reason she is linked both with the earthly river and with a celestial Nile, which the Egyptians could see in the Milky Way, as well as with the river of the underworld along which the sun and the pharaoh sailed. In the Book of the Dead she helps the dead to be reborn, guiding them along that river.

Worship and religion

She had a cult centre at Talmis, in Upper Egypt. Her most common image is that of a cow with a sun disk between her horns, a symbol of nourishment and of the light that, together with water, made harvests possible. Sometimes she appears with a star-spangled belly or with ostrich feathers, and more rarely as a woman with a cow's head. She was identified with Neith, Isis and Hathor, especially the last, and like them she could be called the 'Eye of Ra'. Plutarch identified her with Isis in his work De Iside et Osiride.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts (Old Kingdom) and the Book of the Dead, where she appears in spell 17.
