Maya god of lightning and power

K'awiil

K'awiil is a Maya god linked to lightning, power and creation. He is recognised by his zoomorphic head and by the serpent that replaces one of his legs, and his image became tied to the sceptre of rulers.

The myth

In the Popol Vuh, three lightning deities associated with the «Heart of the Sky», among them Huraqan («One-Leg»), create the earth out of the primordial sea and populate it with animals. According to contemporary Maya myths, the rain gods, wielding lightning, once split open a sacred mountain to give humanity the maize seeds it held.

A Classic scene known only from ceramics shows an aged ancestor or deity emerging from the serpent foot of the lightning god, beside a young woman entwined by the snake. Its meaning is probably mythological, perhaps also ritual.

Worship and religion

His most prominent image is the K'awiil sceptre, held by Maya rulers during accession rites and in sacred kingship. In Palenque the infant form, Une'n K'awiil, was one of the city's three patron deities, and at Yaxchilán the sceptre was used on various occasions throughout the year.

Ancient sources: The Popol Vuh and the Book of Chilam Balam of Chumayel, along with the Dresden and Paris codices.

Trivia

A misread name. For a time Bolon Tz'akab was thought to be the god's name, but it is now known to mean «the many lords in the sequence», that is, a dynasty of rulers.
A dancing king. Yaxun B'alam IV is the only known ruler who danced with the K'awiil sceptre on the day of his accession to the throne.
