Hindu goddess, tantric form of Devi and Lakshmi

Kamalātmikā

Kamalātmikā, also called Kamalā, is a form of the goddess Devi in the Shakta tradition of Hinduism and the tenth and last of the ten Mahavidyas, the goddesses of great wisdom. She is regarded as the tantric depiction of Lakshmi, goddess of prosperity.

The myth

The sources say more about her iconography and role than about a detailed story. In Shaivism she is the consort of Shiva in the fullness of her graceful, kindly aspect. Her name is explained as 'the one beloved of lotus flowers' or 'the one who is a lotus', linked to kamala, meaning lotus.

The most common image shows her with four arms: two hold lotuses, while the other two make the abhaya mudra, a gesture of reassurance, and the vara mudra, a gesture of blessing. Her skin is golden, and she sits in padmasana atop a great lotus, a symbol of purity. Four great elephants bathe her, pouring amrita, the nectar of immortality, from jars. Elephants are linked to clouds and rain, and so to fertility, and also to royal authority.

Whereas Lakshmi is portrayed as a submissive wife to Vishnu, massaging his feet, Kamalātmikā appears more independent, openly carrying out her role as the bringer of bliss and prosperity. Vishnu remains her beloved, yet he is mostly absent from these accounts.

Legacy

Her name, Kamala, is also one of the names of Lakshmi, showing how closely the two figures are linked within the same tradition.

Trivia

The elephants. In Hinduism, elephants are associated with clouds and rain, linking them to fertility. Because of their strength, they also represent royal authority and divinity.
A shared name. The name Kamala is also used for the goddess Lakshmi, wife of Vishnu, which closely links the two figures.
