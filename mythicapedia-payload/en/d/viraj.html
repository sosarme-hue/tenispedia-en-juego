primordial cosmic principle in Hinduism

Viraj

Viraj is a Sanskrit word meaning sovereignty, excellence or splendour, and in Hindu thought it names the primordial being linked to creation, often personified as a secondary creator. Its figure links the Purusha with the formation of the world and of humankind.

The myth

According to some Hindu texts, Viraj is born from the Purusha, and the Purusha in turn is born from Viraj, in a circular relationship between the two. The Manu Smriti tells that Brahman divided his body into a male and a female half; from the female came Viraj, who produced Svayambhuva Manu, who created the ten Prajapatis. The Bhavishya Purana places the beginning of creation in the union of Manu and Shatarupa.

In the Atharvaveda he appears as a cow or joined with prana, the breath of life. In the Mahabharata he is identified with the primordial Purusha, associated with Vishnu and Shiva. In the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad Viraj is food and the eater of food, and in the Aitareya Upanishad he serves as intermediary between the Atman and the world.

Legacy

Viraj also names a metre in Sanskrit prosody. In the Bhagavad Gita he appears as the cosmic body containing all creation, which Arjuna beholds in its universal form.

Ancient sources: The Atharvaveda, the Manu Smriti, the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, the Aitareya Upanishad, the Yajur Veda and the Bhagavad Gita.
