legendary wild boar of Arthurian legend

Twrch Trwyth

Twrch Trwyth is a legendary wild boar from Welsh Arthurian legend, the central quarry of a huge hunt told in the prose romance Culhwch and Olwen. The chase is one of the longest episodes in that tale, sending King Arthur and his companions across several lands after the beast.

The myth

According to the Welsh tale, Twrch was a prince, son of Taredd, turned into a swine by God as punishment for his sins. His bristles dripped venom, and between his ears he carried a comb, a pair of scissors and a razor. The giant Ysbaddaden sets Culhwch the task of taking these objects, which are needed to groom the giant's hair, and Culhwch asks his cousin Arthur for help.

The hunt carries the party from Ireland to Wales and finally to Cornwall. Menw tries to snatch the objects from the boar and only takes one silver bristle. Later, in the Severn, Arthur's men recover the razor, the scissors and the comb, but the beast escapes and is driven into the sea off Cornwall. The tale does not say what happens to it afterwards, leaving open the possibility that it survived.

Legacy

The hunt already appears in the ninth-century Historia Brittonum and in the Latin Mirabilia, where Arthur chases the boar with his dog Cabal, whose pawprint is said to mark a rock in Wales. Some scholars think Chrétien de Troyes may have drawn on the boar when creating Tor, son of Ares, in Erec and Enide.

Ancient sources: Culhwch and Olwen (around 1100), Historia Brittonum (ninth century), the elegy Gwarchan Cynfelyn (seventh century, according to tradition) and the Mirabilia.

Trivia

A boar with objects on its head. Between the animal's ears were hidden a comb, a pair of scissors and a razor, and all of them had to be taken to complete the giant's task.
Another boar in the same story. To finish the giant Ysbaddaden's grooming, the white-tusked boar Ysgithyrwyn also had to be captured so that its tusk could be used.
