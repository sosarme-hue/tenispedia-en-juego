nymph of Greek mythology, lover of Helios

Leucothoe

Leucothoe is, in Greek tradition, a young woman of eastern origin, daughter of Orchamus, who was loved or raped by the sun god Helios. Her story, best known from Ovid, is one of the myths said to explain the origin of frankincense.

The myth

According to Ovid, Aphrodite punished the Sun for exposing her affair with Ares to Hephaestus by making him fall in love with Leucothoe. Helios, disguised as her mother Eurynome, entered her chamber, dismissed the servants and revealed himself. Leucothoe was overpowered without resisting.

Clytie, jealous and still in love with the Sun, told her father. Orchamus buried his daughter alive under a heap of sand. Helios tried to restore her warmth but could not save her, so he sprinkled her body with fragrant nectar and turned her into a frankincense shrub. Lactantius Placidus tells a similar story with small differences; in another version the informer is an unnamed sister and the father is called Orchomenus. Hyginus also names a Leucothoe as mother of the Argonaut Thersanon, though she may be a different woman.

Worship and religion

The story has been read as a myth explaining the use of frankincense in the worship of the sun god, and it has been compared with that of Daphne, turned into a laurel. Some scholars see in the burial a possible echo of ancient human sacrifice linked to tree cults.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Metamorphoses gives the fullest account; Lactantius Placidus and Hyginus also mention her.
