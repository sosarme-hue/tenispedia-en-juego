Taoist fertility goddess

Songzi Niangniang

Songzi Niangniang, "the Maiden Who Brings Children", is a Taoist fertility goddess venerated in China and, under the name Zhusheng Niangniang, also in Taiwan. She has been identified with many historical figures and is often shown as Guanyin herself or as one of her attendants.

The myth

According to tradition, this goddess grew out of three younger sisters of the god of wealth Zhao Gongming, named Zhao Yunxiao, Zhao Qiongxiao and Zhao Bixiao, who appear in the Ming dynasty novel Investiture of the Gods.

Over time the three were merged into a single figure, known as Zhusheng Niangniang, who presides over pregnancy, childbirth and the protection of mother and child.

Worship and religion

She is depicted as an empress and sometimes as Guanyin or her attendant. Many temples have a shrine to her in one of the side halls.

Legacy

Her figure remains present in Taoist temples in China and Taiwan, where she sometimes appears alongside other fertility goddesses.

Ancient sources: The Ming dynasty novel Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen yanyi), which records the three sisters from whom she derives.

Trivia

A book and a brush. In Taoist painting and sculpture she holds a book and a brush, reflecting the Chinese custom of recording newborns in family lineage records.
A goddess with many faces. She has been identified with several historical figures, and in some images she appears directly as Guanyin.
