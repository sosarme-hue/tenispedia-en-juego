Hermione

Hermione is, in Greek mythology, the daughter of Menelaus and Helen, whose story is woven into the cycle of the Trojan War. Her marriages, her quarrels and her fate after death make her one of the most debated female figures in the heroic tales.

The myth

According to the Catalogue of Women, Hermione was born in the palace just as the gods gathered to decide the fate of the Trojan War, which was already destined to happen. When Helen eloped with Paris, the girl was left behind, aged only nine according to Apollodorus. Before the war her grandfather Tyndareus had betrothed her to her cousin Orestes, son of Agamemnon, whom she loved. During the conflict, however, Menelaus promised her to Neoptolemus, son of Achilles, against the girl's wishes, as she despised him for his violent treatment.

Ancient poets disagreed over who was to blame for that promise. Euripides has Orestes complain that Menelaus broke his word, while Ovid claims the idea was Tyndareus's and that Menelaus did not even know of it. The Odyssey places the wedding ten years after the end of the war. Settled in Phthia, the couple faced conflict with Andromache, a captive whom Neoptolemus had received as spoils. Hermione, unable to bear children, accused her rival of bewitching her and asked Menelaus to kill Andromache and her son; Peleus prevented the tragedy.

Neoptolemus travelled to Delphi to settle matters and died there in a quarrel with Orestes, though accounts differ: some say a priest, the temple servants or Apollo himself killed him, while others say Orestes did. Orestes then took Hermione back to Mycenae, where he married her and they had a son, Tisamenus, although some authors give Erigone as the mother of that son. As for her end, some accounts place her in the Elysian Fields with her parents; according to Ibycus, she married Diomedes after his apotheosis and now lives as a goddess with the Dioscuri.

Legacy

Hermione's figure has inspired plays such as Jean Racine's tragedy Andromaque and Gioachino Rossini's opera Ermione. She also appears in Caroline B. Cooney's novel Goddess of Yesterday.

Ancient sources: The Odyssey, Euripides, Ovid, Apollodorus and the Catalogue of Women mention her, and Ibycus records her apotheosis.

Trivia

A girl left behind. According to Apollodorus, Hermione was nine years old when her mother Helen eloped with Paris, and she was left behind in Sparta.
