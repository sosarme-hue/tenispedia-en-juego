mythical place of the Turkic and Mongolic peoples

Ergenekon

Ergenekon is a mythical place in Turkic and Mongolic tradition: an inaccessible valley in the Altai Mountains where, according to the legend, the Turkic people survived a great catastrophe. It is a founding myth for both peoples and, centuries later, it shaped modern Turkish national identity.

The myth

According to tradition, after a military defeat the Turks took refuge in the Ergenekon valley, where they remained trapped for four centuries behind iron mountains. When their numbers had grown, a blacksmith gave a tremendous shout that melted the mountain and opened a passage.

The she-wolf Asena showed them the way out, and from there they founded the first Turkic khaganate, with the valley as its capital.

Worship and religion

A New Year's ceremony still commemorates the ancestors' legendary escape from Ergenekon.

Legacy

Late Ottoman Turkish nationalists embraced the epic, and Yakup Kadri Karaosmanoğlu linked it to the founding of the Turkish Republic in 1923. The wolf became a lasting symbol of Turkish nationalism.

Ancient sources: Rashid-al-Din's Jāmiʿ al-tawārīkh (14th century) records the Mongolic version, and Abulghazi Bahadur's Shajara-i Turk (17th century) records the Turkic version.

Trivia

An annual festival. The story remains present in a New Year's ceremony that recalls the escape from the valley.
The she-wolf and Sirius. According to Ergün Candan, the she-wolf Asena may be linked to Sirius, the so-called 'dog star'.
