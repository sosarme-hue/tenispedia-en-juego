son of Autonous in Greek mythology

Acanthus

Acanthus is a young man from Greek mythology, son of Autonous and Hippodamia, who was turned into a bird along with his whole family. His name, meaning 'thorn' in Greek, reflects the barren lands of his father.

The myth

In the account preserved by Antoninus Liberalis in his Metamorphoses, Autonous, son of Melaneus, owned many herds of horses but neglected farming, so his fields produced only rushes and thistles. He named his children after such plants: Acanthus, Schoeneus and Acanthis, while the eldest, Erodius, took his name from the eroded land.

Erodius cared fondly for the horses in the meadows. One day, his brother Anthus drove the mares away from the pastures, and the enraged animals attacked and devoured him while he cried out to the gods. Autonous and his wife tried to defend him, but fear and weakness kept them from stopping the mares. Zeus and Apollo, moved with pity, turned the whole family into birds: Autonous into a quail, Hippodamia into a crested lark, and the children into birds bearing their former names, Acanthus among them.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis, in his Metamorphoses, is the main ancient source recounting his story.

Trivia

Possible identification. The bird Acanthus became has been identified as the siskin, the linnet or the blue tit, though with little certainty.
A family named after plants. All of Autonous' children received names linked to plants or to soil erosion, according to Antoninus Liberalis.
