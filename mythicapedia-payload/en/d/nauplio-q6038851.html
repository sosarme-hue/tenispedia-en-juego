hero of Greek mythology, Argonaut

Nauplius

Nauplius is a hero of Greek mythology, king of Nauplia or of Euboea depending on the sources, and one of the Argonauts. He matters mainly for the revenge he launched against the Greeks after the Trojan War.

The myth

According to tradition, Nauplius was the son of Clytoneus and a great-grandson of an earlier Nauplius, although some authors identified the two as one person. When the Cretan king Catreus suspected his daughter Clymene, he handed her over to be sold; Nauplius instead married her, and they had three sons: Palamedes, Oax and Nausimedon.

After Palamedes died, betrayed by his fellow Greeks during the Trojan War, Nauplius swore vengeance on Agamemnon and the other leaders. As the Greek fleet sailed home from Troy, a great storm sank part of it and drove the rest onto the coast of Euboea. Nauplius lit fires on the Cape Caphereus to lure the ships onto the rocks, and he killed the survivors who reached the shore. Earlier, he had persuaded several wives of the Greek commanders, including Clytemnestra and Meda, to betray their husbands.

Trivia

An Argonaut. Nauplius also appears among the heroes who sailed with Jason on the Argo in search of the Golden Fleece.
Deceptive fires. The lights he lit on Cape Caphereus, also called Xylophagus, made the sailors believe they were approaching a safe harbour.
