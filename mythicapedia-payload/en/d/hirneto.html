daughter of Temenus and heroine of Greek mythology

Hyrnetho

Hyrnetho is a heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of the Argive king Temenus and wife of Deiphontes. Her death at the hands of her own brothers gave rise to a sacred place in Epidaurus that bears her name.

The myth

According to tradition, Temenus, one of the Heraclidae who claimed the Peloponnese, favoured his daughter Hyrnetho and her husband Deiphontes over his sons. The jealous brothers conspired and killed their father. The army, however, backed the couple, though another version holds that the kingdom passed to Ceisus, the eldest brother.

Cerynes and Phalces wanted to separate Hyrnetho from her husband. They went to Epidaurus, where she lived, and tried to persuade her with accusations against Deiphontes and promises of a better marriage. Hyrnetho defended her husband and reproached them for being murderers of their father. They then carried her off in their chariot.

Deiphontes came to rescue her. He shot Cerynes with an arrow, but did not dare to shoot Phalces for fear of hitting his wife. Phalces, struggling, dragged her with such violence that he killed her, while she was pregnant. Her body was buried in the place later called Hyrnethium, where a heroon was established.

Worship and religion

At Epidaurus a heroon dedicated to Hyrnetho was maintained, and the site was honoured with special rites. The wild olive trees growing there were sacred: no one could take home their broken branches, which had to be left there, consecrated to the heroine.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes her in his Description of Greece, in his account of Epidaurus and Argos.
