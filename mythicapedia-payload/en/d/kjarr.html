legendary king of Germanic and Norse tradition

Kjárr

Kjárr (also Kíarr) is a king of Germanic heroic legend, linked to the Valir, the southern people of Valland. Many scholars think his name comes from Caesar, which makes him a reflection of the Roman emperors in Norse literature.

The myth

In Old Norse sources, Kjárr rules the Valir, the southern people of Valland. Snorri Sturluson makes him a descendant of Auði, founder of the Ödling dynasty, while the genealogy in Hversu Noregr byggðist calls Auði his grandfather.

His daughter Ölrún appears in the Völundarkviða as a Valkyrie who marries Egil, brother of the smith Wayland. Two other texts place Kjárr far back in the Age of Migrations, as a contemporary of the Huns: the Atlakviða in the Poetic Edda and a fornyrðislag poem in the Hervarar saga listing the great kings of old.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál, Hversu Noregr byggðist, the Völundarkviða, the Atlakviða in the Poetic Edda, and the Hervarar saga.

Trivia

Possible origin in Caesar. Many scholars propose that the name derives from Caesar, though the exact route to Scandinavia is unclear. A path through Gothic and, ultimately, Greek kaisar has been suggested.
A Valkyrie daughter. Ölrún, Kjárr's daughter, is a Valkyrie in the Völundarkviða who marries Egil, brother of the smith Wayland.
