Mesopotamian goddess of the air, wife of Enlil

Ninlil

Ninlil is the Mesopotamian goddess of the air, wife of Enlil and a senior deity of the Sumerian and Akkadian pantheon. Her worship centred on Nippur, and over time she absorbed the goddess Sud of Shuruppak.

The myth

According to the myth, Ninlil was born as Sud, and tradition presents her as the daughter of Nisaba and Haya, although other accounts make her the daughter of An and Nammu. She lived in Dilmun, where, in the tale of "Enlil and Ninlil", Enlil seduced her while she bathed naked in the river.

In "The Marriage of Sud and Enlil", the union takes place on a flowery bed, and Enlil then decrees that she will receive a new name: from that moment she will be Ninlil, his queen. With Enlil she bore children such as the underworld god Nergal, the moon god Nanna and the warrior god Ninurta.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped chiefly in Nippur and nearby Tummal, alongside Enlil, where temples and shrines were dedicated to her. In the first millennium BCE she was also honoured at Ḫursaĝkalamma near Kish, alongside the goddess Bizilla.

Legacy

Her figure spread into Syrian and Hurrian religion, where she was linked with Shalash, and in Assyria she became Mullissu, spouse of the god Ashur. Some have connected her to the origin of Lilith.

Ancient sources: Sumerian and Akkadian hymns and texts, such as "Enlil and Ninlil", "The Marriage of Sud and Enlil" and the god list An = Anum, mention her.

Trivia

A name that changes. According to the myth, she received the name Ninlil after marrying Enlil; before that she was called Sud.
Constellations. In Mesopotamian astronomy she was associated with the Wagon, corresponding to Ursa Major, and the Goat, corresponding to Lyra.
