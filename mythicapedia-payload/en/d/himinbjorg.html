heavenly home of Heimdallr in Norse mythology

Himinbjörg

Himinbjörg, meaning "heaven's castle" or "heaven mountain", is the home of the god Heimdallr in Norse mythology. It appears in the Poetic Edda, the Prose Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla.

The myth

In the Poetic Edda, in the poem Grímnismál, Odin, disguised as Grímnir and tortured, names Himinbjörg among several mythical places, where Heimdallr drinks fine mead.

The Prose Edda places it where the burning rainbow bridge Bifröst meets the heavens. There Heimdallr keeps watch over the mountain jotnar trying to cross, seated at the edge of the sky.

In Heimskringla, Snorri presents a euhemerized account in which Heimdallr dwelt at Himinbjörg after Odin settled at Sigtúnir.

Legacy

Scholars have linked the name to place names across Germanic Europe, such as Himmelsberg in Sweden and Himelberc in Liechtenstein. Jacob Grimm translated it as "heavenly hills".

Ancient sources: Grímnismál in the Poetic Edda, the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) and Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla.

Trivia

A name with clues. Himinbjörg translates as "heaven's castle" or "heaven mountain", and scholars debate which meaning is the original.
Different locations. Grímnismál presents it as a place where Heimdallr drinks mead, while the Prose Edda places it at the end of the Bifröst bridge.
