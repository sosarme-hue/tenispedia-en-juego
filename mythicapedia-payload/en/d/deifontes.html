Greek hero, king of Argos

Deiphontes

Deiphontes was a king of Argos and one of the Heracleidae, a descendant of Heracles through Ctesippus. His story, tied to the succession of Temenus's descendants, shows the family tensions that marked the end of the Argive monarchy.

The myth

According to tradition, Deiphontes was the son of Antimachus and husband of Hyrnetho, daughter of Temenus. Temenus, who had received Argos as his share when the Peloponnese was divided, showered his affection on his daughter and her husband rather than on his own sons, and the sons grew resentful. They hired killers to murder their father, but once the crime was done, the army declared Deiphontes and Hyrnetho his rightful successors.

The sources do not agree on what followed. The Bibliotheca gives the throne to the couple, while Pausanias claims that Ceisus reigned instead. Deiphontes, in some versions, settled at Epidaurus and drove out its king, Pityreus, though another account says Pityreus gave up the land without a fight and left for Athens with his people. There, Temenus's sons tried to pull Hyrnetho away from her husband.

The ending is tragic. When Cerynes and Phalces carried Hyrnetho off by force, Deiphontes killed Cerynes and wrestled with Phalces, who held his sister. In the struggle, Phalces unintentionally killed her, and then fled at reckless speed in his chariot. Deiphontes brought her body back to Epidaurus and built a sanctuary there, in a place that later came to be called Hyrnethion.

Ancient sources: The Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus and Pausanias's Description of Greece.

Trivia

A shrine for Hyrnetho. Deiphontes built a sanctuary to his wife at Epidaurus, and the place came to be known as Hyrnethion.
Two versions of the throne. The Bibliotheca and Pausanias disagree: one hands the kingdom to the couple, the other to Ceisus.
