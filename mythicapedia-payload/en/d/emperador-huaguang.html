Chinese deity of fire and patron of Cantonese opera

Emperor Huaguang

Huaguang is a deity of Chinese folk religion associated with fire and protection. He is venerated in southern China, Hong Kong and Cantonese communities, where he is the patron of Cantonese opera performers.

The myth

According to tradition, Huaguang, also called Marshal Ma or Wu Xian, was born with a third eye, which gave rise to the popular saying "Marshal Ma has three eyes". In popular images he wears armour and carries magical weapons, such as a golden brick.

The seventeenth-century Ming novel Journey to the South tells his story at length. Originally a fire spirit, he falls into conflict with Tathāgata after a fight at a Buddhist assembly. He is reborn several times, rebels in the heavenly court by setting fire to the Southern Heavenly Gate, and is finally forgiven by the Jade Emperor, who restores his post. In another legend, the Jade Emperor orders him to destroy the theatres, but he protects the performers instead.

Worship and religion

His worship began in Jiangxi during the Song dynasty and spread to Jiangnan, Fujian and Guangdong in the Ming period. His birthday is observed on the 28th day of the ninth lunar month in some Hakka and southern Chinese communities, and in Yongding some merchants honour him as a deity of wealth.

Legacy

He is the patron of Cantonese opera, and performers call him "Master Huaguang". He appears in the novel Journey to the South and is mentioned in Journey to the West, where he is named a disciple of a Buddha.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Ming novel Journey to the South (17th century) and in chapter 96 of Journey to the West, as well as in the Song text Xianchuang Kuoyi Zhi.

Trivia

Three eyes. Because of his divine origin he is born with a third eye, and he is therefore also known as Sanyan Lingguan, the Three-Eyed Official.
Protector of theatres. According to a Cantonese legend, the Jade Emperor ordered him to destroy the theatres, but he chose to protect the performers instead.
