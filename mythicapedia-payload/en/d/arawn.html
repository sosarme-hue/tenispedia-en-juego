king of the Otherworld in Welsh mythology

Arawn

Arawn is the king of Annwn, the Otherworld of Welsh mythology, and a central figure in the First Branch of the Mabinogi. Linked to hunting and white hounds, he matters as one of the oldest rulers of the underworld in Welsh folklore.

The myth

In the First Branch, the young Pwyll, lord of Dyfed, unknowingly chases off white hounds with red ears that were feeding on a stag; they belonged to Arawn. As compensation, Arawn proposes they swap places for a year and a day, and that Pwyll defeat Hafgan, his rival, something Arawn had failed to do. Pwyll agrees and, wearing Arawn's shape, refrains from sleeping with Arawn's wife, which forges a friendship between the two realms.

This alliance outlasts Pwyll. In the Fourth Branch, his son Pryderi owns otherworldly pigs given to him by Arawn, which end up in the hands of the magician Gwydion fab Don. After a war, Gwydion kills Pryderi in single combat. Beyond the First Branch, Arawn barely appears.

Legacy

A saying from an old Cardigan folktale preserves his name: “Long is the day and long is the night, and long is the waiting of Arawn.” In Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, the host Bertilak shares parallels with Arawn, and some scholars link him to the Gaulish god Cernunnos through his ties to the hunt and horns. Lloyd Alexander makes him an evil Lord of Death in The Chronicles of Prydain, while the Welsh Brut y Brenhinedd presents him as a king of Scotland allied with Arthur.

Ancient sources: The medieval Mabinogi, preserved in the Llyfr Gwyn of Rhydderch, especially the First and Fourth Branches, and the Welsh Triads.

Trivia

The Hounds of Annwn. The Cŵn Annwn, or Hounds of Annwn, ride the skies in autumn and winter. Their baying was linked to the calls of wild geese on migration.
An Old Name. A cleric named Araun witnessed two charters of 860 preserved in the Book of Llandaf, showing the name was in use in Wales long before the legend was written down.
