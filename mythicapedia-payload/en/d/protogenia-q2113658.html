daughter of Calydon and mother of Oxylus

Protogeneia

Protogeneia is a figure of Greek myth, a princess daughter of the eponymous king Calydon and of Aeolia, and sister of Epicaste. Her importance lies mainly in her offspring, since she was the mother of Oxylus of Aetolia.

The myth

According to the tradition, Protogeneia bore Oxylus of Aetolia to the god Ares, linking her name to the genealogy of the Aetolian line. Little is known about her, and the surviving information comes from fragmentary texts.

In the Catalogue of Women she appears in a heavily damaged passage about the descendants of Agenor. There it is suggested that Protogeneia might be the same person as Deidamia, who is also said to have had a union with Ares and to have descended from Agenor, either as his daughter or granddaughter. The text is so damaged that this identification can neither be confirmed nor ruled out.

Ancient sources: The Catalogue of Women, where she appears in a very fragmentary passage alongside Deidamia.
