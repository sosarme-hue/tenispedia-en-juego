young man who loved Narcissus in Greek mythology

Ameinias

Ameinias is a young man from Greek mythology who fell in love with the hunter Narcissus, who rejected him. His story is part of the Narcissus myth and, according to tradition, explains the origin of the flower whose name is linked to his in the ancient Greek language.

The myth

According to Conon's version, Narcissus had already spurned all his male suitors. When he also rejected Ameinias and gave him a sword, Ameinias took his own life at Narcissus's doorstep, after praying to Nemesis to teach Narcissus a lesson for the pain he had caused.

Narcissus, drinking from a pool, saw his reflection and became entranced. Unable to reach the image, he wasted away from hunger and thirst, and the nymphs turned him into a narcissus flower. Other accounts say that, filled with remorse, he killed himself beside the water, and the flower grew from his blood.

Worship and religion

Tradition says the Thespians came to honor Eros in a special way after this tragedy.

Legacy

The BBC series Telling Tales retells the myth in a modern setting, with Ameinias as Narcissus's friend.

Ancient sources: Conon, in his Narrations (24), records the version of the Narcissus myth that includes Ameinias.

Trivia

A fragrant plant. According to the lexicographer Hesychius of Alexandria, ameinasis was another name for a sweet-smelling herb called duosmon, which could be dill, anise or cumin.
A cult of love. Tradition links Ameinias's tragedy with the special honor the Thespians paid to Eros among the gods.
