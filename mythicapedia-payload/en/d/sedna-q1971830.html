Inuit goddess of the sea and sea animals

Sedna

Sedna is a goddess of the sea and marine animals in Inuit religion, also known as the Mother of the Sea. Her story is a creation myth explaining how she came to rule Adlivun, the Inuit equivalent of the underworld.

The myth

According to the tradition, a young woman refuses her human suitors and ends up married to a dog or a bird, depending on the version. When her father throws her overboard to appease a storm, she clings to the side of the boat, but he cuts off her fingers and she sinks. Transformed into mistress of the deep, her severed fingers become seals, walruses and whales.

Worship and religion

Angakkuit (shamans) appease her when hunters break taboos: they travel to her undersea house, comb her hair and braid it. Inuit women traditionally tattooed their fingers as a reference to her story.

Legacy

Her image appears on a Greenlandic commemorative stamp from 1957, on the Mother of the Sea statue in Nuuk, and on a Canadian two-dollar coin issued in 2024. Pitseolak Niviaqsi and other Inuit artists have carved and printed her image many times.

Trivia

A Canadian coin. In 2024 Canada issued a commemorative two-dollar coin showing Nuliajuk surrounded by a walrus, two beluga whales, a seal and an Arctic char.
A statue in Nuuk. On the shore of Nuuk, the capital of Greenland, stands a statue of the Mother of the Sea in which an angakkoq combs her hair, surrounded by sea animals.
