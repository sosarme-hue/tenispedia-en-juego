Sumerian tutelary god of Ĝirsu

Ninĝirsu

Ninĝirsu is the tutelary god of the Sumerian city of Ĝirsu and the chief deity of the pantheon of Lagash. A warlike god also linked to agricultural fertility, he was later identified with Ninurta.

The myth

According to tradition, he was the son of Enlil and Ninhursag, although some scholars think that in an older tradition he was a son of Enki. His sister was Nanshe and his wife was Bau.

As a warrior in Enlil's service, he was described as a colossus with the wings of the Anzû bird, and his main weapon was the mace Sharur. The rulers of Lagash called on him in their long border war with Umma. Over time Ninurta absorbed his role: in the Standard Babylonian versions of Lugale and the Anzû epic, Ninurta is the protagonist.

Worship and religion

Ninĝirsu was worshipped as lord of Ĝirsu, and his rulers saw themselves as administrators of his lands. His emblem, the Anzû bird over two lions, appeared in cultic contexts at Lagash, and he received offerings in the cult of the dead. His veneration declined with the region and does not survive today.

Ancient sources: The Gudea Cylinders, the Old Babylonian versions of the myths Lugale and Anzû, the Lagash King List and the Hymn to Gula.

Trivia

A distinctive emblem. The Anzû bird over two lions was the emblem associated with this god at Lagash, and it could sometimes represent him in cultic settings.
Two names, one god?. Assyriologists debate whether Ninĝirsu and Ninurta were originally separate deities or two aspects of the same god.
