torch-bearers of Mithras in Roman Mithraism

Cautes and Cautopates

Cautes and Cautopates are the two torch-bearers who attend Mithras in the tauroctony, the central scene of the Roman cult of Mithraism. The direction of their torches sets them apart and has inspired many interpretations.

The myth

Few written narratives of Mithraism survive, so almost everything we know about these two figures comes from the images. They are shown smaller than Mithras to emphasise his importance, and they wear Persian-style clothing and a Phrygian cap, which point to the cult's supposed eastern origins. Cautes holds his torch raised up, while Cautopates holds his pointed down.

They have been read as sunrise and sunset, or as the spring and autumn equinoxes. David Ulansey proposed that they represent the equinoxes and the constellations of the celestial equator during the Age of Taurus. Cautopates may symbolise death and Cautes new life.

Worship and religion

They appear in the tauroctony, the central scene of Mithraic sanctuaries, beside Mithras.

Legacy

The scholar M. J. Vermaseren described a relief at Dieburg, Germany, where Mithras and his two torch-bearers are equal in size, and suggested that they form a Mithraic 'Trinity'.
