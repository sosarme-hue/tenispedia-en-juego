Hilda

Hilda, in Old Norse Hildr, is one of the valkyries of Norse mythology. Her story is tied to the legend of Hedin and Högni, and her name is also the Old Norse word for "battle".

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Hilda is the daughter of Högni and the wife of Hedin. Tradition places her in the legend of Hjaðningavíg, the endless battle between Hedin and Högni.

Hilda had the power to revive the dead lying on the battlefield. Thanks to this power, the fighting between her husband and her father could begin again and again, with neither side ever winning.

Her name also appears alongside other valkyries in the Völuspá and the Darraðarljóð, though the texts do not always make clear whether the poets had her in mind as a personification of battle.

Ancient sources: Attested in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and mentioned with other valkyries in the Völuspá and the Darraðarljóð.
