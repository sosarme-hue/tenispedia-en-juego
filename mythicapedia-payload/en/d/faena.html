one of the Graces in Greek mythology

Phaenna

Phaenna is one of the Charites, the Greek goddesses of grace and charm. A minor figure in the pantheon, she is known mainly through local traditions of Laconia, the region where Sparta stood, and her name means "bright".

The myth

Ancient stories about Phaenna are scarce. Most of what survives comes from Pausanias, who in the second century CE described the traditions of Laconia. According to him, the Lacedaemonians claimed that the Charites were only two, named Cleta and Phaenna, which departs from the more common idea of three.

According to a local legend, it was the mythical king Lacedaemon who founded their sanctuary and gave them their names.

Worship and religion

Their sanctuary stood near the river Tiasa in Laconia and was the centre of local worship. The sources do not describe specific rites or festivals, and there is no evidence that the cult survived into later periods.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records the local Laconian traditions about these goddesses.

Trivia

Two instead of three. Pausanias reports that the Lacedaemonians claimed the Charites were two, not three. This local version shows that their number could vary by region.
A river in the story. Her sanctuary stood beside the river Tiasa, a geographical detail that Pausanias records when describing worship in Laconia.
