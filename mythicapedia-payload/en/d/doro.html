eponymous ancestor of the Dorians, son of Hellen

Dorus

Dorus is, in Greek mythology, the eponymous ancestor of the Dorians, one of the great Hellenic peoples. Son of Hellen, his figure explains the origin of this people's name and their migrations across Greece.

The myth

According to tradition, Dorus was the youngest son of Hellen, ancestor of the Hellenes, and of the nymph Orseis, although some writers name Phthia as his mother. That made him a grandson of Deucalion. Other versions make him a son of Zeus and Protogenia, or even of Poseidon, a rare account that names no wife.

Tradition places him in Phthia, in Thessaly, from where he migrated to the region of Olympus and Ossa, then to Pindus, Mount Oeta and finally the Peloponnese. Herodotus says he ruled the Hellenes while they lived in Histiaeotis, from where the Cadmeans drove them out. Apollodorus credits him with founding small Dorian cities such as Erineon, Boion, Kytinion and Pindos. He married a daughter of Phoroneus, and had several daughters; one of them, Iphthime, became the mother of satyrs by Hermes. According to Diodorus Siculus, his son Tectamus led the Dorians who reached Crete, and Aegimius was also regarded as a Dorian king.

Legacy

His name lives on in that of the Dorian people. Modern scholars regard Dorus as a figure formed from the name of the Dorians, not the other way round. A man called Dōrieus also appears on the Linear B tablets from Pylos, a region later subjected by the Dorians.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, the Bibliotheke of Apollodorus, the Catalogue of Women, the play Ion and Diodorus Siculus preserve different versions of his genealogy and migrations.

Trivia

An unexpected father. In one rare account, Poseidon, not Hellen, was said to be Dorus's father.
Nymphs, satyrs and curetes. According to Apollodorus, five Dorian maidens became the collective mothers of the oread nymphs, the satyrs and the curetes.
