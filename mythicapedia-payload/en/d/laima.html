Baltic goddess of fate and good fortune

Laima

Laima is a goddess of fate and good fortune in Latvian and Lithuanian mythology. She was associated with birth, marriage and death, and she was the patron of pregnant women.

The myth

In Latvian tradition, Laima forms a trinity with her sisters Dēkla and Kārta, similar to the Norse Norns or the Greek Moirai. Dēkla looks after birth and children, Kārta after adult life, and Laima, the most popular of the three, after death, though she is mostly linked with mothers. In the modern Dievturi movement, the three are considered one deity in three aspects.

In Lithuanian tradition, Laima sometimes appears as a single figure and sometimes as three, and her main duty is to prophesy the destiny of a newborn. Her predictions were irrevocable, and not even she could change them. Algirdas Julien Greimas argued that Laima did not determine fate but only knew it, against older readings that saw fatalism in her. Laima also takes part in a Lithuanian version of the Great Flood, in the birth of humankind. She is linked to the cuckoo, Gegutė, who governed time and the succession of the seasons, and whose calls were believed to foretell how much life a person had left.

Worship and religion

Birth rituals at the end of the nineteenth century included offerings of hens, sheep and woven cloth to Laima. Only women could take part in the ceremony, held in a sauna known as the pirtis. The linden was her sacred tree. Today the Dievturi movement venerates her as part of its reconstructed faith.

Legacy

Laima is still used as a personal name in Lithuania, Latvia and the Scandinavian countries. In 2014 the Lithuanian folk music group Kūlgrinda released an album titled Laimos Giesmės, meaning "The Hymns of Laima".

Ancient sources: There are no ancient texts as such; the first written mentions date from the seventeenth century, such as Wilhelm Martini in 1666, followed by Matthäus Prätorius, Jacob Brodowski and Philipp Ruhig.

Trivia

An easily confused name. In Lithuanian mythology Laima is often confused with Laimė, good fortune, and with Laumė, a fairy.
A destiny that could not be changed. According to Lithuanian belief, Laima's prophecy was final, and not even the goddess herself could alter it.
