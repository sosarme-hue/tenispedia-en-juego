sister of Odysseus in Greek mythology

Ctimene

Ctimene is a figure of Greek mythology best known as the younger sister of Odysseus, daughter of King Laertes and Anticlea. Her brief role in the Odyssey helps flesh out the domestic life of the hero's family in Ithaca.

The myth

In the Odyssey, Ctimene was raised in the palace on Ithaca by her parents, alongside the swineherd Eumaeus, who was treated almost as her equal. It is Eumaeus himself who recalls, in Homer's telling, that Anticlea cared for the two children with the same affection.

When she came of marriageable age, Ctimene was sent to the island of Same to wed Eurylochus, one of Odysseus's companions. Some accounts describe a considerable bride price. Eurylochus sailed with Odysseus on the journey home from Troy but, like all of the crew, died before reaching Ithaca, leaving Ctimene a widow with no further role in the myth.

Homer gives her the epithet 'of the long robe' or 'of the flowing peplos', the same one he applies to Lampetia, daughter of Helios. The location of Same, as Strabo already noted, remains uncertain.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey is the main ancient source that mentions her, in the passage where Eumaeus recalls being raised alongside her.
