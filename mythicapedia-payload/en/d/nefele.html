cloud goddess of Greek mythology

Nephele

Nephele is a figure of Greek mythology made of cloud, linked to two stories: that of Ixion, in which she appears as a fake Hera, and that of Athamas's first wife, mother of Phrixus and Helle. Her stories show how the tradition connected the house of Aeolus, the Argonaut cycle and the origin of the centaurs.

The myth

According to the tradition, Zeus shaped Nephele in Hera's likeness to deceive Ixion, who had tried to force himself on the goddess and so broke the sacred rules of hospitality. Zeus placed the cloud beside Ixion; their union produced Centaurus, father of the centaur race. Because Ixion boasted of having enjoyed Hera, he was bound to a wheel carried by the winds.

In another version, Nephele is the first wife of Athamas, son of Aeolus. With him she had the twins Phrixus and Helle. Athamas left her to marry Ino, daughter of Cadmus, who tried to destroy the children by roasting the seed grain. Ino bribed the envoys sent to the oracle to claim that the god demanded Phrixus's sacrifice. Nephele rescued them by sending a golden, flying, speaking ram that carried them towards Colchis. During the voyage Helle fell into the sea and drowned; the strait where it happened was named the Hellespont after her. Phrixus reached Colchis, where King Aeetes welcomed him and gave him his daughter Chalciope in marriage; in gratitude he gave the king the Golden Fleece, which was hung in a tree.

Legacy

The name Nephele, from Greek nephos ('cloud'), and its Latin form Nebula, remain associated with the idea of the cloud in language and science.

Ancient sources: Ovid names her as one of the sixty Oceanid nymphs who attended Artemis.

Trivia

A name for a strait. The Hellespont was named after Helle, Phrixus's sister, who fell into the sea during the flight on the golden ram.
A fleece sought later. The Golden Fleece that Phrixus gave King Aeetes was, according to tradition, later the goal of Jason and the Argonauts.
