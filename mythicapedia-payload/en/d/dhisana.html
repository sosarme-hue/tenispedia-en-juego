Vedic goddess of abundance and guardian of the sacred fire

Dhisana

Dhisana is a goddess of Hinduism associated with abundance and prosperity, and regarded as the guardian of the sacred fire. She appears in the Rig Veda, the oldest of the Vedas, and her name also refers to things such as the soma vessel, knowledge, intelligence and speech.

The myth

In the Rig Veda, Dhisana appears intermittently in several mandalas and in hymns dedicated to other deities, so her figure emerges more from allusions than from complete stories. The texts link her to the sacrificial fire, wealth and abundance, and at times she is mentioned alongside fire, the sun, the moon and the stars.

Later Hindu texts use her name for the dual planks on which the soma was prepared, showing how the term moved from naming a goddess to designating ritual objects and functions. Scholars disagree on her meaning: Alfred Hillebrandt linked her mainly to the earth, sometimes within the triad of earth, atmosphere and heaven, while Richard Pischel saw her as a goddess of wealth, similar to Aditi and the earth.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda, in several of its mandalas and hymns.

Trivia

A name with several meanings. Besides a goddess, the term Dhisana came to name knowledge, intelligence and speech, as well as a ritual vessel.
Conflicting readings. The Indologists Hillebrandt and Pischel reached different interpretations, one linking her to the earth and the other to wealth.
