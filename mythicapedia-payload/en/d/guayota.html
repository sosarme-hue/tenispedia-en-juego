Guanche god of evil

Guayota

Guayota was, according to Guanche tradition on Tenerife, the main malignant force in their mythology, linked to fire and the eruptions of Teide. His figure shows how this people understood the volcanic phenomena of the island.

The myth

According to the early chroniclers, Guayota dwelt inside the earth, above all in the Teide volcano, which the Guanches called Echeyde and identified with hell. He was the evil principle of their beliefs, somewhat comparable to the Christian Devil, and was assisted by lesser beings such as Guañajé, Canajá and Jucancha, protectors of goats, sheep and dogs respectively, as well as personifications of the wind and earthquakes.

The best-known story, a modern literary creation with no firm basis in ancient sources, tells how Guayota kidnapped the sun god Magec and imprisoned him in Teide, plunging the world into darkness. The Guanches asked Achamán for help, and after a fierce struggle he defeated Guayota, freed the sun and sealed the crater with the monster inside. In this version, the stopper is the whitish cone known as Pan de Azúcar.

Worship and religion

During Teide's eruptions, the Guanches lit bonfires to frighten Guayota away or, in another version, so that if he escaped he would think he was still in hell and pass by. Remains of vessels with food have been found in volcanic tubes of Teide, which researchers interpret as offerings to calm the eruptions. The cult disappeared after the conquest, though his figure survives in Canarian folklore.

Legacy

Guayota remains present in Canarian popular culture: a pub in Puerto de la Cruz bears his name, as do a firefighting association in Granadilla de Abona and a folk group, and a heavy metal band has adopted him. He is the main antagonist of Patricia Briggs's novel Night Broken and the final boss of the indie video game Gaurodan.

Ancient sources: Fray Alonso de Espinosa, Antonio Sedeño and Tomás Arias Marín de Cubas, Canarian chroniclers who mention him after the conquest; Juan Bethencourt Alfonso collected oral traditions about him.

Trivia

A pub with his name. In Puerto de la Cruz there is a pub named after Guayota, showing that his figure still lives on in Canarian social life.
A novel villain. Author Patricia Briggs made him the main antagonist of Night Broken, the eighth novel in her Mercy Thompson series.
