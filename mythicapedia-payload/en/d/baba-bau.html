Mesopotamian goddess of healing and protector of Lagash

Bau

Bau, also spelled Baba, is a Sumerian and Akkadian goddess of life, healing and childbirth, and tutelary deity of Lagash. Over time she was associated with the healing goddesses Ninisina and Gula, and in some cases partly merged with them.

The myth

According to tradition, Bau was the daughter of the god An, and her mother was the goddess Gatumdug, although other sources give that role to Abau. She was the wife of Ningirsu, the god of war at Girsu, and they had several children, among them Ḫegir. From the Old Babylonian period onwards she is also named as the wife of Zababa, the tutelary god of Kish.

Some texts present her as the mother of mankind, a tradition preserved in a hymn of Ishme-Dagan. Over time she took on the role of a divine physician and midwife, and was called the "merciful mother". She was shown holding a vase with streams of water, a sign of abundance, while other images include snakes, scorpions or waterfowl, whose meaning is debated. She could intercede with angry gods on behalf of those who asked her for help.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Girsu, but she was soon worshipped in Lagash too, where she had the temple E-bau, and also in Ur and Irikug. In Lagash an autumn festival lasting four days drew pilgrims bringing offerings. At Girsu the New Year festival lasted seven days and included a sacred marriage rite in which the king, acting for Ningirsu, took the chief priestess of Bau as his partner.

Legacy

Urbaba, a king of Lagash, took his name in her honour, and Gudea called himself the "son of Baba". Her name also appears in the theophoric names of many ordinary people.

Ancient sources: The Hymn of Ishme-Dagan, the Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur, and the god list An = Anum, which mentions her alongside her family.

Trivia

A disputed name. Scholars do not agree on how her name was read. Bau is the conventional form, but Baba, Bawu and Babu have also been proposed.
A rejected bark. The Assyriologist Thorkild Jacobsen proposed that her name imitated a dog's bark. This is now considered mistaken, since unlike Gula she was not clearly linked to dogs.
