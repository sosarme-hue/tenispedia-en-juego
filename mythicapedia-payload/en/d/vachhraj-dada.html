Hindu deity of Gujarat, warrior hero protector of cattle

Vachhraj Dada

Vachhraj Dada is a Hindu deity from Gujarat, in western India, revered as a warrior hero who died defending cattle. His worship is especially strong among communities such as the Charans, Ahirs and Karadiya Rajputs.

The myth

According to tradition, Vachhraj Dada was a Solanki Karadiya Rajput. The legend says he died protecting the cows of the Charans, a community of herders who traditionally served as priests, when dacoits attempted to raid them.

He is depicted on a stone slab, seated on a horse. He is credited with healing power: devotees who make vows in his name believe they can cure snakebites.

Worship and religion

The site where he became a martyr also bears his name and is today a pilgrimage centre at the edge of the Little Rann of Kutch. It houses his main temple and samadhi, and other temples exist in many towns across Gujarat. Historically, Charans performed priestly duties at his shrine, and his worship remains alive among devotees.
