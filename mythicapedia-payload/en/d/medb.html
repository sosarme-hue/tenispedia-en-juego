queen of Connacht in Irish mythology

Medb

Medb, also spelled Meadhbh, is the queen of Connacht in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. She is the archetype of the warrior queen, some consider her a manifestation of a sovereignty goddess, and she is best remembered for starting the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

The myth

According to the tradition, Medb rose to power in Connacht through a series of marriages. Her father, Eochaid Feidlech, married her to Conchobar mac Nessa, king of Ulster, but the marriage failed and she ended up murdering her sister Eithne, also called Clothru. She then married other kings of Connacht and finally Ailill mac Máta, with whom she had a daughter, Findabair, and seven sons, all named Maine after a druid's prophecy.

Her most famous episode is the Cattle Raid of Cooley. Annoyed that Ailill's bull outshone hers, Medb tried to seize Donn Cúailnge, the bull owned by Dáire, a vassal of Conchobar. She gathered an army that included Ulster exiles, among them Fergus mac Róich, once her lover. Only the young Cúchulainn opposed the invasion, holding the army back at the fords. Medb got the bull, but she had to retreat, and the animal died after fighting Ailill's bull.

Her end was violent too. Ailill had Fergus killed and later Conall Cernach killed Ailill. Medb fell to her son Furbaide, who struck her on the head with a piece of cheese flung from his sling while she bathed in a lake. According to legend, she is buried standing up in the cairn of Knocknarea, facing Ulster.

Legacy

Her name appears in several Irish place names, such as Knockmaa, meaning Medb's hill, in County Galway, or Rath Meave at Tara. In English she is known as Maeve, a form that has spread as a personal name.

Ancient sources: The early poem Conailla Medb míchuru by Luccreth moccu Chiara (c. 600), and the tales of the Táin Bó Cúailnge and Cath Bóinde.

Trivia

A dangerous beauty. According to the tales, her form was so beautiful that it robbed men of two-thirds of their valour when they saw her.
A death by cheese. Furbaide practiced with his sling until he could hit an apple at the distance of the pool, then threw a piece of cheese that struck her head.
