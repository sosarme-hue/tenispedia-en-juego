22nd tirthankara of Jainism

Neminatha

Neminatha is the 22nd tirthankara of Jainism, a teacher who, according to that religion, attained liberation and showed the way out of the cycle of rebirth. He is revered as a cousin of Krishna and as the model of the vow of nonviolence.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, Neminatha was born in Shauripura, in the Saurashtra region, the youngest son of King Samudravijaya and Queen Shivadevi, of the Yadu line. He was a cousin of Krishna. As a boy, the stories say, he effortlessly lifted Krishna's great conch and defeated his cousin in a friendly test of strength.

When he was about to marry Rajimati, daughter of Ugrasena, he heard the cries of animals destined for the wedding feast. Moved with compassion, he called off the marriage, distributed his wealth, shaved his head and retreated to Mount Girnar. Rajimati followed him and became a nun. After meditating for 55 days he attained omniscience and liberation, and tradition says he lived a thousand years and founded a large order of monks and nuns.

Worship and religion

Mount Girnar, in Gujarat, is today an important Jain pilgrimage site, with several temples. In iconography he is recognised by his dark-blue complexion and the conch shell, and he is often shown with his guardian deities Gomedha and Ambika.

Legacy

His separation from Rajimati is a recurring theme in Jain poetry and medieval Gujarati literature, where worldly love is contrasted with spiritual sacrifice. In the 13th century the Kannada poet Janna wrote about his life in an unfinished epic.

Ancient sources: Jinasena's Harivamsa from the 9th century recounts his story, and Jain tradition holds that his name appears in the Rigveda and Yajurveda, though scholars debate this.

Trivia

Pilgrimage to Girnar. Mount Girnar, where he retreated, remains one of the main Jain pilgrimage destinations in India.
A name with a wheel. According to Jain texts, his name alludes to the rim of a wheel, a symbol of dharma, and his mother dreamed of a wheel of jewels before his birth.
