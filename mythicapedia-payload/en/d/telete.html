daughter of Dionysus, personification of Bacchic initiation

Telete

Telete is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of Dionysus and Nicaea, who personifies initiation, celebration and the mysteries of the Bacchic cult.

The myth

According to the story, Nicaea was ashamed of having become pregnant by Dionysus and even tried to hang herself. In due time a daughter was born, and the Horae were said to have served as her midwives. Some versions make Nicaea a naiad, daughter of the river-god Sangarius and the mother goddess Cybele.

Dionysus destined Telete to become a follower of himself and of her half-brother Iacchus, his son. She is imagined dancing through the night after Dionysus, delighting in tambourines and the double drum made of ox hide.

Worship and religion

Pausanias mentions a statue of Telete in the sanctuary of the Heliconian Muses in Boeotia, placed beside one of Orpheus. Her name is linked to the verb meaning to be initiated, and she was associated with nighttime festivities and ritual dances in honour of Dionysus.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions her when describing a statue of her in the sanctuary of the Heliconian Muses in Boeotia.

Trivia

Divine midwives. The Horae, goddesses of the seasons, were said to have helped at her birth.
A statue beside Orpheus. Pausanias places her image next to that of Orpheus in the sanctuary of the Heliconian Muses.
