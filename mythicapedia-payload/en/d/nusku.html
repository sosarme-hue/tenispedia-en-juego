Mesopotamian god of light and fire

Nusku

Nusku is the Mesopotamian god of light and fire, best known as the divine vizier of Enlil. He guarded the night against demons and nightmares, and his emblem was most often the lamp.

The myth

In the tradition, Nusku served Enlil as doorkeeper and adviser, oversaw his court and acted as go-between for the god and human petitioners. Accounts of his parentage differ: some texts make him a son of Enlil, a hymn names Enul and Ninul as his parents, and in Harran he came to be regarded as a son of the moon god Sin and Ningal.

In the Akkadian poem Atrahasis he counsels Enlil when the lesser gods rebel and refuse to keep working. In the myth of Enlil and Ninlil, he carries Enlil across in a boat so that the god can reach the young Ninlil.

Worship and religion

His main cult center was Nippur, where he is attested already in the Early Dynastic period, and he was worshipped in temples of his own as well as in the Ekur complex. Later he was introduced into the pantheons of Babylon, Ur, Uruk, Assur and Harran, the last perhaps his chief sanctuary in the first millennium BCE. He was invoked as a protector against demons such as Lamashtu.

Ancient sources: Akkadian texts such as the poem Atrahasis, the myth of Enlil and Ninlil, the incantation collection Maqlû and several hymns dedicated to him.

Trivia

A name with several readings. His name could be written with a logogram that was also read as Enšadu, usually translated as the good-hearted lord. It is unclear whether this was simply another name for him or a separate deity.
A rooster among his symbols. Besides the lamp and the staff, the rooster appears as one of his symbols, linked to providing light at night.
