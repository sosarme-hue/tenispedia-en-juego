king of Munster in Irish mythology

Cú Roí

Cú Roí is a king of Munster in the Ulster Cycle, a warrior of superhuman abilities, master of disguise and mortal enemy of Cú Chulainn. He links several tales of medieval Irish literature.

The myth

According to medieval tales, Cú Roí is the son of Dáire mac Dedad and belongs to the Clanna Dedad, although T. F. O'Rahilly believed this descent was artificial and that Cú Roí and Dáire were in fact one and the same. He often appears as an intervener or arbitrator. In the Fled Bricrenn he is one of the judges who choose Cú Chulainn as champion, and later he appears disguised as a churl to propose the beheading game.

In the Táin bó Cúailnge he faces Munremar in a stone-throwing contest and later the giant poet Amairgin, though in both cases they withdraw out of honour. His feud with Cú Chulainn ends in the Aided Con Roí: in one version the hero kills him after learning that his soul hides in an apple inside a salmon, while Bláthnat, who had betrayed him, dies with Ferchertne, his poet.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Fled Bricrenn, the Táin bó Cúailnge, the Forfess Fer Fálgae, the Aided Con Roí and the Book of Leinster.
