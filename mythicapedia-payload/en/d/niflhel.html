place of the dead in Norse mythology

Niflhel

Niflhel ("Misty Hel") is a place in Norse mythology associated with the dead, mentioned in the Poetic Edda and in Snorri Sturluson's work. Its exact relationship to Hel and to Niflheim varies between sources, which makes it a hard place to pin down.

The myth

In the poem Vafþrúðnismál, Odin wagers his head in a contest of wits with the giant Vafþrúðnir, who claims to have travelled through all nine worlds, Niflhel included. In Snorri's Gylfaginning, Odin, disguised as three men, tells King Gylfi that the spirits of the just will live forever in Gimlé, while those of evil people will dwell in Niflhel.

In Baldrs draumar, a poem not included in the Codex Regius, Odin travels down to Niflhel to ask about the troubling dreams of his son Baldr. Snorri suggests Niflhel could be read as the lowest level of Hel, though the name is often blended with Niflheim, a term that seems to originate with him.

Ancient sources: Vafþrúðnismál and Baldrs draumar from the Poetic Edda, and Gylfaginning by Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda.
