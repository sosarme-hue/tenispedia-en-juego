primordial goddess of Mesopotamian mythology

Lahamu

Lahamu is a minor goddess of Mesopotamian cosmology, considered the feminine counterpart of Lahmu. She matters because she is one of the ancestors of the first gods in the Babylonian creation story, and some god lists also count her among the ancestors of Anu. Although she is a secondary figure, she helps us understand how ancient Mesopotamians imagined the origin of the universe.

The myth

According to the Enuma Elish, the Babylonian creation poem, Lahamu is the firstborn daughter of Tiamat and Abzu, the primordial waters from which the cosmos emerges. Together with her brother Lahmu, she gives birth to Anshar and Kishar, a pair who in turn become the parents of the first gods. They therefore form the link between the primordial couple and the later generation of gods.

She appears in few stories and without much narrative development, so her role is more genealogical than heroic. The texts always place her at the beginning, as a bridge between primeval chaos and the order of the gods.

Legacy

In the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, some researchers wrongly interpreted Lahmu and Lahamu as representations of the zodiac, parent stars or constellations. That astronomical reading, now rejected, shows how scholars of the time looked for celestial correspondences in the figures of Mesopotamian cosmology. Her study remains useful for reconstructing local variants of the creation story.

Ancient sources: The Babylonian Enuma Elish names her as the firstborn daughter of Tiamat and Abzu, and some god lists include her among the ancestors of Anu.
