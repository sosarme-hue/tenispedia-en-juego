heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of Deucalion

Pandora

Pandora is a heroine of Greek mythology, the daughter of Deucalion and Pyrrha and, according to tradition, the mother of Graecus by Zeus. She should not be confused with the Pandora of the jar, who shares her name.

The myth

According to the Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, Pandora was living in the palace of Deucalion when, joined in love with Zeus, she bore Graecus, a warrior of great courage. Other traditions present her as one of the three daughters of Deucalion and Pyrrha, alongside Protogeneia and Thyia, and as Protogeneia's sister.

These accounts do not agree on every detail. One source says that children born to the two daughters of Deucalion by Zeus included four sons, among them Melaena and Pandorus, without specifying which of the two women was the mother of each. Modern scholars have debated whether Pandora was Deucalion's daughter, his wife, or neither, since the ancient texts do not fully settle the question.

Ancient sources: The Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, names her as the mother of Graecus by Zeus.

Trivia

Same name as her grandmother. According to some sources, she was named after her maternal grandmother, also called Pandora, a figure better known for the myth of the jar or box.
A disputed origin. It is unclear whether she was Deucalion's daughter or his wife, and the ancient texts do not resolve the question with certainty.
