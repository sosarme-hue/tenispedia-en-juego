Etruscan goddess of hunting and animals

Artume

Artume is an Etruscan goddess of hunting and animals, later identified with the Greek Artemis. She matters because she shows how Etruscan religion absorbed and reshaped Greek divinities.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Artume was a daughter of Tinia and Letun and a sister of Fufluns and Apulu, though accounts of her parentage vary. She is shown as mistress of animals and credited with founding Aritie, today the Italian town of Arezzo.

On one mirror she and her twin Aplu slay the Python of Delphi together, whereas in Greek myth Apollo does it alone.

Worship and religion

No temples or shrines dedicated specifically to her are known, and she is almost absent from the Liver of Piacenza, suggesting she was less prominent than Artemis was for the Greeks. No living cult is recorded.

Legacy

The name Artemisia also belongs to a vampire in Italian folklore, though it is unclear whether the two are connected. A Marvel Comics character called Artume, a daughter of Hippolyta, shares the name but is not the goddess.

Trivia

A body carried to Dionysus. On two Etruscan mirrors a woman carries a body labeled Esia before Minerva and Fufluns. It may recall a Greek myth in which Artemis brings Ariadne to Dionysus to be revived.
A namesake vampire. Artemisia is also the name of a vampire in Italian folklore, though it is unknown whether it has any link to the Etruscan goddess.
