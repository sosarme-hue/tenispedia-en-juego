one of the Pleiades in Greek mythology

Sterope

Sterope is one of the Pleiades, the seven daughters of the Titan Atlas and the Oceanid Pleione in Greek mythology. She matters mainly for the story of King Oenomaus of Pisa and for the ancient debate over who was really her husband and who her son.

The myth

According to tradition, Sterope was born on Mount Cyllene in Arcadia and was later placed among the stars with her sisters. Her name takes several forms: Sterope, from the Greek steropē, meaning lightning; Asterope, meaning starred face; or even Asteria, meaning starry one.

The oldest version, which Latin authors cite most often, makes her the mother of Oenomaus through her love affair with Ares. Yet Hippodamia, Oenomaus's daughter, is never named as Sterope's child. A strange version says that Ares fathered Evenus rather than Oenomaus, and that Evenus, united with Alcippe, a daughter of Oenomaus who has a different genealogy in this account, fathered Marpessa.

Later a second version emerged in which Sterope is Oenomaus's wife rather than his mother. Pausanias records this tradition. He says Oenomaus was the son of Ares or of Alxion, and on the pediment of the temple of Zeus at Olympia he is shown beside Zeus, wearing a helmet, with his wife Sterope, one of the daughters of Atlas, at his side.

Legacy

Asterope is the name shared by two stars in the open cluster of the Pleiades.

Ancient sources: Pausanias names her as Oenomaus's wife in the pediment sculptures of the temple of Zeus at Olympia; the version making her Oenomaus's mother is the oldest and is cited by Latin authors.

Trivia

One name, several meanings. Her Greek name, steropē, is linked to lightning, while Asterope means starred face, which fits her transformation into a star.
Present at Olympia. Pausanias describes her figure on the pediment of the temple of Zeus at Olympia, beside Zeus and Oenomaus, placing her in religious art as well as in the stories.
