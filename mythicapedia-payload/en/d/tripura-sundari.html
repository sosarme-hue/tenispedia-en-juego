supreme goddess of Hinduism, lady of Sri Vidya

Tripura Sundari

Tripura Sundari, also called Lalita or Shodashi, is the foremost of the Mahavidyas and the supreme goddess of the Srikula tradition within Hindu Shaktism. She is regarded as the highest form of the Devi, the great goddess, and as the Shakti who rules above Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva.

The myth

Her name is usually explained as "the most beautiful woman in the three worlds" or "she who is beautiful to the destroyer of the Three Cities". The Lalitopakhyana of the Brahmanda Purana and the Lalita Sahasranama tell of her cosmic battle against the demon Bhandasura, which symbolises the triumph of good over evil. According to the story, she emerged from the fire to defeat him, and the demon had been created from the ashes of Kamadeva.

According to some texts, Krishna is the male form of Lalita, and from him she created the five forms known as gopala.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped in temples across India, such as those in Tripura, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Jharkhand, and her main sacred symbol is the Sri Yantra. Her festivals, Lalita Jayanti on Magha Purnima and Lalita Panchami on the fifth day of Sharad Navaratri, are marked with offerings and fasting. Her worship remains alive today among Shakta devotees.

Legacy

Her image and the Sri Yantra remain present in devotional and religious art. The Lalita Sahasranama preserves her thousand names as a hymn of praise.

Ancient sources: The Lalitopakhyana of the Brahmanda Purana, the Lalita Sahasranama, the Tripura Upanishad and the Saundarya Lahari mention her.

Trivia

Sri Yantra. A mystic diagram of nine interlocking triangles representing the union of divine masculine and feminine. It is central to worship in the Shri Vidya school.
Lalita Sahasranama. It is the only sahasranama that never repeats a name, and its thousand names avoid the auxiliary particles other hymns use to fit the metre.
