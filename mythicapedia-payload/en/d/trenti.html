mischievous forest goblin of Cantabrian folklore

Trenti

The trentis are wild goblins from Cantabrian mythology in northern Spain. Playful and elusive, they tease young women but also quietly help shepherds and elderly people.

The myth

According to Cantabrian tradition, trentis live in the thickest parts of the forest and blend into the vegetation, dressed in leaves, moss and roots. Their favourite game is to hide among the brambles, tug at women's skirts or pinch their calves, and then dart away before anyone can react.

They are not only pranksters. Trentis often help people without being noticed, and they show a special fondness for children. They guide shepherds to their scattered flocks after a storm or after the damage caused by the Ojáncanu, and they assist older women who can no longer look after themselves.

In winter they shelter in the torcas, the deep sinkholes of the limestone landscape, and in summer they rest in the shade of the trees. They feed on panojas and endrinas, sloe-like berries, but never drink water, which is poison to them.
