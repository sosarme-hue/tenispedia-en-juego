Roman goddess of infancy

Cuba

Cuba is a minor goddess of Roman mythology who watched over children from the cradle until they grew out of it and slept in a bed. Her name is thought to derive from the Latin cubare, "to lie down", a reference to that resting stage in the cradle.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Cuba looked after children through early infancy, from the time they lay in the cradle until they were no longer small. She was the sister of Cunina, also a protector of children, and of Rumina, a goddess who watched over nursing mothers and, probably, the nursing infants themselves.

She was also linked to a small group of deities concerned with infant nourishment. She was associated with Edulia, who blessed children's food, and with Potina, who blessed their drink, so that her care extended to sleep, food and drink alike.

Worship and religion

Roman mothers invoked her so that their young children would fall asleep and pass a good night. Her role reflects how much Roman families valued sleep and nourishment in the first years of life.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Her name is linked to the Latin verb cubare, "to lie down", which refers to the child resting in the cradle.
A group of protectors. She appears alongside Edulia and Potina, deities who blessed children's food and drink.
