forest guardian spirit of Tupi-Guarani folklore

Curupira

The Curupira is a forest guardian spirit in the myths of the Tupi-Guarani peoples of Brazil, Paraguay and the Guianas. It punishes those who overcut or overhunt, and it is recognised above all by its backward-facing feet.

The myth

According to tradition, the Curupira protects the forest from humanity's destructive habits. It tolerates those who hunt to eat, but it punishes hunters who kill for pleasure, undoing their traps and leading them astray in the woods. Its reversed feet confuse anyone trying to follow its tracks.

Descriptions vary by region. Some present it as a short man with blazing red hair; others as a bald, hairy being with blue or green teeth. It is said to have a family living in the hollows of dead trees, and it can imitate human voices to lure rubber tappers and hunters.

Early Jesuits in São Paulo described it as a feared being that left terrible scars and had to be appeased with offerings. Under European influence it became a more mischievous trickster, often outwitted by humans, though it could still bring misfortune.

Worship and religion

Hunters offer it tobacco and cachaça to win its favour. According to the lore, it rewards those who give it tobacco, though the gift must be kept secret from their wives. To protect themselves, travellers may make a cross or a wheel of vine and let the spirit occupy itself trying to unravel it.

Legacy

Its image with red hair has become fixed in popular imagination, perhaps through confusion with the caipora, with which it is sometimes identified. Its name varies by region: Caipora in central Brazil, Maty-taperé in the north and Saci Pererê in the south.

Ancient sources: The earliest known testimony is a report by the Jesuit José de Anchieta, dated 30 May 1560 in São Vicente, in what is now the state of São Paulo.

Trivia

A hunting companion. In the folklore of Pernambuco he is described riding a deer, accompanied by a dog named Papa-mel.
The monkey's heart. In a story recorded by Hartt, a hunter tricks the Curupira by handing over a monkey's heart, and the spirit ends up cutting out its own.
