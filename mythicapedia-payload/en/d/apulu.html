Etruscan god of light, healing and divination

Apulu

Apulu is the Etruscan god of the sun and light, healing, divination and plague, whom the Etruscans identified with Apollo. His story shows how Etruscan religion absorbed and reshaped Greek and Latin influences.

The myth

According to tradition, Apulu was the son of Tinia and Semla, brother of Fufluns and twin of Artume. Sources describe him as lord of the sun and light, of thunder and lightning, of healing and plague, and as a protector of divination. He was also linked with volcanic and infernal traits, which places him among gods of ambivalent powers.

His name appears in various forms, such as Aplu, and he was also known as Rath, Usil or Vetis. It is thought that the name reached Etruria through a Latin centre, probably Praeneste, rather than directly from Greece. Apulu works as an epithet of the fire god Śuri, and the two names appear together in the Pyrgi inscriptions.

Worship and religion

The sources say little about his worship. His name is linked to the Pyrgi inscriptions, and in art his image is associated with Apollo, but no specific shrines or rites appear in the material consulted.

Legacy

His most famous depiction is the Apollo of Veii, attributed to the sculptor Vulca, in which he wears a crown and laurel branches. Despite his importance, he does not appear on the Liver of Piacenza.
