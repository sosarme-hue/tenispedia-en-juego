Hindu goddess of bees, form of Mahadevi

Bhramari

Bhramari is, in Hindu tradition, the goddess of bees, regarded as an incarnation of Mahadevi within Shaktism. Her best-known story, told in the Devi Bhagavata Purana, explains why she is shown surrounded by insects and why she matters in the worship of the Goddess.

The myth

According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the asura Aruna, an enemy of the devas, obtained a boon from Brahma: he could not die in war, by any weapon, at the hands of a man or a woman, or by any creature with two or four legs. Confident in this protection, he gathered the daityas, invaded Devaloka and seized the moon, the sun, Yama, Agni and the other elemental deities.

The gods turned to Shiva, who also failed to defeat him at the foot of Mount Kailasha. Then Adi Shakti appeared behind Shiva, grew to an enormous size and, closing her eyes in concentration, summoned bees, hornets, wasps, flies, termites, mosquitoes and spiders from the skies. The insects clung to her body and merged with her, forming the divine figure of Bhramari.

In the battle, her great size blocked the daityas' swords while her arms struck the army. When Aruna was the last one left, she sent the insects against him, and they tore his body apart. After his fall the insects returned to her, the gods praised her, and they went back to their celestial abodes.

Worship and religion

Bhramari is worshipped alongside Shiva under the name Bhramaramba at several temples in India, including the Mallikarjuna Temple at Srisailam, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, and the temple at Kateel in Karnataka. At Banbhori in Haryana, fairs are held twice a year and an eternal flame is said to have burned at the temple for at least 400 years. Her worship remains active at these shrines.

Legacy

In yoga, the pranayama known as bhramari is a nasal breath that produces a smooth humming sound, like a buzzing bee. The Lakshmi Tantra identifies Lakshmi with Bhramari, and the Devi Bhagavata Purana gives her traits associated with the goddess of prosperity.

Ancient sources: The Devi Bhagavata Purana narrates her story in detail and links her to Lakshmi; the Markandeya Purana and the Devi Mahatmya mention her briefly, and the Lakshmi Tantra identifies her with the goddess.

Trivia

The humming breath. In pranayama, the bhramari breath imitates the buzzing sound of a bee.
A centuries-old flame. At the temple of Banbhori in Haryana, an eternal flame is reported to have burned for at least four centuries.
