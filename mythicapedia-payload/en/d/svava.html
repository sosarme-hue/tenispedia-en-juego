Norse valkyrie

Sváfa

Sváfa, also written Sváva, is a valkyrie of Norse mythology, daughter of King Eylimi and probably the maternal aunt of Sigurd. Her story is told in the poem Helgakviða Hjörvarðssonar in the Poetic Edda, and she is best known for her bond with the hero Helgi.

The myth

According to the poem, the son of King Hjörvarðr and Sigrlinn of Sváfaland was a silent man who could never be given a name. One day, sitting on a hill, he saw nine valkyries riding by, Sváfa the most beautiful among them. She gave him his name, Helgi, and offered him a gift; he wanted nothing but her, so she told him where to find a great sword engraved with serpents and magic runes. From then on, she protected him in battle.

After winning fame, Helgi asked King Eylimi for his daughter's hand, and they exchanged vows. She nonetheless stayed with her father while he fought. Álfr, son of King Hróðmar, challenged Helgi to a holmgang at Sigarsvoll to avenge his father. Helgi received a mortal wound from a troll woman's curse and died. Before dying, he asked Sváfa to marry his brother Heðinn. According to the tradition, Helgi and Sváfa would be reborn as Helgi Hundingsbane and Sigrún, and their adventures continued.

Legacy

Her name has been linked to the Suebi and to the kingdom of Sváfaland, though no direct connection is established. Some etymologies read it as 'sleep-maker'.

Ancient sources: She appears in the poem Helgakviða Hjörvarðssonar of the Poetic Edda and is also mentioned in the Þiðrekssaga, which names the kingdom of Sváfaland.
