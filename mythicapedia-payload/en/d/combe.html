Greek nymph or maiden, mother of the Curetes

Combe

Combe is a figure of Greek mythology, a maiden or nymph according to the sources, linked to the river god Asopus and to the island of Euboea. She is often identified with Chalcis, and is best known as the mother of the Curetes or Corybantes.

The myth

Ancient authors do not agree on her story. Stephanus says the city of Chalcis was once called Combe, and identifies her with Chalcis, one of Asopus' daughters, who was credited with inventing bronze weapons. Her sons, the Curetes, danced clashing spears against shields.

Zenobius presents her as the first woman to live with a man, and mother of a hundred children. Nonnus gives his own version: united with the Euboean Socus, she bore seven sons, the Corybantes, who led the Euboean troops of Dionysus' retinue. Expelled from the island, she fled with them to Crete, Phrygia and Attica, where King Cecrops took them in. After Socus died, she returned to Euboea.

Ovid also mentions a Combe of Pleuron, daughter or descendant of Ophius, who escaped her sons' wounds by fluttering her wings, perhaps turned into a bird. It is unclear whether she is the same figure.

Ancient sources: Stephanus, Zenobius, Hesychius, Nonnus of Panopolis and Ovid mention her, with differing versions.

Trivia

A metal name. The name Chalcis was linked to the Greek word for bronze, χαλκόν. That is why the nymph was said to have invented bronze weapons.
The first cohabitation. According to Zenobius, Combe was the first woman to live with a man, and had a hundred children.
