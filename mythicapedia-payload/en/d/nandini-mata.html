Hindu goddess, another name of Durga

Nandini Mata

Nandini Mata is a Hindu goddess, mainly venerated in Rajasthan and Gujarat. Her name means 'daughter' in Sanskrit and is also one of the names of Durga.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, she was the daughter of Yashoda and Nanda Maharaja. Shortly after her birth, Vasudeva swapped her for Krishna and took her to the prison where he and Devaki were held. King Kamsa tried to kill her, but she slipped from his hands, took the eight-armed, terrifying form of Kali, and vanished.

The Matsya Purana already presents her as a goddess in her own right.

Worship and religion

She is honoured above all during the Navaratri festival, the 'nine nights'. In Pavagadh, in the Vagad region, she is revered by Hindus, Jains and Buddhists, and in the Vagadi language she is called Ma Nandore. An Adivasi dance, the garba, is also dedicated to her. Her main temple, on hilltops near Barodiya in Rajasthan, houses a new idol: the original black stone statue was destroyed by iconoclasts.

Legacy

The word nandini also names a perfume, a poetic metre and a type of musical composition.

Ancient sources: The Matsya Purana presents her as a goddess, the Mahabharata uses the word only as a common noun, and the Bhagavata Purana narrates the episode of Yashoda's daughter.

Trivia

A name tied to happiness. Nandini is linked to nandin, 'the one who gives happiness', although the latter is a masculine term.
A name with many uses. The word nandini appears in Sanskrit dictionaries as the name of a river, a mythical cow and several plants.
