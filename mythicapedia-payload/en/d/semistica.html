nymph of Greek mythology linked to Byzantium

Semystra

Semystra (also Semestra) is a nymph in Greek mythology associated with the foundation legends of Byzantium, present-day Istanbul. Her sanctuary stood near the city, at the head of the Golden Horn.

The myth

According to the myth, Semystra nurtured Keroessa, daughter of Io and Zeus. Io gave birth beside Semystra's altar and left the baby there to protect her from Hera, and Semystra found the infant and raised her.

One legend makes Semystra the mother of Byzas, the eponymous founder of Byzantium. Another, related by Dionysius of Byzantium, tells that the Greek colonists first planned to found their city on Semystra's site, but during sacrifices a crow snatched a bone from the altar and carried it to the Bosporion promontory, showing where Byzantium was finally built.

Worship and religion

Her sanctuary, near the city at the head of the Golden Horn, where the rivers Kydaros and Barbyses meet, had an altar where sacrifices were offered.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Byzantium relates the version in which the crow pointed out the site of the future city.

Trivia

A sacred crow. In one version, a crow stole a bone from the altar during a sacrifice and carried it to the Bosporus, indicating where Byzantium would rise.
A shrine by the Golden Horn. Her shrine stood where the rivers Kydaros and Barbyses met, at the head of the Golden Horn.
