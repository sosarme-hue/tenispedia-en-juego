Muthappan

Muthappan is a widely venerated deity in northern Kerala, India, understood as a joint manifestation of two gods, Thiruvappan and Vellatom. He matters because he is the group ancestor of the Thiyyar community, and because his shrines, the madappuras, welcome worshippers of any caste or religion.

The myth

According to local tradition, Muthappan arrived miraculously: the couple Ayyankara Vazhunavar and Padikuttyamma, devotees of Shiva, found a boy on a rock by a river and raised him as their son. The boy hunted, fed the poor and mixed with local communities, which clashed with Brahmin custom. When he revealed his divine nature, his parents prostrated themselves before him. As he left home, his sorrow set everything he saw ablaze, so his mother asked him not to destroy the world, and he blinded himself; this is why he is depicted blind.

On his journey he stopped at Kunnathoor, drawn by the toddy of the palms. The tapper Chandan tried to guard his trees, caught an old man stealing sap and fell unconscious. His wife called out to the old man as "Muthappan" ("grandfather"), and Chandan revived. Muthappan later settled at Parassinikkadavu.

Worship and religion

Muthappan is worshipped in the form of theyyam, a ritual dance, and is not represented by a consecrated idol or temple flagstaff. The rites are performed by the Thiyyar community. Offerings include panja-makara, sometimes with toddy and fish, and devotees offer boiled chickpeas and coconut slices. The main shrine is at Parassinikkadavu, and his theyyams are performed year-round. The cult remains alive.
