punishment shore for murderers and oath-breakers in Norse mythology

Náströnd

Náströnd, the «Corpse Shore», is a place in Norse mythology located in Hel, the realm of the dead. It is the destination of those guilty of murder and grave oath-breaking, and the dragon Níðhöggr lives there.

The myth

Náströnd is a shore within Hel, the realm of the dead. According to the stories, it is the place of punishment reserved for those who committed murder or seriously broke their oaths, that is, people who betrayed their word or took another's life. Its name means «corpse shore», and the tradition places it in the world of the dead.

On this shore lives Níðhöggr, a dragon or serpent of Norse tales who feeds on the condemned. Some accounts say it drains the fluids from their bodies, an image that underlines the fate of those who arrive there: no comfort, only the torment of a place where the body is consumed.

Early sources mention it briefly. The Völuspá, within the Poetic Edda, refers to it when describing the fate of the dead. Snorri Sturluson, in the Gylfaginning section of the Prose Edda, quotes that passage and uses the plural, Nástrandir, «corpse shores».

Ancient sources: The Völuspá of the Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning section.

Trivia

Old Norse spelling. In Old Norse it was written Nástrǫnd, and in modern Icelandic the letter ǫ is replaced by ö, so the name becomes Náströnd.
Plural in Snorri. Snorri Sturluson uses the plural form Nástrandir, meaning corpse shores, showing how the place was named in the Prose Edda.
