Inca goddess of potatoes

Axo Mama

Axo Mama is the goddess of potatoes in Inca mythology and one of the daughters of Pachamama, the earth mother. Her worship reflects the crucial role the tuber played in the Andean food supply.

The myth

According to Inca tradition, Axo Mama is one of the daughters of Pachamama, the earth mother. Her domain is farming: the potato, which grew wild in the Andes, reached its full agricultural potential under the Incas, who cultivated thousands of varieties. She is sometimes shown in traditional Inca dress, holding a potato stalk in each hand.

Worship and religion

Each village had a particularly odd-shaped potato to worship and to beg for a good harvest. The Incas buried potatoes with their dead to symbolize the goddess's blessings in the afterlife, the Uku Pacha. They also believed every crop had a protective spirit, the conopas, to which the best portions of the harvest were offered in a special ceremony to boost future yields. Many Andean people still perform rituals during potato planting and harvest, though most offerings go to Pachamama.

Trivia

Potatoes buried with the dead. The Incas buried potatoes with their dead as a symbol of the goddess's blessings in the Uku Pacha.
Ceramic figures. Personified representations of the goddess have been found on ceremonial terracotta vessels of the Moche culture.
