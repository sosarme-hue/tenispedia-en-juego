Gallo-Roman water goddess

Acionna

Acionna was a water goddess worshipped in the Orléans region during the Gallo-Roman period. She is known almost only from a single votive inscription dating to the 2nd century, so very little is documented about her.

The myth

No ancient tale about Acionna has survived: there is no genealogy, no heroic deeds and no episodes linked to her name. The only thing we know is that a devotee made an offering to her to fulfil a vow. Her name, ending in -onna, is a Latinized Gallic form, and the findspot suggests she was a goddess of water.

Worship and religion

Her cult is documented at the fontaine de l'Étuvée in Orléans, where a votive inscription was found in 1822 by Jean-Baptiste Jollois. Excavations in 2007 uncovered remains of a Gallo-Roman temple and a section of aqueduct there. It is not known whether her cult survived beyond the Roman period.

Legacy

Her name may be connected to that of the river Essonne, known in medieval texts as Axiona or Exona, and perhaps to that of the Esse stream, though this remains an unconfirmed hypothesis.

Ancient sources: She is not mentioned in any ancient literary texts; she is known only from a Latin votive inscription of the 2nd century found in Orléans.

Trivia

A drained spring. In 1822 Jean-Baptiste Jollois artificially drained the spring to see whether it could still supply the town's public fountains, and he found the inscription in a former cesspit.
A river with a similar name. In medieval texts the river Essonne appears as Axiona or Exona, a form that recalls the name of the goddess.
