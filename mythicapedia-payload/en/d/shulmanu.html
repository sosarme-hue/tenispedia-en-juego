Mesopotamian god of the underworld, fertility and war

Shulmanu

Shulmanu was a deity of Mesopotamian religion associated with the underworld, fertility and war, worshipped above all by the Assyrians. His name, meaning roughly "the friendly one", became part of the royal names of several Assyrian kings.

The myth

The surviving texts say very little about his stories, so his myth is poorly known. His name may point to a friendly manifestation of the Assyrian national god Ashur, a closer and more benevolent form of the divine.

Worship and religion

His worship is documented mainly in Assyria, and the Middle Assyrian period was the time of his greatest popularity. An inscription of Tukulti Ninurta I records vows for the good future of a temple dedicated to his consort Shulmanitu, along with curses against anyone who tried to destroy it. Later his figure also appears among western Semitic peoples such as the Arameans, Canaanites and Phoenicians.

Legacy

His name became part of the names of five Assyrian kings called Shalmaneser, from Shalmaneser I to Shalmaneser V, who used it as a regnal name.

Ancient sources: Bronze Age inscriptions, such as those of Tukulti Ninurta I and those found at Sidon, where his name and that of his consort Shulmanitu appear.

Trivia

A name for kings. Five Assyrian kings, from Shalmaneser I to Shalmaneser V, carried his name as part of their own.
A name with meaning. Salmānu literally means "the friendly one", and it may indicate a benevolent form of the god Ashur.
