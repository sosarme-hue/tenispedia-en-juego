Vedic goddess of dawn

Ushas

Ushas is the Vedic goddess of dawn, still honoured as a devi in Hinduism. She is the most exalted goddess of the Rig Veda, though not as central as Agni, Soma or Indra, and her name shares a root with Eos, Aurora and other Indo-European dawn goddesses.

The myth

The Vedic hymns portray her as a richly adorned young woman who crosses the sky in a golden chariot drawn by red-gold horses or cows. Her arrival wakes living beings and birds, drives away darkness and evil spirits, and sets everyone to their daily tasks. She is praised as a bringer of light and wealth and as a guardian of the cosmic order called ṛta, and she is called mother of cows and mother of the gods.

In the family books of the Rig Veda she is the daughter of Dyaus Pita, the Sky Father. Her sister is Ratri, goddess of night. In the traditional tellings, Surya, the sun god, follows her, and he is sometimes described as her husband and sometimes as her son.

Worship and religion

Around forty hymns of the Rig Veda are dedicated to her. Her presence continues in modern Hinduism above all through the Gayatri mantra, recited at dawn. She is honoured regionally during the festival of Chhath Puja in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh in India, and in Nepal.

Legacy

Her name comes from the same Indo-European root as the Greek Eos and the Latin Aurora. Some analysts consider several of her hymns among the most beautiful in the Vedas.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda, where she appears in numerous hymns, including those dedicated to her in books 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 10.

Trivia

Relatives in other mythologies. Her name comes from the same Indo-European root as Eos in Greece and Aurora in Rome.
A daily mantra. The Gayatri mantra, still recited today, keeps her presence alive in Hinduism.
