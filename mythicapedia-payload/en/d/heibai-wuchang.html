Chinese deities who escort the dead to the underworld

Heibai Wuchang

Heibai Wuchang, meaning «black and white impermanence», are two deities of Chinese folk religion who guide the spirits of the dead to the underworld. They are also worshipped as deities of fortune.

The myth

According to tradition, Xie Bi'an, the White Guard, and Fan Wujiu, the Black Guard, were constables in a yamen. After losing a convict they were escorting, they agreed to meet under a bridge. Heavy rain delayed Xie Bi'an; Fan Wujiu waited, drowned in the flood, and when Xie Bi'an found his body, he hanged himself. The Jade Emperor appointed them Guardians of the Underworld.

Other versions make them soldiers, neighbouring peasants or unrelated people. In one tale, the White Guard rescues a woman mistreated by her husband, urging her to leave with her children just before her house burns down.

Worship and religion

They are worshipped as deities of fortune and in City God temples in some countries. In Fujian and among overseas Chinese communities in Singapore, Indonesia and Malaysia, they are known as the «First and Second Masters» or «First and Second Uncles».

Trivia

Names with a moral. The guards' names carry a moral meaning: Xie Bi'an can be read as «those who make amends will always be at peace», and Fan Wujiu as «those who commit crimes will have no salvation».
Faces to scare spirits. Some statues show the two guards with fierce snarls and long red tongues sticking out, meant to frighten away evil spirits.
