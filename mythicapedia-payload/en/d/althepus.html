legendary king of Troezen, son of Poseidon

Althepus

A legendary king of Troezen, in the Argolid, son of Poseidon and Leis. According to tradition, he gave his name to the region, Althepia, and his reign fixed how its territory was shared between Poseidon and Athena.

The myth

According to the story, Leis was the daughter of Orus, the former king of the land, and her union with Poseidon produced Althepus. When he came to rule, he renamed the region, until then called Oraea, Althepia, a name said to preserve the older names of the area.

During his reign, Poseidon and Athena contested the land of the Troezenians, as they had also done in Athens. The dispute was settled through the mediation of Zeus, who made them joint guardians of the territory. Althepus was succeeded by Saron.

Worship and religion

The Troezenians worshipped Athena as Polias (Urban) and Sthenias (Strong), and Poseidon under the surname King. Ancient coins of Troezen bore a trident and the head of Athena, a reminder of this arrangement.

Trivia

Coins of Troezen. Ancient coins of Troezen showed a trident and the head of Athena, symbols of the two gods who shared the protection of the city.
A division like Athens. The dispute between Poseidon and Athena over the land of the Troezenians recalls the one the two gods had over Athens.
