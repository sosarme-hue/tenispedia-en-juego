shamanic figure of Hungarian mythology

Táltos

The táltos is a figure of Hungarian mythology, a person with supernatural powers who acted as a spiritual mediator, similar to a shaman. It matters because it brings together healing, the journey of the soul and a warning to the nation in a single figure.

The myth

According to tradition, gods or spirits chose the táltos before birth or in childhood. Being born with teeth, having a sixth finger or being born with the caul were signs of election. If that extra bone broke or was stolen before the age of seven, the power was lost.

Its main gift was the révülés, a trance in which the táltos healed wounds or uncovered hidden truths by sending the soul among the stars. It also had to warn the nation of invading armies or an approaching cultural collapse.

Worship and religion

Táltos were regarded as part of the pagan religion, and there is evidence the figure survived into the Habsburg era. In Christian usage, Jesus Christ was sometimes called égi táltos, or heavenly táltos.

Legacy

The figure remains alive in Hungarian folklore, and Göncöl's coach is identified with Ursa Major.

Ancient sources: The most reliable account comes from the priest Arnold Ipolyi (1854), and the archaic folk tale 'Son of the White Mare' recounts the táltos's great strength.

Trivia

A corrected finger. In the church of Székelyderzs, a painted ceiling showed a figure with six fingers. When it was renovated, the picture was 'corrected' to five.
Kampó and King Matthias. According to the tales, Kampó ate at the same table as King Matthias and fought the Turks with his icy body to win back one of the king's horses.
The coach in the sky. Göncöl's coach is visible in the night sky as Ursa Major, and the bear's tail is the coach's perch.
