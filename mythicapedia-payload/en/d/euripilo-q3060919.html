king of Cyrene, son of Poseidon

Eurypylus

Eurypylus is a figure of Greek mythology, a king of Cyrene regarded as a son of Poseidon. His link with the Argonauts and with the god Triton places him in the stories about North Africa.

The myth

In the tradition, Eurypylus was a son of Poseidon and the Pleiad Celaeno. He married Sterope, a daughter of Helios, and had two sons, Lycaon and Leucippus. A scholium on the Argonautica makes him the brother of Eupemus and Lycus, who were also called Eurytus and Lycaon.

His best-known episode comes when the Argonauts reach Libya and cross Lake Tritonis. There the king offers them a lump of clay as a sign of hospitality, while in another version Triton himself takes his shape when he meets them. Pindar says that Eurypylus embodied Triton, but other authors say he was Triton's brother. It is also said that he was reigning when Apollo seized the nymph Cyrene and carried her to his land, and that the city took its name from her.

Ancient sources: Pindar and the scholia on the Argonautica of Apollonius of Rhodes.
