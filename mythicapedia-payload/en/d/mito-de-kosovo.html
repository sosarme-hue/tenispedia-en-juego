Serbian national myth about the Battle of Kosovo (1389)

Kosovo Myth

The Kosovo Myth is a Serbian national legend about the Battle of Kosovo (1389), in which Prince Lazar chose a heavenly kingdom over earthly victory. It became a pillar of modern Serbian identity and politics.

The myth

According to tradition, the Ottoman sultan Murad I demanded that Prince Lazar either submit or fight on the Kosovo Polje. At the last supper before the battle, Lazar announced that one of his knights would betray him; Vuk Branković accused Miloš Obilić, who replied that he would kill Murad.

That night, a falcon arriving from Jerusalem offered Lazar a choice between an earthly kingdom and the Kingdom of Heaven. Lazar chose martyrdom, and the Serbs became a chosen people who signed a covenant with God. Branković withdrew his troops at crucial moments, which made him a symbol of betrayal, although he in fact fought bravely to the end. Obilić mortally wounded Murad with a dagger and was executed afterwards. Lazar was captured and beheaded.

Worship and religion

The cult of Lazar's martyrdom originated in the Serbian Orthodox Church, with writings by Patriarch Danilo III and the nun Jefimija. St. Vitus Day, known in Serbia as Vidovdan, remains a notable date there.

Legacy

The myth inspired Njegoš's epic drama The Mountain Wreath (1847), the sculptures of Ivan Meštrović and Uroš Predić's painting Kosovo Maiden (1919). Vuk Karadžić collected epic poems into the Kosovo Cycle. It was used in Serbian nationalism from the Congress of Berlin (1878) through the wars of Yugoslavia.

Ancient sources: Writings by Danilo III and Jefimija, Mavro Orbini's The Kingdom of the Slavs (1601) and the Tronoša Chronicle (1791).

Trivia

A blood-red flower. In Serbian tradition, the red color of the peony Paeonia peregrina became a symbol of the "bloodshed in the Battle of Kosovo".
An unjust traitor. Although the myth presents Vuk Branković as a traitor, the sources note that he in fact fought bravely to the end.
