Taoist deity of Bell Mountain and judge of the underworld

Jiang Ziwen

Jiang Ziwen is a Taoist deity from traditional China, once a Eastern Han dynasty official who died in combat during an uprising. He matters because his story shows how a real man could become a god and later a judge of the dead.

The myth

According to tradition, Jiang Ziwen was an official of Moling county, near modern Nanjing, and he came from Guangling. He was addicted to wine and women, yet he was known for his unusual skull shape. He died from a blow to the forehead while suppressing an insurrection.

After his death, many people reported seeing his spirit, and Sun Quan named him god of Bell Mountain, a peak near Nanjing that was then also called Jiangshan. When Buddhism reached China and the figure of Yama, king of the dead, became widely known, Jiang was placed in charge of the first of the ten courts of the underworld as the Qinguang King.

Worship and religion

His veneration began with reports of his apparitions and was recognized by Sun Quan, who gave him divine rank tied to a mountain. Later, once absorbed into Buddhist cosmology, he became associated with justice in the afterlife.

Trivia

An unusual head. Despite his disorderly life, he was recognized for an unusual skull shape, which people considered remarkable under phrenology.
A mountain with two names. The peak Sun Quan assigned him, near Nanjing, was then known as Jiangshan.
