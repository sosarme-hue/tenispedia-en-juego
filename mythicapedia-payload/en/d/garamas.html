mythical ancestor of the Garamantes

Garamas

Garamas is, in Greek mythology, the mythical ancestor of the Garamantes, a Berber people of Libya. His name gave rise to the city of Garama, which makes him important as a legendary origin for a real people.

The myth

According to the tradition, Garamas was a son of Apollo and Acacallis, daughter of Minos. Minos banished his pregnant daughter because of her relationship with the sun god, and the child was born in Libya. According to Apollonius of Rhodes, Garamas was another name for Amphithemis, who had with a Libyan lake nymph, Tritonis, two sons, Nasamon and Caphaurus. Caphaurus, a shepherd, killed the Argonauts Eurybates and Canthus after they plundered his flocks.

A Greek poetic fragment preserved in the Refutation of All Heresies says that humankind sprang from the earth. According to the Libyans, the first man born this way was called Tarbas; some scholars think the name refers to Garamas instead, who appears making an offering of the first fruits.

Legacy

The city of Garama and the Garamantes people took their name from him.

Ancient sources: Apollonius of Rhodes identifies him with Amphithemis, and a fragment preserved in the Refutation of All Heresies mentions him as a possible first man.

Trivia

An exotic name. The name Caphaurus means «camphor», presumably meant to suggest his somewhat exotic African origin.
A disputed first man. Some scholars read the name Tarbas as Iarbas, while others prefer to identify it with Garamas.
