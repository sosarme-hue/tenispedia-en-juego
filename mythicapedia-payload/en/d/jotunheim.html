land of the giants in Norse mythology

Jötunheim

Jötunheim is the realm of the jötnar, the giants of rock and ice in Norse mythology, a land separated from Asgard by the river Iving and from Midgard by barriers that are hard to cross. It matters because it is the setting for many stories in which gods and humans clash with these powers or bargain with them.

The myth

The sources describe Jötunheim as a set of prosperous lands, usually placed to the north and cut off from the world of gods and humans by boundaries that cannot be crossed by ordinary means. Its main city is Utgard. Other places are set there too, such as Þrymheim, home of the giant Þjazi; Gastropnir, where Menglad lived; and Glæsisvellir, the dwelling of the giant Gudmund, father of Höfund. The land was ruled by King Þrymr.

In the Völuspá, the arrival of three women from these lands marks the end of the gods' Golden Age. In Skírnismál, Freyr sees Gerðr, daughter of Gymir, and falls in love with her. He sends his servant Skírnir to her home, which is reached after crossing wet mountains, a flickering flame and darkness. In Þrymskviða, Loki borrows Freyja's feather cloak to fly to Þrymr and recover Thor's hammer. The king demands Freyja's hand in exchange, and Thor goes disguised as a bride, with Loki as his handmaid. In the Prose Edda, Loki also helps Þjazi abduct Iðunn, and the giant dies when he chases them to Asgard.

In Skáldskaparmál, Odin rides to Jötunheim on Sleipnir and meets the stone giant Hrungnir, who, after a wager over horses, chases him back to Asgard. Drunk, the giant boasts that he will carry off Sif and Freyja, and the gods summon Thor. Hrungnir accepts a duel at Grjótúnagarðar, his home in Jötunheim, where Thor kills him with Mjölnir, though a fragment of the whetstone lodges in Thor's head. Later, in Gylfaginning, Thor travels with Loki, Thjálfi and Röskva to the hall of Útgarða-Loki after crossing a great forest.

Legacy

The name survives in Jotunheimen, a large mountain range in Norway whose highest peak, Galdhøpiggen, at 2,469 metres, is the tallest mountain in Scandinavia. It also gives its title to songs by mythology-themed bands, such as Moonsorrow and Therion, and to a track on Danheim's album Hringràs (2019). In the fictional country of Qurac there is a fortress called Jötunheim, which according to legend only giants could have built.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, Skírnismál and Þrymskviða of the Poetic Edda, and Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A mountain with a mythic name. The Norwegian mountain range Jotunheimen takes its name from the world of the giants. Its highest peak, Galdhøpiggen, is the tallest mountain in Scandinavia.
A fragment in Thor's head. After killing Hrungnir, a piece of the giant's whetstone lodges in Thor's head, and the völva Gróa tries to remove it with a chant.
A fictional fortress. In the fictional country of Qurac, a fortress bears the name Jötunheim because, according to legend, only giants could have built it.
