Slavic goddess of the weather

Pogoda (Dogoda)

Pogoda, also written Dogoda, is a supposed Slavic goddess of the weather, described in some Polish sources as responsible for atmospheric conditions. Her existence is doubtful: she is known only from a late mention, and specialists do not agree about her.

The myth

Almost no stories about Pogoda survive. Her name appears in Polish tradition as that of a goddess of the weather, a deity charged with governing rain, wind and atmospheric conditions. In Slavic mythology, those phenomena are mostly linked to Perun, the god of thunder, so Pogoda is often read in relation to him.

The sources say nothing about her family, her deeds or her end. The pagan Slavs left no writings of their own: what is known comes from Christian chronicles and sermons, which sometimes demonized the old gods and at other times transferred their functions to saints. For that reason, the image of Pogoda should be treated with caution.

Ancient sources: Her name appears in the chronicle of the Polish historian Jan Długosz, the only reference that mentions her as a goddess of the weather.
