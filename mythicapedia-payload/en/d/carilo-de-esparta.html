legendary king of Sparta, ward of Lycurgus

Charilaus of Sparta

Charilaus is a king of Sparta from the Eurypontid dynasty, best remembered as the ward of the lawgiver Lycurgus. He belongs to Greek tradition of the 8th century BC, although his existence and the dates of his reign remain debated by specialists.

The myth

According to tradition, Charilaus belonged to the Eurypontids, the second royal house of Sparta, which ruled alongside the Agiads. Pausanias presents him as the son and successor of Polydectes, while other accounts make him the successor of his grandfather Eunomus. He reigned jointly with the Agiad king Archelaus, at the time when several villages came together to form the polis of Sparta.

He is best known as the ward and nephew of the lawgiver Lycurgus, whom he was said to have raised. During his reign the Spartans invaded Argolis, and the long hostility with Tegea is also thought to date from this time. According to the oracle of Delphi, the kings were to dedicate to Apollo half of the land taken from Aigys, in the northwest of Laconia. Pausanias adds that a temple of Apollo Cereatas stood in the area, and he also claims that Spartan expansion was blocked in the south by the resistance of Amyclae.

The lists then give him an orderly end: he was succeeded by his son Nicander, who was in turn the father of Theopompus. Later Spartans, the tradition says, built a story in which the Eurypontid dynasty was as old as the Agiad one, inventing kings to balance the lists. Modern historians regard Charilaus as the first documented Eurypontid king, though they place his reign at different dates depending on the author.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in the 2nd century AD, records his succession and the account of the conquest of Aigys, and the Delphic oracle refers to the division of land for Apollo.

Trivia

An early oracle. If the Delphic oracle on the division of land at Aigys is genuine, it would be the oldest known oracle from Delphi.
A temple in the countryside. Pausanias, writing in the 2nd century AD, states that a temple of Apollo Cereatas stood in the Aigys area.
