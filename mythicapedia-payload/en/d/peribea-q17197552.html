naiad of Greek mythology, wife of Icarius

Periboea

Periboea is a naiad of Greek mythology, the wife of Icarius and mother of Penelope, who later married Odysseus. She barely appears in ancient texts, yet her lineage links the story of Sparta with that of Ithaca.

The myth

According to Apollodorus's Library, the only ancient source that mentions her, Periboea was queen of Sparta through her husband Icarius. Together they had five sons, Tyndareus's rival kin aside, named Thoas, Damasippus, Imeusimus, Aletes and Perileos, and a daughter, Penelope, who would eventually marry Odysseus.

No source specifies who her parents were. Some modern scholars suggest she may have been a daughter of the river Eurotas, though this idea has no direct support in the ancient texts.

Legacy

Margaret Atwood portrays her in the short novel The Penelopiad as a negligent mother to her daughter Penelope, giving a fresh voice to Odysseus's household from another angle.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus's Library, which names her as the wife of Icarius and mother of Penelope and her brothers.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Periboea means «surrounded by cattle», according to the ancient Greek form of her name.
A mother in the background. Although she is Penelope's mother, she almost never appears in the stories, and Atwood's novel is one of the few modern works to portray her in detail.
