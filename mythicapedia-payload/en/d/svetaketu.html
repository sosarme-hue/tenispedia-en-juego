Vedic sage, son of Uddalaka

Svetaketu

Svetaketu is a sage of the Upanishads, son of the sage Uddalaka and a symbol of the pursuit of knowledge. His story in the Chandogya Upanishad is one of the earliest known mentions of reincarnation.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Svetaketu established the custom that a wife should remain loyal to one husband for life. It arose after he saw a brahmin seize his mother's hand in front of his father, and he set that rule in response.

In the Chandogya Upanishad, Svetaketu returns home after years of study, and his childhood friends ask him what he learned about the afterlife. He has no answer, and neither does his father. They turn to the king, who claims to have known the doctrine of reincarnation all along and explains it. He adds that it is a common belief among the kshatriyas, the warrior and administrative class, from which they draw their courage in battle.

Ancient sources: Found in the Chandogya Upanishad, the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad and the Kaushitaki Upanishad, and in the Mahabharata.

Trivia

Reincarnation in the Vedas. Svetaketu's story is, according to the sources, the first time reincarnation appears in the Vedas.
A rule of fidelity. The Mahabharata credits him with originating the custom of a wife remaining loyal to one husband.
