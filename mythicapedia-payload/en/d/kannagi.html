Tamil heroine and goddess of chastity

Kannagi

Kannagi is a legendary woman of Tamil tradition, the heroine of the epic Cilappatikaram, and she is worshipped as a goddess in southern India and Sri Lanka. She is remembered as a model of faithfulness and as a symbol of the pursuit of justice.

The myth

According to the tale, Kannagi was the faithful wife of Kovalan, son of a wealthy merchant from Puhar. Kovalan fell in love with the dancer Madhavi and spent his fortune on her. When he was left penniless, he returned in remorse to Kannagi, who forgave him.

The couple set out for Madurai to start afresh. There Kovalan tried to sell one of Kannagi's anklets, but the royal goldsmith, who had stolen an anklet from the queen, falsely accused him. The king ordered his execution without a trial. Kannagi came before the court, broke open her anklet and showed that it held rubies, while the queen's contained pearls. The king realised his error and died of remorse. Kannagi then cursed the city, which burned to the ground. At the intervention of the goddess Meenakshi, her anger subsided and she attained salvation.

Worship and religion

She was already honoured as a goddess in ancient Tamilakam, and the poem records that the Chera king Senguttuvan dedicated a temple to her. Today her cult has little presence in Tamil Nadu, but it remains alive in Sri Lanka, where she is venerated as Kannaki Amman or Pattini, and in Kerala as Bhagavati, at the Kodungallur temple. Devotees invoke her in the month of Vaikasi with songs, trance dances and offerings of pongal.

Legacy

Her story inspired the first Tamil film based on the epic, released in 1942. A statue of her holding the anklet, installed on Marina Beach in Chennai, was removed in 2001 and re-erected in 2006.

Ancient sources: The poem Cilappatikaram, attributed to Ilango Adigal, tells her story, and the Sangam poem Naṟṟiṇai mentions her in a briefer form.

Trivia

Anklets with different gems. Kannagi proves her husband's innocence by breaking her anklet and revealing rubies, unlike the pearls in the queen's anklet.
Protector of the coast. In Sri Lanka, coastal communities venerated her as a protector because of her link with the sea. According to legend, she revealed her divine form to herdsmen before ascending to heaven.
