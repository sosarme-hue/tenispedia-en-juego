naiad nymph of Greek mythology, mother of Hypseus

Creusa

Creusa is a naiad nymph, daughter of Gaia and Oceanus, remembered as the ancestor of the Lapiths. Unlike other daughters of Oceanus, she is never counted among the Oceanids.

The myth

Pindar, the first author to mention her, says that in the glens of Mount Pindus Creusa took the river god Peneus as her lover, and from their union came Hypseus, the mighty and warlike king of the Lapiths and father of Cyrene.

Pherecydes alludes to her without naming her, noting only that Hypseus's mother was a naiad. Diodorus Siculus places Creusa and Peneus at the origin of Hypseus and Stilbe, who bore Lapithes and Centaurus to Apollo, the eponymous ancestors of the Lapiths and the Centaurs. Ovidio confuses her with another Creusa, daughter of Erechtheus, in a version recorded only by him.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Diodorus Siculus, Pherecydes and Ovid mention her, though with differing genealogies.
