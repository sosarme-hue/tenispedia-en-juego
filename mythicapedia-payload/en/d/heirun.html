mythical goat of Norse mythology

Heiðrún

Heiðrún is a goat from Norse mythology whose udders produce mead instead of milk. She lives on the roof of Valhalla, and her drink feeds the einherjar, the warriors who died in battle and dwell there alongside the gods of Asgard. She appears among the creatures surrounding the world of the gods in medieval Icelandic texts.

The myth

In Norse mythology, Heiðrún lives on the roof of Valhalla, the hall in Asgard, and feeds on the foliage of the tree Læraðr. What makes her special is what she produces: her udders give not milk but mead. The gods who dwell in Asgard drink it, and above all the einherjar, the warriors slain in battle who live in Valhalla.

Her story is told in the Poetic Edda, in the Grímnismál, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda. Snorri's description closely resembles the stanza in the Grímnismál, which suggests he drew on it. As for her name, its origin remains debated among scholars.

Legacy

Her name appears in the song "Heidrun" by the Swedish melodic death metal band Amon Amarth, which is about the goat. The Portland band Agalloch also mentions her in the lyrics of "Not Unlike the Waves", from their 2006 album Ashes Against the Grain. Heiðrún is also a character in the video game Fire Emblem Heroes, revealed in June 2024.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Grímnismál and Hyndluljóð) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

An insult to Freyja. In the Hyndluljóð, the giantess Hyndla uses the name Heiðrún as an insult against the goddess Freyja. Some translators have rendered it simply as "she-goat".
A name split in two. According to Anatoly Liberman, the names of the goat and of the stag Eikþyrnir may have come from a single term, Heiðþyrnir, split into two halves.
