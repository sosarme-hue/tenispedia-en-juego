episode in Greek mythology of the hero disguised as a girl

Achilles on Skyros

An episode in the myth of Achilles in which the hero, hidden by his mother Thetis, lives disguised as a maiden at the court of King Lycomedes of Skyros. Absent from the Iliad, the story became popular in literature and above all in opera.

The myth

To keep her son from dying at Troy, as prophesied, the nymph Thetis sent him to the court of Lycomedes, king of Skyros, disguised as the king's daughter or a lady-in-waiting, under the name Pyrrha, "the red-haired". There Achilles had an affair with Deidamia, one of the king's daughters, and they had one or two sons, Neoptolemus and Oneiros, depending on the version.

When another prophecy said the war could not be won without Achilles, Odysseus went to Skyros to find him. He offered gifts to the princesses, hiding weapons among them, and had his men imitate an enemy attack with trumpet blasts. Achilles chose a sword and revealed himself, and sailed with the Achaeans. In Statius's Achilleid, the most elaborate version, he assaults Deidamia during a night festival of Dionysus, and she keeps silent to protect him. In the end Achilles swears to return to her, though the reader knows he will die at Troy.

Legacy

The episode inspired many works between the seventeenth and nineteenth centuries. The first opera on the theme was Francesco Sacrati's La finta pazza, staged in Venice in 1641, and Metastasio's libretto became the most popular. Tirso de Molina brought it to the stage in El Aquiles (1612), and Handel composed the opera Deidamia in 1741.

Ancient sources: It appears in Statius's Achilleid (1st century CE) and is mentioned in Dante's Divine Comedy; earlier works by Polygnotus and Athenion, and a lost tragedy by Euripides, are also referenced.

Trivia

Early opera. La finta pazza of 1641 was staged at the Teatro Novissimo in Venice, the first building ever built specifically for opera.
A rushed libretto. Metastasio had only 18 days to finish his libretto for Maria Theresa's wedding, instead of the three months he usually needed.
