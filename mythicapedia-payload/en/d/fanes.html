Orphic god of light and procreation

Phanes

Phanes, also called Protogonos, is a primeval deity of Orphic cosmogony, born from the cosmic egg. He is regarded as the origin of procreation and of the creation of all things, which makes him central to Orphic theogonies.

The myth

According to the Orphic rhapsodies, the cosmic egg was the offspring of Aether and the Abyss, and from it Phanes burst out, tearing the shell with a light so dazzling that he remained invisible to everyone except Night, whom the brilliance does not affect. He is described as a winged being with golden feathers and four heads, those of a bull, a lion, a ram and a serpent, and as androgynous, both male and female. Ancient sources present him as the first living being and the cause of sexual reproduction.

Phanes created the Sun, Moon, stars, months and humans, and set the order of the cosmos. Joined with his daughter Night, he fathered Earth and Heaven. He took up a sceptre and ruled as the first king of the heavens, but later lost interest in power, which passed to Night, and finally he withdrew to the highest heavens in a winged chariot, from where he watches over the world.

Genealogies vary among authors. Some traditions make him a son of Aether and Chaos, others of Time or of Aether alone. Macrobius reports that the oracle of Colophon identified him with the god Iao, and Boccaccio made him the seventh son of Demogorgon.

Worship and religion

Orphic hymns invoke him, honour him with many sacrifices and call him to a sacred ritual. In some traditions he is linked with Dionysus. The sources do not document temples of his own or a living cult.

Legacy

In modern scholarship he is often compared with Eros or Mithras, and some specialists have described Orphism as un-Greek or Asiatic because of its dualism. His image of a god emerging from the world egg is a classic example of how ancient cosmogonies imagined the birth of light.

Ancient sources: The Orphic Argonautica, the Orphic Hymns, Nonnus's Dionysiaca and Damascius mention him, along with the Derveni papyrus.

Trivia

Egg with a serpent. He is depicted emerging from an egg entwined with a serpent, one of the most recognisable images of Orphism.
A name meaning light. His name, from a Greek root meaning to shine, means luminous or revealing, and the sources link it to the manifestation of light.
