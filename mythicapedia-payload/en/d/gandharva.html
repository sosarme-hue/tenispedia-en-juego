celestial musicians of Indian religions

Gandharva

Gandharvas are celestial beings in Indian religions, above all Hinduism and Buddhism, known as divine musicians. In Hinduism they are male spirits who are the husbands of the apsarasas, while in Buddhism they rank among the lowest devas.

The myth

In Hinduism, gandharvas are male spirits, the husbands of the apsarasas, handsome and sweetly scented. Some have animal parts, usually a bird or a horse. They have extraordinary musical talent, guard the intoxicating juice of the soma plant and compose music for the gods in their palaces. They usually live in Svarga, serving at Indra's court, although they also have their own realm, the Gandharvaloka.

The Atharvaveda counts 6,333 gandharvas. In the Rigveda they appear as a single being who watches over the soma near the sun and brings things from beyond into this world, such as the soma, humans and the horse. Accounts of their origin differ: some texts make them creatures of Prajapati, Brahma, Kashyapa and Pradha, or Vac. The Bhagavata Purana tells that they were born from Brahma's laughter when he saw some asuras indulging in desire.

In Buddhism, gandharvas are minor devas classed among the Four Great Kings' realm, and those who practised the most basic ethics are reborn among them. They fly, play music with great skill and dwell in flowers and trees. A famous tale tells of the love between Pañcha Sikha and Bhaddā Suriyavacchasā, daughter of the chieftain Timbarū, which is resolved through a love song in which the musician weaves in themes about the Buddha and the arhats.

Worship and religion

Worship of gandharvas is very rare in India and is mostly found in Kerala, where they are enshrined in temples, sometimes alongside yakshis, and prayed to for fertility and good fortune. Angering the gandharvas is believed to bring the curse known as gandharva dosha, which especially affects fertility and childbirth. Kerala also has the gandharvanpattu ritual, a theyyam and kalampattu chant meant to invoke them.

Legacy

In India, the word gandharva also names skilled singers of classical music. In Hindu law, the gandharva marriage is a union by mutual consent without ceremony or financial arrangement between families.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda, the Atharvaveda, the Bhagavata Purana and the Mahabharata mention them extensively, and they appear in the Buddhist suttas Janavasabha (DN 18) and Mahātaṇhāsankhaya (MN 38).

Trivia

A being in transit. In Buddhism, the term gandharva also denotes a being in the intermediate state between death and a new birth, according to a commentary on a sutta.
A sacred tree. In Jainism, the tumbaru is the sacred tree of the gandharvas.
