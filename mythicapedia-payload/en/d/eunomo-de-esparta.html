mythical king of Sparta

Eunomus of Sparta

Eunomus was, according to tradition, a king of Sparta from the Eurypontid dynasty, placed by ancient accounts around 800 BC. His importance lies less in deeds, which are scarcely described, than in his link with Lycurgus and with the ideal of good legal order that his name expresses.

The myth

Ancient authors do not agree on his lineage. Herodotus makes him the son of Polydectes and father of Charilaus, while Pausanias considers him the son of Prytanis and father of Polydectes. Tradition also ties him to Lycurgus, presenting him as either his brother or, more often, his father.

His reign is described as entirely peaceful, with no wars or exploits to recount, and he was succeeded by Charilaus. Many modern scholars suspect that Eunomus was an imaginary figure, born as a personification of eunomia, the good government and well-ordered law attributed to Lycurgus. For this reason, tradition presents him less as a warrior than as a guardian of order, and his figure works more as a symbol of a political ideal than as a historical king. As with other figures in the Spartan king lists, the dates assigned to him vary from one work to another: modern references place his reign between 805 and 775 BC.

Ancient sources: Herodotus and Pausanias, who disagree about his genealogy.

Trivia

A disputed lineage. Herodotus and Pausanias give him different fathers, showing that the genealogy of Spartan kings was not fixed in antiquity.
A name meaning order. His name evokes eunomia, good law, a central concept in the political image that tradition built around Sparta.
