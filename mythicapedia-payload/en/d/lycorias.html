Nereid of Greek mythology

Lycorias

Lycorias is a Nereid of Greek mythology, one of the fifty sea-nymph daughters of Nereus and Doris. She is remembered for her golden hair and for her brief appearance in Virgil's Georgics.

The myth

According to the tradition, Lycorias was one of the fifty Nereids, daughters of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and the Oceanid Doris. Her name points to her yellow or golden hair.

She barely appears in ancient texts. Most of what we know comes from a passage in Virgil's Georgics, where she belongs to the company of Cyrene, the mother of Aristaeus, when Cyrene hears her son's cry from her chamber in the river depths. Meanwhile the Nereids card Milesian fleece dyed with sea-purple. Among them is Lycorias, whom the poet describes already feeling the first pangs of labour, in contrast with the still maiden Cydippe.

Ancient sources: Virgil mentions her in the Georgics, among the Nereids who attend Cyrene.
