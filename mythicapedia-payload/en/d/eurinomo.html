underworld demon of Greek mythology

Eurynomos

Eurynomos is an underworld demon of Greek mythology said to feed on the flesh of corpses, leaving only the bones. He is barely documented, known almost solely from a passage of Pausanias, which makes him a intriguing figure for scholars.

The myth

The only ancient testimony about Eurynomos is Pausanias's description of a painting of Hades by Polygnotus at the sanctuary of Delphi. According to the guides there, Eurynomos was a demon of Hades who devoured dead bodies until only the bones remained. Pausanias portrays him with skin somewhere between blue and black, like that of the flies that settle on meat, his teeth bared, seated on a vulture's skin.

Homer's poems about Odysseus, the Minyas and the Returns mention Hades and its horrors but never name Eurynomos, which suggests he was not a widely known figure in epic tradition.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, is the only ancient source that mentions him.
