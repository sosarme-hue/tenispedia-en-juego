eighth tirthankara of Jainism

Chandraprabha

Chandraprabha, also called Chandranatha, is the eighth tirthankara (ford-maker) of Jainism in the current age. He is central to Jain devotion because of his final liberation, nirvana, on Sammed Shikharji.

The myth

According to traditional accounts, he was born in Chandrapuri, near Varanasi, to King Mahasena and Queen Lakshmana Devi, of the Ikshvaku dynasty. His mother dreamt sixteen auspicious dreams before his birth, and his name, meaning "moon radiance", refers to his white complexion, said to be as bright as the moon.

After a youth of princely comfort, he ruled with indifference to pleasure. Soon after ascending the throne he renounced the world and, after three months of meditation beneath the naga tree, attained kevala jnana, omniscience. For many years he taught his doctrine and, according to tradition, attained nirvana on Sammed Shikharji, becoming a siddha, a liberated soul that has destroyed all its karma.

Worship and religion

He is recognised by the crescent moon emblem and is venerated in temples across India, especially in the east, in Bihar, Bengal and Orissa. He is depicted in lotus or kayotsarga posture. His main sanctuary is on the hill of Sammed Shikharji, where pilgrims worship his footprints, and his worship remains alive in many Jain temples today.

Legacy

He appears in hymn collections such as the Jinastotrani and in Haribhadra's Chandraprabha-charitra. Bronze sculptures from the Hansi hoard, dating to the 8th and 9th centuries CE, show his veneration in medieval northern India.

Ancient sources: Traditional Jain texts narrate his life, and Buddhist scriptures also mention him.

Trivia

Hansi hoard. Excavations at Asigarh Fort in Haryana uncovered 58 bronze idols, many depicting him, buried to protect them from Ghaznavid invasions.
His name. Chandraprabha means "moon radiance", alluding to the whiteness of his complexion.
