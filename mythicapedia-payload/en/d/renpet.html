Egyptian goddess of years, youth and spring

Renpet

Renpet was the Egyptian word for 'year' and also a goddess who personified the passing of time, youth, fertility and spring. She belongs to the Egyptian tradition, and her name and symbol shaped the way the regnal years of the pharaohs were recorded.

The myth

Sources describe Renpet as the Mistress of Eternity or Mistress of Time. She is shown as a woman wearing a palm shoot on her head, a symbol of the passing of time. In other images she is accompanied by crop and harvest symbols, reflecting her role as a personification of fertility, youth, spring and renewal.

Her name is linked to the verb renep, 'to be young' or 'to rejuvenate', which explains her association with cycles of renewal. She became very popular in the late periods of ancient Egypt. At certain times she was linked with Sothis, the goddess associated with the star Sirius, whose first annual appearance heralded the flooding of the Nile. She is sometimes equated with the goddess Aperetiset.

Legacy

Her hieroglyph, a palm branch stripped of leaves with a notch, was used from the reign of Dyet in the First Dynasty to open the phrase recording each pharaoh's regnal year. The Palermo Stone uses these signs to separate the records of different reigns.
