legendary prince of Silla, ancestor of the Tajima clans

Amenohiboko

Amenohiboko is a legendary prince of Silla who, according to Japanese tradition, settled in Japan during the reign of Emperor Suinin, around the 3rd or 4th century. He is regarded as the ancestral god of Tajima Province and as the origin figure of several local clans.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, a woman was lying beside a swamp called Anuguma in Silla when sunlight touched her and she gave birth on the spot to a red ball. A passerby begged her for it and eventually took possession of it.

One day this man was walking his cow to deliver provisions when Prince Amenohiboko saw him and, mistakenly believing he meant to eat the cow, had him imprisoned. To win his freedom, the man offered the red ball as compensation. Amenohiboko took it home, where it became a woman of great beauty. They married and lived fairly happily until he lashed out at her over a trivial matter. Hurt, she declared she would return to her homeland, boarded a small boat and sailed to Naniwa in Japan.

Regretting his actions, Amenohiboko tried to follow, but the guardian kami barred him from entering the land, so he stayed in Tajima, where he took another wife, Maemitsu. The first wife became a deity of the people of Naniwa under the name Akaruhime.

Worship and religion

He is venerated as the ancestral god of Tajima Province and is said to be enshrined at Izushi Shrine in Toyooka, Hyōgo Prefecture, where according to tradition seven or eight treasures he brought from Silla are kept. His cult remains active in the region.

Legacy

His descendants include the mother of Empress Jingū, a controversial figure who according to tradition invaded the so-called promised land, often interpreted as the Korean Peninsula. The linguist Alexander Vovin suggests that, given Amenohiboko's Korean origin, Jingū and Emperor Ōjin may have been native speakers of Korean.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki recount his story and that of Akaruhime.

Trivia

A shrine with treasures. According to tradition, Izushi Shrine in Toyooka houses seven or eight treasures brought by Amenohiboko from Silla.
A Korean parallel. Scholars compare his story with that of Dongmyeong, founder of Goguryeo, who also hatched from an egg after his mother was touched by sunlight.
