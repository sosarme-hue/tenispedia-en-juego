household goblin of northern Spanish folklore

Trasgo

The trasgo is a household goblin from the folklore of Galicia, Asturias, Cantabria and northern Portugal. Restless, mischievous and teasing, it is blamed for night noises and small disorders in the home.

The myth

According to tradition, the trasgo lives in the house and slips in at night while the family sleeps. It is usually pictured as a small, dark-skinned being, sometimes invisible, with a red pointed cap, a limp in the right leg and a hole in its left hand. When in a bad mood it breaks crockery, frightens livestock, rummages through clothes and tangles the hair of sleepers. If treated well, it may do household chores overnight.

It is very hard to be rid of it, since it follows the family even when they move house. To expel it, one must set an impossible task, such as bringing a basket of water, gathering grain from the floor or whitening a black goat. Unable to finish, it leaves in shame.

Legacy

Rafael Dieste titled a collection of stories De los archivos del trasgo. Asturian singer Víctor Manuel wrote the song «Trasgu» in 1981. Ana María Matute made it one of the main characters of Olvidado Rey Gudú. In the Spanish translation of The Hobbit, «trasgo» renders the word goblin, which is often confused with orcs.

Trivia

A name with many variants. In Asturias he is called Trasgu, Cornín or Xuan dos Caminos in the west, and Gorretín Coloráu in the east, after his red hat.
A household sprite on screen. Pumuckl, created by Ellis Kaut for a German animated series, is considered one of the most faithful screen versions of the trasgo.
