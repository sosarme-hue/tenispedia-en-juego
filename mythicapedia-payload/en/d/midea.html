nymph of Greek mythology, loved by Poseidon

Midea

Midea is a nymph in Greek mythology, loved by Poseidon and mother of Aspledon. Her story is brief, but her name survives in the geography of Boeotia, a region of central Greece, where a town once bore it.

The myth

According to tradition, Midea was a nymph loved by the god Poseidon, with whom she had a son named Aspledon. The accounts that have come down to us about her are scarce and say nothing more of her story beyond this bond.

Pausanias quotes some verses by the poet Quernias, whose work was no longer preserved in his own time. According to those verses, Aspledon gave his name to a city in Boeotia, and the nymph gave hers to another town in the same region, also called Midea. In this way, the myth became tied to local place names.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, quotes verses by the poet Quernias that mention Midea.
