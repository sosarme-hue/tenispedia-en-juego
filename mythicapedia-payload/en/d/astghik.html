Armenian goddess of love, fertility and springs

Astghik

Astghik is the Armenian goddess of love, fertility and water sources, and the bride of the warrior god Vahagn. Her name means 'little star', and because she was linked to the planet Venus, the Greeks of the Hellenistic period identified her with Aphrodite.

The myth

According to tradition, Astghik was a sister of Anahit and Nane and belonged to the circle of Aramazd, although other accounts describe her differently. The older texts present her as a goddess of the creation of heaven and earth, a role she lost as Aramazd became the chief creator god and Anahit the moon goddess. A later tradition makes her a daughter of Noah, born after the flood.

The best-known episode tells how Astghik liked to bathe naked in a stream. Young men from nearby villages came to watch her and lit fires by the water to see better. To protect her privacy, the goddess summoned a thick fog that covered the area. The fog stayed, and the place became known as Msho Dasht, from the Armenian word mshoush, meaning 'fog'.

Worship and religion

Her main seat was at Ashtishat. She shared a temple complex with Vahagn at Taron, the site where centuries later, after Armenia's Christianization, the monastery of Surb Karapet was founded. She was also worshipped alongside Aramazd in the district of Andzevatsik, in Vaspurakan, and in various towns and mountains near Lake Van.

Legacy

Her festival, Vardavar, is still celebrated in Armenia. People drench one another with water as a rite of purification and renewal, a custom that blends pre-Christian tradition with the Christian feast of the Transfiguration.

Trivia

Noah's daughter. A later tradition holds that Astghik was a daughter of Noah and was born after the flood.
The origin of Msho Dasht. The name of the foggy place comes from the Armenian word for 'fog', linked to the goddess's episode.
