white fox of Japanese folklore, mother of Abe no Seimei

Kuzunoha

Kuzunoha is a popular kitsune, or supernatural fox, in Japanese folklore. According to legend, she is the mother of the famous onmyōji Abe no Seimei, and her story of love and farewell has inspired kabuki and bunraku plays.

The myth

In the tale, the young nobleman Abe no Yasuna was on his way to a shrine in Shinoda, in Settsu Province, when he met a military official hunting foxes for their livers, which were used as medicine. Yasuna fought the hunter, suffered several wounds, and freed a white fox the man had trapped.

Later, a beautiful woman named Kuzunoha helped him return home and tended to his wounds. She was the fox he had saved, now in human form. They fell in love and married, and had a son, Seimei, who was remarkably clever and inherited part of his mother's supernatural power. One day, while Kuzunoha was admiring chrysanthemums, the boy glimpsed her tail. Her true nature revealed, she prepared to return to the wild, leaving a farewell poem asking Yasuna to seek her in Shinoda Forest. There she appeared to them as a fox, revealed herself as the kami of Shinoda Shrine, and gave Seimei the ability to understand the language of animals.

Legacy

Kuzunoha appears in kabuki and bunraku plays, especially the fourth part of the five-part Ashiya Dōman Ōuchi Kagami, which is often staged on its own. In Izumi Province stands a Kuzunoha Inari shrine, said to be built where she departed, and a nearby pond has been designated a historic site by the city.

Trivia

A famous poem. Kuzunoha's farewell poem has become well known. The folklorist Kiyoshi Nozaki translated it as an invitation to come and find her in the great wood of Shinoda.
