Hawaiian goddess of the Earth, mother of the islands

Papa (Papahānaumoku)

Papa, also called Papahānaumoku, is the Earth Mother in the religion and mythology of the ancient Hawaiians. She is regarded as the mother of the islands and a primordial force of creation.

The myth

According to the myths, Papa is the wife of Wākea, the Father Sky, son of the god Kahiko. Together they created the Hawaiian Islands and became the ancestors of the chiefs and noblemen. Among their most important offspring are the islands of Hawaiʻi, Maui, Oʻahu and Kauaʻi.

The most famous myth tells of Hoʻohōkūkalani, their daughter, who became Wākea's lover. When she gave birth to a stillborn child, Papa buried him in the soft earth and named him Hāloa; from that spot the first taro sprang. Hoʻohōkūkalani later had a living child with her father, who was also named Hāloa.

Worship and religion

The women's temple, called Hale o Papa, is the main religious structure linked to her worship. It is often built next to a luakini, the “men's temples” dedicated mainly to Kū and Lono, though many practitioners believe it may also stand alone. Today some Hawaiians, especially women, still worship her as a life-giving and healing force. In the Aloha ʻĀina movement she is a central figure, a symbol of an Earth abused by human misdeeds.

Legacy

In 2007 the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands Marine National Monument was renamed the Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument, in honour of the goddess.

Ancient sources: Ancient Hawaiian chants mention her as the consort of Wākea and the mother of the islands.

Trivia

A taro born from the earth. According to the myth, the first taro plant grew from the spot where Papa buried Hoʻohōkūkalani's stillborn child.
A monument named after her. Since 2007 the marine monument in the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands has borne a name derived from the goddess's.
