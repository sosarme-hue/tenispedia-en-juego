Trojan hero of Greek mythology

Hyrtacus

Hyrtacus is an obscure figure of Greek mythology tied to the Trojan War, a companion of King Priam and the father of several warriors. He matters mainly as a link between the Trojan court and the family stories that surround the conflict.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hyrtacus was a comrade of King Priam of Troy. He married Arisbe, daughter of King Merops of Percote, after Priam had divorced her to marry Hecabe. Their son Asius fought at Troy.

In Virgil's Aeneid he is credited with two more sons, Nisus and Hippocoon. The sources do not say who his own parents were. His name may be of Cretan origin, given its resemblance to the ancient city of Hyrtacina in southwestern Crete. He is a minor figure who barely appears in the stories, so his tale comes down to these notes on his marriage and descendants.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid mentions him through his sons Nisus and Hippocoon.

Trivia

A possibly Cretan name. The name may derive from the ancient city of Hyrtacina in southwestern Crete.
A wife with a past. Arisbe had been married to Priam before he wed Hecabe.
