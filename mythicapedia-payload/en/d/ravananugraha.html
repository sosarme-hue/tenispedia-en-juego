episode of the god Shiva and the demon-king Ravana

Ravananugraha

A benevolent aspect of the Hindu god Shiva, seated on Mount Kailash with Parvati while the demon-king Ravana tries to uproot the mountain. It is one of the most popular themes in Indian art.

The myth

According to tradition, the demon-king Ravana, after stealing Kubera's flying chariot, tried to lift Mount Kailash, home of Shiva and Parvati. Shiva pressed the mountain down with his big toe, trapping Ravana beneath it. For a thousand years the demon sang hymns to the god, who finally forgave him and granted him an invincible sword. The Uttara Kanda of the Ramayana records this story. In the Tevaram, a Tamil Shaiva work, Ravana composes the Shiva Tandava Stotram playing an instrument made from one of his heads. Shiva gave him the Atmalinga on condition that it stay wherever it was placed; Ganesha tricked Ravana into setting it on the ground at Gokarna.

Worship and religion

Tradition holds that the Atmalinga was fixed at Gokarna, a place associated with temples dedicated to Shiva.

Legacy

The episode appears in reliefs such as the one at the Kailasha Temple in Ellora, in the Albert Hall Museum in Jaipur, and at the Hoysaleswara temple in Halebidu.

Ancient sources: The Uttara Kanda of the Ramayana and the Tevaram, a Tamil Shaiva work.

Trivia

A name born from tears. Ravana means «the one who cried», a name he received after crying out in pain beneath the mountain.
A chariot that could not fly. The flying chariot Pushpaka, stolen from Kubera, could not fly over Shiva's sacred place.
