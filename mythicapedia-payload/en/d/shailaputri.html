Shailaputri

Shailaputri, meaning 'daughter of the mountain', is the first of the nine forms of the goddess Durga (Navadurga) in Hinduism and one of the manifestations of Parvati. She is worshipped on the first day of Navaratri.

The myth

According to tradition, Shailaputri is the daughter of Himavat, the king of the Himalayan mountains. In an earlier life she was Sati, daughter of Daksha. When Daksha held a great fire sacrifice and did not invite her husband Shiva, Sati attended anyway. There Daksha insulted Shiva, and she, unable to bear the insult, threw herself into the sacrificial fire. In her next birth she was reborn as Parvati and married Shiva again.

In yoga she is seen as the goddess of the root chakra, Muladhara. When a practitioner awakens spiritually, she begins her journey upward toward Shiva, who waits at the crown chakra, Sahasrara.

Worship and religion

Her festival forms part of Navaratri. The puja begins with the Ghatasthapana ritual, which symbolises Shakti: layers of mud and seeds are spread in a shallow clay dish, a pot is filled with Ganges water, and coins, turmeric-coloured rice and durva grass are placed in it, with mango leaves around its mouth and a coconut on top. She is shown with two hands, a crescent moon on her forehead, a trident in one hand and a lotus in the other, mounted on the bull Nandi. Her mantra is Om Devi Shailaputryai Namah. Temples dedicated to her stand in Varanasi, Baramulla and Mandi, and her worship remains alive among devotees.

Trivia

Mountain name. Her name means 'daughter of the mountain', alluding to her origin as the daughter of King Himavat.
Root chakra. In yoga she is associated with the root chakra, Muladhara, from which her spiritual ascent toward Shiva begins.
