healing goddess of Irish mythology

Airmed

Airmed is a goddess of Irish mythology, daughter of Dian Cecht and one of the Tuatha Dé Danann. She is remembered as a healer and as the keeper of knowledge about medicinal herbs.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Airmed and her brother Miach helped heal the wounded of the Second Battle of Magh Tuiredh. When Dian Cecht gave Nuada, king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, a silver hand to replace the one he had lost, Airmed and Miach made flesh grow over the silver, so the king regained full use of his hand.

Dian Cecht's jealousy led him to kill Miach. Airmed wept over her brother's grave, and from her tears sprang all the healing herbs of the world, 365 in number, matching the joints and veins of Miach's body. Airmed gathered them and laid them out on her cloak to sort them, but her father crept up behind her and scattered them. For this reason, the story goes, no living person knows all the secrets of herbalism; only Airmed remembers them.

Trivia

365 herbs. In the story, the herbs that sprang from Miach's grave matched his joints and veins. The ancient Irish believed that losing this knowledge greatly increased human suffering.
The well of Sláine. Airmed was one of the enchanters who, together with Dian Cecht, Ochtriullach and Miach, sang an incantation over the well of Sláine that could resurrect the dead.
