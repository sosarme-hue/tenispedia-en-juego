queen of the banshees and Irish goddess of love

Clíodhna

Clíodhna is a queen of the banshees in Irish mythology, linked to the Tuatha Dé Danann and, in some stories, a goddess of love and beauty and patron of County Cork. She matters because she ties the folklore of South Munster to local families and lineages.

The myth

According to tradition, Clíodhna had her palace among the rocks of Carrigcleena, near Mallow, and ruled over the sióga of South Munster. She was said to keep three brightly coloured birds that ate apples from an otherworldly tree, and whose song healed sickness.

In the tale preserved in the Acallam na Senórach, the young Ciabhan is rescued from the sea and reaches Tír Tairngire. There he meets Clíodhna, daughter of the druid Gebann, falls in love with her, and they sail away together. During a stop, Manannán's people pursue them and a great wave sweeps her away. The place became known as Tonn Chlíodhna, Clíodhna's Wave. Depending on the version, she either drowns or does not.

Worship and religion

Munster folk regarded her as a benefactor and told many legends about her. The sources do not describe specific rites or temples.

Legacy

The revolutionary Michael Collins knew her stories from the school at Rosscarbery he attended, and as a boy he went on Sunday trips to her rock.

Ancient sources: The Acallam na Senórach (Colloquy of the Ancients) and the Book of Lismore, which recounts Teigue's voyage to her island.

Trivia

Clíodhna's birds. Her three birds do more than sing: in the Voyage of Teigue they also guide the heroes across the sea.
