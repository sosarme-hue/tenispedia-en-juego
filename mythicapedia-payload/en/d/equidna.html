half-maiden, half-serpent creature of Greek mythology

Echidna

Echidna is a creature of Greek mythology, half nymph and half serpent, who lived underground. With Typhon she became the mother of many of the most famous monsters in Greek myth, which is why she is known today as the mother of monsters.

The myth

According to Hesiod, the first author to mention her, Echidna lived in a deep cave beneath a hollow rock, far from gods and mortals, in the land of the Arimoi. She was half a maiden with bright eyes and half a huge, speckled, bloodthirsty serpent. Her parentage varies from author to author: Hesiod does not clearly name her mother, who might be Ceto, Medusa or Callirhoe; Pherecydes makes her a daughter of Phorcys; Epimenides and Pausanias name the Oceanid Styx and a certain Peiras; Apollodorus gives Tartarus and Gaia; and the Orphic tradition makes her a daughter of Phanes.

With Typhon she bore a monstrous brood. Hesiod names Orthrus, Cerberus and the Lernaean Hydra; other sources add the Sphinx, the Nemean lion, the Chimera, Ladon, the Crommyonian Sow and Scylla. Herodotus records a story from the Pontus in which Heracles met a serpent-woman in a cave, who returned his horses in exchange for sleeping with her. Their three sons were Agathyrsus, Gelonus and Scythes, the last of whom became the eponymous founder of the Scythians.

Most of her offspring met their end at the hands of heroes: Oedipus defeated the Sphinx, Bellerophon the Chimera, Theseus the Crommyonian Sow, and Jason, with Medea's help, the dragon of Colchis. Apollodorus also reports that Argos Panoptes killed Echidna while she slept.

Worship and religion

The sources say little about worship. She was depicted alongside Typhon in a temple of Apollo at Amyclae, and there is no record of a cult of her own or of its survival.

Legacy

In modern times she is known as the mother of monsters. Her Greek name means viper, and she is linked to other creatures of similar nature, such as the dracaenae, the female dragons.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the earliest mention; she also appears in Apollodorus, Herodotus, Aristophanes, Pausanias and Nonnus.

Trivia

A hundred heads. Aristophanes gives her a hundred heads, matching the hundred heads Hesiod gives her mate Typhon.
A son of Heracles. Herodotus says the youngest of her sons with Heracles became the founder of the Scythians.
