ancestral she-wolf of the Turkic peoples

Asena

Asena (or Aşina) is a mythical she-wolf at the heart of the Turkic foundation story, regarded as the ancestress of the Göktürks and the Ashina clan. Her tale explains the origins of a dynasty of nomadic rulers and remains a powerful emblem of Turkish identity.

The myth

According to the myth, the story begins with a young boy, the only survivor of a tribe massacred in battle. A she-wolf finds him, saves him and carries him off to a cave in the mountains. In some versions he is an infant she nurses; in others he is a ten-year-old wounded boy she tends until he recovers.

When the boy grows up, he becomes her mate, and she falls pregnant. Pursued by his enemies, she crosses the Western Sea to a cave near the Qocho mountains and a city of the Tocharians, where she gives birth to ten half-wolf, half-human sons. One of them, Ashina, becomes their leader and founds the Ashina clan, which ruled over the Göktürks and other Turkic nomadic empires. Other accounts name the leader as Yizhi Nishidu. Tradition holds that these early Turks later moved to the Altai region, where they were known as skilled blacksmiths, much like the Scythians.

Legacy

With the rise of Turkish ethnic nationalism in the 1930s, Asena revived alongside Bozkurt, the 'gray wolf', and Ergenekon as a symbol of national pride. A relief showing her image decorated the stage of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk's personal theatre at his residence in Ankara. Today, a squadron of Turkish Air Force Boeing KC-135 Stratotanker aerial refuelling aircraft is nicknamed Asena.

Trivia

A squadron with her name. A Turkish Air Force tanker squadron flying Boeing KC-135 Stratotankers is nicknamed Asena.
The gray wolf. In Turkish, Bozkurt means 'gray wolf', a name used for the she-wolf and her symbolism in Turkish culture.
