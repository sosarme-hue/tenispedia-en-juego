queen of Tyre and mother of Cadmus and Europa in Greek myth

Telephassa

Telephassa is a figure of Greek myth, wife of Agenor and mother of Europa and Cadmus, among other children. Her story links Zeus's abduction of Europa with the founding of Thebes, which gives her a place in the family lineages of both Phoenician and Greek tales.

The myth

According to the Library of Apollodorus, Agenor settled in Phoenicia, where, married to Telephassa, he had Europa, Cadmus, Phoenix and Cilix. Her own parentage is unclear, as no one knows who her parents were. One day Zeus saw Europa gathering flowers, fell in love with her and carried her off in the form of a white bull. Agenor sent his sons to search for her, forbidding them to return without her. Unable to find her, they decided not to go home and settled in different regions.

Telephassa also set out with her children to look for Europa, but she eventually settled in Thrace with Cadmus, where she died of exhaustion and was buried by her son. Moschus, in his Europa, makes her a descendant of Libya and has her give her daughter a golden basket made by Hephaestus, a gift Poseidon had originally given to Libya. Scholia on Euripides call her Telephe and make her the daughter of Epimedusa, and another version has her as the wife of Phoenix. Modern scholars identify her with Argiope, a naiad daughter of the Nile.

Ancient sources: The Library of Apollodorus, Moschus's Europa and the scholia on Euripides mention her, with variants in her genealogy.
