Japanese deity of food and grain

Ukanomitama

Ukanomitama is a kami of Japanese mythology associated with food and agriculture, often identified with Inari, the deity of rice. Its name is understood as "august spirit of food".

The myth

In the Kojiki, Ukanomitama is the son of Susanoo and his second wife, Kamu-Ōichihime, a daughter of Ōyamatsumi, god of the mountains. The deity appears as the younger sibling of Ōtoshi-no-Kami, the god of the harvest.

The Nihon Shoki tells a different story: here the deity is born to Izanagi and Izanami when the two became hungry. The ancient texts do not state the deity's gender, but it has long been regarded as female, perhaps because of its links with other farming deities such as Toyouke and Ukemochi.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, which record different versions of its origin.

Trivia

An undefined gender. The ancient texts do not specify whether Ukanomitama is male or female. Its interpretation as a goddess came later.
Two different parents. According to the Kojiki it is the son of Susanoo, while the Nihon Shoki has it born to Izanagi and Izanami.
