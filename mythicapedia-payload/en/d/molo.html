Aetolian prince, son of Ares

Molus

Molus is a figure of Greek mythology, a member of the Aetolian royal family and son of the god Ares and the princess Demonice. His importance lies mainly in his place within the genealogy of the Molionides.

The myth

According to the tradition, Demonice was the daughter of King Agenor of Pleuron, and with Ares she had four sons: Evenus, Molus, Pylus and Thestius. Molus is the only one of them to whom a notable line of descendants is attributed, since he was the father of Moline, who had the Molionides with Actor of Elis.

The sources that record him are few. Apollodorus mentions him while discussing the descendants of Aeolus, son of Hellen, and his account is brief and genealogical, with no personal deeds recorded. For this reason, he functions mainly as a link between the lineage of Ares and the family of the Molionides.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus is the only ancient source that mentions him, in his Library, while discussing the descendants of Aeolus.

Trivia

A single source. Molus appears only in Apollodorus, who mentions him in passing while discussing the family of Aeolus.
One of four brothers. Together with Evenus, Pylus and Thestius, Molus belongs to a group of four brothers born to Demonice and Ares.
