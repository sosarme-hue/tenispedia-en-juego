satyr companion of Dionysus, turned into a vine

Ampelos

Ampelos is a young satyr, companion and beloved of Dionysus in Greek mythology, linked to the grapevine. According to the traditions, his story explains the origin of the vine and of wine, and his death is also told as the origin of a constellation.

The myth

According to Nonnus of Panopolis, Ampelos was the son of a satyr and a nymph, the same age as Dionysus, more beautiful than him, and his first love. The two lived among satyrs and sileni by the river Pactolus. Dionysus, who feared for his life, had been warned of his death: a horned dragon hurled a deer against an altar. Ate, sent by Hera, urged Ampelos to ride a bull to win the god's favour. As he rode, the youth mocked the moon, Selene, who sent a gadfly to sting the animal. The bull threw him, gored him and killed him.

Dionysus, grief-stricken, sprinkled the wound with ambrosia. Atropos, one of the Moirai, restored the body to life, and it took root and became the first grapevine. Dionysus squeezed its clusters and wine appeared. In another variant, the god made the wine from the youth's blood.

Ovid tells another version. On the Ismarus mountains in Thrace, Ampelos climbed an elm to pick grapes from a vine, lost his balance and died. Dionysus raised him to the stars as Vindemitor or Vindemiatrix, the Vintager, in the constellation of Virgo, whose rising marked the start of the grape harvest.

Legacy

His presence in ancient art is rare, but he appears beside Dionysus in the triumphal procession of some mosaics, such as the one at the Roman villa of Baños de Valdearados in Castile and León, and the Triumph of Bacchus mosaic in Zaragoza, both in Spain. His name survives in the star Vindemiatrix, in the constellation of Virgo.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis, in his Dionysiaca, and Ovid, who tells of Ampelos's death on the vine and his placement among the stars.

Trivia

A name meaning vine. Ampelos comes from ancient Greek and means grapevine, a name that sums up his link with the plant.
Mosaics in Spain. His figure appears in Roman mosaics found in Spain, such as those at Baños de Valdearados and Zaragoza, where he accompanies Dionysus in his triumph.
