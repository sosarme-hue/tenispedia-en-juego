Norse god, father of the social classes

Rig

Rig is a Norse deity, described as old, wise and mighty, who wanders the world and fathers the three classes of humankind. The prose introduction of the Rígsthula identifies him with Heimdall, and his name, related to the word for 'king', links him to the mythical origin of kingship.

The myth

In the Eddic poem Rígsthula, Rig walks along the shore and stops at three homes. In the first, that of Ái and Edda, a dark and ugly boy named Thræl is born, ancestor of the thralls. In the second, that of Afi and Amma, a ruddy-faced son named Karl is born, ancestor of the free farmers. In the third, that of Faðir and Móðir, a fair-haired and noble-looking boy named Jarl is born.

Rig returns to claim Jarl as his son, teaches him the runes and advises him to seek lordship. Jarl wins lands and marries Erna, with whom he has eleven sons. The youngest, Kon, masters magic, has the strength of eight men and also inherits the name Rig. The poem breaks off as a crow urges him to seek Dan and Danp. Other sources, such as Snorri Sturluson's Saga of the Ynglings, present Kon as the origin of the title konungr, 'king'.

Ancient sources: The Rígsþula, preserved in the Codex Wormianus after Snorri's Prose Edda, and the Völuspá, which calls the god a kinsman of mankind.

Trivia

A name with relatives. Ríg seems to derive from an oblique form of the Old Irish word rí, 'king', related to Latin rex and Sanskrit rajan.
A god who may not have been him. Some scholars believe the name Rígr was transferred to Heimdall from Odin, and that the poem may originally have referred to another god.
