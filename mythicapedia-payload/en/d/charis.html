Greek goddess of grace, wife of Hephaestus

Charis

Charis is a goddess in Greek mythology, the wife of Hephaestus and a member of the Charites, goddesses of charm, beauty and grace. Her name means "grace" in Greek.

The myth

In Homer's Iliad, Charis lives with Hephaestus in a bronze-built home on Mount Olympus. There she welcomes Thetis, who comes to ask the god to forge armour for her son Achilles.

Other traditions give the god a different wife. In the Odyssey, the bard Demodocus sings that Hephaestus was married to Aphrodite, whom he caught with Ares. Hesiod names Aglaea as the Charis married to Hephaestus, and some scholars think both names refer to the same goddess. Nonnus reconciles the versions by making Charis the jealous bride Hephaestus took after separating from Aphrodite. Lucian offers another explanation: in one of his dialogues, Hephaestus has both Aphrodite and Charis at once, one on Lemnos and one in heaven, without saying which is which.

Ancient sources: She appears in Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, in the works of Hesiod, in Lucian's Dialogues of the Gods and in Nonnus's Dionysiaca.

Trivia

One name, several goddesses. Charis can refer to any of the Charites in general, but in the sources it also appears as a specific goddess with her own name.
A rival of Aphrodite. In the Dionysiaca, Charis serves Aphrodite as a less loyal attendant than the others and is shown as jealous of the goddess.
