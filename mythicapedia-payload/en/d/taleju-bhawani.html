royal goddess of the Malla dynasty in Nepal

Taleju Bhawani

Taleju Bhawani is a goddess worshipped mainly in the Kathmandu Valley of Nepal, and the tutelary deity of the Malla dynasty. Her precise theological identity is uncertain, as tradition links her with Durga, Kalika and Tara.

The myth

According to 19th-century Nepalese chronicles, Taleju was originally the protective goddess of the Karnata dynasty of Mithila. The story goes that King Harisimhadeva, the last Karnata ruler, fled his capital after a Delhi Sultanate invasion, reached Bhaktapur as a refugee, eventually seized power in Nepal and established his goddess there.

The 17th-century Sanskrit texts Talejvāgama and Tulyāgama tell a similar story, but scholars see this link as a construction of the chronicles rather than a historical fact.

Worship and religion

Her cult is concentrated in Bhaktapur, where her ceremonies form an important part of Mohani, the Newar autumn festival. She is also closely linked to the kumari tradition, in which chosen young girls are venerated as living manifestations of the goddess, and her worship remains alive in many Newar towns.

Ancient sources: The 17th-century Sanskrit texts Talejvāgama and Tulyāgama are the oldest sources recounting her origin in Nepalese tradition.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. One proposed etymology links her name to the Newar word tale, meaning "high", a reference to her tall temple in Kathmandu.
A golden statue. In 1744, a Capuchin friar wrote that the statue of Bhavani of Batgao was the most esteemed in Nepal, because it was made of gold and was said to have descended from heaven.
