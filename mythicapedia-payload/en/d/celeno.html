Celaeno

Celaeno is one of the Pleiades, the daughters of the Titan Atlas, in Greek mythology. She is the least known of her sisters, and her name means "dark" in ancient Greek.

The myth

According to the tradition, Celaeno was the daughter of Atlas and the Oceanid Pleione, although some authors name Aethra as her mother. She caught the eye of the god Poseidon, who fathered a son with her, Lycus, whom the sea god himself settled on the Fortunate Isles.

The sources do not agree on her offspring. Hyginus gives her three children and confuses her with the nymph Clonia. Proclus credits her with Lycus and Chimaereus, fathered by Prometheus, and other texts add Eurypylus or Triton. In one version, Menelaus sought the bones of the heroes Lycus and Chimaereus to end a plague that was ravaging Sparta.

Legacy

Her name lives on in the sky: Celaeno is one of the stars in the open cluster of the Pleiades. The United States Navy named a cargo ship after her, the USS Celeno (AK-76), and in 1863 a wooden sailing ship called Celaeno was launched in Aberdeen, making eleven trips to New Zealand.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus, a scholium on the Argonautica and the Byzantine author John Tzetzes give differing accounts of her children and parentage.

Trivia

A star in the cluster. Celaeno gives its name to one of the visible stars in the open cluster of the Pleiades.
Bones against a plague. In one version, Menelaus searched for the bones of the heroes Lycus and Chimaereus to end an epidemic that struck Sparta.
