Spartan princess and queen of Arcadia in Greek myth

Leanira

Leanira, also known as Laodamia, is a Spartan princess in Greek mythology who became an Arcadian queen through marriage. She is best known as the wife of the eponymous hero Arcas.

The myth

Leanira was the daughter of King Amyclas and, possibly, of Diomede, daughter of Lapithes. Through her father she was considered the sister of Cynortes, Hyacinthus and others, and in some versions also of Daphne. She married Arcas, son of Callisto and Zeus. According to the mythographer Apollodorus, the couple had two children, Elatus and Apheidas. A scholion on Euripides adds Azan to the list, while Pausanias also mentions Triphylus.

After their father's death, the two sons divided Arcadia between them. The sources do not fully agree on the couple's offspring or on the identity of Arcas's wife, who in some versions appears as Meganira, daughter of Crocon, or as the nymph Chrysopeleia.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus records her in his Library, while Pausanias and a scholion on Euripides add further details about her children.

Trivia

Several identities. Her name appears as Leanira or Laodamia, and Arcas's wife may be given other names depending on the source.
A division of Arcadia. Her sons Elatus and Apheidas divided Arcadia between them after their father died.
