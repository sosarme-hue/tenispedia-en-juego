aspect of Shiva, conqueror of death in Hindu mythology

Kalantaka

Kalantaka, "ender of time", is a form of Shiva who defeats Yama, the god of death, to save his devotee Markandeya. He is a central figure in the Shaiva tradition of South India, where he is seen as a guarantor of liberation from death.

The myth

According to the tradition, the sage Mrikandu asked Shiva for a son, and the god offered him a choice between a virtuous son who would live sixteen years or a hundred foolish sons with long lives. Mrikandu chose the first, who was named Markandeya.

When he reached fifteen, Brahma taught him the Mahamrityunjaya Mantra, which granted him long life. Markandeya worshipped a lingam, Shiva's symbol, and Yama's messengers could not approach him. Yama came himself, and when he cast his noose, Shiva burst forth from the lingam in wrath, struck Yama with his trident and kicked his chest. Later, at the gods' request, Shiva revived Yama, who promised never to touch the god's devotees.

Worship and religion

In South India, Shiva as Kalantaka is mainly venerated at Thirukkadavoor, near Mayiladuthurai, and at a relief in the Chidambaram temple that receives special worship on Kartik Purnima. In Kerala he is also associated with the Kalasamharamurthy Temple at Triprangode.

Ancient sources: The Tamil Tevaram hymns of Sambandar and Appar praise Shiva as the conqueror of Death, and Agamic texts describe his iconography.
