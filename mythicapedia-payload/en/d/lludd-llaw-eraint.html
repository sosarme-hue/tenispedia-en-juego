Welsh legendary hero, “Lludd of the Silver Hand”

Lludd Llaw Eraint

Lludd Llaw Eraint, “Lludd of the Silver Hand”, is a legendary hero of Welsh mythology and the son of Beli Mawr. He matters because he may have inspired King Lud in Geoffrey of Monmouth’s History of the Kings of Britain.

The myth

In the Welsh tale of Lludd and Llefelys, preserved in the Mabinogion, Lludd rules Britain while his brother Llefelys rules Gaul. To rid Britain of three plagues, Lludd asks Llefelys for advice.

The first plague was the Coranians, dwarfs “from Asia” who may represent the Romans. The second was two dragons, one white and one red, who fought every May Day; the red dragon’s shriek withered people, animals and land alike. The dragons were captured with a pit dug at Oxford, the exact centre of the island, and buried at Dinas Emrys in Snowdonia.

The third plague was a giant thief who lulled the court to sleep with illusions and music and stole its food. Lludd defeated him in combat and, after granting him mercy, made him his loyal vassal.

Legacy

His name is linked to Ludgate, a gate in London near St Paul’s Cathedral, where a memorial to him may once have stood. He may have inspired Geoffrey of Monmouth’s King Lud, and in Welsh tradition he is the father of Gwyn ap Nudd.

Ancient sources: The Mabinogion, specifically the tale of Lludd and Llefelys, medieval Welsh texts.

Trivia

Silver hand. The epithet llaw ereint is usually translated as “of the silver hand”, though ereint has also been read as “silver cup”. Tolkien saw in the epithet an echo of the magic hand of Nodens.
Possible origin of Lud. Geoffrey of Monmouth recorded King Lud, a figure who may derive from Lludd. A Welsh hero thus ended up in medieval British history.
