Greek goddess of good order, one of the Horae

Eunomia

Eunomia is one of the Horae in Greek mythology, goddess of good order, law and legislation. Her name means 'good order' or 'good law', and she stands for a harmonious society that stays stable instead of sliding into chaos.

The myth

According to Hesiod, the earliest author to name her, Zeus took the bright Themis as his wife, and she bore the Horae: Eunomia, Dike (justice) and Eirene (peace). These sisters protected the crops of mortals and embodied social order. Other writers give different parentage: Alcman makes her a daughter of Promatheia, or Foresight, alongside Peitho (persuasion) and Tyche (fortune).

The Orphics linked her with the Graces, whom they considered daughters of Zeus and Eunomia. Pindar praises her in an ode as a saviour and a glorious daughter of Themis, sister of Dike and Eirene, who flourishes in victories beside the waters of Castalia and the Alpheus. On Athenian vases she appears among the companions of Aphrodite, representing the orderly, obedient conduct of women in marriage.

Worship and religion

Sources indicate that she was worshipped mainly in Athens, Argos and Olympia, cities where the Horae were honoured. They do not describe her temples or rites in detail, and her role seems more symbolic than tied to an independent cult.

Legacy

The asteroid 15 Eunomia and the Eunomia family of asteroids are named after her. The Greek term eunomia, 'good government', has also remained in political language to describe a stable legal order.

Ancient sources: Hesiod cites her as a daughter of Themis and Zeus; Alcman, Pindar, Solon, Theognis and Tyrtaeus also mention her, using eunomia in its political and social sense.

Trivia

An asteroid named for her. The asteroid 15 Eunomia, and a whole family of asteroids, take their name from the goddess.
Her opposite. Her mythological opposite is Dysnomia, a state without laws, sunk in social anarchy.
