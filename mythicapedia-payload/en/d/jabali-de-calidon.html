Calydonian Boar

The Calydonian Boar was a monstrous animal sent by Artemis to ravage the region of Calydon, in Aetolia. Its hunt gathered many of Greece's greatest heroes and became one of the points where many Greek myths meet, as well as a popular subject in classical art.

The myth

According to the tradition, King Oeneus of Calydon, in central-western Greece, forgot to include Artemis in his annual sacrifices. Offended, the goddess unleashed a huge and fierce boar that destroyed vineyards and crops and forced the people behind the city walls, where they began to starve.

Oeneus summoned the finest hunters of Greece, offering the beast's hide and tusks as a prize. Among those who came were Meleager, the king's son, several Argonauts and the huntress Atalanta. Some men, led by Cepheus and Ancaeus, refused to hunt alongside a woman, but Meleager won them over. Atalanta was the first to wound the boar with an arrow, and Meleager finished it off and gave her the trophy. Meleager's uncles, the sons of Thestius, took it from her, and Meleager killed them and returned the hide to Atalanta. Althaea, Meleager's mother, threw the brand that kept him alive into the fire, and he died, as the Fates had foretold. Thus Artemis's vengeance was complete.

During the hunt and afterwards, the hunters quarrelled over the spoils, and Peleus accidentally killed his host Eurytion.

Legacy

The boar's hide was kept in the Temple of Athena Alea at Tegea, in Arcadia; according to Pausanias, it was rotten and bristleless when he saw it. The hunt was a frequent theme in classical art, and the main pediment of that temple depicted it.

Ancient sources: Homer and Hesiod knew the myth but left no full version; fragments by Stesichorus, the Bibliotheca and Ovid's Metamorphoses preserve it.

Trivia
