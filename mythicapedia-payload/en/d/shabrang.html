Siyavash's horse in Persian mythology

Shabrang

Shabrang is the horse of the Persian hero Siyavash in the Shahnameh, Persia's great epic poem. His figure is tied to a trial of righteousness and to the hope of a just return.

The myth

According to the Shahnameh, Siyavash proved his innocence by riding Shabrang through a great fire. The hero emerged unharmed, a trial that confirmed his purity and rectitude before those who doubted him.

After Siyavash's death, his son Kay Khosrow came to rule Persia as one of the most admired and benevolent rulers in the poem. Zoroastrian tradition adds that Kay Khosrow vanished without dying. According to this belief, he will one day return riding Shabrang to bring justice.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Shahnameh, the Persian epic by Ferdowsi, which narrates the ordeal by fire and the fate of Kay Khosrow.
