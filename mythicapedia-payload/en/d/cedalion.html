servant of Hephaestus on Lemnos

Cedalion

Cedalion is a servant of Hephaestus on the island of Lemnos, best known for his role in the healing of Orion. He belongs to Greek mythology, and his story is notable for the uncertainty surrounding both his name and his part in the tales.

The myth

According to one tradition, Cedalion was the tutor of Hephaestus. Hera entrusted her son to the island of Naxos so that he could learn the craft of smithing, and Cedalion was the one who taught him.

The more common story places him beside the hunter Orion, who came to Lemnos after being blinded by Oenopion. Orion lifted Cedalion onto his shoulders so that the youth could guide him east, where the rays of Helios restored his sight.

Legacy

Sophocles wrote a satyr play titled Cedalion, of which only a few words survive. Its plot is uncertain and may have dealt with Orion's blinding or with the recovery of his sight on Lemnos.

Ancient sources: Sophocles, in his lost satyr play Cedalion.

Trivia

A disputed name. One traditional etymology links it to a verb meaning to take charge or to care for. Since Wilamowitz, many scholars prefer a different reading, connected to phallos and to the verb meaning to marry.
A dwarf in a relief. Wilamowitz suggested that Cedalion is the dwarf in a Louvre relief showing Dionysus in Hephaestus's workshop.
