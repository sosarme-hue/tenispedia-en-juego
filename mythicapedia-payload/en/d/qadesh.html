Egyptian goddess of Canaanite origin

Qetesh

Qetesh, also spelled Qadesh, was a goddess incorporated into the Egyptian religion in the late Bronze Age. Her name probably derives from the Semitic root Q-D-Š, meaning 'holy' or 'blessed', and her figure reflects the exchange of cults between Egypt, Syria and Canaan.

The myth

Egyptian texts say little about Qetesh, so her mythology is hard to reconstruct. Older accounts described her as a goddess of love and sexual pleasure, even as a 'sacred harlot', but modern scholars regard these views as obsolete for lack of evidence, and do not link her with fertility or sex.

Her epithets point to an astral and protective character: she was called 'Mistress of All the Gods', 'Lady of the Stars of Heaven' and 'Eye of Ra, without her equal'. She bore the title 'Beloved of Ptah', and she was sometimes identified with Hathor, through her wig and horns, and with Sekhmet, through her link to Ptah. She was the consort of Min.

In her iconography she appears frontal, nude or in a light dress, standing on a lion and holding a snake in one hand and lotus or papyrus flowers in the other. This frontal pose, rare in Egyptian art, is often read as a sign of her foreign origin.

Worship and religion

She was venerated mainly in workers' villages and among the artisans of the royal necropolises. According to the tradition of her cult, it was introduced to the Memphis area by Asian prisoners who sought protection against venomous animals. She hardly appears in the royal cult, which suggests that ordinary people were her main devotees.

Legacy

Her name has reached modern popular culture. Qetesh is the name of the Goa'uld who possesses Vala Mal Doran in the science fiction series Stargate SG-1, and it is also used for the soul-stealing humanoid species in the episode 'Goodbye, Sarah Jane Smith' of The Sarah Jane Adventures.

Trivia

A proposal from 1939. In 1939 the scholar William F. Albright proposed that Qetesh was a form of the 'lady of Byblos', a Canaanite goddess. Later, René Dussard linked her to Asherah.
A name from science fiction. The name was given to a Goa'uld villain in Stargate SG-1 who possesses a recurring character, giving the goddess unexpected fame outside Egyptology.
