floating island of the king of the winds

Aeolia

Floating island of Greek myth, home of Aeolus, ruler of the winds. It appears in Homer's Odyssey, and later writers linked it to the Aeolian Islands off northern Sicily.

The myth

In Homer's account, Odysseus reaches Aeolia after visiting the land of the lotus-eaters. Aeolus gives him control of the winds: he seals them in an ox-hide bag and fastens it to the ship's mast with a silver thread. With a gentle west wind they sail for nine days and, sighting land on the tenth, his jealous companions believe the bag holds gold and open it. The winds burst out and drive the fleet back to the island. Aeolus refuses to help again and sends Odysseus away under a dark omen.

Worship and religion

Excavations on Lipari confirm a cult of Aeolus. In 1964, a votive deposit near the cathedral on the acropolis yielded a jug inscribed with a dedication to him, which allows scholars to identify the deity with certainty.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey (Book X), which describes it as the floating island of King Aeolus.

Trivia

Stromboli, Aeolus' island. The geographer Strabo reports that Strongyle, today's Stromboli and one of the Lipari Islands, was said to be the island of Aeolus.
A find that confirms the site. A votive deposit found in 1964 on Lipari holds a jug dedicated to Aeolus, so archaeologists consider the site of his cult confirmed.
