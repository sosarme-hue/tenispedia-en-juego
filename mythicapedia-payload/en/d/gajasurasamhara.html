fierce aspect of the Hindu god Shiva, slayer of the elephant demon

Gajasurasamhara

Gajasurasamhara is a fierce aspect of the Hindu god Shiva, depicted as the destroyer of the elephant demon Gajasura. The image, very popular in Pallava and Chola art, shows the god dancing wrapped in the hide of the slain elephant.

The myth

In the Kurma Purana, an asura took the form of an elephant and terrorized Brahmins who were worshipping the lingam of Krittivasvara in Varanasi. Shiva emerged from the lingam, killed the asura, stripped off its skin and thereafter wore the hide over his upper body.

Another version, in the Varaha Purana, places the story in the Deodar Forest (Darukavana), where Shiva arrives as a young naked mendicant accompanied by the enchantress Mohini. The sages fall for her and, once they recover their senses, perform a black magic sacrifice that produces the elephant demon, which Shiva slays and flays. Some traditions also place the event at Valuvur.

Worship and religion

The chief temple of Gajasurasamhara is at Valuvur in Tamil Nadu, where a bronze image with eight arms is the main icon. Valuvur is one of the Atta-virattam sites, the eight places of Shiva's heroic deeds.

Legacy

Scholar Pal describes this furious dancing image as the most dynamic of all Shaiva themes created by South Indian sculptors.

Ancient sources: The Tevaram hymns refer to Shiva as the one who wears the elephant hide, and the Shiva Sahasranama calls him Gajaha, the slayer of the elephant.
