witch of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology

Fuamnach

Fuamnach is a witch of the Tuatha Dé Danann and the first wife of Midir in Irish mythology. Her story, told in the medieval Tochmarc Étaíne ("The Wooing of Étaín"), is that of a jealous rival whose spells set off a long chain of transformations.

The myth

When Midir falls in love with Étaín and marries her, Fuamnach, consumed by jealousy, casts several spells as soon as the couple reaches Brí Léith. First she strikes her rival with a wand of scarlet rowan and turns her into a pool of water; then into a worm and finally into a beautiful scarlet fly, which returns to Midir's side.

Later, during a visit by Midir, she announces that she will drive Étaín away from him. Using a spell she learned from her foster-father, Bresal Etarlám, she raises a mighty wind that carries Étaín through the air for seven years until she reaches the Brug, where Óengus takes her in and keeps her in a protected bower. Fuamnach sends another wind to drive her across the sky for seven more years, until Étaín falls into the cup of a woman, is reborn as that woman's daughter and eventually settles in Ulster.

Fuamnach dies not long afterwards. Óengus follows her trail to the house of Bresal and cuts off her head, which he carries back to the Brug as a trophy.

Ancient sources: She appears in the medieval Irish tale Tochmarc Étaíne, which describes her as a witch versed in the knowledge and power of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Trivia

Possible origin of the name. The name may derive from fúaimm, "noise, sound". A dindsenchas poem on Nás mentions the lamentations of the fair-skinned, vocal women of Fáil, using a related adjective.
A similar name. A woman named Fúaimnech, daughter of Conn Cétchathach, appears in the legal tract Mellbretha, showing that a form of the name was also used for people.
