magic mill of Norse mythology

Grótti

Grótti is a magic mill in Norse mythology that grinds out whatever wealth its owner wishes. Its story is tied to the Danish king Fróði and the giantesses Fenja and Menja, and it appears in the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda.

The myth

According to the tradition, King Fróði of legendary Denmark bought two strong enslaved women, Fenja and Menja, in Sweden. He owned a pair of magic millstones called Grótti, which granted whatever its grinder asked for. Fróði forced the young women to grind gold, peace and happiness for him, giving them no rest and no sleep.

The Grottasǫngr, or "Song of Grótti", tells how the women, instead of giving in, sang and ground even harder, and from their song an army arose led by the sea-king Mysing. He attacked Fróði in the night, killed him and seized the booty, ending the peace of Fróði. Mysing took the mill and the women and ordered them to grind salt. When the ship sank, the sea began to turn around the centre of the stone, forming a whirlpool, and the water became salt.

Legacy

The poem inspired Viktor Rydberg's Den nya Grottesången, a critique of harsh factory conditions in the nineteenth century. Johannes V. Jensen included it in his novel The Fall of the King, and the character of the Strength and Patience of the Hill in Ann Leckie's The Raven Tower draws on it.

Ancient sources: The Grottasǫngr in the Poetic Edda, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which tells the story of the mill and the whirlpool.

Trivia

Scottish whirlpool. In Orcadian and Shetlandic folklore, the witches Grotti Finnie and Grotti Minnie create a whirlpool in the Pentland Firth called the Swelkie.
Name of the whirlpool. The word maelstrom comes from mal, "mill", and ström, "stream".
