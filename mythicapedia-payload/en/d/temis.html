Greek Titaness of divine order and justice

Themis

Themis is a Titaness in Greek mythology, the personification of divine order, fairness, natural law and custom. Regarded as a pre-Olympian deity, she is associated with the scales of justice and with her role as adviser to the gods and with the Oracle of Delphi.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Themis was a daughter of Uranus and Gaia and one of the twelve Titans. With Zeus she became the mother of the Horae (Eunomia, Dike and Eirene) and, in other versions, of the Moirai, though a fragment of Pindar has her present at the wedding and brought from the springs of Oceanus. Hyginus, by contrast, makes her a daughter of Aether and Earth and does not count her among the Titans.

Tradition gives her many episodes. She warned the gods of the civil war coming to Thebes and warned Zeus and Poseidon that Thetis's son would be greater than his father. She advised Deucalion and Pyrrha after the flood and was present at the birth of Apollo, nursing him with nectar and ambrosia. In the Iliad she summons the gods to Zeus's assemblies, and it was she who offered the first cup to Hera when Hera returned distressed to Olympus. When order was ignored, Nemesis brought punishment. Aeschylus makes Prometheus her son, while other traditions name Iapetus as his father.

Themis was also linked to the Oracle at Delphi. Some accounts say she built it or received it from Gaia, then passed it to Phoebe, grandmother of Apollo. Hesiod presents her as the mother of Dike, personified justice, who carried out the decisions of the Fates alongside her mother.

Worship and religion

Themis had several temples in Greece, including one at Tanagra, another near the Acropolis in Athens, and one at Rhamnous shared with Nemesis. At Thebes an account describes a sanctuary with a white marble image beside one of the Fates, and at Olympia there was an altar. She was also honoured within the shrines of other gods, such as Asclepius at Epidaurus, where she shared space with Aphrodite. Judges were called themistopoloi, servants of Themis. There is no reliable evidence of a cult that survives today.

Legacy

In Rome, the personification of justice was Iustitia, shown blindfolded with scales and a cornucopia. Her origins are more civic than mythological, so comparing the two is only partly apt. The scales we associate with Themis remain a common symbol of justice in Western culture. The historian Moses Finley stressed that the Greek word themis denoted custom and social order, the force of 'it is done, or it is not done'.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the first record of Themis as a personification of justice; she also appears in the Iliad, the Hymn to Apollo, and in works by Pindar, Aeschylus, Euripides, Ovid and Hyginus.

Trivia

A name with meaning. The name Themis comes from the Greek verb títhēmi, 'to put', so it refers to what is established rather than to human authority.
Themis and Nemesis. When Themis was ignored, Nemesis brought punishment. The two shared a small temple at Rhamnous.
