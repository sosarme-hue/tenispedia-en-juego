Greek hero, king of Phthia and father of Achilles

Peleus

Peleus is a hero of Greek mythology, king of Phthia in Thessaly, best known as the father of Achilles, the great warrior of the Iliad. His story, already familiar to the audience of Homer, combines murder, exile, purification and a marriage to a sea goddess. According to myth, Peleus was the son of Aeacus, king of Aegina, and Endeis. After he and his brother Telamon accidentally killed their half-brother Phocus, he was expelled from the island and reached Phthia, where Eurytion purified him and gave him his daughter Antigone. Later, during the hunt for the Calydonian boar, he accidentally killed Eurytion and fled to Iolcus, where Acastus purified him. In Iolcus, Acastus's wife Astydamia fell in love with him and, after being rejected, falsely accused him before her husband. Acastus abandoned him asleep on Mount Pelion, hid his sword and left him to the centaurs. Chiron rescued him and returned the weapon. Peleus also sailed with the Argonauts, where he rallied the crew and solved the riddle that let them escape the sands of Libya. His most famous moment was his marriage to the sea-nymph Thetis. Zeus and Poseidon had both courted her, but a prophecy warned that her son would be stronger than his father, and they withdrew. To win her, Peleus held her fast while she changed into fire, water and animals, following Chiron's advice. Their son was Achilles. Thetis tried to make him immortal, but Peleus interrupted her and she left him, leaving his heel vulnerable.

The myth

According to myth, Peleus was the son of Aeacus, king of Aegina, and Endeis. After he and his brother Telamon accidentally killed their half-brother Phocus, he was expelled from the island and reached Phthia, where Eurytion purified him and gave him his daughter Antigone. Later, during the hunt for the Calydonian boar, he accidentally killed Eurytion and fled to Iolcus, where Acastus purified him.

In Iolcus, Acastus's wife Astydamia fell in love with him and, after being rejected, falsely accused him before her husband. Acastus abandoned him asleep on Mount Pelion, hid his sword and left him to the centaurs. Chiron rescued him and returned the weapon.

His marriage to the sea-nymph Thetis was the most famous episode. Zeus and Poseidon had both courted her, but a prophecy warned that her son would be stronger than his father. To win her, Peleus held her fast while she changed into fire, water and animals. The wedding took place on Mount Pelion with the gods as guests, and their son was Achilles.

Ancient sources: Homer and Hesiod mention him, and Euripides makes him a character in his tragedy Andromache.
