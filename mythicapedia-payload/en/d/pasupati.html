Pashupati

Pashupati, meaning "lord of beasts", was an epithet of the god Rudra in the Vedic period that later came to designate a benign form of the Hindu god Shiva. He is especially revered in Nepal, where he is unofficially regarded as a national deity.

The myth

In the Vedic texts, Pashupati was originally an epithet of Rudra, whom the Atharvaveda describes as lord of bipeds and quadrupeds, including creatures of the earth, forests, waters and skies. His lordship over cattle and beasts had a double character: he struck down animals that angered him, but also blessed with health and prosperity those who honoured him.

The best-known story comes from the Nepala Mahatmya, found in regional versions of the Skanda Purana. Shiva, accompanied by Parvati, went to the Śleṣmātaka forest on the banks of the Bagmati river in the form of a deer, while she took the form of a doe. Brahma, Vishnu and Indra searched the three worlds for him and finally found him in the Himalayas, with one horn, three eyes and surrounded by deer. When they tried to bring him back to his divine form, they grasped his horn, which broke into four pieces. Shiva promised to remain in the forest forever in that form, to be known as Pashupati, and ordered the four fragments to be consecrated as lingams across the region. According to the story, those who worship him in this aspect will never be reborn as animals.

Worship and religion

The Pashupatinath Temple, on the bank of the Bagmati river, is the most sacred place in Nepal and one of the great pilgrimage centres of Hinduism. In India, another Pashupatinath temple stands on the banks of the Shivana river in Mandsaur, where its main attraction is a rare lingam of Shiva with eight faces. Worship continues today.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda and the Atharvaveda mention Rudra and his epithets, and the Mahabharata treats Shiva as a manifestation of Rudra.

Trivia

Five faces. The five faces of Pashupati represent forms of Shiva and are associated with the five elements: earth, water, air, fire and ether.
An Indus Valley seal. Archaeologist John Marshall named a seal from Mohenjo-Daro the Pashupati seal, proposing that it showed Shiva surrounded by animals, although this identification remains unconfirmed.
