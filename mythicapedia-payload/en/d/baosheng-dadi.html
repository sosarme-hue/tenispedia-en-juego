Chinese god of medicine

Baosheng Dadi

Baosheng Dadi, also called Poh Seng Tai Tay or Taitokong, is the god of medicine worshipped by the Hoklo people of Fujian, in Taiwan, and by Hokkien communities across Southeast Asia. He originates from a Song dynasty doctor whom popular religion later deified.

The myth

According to legend, Gô͘ Tho was born in 979 into a poor fishing family and, after losing his parents, learned medicine and alchemy. His feats include healing a tiger, which then carried him on its back in gratitude, and bringing a boy back to life with a willow branch. Late in life he treated a young woman who had become pregnant by a dragon, killed her dragon offspring and, in 1036, rose to heaven riding a crane.

A less orthodox version makes him the husband of Ma Cho Po in heaven, with whom he quarrelled; their disputes explain the wind and rain on his birthday.

Worship and religion

His oldest temples stood in Pehta, Chheta and Amoy, and in 1166 he received the title Tsz Tse. His cult reached Taiwan with migrants from Tangwa, and his birthday is marked each year with the Baosheng Cultural Festival in Taipei, from mid-April to mid-June.

Legacy

He remains present in popular proverbs and in many temples in Taiwan and Malaysia, several with tiger figures.

Trivia

The tiger mount. In a popular legend, Gô͘ Tho healed a tiger with a bone stuck in its throat, and the grateful animal let him ride it. For this reason many of his temples feature tiger figures.
Weather proverbs. The dispute with Ma Cho Po gave rise to proverbs linking windy days to Lord Taito and rainy days to Lady Ma Cho.
