legendary lake creature of Argentine Patagonia

Nahuelito

Nahuelito is a supposed aquatic creature that, according to popular belief, lives in Nahuel Huapi Lake in Argentine Patagonia. It is the local counterpart of the Loch Ness Monster and one of the best-known legends in Argentine cryptozoology.

The myth

According to popular tradition, the monster lives in the lake's waters and emerges at night. The legend is believed to have roots in indigenous stories told before the colonization of the Americas, which described aquatic monsters in many Patagonian lakes. Local residents also speak of a creature called «el sueiro», said to have a body the size of a cow and footprints like those of a giant duck. Some think it is a local mestizo reworking of the Mapuche myth of the «cuero», a monster with no head or legs.

The modern case began in 1910, when George Garret, who worked for a company near the lake, saw a creature about 400 metres away, though he did not make it public until 1922. That year, Dr. Clemente Onelli, director of the Buenos Aires Zoo, organized an expedition with hunters and dynamite that found nothing. Soon afterwards came the news that «the plesiosaur had been captured», but it was a prank by Primo Capraro, who had paraded a float shaped like a plesiosaur during carnival.

Theories in favour of its existence include a surviving marine reptile, although the Patagonian lakes formed long after the dinosaurs died out. Against it, many attribute the sightings to drifting logs.

Legacy

The lake's name gives the creature its name, just as with Nessie. The legend appears in classic books and articles on cryptozoology. In 1960, the Argentine Navy spent 18 days chasing an unidentified underwater object that some people linked to the creature.

Trivia

Plesiosaur's Lagoon. A small lake where the creature's presence was claimed is today known as Laguna del Plesiosaurio, or Plesiosaur's Lagoon.
A name the Spanish misread. The Mapuche word nahuel means yaguareté, but the Spanish translated it as «tiger», confusing the two animals.
