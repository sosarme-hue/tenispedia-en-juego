Hindu king of generosity and virtue

Shibi

Shibi is a king in Hindu mythology, son of Ushinara of the lunar dynasty, famed for his extreme generosity. His story also appears in Buddhist Jataka tales.

The myth

In the Mahabharata, King Suhotra of the Kuru line meets Shibi while both are returning from visiting great sages. Neither will yield the road with his chariot, until the sage Narada points out that both are magnanimous and one must give way. Suhotra recognises Shibi's virtue and steps aside.

Later, the gods decide to test his reputation. Agni, in the form of a dove, seeks shelter in his lap, pursued by a kite that is Indra. The kite demands the dove's flesh, and Shibi offers his own, but the dove weighs more. He then sits on the scale himself. The gods reveal their identities and promise him a son, Kapotaroma.

Worship and religion

According to its sthala purana, the Pundarikakshan Perumal Temple is said to have been built by this monarch. In Pakistan, the town of Sehwan, formerly called Shibistan, is traditionally said to take its name from him.

Legacy

His story was painted in the Buddhist cave sites of Ajanta and Mogao, where it appears in murals and paintings.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata recounts his meeting with Suhotra and the episode of the dove and the kite.

Trivia

Buddhist murals. His story is depicted in the caves of Ajanta and in those of Mogao.
A name in Pakistan. The town of Sehwan, formerly Shibistan, is traditionally associated with his name.
