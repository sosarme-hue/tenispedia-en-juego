Greek muse of epic poetry

Calliope

Calliope is the Muse of epic poetry and, more broadly, of eloquence in Greek mythology. Daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, she is considered the eldest of the nine Muses and, according to Hesiod, the most important, since she attends venerable kings.

The myth

Tradition holds that Calliope invented song and presides over the reason of the ruler. She crowns poets with laurel and grants wisdom in general. She is usually shown as a dignified young woman, with a golden crown or garlands, a trumpet in one hand and a scroll, tablet or book in the other.

The stories give her several children. With Oeagrus, or according to other versions with Apollo, she bore Linus, killed by Heracles, and Orpheus, the lyre player whose singing moved stones and trees. She married Oeagrus at Pimpleia, a town near Mount Olympus. Other traditions make her the mother of the Corybantes by Zeus, and of Rhesus by the river-god Strymon; Rhesus was later killed by Diomedes at Troy. With the river-god Achelous she is said to have been the mother of the Sirens.

A famous episode tells of a singing contest with the daughters of Pierus. Calliope won and, to punish their arrogance, turned them into magpies. On another occasion Zeus asked her to settle the dispute between Aphrodite and Persephone over Adonis, and she ruled that he spend half the year with each. Aphrodite's anger led the women of Thrace to tear Orpheus, Calliope's son, to pieces.

Legacy

Virgil invokes her at the opening of the Aeneid to ask for help with his martial song. Dante mentions her in the Divine Comedy. Some ancient writers believed she was Homer's muse, or even his mother or ancestor. Artists often depict her with her children. Her name lives on in place names: a beach in Antarctica, a hummingbird of North and Central America, a steam organ, and a town in Queensland.

Ancient sources: Hesiod calls her the most important of the Muses, and Ovid calls her the chief of all the Muses; Strabo and Plutarch record other genealogies.

Trivia

Name of a hummingbird. The calliope hummingbird of North and Central America takes its name from the Muse.
