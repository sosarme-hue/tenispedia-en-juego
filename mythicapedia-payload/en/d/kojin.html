Japanese kami of fire and the hearth

Kōjin

Kōjin is the kami of fire, the hearth and the kitchen, also called Kamado-gami, "god of the stove". In Japanese tradition he embodies violent forces turned toward the good of people.

The myth

According to myth, fire is a destructive force, as shown by Kagu-tsuchi, the original fire deity, whose birth caused his mother's death. Kōjin embodies that fire tamed and put to good use: he destroys all impurity and watches over the household, reporting the misdeeds he sees to the village kami. These reports are judged at an assembly of gods in Izumo province, in the tenth month of the lunar calendar, which decides rewards or punishments.

Worship and religion

A representation is usually placed near the hearth: in many homes a simple fuda, or memorial tablet, is enough, while Buddhist temples may have a statue showing him with flaming hair, fangs, a bow and arrows, two pairs of arms and sometimes three heads. His worship is believed to come from Shugendō practitioners between the late Heian and early Kamakura periods.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki mentions an imperial document with instructions for worshipping him as Kamado-gami.

Trivia

Unusual images. As Kamado-gami he is sometimes depicted as a woman, and some representations give him three heads.
Link to Fudō Myō-Ō. He is sometimes identified as an incarnation of Fudō Myō-Ō, who is also surrounded by flames and tasked with punishing misdeeds.
