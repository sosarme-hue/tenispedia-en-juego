Egyptian solar goddess, wife of Sopdu

Khensit

Khensit is an Egyptian goddess linked to the sun, wife of Sopdu and, according to tradition, a daughter of Ra. Her name means "placenta", and she was associated with protection and with reuniting the deceased with their family.

The myth

The Pyramid Texts mention her as one of Ra's many daughters, a solar goddess connected with royal beads and diadems. One of her titles, "mistress of crowns" (nebet khau), reflects that royal link.

For a time it was thought that her name also designated certain hairstyles or ornaments, based on phrases such as "Khensit who is upon the head of Ra". Some writings present her as responsible for reuniting the deceased with their loved ones. By syncretism she is related to Hathor, just as her husband is linked to Horus.

Worship and religion

She was the patron goddess of the twentieth nome of Lower Egypt, centred on Saft el-Hinna, where she was worshipped as a divine pair with Sopdu. She was depicted wearing the solar disc between horns, the feather of Maat, or as an uraeus.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, which name her as a daughter of Ra and a solar goddess.
