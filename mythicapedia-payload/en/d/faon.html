mythical ferryman of Lesbos favoured by Aphrodite

Phaon

Phaon is a ferryman in Greek myth who, according to the story, received youth and beauty from Aphrodite after carrying her across the water without charge. His tale, preserved mainly in late sources and lost comedies, made him a model of male attractiveness and linked him with the poet Sappho.

The myth

According to tradition, Phaon was an elderly ferryman of plain appearance, yet the people of Lesbos respected his honest character. He earned his living carrying passengers to the Anatolian mainland, charged a fair fare and took nothing from those who could not pay. One day Aphrodite approached him disguised as an old woman and asked to be taken across. Phaon treated her courteously and asked for no payment. In gratitude, the goddess gave him an alabaster box of ointment; once he applied it, he regained his youth and became so handsome that many women of Mytilene fell in love with him.

Other versions place the episode on Chios and describe many women pursuing him. One of them threw herself from the Leucadian Rock when she could not win him. Some traditions also credit him with founding Aphrodite's temple on Leucas. According to Aelian, he was killed for adultery.

The best-known story links him with Sappho, whose unrequited love for him supposedly drove her to leap from the Leucadian Rock. Many scholars suspect the tale arose from confusion with another woman named Sappho, or from a misreading of her poems.

Worship and religion

Sources credit him with helping to establish Aphrodite's temple on Leucas, but they do not describe a cult devoted to him or say whether that tradition survived.

Legacy

Attic comedies of the fifth century BC, now lost, staged him. Plautus mentions him in Miles Gloriosus as one of only two men in the world so passionately loved by a woman. Ovid dramatised Sappho's love for him in his Heroides. In the Suda, his name came to describe someone desirable yet arrogant.

Ancient sources: Cratinus and the Attic comedies, Lucian, Aelian, Plautus, Ovid's Heroides and the Suda.

Trivia

Lettuce or barley. According to the comic poet Cratinus, Aphrodite hid Phaon among lettuces so she could keep him for herself. Marsyas of Philippi, however, said it was unripe barley.
A name for the arrogant. The Suda records that the name Phaon came to be used for someone who is desirable but haughty.
