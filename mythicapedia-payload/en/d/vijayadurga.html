Hindu goddess of Goa, a form of Durga

Vijayadurga

Vijayadurga is a Hindu goddess from Goa, India, regarded as a form of Durga. She is especially venerated as a family deity (kuldevi) by Saraswat and Karhade Brahmins.

The myth

According to local tradition, the goddess, on her way to the sea, found the demon Kalantakasura harassing the Saraswat Brahmins of Sancoale and disrupting their rituals. Taking the form of Mahishasurmardhini, mounted on an elephant, she defeated him in a fierce battle. The people hailed her as Vijaya, the victorious one, and a temple was built in her honour.

Some accounts instead link her to a battle between Shiva and Vishnu, while others present her as a fierce form of Shantadurga.

Worship and religion

Her original temple stood in Sancoale, but in 1567, during the Portuguese persecution, it was destroyed and the image was moved first to Agapur and then to Kerim, where she is worshipped today. For Karhade Brahmins she is the sole object of their prayers and rituals.

Legacy

Devotees affectionately call her Vaijari, a colloquial name that is part of the religious speech of the Konkani Saraswats.

Trivia

Restricted access. Her temple recently banned foreigners from entering, citing their clothing and conduct as objectionable.
Family nicknames. The Saraswats give their goddesses affectionate nicknames: Shantadurga is Sateri across Goa, and Vijayadurga is Vaijari.
