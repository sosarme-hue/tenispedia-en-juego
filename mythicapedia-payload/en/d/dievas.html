supreme god of Baltic mythology

Dievas

Dievas is the supreme god of Baltic mythology, honoured in Lithuanian and Latvian tradition as the god of light, the sky and the origin of the world. His name comes from the same Indo-European root as the sky father Dyēus, which links him to other ancient traditions.

The myth

According to the traditions of Lithuania and Latvia, Dievas and his twin brother Velns, the trickster, created the universe. The two fought on a rock in the ocean, which became the centre of the world. Dievas also appears as Praamžius, god of time, peace and friendship.

The myths almost always show him in human form, as an old wise man or an old beggar who comes down from the heavens to test people's goodness and generosity. However, the Lithuanian name for southernwood, diemedis ('God-tree'), and some legends suggest he may also have taken the shape of animals, birds or plants.

Worship and religion

The sources say little about his worship. Some scholars think Dievas was a distant god, a deus otiosus, so he received little direct veneration and had no sacred places of his own. Texts often avoid naming him and use respectful epithets instead, such as Aukščiausiasis ('the Highest') or Visagalis ('the Omnipotent'). Today, in Lithuanian and Latvian, the word is also used for the God of the major world religions.

Legacy

The name survives in language: in Lithuanian and Latvian, dievas and dievs are used for the Christian God and, in modern Lithuanian, for any deity. Latvian folk songs preserve the phrase Saule noiet dievā ('the sun sets toward God'), which keeps the original celestial sense. The same root gives deva in Hinduism.

Trivia

Heavenly Twins. According to some traditions, Dievas had two sons known as the Heavenly Twins.
A god left unnamed. Lithuanian texts often avoid saying his name and prefer epithets such as Praamžis, the eternal one.
