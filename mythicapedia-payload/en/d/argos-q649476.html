king and eponymous hero of Argos in Greek mythology

Argus

Argus is the legendary king who gave his name to the region of Argos in the Peloponnese. The son of Zeus and Niobe, tradition makes him heir to his maternal grandfather Phoroneus and, according to some authors, successor of Apis. His myth has no major epic of its own; it survives as a web of genealogies and scattered mentions in several ancient writers.

The myth

According to tradition, Argus was a son of Zeus and Niobe, daughter of Phoroneus, and may have been the brother of Pelasgus. He inherited his grandfather's power over the Peloponnese and gave his name to the kingdom. A scholiast on Homer calls him the son and successor of Apis, a version also followed by Jerome and Eusebius, who cite the now-lost history of Castor of Rhodes and give his reign as 70 years.

The genealogies vary from author to author. Apollodorus and Hyginus say he married Evadne, daughter of the river god Strymon and Neaera, and had Ecbasus, Peiranthus, Epidaurus and Criasus, who inherited the kingdom. Hesiod names Epidaurus as a son, and a scholiast on Euripides gives his wife as the Oceanid Peitho. Pausanias adds Peirasus and Phorbas. Apollodorus and Cercops also speak of Argus Panoptes, son of Argus and Ismene, daughter of Asopus.

Worship and religion

The tomb of Argus in the city of Argos was still shown in the time of Pausanias. He also describes a grove sacred to Argus in Lacedaemon, where some Argive soldiers took refuge after being defeated by Cleomenes I and were burned to death there.

Ancient sources: Homer (via a scholion), Hesiod, Euripides, Apollodorus, Hyginus, Pausanias, and Jerome and Eusebius, citing Castor of Rhodes.
