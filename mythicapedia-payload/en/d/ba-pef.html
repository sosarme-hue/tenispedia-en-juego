minor underworld god of ancient Egypt

Ba-Pef

Ba-Pef is a minor underworld deity in Egyptian mythology, known mainly for his obscure, malevolent character and for the unusual role of his priestesses. His name is linked to the ba, one of the parts of the person that the Egyptians distinguished, associated with personality and with the ability to move between the world of the living and that of the dead.

The myth

The surviving sources say very little about him, so it is hard to reconstruct his story. He is usually described as a little-known and malevolent deity, one of those who appear in the underworld without taking part in extended tales.

In Egyptian thought, the ba was a manifestation of the individual that could leave the tomb by day and return to it at night, so the afterlife was a realm of mixed forces, some benevolent and some dangerous. Ba-Pef would fit into that landscape as a troubling power, though without a fixed place in the major pantheon.

Worship and religion

The most striking feature of his cult is that his priesthood was held by queens during the Old and Middle Kingdoms, which was unusual within Egyptian religion as a whole. The sources do not describe temples, festivals or offerings, and there is no evidence of his cult surviving beyond those periods. Scholars find this link between women and an underworld god notable, although the texts do not explain their functions in detail.

Trivia

A name with a soul. His name is linked to the ba, the part of the being that the Egyptians saw as mobile and personal.
A priesthood of queens. During the Old and Middle Kingdoms his priesthood was held by queens.
