White Buffalo Calf Woman

White Buffalo Calf Woman is a sacred figure of Lakota tradition, regarded as the primary cultural prophet who brought the people their most important rites, including the sacred pipe. She belongs to the Native American cultures of the Great Plains.

The myth

According to oral tradition, during a time of famine the Lakota chief sent two young scouts to hunt. From a white cloud they saw a beautiful woman dressed in white buckskin. One of them approached with lustful intentions, while the other sensed she was holy. A cloud enveloped the woman and the first man, and when it lifted, only she and his bones remained.

The surviving scout was welcomed by her, and she told him she was holy and that his people would rise again if they followed her instructions. She gave the sacred pipe and taught the tobacco rite. As she left, she turned into a white buffalo and promised to return after the four ages of humankind.

Worship and religion

The sacred pipe, or calumet, lies at the heart of her worship. Tobacco is offered to the four directions, the sky and the earth, then smoked to address Wakan Tanka. Consecrated copies of the pipe are as holy as the original. Along with it she taught seven rites, including the Sun Dance and the purification lodge, which the Lakota still practice as part of their faith.

Ancient sources: The story was passed down orally and through winter counts, pictographic Lakota records that place it around the tenth century.

Trivia

The seven ceremonies. Besides the pipe, she is credited with seven sacred ceremonies, including the purification lodge and the crying-for-vision rite.
Symbolism of the pipe. Each part of the pipe, including the stem, bowl, tobacco, breath and smoke, represents a relationship between the natural world, the elements, humans and spirits.
