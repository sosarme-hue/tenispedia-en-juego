Mephistopheles

Mephistopheles is a demon from German folklore who became one of the most recognisable devils in European literature, mainly through the legend of Doctor Faustus. He is usually seen as a subordinate of Satan tasked with collecting souls, though in some versions he almost resembles Satan himself.

The myth

According to the legend, Mephistopheles appears to Faust, an ambitious scholar frustrated by the limits of his knowledge, and offers to fulfil his wishes in exchange for his soul. In the earliest versions, the late sixteenth-century Faustbuch chapbooks, the demon is a cunning servant of the Devil who comes to those who are already damned within.

In Christopher Marlowe's The Tragical History of Doctor Faustus, Mephistopheles is written more as a fallen angel than as a familiar demon, and he shows a painful awareness of his own damnation. Goethe's Faust turns him into a cold, ironic and humorous figure, able to take on disguises and to handle those around him with relentless logic.

The character has its roots in German folklore, but the origin of his name remains uncertain. Several explanations have been proposed: a corrupted Greek compound meaning "not loving light", a Hebrew root suggesting "disperser" or "plaster of lies", or a link to the poisonous vapours known as mephitic.

Legacy

The character gave his name to a whole literary tradition. Through Goethe he became a type: he appears in Arrigo Boito's opera Mefistofele, premiered in 1868 at La Scala in Milan, and in Franz Liszt's Mephisto Waltzes. He has also inspired songs by bands such as Enigma, Kamelot and Radiohead, and video games such as Diablo II and Neverwinter Nights, where he appears as a demon or as the lord of a frozen hell.

Ancient sources: The earliest references come from the Faustbuch chapbooks, especially the one published in Frankfurt in 1587, and from Marlowe's play, first printed in 1604.

Trivia

A name with several etymologies. His name has been explained as Greek, Hebrew or Latin in origin, and no explanation is definitive.
From devil to fallen angel. Marlowe presents him as a fallen angel who laments his damnation, far more complex than the comic demon of the Middle Ages.
