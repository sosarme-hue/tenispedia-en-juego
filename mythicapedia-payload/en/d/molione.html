mother of the Molionides in Greek myth

Molione

Molione is a figure of Greek myth, the mother of the Molionides, the twins Eurytus and Cteatus, who were born joined at the body. Her story links Elis with the Isthmian Games and with the hero Heracles.

The myth

According to tradition, Molione was the daughter of Molus and was kept away from men until suitors arrived. She married Actor of Elis, but Poseidon had also been with her, and the twins were born of that union. Ibycus tells that she laid a silver egg from which they hatched, comparing them to the Dioscuri.

When Heracles killed the twins in an ambush, Molione set out to hunt down the unknown slayer. The Eleans demanded satisfaction from the Argives, who refused, and then pressed Corinth to bar the Argive people from the Isthmian Games, without success. Molione then cursed her countrymen should they refuse to boycott the games.

Worship and religion

Her curse is still respected today: no athlete from Elis usually competes in the Isthmian Games.

Ancient sources: Ibycus, the Greek poet, preserved only in fragments, mentions her silver egg.

Trivia

A silver egg. According to Ibycus, Molione gave birth after laying a silver egg, an image that recalls the birth of the Dioscuri.
A possible moon goddess. Robert Graves speculated that Molione might be a title of the Elean moon goddess, patron of the Games, meaning Queen of the Moly.
