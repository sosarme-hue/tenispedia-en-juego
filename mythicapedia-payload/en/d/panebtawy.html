Egyptian deity, son of Haroeris

Panebtawy

Panebtawy is an Egyptian deity who represents the pharaoh as the son of Haroeris (Heru-ur). His name, meaning lord of the two lands, presents the pharaoh as the legitimate ruler of all Egypt, united across Upper and Lower Egypt.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved at the temple of Kom Ombo, Panebtawy is the divine son of Haroeris and Tasenetnofret. He is shown as a child wearing the hemhem crown, which emphasises his status as heir.

The story thus places the earthly ruler within a divine family, where his filiation with Haroeris grants him legitimacy.

Worship and religion

His image appears in the sanctuary of Kom Ombo, an Egyptian town where Haroeris was worshipped, and his place in the divine family made sense within local cult practice. The sources consulted do not describe specific festivals, offerings or priesthood devoted to him.
