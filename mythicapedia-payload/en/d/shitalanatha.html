tenth Jain tirthankara

Shitalanatha

Shitalanatha is, according to Jainism, the tenth tirthankara of the present age, a teacher who attained final liberation. He matters to Jains as a model of renunciation and of the path to liberation.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, Shitalanatha was born in the city of Bhaddilpur, in the ancient Ikshvaku dynasty, the son of King Dradhrath and Queen Nanda. His birth is observed on the twelfth day of the Magha Krishna month of the Indian national calendar.

After ruling his kingdom for a time, the narrative says he renounced worldly pleasures to become an ascetic and attained omniscience. After a long period of preaching, he achieved liberation from the cycle of rebirth on the peaks of Mount Shikharji, in today's Jharkhand. As a siddha, he is said to have destroyed all of his karma.

Worship and religion

He is venerated as one of the twenty-four tirthankaras. He is identified by his golden complexion and depicted in meditation, with the wishing tree or the svastika mark as his emblem depending on the tradition. The shrine of his footprints on Mount Shikharji is a pan-Indian pilgrimage destination, and the Badridas Temple in Kolkata is dedicated to him. His worship continues today.

Legacy

His presence is largely confined to the Jain religious sphere, with temples and shrines across the Indian subcontinent.
