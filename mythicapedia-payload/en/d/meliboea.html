young woman of Ephesus, lover of Alexis

Meliboea

Meliboea is a young woman from Ephesus who, according to Greek tradition, was able to marry her beloved Alexis only through divine intervention. Her story, recorded by the Latin grammarian Servius, explains the epithets under which Aphrodite was worshipped in Ephesus.

The myth

Meliboea lived with her parents in Ephesus, an ancient Greek city on the western coast of Asia Minor. She had fallen in love with Alexis, a boy her own age, and the two had promised to marry when the time was right.

Her parents, however, arranged for her to wed someone else and would not change their minds. Heartbroken, Alexis chose a self-imposed exile and left Ephesus for good. On the day of the wedding, Meliboea tried to take her own life by jumping from the roof of her house, but she landed unharmed. She fled to the seashore and boarded a boat she found there; its ropes untied themselves, and the vessel carried her to the land where Alexis lived, as he was holding a feast for friends. By the will of the gods, the lovers were reunited and allowed to marry.

The couple then built a temple to Aphrodite, whom they called Automate, "the spontaneous", after the ropes that brought them together, and Epidaetia, "she of the feast", because Meliboea had arrived during a celebration.

Ancient sources: The story survives in the works of Servius, a Latin grammarian of the fifth century, who presents it as an aetiological tale explaining the epithets of Aphrodite in Ephesus.
