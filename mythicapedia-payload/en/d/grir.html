Norse giantess and witch

Gríðr

Gríðr is a giantess (jötunn) in Norse mythology, a powerful witch (völva) from Jötunheim. She matters mainly for her role in the episode where Thor loses his hammer, and for the tradition that makes her the mother of Víðarr by Odin.

The myth

According to the stories, Gríðr learned that Loki was plotting to have Thor killed by the giant Geirröðr. To help the god, she gave him three magical objects that temporarily replaced his usual attributes: the belt of strength Megingjörð, the iron gloves Járngreipr and the staff Gríðarvöl. With them Thor could face Geirröðr even without Mjöllnir, his hammer, and so he saved his life.

Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda, places her on Thor's journey to Geirröðr's home and includes her in a list of troll-wives. According to the same source, she was the mother of Víðarr the Silent by Odin. The poem Þórsdrápa, by the skald Eilífr Goðrúnarson, also mentions her staff. A witch of the same name appears in the Illuga saga Gríðarfóstra.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons, Gridr, was named after this giantess. Skaldic poets also used her name in kennings: in Viking Age poetry, the wolf could be called the steed of the troll-wife, and the axe, something dangerous to the protector of life.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál), the poem Þórsdrápa, the work of Saxo Grammaticus and the Illuga saga Gríðarfóstra.

Trivia

A moon of Saturn. One of Saturn's moons is named Gridr, in honour of this giantess.
Grytha in Saxo. Saxo Grammaticus calls her Grytha and presents her as the wife of the legendary king Dan I of Denmark, whom the Teutons held in the highest honour.
An uncertain name. Her name has been translated as vehemence, violence or impetuosity, although its etymology is unclear.
