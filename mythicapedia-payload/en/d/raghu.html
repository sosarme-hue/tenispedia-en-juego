Raghu

Raghu is a legendary king in Hindu mythology, founder of the dynasty that bears his name and ancestor of Rama, hero of the Ramayana. He matters chiefly because of the epic poem Kalidasa devoted to his line, the Raghuvamsha, which made his lineage a lasting literary tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, Raghu was the son of the pious king Dilipa, who had performed one hundred sacrificial rites, a feat only Indra, king of the gods, had achieved before. To keep Dilipa from equalling his record, Indra placed obstacles in the hundredth sacrifice, but Raghu overpowered Indra himself and the rite was completed.

As king, Raghu was known for his generosity; it was said that no one left his palace empty-handed. In Kalidasa's poem he conquers many kingdoms and, after giving away all his wealth, is asked by the sage Kautsa for fourteen million gold coins to pay his teacher. Raghu planned to plunder the treasury of Kubera, but the god, learning of this, filled his coffers with a rain of gold. Grateful, the sage blessed him with a son, Aja, who would later become king.

The sources disagree about his genealogy. Several Puranas, including the Vishnu, Vayu and Linga, make Raghu the son of Dirghabahu, who in turn was the son of Dilipa; the Harivamsha, the Brahma and Shiva Puranas make him Dilipa's direct son, with Dirghabahu as an epithet.

Legacy

Raghu is today remembered mainly as Rama's great-grandfather. In the classical period, however, he seems to have held a far greater heroic importance. Kalidasa's epic Raghuvamsha narrates the lives of his ancestors, and Rama bears titles such as Raghava or Raghu-Nandana that tie him to this family and reflect the prestige this king enjoyed.

Ancient sources: Kalidasa's Raghuvamsha (6th century) is the main literary source; the Puranas, the Harivamsha and the Brahma Purana provide his lineage.

Trivia

A name meaning speed. According to the etymology in the sources, raghu originally meant 'swift' in the Rig Veda, and the name may have been given to the king for his skill as a chariot driver.
A dynasty with several names. His lineage appears as the Raghu-kula in tradition and as the Suryavansha, the solar dynasty, in other sources.
