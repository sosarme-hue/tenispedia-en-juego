Élimian boxer of the Aeneid

Entellus

Entellus is an aged but formidable boxer in Virgil's Aeneid, known for his bout with the younger Dares during the games Aeneas held in Sicily. He is traditionally said to have given his name to the Sicilian town of Entella.

The myth

In Virgil's account, Aeneas held games in Sicily on the anniversary of his father Anchises' death, and one event was a boxing match. Entellus, though old, was knocked down by the younger Dares, but he rose with fierce energy, resumed the fight and eventually defeated his opponent, so that his friends had to pull him away from the beaten man.

To show that his strength had not left him, Entellus walked up to the bull that served as the prize, struck it with his gloved fist and split its skull. After this feat he vowed never to take part in boxing contests again.

Legacy

The gray langur, Presbytis entellus, bears his name in scientific usage, a tribute to the figure.

Ancient sources: He appears in Book V of Virgil's Aeneid.

In art

Trivia

A primate with his name. The gray langur carries the scientific name Presbytis entellus, in honor of this figure from the Aeneid.
