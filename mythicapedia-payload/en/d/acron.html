Sabine king of the Caeninenses in Roman legend

Acron

Acron was, according to Roman legend, king of the Caeninenses, a Sabine people neighbouring Rome. His war against Romulus is tied to the story of the Rape of the Sabine Women.

The myth

According to tradition, Acron rejected Romulus's proposal that Romans intermarry with the women of Caenina and other nearby towns. Insulted, Romulus abducted Latin and Sabine women and forced them to marry his men, an episode known as the Rape of the Sabine Women. Enraged, Acron allied with Antemnae, Crustumerium and Titus Tatius, king of Cures, and invaded Rome, laying waste to the countryside. Romulus struck back, captured the raiders' camp and, during the battle, slew Acron in single combat, taking his armour as spoils.

After Acron's death, Romulus marched on Caenina, stormed it and sacked it. He disarmed the survivors, sent many adult men to settle in Rome, took children as hostages and ordered the Caeninenses to tear down large parts of their city. The story goes that the Caeninenses never challenged Rome again.

Trivia

Historicity. No contemporary sources from the 8th century BC mention this king, so he is probably a mythical figure.
