Melia

Melia is a figure of Greek mythology, a daughter of the Titan Oceanus and the lover of Apollo, especially worshipped at Thebes. Her story is tied to the founding of the city and the Apollo cult of the Ismenion, which makes her a key figure in Theban religion.

The myth

Pindar, the Theban poet of the sixth and fifth centuries BC, says Melia, daughter of Oceanus, bore the hero and prophet Tenerus to Apollo. He calls her "Melia of the golden spindle".

The second-century AD geographer Pausanias gives a fuller story: Melia was seized and held at Thebes by Apollo. Her father sent his son Caanthus to bring her back, but unable to take her from the god, Caanthus set fire to Apollo's sanctuary and was shot dead by the god. Pausanias adds that Melia had another son by Apollo, Ismenus, who gave his name to the Theban river.

The tale closely parallels that of Europa and Cadmus, where a brother is likewise sent after a girl carried off by a god. Other traditions make Melia and Ismenus siblings, and some place her among the ash-tree nymphs, the Meliae, who according to Hesiod were born from the blood of Uranus.

Worship and religion

Melia was an important figure of worship at the Ismenion, the great sanctuary of Apollo Ismenios in Thebes. Pindar mentions her "immortal couch" within the precinct, and a nearby spring was identified with her. The Thebans traced their descent from Apollo and Melia through Tenerus and Ismenus, linking their lineage both to the panhellenic god and to the land they inhabited.

Ancient sources: Pindar (sixth-fifth centuries BC), Pausanias (second century AD), Hesiod and Callimachus.

Trivia

A river named after a son. The Theban river Ismenus took its name from Ismenus, the son that Pausanias attributes to Melia and Apollo.
A parallel with Europa. The story of Caanthus, sent to find his sister, closely mirrors that of Cadmus and Europa, showing how Theban tales echoed one another.
