tantric female Buddha of Tibetan Buddhism

Vajrayogini

Vajrayogini is a female deity of tantric Buddhism, regarded in Vajrayana as a female Buddha and a dakini. She is especially revered in Tibetan Buddhism, where her practice is counted among the most powerful within Anuttarayoga Tantra.

The myth

According to tradition, her teachings go back to Vajradhara, who manifested as Heruka to expound the root Chakrasamvara Tantra. Vajrayogini appears as the consort of Heruka, though she is also practised on her own.

She is depicted as a naked young woman with red skin, a vertical third eye, a skull cup and a curved knife. The cremation grounds surrounding her symbolise the death of ordinary world and the transformation of everyday life into a spiritual path.

Worship and religion

Je Tsongkhapa considered her practice a swift path to enlightenment, and it remains alive in Tibetan Buddhism. Her temples in the Kathmandu Valley are revered as power places by both Newar and Tibetan Buddhists, and her teachings are passed from master to disciple through lineages.

Legacy

She is one of the most venerated figures in the tantric pantheon. The abbesses of Samding Monastery in Tibet have traditionally been seen as emanations of Vajravārāhī, one of her forms.

Ancient sources: Tantric Buddhist texts from the tenth to twelfth centuries, drawing on Hindu Shaiva tantras.

Trivia

A matter of pigs. In her form as Vajravārāhī she appears with a sow's head, a symbol of ignorance in Buddhism that she transforms into wisdom.
Five skulls. According to her depictions, she wears a crown of five human skulls and a necklace of fifty.
