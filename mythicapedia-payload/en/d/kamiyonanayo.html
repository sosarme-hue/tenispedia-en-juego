the seven divine generations of Japanese mythology

Kamiyonanayo

The Kamiyonanayo, or seven divine generations, are the groups of kami that, in Japanese mythology, emerged after heaven and earth were formed, ending with the couple Izanagi and Izanami. They matter because they link the origin of the cosmos with the creation of the Japanese archipelago.

The myth

According to the old accounts, once heaven and earth had taken shape, the Kotoamatsukami, independent heavenly deities, appeared first. Then came the seven generations. In the first two, a single spontaneous kami appeared and promptly hid itself. In the five that followed, male and female kami were born in pairs, siblings who were also spouses. The Kojiki counts twelve deities in total.

The last generation is made up of Izanagi and Izanami, the couple tasked with creating the Japanese archipelago and giving birth to other deities. The Nihon Shoki, by contrast, says this group was the first to appear after the creation of the universe, that its first three generations were spontaneous, and that the following four were pairs of opposite-sex siblings.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Japan's two great ancient chronicles, record this group of deities with differences in the number of spontaneous generations and in the spelling of the names.

Trivia

Two chronicles, two versions. The Kojiki counts twelve deities in total, while the Nihon Shoki describes three spontaneous generations followed by four of sibling pairs. It also spells the names differently.
Siblings and spouses. The pairs in these generations were siblings who were also married to each other, a motif found in many other mythologies around the world.
