Greek goddess of consolation, worshipped at Megara

Paregoros

Paregoros, whose name means "consoler", is a goddess of Greek religion worshipped at Megara and linked to Aphrodite. Her figure is known mainly from Pausanias's description of the city's temples.

The myth

Ancient texts say very little about Paregoros. Her name is descriptive: it comes from the Greek Paregoros, "the one who consoles", and the epithet itself describes her role more than any story.

No genealogies or deeds connect her to other gods have survived, so her image is known mostly through the places where she was honoured.

Worship and religion

At Megara, according to Pausanias, Paregoros was honoured in the temple of Aphrodite, located beyond the sanctuary of Dionysus. There stood an ivory image of Aphrodite called Praxis and a statue of Paregoros by Praxiteles, next to one of Peitho, goddess of persuasion. The sources offer no information on whether her cult continued beyond antiquity.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece (2nd century AD), which describes the temple of Aphrodite at Megara.

Trivia

A statue by Praxiteles. Pausanias attributes the image of Paregoros in the temple of Aphrodite at Megara to the sculptor Praxiteles.
A goddess with a descriptive name. The people of Megara called her the Consoler, and that name is the very epithet by which she was recognised.
