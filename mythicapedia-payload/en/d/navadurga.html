nine forms of the goddess Durga in Hinduism

Navadurga

The Navadurgas (‘nine Durgas’) are the nine manifestations of the goddess Durga in Hinduism, worshipped especially during the festival of Navaratri. They are often regarded collectively as a single deity, mainly among followers of Shaktism and Shaivism.

The myth

According to Hindu mythology, the nine forms are the nine stages of Durga during the nine days of her war against the demon king Mahishasura. The tenth day is celebrated as Vijayadashami, one of the most important Hindu festivals.

Tradition presents Durga as a mother goddess and a form of Devi and Shakti, whose most sacred aspect is found in the Navadurgas. Her three main forms are Mahasaraswati, Mahalakshmi and Mahakali, active energies (shakti) of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva respectively. Each manifests in two forms, giving rise to the nine: Brahmachārinī, Chandraghaṇṭā, Kālarātrī, Kātyāyanī, Kuṣmāṇḍā, Mahāgaurī, Śailaputrī, Siddhidātrī and Skandamātā. In the tradition, without their consorts these gods would be powerless.

Worship and religion

During Navaratri the nine goddesses are worshipped together. In Durga Puja, young virgin girls up to the age of nine are honoured, fed and worshipped as incarnations of the goddesses. The nabapatrika rite binds the branches and leaves of nine plants to a banana plant. In India, the Navadurga are the kula devata, or family deity, of many Gauda Saraswata Brahmins in Goa and Maharashtra, and there are numerous Navadurga temples in Goa, such as Madkiam, Kundaim and Pale. Their worship remains alive.

Ancient sources: The Agni Purana lists the Navadurgas under different names, such as Rudrachanda and Chandogra. The Vajasaneyi Samhita includes verses associated with Kushmanda, chanted in penance rites.

Trivia

Plants of the rite. The nabapatrika uses leaves from nine different plants, tied to a banana plant, during Durga Puja.
Relocated temple. The Navadurga of Redi were moved from Goa to their current location in Vengurla.
