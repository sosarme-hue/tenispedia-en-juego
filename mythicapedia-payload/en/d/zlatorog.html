golden-horned chamois of Slovene folklore

Zlatorog

Zlatorog is a white chamois with golden horns who, according to Slovene legend, lives on the heights of Mount Triglav, the highest mountain in Slovenia. His story, best known through Rudolf Baumbach's poem, tells of a hidden treasure, a reckless hunter and the power of nature over human greed.

The myth

According to the tradition, Zlatorog rules the heights of Triglav, in a celestial garden, and guards a hidden treasure. His golden horns are the key to the cavern that holds it. A hunter from the Trenta Valley, eager to win his sweetheart with gifts, climbed the mountain to seize the treasure.

The animal was shot, and red flowers sprang from its blood. Zlatorog ate one and was restored to life. Enraged, he killed the hunter and destroyed his garden, then vanished so that no one would ever learn the secret of his treasure. In some versions, the fairies of the mountain, the Rojenice, leave the place, which is sealed by a landslide.

Legacy

The German poet Rudolf Baumbach turned the legend into a poem in 1877, his most popular work, which made the tale known beyond Slovenia. Operas were later based on it, and the first full-length Slovene film, made in 1931, was titled V kraljestvu Zlatoroga. Today the name is used for a Slovene beer brand, and a statue of the chamois stands by Lake Bohinj.

Trivia

A poem that made him famous. Rudolf Baumbach's verse version, published in 1877, was what made the legend widely known, far more than earlier prose versions.
A beer named after him. The Slovene Laško Brewery produces a beer brand called Zlatorog.
