Sumerian god of shepherds and fertility, consort of Inanna

Dumuzid the Shepherd

Dumuzid, known as the Shepherd, is a Mesopotamian deity linked to flocks, milk and the fertility of plants, and the first consort of the goddess Inanna. His yearly death and return were believed to explain the cycle of the seasons.

The myth

According to the Sumerian King List, Dumuzid was the fifth antediluvian king of Bad-tibira, although his story belongs to legend. As a shepherd, he was thought to provide milk, a rare commodity in Sumer. In the poem Inanna Prefers the Farmer, he competes with the farmer Enkimdu for the goddess's hand.

In Inanna's Descent into the Underworld, the goddess returns from the realm of the dead and, noticing that Dumuzid has not mourned her, lets the galla demons drag him below as her replacement. His sister Geshtinanna takes his place in other tellings. Later Inanna regrets this and decrees that Dumuzid spend half the year underground and half with her, which is the origin of the seasons. In The Dream of Dumuzid, he foresees his own downfall.

Worship and religion

During the month named after him, in midsummer, people across Mesopotamia mourned him publicly, and the rite spread to Babylonia. Women were the main mourners and also baked cakes for Ishtar. His cult later reached the Levant and Greece, where he became Adonis, and the Adonia festival featured small gardens of quick-growing plants that withered in the sun.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Tammuz, who is mentioned in the Book of Ezekiel, where women are described weeping for him at the temple. In the Epic of Gilgamesh, Gilgamesh reminds Ishtar that she struck Tammuz, the love of her youth, and turned him into a bird with a broken wing. Today he is studied as a classic example of the dying-and-rising god.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian King List, the Sumerian poems on Inanna and Dumuzid, the Epic of Gilgamesh (Tablet VI) and the Book of Ezekiel.

Trivia

A broken wing. In the Epic of Gilgamesh, Dumuzid is pictured as a bird with a broken wing lamenting in the forest.
Two Dumuzids. The Sumerian King List mentions another Dumuzid, the Fisherman, a post-flood king of Uruk, who should not be confused with the Shepherd.
