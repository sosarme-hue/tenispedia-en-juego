Roman god of Syrian origin, local form of Jupiter

Jupiter Dolichenus

Jupiter Dolichenus was a Roman god born from the fusion of Baal of the city of Doliche with the Roman king of the gods. His mystery cult peaked across the Empire between the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD.

The myth

Tradition linked the Baal of Doliche to the ancient Hittite-Hurrian storm god Teshub-Hadad, worshipped on a hill near the city and associated with the origin of iron. The Romans presented him as an eastern deity, although modern scholars believe that this exoticism was largely superficial.

His decline came in the mid-3rd century, when the Sasanians sacked Doliche. The god lost his main sanctuary and, with it, much of his prestige.

Worship and religion

He was addressed as Iuppiter Optimus Maximus Dolichenus, and his cult was closed to outsiders. At the Aventine temple in Rome, an inscription lists a community with patrons, candidates and a priest. His consort was Juno Doliquena, who appears at his right in images.

Legacy

His legacy is mostly archaeological: altars, inscriptions and temple remains scattered across the Empire.

Trivia

A temple on the Aventine. An inscription from a temple on the Aventine Hill in Rome lists the members of a cult community, with seven patrons and numerous candidates.
An altar in Algeria. The oldest dedication outside Doliche comes from Lambaesis, in present-day Algeria, where an officer raised an altar to him.
