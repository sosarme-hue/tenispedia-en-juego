Grand Duke of Hell in the grimoire demonology

Murmur

Murmur is a demon of medieval and early modern demonology, a Grand Duke and Earl of Hell known mainly for teaching philosophy and for compelling the souls of the dead to answer questions. He belongs to the tradition of ceremonial grimoires rather than to any single culture.

The myth

In the demonological tradition of the grimoires, Murmur is a Grand Duke and Earl of Hell who commands thirty legions of demons. His name seems to come from the Latin murmur, meaning noise, whisper, murmur or the sound of a trumpet. According to these texts, he teaches philosophy and can force the souls of the dead to appear before the magician to answer each question put to them.

Descriptions vary between authors. The most common version shows him as a soldier riding a vulture or a griffin, and carrying a raven. Two ministers go before him, announcing his arrival with trumpet blasts. Some writers depict him more simply, as a plain vulture.

Ancient sources: The grimoires of demonology Ars Goetia and Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, which list him among the demons of Hell.

Trivia

Latin name. In Latin, murmur means noise, whisper, murmur and also the sound of a trumpet, meanings that fit his role as a herald.
Other spellings. His name appears in several spellings in the texts, such as Murmus, Murmuur or Murmux.
