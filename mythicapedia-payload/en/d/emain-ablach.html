mythical island paradise in Irish mythology

Emain Ablach

Emain Ablach is a mythical island paradise in Irish mythology, often regarded as the realm of the sea god Manannán Mac Lir. Its name means 'Emain of the apples', and it is remembered as an idyllic otherworld linked to gods and heroes.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Emain Ablach is an island of unmatched beauty. It is often considered the realm of the sea god Manannán Mac Lir and is identified with the Isle of Man, though the Isle of Arran has also been proposed, less plausibly.

A medieval Irish poem, Baile Suthain Sith Eamhna, says that the god Lug Lamfada was reared there. Another poem from the 14th century describes it as filled with swans and yews.

Legacy

Some scholars think Geoffrey of Monmouth's island of Avalon, where the sword Excalibur was forged and where King Arthur was taken after the Battle of Camlann, may have been influenced by legends of Emain Ablach. It is also linked to Ynys Afallach, the Welsh equivalent of Avalon.

Ancient sources: Medieval Irish poems such as Baile Suthain Sith Eamhna and a 14th-century poem describing the island.

Trivia

An ancient name. Emain Macha, a place in Ireland, appears as 'Isamnion' in Ptolemy's Geography of the 2nd century AD. Emain Ablach's name may be related to it, though its origin is uncertain.
