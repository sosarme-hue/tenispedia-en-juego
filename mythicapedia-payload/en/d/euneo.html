Lemnian hero, son of Jason

Euneus

Euneus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Jason and Hypsipyle and king of Lemnos. His story links the voyage of the Argonauts with the Greek expedition against Troy.

The myth

According to the myth, Euneus and his twin brother were born to Queen Hypsipyle, whom tradition holds responsible for the death of Opheltes. After she was exiled from the island for sparing her father Thoas, mother and children were separated. The twins, whose name varies among authors between Nebrophonus, Deipylus and Thoas, later took part in the funeral games of Opheltes.

Homer presents him as king of Lemnos and tells how, on his orders, the Greek fleet sailing to Troy was resupplied on the island, a generation after the Argo quest. Euneus also ransomed Lycaon, a Trojan prisoner, from Patroclus in exchange for a silver urn that the Phoenicians had once given to his grandfather Thoas. In a rarer account, he fought alongside Philoctetes against the Carians, allies of Troy.

Legacy

The Euneidae, a Lemnian clan of cithara players, regarded him as their ancestor.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions him as king of Lemnos in the Iliad.

Trivia

An island as a reward. In a rare account, Euneus gave part of Lemnos to Philoctetes as a reward for his help, and Philoctetes named it Akesa.
A ransom in silver. Lycaon's ransom was paid with a silver urn that the Phoenicians had given to Euneus' grandfather, King Thoas.
