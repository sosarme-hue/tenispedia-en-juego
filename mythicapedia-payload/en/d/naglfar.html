nail-ship of Norse mythology

Naglfar

Naglfar is a giant ship in Norse mythology, built entirely from the fingernails and toenails of the dead, which will sail during Ragnarök carrying the enemies of the gods. It matters because it illustrates the Norse belief in caring for the body after death.

The myth

According to the ancient texts, the ship belonged to Muspell, the realm of fire. At Ragnarök it will break free from its moorings when rising waters flood the earth. The Völuspá describes it sailing with the jötunn Hrym at the helm and Loki among a horde of beings that will fight the gods.

Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda, adds a warning: anyone who dies with untrimmed nails contributes material to the ship, so it is wise to trim them before burying the dead. Snorri also says Naglfar is the greatest of all ships, surpassing Skíðblaðnir, considered the best built.

Legacy

The ship appears in the video game The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt, where the Wild Hunt uses it to travel between worlds, and in World of Warcraft, in the Maw of Souls dungeon. In StarCraft it is one of the great colony ships, and in EVE Online it is a dreadnought. It also gave its name to a Swedish black metal band and is the focus of Rick Riordan's novel The Ship of the Dead. Scholars link it to an Indo-European pattern of rituals involving hair and nails.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda's Völuspá and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, where it appears in several chapters.

Trivia

A ship of nails. Åke Ohlmarks proposed that nagl also means peg or nail, so Naglfar could refer to clinker-built ships joined with metal nails, used as funeral ships in the Migration period.
A ship on a stone. The Tullstorp Runestone in Scania may show Naglfar beneath the wolf Fenrir. Its ship has beakheads at both bow and stern, unlike any known Viking ship.
