Roman goddess of pleasure

Voluptas

Voluptas is the Roman goddess of pleasure, the personification of sensual delight. According to Apuleius, she was the daughter of Cupid and Psyche, and her figure links Roman myth with the Greek idea of Hedone.

The myth

According to Apuleius, once Psyche's situation was settled, she remained with Cupid, and in due course they had a daughter whom they called Voluptas, or Pleasure.

Other Roman authors mention a goddess named Volupia, a name that seems to signify willingness. Varro describes a shrine to her on the Via Nova in Rome, and some link her to the delight felt by an infant, placing her alongside other divinities of childhood.

Worship and religion

Varro mentions a shrine of Volupia on the Via Nova, by the Porta Romana, where sacrifices were offered to the goddess Angerona.

Legacy

The Latin word gave English the term 'voluptuousness'. In the Renaissance, Pico della Mirandola and Ficino identified Voluptas with Desire among the Three Graces, and Botticelli painted the Graces in Primavera.

Ancient sources: Apuleius in The Golden Ass, Cicero and Varro, as well as Augustine of Hippo, who speaks of Volupia as a goddess of delight.

Trivia

Greek Hedone. The corresponding Greek goddess is Hedone, whose name means pleasure. Plato mentions her as the Greek equivalent of libido.
Renaissance Three Graces. On a medal belonging to Pico della Mirandola, the Graces bear Latin names: Chastity, Beauty and Desire (Voluptas).
