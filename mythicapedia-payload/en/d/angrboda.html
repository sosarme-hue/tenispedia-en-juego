Angrboda

Angrboda is a giantess in Norse mythology whose name is often rendered as 'she who brings grief'. Together with Loki she is the mother of three of the most feared beings in the myth cycle: the wolf Fenrir, the Midgard serpent Jörmungandr and Hel, ruler of the dead.

The myth

The sources mention her mainly as the mother of monsters. The Völuspá hin skamma, preserved in the Hyndluljóð of the Poetic Edda, presents her as the mate of Loki and the mother of the wolf Fenrir. Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning, in the Prose Edda, calls her a giantess in Jötunheimr and says that she and Loki had three monstrous children: Fenrir, Jörmungandr and Hel.

Some texts place her in Járnvid, an ironwood forest east of Midgard where female trolls live. The Völuspá describes an old crone who breeds wolves, one of which will swallow the moon and stain the heavens with blood at Ragnarök. Snorri paraphrases this prophecy. Some scholars think the giantess of Járnvid may be Angrboda herself, though the texts do not say so clearly; Snorri's list of troll-wives includes an Iárnvidia who might be the same figure.

In the Baldr's Dreams, Odin addresses a völva he has raised from the dead and tells her she is not a wise woman but the mother of three giants, a line often linked to Angrboda. The seeress adds that she will not return from the dead through spells until Loki is freed from his bonds.

Ancient sources: Völuspá hin skamma (within the Hyndluljóð) and Gylfaginning of the Prose Edda, with references in the Völuspá and Baldr's Dreams of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A name with an English echo. The first element of her name, angr, gave English the word anger. Its original sense refers to constriction or distress.
A possibly late name. Some scholars believe the name Angrboda may be a late invention, dating from no earlier than the 12th century, although the tradition of three monsters born of Loki and a giantess could be older.
