Basque goddess of the Earth

Amalur

Amalur, or Ama Lurra ("Mother Earth" in Basque), is the personification of the Earth in Basque mythology. She is regarded as the mother of the sun and the moon, and her faith predates the arrival of the Indo-European peoples.

The myth

According to Basque legends, Amalur is the primary deity and the source of the life force that sustains the world. The Earth is a boundless vessel where living beings, mythological creatures and the souls of the dead dwell.

Amalur created Ekhi, the sun, and Ilargi, the moon, whom some versions call her children and others her siblings. She also brought forth the eguzkilore, a stemless carline thistle placed on doors to ward off genies, lamias and malicious spirits. Legend says that an intruder who found one would stop to count its countless bracts and be caught by daybreak before finishing.

Within her lie vast treasures that people search for in vain, because they always slip beyond reach.

Worship and religion

Leaving offerings in caves and pits was a deeply rooted custom, since these were believed to be the doorways into the Earth. Amalur is closely linked with Mari, the supreme deity of nature, and in older beliefs the two were sometimes seen as one figure.

Legacy

The eguzkilore is still hung on doors and windows across the Basque Country as a protective charm. A 1968 Basque documentary, Ama lur, celebrated the Basque countryside under her name.
