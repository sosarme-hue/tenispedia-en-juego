nine-headed serpent monster of Chinese mythology

Xiangliu

Xiangliu is a venomous nine-headed serpent monster who brings floods and ruin in Chinese mythology. It appears in the Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shanhaijing), where it is also called Xiangyao, as one of the great threats that the heroes of the flood had to overcome.

The myth

According to the Shanhaijing, Xiangliu was a minister of Gonggong, the snake-like water deity. With nine heads that all fed at the same meal, it devastated everything in its path: wherever it rested or breathed, the land turned into bitter, poisonous bog where no animal could survive.

According to tradition, Xiangliu was killed by Yu the Great, who ended the Great Flood. In other versions, Nüwa killed it after defeating it at the hands of Zhurong. Its blood stank so badly that the soaked land could not grow grain, and Yu had to hold back the waters in a pond over which the Sky Lords built their pavilions. An oral version collected in Sichuan in 1983 depicts it as a venomous dragon that devoured people and livestock.

Legacy

Modern depictions show it as a hydra, with each head on a separate neck, while older woodcuts cluster the heads on a single neck.

Ancient sources: The Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shanhaijing), which names it Xiangyao.

Trivia

Heads in a stack. Older woodcuts show the nine heads clustered on one neck, sometimes facing three directions, unlike the modern hydra-like image.
