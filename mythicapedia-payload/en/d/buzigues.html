Athenian culture hero, inventor of the plough

Bouzyges

Bouzyges is a culture hero of Greek mythology, credited in Athens with yoking oxen to the plough and inventing the cart. His clan, the Bouzygai, held priestly and ritual duties linked to marriage and sowing.

The myth

Tradition holds that Bouzyges was the first to yoke a pair of oxen to a plough and to bring cultivation to Athens. He also banned the killing of oxen and bulls because of their value to farming. Sometimes he was identified or confused with Epimenides, who visited and purified Athens.

His descendants were said to form a clan that claimed descent from him, and Pericles may have belonged to it, although some scholars dispute this.

Worship and religion

His cart was kept as a relic on the Acropolis. Each year, a member of the clan performed a sacred ploughing rite at the foot of the Acropolis. The Bouzygai, priests of Zeus Teleios, also announced the start of sowing and pronounced ritual curses against those who broke certain norms of neighbourly conduct.

Legacy

The only ancient depiction of him is on a krater attributed to the Painter of the Naples Hephaistos, showing a nude, bearded Bouzyges driving a plough pulled by bulls. It is now in the Fogg Museum at Harvard.

Ancient sources: The 6th-century BC poet Lasus of Hermione mentions him, and Plutarch, Varro and Pliny the Elder also refer to him.

Trivia

A relic cart. The cart attributed to Bouzyges was kept as a relic on the Acropolis of Athens.
Protected oxen. He was said to have banned the killing of oxen and bulls because of their importance to farming, the first such rule in the tradition.
