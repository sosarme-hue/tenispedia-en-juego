Tartarus

Tartarus is, in Greek mythology, both a primordial deity and a deep abyss beneath the earth, as far below it as the sky is above. It served as the prison of the Titans and as a place of punishment for the wicked, which makes it a central image of the Greek afterlife.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Tartarus is counted among the earliest beings to arise, alongside Chaos, Gaia and Eros. With Gaia he fathered the monster Typhon, and according to different versions also Echidna and the giants who fought in the Gigantomachy. The name also designates a place: Hesiod describes an abyss so deep that a bronze anvil would take nine days to fall from heaven to earth and nine more to reach the bottom.

In the story of the war between the gods and the Titans, Cronus imprisoned the Cyclopes and the Hecatonchires in Tartarus, guarded by the monster Campe. Zeus freed them to help him, and after the Olympian victory he cast Cronus and many Titans into the pit. The Hecatonchires remained as its jailers. Later, Zeus also threw the monster Typhon into Tartarus.

Later authors turned Tartarus into a realm of punishments matched to each crime. There Sisyphus was condemned to push a boulder that always rolled back down; Tantalus could never reach the water or fruit before him; and Ixion was bound to a flaming wheel that never stopped spinning. In the Aeneid, Virgil describes it surrounded by triple walls and guarded by a hydra, with the Fury Tisiphone watching from its tower.

Legacy

The name of Tartarus gave rise to the English verb 'tartarize' and to expressions referring to a place of punishment. In Christian theology and later literature it helped shape the image of hell.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions it in the Theogony as a primordial deity, Homer and Apollodorus place it beneath Hades, Plato describes it in the Phaedo as the place where souls are judged, and Virgil in the Aeneid as the prison of the wicked.

Trivia

A falling anvil. Hesiod calculates that a bronze anvil would take nine days to fall from heaven to earth and nine more to reach Tartarus from the earth.
A guardian hydra. In Virgil's Aeneid, the entrance to Tartarus is guarded by a hydra with fifty black jaws.
