goddess of Irish Celtic mythology

Dana (Danu)

Dana, or Danu, is a goddess of Irish mythology whose name survives almost only in that of the Tuatha Dé Danann, "the folk of the goddess Danu". Medieval texts say very little about her, so much of what is now attributed to her comes from later periods.

The myth

Medieval Irish texts tell almost no stories about her. The name survives in the genitive Danann, and the nominative Danu is a modern scholarly reconstruction. In the Lebor Gabála Érenn (The Book of the Taking of Ireland), the Tuatha Dé Danann take their name from Danand, daughter of Delbáeth, and from her three sons, Brian, Iuchar and Iucharba, who are called the "Gods of Dannan".

In Cormac's Glossary, which predates the Lebor Gabála, the mother of the gods is Anu, and some scholars believe Danu and Anu are the same goddess, or that one name grew out of the other. A poem in the Dindsenchas also mentions a Danainn, wife of Ganaan. Outside Ireland, the Welsh Mabinogion preserve Dôn, whom most specialists regard as a mother goddess, although the text does not specify her gender.

Legacy

In modern Neopaganism she is often pictured as a pregnant woman linked to the full moon, and she is included in the idea of the Triple Goddess: maiden, mother and crone. In the Victorian era some folklorists credited her with motherhood and agricultural prosperity, traits that do not appear in the medieval texts. Later Christian tradition came to associate her with Saint Brigid, though the ancient sources do not document this.

Ancient sources: The medieval Irish Lebor Gabála Érenn and Cormac's Glossary, and the Welsh Mabinogion, for the parallel with Dôn.

Trivia

A possible origin for the Danube. The ancient name of the Danube river, Danuuius, has been linked to the same root as the goddess's name, although its Celtic origin is not certain.
People of skill. In early Ireland, craftsmen were called aes dána, "people of skill". Some scholars connect Danu's name with the Old Irish word dán, meaning artistic skill.
