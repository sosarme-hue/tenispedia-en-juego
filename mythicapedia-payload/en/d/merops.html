king of Aethiopia in Greek myth

Merops

Merops is a king of Aethiopia, a distant land in the far east, in Greek mythology. He was the husband of Clymene and the adoptive father of Phaethon, who was really the son of Helios, and his story survives mainly through Euripides' lost tragedy Phaethon.

The myth

According to the tradition, Merops ruled Aethiopia, close to where Helios and Eos kept their horses. He married Clymene, daughter of Oceanus, not knowing that she had borne Phaethon to the sun god.

When the boy grew up, Merops tried to arrange a marriage for him, but Phaethon refused. Clymene then revealed his true origin, and the young man went to visit Helios, who allowed him to drive the sun-chariot for a day. The attempt ended in disaster and Phaethon was thrown from the sky. His body was brought to the palace as the wedding preparations were underway. When Merops learned the truth, he attacked Clymene, who in some versions was saved by a god's intervention, and the couple were eventually reconciled.

Ancient sources: Euripides, in his lost tragedy Phaethon (5th century BC), and Ovid in the Metamorphoses, where he appears briefly and without the central conflict of Euripides' play.

Trivia

A possible contrast of colour. The scholar James Diggle notes that in Euripides' play Merops may have been dark-skinned, while Clymene and Phaethon appear as white. Another reading holds that the whole palace was portrayed as white in contrast with its dark-skinned subjects, a theory Diggle finds unconvincing.
Almost reversed names. In a version recorded by Hyginus, Phaethon is born to Clymenus, a son of Helios, and Merope, names that nearly reverse those of Clymene and Merops.
