19th tirthankara of Jainism

Mallinatha

Mallinatha is the nineteenth tirthankara, or ford-maker, of Jainism, an enlightened teacher who shows others the way to liberation. Belonging to the Indian religious tradition, he is central to the Jain debate over whether a woman can attain liberation.

The myth

According to Jain scriptures, Mallinatha was born at Mithila into the Ikshvaku dynasty as a princess. The tradition says they lived about 55,000 years, most of it in a state of omniscience, kevala jnana. In the end they freed themselves from all karma and became a liberated soul, a siddha.

The Svetambara and Digambara sects disagree about their gender. The Svetambaras hold that Mallinatha was a woman. In their account, in an earlier life a king fasted alongside friends and secretly outdid them; this deceit led to rebirth in a female body, though the fasting had earned great merit. As a princess, Mallinatha attracted powerful suitors and persuaded them to take up renunciation. The Digambaras maintain that Mallinatha was male. Liberation was attained at Shikharji, today one of the great Jain pilgrimage sites.

Worship and religion

Mallinatha is venerated by both traditions and has many temples across India, such as the Mannargudi temple in Tamil Nadu and the Chaturmukha Basadi in Karkala, Karnataka. The image usually shows a meditating figure with blue complexion and a water pitcher emblem. Shikharji remains a pilgrimage destination.

Legacy

Mallinatha serves as a reference point in the debate over women's capacity to attain spiritual liberation, a question that has gained importance in Indian society.

Ancient sources: Jain scriptures, including the Jnatadharmakatha, contain the canonical narrative, and Nagachandra's Kannada work of 1105 retells it according to tradition.
