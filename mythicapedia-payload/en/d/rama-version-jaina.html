hero of the Ramayana in Jain tradition

Rama (Jain version)

In Jain tradition, Rama is one of the sixty-three illustrious persons, or salakapurusa, and specifically the eighth Balabhadra. His story, related to the Hindu Ramayana, is told here with a different ending.

The myth

According to the Jain scriptures, Rama is a young prince who is robbed of his throne and lives in exile. During that exile, Ravana, king of Lanka, kidnaps his wife Sita. Rama rescues her with the help of his brother Lakshmana and King Sugriva.

Unlike the Hindu version, here Lakshmana is the one who kills Ravana, and both of them end up in hell. Rama, for his part, becomes a Jain monk (muni) and attains moksha, liberation from the cycle of birth and death. Sita also becomes a nun (sadhvi) and is reborn in heaven as Indra.

Worship and religion

According to the Nirvana Kanda, Rama attained nirvana at Tungi Giri in Maharashtra, and there Jains worship his footprints and a sacred image.

Ancient sources: The Nirvana Kanda, a Jain text that places his nirvana at Tungi Giri, and the Jain scriptures that count him among the salakapurusa.

Trivia

A different ending. In the Jain version, Ravana is killed by Lakshmana rather than by Rama, as happens in the Hindu Ramayana.
Revered footprints. Jains venerate Rama's footprints at Tungi Giri, the place where tradition places his nirvana.
