benevolent daimon of Greek mythology

Agathodaemon

Agathodaemon, or Agathos Daimon ("good spirit" or "good god"), was a benevolent daimon of Greek religion, a protector of the household whose name was invoked after meals. In Alexandria, during the Hellenistic period, he took on a serpent form and blended with Egyptian traditions.

The myth

Tradition presents him as a spirit who accompanies each person throughout life, bringing luck, health and wisdom, and who appears whenever needed. As a protector of the state, he also watched over the city. His companion was Tyche Agathe, "Good Fortune", with whom he was associated at Lebadeia.

Ancient texts offer different accounts of his origin. Pausanias suspected that his name was merely an epithet of Zeus, while in Alexandria he was identified with Serapis and with Egyptian deities such as Shai or Kneph. In Aristophanes' comedy Peace, Hermes calls for a toast in his honour once Eirene, Peace, is freed from a pit.

Worship and religion

It was customary to drink a cup of unmixed wine at the end of every meal in his honour, sometimes alongside Zeus Soter. A sanctuary stood on the road from Megalopolis to Maenalus in Arcadia. In Alexandria he was shown as a serpent and was given the role of protector of the city. There is no evidence of a living cult today.

Legacy

Christian thought later likened him to a guardian angel. Al-Biruni mentions a Harranian prophet called Aghathadhimun, and Muslim writers credited him with a clock that lured out snakes and scorpions; Ibn al-Nadim lists him among the foremost alchemists. A late antique gem shows him as a lion-headed serpent beside Serapis.

Ancient sources: Aristophanes (Peace), Pausanias, and Manetho of Sebennytos, preserved by the monk George Synkellos.

Trivia

The Oracle of the Potter. An Egyptian nationalist text predicts the fall of Alexandria, with Kneph and Agathos Daimon leaving the city for Memphis.
Socrates' daimon. According to legend, the philosopher Socrates had a similar daimon that always accompanied him.
