leader of the Phlegyans in Greek myth

Hyperphas

Hyperphas is a leader of the Phlegyans, a people allied with the Thebans, in Greek mythology. He matters mainly as a father: his daughter Euryganeia, according to Pausanias, married Oedipus.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Pausanias, Hyperphas was a chieftain of the Phlegyans who fought alongside the Thebans. His daughter Euryganeia married Oedipus after the death of Iocaste, and in this version it was she, not Iocaste, who bore Oedipus his four children: Eteocles, Polynices, Antigone and Ismene.

Pausanias defends this account by citing the poem Oedipodea and a painting by Onasias that showed Euryganeia grieving over the conflict between her sons. Hesiod, for his part, gives Hyperphas another daughter, Euryanassa, who according to him became the mother of Minyas by Poseidon.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, and Hesiod, who names him as the father of Euryanassa.

Trivia

A painting by Onasias. Pausanias cites a work by Onasias in which Euryganeia appears grieving over the feud between her two sons.
A poem about Oedipus. Pausanias supports his version by pointing to the Oedipodea, an epic poem in the tradition about Oedipus.
