mythical sea turtle of Chinese mythology

Ao

Ao is a giant sea turtle in Chinese mythology, linked to the southeastern seas of China and to the creation of the world. His image may have shaped the dragon-turtles that carry memorial stelae in imperial sculpture.

The myth

According to tradition, Ao lived in the South China Sea at the time the world was formed. When Nüwa, the goddess who created humankind, was repairing the sky after a disaster, she cut off his four legs and used them as pillars to hold it up.

Another story holds that Ao still lives in the Bohai Sea, where he carries on his back the three islands of the Eight Immortals: Penglai, Fangzhang and Yingzhou.

Legacy

Ao is thought to have influenced Bixi, a half-dragon, half-turtle son of the Dragon King who could bear enormous weights. Dragon-turtles carrying memorial stelae are common monuments across East Asia. In place names, words containing ao cluster along the southeastern coast, such as the Ao River (Aojiang) in Zhejiang and the Liu'ao peninsula in Fujian.

Trivia

Turtle place names. Place names with gui (turtle) are very common across China, often for hills shaped like a turtle, known as gui shan. Names with ao are more typical of the southeastern coast.
A river named after Ao. The Ao River, or Aojiang, in southern Zhejiang gives its name to the town of Aojiang on its northern shore.
