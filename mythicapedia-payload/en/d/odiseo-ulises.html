Odysseus (Ulysses)

Odysseus, known to the Romans as Ulysses, is the king of Ithaca and the central figure of Homer's Odyssey. He is one of the best-known heroes of Greek myth, admired above all for his cleverness and guile and for the long journey home that followed the Trojan War.

The myth

In the Homeric tradition, Odysseus is the son of Laertes and Anticlea, husband of Penelope and father of Telemachus. As a baby, his maternal grandfather Autolycus gave him his name, which hints at the anger (odyssamenos) the old man had stirred in many men and women. Other traditions claim that his true father was Sisyphus, and they explain his cunning as an inheritance from him.

As a young man he was taught by the centaur Chiron, and during a boar hunt on Mount Parnassus he received a scar on his knee that would later give him away when he returned home. He obtained the bow of Eurytus, with which he would later kill the suitors, and he became known for his hospitality and his respect for the gods, especially Zeus and Athena, who protected him constantly. When Helen was carried off by Paris, Odysseus had sworn an oath, along with the other suitors, to defend whoever Helen chose. To avoid joining the expedition he pretended to be mad, yoking an ox and a donkey to a plough and sowing salt, but Palamedes placed Odysseus's infant son in front of the plough and the hero swerved away, exposing his trick. He never forgave Palamedes for this humiliation.

In the Trojan War he commanded the twelve ships of the Cephallenians. In the Iliad he is entrusted with returning Chryseis to her father, the priest Chryses, and he restrains the Greek army when it falls into disorder. After Troy fell his nostos, or homecoming, lasted ten years. The Odyssey recounts that journey full of trials, from the Cyclops Polyphemus to his stay with Calypso, and it ends with his return to Ithaca, where he reveals himself to his family and confronts Penelope's suitors.

Legacy

The name of Odysseus passed into Latin as Ulixes and later as Ulysses, a form that was transmitted into medieval Latin. His figure also reached Etruscan art, where he appears under the adapted name Uthuze on scarabs, mirrors and cinerary urns between the fifth and second centuries BC. His epithet polytropos, usually rendered as "man of many turns", has been translated in many different ways and remains linked to the idea of versatile ingenuity.

Ancient sources: Homer, in the Iliad and the Odyssey, and the lost poems of the epic cycle.

Trivia

A name with a story. In the Odyssey, his grandfather Autolycus named him because he had been angered by many people. Other ancient authors linked the name to verbs meaning "to lament" or "to perish".
The scar that gave him away. A wound on his knee, caused by a boar during a hunt on Mount Parnassus, later served to identify him when he returned to Ithaca after the Trojan War.
