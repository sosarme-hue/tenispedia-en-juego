king of Iton and figure of Greek mythology

Itonus

Itonus is a figure of Greek mythology, king of Iton in Phthiotis and, according to some traditions, the father of Athena. He matters mainly for the cult of the goddess attributed to him in his region.

The myth

According to the tradition, Itonus was the son of Amphictyon and was married to the nymph Melanippe. Their union produced Boeotus, who in other versions gave his name to the Boeotians, and two daughters, Chromia and Iodame. In some accounts Itonus is the father of Athena herself, which would lead the goddess to kill her half-sister Iodame.

Iodame, priestess of the sanctuary of Athena founded by her father, died when she looked at the head of Medusa that the goddess carried on her tunic, and was turned to stone.

Worship and religion

Itonus founded a sanctuary of Athena, where his daughter Iodame served as priestess. The goddess's surnames Itonis and Itonia were believed to derive from his name. There is no evidence that this cult survived.

Trivia

Another Itonus. A second Itonus appears in the genealogies as the son of another Boeotus, himself a son of Poseidon. He was the father of Electryon and the grandfather of Trojan War heroes.
A sacred willow. According to Robert Graves, the myth of Itonus reflects a claim that the Itonians worshipped Athena with a willow cult in Phthiotis, before the Athenians did.
