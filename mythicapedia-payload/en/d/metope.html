naiad of the rivers in Greek mythology

Metope

Metope is the name of several figures in Greek mythology. The best known is an Arcadian naiad, daughter of the river god Ladon, whose story appears in the genealogies of Greek rivers and cities, alongside that of an Epirote princess who bore the same name.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by ancient authors, Metope was a naiad of the rivers, daughter of the river god Ladon. Her home was Arcadia, where a stream near Stymphalus bore her name, and the people of the area were said to honour the rivers Metope and Erasinus in the form of oxen. According to Apollodorus, she married the river god Asopus and bore two sons, Ismenus and Pelagon, and twenty daughters, among them Aegina, whom Zeus carried off. Pindar mentions her as the maternal grandmother of Thebe, the horse-taming woman after whom the city is named.

Diodorus gives a different list, with two sons, Pelasgus and Ismenus, and twelve daughters, among them Corcyra, Salamis, Peirene, Cleone, Thebe, Tanagra and Sinope. The genealogies do not agree, and the offspring of Asopus is vague among authors.

The Library of Greek Mythology mentions another Metope, consort of the river god Sangarius, whom some name as the mother of Hecuba, wife of Priam. She may be the same figure or a different one, and the texts do not clarify this.

There is also an Epirote princess, daughter of the cruel King Echetus. Metope had taken a lover, and her father punished them both: he mutilated the man and blinded the young woman by piercing her eyes with bronze needles. He then imprisoned her in a tower and gave her grains of bronze, promising she would regain her sight if she could grind them. Medea recalls this case in the Argonautica as an example of her father Aeetes' cruelty.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Apollodorus, Diodorus Siculus, the Library of Greek Mythology and Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica, which record the different Metopes.

Trivia

Rivers in the shape of oxen. The people of Stymphalus were said to honour the rivers Metope and Erasinus in the form of oxen, an unusual image in Greek religion.
An example of cruelty. Medea cites Metope's case in the Argonautica to show her father Aeetes' cruelty, alongside other punishments of daughters by fathers, such as Acrisius with Danae.
