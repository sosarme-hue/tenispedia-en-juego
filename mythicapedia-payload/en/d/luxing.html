Taoist god of prosperity and official rank, linked to the star Mizar

Luxing

Luxing is one of the three gods of good fortune (the Sanxing) in Chinese folk religion. He personifies the star Mizar, the sixth star of the Wenchang asterism, and stands for prosperity, rank and influence.

The myth

According to popular tradition, the star Mizar came to be personified over time as a man, identified with Zhang Xian, who lived during the Later Shu dynasty. The character Lu refers to a government official's salary, which is why this god is linked to advancement in the imperial bureaucracy.

The image of the Sanxing as three bearded, wise old men dates back to the Ming dynasty, when the stars were first shown in human form. Luxing is usually depicted in mandarin dress, holding a ruyi sceptre.

Worship and religion

Luxing is venerated alongside Fuxing and Shouxing in folk temples, ancestral shrines and homes, and in many Chinese-owned shops, often on small altars with a glass of water, an orange or other auspicious offerings, especially at Chinese New Year. The three are arranged right to left: Shouxing on the viewer's left, Luxing in the middle, Fuxing on the far right. He was also worshipped separately as the deity who decided success in the imperial examinations. His cult remains alive in Chinese communities.

Legacy

The three gods are closely associated with feng shui and are considered auspicious presences in the home. The term Sanxing is commonly used in Chinese culture to denote the three attributes of a good life: fortune, prosperity and longevity.
