fish avatar of Vishnu

Matsya

Matsya is the fish avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu and, in several lists, the first of his avatars. According to tradition, he rescued the first man, Manu, from a great flood, making his story one of the best-known Hindu versions of the world-deluge motif.

The myth

According to the Shatapatha Brahmana, one of the oldest Vedic texts to mention him, King Manu, lawgiver and progenitor of humankind, was performing a water offering when he found a tiny fish in the palm of his hand. The fish feared being swallowed by a larger one and asked for protection, promising in return to save him from a coming flood. Manu moved it to ever larger vessels, from a jar to the ocean. The fish then warned him of the date of the great deluge and asked him to build a boat and tie it to its horn.

When the day came, the waters covered the earth, and the fish towed the boat to the high northern mountains, understood as the Himalayas. Manu, the sole survivor, restored life through austerities and sacrifice, and from the offering arose the goddess Ida, with whom he began the human race. Modern interpreters such as Bonnefoy also read the tale symbolically: the fish alludes to the «law of the fishes», in which the weak need the protection of a lawgiver to fulfil their potential.

In the Mahabharata, the episode features Manu with the seven sages and the seeds of all beings, and the fish is identified with Brahma, who grants Manu the power of creation. In the Matsya Purana, however, the fish is Vishnu. In later versions, Matsya also slays the demon Hayagriva, who had stolen the Vedas, and so becomes the saviour of the scriptures.

Legacy

He is usually shown as a fish with a horn on its forehead, to which the rope of Manu's boat is tied, or as a four-armed, blue-skinned Vishnu with a fish's tail. The Matsya Purana, a Hindu scripture, takes its name from him.

Ancient sources: The Shatapatha Brahmana is the oldest text to mention him; he also appears in the Mahabharata, the Bhagavata Purana, the Matsya Purana and the Garuda Purana.

Trivia

Two explanations of the name. Some scholars link the word to the root mad, «to rejoice», so that matsya would mean «the joyous one». The etymologist Yaska, by contrast, explained that fish are so called because they «revel in eating each other».
A golden fish. Matsya is often described as a giant fish, frequently golden in colour, though he may also be shown as Vishnu joined to the rear half of a fish.
