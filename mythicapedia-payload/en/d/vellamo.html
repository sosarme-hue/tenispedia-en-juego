Finnish goddess of water, lakes and seas

Vellamo

Vellamo is the goddess of water, lakes and seas in Finnish mythology, also called the Mistress of Water. She is linked to fishing and to the abundance of fish.

The myth

According to tradition, Vellamo is the wife of Ahti, the Finnish sea god. In fishing spells, both are asked to bring their cattle, that is, the fish, into the nets. In runic songs, the Mistress of Water rises onto land, sits on a rock to listen to Väinämöinen play the kantele, and owns cows that she sometimes sends to poor people as help.

In a Karelian song, a hero catches a fish that turns out to be a watery maiden of Vellamo and Ahti's daughter. Elias Lönnrot drew on songs of this kind to create the character of Aino in the Kalevala. A South Karelian song makes the nixies the offspring of Vellamo and Ahti.

Worship and religion

She was especially honoured by fishermen, who recited spells to her before casting their nets, believing her presence guaranteed a good catch.

Legacy

A track by ambient folk artist Archaic Earth, "Vellamo's Song", bears her name, and a building in the Finnish city of Kotka is named after her.

Ancient sources: Finnish and Karelian runic songs mention her; in 1551 Mikael Agricola names the "Mother of Water" without naming Vellamo.

Trivia

A goddess on a building. In the Finnish city of Kotka, a building is named after Vellamo.
Modern music. Ambient folk artist Archaic Earth included the track "Vellamo's Song" on his EP Hiraeth.
