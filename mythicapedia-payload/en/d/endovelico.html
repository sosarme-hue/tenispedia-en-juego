Celtic god of the Lusitanians and Vettones, lord of the underworld and of health

Endovelicus

Endovelicus is an Iron Age god worshipped by the Lusitanians and Vettones of the Iberian Peninsula, linked to the underworld, health, dreams and prophecy. He was one of the most important pre-Roman deities of the region, and the Romans later identified him with Pluto or Serapis.

The myth

Few narratives about Endovelicus survive, so his image is largely reconstructed from inscriptions and remains of worship. He is considered a chthonic god, lord of the dead and of the underworld, but also a protector of health and of those who sought answers in dreams.

His sacred animals were the boar and the wolf, linked to passage to the afterlife, and his plant associations were the palm and the laurel wreath. The origin of his name is uncertain: it has been read as a mix of Celtic and Phoenician elements, or as a Celtic term meaning something like "very good".

Worship and religion

His main sanctuary stood at São Miguel da Mota in the Alentejo, and his religious importance was matched only by that of his consort, Ataecina. Finds suggest the practice of incubatio, in which the worshipper hoped to receive a divine revelation in a dream. Studies also point to animal sacrifices at the site. The cult lasted until the 5th century, as Christianity spread through the region.

Legacy

His inscriptions and votive offerings are kept in the Ethnological Museum in Lisbon. The stream near the sanctuary, Lucefecit, was associated with Lucifer in the Middle Ages.

Trivia

A name with several readings. His name has been explained as a mix of Celtic and Phoenician words, and also as a possible loanword from a proto-Basque root.
A stream with a luminous name. The Lucefecit stream near the sanctuary was linked to Lucifer from the Middle Ages, though some connect its name with the meaning "glimpse of light".
