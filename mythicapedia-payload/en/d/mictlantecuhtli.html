Aztec god of the underworld and the dead

Mictlantecuhtli

Mictlantecuhtli is the lord of the dead and of the underworld in Aztec religion, ruling Mictlan alongside his wife Mictecacihuatl. He matters because he was the destination of most people who died natural deaths, regardless of how they had behaved in life.

The myth

According to Aztec myth, Mictlantecuhtli and Mictecacihuatl governed Mictlan, a dark realm in the north, at the deepest of nine underground levels. Most people who died a natural death went there, while warriors who fell in battle or sacrifice, and women who died in childbirth, had different fates.

The best-known story tells how Quetzalcoatl descended into the underworld to fetch the bones of earlier generations. The lord of the dead agreed to hand them over if Quetzalcoatl passed a test, but then changed his mind and sent his creatures after him. A quail startled the god into a pit, and the bones scattered. Quetzalcoatl gathered them and mixed them with his own blood to give life to humanity.

Worship and religion

The Mexica buried their dead with amulets, food and objects for a long journey through the nine regions of Mictlan, and a dog was interred with the body to help it cross the river Chignahuapan. Among the Zapotecs, a turkey was offered to the god when someone died, and prayers for victory in war and relief from deadly epidemics were addressed to him. Two life-size clay statues guarded the House of Eagles beside the Great Temple of Tenochtitlan.

Legacy

He appears in the animated film La leyenda de las Momias (The Legend of the Mummies) by Anima Estudios, where he brings the dead back to life in Guanajuato, and he is the main antagonist of the animated series Onyx Equinox (2020). He also appears near the end of the short film God of Death, in the horror anthology V/H/S/85 (2023).

Ancient sources: Aztec codices depict him, such as the Codex Borgia, which shows him carrying a black sun on his back, and the Codex Vaticanus 3738, which labels him in Spanish.

Trivia

Star-like eyes. When drawn, he had black, curly hair and star-like eyes, because he dwelt in complete darkness.
Patron of a calendar day. He was the patron of the day Itzcuintli, meaning dog, in the divinatory calendar, and was believed to supply the souls of those born on that day.
