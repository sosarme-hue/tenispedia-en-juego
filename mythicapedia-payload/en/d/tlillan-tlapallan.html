legendary Nahua place of black and red

Tlillan-Tlapallan

Tlillan-Tlapallan, meaning "place of black and red," is a legendary place in Nahua tradition, placed by the sources on the Gulf coast of Mexico or, in another account, in Campeche or Yucatán. It stands out as the final destination of Quetzalcoatl in several accounts.

The myth

According to the Annals of Cuauhtitlan, preserved in the Chimalpopoca Codex, Quetzalcoatl flew from Tollan to this place intending to burn himself and be transformed into the Morning Star, that is, Venus. This account links the transformation to Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli, a manifestation of Quetzalcoatl associated with Venus in this story.

Bernardino de Sahagun records a different version in his General History of the Things of New Spain. Here Quetzalcoatl, old, sick and outraged by the violence of the trickster Titlacahuan, flees to Tlapallan and sails away on a raft of serpents, though the text does not clearly explain how he reached his destination.

Ancient sources: The Annals of Cuauhtitlan (Chimalpopoca Codex) and Bernardino de Sahagun's General History of the Things of New Spain.

Trivia

A name with symbolism. The name is often interpreted as a reference to writing and books, and the colours black and red are understood as symbols of wisdom.
A place that is hard to locate. The sources do not agree on its location: some place it on the Gulf coast of Mexico, while another puts it in Campeche or Yucatán.
