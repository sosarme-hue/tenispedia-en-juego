cosmic buddha of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism

Vairocana

Vairocana, whose Sanskrit name means "the sun" or "shining", is one of the most important buddhas in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. In East Asia he is known as Dainichi Nyorai, "the Great Sun", and he is regarded as the Dharma-body, the ultimate reality of all buddhas, and the central figure of the Five Buddhas.

The myth

According to Mahayana scriptures, Vairocana attained enlightenment countless eons ago, while still a bodhisattva, and purified the world where he dwells. He presides over an assembly of innumerable bodhisattvas, and his body is described as the entire universe, containing all worlds, with vast oceans within a single pore. The Avatamsaka Sutra presents him as a cosmic buddha whose wisdom is present in every living being.

Tradition links him closely to Shakyamuni. The Brahmajala Sutra says Shakyamuni was originally named Vairocana, and the Avatamsaka describes the historical Buddha as an emanation of this cosmic buddha, his earthly manifestation. In the Samantabhadra Meditation Sutra, Vairocana is an epithet of Shakyamuni, whose abode is a realm of eternally quiet light.

Esoteric texts give him a different role. In the Mahavairocana Tantra and the Vairocanabhisambodhi Sutra he is the central buddha, and his virtues are said to be inherent in all beings. His name comes from vi-rocana, "coming from the sun"; the Rigveda associates the word with a bright sun, which is why in Tibetan he is called Namnang, "the illuminator". He should not be confused with Virochana, the asura king.

Worship and religion

In East Asia Vairocana was worshipped in the Tiantai and Huayan schools and in Chinese esoteric Buddhism, and in Japan in Shingon and Tendai. There he was called Dainichi Nyorai, and at one point was identified with the Shinto goddess Amaterasu. His cult gradually yielded to Amitabha, largely because of the Pure Land movement, but it survives in Shingon and at Todai-ji temple in Nara, which houses a huge statue made in his honour. He is usually shown making the Wheel of Dharma mudra, seated on a lotus throne flanked by lions.

Legacy

His image shaped Buddhist art, especially monumental statues, such as the Buddha of the Spring Temple in Henan, one of the largest depictions of him in the world. Huayan philosophy and its idea of emptiness are closely associated with him. In Japan, the missionary Francis Xavier used the term Dainichi for the Christian God, but later replaced it with Deusu once he grasped its Buddhist connotations.

Ancient sources: Brahmajala Sutra, Buddhāvataṃsaka Sutra (Avatamsaka), Samantabhadra Meditation Sutra, Vairocanabhisambodhi Sutra and Mahavairocana Tantra, plus the Rigveda for its etymology.
