king of Crete in Greek mythology

Deucalion of Crete

Deucalion is a king of Crete in Greek mythology, son of Minos and father of the warrior Idomeneus, who led the Cretans in the Trojan War. He matters because he links the royal line of Crete with Homeric epic and with the cycle of Theseus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Deucalion was one of the sons of Minos. His mother was usually Pasiphae, although some authors name instead a Crete, daughter of Asterion. With his wife Cleopatra, a name transmitted only by Tzetzes, he fathered Idomeneus, who succeeded him on the throne and led the Cretans to Troy. He also had a daughter called Crete and an illegitimate son, Molus.

The Iliad mentions his lineage through the words of Idomeneus, and Hesiod speaks in similar terms. Diodorus, however, makes Deucalion and Molus brothers, with their sons Idomeneus and Meriones as the Cretan leaders of the expedition against Ilium.

The few deeds attributed to him concern Athens. According to one version, Deucalion made an alliance with the Athenians by marrying his sister Phaedra to Theseus. According to another, he was hostile to Athens and, when he demanded that Daedalus be handed over, Theseus refused and killed him at the gate of the Labyrinth, freeing Daedalus in the process. Hyginus also places him among the Argonauts and the Calydonian Hunt, a claim no other author confirms.

Ancient sources: The Iliad and Hesiod mention him as the father of Idomeneus; Diodorus, Hyginus and Tzetzes offer differing accounts of his family and deeds.

Trivia

Two versions of the family. While the usual tradition makes Deucalion a son of Minos, Diodorus presents him as the brother of Molus, with their sons as leaders of the Cretans at Troy.
A doubtful son. Disguised, Odysseus pretends to his wife to be Aethon, a supposed second son of Deucalion. It is unclear whether Aethon existed or was invented by Odysseus.
Argonaut and hunter. Hyginus credits him with taking part in the voyage of the Argonauts and the Calydonian Hunt, a view no other ancient author shares.
