Mongolian death worm, cryptid of the Gobi Desert

Aka Allghoi Khorhoi

The aka allghoi khorhoi, or Mongolian death worm, is a creature of Mongolian folklore said to dwell in the Gobi Desert and to kill with acid or electric shocks. Its fame in the West stems from the tales that Roy Chapman Andrews recorded in the 1920s.

The myth

Mongolian herders of the Gobi describe the olgoi-khorkhoi, whose name means «intestine-worm», as a long, thick, reddish creature with no visible eyes, nose or mouth, that moves by rolling or arching its body. According to the tradition, it lives underground and surfaces mostly after rain. Its most feared trait is that it can kill at a distance, by spitting venom or acid or by electric discharges.

Accounts gathered by Andrews and by the Czech researcher Ivan Mackerle agree on the essentials: nobody claimed to have seen it directly, yet nearly everyone believed it existed. Some witnesses blame the creature for the death of an entire herd of camels.

Legacy

The legend inspired a 1944 short story by Russian paleontologist and science fiction writer Ivan Yefremov. In his searches of 1990 and 1992, Mackerle drew on Frank Herbert's Dune to build a motorised device that thumped the ground to lure the creature. Later expeditions, such as Richard Freeman's in 2005 and the one by the television series Destination Truth, also failed to find it.

Trivia

A drum against the worm. Mackerle built a motorised device that thumped the ground, inspired by the sandworms of the novel Dune, and even used small explosions to try to bring the creature to the surface.
A name meaning intestine. Olgoi-khorkhoi translates as «intestine-worm», a name that reflects the appearance witnesses attribute to the creature.
