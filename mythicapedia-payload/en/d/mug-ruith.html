blind druid of Irish mythology

Mug Ruith

Mug Ruith is a blind and extraordinarily powerful druid in Irish mythology, linked to Munster and to Valentia Island. His shifting chronology has led some scholars to see him as an old sun or storm god turned into a human figure.

The myth

According to the stories, Mug Ruith could grow to enormous size, and his breath brought storms and turned men to stone. He wore a hornless bull-hide and a bird mask, and flew in the roth rámach, an oared ship shaped like a wheel whose glare blinds, whose sound deafens and whose touch kills.

The legends place him in different periods: in the reign of High King Cormac mac Airt, in Jerusalem at the time of Christ, or long before Cormac. Some texts say he was a student of Simon Magus, who taught him magic and helped him build the ship. In The Siege of Knocklong he defeats Cormac's druids, and in other poems he appears as the executioner of John the Baptist. His daughter Tlachtga gave her name to a hill in County Meath.

Ancient sources: Medieval Irish texts such as the Lebor Gabála Érenn and poems like The Siege of Knocklong, along with later legends of his adventures in the Holy Land.

Trivia

A flying ship. The roth rámach is described as a flying machine of great destructive power. A prophecy attributed to Saint Columba links it to an omen of the Last Judgement.
A possible sun god. Scholar Aideen M. O'Leary suggests Mug Ruith may have been a sun god reinterpreted as a druid, based on his fiery ox-driven chariot.
