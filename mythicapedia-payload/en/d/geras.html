Greek god of old age

Geras

Geras is the Greek personification of old age, a minor figure linked to the physical wear and loss of strength that come with the passing of time. He appears in very few ancient stories, and most of what we know of him comes from images on painted vases.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Geras was one of the many children Nyx bore on her own, without a partner, and he was the next-to-last to be born. Later writers such as Hyginus and Cicero made Erebus, Nyx's consort, his father, and in the Roman tradition he is called Senectus. He has almost no stories of his own, and the mythographers say very little about him.

Attic red-figure vases show him as a tiny, shrivelled, bald old man begging for mercy, often confronted by Heracles, who strikes him with a club or chases him. No ancient text narrates that encounter, so its meaning is read from the images as youthful strength set against old age. The writer Philostratus claimed that the people of Gadeira built altars to Geras and to Thanatos, Death.

Legacy

The Greek root gēras gives English words such as "geriatric" and "progeria". The Roman poets called him Senectus, the Latin word from which "senescence" and "senile" derive.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Hyginus and Cicero in their cosmogonic passages, and the scholiast on Homer's Odyssey, which describes the gathering of the gods on Olympus.

Trivia

Dead skin. In Greek, gēras could also mean "dead skin" or "the slough of a snake", a sense that captures the idea of something shed with time.
Altars at Gadeira. Philostratus reports that the people of Gadeira, the ancient name of Cádiz, built altars to Geras and Thanatos, and were said to be the only ones who sang hymns to Death.
