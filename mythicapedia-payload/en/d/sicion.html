Greek hero, second founder of the city of Sicyon

Sicyon

In Greek mythology, Sicyon was the second founder and eponym of the Peloponnesian city that bears his name, which was earlier called Aegiale and, even earlier, Mecone. His story explains the city's name and the succession of its first kings.

The myth

Tradition does not agree on his parentage. Pausanias records up to four versions: that he was a son of Marathon, of Metion, of Erechtheus, king of Athens, or of Pelops, without settling on any one.

King Lamedon called him to ally against his Argive enemies, Archander and Architeles. As a reward he gave him his daughter Zeuxippe in marriage, and Sicyon had a daughter with her, Chthonophyle. When his father-in-law died, he inherited the throne. Some accounts give Chthonophyle children by Hermes and Dionysus, and her son Polybus succeeded to the kingdom.

Ancient sources: Pausanias records several versions of his genealogy.
