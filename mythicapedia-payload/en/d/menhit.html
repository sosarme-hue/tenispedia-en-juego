Egyptian warrior goddess of Nubian origin

Menhit

Menhit is a war goddess of Egyptian mythology, originally from Nubia, linked to solar light, heat and the north wind. She protected the pharaoh and led him in battle, and her figure merged with that of other warrior goddesses.

The myth

According to tradition, Menhit was regarded as the wife of Khnum and the mother of Heka in the 3rd Nome of Upper Egypt, above all at Esna. Some accounts also make her the mother of Shu. At Tinis she was the companion of Onuris (Anhur), a god who, like her, came from Nubia.

Her best-known story is the myth of the Distant Goddess. The Eye of Ra flees from Egypt and settles in Nubia, where it takes the form of a lioness. Ra sends a god to bring her back, and depending on the version she either becomes Menhit or gives birth to her. She was also imagined marching ahead of the Egyptian armies and cutting down their enemies with fiery arrows.

Because she resembled other warrior goddesses, she was identified with Tefnut, Sekhmet and Hathor. At Esna she was seen as a form of Neith, and in her gentler aspect she was linked with one of the “Two Ladies”, the symbols of the kings of Upper and Lower Egypt, and with Isis at Heliopolis.

Worship and religion

Her main role appears in royal ritual: during the Heb Sed ceremony, the festival that renewed the pharaoh's power, she protected the king. The sources do not describe her temples or festivals in detail.

Ancient sources: The Coffin Texts associate her with protection and with the sun.

Trivia

The Distant Goddess. In one myth, the Eye of Ra flees Egypt for Nubia, where it turns into a lioness. When it returns to Ra, it either becomes Menhit or gives birth to her, depending on the version.
A lioness with a solar disk. She is always depicted as a lioness with a solar disk and the uraeus, the sacred cobra that adorns royal crowns.
