Aztec god of healing and pulque

Patecatl

Patecatl is a god of Aztec (Mexica) mythology linked to healing, fertility and pulque, the fermented drink made from agave. He is credited with discovering peyote and with ruling one of the thirteen-day periods of the Aztec calendar.

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Patecatl discovered peyote and is known as the "lord of the root of pulque", the one who watched over the plant from which the drink was made. Married to Mayahuel, a goddess connected with the agave, he helped create pulque, and together they became the parents of the Centzon Totochtin, the four hundred rabbits associated with drunkenness.

In the Aztec calendar, Patecatl was the lord of the thirteen days from 1 Monkey to 13 House. The thirteen days before were ruled by Mictlantecuhtli and the thirteen after by Itztlacoliuhqui, forming part of a regular cycle of twenty thirteen-day periods.
