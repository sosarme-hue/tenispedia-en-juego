female figure of Norse mythology

Torgerd Hølgebrud

Torgerd Hølgebrud is a female figure of Norse mythology, linked to the Håløygætten dynasty and the jarls of Lade. Her nature is uncertain: she may have been a deity, an elf or a valkyrie.

The myth

In pre-Christian legends, Torgerd was the wife or daughter of King Hølgi, and her epithet, Holgabrúðr, means "Hølgi's bride". In the sagas she appears as the protector of Haakon Jarl's family.

During the battle of Hjörungavágr (986), facing fierce resistance from the Jomsvikings, Haakon called on her and sacrificed his son Erling to secure victory. After the sacrifice a violent hailstorm broke out, and Torgerd and her sister Irpa shot arrows from their fingers, each striking down one of the enemy.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or rites. Her presence in several sagas suggests that a cult may have spread across Scandinavia, not only in Hålogaland.

Ancient sources: The Saga of the Jomsvikings (ch. 21), Njál's Saga (ch. 88) and Þorleifs þáttr jarlaskálds, where she appears alongside Irpa; in other sagas she appears alone, as in Ketils saga hœngs.

Trivia

An inseparable sister. Torgerd usually appears alongside her sister Irpa, who shares with her the episode of arrows shot from the fingers during the battle.
The meaning of her name. Her epithet Holgabrúðr means "Hølgi's bride", a clue to the relationship the legends established with King Hølgi.
