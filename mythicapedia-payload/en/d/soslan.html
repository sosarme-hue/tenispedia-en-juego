trickster hero of the Nart sagas of the Caucasus

Soslan

Soslan, also known as Sosruko or Sosriqwa, is the most cunning of the Narts, the giant heroes of the North Caucasus sagas. Often portrayed as a trickster, he is compared with Prometheus, Loki and other figures from neighbouring traditions.

The myth

According to the tradition, his father was a shepherd who was so taken with the beauty of Satanaya that he left his seed on a stone. Nine months later, Satanaya found the stone and raised the boy there.

Soslan was handsome and invulnerable, although depending on the version his body was vulnerable at the knees or the hips. One day he bathed in a small tub, and the fluid that seeped from his skin never reached his knees. That was his only weak point: he died when his enemies struck him there with a wheel studded with steel spikes.

Trivia

A name with several roots. A folk etymology links it to Circassian words meaning roughly son of the fiery sword's blow. Other theories point to Turkic or Persian roots.
A possible inspiration for Prometheus. In the Circassian version, Soslan steals fire from a giant for his mother, Satanaya. Some scholars believe this story may have directly inspired the Greek myth of Prometheus.
