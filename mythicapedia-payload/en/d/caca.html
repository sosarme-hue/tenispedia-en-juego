Roman fire goddess, sister of Cacus

Caca

Caca is a figure of Roman myth, the sister of Cacus, who according to tradition betrayed her brother by showing Hercules where he had hidden the cattle stolen from Geryon. She matters because some modern scholars see her as an older fire deity, comparable to Vesta.

The myth

According to the myth, Cacus was the son of Vulcan, and he stole part of the herd that Hercules had driven from the west after taking it from Geryon. Caca betrayed her brother by revealing to Hercules where the cattle were hidden, and for this betrayal she was honoured as a deity. Lactantius and Servius link her cult to this service rendered to the god.

Caca shares with her brother the ability to breathe fire, a capacity inherited from Vulcan. For this reason she has been seen as a sort of «proto-Vesta», a fire goddess.

Worship and religion

Servius says she had a sacellum, or small shrine, probably in Rome, where sacrifices were made to her through the agency of a Vestal Virgin. The sources give no information about festivals or whether her cult survived.

Legacy

Outside the ancient texts, Caca barely appears. Michael Lipka cites her as an example of divine pairs differentiated by gender but bound by kinship, like Libera and Liber, and the idea that she was a «proto-Vesta» is a modern interpretation.

Ancient sources: Servius and Lactantius, both late authors, are the only ancient sources that mention her.

Trivia

Sister of a cattle thief. Cacus stole some of Hercules' cattle, and it was his sister who revealed where they were hidden.
Fire from the mouth. Like her brother, Caca inherited from Vulcan the ability to breathe fire.
