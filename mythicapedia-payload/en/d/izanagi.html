Japanese god of creation and life

Izanagi

Izanagi is a primordial deity of Japanese mythology and Shinto who, together with his sister-wife Izanami, is held to have created the Japanese archipelago and many gods. His story explains the origin of light, the sea and the imperial line, and it is one of the founding narratives of Japanese religion.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Izanagi and Izanami are the seventh and last generation of deities to appear after heaven and earth came into being. Charged with shaping a still chaotic world, they stir the sea with a jewelled spear; the drops that fall form the island of Onogoro, where they raise a heavenly pillar and perform a ritual marriage. Their first children are imperfect, so the order of the union has to be corrected. After bearing the islands of the archipelago and many deities, Izanami dies giving birth to the fire god Kagutsuchi, and Izanagi, in grief, kills him.

Izanagi goes down to Yomi, the land of the dead, to bring her back. She warns him that she has already eaten the food of the underworld, but he breaks his promise not to look and finds her body rotting. He flees, pursued by warriors and the hags of Yomi; to slow them he throws objects that turn into fruit and shoots, and he drives them off with three peaches. Izanami vows to kill a thousand people a day, and he answers that fifteen hundred will be born each day. Back in the world he purifies himself in a river, and from his eyes and nose are born Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi and Susanoo, to whom he divides the world. The rebellious Susanoo is eventually expelled and vanishes from the story.

The Nihon Shoki offers variants about his parentage, naming Ayakashikone or Awanagi as his progenitors, showing that the myth never had a single fixed genealogy.

Worship and religion

Izanagi remains a living deity of Shinto, still honoured in Japanese shrines and in purification rites such as misogi. His story is tied to the Shinto concern with purity and impurity (kegare) and to the kami who inhabit nature and lineages.

Legacy

His story has inspired Japanese art, literature and film, and has been compared with the Greek myth of Orpheus and Eurydice and with the tale of Cupid and Psyche. Some scholars suggest his name comes from the Japanese verb meaning 'to invite'.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Nihon Shoki (720) record him as a creator deity and ancestor of the principal gods.

Trivia

The three precious children. Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi and Susanoo were born from Izanagi's left eye, right eye and nose during his purification.
The peaches. Izanagi uses three peaches to repel his pursuers and declares them divine, naming them Ōkamuzumi.
