household sprite of Asturian folklore

Sumiciu

The sumiciu is a sprite from Asturian folklore, related to the Trasgu, who is said to appear wearing a red cap. People blame him for lapses of memory and for household objects that go missing, so he serves as a popular explanation for everyday small losses.

The myth

According to Asturian tradition, the sumiciu is a household sprite, less well known than the Trasgu but of the same family. He is so tiny that he is almost never seen, so his presence is noticed only through his effects: things that vanish without explanation.

Folklore also places him in specific spots, such as the Camín Encantau in the Ardisana valley (Llanes), alongside other beings of Asturian myth.

Worship and religion

In the municipality of Somiedo it was customary to pray to Saint Anthony so that the sprite would return what he had taken. The prayer had to be recited without stumbling, as the journalist and writer Constantino Cabal described in 1925.

Legacy

His presence survives in speech: in Asturias people say «llevolu'l sumiciu» or «paez obra del sumiciu» to explain away a lost object.

Trivia

Latin origin. His name comes from the Latin sumere, meaning to take, appropriate or acquire, in keeping with his reputation for taking what is not his.
An enchanted path. The Camín Encantau, in the Ardisana valley in Llanes, is one of the places where tradition locates other beings of Asturian mythology.
