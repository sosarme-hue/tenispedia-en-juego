Peloreus

Peloreus, also called Pelorus, is one of the Gigantes of Greek mythology. The sources do not agree on his parentage: the record makes him a son of Uranus and Gaia, while other accounts place him among the children of Gaia and Tartarus. He appears mainly in the war of the Giants against Dionysus.

The myth

In Nonnus' Dionysiaca, Hera, still burning with resentment toward Dionysus, urges her Giant sons to attack him. Peloreus takes up Mount Pelion, with its high peak, and hurls it as a missile with his countless arms, leaving the cave of Philyra bare.

Claudian tells a different end for him. During the Gigantomachy, Mars, the Roman equivalent of Ares, charges into the fray and meets Pelorus first. He runs him through with his sword at the groin, where the two-bodied serpent joins his giant form, and with one blow ends three lives. He then drives his chariot over the dying giant's limbs until the wheels run red with blood.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis, Dionysiaca, and Claudian, who describes his death at the hands of Mars during the Gigantomachy.
