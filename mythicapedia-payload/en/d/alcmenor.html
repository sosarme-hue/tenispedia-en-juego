Egyptian prince in Greek myth

Alcmenor

Alcmenor is an Egyptian prince in Greek mythology, a son of King Aegyptus and one of the brothers at the centre of the story of the Danaids. His story matters because it links the family line of Egypt with that of Libya and the tragic fate of his brothers.

The myth

According to tradition, Alcmenor was one of the many sons of King Aegyptus, born to an Arabian woman, and so a full brother of Istrus, Chalcodon, Agenor, Hippothous, Hippolytus and others. Some accounts make him a son of Aegyptus by another mother: either Eurryroe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre.

His fate was the same as that of most of his brothers: they were slain on their wedding night by their wives, who obeyed the command of their father, King Danaus of Libya. Only Lynceus was spared. Alcmenor's wife was the Danaid Hippomedusa, daughter of Danaus, born according to some versions to the hamadryads Atlanteia or Phoebe.

Trivia

Different mothers. The accounts do not agree on Alcmenor's mother: some name an Arabian woman, others the naiad Eurryroe or Isaie, daughter of the king of Tyre.
A wife of divine descent. According to some versions, Hippomedusa was a daughter of Danaus and a hamadryad, so her lineage mixes with that of the tree nymphs.
