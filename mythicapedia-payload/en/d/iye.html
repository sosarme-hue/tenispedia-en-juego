tutelary spirit of Turkic mythology

İye

The İye is a tutelary spirit in Turkic traditions, guardian of a place, a person, a lineage, an animal or a natural element. It matters because it describes a world divided among spirit owners who must be respected and negotiated with.

The myth

According to Aynur Gazanfargizi, who draws on Tengrism, God (Tengri) created the İye and gave each one dominion over a specific place, much like the angels of Abrahamic religions. Their nature is neither good nor bad: it depends on how people treat them. Disrespect toward the İye of a garden makes it wither, while honour makes it flourish.

A story collected by Verbitsky Vasily presents them as fallen angels. When Erlik wished to build a world of his own, Ülgen threw him and his servants out of the sky. Falling into water, fire or other elements, each servant became the spirit of that element, such as the od-iyeler (İye of fire) or the su-iyeler (İye of water).

Worship and religion

In Azerbaijan, Anatolia and Ahıska, İye were venerated as saint-like beings. They were linked to sacred shrines and cemeteries, and the sick who could find no cure made pilgrimages to the graves thought to be where they rested.
