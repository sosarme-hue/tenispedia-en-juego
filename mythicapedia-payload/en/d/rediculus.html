Roman god of the turning back and of laughter

Rediculus

Rediculus is a Roman divinity, possibly one of the protector lares of the city, linked to Hannibal's retreat from Rome in 211 BC and to a temple outside the Porta Capena.

The myth

According to tradition, Hannibal, camped outside Rome, saw apparitions in the air that terrified him and made him turn back. In one version, the warning took the form of a hailstorm. Afterwards the Romans raised an altar to "Rediculus Tutanus", the god who turned back and protected.

Others derive the name from ridiculus, meaning laughable: Hannibal's humiliation supposedly led the Romans to build a temple to the god of laughter. Varro puts the epithet Tutanus, protector, in the god's own mouth.

Worship and religion

Travellers leaving the city by the Appian Way would pray at the temple before setting out. Pliny the Elder places it on the opposite side of the road.

Legacy

A monument in the Parco della Caffarella, often mistakenly called the Temple of Deus Rediculus, is also confused with the Cenotaph of Regilla.

Ancient sources: Festus, Varro (Saturae Menippeae) and Pliny the Elder (Natural History).

Trivia

A famous crow. Pliny the Elder tells of a cobbler in the Roman Forum who had a tame crow that became a favourite among young Romans. When a rival of the cobbler killed it, the rival was executed and the bird received a public funeral in the field of Rediculus.
