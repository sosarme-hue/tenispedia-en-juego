Amazon queen of Greek mythology

Antiope

Antiope is an Amazon queen of Greek mythology, daughter of Ares and sister of Hippolyta, Melanippe and Penthesilea. Her story, tied to Theseus and to the war between the Amazons and Athens, is one of the best-known episodes in the cycle of Theseus.

The myth

In the myth, Antiope was a daughter of Ares and co-ruled the Amazons with her sisters. When Heracles was set his ninth labour, to win the girdle of Hippolyta, he set out with Theseus against the Amazons. According to the tradition, Hippolyta handed over the girdle, either willingly or under pressure from the abduction of Melanippe, but Theseus carried off Antiope, or Hippolyta according to other authors, and made her his lover.

The Amazons then attacked Athens to rescue her, and were eventually defeated there. Some versions say that Antiope fought with them and died from an arrow shot by her companion Molpadia, to spare her from being abused by the Athenians. Other authors hold that the captive was Hippolyta, who married Theseus and bore him a son, Hippolytus. When Theseus abandoned his wife to marry Phaedra, the wronged wife, whether Antiope or Hippolyta, plotted revenge by attacking the wedding with the Amazons, but was killed, according to an oracle, by Theseus, or by Penthesilea.

Legacy

Antiope appears in modern literature and film: the Italian film War Goddess (1973) tells her story with Theseus, and Evangeline Walton's novel The Sword Is Forged (1983) retells it. The DC Comics character Antiope, aunt of Wonder Woman, takes her name, and Robin Wright played the character in the film Wonder Woman (2017).

Ancient sources: Pausanias recounts her story, including the tomb shown in Athens, and Ovid mentions her death at the hands of Theseus while she was pregnant. Giovanni Boccaccio devoted a chapter to her in Famous Women.

Trivia

Tomb in Athens. According to Pausanias, the tombs of both Antiope and Molpadia were shown in Athens.
An unrequited love. In one tale, the sailor Solois fell for Antiope on the voyage to Athens. She rejected him respectfully, and he drowned in a river in Bithynia.
