daughter of the river god Asopus in Greek myth

Thespia

Thespia is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Asopus, whose name is linked to the Boeotian city of Thespiae, not far from Thebes. According to tradition, her story explains the origin of that town.

The myth

According to tradition, Thespia was the daughter of the river god Asopus and Metope, herself a daughter of the river god Ladon. Like her sisters, she was carried off by the gods, though the accounts differ on who was responsible: in one version, Aphrodite and Eros arranged the abduction; in another, Apollo took her to Thespiae, the city west of Thebes.

The name of the city was connected with her. Pausanias records two explanations: some said Thespia gave her name to the city, while others credited Thespius, a descendant of Erechtheus who was said to have come from Athens.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records Thespia's link to the city of Thespiae and the different accounts of its name.
