river god of the Strymon in Greek mythology

Strymon

Strymon is the river god of the river that bears his name, which flows along the border of Thrace and Macedonia, and in Greek myth he appears as a king of Thrace. He matters because his descendants link several figures of the tradition, from the Trojan-allied Rhesus to Evadne, wife of King Argus.

The myth

In Greek tradition, Strymon was a son of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, sharing with them the status of primordial deities of the waters. As king of Thrace, he had many descendants, though the sources do not fully agree on the mothers of his children.

According to the reference text, he fathered Rhesus by one of the Muses, named as Euterpe, Calliope or Terpsichore. His other sons were Olynthus and Brangas. By the nymph Neaera he had a daughter, Evadne, who became the wife of King Argus, and Tereine, mother of Thrassa by the god Ares. Another daughter, Rhodope, bore Athos to Poseidon.
