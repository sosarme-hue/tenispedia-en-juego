Hindu hero, son of Krishna and reincarnation of Kamadeva

Pradyumna

Pradyumna is a hero of Hindu mythology, the son of the god Krishna and Rukmini. According to the Bhagavata Purana he is the reincarnation of Kamadeva, the god of love, and one of the best-known figures of the Krishna cycle.

The myth

According to the tradition, Kamadeva was reduced to ashes by Shiva's wrath and was promised a new birth as Krishna's son. Pradyumna was born in Dvaraka, but within days he was kidnapped by the asura Shambara, who feared that the child would destroy him. He was thrown into the sea, swallowed by a great fish, and found alive when the fish was caught and cut open in the asura's kitchen.

He was raised by Mayavati, in truth the incarnation of Rati, who revealed his identity to him. When he grew up, she fell in love with him; Pradyumna refused, and after learning his origin he challenged Shambara and killed him. He returned to Dvaraka with Mayavati, and Krishna recognised him. Later he abducted Rati, daughter of King Bhimaka-Sena, and they had several children, the eldest of whom was Aniruddha. Tradition holds that he and Aniruddha died in a drunken brawl among the Yadavas, shortly before the island sank.

Worship and religion

His name is among the twenty-four keshava namas, names of Vishnu recited in most Hindu pujas, or acts of worship. Some Hindus regard him as an expansion of Vishnu, although the sixteenth-century Gosvamis of Vrindavan placed him on a different level, as an avatar of a soul-type deity.

Legacy

In Jain tradition he is Kamadeva and is called Makaraketu, 'he whose banner is the makara'. A pillar capital at Besnagar bearing a makara, the mythical crocodile, is attributed to him, and the Mahabharata links the makara with him and with the god of love. His epithet Makaradhvaja and the Jain Pradyumna Charitra keep his adventures alive.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions him for the first time in Sanskrit literature; the Bhagavata Purana and the Harivamsa narrate his legends in detail.

Trivia

A name of power. His name is explained as 'the preeminently powerful', formed from pra, meaning increase, and diumna, meaning splendour, strength or majesty.
A unique word. Pradyumna is the only word in Sanskrit written in Devanagari that contains the three conjunct letters known as yada-aksara.
