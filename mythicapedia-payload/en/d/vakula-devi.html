foster mother of the Hindu god Venkateswara

Vakula Devi

Vakula Devi is, in the Hindu tradition of Tirumala, the foster mother of the god Venkateswara, a form of Vishnu. Her worship centres on a temple in Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh.

The myth

According to the legend of Tirumala, the story goes back to the Dvapara Yuga. Yashoda, foster mother of Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, complained to him that she could not witness his wedding to Rukmini, an avatar of Lakshmi. Krishna promised that in the Kali Yuga she would see his marriage to a form of Lakshmi.

The promise was kept in the Kali Yuga. Vishnu took the form of Venkateswara, and Yashoda was reborn as Vakula Devi, his foster mother. As the god had vowed, she arranged her foster son's wedding to Padmavati, foster daughter of King Akasha Raja and Queen Dharani, who was a form of Lakshmi.

Worship and religion

The Vakula Devi Temple stands in the city of Tirupati and was built so that her image faces the seven hills where Venkateswara resides. By tradition, the consecrated food is offered first to Vakula Devi before the god at Tirumala. According to the source, the shrine was destroyed in the 17th century and restored between 2011 and 2022, when it was reopened.

Trivia

Facing the seven hills. The temple is designed so that Vakula Devi's image faces the seven hills of Tirumala, where her foster son lives.
A hill under threat. The hill on which the temple stands was illegally quarried for construction stone. It is said that as much as 80 percent of it was flattened.
