Minyan princess of Greek mythology

Chrysogeneia

Chrysogeneia is a Minyan princess of Greek mythology, daughter of King Almus of Orchomenus. Her name, which can mean "golden", reflects the reputation for wealth enjoyed by the Orchomenians.

The myth

According to tradition, Chrysogeneia was the sister of Chryse and bore Chryses to the sea-god Poseidon. Chryses became the father of the eponymous Minyas, ancestor who gave his name to the Minyans.

In some accounts, however, Minyas was instead the direct son of Chrysogone and Poseidon, showing that genealogies varied from one version to another. The myth thus places her at the origin of the Minyan lineage of Orchomenus, a city whose prosperity was credited to this line.

She should not be confused with Chrysogenia, a naiad and daughter of the river-god Peneus, who bore Thissaeus to Zeus. The two share a name but belong to different stories.

Trivia

Two namesakes. The name Chrysogenia also belongs to a naiad, daughter of the river-god Peneus and mother of Thissaeus by Zeus. She is not the same character.
A name meaning gold. Her name can be rendered as "golden". According to traditional opinion, it expresses the wealth of the people of Orchomenus.
