king of Sicyon of the Inachid line

Telchis

Telchis is a minor figure in Greek mythology, linked to the Inachid lineage. Some sources make him the third king of Sicyon, while others present him as an Argive companion of Thelxion, most likely two variants of the same character.

The myth

In the Sicyonian genealogy, Telchis was the son of Europs and the father of Apis, and he ruled Sicyon for twenty years. This version makes his mother Europe, daughter of Aegialeus, and presents Apis as his heir; Thelxion was later born to Apis. Eusebius places him as the third king of the Sicyonians and adds that his reign overlapped with that of Semiramis.

The Argive genealogy tells a different story. In it, Phoroneus fathered Apis and Niobe with the nymph Teledice, and Apis, having become a tyrant, gave the Peloponnese the name Apia. Telchis and Thelxion plotted against him, deposed him and killed him. Apis died without offspring and was deified as Sarapis, though the myth also says Argus Panoptes later avenged his death on the two conspirators.

Ancient sources: Eusebius of Caesarea preserves the list of kings of Sicyon, and accounts of the Inachid lineage appear in Argive and Sicyonian traditions transmitted by ancient authors.
