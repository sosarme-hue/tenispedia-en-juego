hero of feasts in Greek myth

Deipneus

Deipneus is a hero of Achaea in Greek tradition, associated with banquets and shared meals. His name is explained by that connection to feasting, which is why he has a place in the cult of heroes linked to the table.

The myth

Deipneus appears in ancient sources as a hero linked to banquets, and his name is explained by them. The testimonies are scarce and do not recount any deeds of his own. According to Polemon, in Sparta cooks erected statues of the heroes Maton ("Kneader") and Ceraon ("Mixer") in the communal dining halls, while in Munichia Acratopotes, "Drinker of Unmixed Wine", was honored. Deipneus belongs to this same group of figures tied to food, although his story is left undeveloped.

Some traditions also connect him with bread-making, which widens his role as a guardian of the shared table.

Worship and religion

He was honored in Achaea, a region of the Peloponnese, as a hero connected with banquets. No detailed descriptions of his rites survive.

Ancient sources: Polemon, whose account of heroes linked to communal meals survives in later sources.

Trivia

Statues in the dining halls. According to Polemon, in Sparta cooks raised statues of heroes such as Maton and Ceraon in the communal dining halls.
