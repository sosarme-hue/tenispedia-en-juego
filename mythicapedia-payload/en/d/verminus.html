Roman god protecting cattle from disease

Verminus

Verminus was a minor god of Roman religion who protected cattle from disease. His cult is linked to infections caused by worms, a problem that troubled both animals and people.

The myth

Few stories survive about Verminus, so his figure is known mainly through his role. Tradition connects him with the care of herds and the protection of livestock from illness.

Some researchers believe he may have been inherited from the Indigetes, a people of the Iberian Peninsula, although this remains an unconfirmed hypothesis.

Worship and religion

An altar dedicated to Verminus was set up in 151 BC by the consul or duovir Aulus Postumius Albinus. It was discovered in 1876 and is now housed in the Antiquarium Comunale in Rome. Another altar to the god was found on the Viminal Hill, also in Rome. A 2nd century inscription to him has been read as a reaction to increased worm infections among humans.

Legacy

The Spanish veterinary scientist M. Cordero del Campillo argued that this inscription instead reflected an epidemic infectious disease affecting both humans and animals. This is a modern interpretation rather than a cultural legacy of the god himself.

Ancient sources: He is known only from Latin inscriptions, such as his altars, and no ancient literary texts are known to mention him.

Trivia

Worms or epidemic. A 2nd century inscription was read as a response to worm infections, but a Spanish veterinary scientist proposed that it reflected an epidemic affecting both people and animals.
Two altars in Rome. Besides the altar found in 1876, another altar to the god was discovered on the Viminal Hill, in Rome itself.
