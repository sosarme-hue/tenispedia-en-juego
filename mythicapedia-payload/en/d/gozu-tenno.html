Japanese plague deity, syncretized with Susanoo

Gozu Tennō

Gozu Tennō is a syncretic Japanese deity linked to epidemics and, at the same time, to protection from them and to healing. Arriving in Japan from mainland Asia, he eventually merged with the native kami Susanoo, the stormy god of classical Japanese mythology.

The myth

The origins of the cult are unclear. One theory places him as a minor Buddhist deity, protector of the Jetavana monastery, whose Sanskrit name might be Gavagrīva, "ox-necked", and who reached Japan through Tibet and China. Another theory proposes a Korean origin.

In the legend of Somin Shōrai, the god Mutō, arriving from the northern sea, stays at the house of a poor man after the rich brother refuses him lodging. In return, Mutō gives the family a magical means to escape future epidemics and punishes the rich man. In the oldest version, from the Nara period, Mutō reveals himself as Susanoo, showing the two were already conflated in the 8th century. That version also has Mutō marry the daughter of the god of the southern seas.

The Nihon Shoki tells how Susanoo, banished from Takamagahara, came down to Silla and crossed from there to Japan. The epithet gozu, "ox-head", has been linked to the Korean place name Soshimori. His iconography shows a fierce man with an ox's head, sometimes with several arms and faces.

Worship and religion

Gozu Tennō was venerated in shrines of the Gion cult, such as Yasaka in Kyoto. After the Meiji separation of Shinto and Buddhism, many shrines officially declared their deity to be Susanoo.

Legacy

Hachiōji Castle in western Tokyo takes its name from the eight children that tradition attributes to Gozu Tennō and his consort Harisaijo.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Somin Shōrai legend, whose earliest version dates from the Nara period, and the Nihon Shoki (720) recounts Susanoo's arrival in Silla.

Trivia

Ox head. His name gozu means "ox-head", and his iconography shows him with an ox's head.
A many-headed god. Some statues give him three, four or even five faces and as many as twelve arms.
