Mesopotamian goddess, wife of Tishpak

Ukulla

Ukulla is a Mesopotamian goddess regarded as the wife of Tishpak and the mother of Nanshak. Her cult was centered mainly in the city of Eshnunna, where she was one of the most important deities of the community.

The myth

The sources present her as the companion of Tishpak and the mother of Nanshak, another god in the same tradition. Her origin is unclear: according to Frans Wiggermann, her name derives neither from Sumerian nor Akkadian, and the same has been suggested for Tishpak and Nanshak.

In some sources she also appears as the wife of Ninazu, though this is a minority tradition. A late hymn dedicated to Bau describes her as one who "creates meadows" and "surveys the universe", an interpretation that seems to offer a scholarly explanation of her name. A seal shows her alongside her husband, and she was called the "queen of Eshunna".

Worship and religion

Eshnunna was her main cult center, where she had two separate shrines. One of them, the Ganun, may have been located within the temple of Tishpak, the Esikila. The kings of Eshnunna took part in her worship, as shown by her presence in their year names and by offerings of oil and beer made on behalf of the royal palace. A month in the local calendar used in Eshnunna and Me-Turan bore her name. Settlements named after her, such as Dūr-Ukulla, are also known.

Ancient sources: She appears in Old Babylonian texts, such as a late hymn to Bau (KAR 109) and a balag song from Me-Turan.
