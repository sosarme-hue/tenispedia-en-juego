Rhodian princess and queen in Greek myth

Cydippe

Cydippe is a Rhodian princess, daughter of King Ochimus, and mother of the three founders of the island's cities. A figure of Greek mythology, her story is tied to Rhodes and explains how several of its places got their names.

The myth

According to the myth, Ochimus promised his daughter to Ocridion, but her uncle Cercaphus, who was in love with her, had the herald escorting the bride hand her over to him instead. Afterwards, heralds were barred from entering Ocridion's shrine.

Cercaphus fled with Cydippe, and they did not return to Rhodes until Ochimus was an old man. When the king died, Cercaphus inherited the throne and Cydippe became queen. They had three sons, Cameirus, Ialysus and Lindus, who founded cities named after themselves and divided the island into three regions. In another version, Ocridion did marry her, but Cercaphus abducted her.

Legacy

The Rhodian painter Protogenes, born in Caria, made Cydippe the subject of one of his paintings.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus describes the great flood that buried Cyrbe, the city that bore her name.
