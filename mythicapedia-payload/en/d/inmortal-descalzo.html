Barefoot Immortal

The Barefoot Immortal, also called the Barefoot Master, is a Taoist deity of Chinese religion, known for never wearing shoes and for his friendly smile. He appears often in Chinese opera and classic novels such as Journey to the West, so he is a familiar figure in Chinese popular imagination.

The myth

According to tradition, his original name was Liu Hai, before he became known as the Barefoot Immortal and also as Liu Cao. In Journey to the West he appears as an innocent god: the Monkey King tricks him with a false order from the Jade Emperor, takes his identity and ruins the Peach Banquet in heaven.

A legend from Shaxian in Fujian tells that, after Pangu created the world, he stubbed his foot on a cliff. He decided to plough the mountain with a divine ox to protect travellers, but the Mountain God tricked him with a false dream about his sick mother. When he returned to heaven, the ox was left unguarded and was killed with cymbals and a gong.

Legacy

He appears in Chinese opera and classic novels such as Journey to the West and Outlaws of the Marsh, and has become part of the country's popular folklore.

Trivia

A name with history. Before being known as the Barefoot Immortal, he was called Liu Hai, and he also appears as Liu Cao.
The Cymbal Peak. According to the Shaxian legend, the mountain where the ox was killed was renamed Cymbal and Gong Peak.
