Aztec goddess of steam baths

Temazcalteci

Temazcalteci is the Aztec goddess of steam baths, known as temazcalli, and her name combines the Nahuatl words for 'steam bath' and 'grandmother'. According to Sahagún, she was identified with Toci, the goddess of medicine.

The myth

Texts preserve little of her own story. Her name, built from temazcalli ('sweat bath') and tecitl ('grandmother'), links her directly to these baths, which in Aztec culture were used to purify the body and treat ailments. The suffix tecitl suggests the image of a protective grandmother, although the sources do not detail her kinship with other deities.

Sahagún explains that, for physicians, she was the same as Toci, so her figure blended with that of the goddess of medicine.

Worship and religion

Physicians venerated her as Toci. People who had a temazcal in their homes also worshipped her within the domestic sphere. This bond with the household makes her a deity close to everyday life.

Ancient sources: Friar Bernardino de Sahagún records her figure in the sixteenth century, describing Toci and the practices of physicians and of those who had a temazcal at home.
