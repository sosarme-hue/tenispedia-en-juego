Greek hero, leader of the Argonauts

Jason

Jason is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Aeson and rightful heir to the throne of Iolcos, and the central figure of the voyage of the Argonauts in search of the Golden Fleece. His story, preserved in ancient literature, made him one of the best-known heroes of Greece.

The myth

Jason was the son of Aeson, king of Iolcos in Thessaly. His half-brother Pelias seized the throne and killed many of Aeson's descendants. According to the tale, his mother saved the infant by pretending he had been stillborn, and the boy was raised by the centaur Chiron. When Jason grew up and arrived in Iolcos, he had lost a sandal while crossing a river after helping an old woman, who was in fact the goddess Hera. Pelias, warned by an oracle that a man wearing only one sandal would overthrow him, set him an almost impossible task: bring back the Golden Fleece, the golden hide of a fabulous ram, from Colchis.

Jason gathered a band of heroes, the Argonauts, and with Athena's help built the ship Argo. Along the way, tradition remembers episodes such as the stay on the island of Lemnos, where the queen Hypsipyle fell in love with him, and the passage through the Clashing Rocks, which he crossed by following advice given by Phineus. In Colchis, King Aeetes imposed tasks on him: yoking two fire-breathing bulls, ploughing a field and sowing dragon's teeth, from which armed warriors sprang up, and overcoming the serpent that guarded the fleece. He succeeded thanks to Medea, the king's daughter, who fell in love with him and provided a potion and advice. They fled together with the fleece, and during the escape Medea killed her brother Apsyrtus; ancient versions differ about how it happened.

Back in Iolcos, Jason handed the fleece to Pelias, but the king's death came through Medea, who convinced his daughters that she could restore his youth if they cut him into pieces and boiled him. They did so, and the king died. These episodes form the heart of Jason's myth, with its mix of feats, betrayals and love.

Legacy

Jason and the Argonauts have had a strong presence in literature: the epic poem Argonautica and the tragedy Medea treat the story from different angles. On screen, the 1963 film Jason and the Argonauts and a 2000 television miniseries of the same name brought his story to a wide audience.

Ancient sources: The Argonautica, an ancient epic poem narrating the voyage of the Argonauts and the winning of the Golden Fleece.

Trivia

A lost sandal. According to the tale, Jason lost a sandal while crossing a river, so he arrived in Iolcos wearing only one shoe, which alarmed his uncle Pelias.
An island of women. The island of Lemnos was inhabited only by women, who had been punished by Aphrodite; there the Argonauts joined them before setting sail.
