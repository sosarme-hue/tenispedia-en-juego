Norse goddess

Ilmr

Ilmr is a Norse goddess known only by her name. She appears in lists of ásynjur and in skaldic poetry, but her nature and functions remain unknown.

The myth

The sources record no stories about Ilmr. Her name appears in the Nafnaþulur, lists appended to the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, where she is placed between Iðunn and Bil among the ásynjur, and in a list of words usable in poetic kennings for "woman". She does not appear in the Poetic Edda, but her name occurs several times in skaldic poetry of the 10th and 11th centuries, especially in verses by Kormákr Ǫgmundarson.

Some scholars think she may have been a valkyrie, since Kormákr used valkyrie names in his kennings, and in another verse her name appears in a battle kenning that is otherwise attested only with valkyries. Jacob Grimm linked her name to the masculine word ilmr, "pleasant scent", a feature without parallel among Norse deities. In 1989 Ásgeir Blöndal Magnússon proposed other readings: a link with jalmr, "noise", or with the elm, almr, which would make her a tree dís.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda (Nafnaþulur appended to the Skáldskaparmál) and skaldic poetry of the 10th and 11th centuries, especially verses by Kormákr Ǫgmundarson.

Trivia

A name with scent. The feminine name Ilmr resembles the masculine ilmr, "pleasant scent". According to Grimm, no other Norse deity is associated with smell.
Perhaps a valkyrie. A battle kenning using her name is attested only with valkyrie names, which suggests she may have been considered one of them.
