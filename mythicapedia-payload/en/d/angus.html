Irish god of love and youth

Aengus

Aengus, also called Aengus Óg or Mac Óc, is a god of the Tuatha Dé Danann linked to love, youth, summer and poetry. He is best known for his story of love for Caer Ibormeith, one of the most remembered tales in the Irish mythological cycle.

The myth

According to tradition, Aengus was born from the secret union of the Dagda and Boann, goddess of the River Boyne. To hide the affair from Elcmar, Boann's husband, the Dagda made the sun stand still for nine months. Aengus was raised by Midir, who acted as his stepfather while also being his half-brother. Later, exploiting an ambiguity in Old Irish, he asked for the palace of Brú na Bóinne for «a day and a night» and claimed it forever.

The most famous story concerns a maiden he saw in his dreams. Aengus fell ill with love, and after years of searching by Boann, the Dagda and King Bov the Red, she was found at the Lake of the Dragon's Mouth. She was Caer Ibormeith, one of a hundred and fifty girls chained in pairs, who every second year spent a year as swans. Aengus had to recognise her in swan form; he managed it by turning himself into a swan too, and the two flew away singing a melody that put anyone who heard it to sleep for three days and nights.

In another tale, Aengus restores to life Dermot, who had been killed by a wild boar of Ben Bulben.

Ancient sources: The Wooing of Etain, the medieval Dindsenchas and the Life of St Columba, which records an early form of his name, mention him.

Trivia

A melody that sends you to sleep. The song sung by Aengus and Caer put anyone who heard it to sleep for three days and three nights.
Compared with Hermes. Some scholars have pointed to similarities between Aengus and the Greek god Hermes.
A palace named after him. Brú na Bóinne came to be called Brug maic ind Óig in his honour.
