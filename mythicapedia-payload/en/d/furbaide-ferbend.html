Irish hero of the Ulster Cycle

Furbaide Ferbend

Furbaide Ferbend is a hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, son of King Conchobar mac Nessa. He is remembered for his beauty, the horns on his head, and the revenge he took on Queen Medb.

The myth

According to tradition, his mother, a daughter of High King Eochu Feidlech, was murdered by her sister Medb, Conchobar's former wife, and the boy was born by posthumous Caesarean section. Cath Boinde says he was originally called Diarmaid and explains the name Furbaide from Old Irish urbad, 'cutting'. The Dindsenchas describes him as smooth-skinned with two horns, hence his epithet ferbend, 'horned man'.

At seventeen he fought in the battle of Gáirech and Ilgáirech, at the end of the Táin Bó Cúailnge. According to Aided Meidbe, as an adult he avenged his mother: he measured the distance to Medb's pool on the Shannon, practised with his sling, and when he saw her bathing again, he threw a piece of cheese at her and killed her.

Ancient sources: Cath Boinde, the Dindsenchas poem Carn Furbaide, Cóir Anmann, Mesca Ulad and Aided Meidbe, medieval Irish texts of the Ulster Cycle.

Trivia

Helmet with horns. The glossary Cóir Anmann places his horns on his helmet: two of silver and one of gold.
Original name. Cath Boinde states that his original name was Diarmaid, and that Furbaide derives from a word meaning 'cutting'.
