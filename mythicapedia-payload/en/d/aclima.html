Cain's twin sister in extra-biblical traditions

Aclima

Aclima is, in several Jewish, Christian and Muslim traditions, the eldest daughter of Adam and Eve and Cain's twin sister. Her story answers an old question: where the first men found their wives.

The myth

According to extra-biblical accounts, Adam and Eve had twins: Cain with a sister called Aclima, and Abel with Azura. Adam wanted each brother to marry the other's twin, but Cain refused the arrangement. To settle the matter, they agreed on a sacrifice that God would judge; when God rejected Cain's offering, Cain killed Abel in jealousy.

Other sources call her Kalmana, Lusia or Cainan, and in the Book of Jubilees Cain's wife is named Awan.

Legacy

Her name, as Calmana, appears in the poem Aurora by Petrus Riga and in Peter Comestor's Historia Scholastica, a standard textbook of biblical education for centuries.

Ancient sources: The Book of Jubilees, the Cave of Treasures and the Jewish work Seder Hadorot name her; Muslim tradition also records her.
