Greek river god of the Peneus, in Thessaly

Peneus

Peneus is the river god of the river that flows through Thessaly. Son of Oceanus and Tethys, he was considered the ancestor of the Lapiths and the father of Daphne, the nymph turned into a laurel who became sacred to Apollo.

The myth

In Greek tradition, Peneus was one of the three thousand rivers, children of Oceanus and Tethys. His waters run through Thessaly, and several lineages were traced to him. With the nymph Creusa he fathered Hypseus, king of the Lapiths, and with an unnamed partner he had a daughter, Stilbe, who bore Centaurus and Lapithes to Apollo, eponyms of the Centaurs and the Lapiths. Accounts differ: some make Cyrene his daughter, others his granddaughter through Hypseus.

The best-known episode is that of Daphne. Ovid tells how Eros, angered by Apollo mocking his archery, shot the god with a golden arrow to make him fall in love with Peneus's daughter, and struck her with a leaden one so she would reject him. Pursued by Apollo, Daphne begged her father for help, and he turned her into a laurel, a tree that became sacred to the god. In an Arcadian version, however, Daphne was the daughter of the river Ladon.

Ancient sources: Ovid recounts the story of Daphne in the Metamorphoses, and other ancient authors mention him in their genealogies of rivers and Thessalian lineages.
