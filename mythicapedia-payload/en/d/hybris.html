Greek personification of insolence and arrogance

Hybris

Hybris is, in Greek mythology, the spirit and personification of insolence, the excessive arrogance that leads to defying the natural order and disrespecting the gods, which in turn brings divine punishment. Her figure is often read as a moral warning against a lack of measure. She barely appears in the stories as a character in her own right, and her role is understood mainly through her offspring.

The myth

According to Aeschylus, her mother is Dyssebeia, the personification of impiety. The mythographer Apollodorus presents her as the mother of Pan, son of Zeus, to whom he attributes teaching Apollo the art of prophecy.

It remains unclear whether this Pan is the same god of shepherds and flocks, who was usually said to be the son of Hermes. The ancient sources do not offer a full account of his deeds or his end.

Ancient sources: Aeschylus names her as the daughter of Dyssebeia, and Apollodorus as the mother of Pan.

Trivia

Hybris or Hubris. The name appears in sources with two spellings, Hybris and Hubris. The second is the form that reached English with the sense of excessive pride.
A Pan of uncertain identity. The Pan who is Hybris's son in Apollodorus may not be the usual shepherd god, a point scholars leave open.
