Chinese goddess of weaving and the star Vega

Zhinü

Zhinü is the Chinese goddess of weaving and the star Vega. Alongside the cowherd Niulang, she stars in one of China's best-loved folk tales, a love story divided by the heavens.

The myth

According to tradition, Zhinü was the youngest daughter of the Jade Emperor and wove her father's royal robes out of clouds. One day she came down to Earth to bathe in a river, and the cowherd Niulang stole her clothes, so she could not return to heaven and married him instead. She wove at home and cared for their children while he farmed. When the Jade Emperor discovered the union, he ordered her brought back. Niulang tried to follow with his sons, helped by his ox, a former god of cattle punished for breaking heaven's laws, but the Queen Mother of the West created the River of Heaven, the Milky Way, separating them forever. Zhinü became Vega and Niulang became Altair, and they can meet only once a year, when magpies form a bridge.

Worship and religion

That day is celebrated as the Qixi Festival, often called China's Valentine's Day.

Legacy

The phrase "Niulang Zhinü" is still used today to describe loving married couples. The 1920s Folklore Movement chose the story as one of China's Four Great Folktales, alongside the Legend of the White Snake, Lady Meng Jiang, and Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai.
