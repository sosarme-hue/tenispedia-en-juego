Celtic god of the Douro river

Durius

Durius, also known as Durio, was a god worshipped by the peoples of the Iberian Peninsula, the personification of the Douro river. He was usually shown holding a fishing net, and his name is linked to a Celtic root that likely meant "water".

The myth

Little narrative survives about Durius. Following the Celtic habit of seeing rivers as spirits, he was understood as the river itself, so his figure and its course were one and the same.

He is usually depicted with a fishing net, an attribute that ties him to the life the water sustains. His name comes from the Celtic root dubro-, which in turn points to dur, probably meaning "water".

Worship and religion

He was venerated among the tribes living along the banks of the Douro. A shrine existed near Porto in Roman times, showing that his worship lasted and was absorbed into the Roman world.

Legacy

His presence survives mainly in place names and in the name of the river itself. Beyond that, the sources record no notable presence in later art or literature.

Ancient sources: Ancient references come mainly from inscriptions and remains of the Porto shrine rather than from major literary works.

Trivia

A shrine near Porto. A shrine dedicated to this deity existed in the vicinity of Porto in Roman times.
A river as a god. As with other Celtic peoples, the river was seen as a spiritual being, and the god was its personification.
