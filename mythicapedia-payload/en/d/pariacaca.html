Inca god of water, winds and torrential rains

Pariacaca

Pariacaca is, in Incan and pre-Incan mythology of the central Peruvian highlands, the god of water, winds and torrential rains, and also a creator god.

The myth

According to the Huarochirí Manuscript, Pariacaca hatched with his brothers from one of five eggs on the summit of the sacred mountain Kuntur Quta. To punish a rich man who had declared himself a god, he unleashed a great flood. Earlier, disguised as a beggar, he visited a festival where only one compassionate woman welcomed him; she and her family were saved by fleeing to higher ground.

In another version, Pariacaca is the father of Huathiacuri. He is also said to have defeated the fire god Huallallo Carhuincho, who demanded human sacrifices: his rains put out the rival's fireballs and condemned him to eat carrion. From then on water ruled and human sacrifices ceased.

Worship and religion

Animals, mullu shells and coca leaves were offered to him in place of human victims.

Ancient sources: The Huarochirí Manuscript, whose first chapter describes his life.

Trivia

A five-part god. The sources describe Pariacaca as a god who divides into five atmospheric deities, each linked to a direction of the cosmos.
A trinity with Christian echoes. The chronicler Guamán Poma de Ayala described Illapa as a triple deity, and researchers think this version may have sought parallels with the Holy Trinity.
