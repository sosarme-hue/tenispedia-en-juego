hero and magician of Welsh mythology

Lleu Llaw Gyffes

Lleu Llaw Gyffes is a warrior and magician of Welsh mythology, the central figure of the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi. His name is considered the Welsh counterpart of the Irish Lugh and the Gaulish Lugus.

The myth

According to the Mabinogi, Lleu is born to Arianrhod, sister of the magician Gwydion, after a virginity test set by King Math fab Mathonwy. The tale tells that Gwydion secretly raises the baby in a chest, and that his mother later lays three tynghedau, or fates, upon him: that only she can give him a name, that only she can arm him, and that he will never have a human wife. Gwydion outwits his sister on the first two.

To get around the third, Gwydion makes Blodeuwedd from flowers and gives her to Lleu as his wife. She takes up with Gronw Pebr, lord of Penllyn, and the two plot his death. Lleu reveals that he can only be killed at dusk, with one foot on a cauldron and one on a goat, by a spear made over a year. Struck, he turns into an eagle, but Gwydion brings him back to human form. Lleu later kills Gronw with a spear that pierces a stone placed in front of him, and Blodeuwedd is turned into an owl.

Legacy

He appears in early Welsh poetry, such as the poem Prif Gyuarch Taliessin, and is named among the three Golden Shoemakers and the three Red Ravagers of the Island of Britain. His name is linked to the Irish Lugh and the Gaulish Lugus.

Ancient sources: The Mabinogi (especially the Fourth Branch), early Welsh poems in the Book of Taliesin, and medieval Welsh genealogies.
