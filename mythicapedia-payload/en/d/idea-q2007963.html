Greek nymph, wife of the river-god Scamander

Idaea

Idaea is a Greek nymph, wife of the river-god Scamander and a principal ancestor of the royal house of Troy. She was presumably linked to Mount Ida in the Troad, in western Anatolia.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus and the mythographer Apollodorus, Idaea bore Teucer to the river-god Scamander, and Teucer was the first to rule as king over the region later known as Troy. Apollodorus adds two daughters from the same union, Callirrhoe and Strymo. Callirrhoe married Tros, the eponymous hero of Troy and the Trojans, while Strymo became the wife of Laomedon, king of Troy and father of Priam, who ruled during the Trojan War.

In this way the nymph appears as one of the roots of the Trojan royal line, linking its kings with water and mountain. Her importance is mainly genealogical, since her figure connects the rulers of Troy with the river and the mountain from which they were said to descend.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus and the mythographer Apollodorus name her as the mother of Teucer and other children of Scamander.

Trivia

Scamander's daughters. Apollodorus names Callirrhoe and Strymo as daughters of Scamander. Both married key figures of the Trojan line: Tros and Laomedon.
A name tied to the mountain. Her name, Idaía, means 'she who comes from Ida' or 'she who lives on Ida', which explains her link to the mountain.
