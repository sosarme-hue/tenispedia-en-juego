Hindu goddess of the eternal flame

Jwala Ji

Jwala is a Hindu goddess whose physical manifestation is a set of eternal flames, most famously at the shrine of Jawalamukhi in the Indian Himalayas. She matters because she links the worship of sacred fire with the story of Sati and the Shaktipeeths, the shrines of the goddess.

The myth

According to Hindu legend, in ancient times demons ruled the Himalayas and harassed the gods. Vishnu led the gods against them, and their combined strength made huge flames rise from the ground. From that fire a young girl was born, regarded as Adishakti, the first Shakti.

This girl was Sati, who grew up in the house of Daksha and became Shiva's consort. When her father insulted Shiva, she could not bear it and took her own life. Enraged, Shiva wandered the three worlds carrying her body. The frightened gods asked Vishnu for help, and his Sudarshana chakra cut the body into pieces. Where the fragments fell, the fifty-one Shaktipeeths arose.

According to the tradition, Sati's tongue fell at Jawalamukhi, where the flames represent it. A cowherd discovered that one cow never gave milk; following her, he saw a girl drinking the milk, who vanished in a flash of light. The king later found the holy flame and had a temple built there. Another legend tells how the Mughal emperor Akbar tried in vain to extinguish the fire, and how a golden parasol he offered turned into an unknown metal.

Worship and religion

The temple at Jawalamukhi, in Kangra district of Himachal Pradesh, is the best-known shrine. A stone cavity holds the main flame, and a fair is held each year in July or August during Navratras. Pilgrims visit throughout the year. The worship remains alive; the shrine at Muktinath in Nepal is also sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists.

Legacy

In Cambodia the goddess is known as Reakyaksa Devi, protector of Tuesday and linked to the Khmer New Year. Her symbols are the bow and the trishula, and she is shown with two hands, riding a horse and dressed in purple.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions the Jwalamukhi temple and its figure with a face glowing like fire.

Trivia

Flames that will not die. According to tradition, Emperor Akbar tried to extinguish the flame with an iron disk and by diverting water, but the fire resisted.
Natural gas. At Muktinath, a little natural gas rises through a spring, giving the impression that the water itself is burning.
