form of the goddess Durga in Hinduism

Chandraghanta

Chandraghanta is the third of the nine forms of the Hindu goddess Durga, known collectively as the Navadurga. She is worshipped on the third day of Navaratri, and her name refers to the bell-shaped crescent moon on her forehead.

The myth

Ancient texts give her little narrative of her own. The Devī Kavaca, a protective text recited alongside the Durga Saptashati, names her as the third of the Navadurga and sets out the order of the nine forms, but it recounts no episodes about her. Her story is known mainly from modern devotional accounts.

Some of these link her to the wedding of Parvati and Shiva. In some versions, Parvati became known as Chandraghanta after wearing a crescent moon following her marriage; in others, she took an armed form when Shiva arrived at the wedding looking fearsome. The Shiva Purana tells of this wedding, with Shiva riding a bull, bearing five faces and his body smeared with ash, and with Mena, Parvati's mother, frightened and opposed to the match until Shiva assumes a radiant form. That account, however, does not identify Parvati with Chandraghanta.

Worship and religion

She is depicted with golden skin, ten arms, weapons including a sword and arrows, and riding a lion. Although shown ready for battle, she is described as benevolent towards her devotees, and the sound of her bell is said to frighten malevolent beings. The Nava Durga devotional booklet links her worship with the Manipura chakra. At the Chitra Ghanta Devi Temple in the Chowk area of Varanasi, she is identified with Chandraghanta, and a pilgrimage tied to the Kashi Khanda connects that shrine with Chitra Koop and Chitra Gupteshwar. Her worship continues today.

Legacy

Her image remains part of popular devotion, and some modern depictions show her riding a tiger instead of a lion. The writer Ramesh Menon retells the wedding of Shiva and Parvati in condensed form, including Mena's objection to Shiva's appearance.

Ancient sources: The Devī Kavaca names her as the third of the Navadurga, while the Shiva Purana narrates Shiva's wedding without naming her.

Trivia

A bell on her forehead. Her name roughly means « she who bears the bell-shaped moon ». The crescent moon on her forehead is shaped like a bell.
A sound that drives off evil. According to the Nava Durga booklet, the sound of her bell frightens malevolent beings.
