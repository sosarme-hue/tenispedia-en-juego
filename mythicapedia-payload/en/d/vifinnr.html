father of Hjúki and Bil in Norse mythology

Viðfinnr

Viðfinnr is a figure of Norse mythology, the father of Hjúki and Bil, two siblings whom tradition remembers for their strange rise up to the Moon. His name, in Old Norse, means 'wood-Finn' or 'forest of the Finns'.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning section, Viðfinnr is the father of Hjúki and Bil, a brother and a sister. The two went out to fetch water from the well Byrgir when Máni, the personified moon, lifted them up from the earth. From then on their story was tied to the night's luminary.

The surviving account says little more about Viðfinnr, who appears mainly as the father of his children. His role is understood mostly through his family's link to the Moon and to the water of that well.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda (Gylfaginning), which tells the story of Hjúki and Bil.

Trivia

A revealing name. The name Viðfinnr means 'wood-Finn' or 'forest of the Finns', a hint at how the Norse named their neighbours.
Children of the Moon. According to Gylfaginning, Máni took Hjúki and Bil as they fetched water, linking the children to the Moon.
