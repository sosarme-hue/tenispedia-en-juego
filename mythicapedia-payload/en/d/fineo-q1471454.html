Greek prince, uncle of Andromeda

Phineus

Phineus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Belus and brother of Cepheus. He appears in the story of Perseus and Andromeda as the rejected suitor who tried to seize the bride and ended turned to stone.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phineus had been betrothed to Andromeda, daughter of his brother Cepheus. After Perseus freed her from a sea monster sent as punishment for her mother Cassiopeia's boasting, he claimed her as his wife.

In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Phineus burst into the hall at the wedding feast, spear in hand, furious at losing the woman he regarded as his. Cepheus scolded him for doing nothing to save her, yet Phineus attacked regardless, and a bloody battle followed. Perseus raised the Gorgon's head and his enemies were turned to stone; only Phineus remained, pleading with averted gaze. Perseus brought the head before his eyes and petrified him too. Other versions present the affair as a plot against Perseus, and Euripides treated the story in his now-lost Andromeda.

Ancient sources: Ovid treats the story at length in the Metamorphoses, while Apollodorus gives it a single line in his Bibliotheca; it also featured in Euripides' lost Andromeda.
