daughter of Odysseus and Circe in Greek myth

Cassiphone

Cassiphone is a minor figure in Greek mythology, the daughter of Odysseus and the sorceress Circe. Her name means "brother killer", and she appears so rarely in the sources that her story mainly serves to expand the myth of her half-brother Telegonus.

The myth

Cassiphone does not appear in the Odyssey. Her story comes from obscure lines in Lycophron's Alexandra, clarified centuries later by the Byzantine scholar John Tzetzes in his commentaries.

According to Tzetzes, Odysseus spent a year with Circe, and their daughter Cassiphone was born, full sister of Telegonus. After Telegonus accidentally killed Odysseus, Circe revived him with a potion and married Cassiphone to Telemachus. Telemachus eventually killed Circe after a quarrel, and Cassiphone avenged her mother by killing him. Odysseus then died a second time, of grief. Another version in Tzetzes places the weddings in the Isles of the Blessed, where Telegonus married Penelope.

Ancient sources: She appears in Lycophron's Alexandra, and her story is known mainly from the twelfth-century commentaries of John Tzetzes.

Trivia

A telling name. Her name is a compound meaning "brother killer". According to Stanford, it may have been a nickname applied later because of her actions in the myth, and she may originally have had another name.
A lost sequel. Her story is linked to the Telegony, a lost sequel to the Odyssey in which Telegonus arrives in Ithaca in search of the father he never knew.
