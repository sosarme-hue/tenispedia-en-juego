Coyote

Coyote is a mythological character found in many Indigenous cultures of North America, sometimes in animal form and sometimes as a human-like being. Depending on the story, he appears as a creator, a benefactor of humankind, a trickster or a fool, and his figure matters for the wide range of roles that tales give him.

The myth

Stories about Coyote vary widely from one culture to another. In some he is a sacred being with creative powers; in others he is a malevolent and often comical trickster. Frequently he combines both roles. In a Maidu myth, Coyote and Earth Maker sing together to create the world, but Coyote then vows to spoil it, and Earth Maker eventually recognizes that Coyote's power equals his own.

In another group of tales, Coyote steals fire or the sun from the supernatural beings who keep them for themselves, becoming a benefactor of the people. His role as a trickster also leaves him open to being tricked. In Tongva tradition, for example, he challenges the river to a race, wins but collapses from exhaustion, and the river laughs at him.

Worship and religion

Among the Navajo, Coyote is the tutelary spirit of Coyoteway, a healing ceremony intended to restore the patient's harmony with Coyote and the world.

Legacy

Coyote appears in contemporary literature, such as the stories of Peter Blue Cloud, and in efforts to teach young people about Native American languages and cultures. The American organization COYOTE, founded in San Francisco in 1973 to defend sex workers, takes its name from the character's trickster role. He also seems to have influenced the Warner Bros. cartoon character Wile E. Coyote, known in Spanish as El Coyote.

Trivia

A name for a river. In Tongva mythology, the sound heard in the upper Arroyo Seco is explained as the river laughing at Coyote after he loses the race.
Coyote and Loki. Coyote has been compared with the Norse god Loki and with Prometheus, since both stole fire for humankind.
