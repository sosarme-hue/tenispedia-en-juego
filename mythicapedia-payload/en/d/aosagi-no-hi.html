blue heron fire of Japanese folklore

Aosagi-no-hi

The aosagi-no-hi ('blue heron fire') is a nocturnal phenomenon in Japanese folklore: a ball of bluish light that seems to drift at a distance. It belongs to the yōkai bestiary of the Edo period and still appears in modern legends.

The myth

According to the most widespread Edo-period explanation, these lights came from a heron whose wings and eyes glowed as it flew at night, which people mistook for a supernatural fire. Some accounts tell of an impoverished oil merchant who died in misery, after which strange lights were seen near his former business.

Another story describes a man who killed a glowing heron and ate it, giving rise to the rumour that he had dined on ghost stew. The bird was believed to have magical qualities, and animals were said not to fear it. In later legends, it was said to carry a burning branch in its beak.

Legacy

In the anime film Howl's Moving Castle (Howl no Ugoku Shiro), the protagonist Howl is cursed and turned into an aosagi-no-hi.

Ancient sources: Edo-period illustrated books such as Toriyama Sekien's Konjaku gazu zoku hyakki (1779) and Tōsanjin's Ehon hyaku monogatari (1841).

Trivia

Ghost stew. The rumour that a man from Yotsuya killed and ate a heron earned him the reputation of having eaten ghost stew.
Fire over the Tama River. In the Tokyo area, sightings have been reported of night herons apparently spewing fire over the surface of the Tama River.
