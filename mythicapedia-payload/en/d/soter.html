Greek personification of safety and salvation

Soter

Soter is, in Greek mythology, the personification or daimon of safety, preservation and deliverance from harm. His name means 'saviour' or 'deliverer'.

The myth

According to the Suda, Soter was the brother and husband of Praxidike, and by her the father of Ktesios, Arete and Homonoia. Mnaseas, in his treatise On Europe, gives him the same children, whom he calls the Praxidikai after their mother.

Another tradition, recorded by Aeschylus, makes him the husband of Peitharchia (Obedience) and the father of Eupraxia (Success). The Orphic Hymns identify Praxidike with Persephone, Soter with Zeus, and their daughters with the Erinyes.

Worship and religion

Soter and Ktesios were also cult titles of Zeus. The Greeks additionally worshipped Soteria, the personification of safety and salvation, who had a sanctuary at Patras, believed to have been founded by Eurypylos of Thessaly.

Ancient sources: The Suda, Mnaseas, Aeschylus and the Orphic Hymns.

Trivia

A concept turned into a god. Soter and Ktesios were both personified beings and epithets of Zeus. An abstract idea such as salvation could thus become a title of the chief god.
