Greek hero, inventor and victim of Odysseus

Palamedes

Palamedes is a Greek hero, son of Nauplius, famous for his cleverness and credited with inventing dice, numbers and part of the alphabet. His story is tied to the Trojan War and to his rivalry with Odysseus.

The myth

According to tradition, Palamedes joined the Greeks in the Trojan War after Paris abducted Helen. Ancient sources give his mother different names, Clymene, Hesione or Philyra, which shows that genealogies varied from one author to another.

His best-known episode pits him against Odysseus. Odysseus feigned madness by plowing with an ox and a donkey, but Palamedes placed the infant Telemachus in front of the plough and exposed the trick. Odysseus never forgave him. He forged a letter supposedly from Priam, hid gold in Palamedes' tent, and the army accused him of treason and stoned him to death. Other versions have him drowned while fishing or trapped in a well while searching for treasure. In a later Latin tradition, attributed to Dares the Phrygian, he becomes leader of the expedition and is killed by Paris. His father, Nauplius, avenged him by luring Greek fleets onto rocks with false signals and urging the wives of Greek leaders to commit adultery.

Legacy

Plato has Socrates look forward to speaking with him after death, and in the Phaedrus credits him with a work on rhetoric. Euripides and other dramatists wrote plays about his fate, and Gorgias composed a Defense of Palamedes. Ovid and Virgil also mention him in the Metamorphoses and the Aeneid. In 1625 the Dutch playwright Joost van den Vondel used his death as a symbol of a judicial killing he considered unjust.

Ancient sources: Plato, Gorgias, Euripides, Ovid, Virgil, Hyginus, Pausanias and Philostratus mention him, although Homer's Iliad does not name him.

Trivia

Dice offered to Fortune. Pausanias says that in Argos there was a Temple of Fortune to which Palamedes dedicated the dice he had invented.
An inventor of letters. Hyginus credits him with creating eleven letters of the Greek alphabet, although other sources do not agree.
