Demon-god who causes eclipses in Hinduism

Rahu

Rahu, also written Raju, is a daitya or asura whose attack on the Sun and Moon is said to cause eclipses. In Hindu astrology he is considered a shadow planet, always paired with Ketu, the body that was severed from his head.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Rahu was a son of Diti with a serpentine dragon's tail, who joined the churning of the cosmic ocean of milk in search of amrita, the nectar of immortality. While the demons were distracted by Mohini, a female form of Vishnu who was sharing the nectar among the gods, Rahu slipped into the row of devas and drank a drop. The Sun and Moon noticed and warned Mohini, who beheaded him with the Sudarshana Chakra.

Because he had tasted the nectar, Rahu could not die. His head became Rahu and his body became Ketu, two invisible celestial points. Ever since, Rahu chases the Sun and Moon out of revenge. When he swallows them, the eclipse lasts until they emerge through his open throat. In some tellings, Rahu was originally the asura Svarbhanu.

Worship and religion

In Hindu astrology, Rahu is one of the nine major celestial bodies, the navagrahas, and is generally considered malefic. His influence marks the period called Rahu kala, which is regarded as inauspicious. Temples are dedicated to the navagrahas as a group, although the sources do not describe rites devoted to Rahu alone. In Buddhism he appears as one of the krodha devatas, the wrathful deities.

Legacy

His figure gave rise to the notion of the shadow planet, the term for the lunar nodes where the paths of the Sun and Moon intersect. Rahu and Ketu mark a cycle of about 18 years used to predict eclipses, and the nakshatras of Ardra, Swati and Shatabhisha fall under his rule. In modern astrology he is linked to deceit and addiction, and in favourable configurations to science, technology and humour.

Ancient sources: The Puranas, which narrate his origin in the churning of the ocean, and the Samyutta Nikaya of the Pali Canon, which mentions him in Buddhism.

Trivia

A cycle of 18 years. Rahu and Ketu are always 180 degrees apart in their orbit, and their cycle lasts about 18 years, the same period used to predict eclipses.
Hanuman and the Sun. In one story, as a baby Hanuman mistook the Sun for a fruit and leapt at it. Later he tried to eat Rahu too, and Rahu fled to Indra for help.
