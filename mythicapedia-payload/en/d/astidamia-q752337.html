heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of Pelops

Astydameia

Astydameia is a heroine of Greek mythology, one of the three daughters of Pelops and Hippodamia and the wife of the Perseid Alcaeus. She matters chiefly as the mother of Anaxo and, according to the Bibliotheca, of Amphitryon, which places her in one of the most cited genealogical lines of ancient Greece.

The myth

According to the tradition, Astydameia was one of the three daughters of Pelops and Hippodamia. Ancient authors disagree about her parentage: some call her Laonome, daughter of Guneus, and others Hipponome, daughter of Menoeceus.

Like her sisters, she married one of the Perseids, namely Alcaeus, whom an ancient verse praises as a lord equal to the gods who made his wife fruitful. From this union came Anaxo and Perimede, and also Amphitryon according to the Bibliotheca.

The texts do not describe any deeds of Astydameia's own. Her role is mainly genealogical, a link between Pelops and the line of the Perseids.

Ancient sources: The Bibliotheca, which lists her among the children of Alcaeus, and a fragment attributed to Hesiod that names her as Alcaeus's fruitful wife.
