Gaulish-Aquitanian god of apple trees and the sun

Abellio

Abellio was a god worshipped in Gaul and southwest France, known mainly from inscriptions found at Comminges in the Pyrenees. His name suggests a solar deity linked to apple trees, and some scholars have identified him with Apollo.

The myth

Little is preserved about Abellio, and no detailed myth survives. Nearly everything known comes from Latin inscriptions, where the name appears in variants such as Abellio, Abelio or Abello. Scholars have linked the name to Apollo, who in Crete and elsewhere was called Abelios, and to the Gallic Apollo mentioned by Caesar, as well as to Belenus, cited by Tertullian and Herodian.

Other researchers have reversed this relationship and argue that Abellio was a solar deity of Celtic origin, or that the Cretan god was a local variant of Apollo. What is certain is that the name is attested only among the Aquitani, a people who were neither Greek nor Celtic, which makes any simple identification difficult.

Worship and religion

Abellio was venerated in the Garonne Valley in Gallia Aquitania. Several inscriptions from Comminges name him, and votive offerings dedicated to him survive in Lugdunum, the civitas of the Convenae, suggesting gifts made by individual worshippers. The sources do not mention specific temples or festivals.

Legacy

Abellio has very little presence in later culture. In the twentieth century, the writer Otto Rahn connected him with Grail legends and the Cathars, equating his Pyrenean cult with Belenus-Apollo and then with Lucifer. This reading is highly disputed and has little support among specialists.

Ancient sources: Roman inscriptions found at Comminges and Lugdunum; no ancient literary texts mention him.

Trivia

A name with several forms. The god appears written as Abelio, Abellio or Abello, and in some sources as Abelionni.
Similar names. Three other similar names are attested in the Roman Empire, all used as personal names and without the final N: one in southern Italy and two in ancient Dalmatia.
