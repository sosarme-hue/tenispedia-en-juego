legendary princess of the founding of Massalia

Gyptis

According to legend, Gyptis was the daughter of Nann, chief of a local tribe of Ligurian or Celto-Ligurian origin. Her choice of husband, the Phocaean sailor Protis, is part of the founding story of Massalia, the Greek colony that became modern Marseille.

The myth

Tradition tells that Protis, a native of Phocaea, was exploring the coast for a site to set up a trading post when he discovered the Lacydon cove, sheltered by two rocky headlands and fed by a freshwater stream. He was invited inland to a banquet that the local chief, Nann, was hosting for suitors seeking the hand of his daughter Gyptis.

At the end of the feast, Gyptis offered Protis the ceremonial cup of wine, making her choice unmistakable. After the marriage, the couple settled on the hill just north of the Lacydon, and around that settlement Massalia grew. Later, the natives plotted to destroy the new colony, but the scheme was revealed, and in the battle that followed Conran, the native king, was killed.

Ancient sources: Aristotle recorded the story with different names, calling the princess Petta and the sailor Euxenes.

Trivia

The cup of wine. In the story, Gyptis's choice is expressed through a ritual gesture: she hands Protis the ceremonial cup of wine during the banquet.
Two sets of names. Aristotle passes the story on with other names: Petta for the princess and Euxenes for the sailor.
