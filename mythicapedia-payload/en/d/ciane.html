Sicilian nymph in Greek mythology

Cyane

Cyane is a nymph from Syracuse in Sicily, whose name was given to the pool and river where she lived. She is one of the few figures who confronts a god of the underworld directly.

The myth

According to tradition, Cyane was a playmate of Persephone. When Hades burst out of the earth to seize her, Cyane tried to block his way and insisted that he could not claim the girl by force. Hades ignored her and vanished underground with Persephone. Cyane wept until she dissolved into her own tears and merged with the waters of her spring.

In Ovid's version, Cyane cites her love for the river god Anapus as an example of a consensual union. When Demeter came searching, the nymph, having no mouth to speak, made Persephone's girdle rise to the surface to reveal what had happened.

Worship and religion

Syracusans made annual sacrifices at the spring. During the Koreia festivals, held in honour of Kore (another name for Persephone) and traditionally founded by Heracles, bulls were submerged in her waters. Sacred gates near Agrigento were also dedicated to her.

Legacy

Her name comes from the Greek kyanos, 'dark blue', which gave us the word cyan for the colour. She also appears in Ovid's Metamorphoses.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Metamorphoses and Diodorus Siculus's Historical Library, which links her name to a sacred spring that arose when Hades abducted Persephone.

Trivia

A colour with a name. The Greek word kyanos meant bluish-green and referred to the water of lakes and streams. The colour cyan takes its name from it.
Sacred gates. The sacred gates of Agrigento, near the Syracusan tomb, were dedicated to Cyane.
