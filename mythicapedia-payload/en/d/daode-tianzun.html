supreme Taoist deity, identified with Laozi

Daode Tianzun

Daode Tianzun, also called Taishang Laojun, is one of the highest deities in Taoism and one of the Three Pure Ones. He is considered to be the manifestation behind Laozi, author of the Dao De Jing, which is why he is traditionally regarded as the founder of this Chinese religion.

The myth

According to Taoist tradition, Taishang Laojun took many incarnations to teach living beings, and Laozi was the principal one. Accounts collected by Ge Hong in the Biographies of the Immortals say that Laozi was born before Heaven and Earth, after staying seventy-two years in his mother's womb, and came into the world under a plum tree, already able to speak; he took the surname Li from the tree. Another text, the Inscription in Honor of Laozi, presents him as emerging from the Vital Breath of Chaos and as eternal as the three lights of the Sun, Moon and Stars.

Tradition adds that he passed the revelation to Zhang Daoling, founder of the Way of the Five Pecks of Rice, in the year 142. Before advising the Jade Emperor, he dwelt in the Great Pure Heaven. He is also believed able to foresee and control what will happen in the future.

Worship and religion

In popular Taoism the Jade Emperor holds the главное place, while orthodox Taoists of the Celestial Masters school place Laozi and the Three Pure Ones at the summit of the pantheon. During the Tang dynasty the imperial family adopted him as an ancestor and granted him many titles. His anniversary is celebrated on the 15th day of the second lunar month. Since 1996 the Singapore Taoist Federation has marked that day each year as The Taoist Day.

Ancient sources: Accounts of Laozi appear in Ge Hong's Biographies of the Immortals, and the Inscription in Honor of Laozi, written by Bian Shao, also records them.
