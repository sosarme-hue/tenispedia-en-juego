princess of Mycenae, daughter of Agamemnon

Chrysothemis

Chrysothemis is a princess of Mycenae, daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra and sister of Electra and Orestes. Her role is minor, but she stands out as the cautious, submissive sister in Sophocles' tragedy Electra.

The myth

In the Iliad, Agamemnon offers Achilles any of his three daughters, Iphianassa, Laodice or Chrysothemis, as a wife without bride-price, as compensation for taking away his concubine Briseis. Odysseus repeats the offer when the embassy visits Achilles in his tent to persuade him to rejoin the fighting, but Achilles refuses. The sources do not fully agree on names: in later tradition Laodice becomes Electra, and Iphianassa may be Iphigenia or a different sister.

The fullest account is in Sophocles' Electra, set years after Clytemnestra and Aegisthus murdered Agamemnon, while Orestes has been smuggled away to safety. Chrysothemis lives under her mother's roof, resenting the killing but keeping quiet out of fear. After a troubling dream, Clytemnestra sends her to pour libations at Agamemnon's tomb. There she finds offerings and a lock of cut hair, and concludes that Orestes has returned. She hurries to tell Electra, but Electra falsely reports that Orestes is dead, which devastates her. Later Electra tries to recruit her to kill Aegisthus; Chrysothemis refuses, arguing that women are not fit for such deeds, and Electra decides to go ahead alone. She is also named in Euripides' Orestes, though without a prominent role.

Ancient sources: She appears in Homer's Iliad, Sophocles' Electra and Euripides' Orestes.

Trivia

No recorded children. The sources record no children of hers. Although her family's story appears in the fragmentary Catalogue of Women, she is not included in it.
Half-siblings through their mother. Clytemnestra had three children with Aegisthus: Aletes, Erigone and Helen. Chrysothemis grew up under the same roof as them.
