river god of the Hermus, in ancient Lydia

Hermus

Hermus is the river god of the river that bears his name, today's Gediz, in ancient Lydia (now western Turkey). He belongs to the large family of Greek river gods, sons of Oceanus and Tethys.

The myth

According to tradition, Hermus was the father of the Lydian nymphs. The river rose in the region of Dindymene in Ionia, though Pliny the Elder placed its source near Dorylaeum. It flowed into the Gulf of Hermus, near Leuce in Strabo's time, and later reached the sea farther south.

One famous episode linked to the region is the consultation of Croesus, king of Lydia: the Delphic oracle warned him that if he crossed the river Halys, he would destroy a great kingdom, and that kingdom turned out to be his own, falling to Cyrus the Great, as Herodotus recounts. The river also witnessed historical events, such as the battle on its plain between the satrap Tissaphernes and the Spartan king Agesilaus II, where the latter defeated the Persian cavalry and infantry.

Ancient sources: Herodotus recounts Croesus' oracle, Strabo describes the river and its gulf, and Pausanias mentions its catfish.

Trivia

Catfish in its waters. According to Pausanias, the Hermus was a place where catfish were raised, large freshwater fish.
A double-edged oracle. The prophecy Croesus received about the river Halys came true in a way he did not expect: the kingdom destroyed was his own.
