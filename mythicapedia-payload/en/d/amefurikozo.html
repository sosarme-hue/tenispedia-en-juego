Japanese rain spirit (yōkai)

Amefurikozō

The amefurikozō is a yōkai, a spirit from Japanese folklore linked to the weather. It is depicted as a child who controls the rain, and its best-known image comes from the illustrations of Toriyama Sekien.

The myth

Toriyama Sekien drew it in his Konjaku Gazu Zoku Hyakki as a strange child wearing a hat made from the cloth of an old umbrella and holding a paper lantern. According to his text, it serves U-shi, a Chinese god of rain, and this is why it has the power to bring rain.

In Edo-period works such as the kibyōshi, it appears as a servant among the yōkai. In a kibyōshi from 1792 it is shown as a one-eyed being that steps out to meet a man on a rainy night, holding something in each hand. Scholars note a link with the tōfu-kozō, another yōkai that appears on rainy nights carrying tofu.

Modern literature gives it more mischievous traits: whoever steals its umbrella will be unable to take it off, and it enjoys seeing people troubled. One story recorded by Norio Yamada tells how a fox asked it to make rain for its wedding, and the amefurikozō waved its lantern and the rain fell at once.

Legacy

Osamu Tezuka wrote a short manga story with the same name. The character also appears as a supporting figure in the anime Rosario + Vampire. In Sakaiminato, Tottori Prefecture, the road dedicated to Mizuki Shigeru describes it as the spirit that regulates rain, something vital to the life of all living things.

Trivia

A cursed umbrella. According to modern yōkai literature, whoever steals an amefurikozō's umbrella will be unable to take it off afterwards.
The fox's wedding. In a story from Iwate, a fox asked the amefurikozō to make rain for its wedding, and the rain fell when it waved its lantern.
