third heaven of the desire realm in Buddhist cosmology

Yāma

Yāma is the third of the six heavenly worlds of the desire realm in Buddhist cosmology, located between Trāyastriṃśa and Tuṣita. Its name refers to an ancient Sanskrit unit of time, and its heaven is a realm of pleasure where day and night never divide.

The myth

According to Buddhist texts, the heaven of Yāma is always illuminated. Its inhabitants, the devas, enjoy the satisfaction of the five desires linked to the five senses. Positioned above Trāyastriṃśa on Mount Meru, they remain free of conflict with the asuras.

Time runs very differently from on Earth. According to the Visakhuposatha Sutta of the Pali Canon, two hundred human years equal one day and night of the Yāma devas, and their year lasts 72,000 human years. Their lifespan reaches 144 million years.

The Dāna Sutta notes that those who practise generosity in keeping with a family custom create the karmic conditions to be reborn there. In Mahāyāna Buddhism, the Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra describes the river Jambū and a palace whose canopy is decorated with five hundred million jewels. According to the Avataṃsaka Sūtra, bodhisattvas of the fourth stage may come to rule this heaven.

Ancient sources: Visakhuposatha Sutta and Dāna Sutta of the Pali Canon; Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra and Avataṃsaka Sūtra of Mahāyāna Buddhism.

Trivia

Telling time by flowers. A Chinese text known as the Sse-kiau-tsih states that the gods of this world tell time by the opening and closing of flowers.
A name with many forms. Yāma has several Chinese transcriptions, such as 夜摩天, 唱樂天 and 炎魔, reflecting its spread across East Asia.
