stone giant of Norse mythology

Hrungnir

Hrungnir is a stone giant from Norse mythology, known for his race against Odin, his drunken behaviour in Asgard and his death at the hands of Thor. His story shows how Norse tales pictured the jötnar, the giants who rivalled the gods.

The myth

According to the tale, Hrungnir was a jötunn who rode the horse Gullfaxi. One day he met Odin, mounted on Sleipnir, and the two argued about whose horse was better. Odin wagered his head on his mount's speed, and in the race towards Asgard Sleipnir proved faster, though only just.

Once in Asgard, the gods offered him drink and the giant grew drunk. In his boasting he threatened to carry Valhalla off to Giantland and claimed he would keep Freyja and Sif for himself. The gods summoned Thor, who came to the duel with his servant Thjalfi. Hrungnir hurled a whetstone at Thor, Thor struck back with Mjölnir, and the giant fell dead, pinning Thor beneath one leg.

Magni, Thor's young son, lifted the leg that neither his father nor the gods could move. Back in Asgard, the sorceress Gróa tried to remove the stone fragment lodged in Thor's forehead, but she was distracted by the news that her husband might return, and the stone stayed where it was.

Legacy

His heart, which was triangular, gave its name to the valknut and the triquetra, symbols sometimes called 'Hrungnir's Heart'. Scholars also compare him with the Hurrian stone giant Ullikummi, who was slain by the god Teshub.

Ancient sources: Skáldskaparmál in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, along with the poems Haustlöng by Þjóðólfr and Ragnarsdrápa by Bragi Boddason.

Trivia

The triangular heart. According to the sources, his heart was triangular, which is why the valknut and the triquetra have been called 'Hrungnir's Heart'.
An unusual reward. After the fight, Thor offered Magni Hrungnir's horse Gullfaxi as a reward for lifting the giant's leg.
