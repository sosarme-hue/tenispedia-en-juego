Trojan warrior of Greek mythology

Podes

Podes is a Trojan warrior in Greek mythology, son of Eetion and brother of Andromache, wife of Hector. He matters mainly as Hector's feast companion and for his death at the hands of Menelaus.

The myth

According to tradition, Podes fought for the Trojans in the Trojan War and was killed by Menelaus. He is described as a feast companion of Hector, a bond that Athenaeus saw as closer to that of a parasite and flatterer (kolax in Greek). On that reading, Menelaus wounding him in the belly was symbolic.

In the Iliad, Andromache claims that Achilles had killed her seven brothers, which conflicts with Podes dying at Menelaus' hands. The story never resolves this contradiction.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, which mentions Andromache and her brothers, and Athenaeus' Deipnosophistae.

Trivia

Seven brothers. In the Iliad, Andromache says Achilles killed her seven brothers, which does not fit with Podes being killed by Menelaus.
A symbolic wound. According to Athenaeus, the term feast companion was closer to parasite and flatterer, so the wound in the belly had symbolic meaning.
