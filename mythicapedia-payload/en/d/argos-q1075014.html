builder of the ship Argo in Greek mythology

Argus

Argus is, in Greek mythology, the builder of the ship Argo and, as a result, one of the Argonauts. He matters because he links the quest for the Golden Fleece to the idea of a vessel built with divine help.

The myth

According to the myth, Argus built the ship Argo, which carried Jason and his companions in search of the Golden Fleece. King Pelias commissioned the vessel because he doubted the expedition would return: the king of Colchis would not part with the fleece easily, and a dragon that never slept guarded it. Pelias gave Jason the timber and the crew he needed, and Argus raised the ship under Athena's guidance.

The tradition says Athena set a piece of sacred oak from Zeus's grove at Dodona into the prow. This wood could speak in times of danger to advise Jason. The crew included heroes such as Theseus, Meleager, Peleus, Orpheus and Heracles.

Accounts of his parentage differ: Apollonius Rhodius and John Tzetzes name Arestor, a member of the Argive royal house, as his father, while Hyginus says his parents were Polybus and Argia. Valerius Flaccus says he came from Thespiae.

Worship and religion

He is credited with creating a wooden statue of Hera that was a cult image at Tiryns. The sources give no further detail about its worship and do not say whether it survives today.

Legacy

Argus appears in retellings of Jason's story. The Italian actor Aldo Fiorelli played him in two 1958 films about Hercules. In Jason and the Argonauts (1963) the British actor Laurence Naismith played him as an elderly man, and in the 2000 television miniseries of the same name David Calder took the role.

Ancient sources: Apollonius Rhodius, in his Argonautica, and Hyginus, who gives a different genealogy, are the main references; Valerius Flaccus also mentions him.

Trivia

An oak that speaks. According to the myth, a piece of sacred oak set into the ship could speak to warn Jason in moments of danger.
Not the same as another Argus. The shipbuilder Argus should not be confused with Argus Panoptes, the giant with a hundred eyes.
