regional Hindu goddess of North Malabar

Arya Poonkani

Arya Poonkani, also called Aryakkarakani, is a regional Hindu goddess worshipped in North Malabar (Kerala, India). She is honoured through the theyyam ritual, and her story is a symbol of religious coexistence.

The myth

According to tradition, she was the daughter of Aryapattar and Aryapattathi, and so fond of jewels that she set out with her brothers to find pearls for her wedding. A storm wrecked their ship, and after seven days adrift they reached shore and parted ways. Arya Poonkani called for help to Bappiriyan, a Muslim sailor, and used her magical powers to persuade him to take her in search of her brothers. The brothers chose to stay in Venmalatinkara, while the two travelled on to the Kooran hills, where they are enshrined at Thaliparamba.

Worship and religion

She is honoured in theyyam, a ritual dance in which the performer embodies the goddess, with elaborate facial art and slow movements. Unlike most theyyam, she appears wearing wooden footwear. Bappiriyan is also performed at the shrine, and the Thaliparamba temple was followed by many others across the region.

Legacy

Her pairing with a Muslim sailor is cited as an example of Hindu-Islamic harmony in Kerala.
