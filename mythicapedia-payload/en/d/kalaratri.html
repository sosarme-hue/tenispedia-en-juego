seventh form of the goddess Durga in Hinduism

Kalaratri

Kalaratri is the seventh of the nine Navadurga forms of the goddess Mahadevi and one of her most fearsome manifestations. She is regarded as the ruler of the night and the destroyer of demons, ghosts and negative energies, and her name roughly means "the night of time".

The myth

According to the Devi Mahatmya and the Markandeya Purana, the goddess is the primordial force that brings the three qualities of the world (sattva, rajas and tamas) into play and the great night of final dissolution. In the Mahabharata, after the battle between the Pandavas and Kauravas, Ashwatthama creeps into the Pandava camp at night and kills the sleeping followers; amid the slaughter Kalaratri appears, a dark figure with bloody mouth and eyes, dressed in red and holding a noose.

The Skanda Purana tells how Parvati sends Kalaratri against the demon Durgamasur, and her beauty and the breath from her mouth reduce the inhabitants of the three worlds to ashes. In the Devi Bhagavata Purana, after the goddess Ambika emerges from Parvati's body, Parvati's skin turns dark and she receives the names Kalika and Kalaratri; the goddess then kills the demon king Shumbha. In another episode, Kalaratri uses her long tongue to catch the drops of blood of Raktabija so that they cannot form new clones, and after the victory she grows so furious that Shiva must calm her by lying beneath her feet.

Worship and religion

Kalaratri is worshipped during the nine nights of Navaratri, and the seventh day is dedicated to her. In Tantric tradition each period of the night is ruled by a terrifying goddess, and Kalaratri governs the night portion of every day. She is associated with the crown chakra (sahasrara), where devotees seek siddhis and nidhis. Her worship remains alive in Hindu religious practice.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions her in the Sauptika Parva, and the Puranas, particularly the Markandeya, Skanda and Devi Bhagavata, describe her in their stories.
