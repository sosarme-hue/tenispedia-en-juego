nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of Asopus

Chalcis

Chalcis is a nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of the river-god Asopus and the river-nymph Metope. Her name, linked to bronze, is associated with the city of Chalcis on the island of Euboea.

The myth

In the myth, Chalcis was one of the many daughters of Asopus, and accounts place her among his siblings, such as Pelasgus, Ismenus and Aegina.

Ancient sources do not agree on her offspring. Some make her the mother of the Curetes, the earliest inhabitants of Chalcis, who were associated with bronze and bronze shields. Strabo notes that these Curetes were the first in Euboea to carry bronze weapons, which is why they were also called Chalcidians. Other versions identify Chalcis with Euboea or Combe, also daughters of Asopus.

Legacy

Her name is thought to have given rise to the city of Chalcis, metropolis of Euboea, and some ancient coins from that city showed the head of the nymph.

Ancient sources: The Phoronis mentions the Curetes, and Strabo links them with bronze in Euboea.

Trivia

Coins with her head. Some ancient coins from the city of Chalcis bore the head of the nymph.
A name of bronze. Her Greek name, Khalkhís, is related to the word for bronze, linking her to the metal of the Curetes' weapons.
