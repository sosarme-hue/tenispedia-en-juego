king of the giants in Greek mythology

Eurymedon

Eurymedon is the king of the giants in Greek mythology and, according to Homer, the ancestor of the Phaeacians. He matters because his lineage links the Phaeacians with the giants and the Cyclopes in the Odyssey.

The myth

In the Odyssey, Alcinous, king of the Phaeacians, tells Odysseus that his line descends from Eurymedon, a proud king of the giants whose reckless deeds brought ruin on his people. His youngest daughter, Periboea, united with Poseidon, and from that union came Nausithoos, first king of the Phaeacians and father of Rhexenor and Alcinous.

Homer also links the Phaeacians, Cyclopes and giants as a common lineage, honoured with great sacrifices and close to the gods. Hesiod, by contrast, gave the Phaeacians a different origin, in the eponymous hero Phaeax.

The sources do not say who Eurymedon's parents were. The Theogony has the giants born from Gaia and the blood of Uranus, while Hyginus makes them children of Earth and Tartarus. In a minority version, Eurymedon violated Hera when she was a girl, which provoked the wrath of Zeus.

Ancient sources: He appears mainly in Homer's Odyssey, and Hesiod's Theogony describes the giants to which he belongs.

Trivia

A version about Hera. In a minority version, Eurymedon is said to have violated Hera when she was a girl, and Prometheus is said to have been born from that episode.
A son of Zeus with a daughter of Eurymedon. A late source credits Zeus with a son, Coron, born to Leandia, daughter of Eurymedon.
