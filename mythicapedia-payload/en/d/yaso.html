Greek goddess of recovery from illness

Iaso

Iaso, or Yaso, is a minor goddess of Greek mythology associated with recovery from illness. Her name means cure, and she reflects how healing came to be personified within the divine world.

The myth

According to tradition, Iaso was a daughter of Asclepius, god of medicine, though ancient authors disagreed about her parentage. The Suda makes her a daughter of Asclepius and Epione, sister of Hygieia, Aegle, Aceso and Panacea, all linked to some aspect of health. Another version, tied to the temple of Amphiaraus at Oropus, presents her as a daughter of Amphiaraus, descended from the healing hero Melampus.

Pausanias suggests that at Oropus she had once been an independent goddess who was later absorbed into the family of Asclepius, perhaps as a divine name of the heroine Alexina. Her name was explained by the benefits mortals received through tempered air, including the power to heal, and she was said to have had many children.

Worship and religion

At the sanctuary of Amphiaraus in Oropus, a site famous for its incubation rituals, in which the sick slept within the precinct hoping for a cure in dreams, Iaso shared an altar with Aphrodite, Panacea, Hygieia and Athena Healer. Pausanias, writing in the second century AD, describes how the altar was divided among different deities and heroes. The sources do not tell us whether her worship survived beyond antiquity.

Legacy

Aristophanes mentions her with humour in his comedy Ploutos. When two Athenians try to cure the blindness of Ploutos, Asclepius appears with his daughters Iaso and Panacea, and Iaso's flatulence makes Panacea cover her nose. The Romans identified her with Meditrina.

Ancient sources: The Suda, Pausanias's Description of Greece and Aristophanes's comedy Ploutos.

Trivia

A shared altar. Pausanias describes an altar divided into sections, one of them dedicated to Iaso alongside Aphrodite, Panacea, Hygieia and Athena Healer.
A comic cameo. In Ploutos, Aristophanes gets a laugh from Iaso's flatulence, which makes her sister Panacea cover her nose.
