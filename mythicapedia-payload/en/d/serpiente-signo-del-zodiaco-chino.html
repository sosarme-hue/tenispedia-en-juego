sixth sign of the Chinese zodiac

Snake (Chinese zodiac)

The Snake is the sixth of the twelve animals in the Chinese zodiac, the cycle that assigns an animal to each year and to each two-hour period of the day. It is associated with intelligence and wisdom, though also with a certain lack of scruples.

The myth

According to a popular legend, the order of the animals in the cycle was decided by a race to cross a great river. The Snake, a poor swimmer, hid in the Horse's hoof and, as the Horse neared the finish, leapt out and startled it, edging it out for sixth place.

People born in a Snake year are believed to share its traits, and each Snake year is linked to one of the five elements of wuxing, which change over each sixty-year cycle. In the daily clock, the Snake rules the period from 9 to 11 a.m., when the sun warms the earth and snakes are said to slither out of their holes. Its month is the fourth of the Chinese lunar calendar, usually falling between May and June. In popular astrology the Snake is considered highly compatible with the Ox and the Rooster, and incompatible with the Pig.

Legacy

In China a Snake year is sometimes called a Little Dragon year, a kindly term meant to soften any sense of inadequacy among those born in it. The sign appears in art and public spaces: a terracotta Snake from the Sui dynasty (581-618) is held in the Musée Cernuschi in Paris, and a stone circle inscribed with the twelve signs greets visitors near the entrance to the Old Town of Lijiang in Yunnan. A Snake statue also stands in the Kowloon Walled City Park in Hong Kong.

Trivia

Year of the Little Dragon. Snake years are sometimes called Little Dragon years, a nickname meant to make up for the Snake's lower prestige compared with the Dragon.
Two signs at once. The Chinese new year falls on a new moon between 21 January and 20 February, while Japan changes sign on 1 January. People born in January or February may therefore have different signs in the two countries.
