father of the dwarves in Norse mythology

Mótsognir

Mótsognir is, in Norse mythology, the father and lord of the dwarves, who dwell in Nidavellir. He matters mainly because his name appears in the Völuspá, one of the best-known poems of the Poetic Edda.

The myth

According to the Völuspá, the gods made the dwarves from the blood and bones of the giant Ymir. In the Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson tells a different version: the dwarves first arose as maggots in Ymir's flesh, then received intelligence and human form, with Mótsognir as the first of them, alongside Durinn.

Scholars debate this reading, since the original manuscripts of the poem seem to describe man-shaped figures shaped by the dwarves and later brought to life by the gods.

Legacy

He appears in the video game The Lord of the Rings Online, where he becomes the second dwarf of Durin's clan, reversing the Norse order. His name also gives title to a relic weapon in God of War Ragnarök.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá of the Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A name with several readings. The meaning of Mótsognir is uncertain. Readings such as "he who drinks in courage" or "the mead-sucker" have been proposed, and the name appears in variant spellings across manuscripts.
From Norse myth to video games. Tolkien drew names from the Völuspá for his dwarves, and in The Lord of the Rings Online Mótsog appears as the second dwarf of Durin's clan.
