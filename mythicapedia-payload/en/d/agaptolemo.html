Egyptian prince in Greek mythology, son of Aegyptus

Agaptolemus

Agaptolemus is an Egyptian prince in Greek mythology, one of the fifty sons of King Aegyptus. He matters chiefly for his place in the story of the Danaids, as he was killed on the wedding night that ended in slaughter.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Agaptolemus was one of the sons of King Aegyptus, and most accounts place him as a brother of Cercetes, Eurydamas, Aegius, Argius, Archelaus and Menemachus. The sources disagree about his mother: in some versions she was a Phoenician woman; in others he may have been a son of Eurrhoe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or of Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre.

The myth tells how the fifty brothers sailed to Argos to marry the fifty daughters of Danaus, Aegyptus's brother and king of Libya, who had fled from them. Fearing revenge from his nephews, Danaus ordered his daughters to kill their husbands on the wedding night. Agaptolemus fell like his brothers, except Lynceus. He is credited with marrying Pirene, daughter of Danaus and an Ethiopian woman.

Ancient sources: His story appears in ancient accounts of the Danaids and the sons of Aegyptus, transmitted through the Greek tradition.
