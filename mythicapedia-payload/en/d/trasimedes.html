Greek hero, son of King Nestor of Pylos

Thrasymedes

Thrasymedes is a hero of Greek mythology, the eldest son of King Nestor of Pylos and a participant in the Trojan War. He matters chiefly for his role in the Iliad and for the genealogy that links him to the Alcmaeonids, an influential Athenian family.

The myth

According to the tradition, Thrasymedes was the eldest son of Nestor, who ruled Pylos, and the elder brother of Antilochus, with whom he shared the Trojan War. The Iliad portrays him as a bold young leader who fought throughout the conflict. He was one of the sentries who kept watch at night while the Greek wall was built, and before Diomedes set out on a spying mission with Odysseus, he gave him his armour and sword. When Memnon killed Antilochus, Thrasymedes helped his father fight for the body, but Memnon's greater strength forced them to withdraw and seek help from Achilles. In another episode, he whipped Odysseus, mistaking him for a beggar, after the latter had stolen the Luck of Troy. He also numbered among the Greeks who entered the Wooden Horse.

Other accounts say that Thrasymedes survived the war and returned home with his father, and that he inherited the kingdom. He is said to have fought for years against the invaders who sacked Pylos, the ancestors of the Spartans, which marked the start of a long rivalry between Messene and Sparta. In the Odyssey, Telemachus visits him at Pylos while searching for news of his father. Later genealogy places him as an ancestor of the Alcmaeonids through his son Sillus and grandson Alcmaeon.

Worship and religion

His burial mound stood a short distance from Pylos. Ancient texts also mention a temple at Messene with paintings showing, among others, Thrasymedes, Nestor and Antilochus.

Legacy

In the 1960s, the Greek archaeologist Spyridon Marinatos excavated a Bronze Age tholos tomb overlooking Voidokilia beach and, with some flair, named it the "Tomb of Thrasymedes".

Ancient sources: Homer, in the Iliad and the Odyssey, presents him as a prince of Pylos and a Greek warrior.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His Greek name is usually translated as "bold of thought", an etymology that fits his portrayal as a resolute young warrior.
A tomb with a borrowed name. The Voidokilia tomb received Thrasymedes's name from the archaeologist who excavated it, not from a confirmed identification.
