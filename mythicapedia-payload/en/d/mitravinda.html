wife of Krishna in Hindu mythology

Mitravinda

Mitravinda is one of the eight principal wives (ashtabharya) of the Hindu god Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, and queen of Dvaraka in the Dvapara Yuga. Her name means "the virtuous," and her story appears in several Puranas and in the Mahabharata.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, she was the daughter of King Jayasena of Avanti. Her brothers Vinda and Anuvinda, allies of Duryodhana, opposed her marriage to Krishna, who supported the Pandavas. In Vallabhacharya's commentary, Mitravinda asked Krishna to abduct her during the svayamvara, and he did so after defeating the suitors. Another version says Balarama urged him to act, and Krishna tested the young woman's love with the help of his sister Subhadra.

According to the Bhagavata Purana, she had ten sons, while the Vishnu Purana speaks of many sons led by Sangramajit. After Krishna's death, according to the Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata, Mitravinda burned herself alive after being attacked by robbers as she left Dvaraka.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Bhagavata Purana, the Vishnu Purana, the Harivamsa and the Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata.

Trivia

Other names. In the Vishnu Purana she is called Shaibya or Shaivya, meaning descendant of King Shibi. The commentator Ratnagarbha identifies her with Kalindi, another chief queen of Krishna.
A wedding with Subhadra's help. In one version, Krishna was unsure of Mitravinda's love, so he brought his sister Subhadra to discreetly learn her feelings before acting.
