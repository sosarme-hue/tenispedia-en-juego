Melusine

Melusine is a female spirit of fresh water from medieval European folklore, mainly in France, Luxembourg, Germany and the Low Countries, usually shown as a woman with a serpent or fish tail. She matters because her story joins a marriage to a supernatural being with a forbidden secret, and because noble houses across Europe claimed her as an ancestor.

The myth

According to the tradition, Melusine was the eldest daughter of King Elinas of Albany and the fairy Presina. Elinas broke his promise never to see his wife during the births and baths of their children, so Presina took the three girls to the isle of Avalon. Once grown, Melusine and her sisters trapped their father inside a mountain. Presina then cursed Melusine to become a serpent from the waist down every Saturday, unless a man married her and never saw her on that day.

Melusine met Raimondino, son of the Count of Forez, in a forest and married him on that condition. For years she was a prosperous wife who built castles for the Lusignan family and bore him several children. One Saturday Raimondino spied through a door and saw her bathing with a serpent's tail. After a tragedy caused by one of their sons, her husband accused her, and Melusine turned into a dragon, left, and returned only at night to nurse their youngest children.

The versions vary: Jean d'Arras wrote her story around 1392, Couldrette retold it in prose about 1401-1405, and Thüring von Ringoltingen produced a German version in 1456, and some accounts say her sons were deformed.

Legacy

Melusine has inspired paintings, sculptures, songs and poems, especially in her form as a half-woman, half-serpent with wings or as a dragon. Her story was translated into Castilian, Dutch and English at the end of the fifteenth century.

Ancient sources: The earliest known literary version is that of Jean d'Arras (1392-1393), followed by those of Couldrette and Thüring von Ringoltingen.

Trivia

A claimed ancestor. In folk tales and medieval literature, the Lusignan, Anjou and Plantagenet houses, along with the Limburg-Luxemburg dynasty, were said to descend from Melusine.
Regional names. In France she goes by names such as Merlusse in the Vosges or Merluisaine in Champagne.
