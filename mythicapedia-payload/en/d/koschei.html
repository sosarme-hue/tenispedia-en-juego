immortal villain of Slavic folklore

Koschei

Koschei is a malevolent, skeletal old figure of Russian and wider East Slavic folklore, usually a threat to young women. As an archetypal male antagonist, he often rivals a hero for a woman's love, and he is best known for his magical immortality.

The myth

According to tradition, Koschei cannot be killed by ordinary means, because his soul is hidden in a needle. The needle lies inside an egg, the egg inside a duck, the duck inside a hare, and the hare inside an iron chest buried under an oak on the mythical island of Buyan. Whoever breaks the egg holds the sorcerer's fate and can weaken him until he dies.

In the tale of Marya Morevna, Koschei abducts the wife of Prince Ivan. After several failed pursuits, he hacks Ivan to pieces and throws the remains into the sea, but the water of life brings him back. Ivan seeks out Baba Yaga, obtains a horse faster than Koschei's, and rescues the princess. In another tale, Prince Petr uncovers the secret of the egg and sets out to find it.

Legacy

Koschei appears as the villain in Stravinsky's The Firebird and in Rimsky-Korsakov's opera Kashchei the Immortal. He stars in the 1958 Soviet animated film Beloved Beauty, and he has featured in Mercedes Lackey's novels, the comic Hellboy: Darkness Calls and the TV series Grimm.

Ancient sources: The Tale of Igor's Campaign and the Ipatiev Chronicle use a related word meaning captive or slave, which is probably the origin of his name.

Trivia

A possible human origin. Some scholars link the tale to Konchak, a twelfth-century Polovtsian leader mentioned in Russian chronicles. Over the centuries his figure may have turned into that of a villain.
A name with several meanings. In Old Russian, kosh meant camp, while kost means bone, which fits his skeletal appearance.
