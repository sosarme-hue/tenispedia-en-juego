Norse god, father of the Earth

Annar

Annar is a deity of Norse mythology, husband of Nótt, the Night, and father of Jörð, the Earth, according to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda. His figure is obscure, and his name appears in variants such as Ónar.

The myth

According to the Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson, Annar is the father of Jörð, the Earth, whom he had with Nótt, the Night. Nótt had previously been married to Naglfari, with whom she had a son named Aud; afterwards she was joined to Annar.

Snorri may have taken the figure from a source in which annar, meaning "second" or "another", referred to Odin, since he had just written that the Earth was Odin's daughter and wife. In the Skáldskaparmál, however, he uses the form Ónar and calls Jörð "daughter of Ónar". A verse by Hallfreðr vandræðaskáld repeats this image. The Völuspá also names a dwarf called Annar.

Ancient sources: The Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the Völuspá, and a verse by Hallfreðr vandræðaskáld.

Trivia

A name with variants. In the Skáldskaparmál Snorri uses the form Ónar, which also appears in a poetic kenning for the Earth.
A namesake dwarf. The Völuspá includes a dwarf called Annar in its catalogue of dwarfs, a name Snorri repeats in the Gylfaginning.
