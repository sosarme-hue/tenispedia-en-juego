wife of Lleu Llaw Gyffes in Welsh mythology

Blodeuwedd

Blodeuwedd is a woman made from flowers by the magicians Math and Gwydion to be the wife of the hero Lleu Llaw Gyffes in Welsh tradition. Her story, part of the Mabinogi, is one of the best-known tales of Welsh mythology, marked by betrayal, murder and a final transformation into an owl. Her name means "Flower-Face", from blodau (flowers) and gwedd (face). Myth

The myth

According to the tradition, the hero Lleu Llaw Gyffes had been placed under a tynged (doom) by his mother, Arianrhod, that he might never have a human wife. To counteract the curse, Math and Gwydion created Blodeuwedd from the flowers of broom, meadowsweet and oak, and gave her that name.

While Lleu was away on business, Blodeuwedd began an affair with Gronw Pebr, lord of Penllyn, and the two lovers plotted his death. Blodeuwedd tricked Lleu into revealing how he could be killed: only at dusk, wrapped in a net, with one foot on a bath and one on a black goat, beside a river, and by a spear forged for a year during the hour of Mass. Gronw hurled the spear, and the wounded Lleu transformed into an eagle and flew off. Gwydion found him perched high in an oak and, with a short poem, lured him down and restored his human form.

Gwydion overtook Blodeuwedd and turned her into an owl, condemned to be hated by all other birds and never to show her face in daylight. Gronw died when, after placing a large stone between himself and Lleu, he was struck by the spear, which pierced the stone. A holed stone in Ardudwy is still called Llech Ronw.

Legacy

In Alan Garner's novel The Owl Service (1967), the story becomes a cycle repeated in a Welsh valley. Louise M. Hewett reinterprets it from a feminist perspective in her Pictish Spirit series. Francis Ledwidge evokes it in the poem "The Wife of Llew", and John Cowper Powys incorporates it into Porius (1951). Judy Chicago included her name in The Dinner Party.

Trivia

A stone with a name. A holed stone in Ardudwy is still known as Llech Ronw, Gronw's Stone.
An owl with a flower name. According to the tale, the owl keeps the name Blodeuwedd, which is why birds and owls are hostile.
