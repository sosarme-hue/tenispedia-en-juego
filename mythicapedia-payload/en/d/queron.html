Greek hero, son of Apollo and Tero

Chaeron

Chaeron is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo and Tero. His story links him to two illustrious lineages and ties him to the Boeotian city of Chaeronea, which bears his name.

The myth

According to tradition, Tero, daughter of Philantus, was seduced by the son of Zeus, that is, by Apollo. From his father he inherited a divine lineage; through his mother, granddaughter of Antiochus, son of Heracles, he also descended from that famous hero.

As he grew up, Chaeron became a horse tamer as skilled as he was strong, a talent the story highlights as part of his heroic character. The myth thus presents him as a young man who combines the blood of gods and heroes.

Legacy

Chaeron lent his name to Chaeronea, a Boeotian city formerly called Arne. For this reason he is regarded as the eponymous hero of the place, the figure whose name passed on to the town.

Trivia

A city with two names. Chaeronea, in Boeotia, was formerly called Arne. Its eponymous hero, Chaeron, gave the town the name it keeps today.
