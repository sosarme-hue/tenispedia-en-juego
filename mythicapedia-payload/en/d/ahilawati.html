Ahilawati

Ahilawati is a female figure in some regional versions of the Mahabharata, wife of the hero Ghatotkacha. Her story blends a tale of a warrior maiden of demonic descent with folklore about the Nāga, snake-maidens.

The myth

In one version, Ahilawati, also called Mauravi, was the daughter of Mura, the general of the demon Narakasura. When Krishna, together with his wife Satyabhama, set out to defeat Narakasura, Mauravi first fought Satyabhama. After Narakasura fell, Krishna also killed Mura, and she resolved to avenge her father. However, once she recognised Krishna's divinity, she surrendered, and he promised that she would soon marry.

Another popular tradition presents her as a Nāga Kanyā, a snake-maiden, daughter of Vasuki, the sacred serpent of Shiva. According to the tale, Parvati cursed her for offering stale flowers to Shiva.

Ancient sources: Regional versions of the Mahabharata, which name her as the wife of Ghatotkacha.

Trivia

Several names. Depending on the version, she is also known as Mauravi, Mourvi, Maurvi and Kamkanthika.
Stale flowers. Parvati's curse came from a seemingly small gesture: offering Shiva stale flowers.
