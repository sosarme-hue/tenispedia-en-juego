Greek daimon of laws and ordinances

Nomos

Nomos is, in Greek mythology, the personification of law, statutes and ordinances. Rather than an independent god, he is understood as an aspect of Zeus, and his name gave rise to a key concept in Greek philosophy.

The myth

According to the Orphic tradition, Nomos married Eusebia, Piety, and their daughter was Dike, Justice. Orphic Hymn 64, from the Rhapsodic tradition, is dedicated to him and presents him as king of gods and men, the seal of all things and the stable foundation of the cosmos.

The hymn credits him with ordering the stars, protecting mortal life and punishing the lawless, while calling him the companion of those who act uprightly. In these accounts Nomos has no adventures of his own; he works as the principle that holds the world and human society together.

Legacy

The word nomos, meaning law, was central to the Sophists, who compared the laws of different cities and asked whether they were natural or conventional. The contrast between physis, nature, and nomos, convention, is one of the main themes of Greek philosophy. The word also named territorial divisions in Greece, and modern social science uses it for social norms and conventions. Several modern companies use the name as a brand, such as the German publisher Nomos Publishers.

Ancient sources: The Orphic hymns, especially Hymn 64 dedicated to Nomos, and the Orphic Rhapsodic Theogony, which makes him the husband of Eusebia and father of Dike.

Trivia

Nomos basileus. The phrase «the law is king» sums up the idea that no one should submit to a man, only to the law.
A word rooted in distribution. The term comes from the verb némō, meaning to dispense, assign or allot, reflecting the idea of law as a fair distribution.
