Trojan prince and seer, son of Priam

Helenus

Helenus is a Trojan prince and seer, son of Priam and Hecuba and twin brother of Cassandra. He matters chiefly for his role in the fall of Troy, since in the myths he revealed to the Greeks the conditions needed to take the city.

The myth

In the tradition, Helenus and his sister Cassandra received the gift of prophecy from Apollo; in some versions, snakes licked their ears. As children they warned that their newborn brother Paris would bring about the ruin of Troy. Priam ordered the baby exposed on Mount Ida, but fate ran its course anyway. Helenus became the city's chief augur, yet he failed to recognise the danger when Paris returned alive.

During the war, Helenus was no warrior. Homer calls him the greatest of augurs and tells how Menelaus wounded him. After Paris died, he contested the hand of Helen with his brother Deiphobus and lost. Resentful, he withdrew to Mount Ida, where Odysseus captured him. Under pressure he revealed that Troy would not fall without the bones of Pelops, without Neoptolemus joining the war, without the bow of Heracles held by Philoctetes, and without the theft of the Palladium. The Greeks met these conditions, and the stratagem of the horse followed.

After the fall, Helenus was given to Neoptolemus as a slave and taken to Epirus. In one version Neoptolemus gave him his mother Deidamia as wife and he founded a city in Molossia; in another, after Neoptolemus died, he married Andromache and ruled with her in Epirus.

Legacy

Virgil, in Book III of the Aeneid, presents him as a prophet who foretells to Aeneas the founding of Rome. Geoffrey of Monmouth, in his History of the Kings of Britain, has him captured by Neoptolemus, and tradition credits him with a son, Cestrinus, identified with a legendary Trojan king.

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad) calls him the greatest of augurs, and Apollodorus (Epitome 6.13) records his journey with Neoptolemus.

Trivia

The Dog's Tomb. In one version, Hecuba was turned into a dog in the Chersonese. Helenus buried her in the place later called the Dog's Tomb.
A changed name. In one account, a Thracian soothsayer renamed him from Scamandrius to Helenus after he gained the gift of prophecy.
