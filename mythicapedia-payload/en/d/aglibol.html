Palmyrene moon god

Aglibol

Aglibol is a moon god from Palmyra, the ancient Syrian city whose name is often rendered as «calf of Bel». He matters because his worship, documented mainly through inscriptions and reliefs, shows how Palmyrene religion blended Semitic deities with Roman forms.

The myth

Texts about Aglibol are scarce, so his figure is known mainly from iconography and inscriptions. He is shown with a lunar halo around his head and sometimes his shoulders, and a crescent moon is one of his attributes.

He is linked to the supreme god Bel and the sun god Yarhibol in a well-known triad. He is also associated with Astarte, identified with Venus, and with Arsu, the «Star of the Night». In inscriptions he appears alongside the sun god Malakbel, and in Palmyra he forms a local triad with Baalshamin and Malakbel, depicted in Roman armour.

Worship and religion

His worship lasted into the Hellenistic period and later spread to Rome, carried by Palmyrene soldiers serving in the Roman army. The earliest known inscription linking him to Malakbel dates to around 17 BCE. A bilingual inscription from 122 CE shows Aglibol and Malakbel sponsoring a citizen named Manai for his piety.

In the second century CE both gods were venerated in the Sacred Garden, one of the city's four main sanctuaries, tended by the Bene Komare. It had two altars, a sacred cypress and a bath. A relief from the Temple of Bel shows the two altars and the two gods. There is no information about his worship today.

Ancient sources: Mainly Palmyrene inscriptions and reliefs. The sources do not cite surviving literary texts from antiquity that mention him.
