king of Sindhu in the Mahabharata

Jayadratha

Jayadratha is the king of the Sindhu kingdom in the Sanskrit epic Mahabharata and the husband of Duhsala, sister of the Kaurava brothers. He is remembered for abducting Draupadi and for being killed by Arjuna during the Kurukshetra War, one of the epic's most dramatic episodes.

The myth

In the story, Jayadratha is the son of King Vriddhakshatra and is married to Duhsala, the only sister of the hundred Kaurava brothers. While the Pandavas were in exile, he saw Draupadi alone at their ashram, became obsessed with her and, when she refused him, carried her off by force. Her husbands rescued her and pursued him; Bhima humiliated him harshly, but Draupadi begged for mercy and Yudhishthira let him go.

After this disgrace, the tradition says Jayadratha performed penance to Shiva, who granted him a boon: he could hold back the Pandavas for one day. In the Kurukshetra War, on the thirteenth day, this boon kept the Pandavas from aiding Abhimanyu, who was killed. Arjuna vowed to kill him before sunset. Dronacharya shielded Jayadratha with three formations, but Krishna hid the sun with his discus, Arjuna beheaded him, and the head fell into his father's lap, killing the old king when he rose in shock, since his own boon condemned him. Years later, his son Suratha died of grief on learning Arjuna was marching to Sindhu, and Arjuna placed Suratha's infant son on the throne without battle.
