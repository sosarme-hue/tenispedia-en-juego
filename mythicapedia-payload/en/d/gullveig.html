goddess or giantess of Norse mythology

Gullveig

Gullveig is an enigmatic figure of Norse mythology, presented in the sources as a goddess or giantess, whose mistreatment at the hands of the Æsir triggered the war with the Vanir. Many scholars today identify her with Freyja.

The myth

The only ancient testimony is the Völuspá, included in the Poetic Edda, compiled in the 13th century from earlier traditional material. According to the poem, a völva recalls that Gullveig came to the hall of Hárr, one of Odin's names, where the Æsir pierced her with spears and burned her. Yet she was reborn three times after each burning. After this episode she took the name Heiðr, “the bright one,” and practiced seiðr, a form of magic associated with the völur, through which she performed great feats.

The narrative does not explain in detail why the Æsir punished her, but tradition places her as the trigger of the war between the Æsir and the Vanir. Another tradition, recorded in the Saga of the Ynglings, credits Freyja with bringing seiðr to the Æsir. For this reason, scholars such as Gabriel Turville-Petre, Rudolf Simek and John Lindow propose that Gullveig and Heiðr may be the same figure as Freyja. Lindow suggests that her magic may have been a strategy in the war, or that her arrival sparked the conflict.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, within the Poetic Edda (13th century), is the only ancient text that mentions her.

Trivia

A name that shines. Heiðr, the name she receives after her resurrection, means “bright” or “clear” in its adjectival form. According to Rudolf Simek, the meaning of the name remains basically the same as that of Gullveig.
An open etymology. The second element of Gullveig, veig, remains uncertain. Proposed meanings include “power,” “intoxicating drink” or “lady,” which leads to different translations of the name.
