Cypriot god later identified with Apollo

Hylates

Hylates was a god worshipped on the island of Cyprus, later identified with the Greek god Apollo. His most important sanctuary was at Kourion, where he was honoured as god of the woodlands.

The myth

The name Hylates may derive from a word meaning "barking" or from the word for forest, which gave rise to the idea of an "Apollo of the woods". Ancient sources connect him with several Cypriot places, including Amamassos, Erystheia, Hyle and Tembros. According to John Tzetzes, the epithet came from Hyle, a location near the Kourion region.

Worship and religion

The sanctuary lies about two and a half kilometres west of the ancient town, along the road to Paphos. Worship there is documented from the eighth century BC until the fourth century AD. The complex included a temple, a circular monument probably used for processions or dances around a grove of sacred trees, and an archaic altar. Today the site is part of the Kourion Archaeological Park.

Ancient sources: The Byzantine writers Stephanus of Byzantium and John Tzetzes mention Apollo Hylates and his places of worship in Cyprus.

Trivia

A sacred grove. The sanctuary included a circular monument that was probably used for dances or processions around a grove of sacred trees.
Nabatean columns. In the first century AD the temple was rebuilt in a different architectural style, with Nabatean columns.
