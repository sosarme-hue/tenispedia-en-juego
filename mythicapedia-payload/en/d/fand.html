otherworldly woman in Irish mythology

Fand

Fand is an otherworldly woman in Irish mythology, linked to the waters and famous for her love affair with the hero Cúchulainn. Her name means "tear" or "teardrop of beauty".

The myth

In the tale Serglige Con Culainn, from the Ulster Cycle, Fand is the daughter of Áed Abrat and the wife of Manannán. She appears as a sea bird alongside her sister Lí Ban, and a stone thrown by Cúchulainn passes through the feathers of one of her wings.

Later, the two sisters beat the hero with horsewhips until he lies ill for a year. To recover, Cúchulainn agrees to travel to her island and help her in a battle, and the two become lovers. But his wife Emer arrives armed with a troop of women; Fand, seeing that Emer is worthy of him, returns to Manannán, who parts the lovers with his cloak of mists.

Legacy

The story inspired William Larminie's collection Fand and Other Poems (Dublin, 1892) and Arnold Bax's tone poem The Garden of Fand (1916). She also appears as a recurring character in Kevin Hearne's The Iron Druid Chronicles series.

Ancient sources: She appears mainly in the Irish tale Serglige Con Culainn, from the Ulster Cycle; other versions make her the daughter of the woodland deity Flidais.

Trivia

Two names, two words. Fand and Fann are not phonetic variants but two different words with different meanings. Fann means "weak, helpless person".
A possible daughter. Niamh of the Golden Hair, said to be a daughter of Manannán, shares traits with Fand, so she may also be Fand's daughter.
