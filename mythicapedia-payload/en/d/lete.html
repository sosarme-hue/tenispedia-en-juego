river of forgetfulness in the Greek underworld

Lethe

Lethe is, in Greek mythology, one of the rivers of Hades whose waters caused immediate forgetfulness in anyone who drank from them. Its name means "forgetting", and in some sources it is also personified as the goddess of oblivion, which makes it a key image of forgetting in the Greek afterlife.

The myth

According to the tradition, the Lethe flowed through the underworld near the palace of Hades, and its waters washed away the memories of the dead. Some ancient Greeks believed that souls had to drink from it before being reborn, so that they would not remember their previous lives. Ovid placed its course beside the cave of Hypnos, god of sleep, in a dark land where poppies grow and whose murmur brings drowsiness. Statius has it border the Elysian Fields, the destination of the virtuous, and Virgil describes it flowing through quiet meadows where souls wait for rebirth.

The genealogies vary. Hesiod, in the Theogony, makes Lethe a child of Eris, Strife, alongside other personifications of human suffering. The Orphic Hymns call it a brother of Hypnos and Thanatos, Death, while other writers imagine it as a child of Aether and Earth. Apollonius of Rhodes says that the hero Aethalides never drank from its waters and therefore kept his memories. In the Myth of Er, at the end of Plato's Republic, the souls reach a barren plain beside the river Ameles, "of carelessness", and those who drink without measure forget everything, except those whom wisdom preserves.

Worship and religion

At the oracle of Trophonius in Boeotia there were two rivers, Lethe and Mnemosyne, and worshippers drank from them before consulting the god. Some mystery religions taught that after death an initiate could choose to drink from Mnemosyne, whose waters gave memory and complete knowledge, instead of Lethe. Gold plates found at Thurii in southern Italy and elsewhere in the Greek world bear verses that mention both rivers.

Legacy

Martin Heidegger used "lēthē" to describe the forgetting of Being, an idea that shaped much twentieth-century philosophy. In the real world the name was given to several rivers: according to Strabo, the Lima, between northern Portugal and Galicia, was known in antiquity as the River Lethe, and the Guadalete in Cádiz received the name from Greek and Phoenician colonists and later, in Arabic, became "river of forgetfulness". In Alaska, a river in Katmai National Park still bears the name Lethe, chosen in 1917.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, the Orphic Hymns, Ovid's Metamorphoses, Statius's Thebaid, Virgil's Aeneid, Apollonius of Rhodes's Argonautica and Plato's Republic.

Trivia

A river that made soldiers forget. According to Strabo, the Lima was known as the River Lethe. In 138 BC, the Roman general Decimus Junius Brutus crossed it and called his soldiers by name, one by one, to show that the myth was false.
Guadalete, river of forgetting. Greek and Phoenician colonists in Cádiz named the Guadalete Lethe after settling their disputes through diplomacy. Centuries later the Arabs called it the "river of forgetting".
An Alaskan river. A river flowing through the Valley of Ten Thousand Smokes in Katmai National Park is called the Lethe. R. F. Griggs named it in 1917, inspired by the river of Greek mythology.
