Inca sun god

Inti

Inti is the sun god of Inca mythology and the mythical ancestor of the Inca rulers, whose cult became the principal religion of the empire. He combines the light of the sun with the protection of crops and the favour of the emperors.

The myth

According to the most widespread traditions, Inti was the son of Viracocha, the creator god, and married his elder sister Mama Quilla, the Moon goddess, with whom he had two children. The two shared equal rank in the celestial court and were regarded as benevolent deities. Their court was served by the rainbow, Venus, the Pleiades and others.

The myth also links him to the origins of the Inca people. Manco Capac, the founding ancestor, was thought to be Inti's son, and Inti taught him and his daughter Mama Ocllo the arts of civilisation. Another legend, however, makes Manco Capac the son of Viracocha. In a different myth, Inti is the son of the Earth goddess Pachamama and the sky god, and he becomes her second husband. Tradition holds that Inti ordered his children to found the capital where a golden staff sank into the earth, and so Cusco was born.

Worship and religion

The cult of the Sun was the most important in the Inca Empire. Provincial temples staffed by priests and priestesses, and the Qurikancha in Cusco, were its main centres, and conquered provinces had to give him a share of their lands and herds. Offerings included food, coca leaves, woven cloth and chicha, and the most prominent image was a golden disk with a human face. The Inti Raymi festival, held around the June solstice, still draws visitors to Cusco today as a cultural celebration.

Legacy

Inti appears in Andean popular culture and in many artistic depictions of the Andean world. His name is used in Peruvian culture and in the Inti Raymi festival, and historians and political scientists have studied his cult as a tool for legitimising imperial power.

Ancient sources: Early colonial sources, including Spanish chroniclers, record his cult and myths, though filtered through their own perspectives.

Trivia

A lost golden disk. In 1571 Spanish conquistadors seized a large golden disk representing Inti and sent it to the Pope via Spain. It has been lost since then.
A disputed name. The word inti may be a loanword from Puquina rather than Quechua, which would explain its similarity to words for the Sun in Aymara and Mapuche.
