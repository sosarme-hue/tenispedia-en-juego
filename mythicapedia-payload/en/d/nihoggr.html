dragon or serpent of Norse mythology who gnaws at the roots of Yggdrasil

Níðhöggr

Níðhöggr is a supernatural being in Norse mythology, a serpent or dragon that endlessly gnaws at one of the roots of the world tree Yggdrasil. He matters because he links the cosmic tree to the realm of the dead and to the prophecy of Ragnarök.

The myth

According to the Völuspá, Níðhöggr lives in Niflheim, the realm of ice and mist, where one of the three roots of Yggdrasil stretches above him. As long as he gnaws at that root, the tree keeps standing, but the gnawing does not stop until Ragnarök, when everything is destroyed.

The tradition also gives him a role in the afterlife: he devours the bodies of the dead and fills their skulls with their blood, tormenting the souls left in Niflheim. In the Grímnismál, the squirrel Ratatoskr runs up and down the tree carrying insults and gossip between the eagle at the top and Níðhöggr, hoping to stir up quarrels between them.

How his story ends is debated. Many interpreters take his arrival as a sign of Ragnarök and read the poem as a warning. Others suggest a reading of renewal, in which the serpent sheds the corpses and begins a new life. According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the serpents of Náströnd, the realm of the dead, are said to be his offspring.

Legacy

His name appears in the Prose Edda's list of serpent names and as a name for a sword in the same work. In modern culture he appears in the Japanese light novel series High School DxD, the video game World of Warcraft: Legion, Fire Emblem Heroes, and the video games Nidhogg and Nidhogg 2.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and Grímnismál of the Poetic Edda, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

Name for a sword. Snorri Sturluson includes Níðhöggr among the terms for swords, as well as in his list of serpents.
Meaning of the name. The name has been translated as "malice striker" or "striker in the dark", depending on how the prefix is read.
