Greek mythical creature defeated by Oedipus

Theban Sphinx

The Theban Sphinx is a female monster from Greek mythology, with a woman's head, a lion's body and bird's wings. She is remembered for posing riddles to those who crossed her path and devouring them when they failed; her story is woven into the myth of Oedipus.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, the Sphinx was a cruel and merciless creature who challenged travellers with a riddle. Those who could not solve it died and were devoured. She appears this way in the myth and drama of Oedipus, the hero who managed to defeat her.

Unlike the Egyptian sphinxes, which were male and benevolent, the Greek one is a dangerous being. Both were thought of as guardians, but in the story her role is that of a threat to be overcome.

Legacy

Oscar Wilde wrote the poem The Sphinx, and Symbolist and Decadent artists of the nineteenth century used her as an image of the femme fatale. During the Renaissance and afterwards, the sphinx with a woman's face became a common decorative motif in European palaces and gardens, usually linked to the Greek version and to Oedipus.

Trivia

Origin of the name. The Greek name Σφίγξ was linked by folk etymology to the verb meaning «to squeeze». Some connect it to the way lions kill their prey by strangling them. The historian Susan Wise Bauer proposes another origin: a corruption of the Egyptian «shesepankh», meaning «living image».
Guardians of entrances. Both Greek and Egyptian sphinxes were considered guardians, and their statues often flanked the entrances to temples.
