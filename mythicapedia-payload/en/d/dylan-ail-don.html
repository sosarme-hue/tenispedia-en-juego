Celtic Welsh sea god

Dylan ail Don

Dylan ail Don is a figure of Welsh mythology, son of Arianrhod and nephew of King Math, closely tied to the sea. He appears in Math fab Mathonwy, the fourth branch of the Mabinogion, and his name is linked to the wave.

The myth

In Math fab Mathonwy, King Math of Gwynedd had to keep his feet in the lap of a virgin when he was not at war, or he would die. After Goewin was violated, Math asked Gwydion to find a new foot-holder, and Gwydion proposed his sister Arianrhod, who gave birth to twin sons after being tested.

One of the boys, Lleu Llaw Gyffes, was secretly taken and raised. The other, a sturdy blond boy, was abandoned by his mother, but Math acknowledged him and named him Dylan. As soon as he was baptised, he plunged into the sea and took on the nature of a fish, swimming as well as the best of them; for this he was called Dylan, son of the Wave. He later died by accident at the hands of his uncle Gofannon.

Legacy

Dylan is a popular traditional name for boys in Wales. The Welsh word dylanwad, 'influence', contains the same element dylanw-, related to llanw, 'tidal flow'. An elegy, Marwnad Dylan Ail Don, is attributed to the bard Taliesin.

Ancient sources: The fourth branch of the Mabinogion, Math fab Mathonwy, and the poem attributed to Taliesin, Marwnad Dylan Ail Don.

Trivia

Popular name. Dylan is one of the most popular traditional Welsh names for boys in Wales.
Son of the Wave. His epithet links to the Welsh word for tidal flow and to his baptism in the sea.
