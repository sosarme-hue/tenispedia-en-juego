Greek hero, son of Perseus and father of Amphitryon

Alcaeus

Alcaeus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Perseus and Andromeda and direct ancestor of Heracles. He matters because he links two great heroic lineages and opens the genealogy that leads to the famous demigod.

The myth

According to tradition, Alcaeus was born in Mycenae, although he ruled Tiryns as his father's successor. His name evokes the idea of strength in Greek. The accounts stress that he was the first son of Perseus born in Greece; through his father he descended from Zeus and the house of Danaus, while through his mother he was linked to Belus, father of Danaus and Poseidon.

Alcaeus married Astydameia, daughter of Pelops, thus uniting two of the most important heroic lines. Other authors name different wives: Laonome, daughter of Guneus, or Hipponome, daughter of Menoeceus. The marriage produced Amphitryon and two daughters, Anaxo and Perimede. According to some versions, Alcmene was the daughter of Electryon and Lysidice; others make her the daughter of Anaxo, who in turn would have married Electryon. Alcmene was taken as wife by her uncle Amphitryon and, through Zeus, became the mother of Heracles; she also bore Iphicles, conceived with Amphitryon as father.

In his youth Heracles received the name Alcaeus, or Alcides in other versions, in honour of his great-grandfather. For this reason, authors such as Lucan in the Pharsalia call the hero the Alcides.
