hero eponym of the Taphians

Taphius

Taphius is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Poseidon, founder of the city of Taphos and eponym of the Taphians. His story links the islands of the Ionian Sea with the kingdom of Odysseus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Taphius was the son of Poseidon and Hippothoë, daughter of Mestor and granddaughter of Perseus. Another genealogy, considered the more plausible, makes him the son of Pterelaus, who was in turn a son of Lelex and ruled in Acarnania.

The myth tells that the god carried Hippothoë off to the Echinadian Islands, where they lay together and she conceived Taphius. He colonized Taphos and called his people Teleboans, because he had travelled far from his native land. His son Pterelaus was granted immortality by Poseidon, who planted a golden hair in his head. At the time of the Trojan War, the islands of the Taphians formed part of Odysseus's kingdom.

Trivia

A name with a story. The name of the Teleboans is linked to the Greek phrase telou ebē, "he went far", alluding to Taphius's long journey away from his homeland.
A golden hair. Poseidon made Pterelaus immortal by planting a golden hair in his head.
