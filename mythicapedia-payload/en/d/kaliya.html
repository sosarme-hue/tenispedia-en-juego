venomous nāga of the Yamunā river in Hindu tradition

Kāliya

Kāliya is a venomous nāga serpent said to have lived in the Yamunā river at Vṛndāvana, in Hindu tradition. His story is one of the best-known episodes of Krishna's youth, in which the god subdues him and drives him from the river.

The myth

According to the tale, Kāliya's proper home was the island of Ramaṇaka, but he fled from there in fear of Garuḍa, the enemy of all serpents. The sage Saubhari had cursed Garuḍa so that he would die if he came to Vṛndāvana, so Kāliya chose that place as his refuge. The river water boiled with poison for four leagues around him, no bird or beast could approach, and only a single kadamba tree grew on the bank.

The tradition says that when the sage Durvasā was a guest, Rādhā, crossing the river afterwards, was terrified by the huge serpent. Krishna, angered that his beloved had been frightened, went to confront Kāliya, who coiled around his legs. After Kāliya was warned and promised to trouble no one again, Krishna dived back into the river, was caught and dragged to the bottom. There he expanded his body to free himself, leapt onto Kāliya's heads and stamped on them until the serpent, vomiting blood, was near death. Then Kāliya's wives begged for mercy. Kāliya recognised Krishna's greatness and promised to harm no one again, and Krishna pardoned him after dancing on his head. He sent Kāliya back to Ramaṇaka, and according to the myth Kāliya ended up in Pātāla, where he is said to live to this day.

Worship and religion

The episode gives its name to the celebration Nāga Nṛitya and to the festival Kāliya Nāga Mardan, which commemorates Krishna's dance upon the serpent. For Hindu devotees it is part of devotion to Krishna rather than a mere story.

Ancient sources: It is told in the sixteenth chapter of the tenth canto of the Bhagavata Purana.
