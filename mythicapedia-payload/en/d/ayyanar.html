Tamil village deity of South India

Ayyanar

Ayyanar (also Aiyanar) is a Tamil folk deity worshipped mainly in Tamil Nadu in South India and also in Sri Lanka. He is regarded as the guardian of rural villages and forms part of popular Tamil Hinduism.

The myth

According to tradition, Ayyanar protects the villages and drives off demons. He is shown riding a horse or an elephant, armed with a sword, a spear or a trident. The name comes from the Tamil word ayya, a term of respect.

Ancient texts also know him as Sastha or Chattan. Sangam poems mention poets and traders called Chattan, who may have revered him as their clan deity. The Tamil epic Silappatikaram records temples and devotees of Cattan. In Sanskrit texts he is the son of Shiva and Mohini, the feminine form of Vishnu. In the Kanda Purana, his commander Mahakala protects Indrani from the demon Surapadman.

Worship and religion

His shrines stand on the outskirts of villages. Terracotta horses are placed outside them and offered to the god as mounts for his night-time rounds. His priests are usually from the potter caste, though members of other castes may also officiate. His worship remains alive in Tamil villages.

Legacy

Many Ayyanar shrines have been turned into shrines of Ayyappan, a deity widely venerated today. According to some researchers, Ayyappan emerged from Ayyanar, and the Sabarimala deity keeps the horse and the yoga band of his predecessor.

Ancient sources: The Tamil Sangam poems, the epic Silappatikaram, Sanskrit texts such as the Brahmanda Purana and the Kanda Purana, and Appar's Tevaram.

Trivia

A name in many forms. He is called Ayyanar, Aiyanar, Sastha or Chattan depending on the language and the text, and scholars debate whether Aryankavu, a temple name in Kerala, derives from the same Tamil word.
