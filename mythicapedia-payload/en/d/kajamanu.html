Mesopotamian name for the planet Saturn

Kajamānu

Kajamānu is the ancient Mesopotamian name for the planet Saturn, which was also called the «star of Ninurta», linked to the fertility deity Ninurta.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian tradition, Saturn was associated with Ninurta, a god of the fertility of flocks and fields who also had a warlike side. One of his feats was recovering the Tablets of Destiny stolen by the storm bird Anzu.

In omen texts, the planet was compared to the position of the Sun in the lunar halo, making it the Sun's official representative in the sky. The Sumerians also called it «Sun of the Night», and according to Babylonian tradition the name kajamanu alluded to its slow orbital speed.

Legacy

Its name spread to other languages: the Mandaic Kiwan for Saturn derives from the Mesopotamian name, the Persian Kayvan is its equivalent, and the Syriac Kēwān, a loan from Akkadian, was used among later Assyrians.

Ancient sources: The Hebrew Bible cites it in Amos 5:26, where the name is deliberately vocalized to resemble shiqqùts, «a repugnant thing», and Acts quotes that passage at 7:43; the Septuagint renders it as Raiphan.

Trivia

A deliberate vocalization. In the Masoretic text of Amos 5:26 the name is vocalized so that it sounds like the Hebrew word shiqqùts, «a repugnant thing».
Sun of the Night. Among Saturn's names is «Sun of the Night», which Eratosthenes later recorded.
