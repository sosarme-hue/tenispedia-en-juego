epithet of Athena in ancient Greece

Athena Itonia

Itonia (also Itonias or Itonis) is an epithet of the goddess Athena, widely worshipped in Thessaly. Its name comes from the town of Iton, in the south of Phthiotis, and shows how one goddess could have very different local cults.

The myth

According to one tradition, the goddess received the epithet Itonia from Itonus, a king or priest. Ancient writers describe a cult that linked her in a mysterious way with the god of the underworld: Strabo calls him Hades, while Pausanias calls him Zeus. Some scholars suggest that her early cult had traits tied to the fertility of the earth, close to the chthonic deities, but this is a modern hypothesis.

Worship and religion

Her most famous sanctuary was at Iton, where festivals in her honour were held. From there her worship spread into Boeotia, where she was the chief war deity. In her temple between Pherae and Larissa, shields won from the Gauls were hung up. At Coronea, her temple was the meeting place of the Panboeotian Confederacy and of the Pamboeotia, near a sacred grove of Athena. No evidence survives of a living cult today.

Ancient sources: Strabo and Pausanias mention her, and a fragment of Bacchylides suggests she also presided over the arts of peace, especially poetry.

Trivia

Shields of the Gauls. In Athena Itonia's temple between Pherae and Larissa, shields taken from the Gauls were hung up after what was considered Greece's last victory over barbarians.
Meeting place of a confederacy. Her temple at Coronea was the gathering place of the Panboeotian Confederacy, which celebrated the Pamboeotia there.
