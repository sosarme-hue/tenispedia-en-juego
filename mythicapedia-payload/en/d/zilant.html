creature of Tatar folklore, symbol of Kazan

Zilant

Zilant is a legendary creature of Tatar folklore, something between a dragon and a wyvern, which has been the official symbol of Kazan, capital of the Republic of Tatarstan in Russia, since 1730. Its stories are tied to the founding of the city.

The myth

According to the stories, the hill of Zilantaw, beside the Kazanka River, was home to numerous giant snakes. Their leader was a two-headed serpent: one head ate only grass, while the other swallowed young women and men. A wizard advised the khan to build a pile of straw and wood near the hill. When the snakes crawled out of their winter burrows and into the pile, a knight errant set it on fire. Many snakes died, but the giant serpent escaped to the Qaban lakes.

The accounts differ on its end. In one, it turned into Diü, a spirit who rules the underwater kingdom of the lake. In another, it chased the knight, cut him into six pieces, but the hero managed to stab it with a poisoned lance and Zilant died. A third tells that it returned to a cave near the hill, and that the townspeople finally killed it with a wizard's help. Taken together, the legends about the founding of Kazan contradict one another.

Legacy

It has been the emblem of Kazan's coat of arms since 1730 and appears in drawings of coats of arms for several towns of the former Kazan Governorate made in 1781, as well as on the 1998 coat of arms of Kashira in Moscow Oblast. The popular image of the creature has moved closer to the Western dragon seen in films.

Trivia

A snake with wings. According to a legend of the Bulgars, a snake living near the town of Bilär begged Allah for mercy and was given wings, with which it flew away.
Flying snakes on the border. The Arab traveler Ibn Fadlan, who visited Volga Bulgaria in the 10th century, described huge snakes living in trees, and the Bulgars assured him they were not dangerous.
