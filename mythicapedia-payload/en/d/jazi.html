giant in Norse mythology, father of Skaði

Þjazi

Þjazi is a giant (jötunn) in Norse mythology, the father of Skaði and best known for kidnapping the goddess Iðunn. His story explains how the giant's eyes became stars and how Skaði came to be reconciled with the gods.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda and the skaldic poem Haustlöng, Þjazi appears in an episode where Odin, Loki and Hœnir, hungry during a journey, try to cook an ox in an earth oven without success. From an oak tree, Þjazi, in the shape of an eagle, offers to make the meat cook if he may eat some of it. He devours so much that Loki strikes him with his staff, but the staff sticks to the giant, who flies off carrying Loki with him.

To free himself, Loki promises to lure Iðunn, keeper of the apples of youth, out of Asgard. He keeps his word, and Þjazi seizes the goddess and takes her to his realm, Þrymheim. Without her apples the gods grow old, and they threaten Loki until he agrees to rescue her. Borrowing Freyja's falcon coat, he flies to the giant's hall, turns Iðunn into a nut and escapes with her. When Þjazi returns and gives chase in eagle form, the gods light a fire that burns his feathers; he falls and is killed.

His daughter Skaði arms herself and goes to Asgard seeking revenge, but the gods offer her compensation and give her Njörðr as husband. As a further reparation, according to the tradition, Odin sets Þjazi's eyes in the sky as stars, though other sources credit Thor or Loki instead.

Legacy

The moon of Saturn Thiazzi was named after him. Several skaldic kennings for gold and for Þjazi himself draw on his story and on the inheritance of his father Ölvaldi.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson, the poem Haustlöng, Grímnismál, Hárbarðsljóð, Lokasenna and Hyndluljóð.

Trivia

Varied names. The giant is written in several forms, such as Thiazi, Thjazi or Thiassi, in anglicized spelling.
Three versions of the stars. Grímnismál and the Edda credit Odin or Thor with placing his eyes in the sky, while Lokasenna has Loki claim the killing.
