Hermes' herald's staff, symbol of commerce

Caduceus

The caduceus is the herald's staff carried by Hermes, the messenger of the Greek gods, shown as a rod entwined by two serpents and often topped by wings. Over time it became a symbol of commerce, negotiation and eloquence, though it is frequently confused with the rod of Asclepius, the emblem of medicine.

The myth

The Homeric Hymn to Hermes tells how the god gave his half-brother Apollo a lyre made from a tortoise shell, and in return Apollo gave him the caduceus as a token of friendship. Through this exchange the staff became linked to Apollo and, through him, to the serpent, a motif that later connected to Asclepius, the son of Apollo.

Other stories explain its origin. In one, Hermes saw two serpents locked in combat and separated them with his wand, bringing peace between them; the wand with two snakes thereafter became a sign of peace. In another, tied to the story of Tiresias, the seer struck a female serpent copulating with a male with his staff and was turned into a woman; seven years later, after repeating the act with the male snake, he regained his manhood, and the staff eventually passed to Hermes along with its transforming powers. Tradition held that the wand could wake the sleeping, lull the wakeful to sleep, and bring a gentle death to the dying.

Its shape changed over time. Early vase paintings show a long, slender staff, and later depictions show a shorter rod with wings and two snakes.

Legacy

The caduceus entered astrology and alchemy as a sign for the planet Mercury and the metal named after it. In the early modern period it was a symbol of rhetoric, and several early printers used it as their mark. Today it stands for commerce and appears in schools of economics and customs services in many countries, although it is often mistakenly used as a symbol of medicine.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Hermes recounts his exchange of the lyre for the caduceus with Apollo, and Livy mentions the caduceator who negotiated peace in Rome under the protection of this staff.

Trivia

Confusion with medicine. Although the caduceus symbolises commerce, some countries use it as a medical emblem. The cause is its resemblance to the rod of Asclepius, which has a single snake and no wings.
Symbol of the planet Mercury. In late Antiquity the caduceus was stylised into the astronomical sign for the planet Mercury, and it passed into astrology and alchemy in this form.
