serpent of the storms in Zulu folklore

Inkanyamba

The Inkanyamba is a serpentine creature from Zulu folklore in South Africa, linked to waterfalls and river pools in KwaZulu-Natal. It matters because tradition blames it for storms, tornadoes and the seasonal rains, and it still lives on in local imagination and popular culture.

The myth

According to Zulu tradition, the Inkanyamba is a huge serpent, sometimes with a horse-like head, whose coiling body links heaven and earth. It may be a goddess of storm, water and hail, or a male seeking a mate, whose search brings disaster.

It lurks in the deep pools of rivers, especially at Howick Falls, where it is said to have seven heads. Its anger causes the summer storms, and anyone who tricks it into visiting a false pool, for example by owning shiny corrugated metal roofs, suffers wind, hail and destruction to trees, crops and homes. Appeasing it, by contrast, brings rain and fertility.

Legacy

It appeared in the television series Animal X and in the fourth season of the Canadian documentary series Mystery Hunters. Local artisans depict it in clay figurines, often as a dragon with a horse or hog-like head, wings and horns.

Trivia

Seven heads. In Zulu folklore from Howick Falls, the Inkanyamba is described as a creature with seven heads.
1962 sighting. In 1962, a game ranger named Buthelezi claimed to have seen a horse-headed, serpentine animal basking on a sandbank in the Umgeni River.
