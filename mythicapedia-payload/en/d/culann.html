smith in Irish mythology

Culann

Culann is a smith in Irish mythology, whose house was guarded by a fierce watchdog. He matters mainly because the death of that dog at the hands of Sétanta gave rise to the name Cú Chulainn.

The myth

According to the Ulster Cycle, Culann invited King Conchobar mac Nessa of Ulster and his retinue to a feast at his house. On the way, the king saw his young nephew Sétanta playing hurling and invited him to join the feast. The boy replied that he would catch up once the game was over.

During the feast, Culann asked whether Conchobar was expecting anyone else. Conchobar, who had forgotten about Sétanta, said no, and the smith unleashed his watchdog. When Sétanta arrived, he was forced to kill the animal in self-defence. Out of obligation, he offered to take the dog's place as guardian until a replacement could be reared, and for this he became known as Cú Chulainn, «Culann's hound».

Legacy

In 1979, NASA named Culann Patera, a large caldera on Jupiter's moon Io, after this legendary smith.

Trivia

Three pups in a skull. According to the tale, Culann's pup was found in the skull of Conganchnes mac Dedad, along with two other pups belonging to the hounds of Celtchar and Mac da Tho.
A name born from a dog. The name Cú Chulainn means «Culann's hound», and it recalls the obligation Sétanta took on after the animal's death.
