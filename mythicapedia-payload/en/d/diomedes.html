Achaean hero, king of Argos and warrior of the Trojan War

Diomedes

Diomedes is an Achaean hero, son of Tydeus and king of Argos, and one of the most distinguished warriors of the Trojan War. He ranks just behind Achilles among the Greek fighters, alongside Ajax and Agamemnon, and his legend extends to the journeys he made after the war ended.

The myth

Diomedes was born in Argos, the son of Tydeus, an Aetolian prince, and Deipyle, daughter of King Adrastus. His father died during the expedition of the Seven against Thebes when Diomedes was still a small child. Later, when his grandfather Oeneus was overthrown by the sons of Agrius, Diomedes went to Calydon, restored the old king to his throne and killed nearly all the usurpers, except two cousins who eventually murdered Oeneus. He also took part in the second campaign against Thebes, led by the Epigoni under Alcmaeon.

In the Trojan War, Diomedes led eighty ships and soon became one of the most feared heroes on the Achaean side. In books V and VI of the Iliad, Athena fills him with menos, a fury of battle that leads him to wound Aphrodite in the hand and Ares in the side, forcing the god to flee to Olympus. Athena also deflected Ares' spear to protect her favourite, and Zeus, angered, later forbade the gods from intervening directly in the war.

Before the fleet set sail, Diomedes and Odysseus travelled to Scyros to bring Achilles to the Greek camp, since his mother Thetis had hidden him disguised as a woman. The two fought side by side in many exploits, including the capture and killing of the spy Dolon and the theft of the Palladium and the horses of Rhesus. In one variant, Odysseus tried to kill Diomedes so that he alone would receive the credit for stealing the Palladium, but Diomedes noticed in time and defended himself.

After Troy fell, Diomedes returned to Argos. In some versions his homecoming was happy, but in others his wife Aegialia and her lover Cometes tried to kill him. He escaped and fled to Italy, where tradition credits him with founding several cities, among them Canusium, Siponto and Argyrippa, near modern Foggia.

Worship and religion

According to the sources, after his death he was worshipped as a divine being in both Italy and Greece, although the texts do not describe specific temples or rites.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad is the main source for his deeds at Troy. Dares the Phrygian describes his appearance and character, and later writers such as Hyginus and Pseudo-Apollodorus record his role as a suitor of Helen.

Trivia

A famous father. Tydeus was one of the Seven against Thebes. According to some accounts, Athena wanted to grant him immortality, but withdrew the favour after seeing him devour the brains of his enemy.
Cities in Italy. After the war, tradition credits him with founding ten or more Italian cities.
An unexpected kindness. Diomedes did not mistreat Thersites, whom the other Achaeans despised, and he was the only one who wanted to punish Achilles after Thersites was killed.
