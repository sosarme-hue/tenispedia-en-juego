female spirit of Greek folklore

Mormo

Mormo, also written Mormō, was a female spirit in Greek folklore, similar to the bogeyman, whose name mothers and nurses invoked to frighten children into behaving. Sources range from ancient comedy to the Byzantine era, and she was often confused with Lamia and with the mormolyce.

The myth

Mormo was a female spirit whom mothers and nurses called upon to make children behave. Her name, in the plural mormones, is linked to words for fright and is often rendered as "the fearful ones" or "the hideous ones". According to the Suda, a Byzantine lexicon, Mormo was another name for the bogeyman, the bugbear or the nightmare, and she was also identified with Lamia, a monster who drank the blood of her victims.

Sources describe her as a creature who punished naughty children by biting them and leaving them lame. The poet Erinna, in her poem The Distaff, recalls that she and her friend Baucis feared Mormo when they were little girls. Aristophanes mentions her among the names used to scare children, and the Alexiad, written by a Byzantine princess, shows that infants still feared her around the time of the First Crusade.

One account, attested in only a single source, tells of an original Mormo, a woman of Corinth who ate her children and then flew away. The mormolyce, or "she-wolf Mormo", is a related figure tied to the dead and to ghosts. Sophron, around 430 BC, called her the wetnurse of the river Acheron, which was associated with the underworld.

Ancient sources: Sophron, Aristophanes, the Suda, the poet Erinna in The Distaff, Philostratus and the Alexiad, written by a Byzantine princess.

Trivia
