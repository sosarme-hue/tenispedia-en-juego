satyr of Greek mythology, brother of Marsyas

Babys

Babys is a satyr of Greek mythology, brother of the Phrygian satyr Marsyas. He appears only briefly in the stories, but his tale offers a comic counterpoint to the punishment his brother suffered after the musical contest with Apollo.

The myth

According to the tradition, Babys was the brother of Marsyas, the Phrygian satyr who challenged Apollo to a flute-playing contest. While Marsyas owned a double flute, Babys's flute had only one pipe and hardly made music. When Apollo won the competition, the goddess Athena, who saw Babys as a simpleton with no apparent skill, persuaded the god not to vent his anger on him, since his playing was so poor that it did not deserve punishment. The episode contrasts the mastery of the gods with the clumsiness of a minor figure, and Apollo's forgiveness comes through Athena's intercession.

Trivia

A flute with a single pipe. Unlike his brother, who played a double flute, Babys played an instrument with only one pipe, a detail that put him at a disadvantage from the start of the contest.
Forgiveness through intercession. The punishment Apollo might have imposed on Babys never came about thanks to Athena, who argued before the god that his clumsiness did not deserve divine wrath.
