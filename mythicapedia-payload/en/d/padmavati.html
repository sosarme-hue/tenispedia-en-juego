Hindu goddess of prosperity, consort of Venkateswara

Padmavati

Padmavati, also known as Alamelu Manga, is a Hindu goddess regarded as an avatar of Lakshmi and the consort of Venkateswara, a form of Vishnu. She is revered as the goddess of wealth, fertility and beauty, and her worship is central at the Tiruchanur temple, linked to the pilgrimage to Tirumala.

The myth

According to the legend of Tirumala, the sage Bhrigu was sent by the rishis to determine which god deserved the sacrifice. Feeling ignored by Brahma and Shiva, he kicks Vishnu's chest; Vishnu massages his feet and crushes the extra eye on the sole of his foot, a symbol of his ego. Bhrigu then chooses Vishnu, but Lakshmi takes the kick as an insult and leaves Vaikuntha for Kolhapur. Vishnu follows her to the Seshachalam forest, where he performs a twelve-year penance.

Tradition holds that Padmavati was born inside a golden lotus in a pond in the kingdom of Narayanavanam and raised by King Akasha Raja and Queen Dharani Devi. Vishnu, incarnated as Srinivasa, sees her in a garden and falls in love; after a ruse in which he poses as a fortune teller, the wedding takes place in Narayanavanam. Months later Lakshmi learns of the new marriage, and Vishnu turns into stone on the hills to help humanity in the Kali Yuga. Padmavati and Lakshmi also choose to become stone to stay with him. Some accounts treat Padmavati and Alamelu Manga as distinct deities.

Worship and religion

She is the principal deity of the Tiruchanur temple, where rituals follow the Pancharatra Agama. Worship centres on her grace as a compassionate mother, and a visit to the temple is considered essential to complete the pilgrimage to Tirumala. Key rites include the kumkumarchana, in which vermilion is offered, and the kalyanotsavam, a ritual union with an image of Srinivasa. In the Tenkalai branch of Sri Vaishnavism she is revered as the ultimate mother, whose intercession is necessary to gain Vishnu's favour and moksha. Her worship remains alive and draws many pilgrims.
