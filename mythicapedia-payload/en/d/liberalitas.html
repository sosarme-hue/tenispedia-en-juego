Roman goddess of generosity

Liberalitas

Liberalitas was, in Roman religion, the personification of generosity, understood as the virtue of giving freely. Her name comes from liber, 'free', and her cult was tied to the harvest and to abundance.

The myth

According to tradition, Liberalitas was one of the companions of Liber and Libera, and her bond with Jupiter was so close that one of the god's epithets was Liberalitas itself.

Her image gained strength in the imperial era. The goddess was believed to manifest herself when the emperor distributed money to the people, and so, during Trajan's formal arrival ceremony (adventus) in 99 AD, she was thought to be present in the cash gifts he handed out to the population.

Worship and religion

She was venerated above all at harvest time, to ask for an abundant yield. She had a chapel in the temple of Jupiter on the Capitoline Hill.

Legacy

Her figure appears on coins of emperors such as Trajan, Antoninus Pius, Septimius Severus and Macrinus, often beside the ruler handing out gifts. The idea behind her name survives in the English word 'liberality'.

Ancient sources: Pliny the Younger mentions the liberalitas of the emperor in his Panegyric to Trajan.

Trivia

Coins with togas. On one coin, a Roman holds out his toga to catch the coins poured by Liberalitas, while the emperor watches from an elevated seat.
Deified virtues. Divine virtues were linked to a specific imperial duty. For Liberalitas, it was the giving of gifts directly to individuals.
