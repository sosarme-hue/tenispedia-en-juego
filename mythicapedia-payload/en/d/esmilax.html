nymph of Greek mythology

Smilax

Smilax is a nymph in Greek mythology, the lover of the young Crocus, who according to the stories was turned into the bindweed plant that bears her name. Her story is one of the least documented in Greek myth, and it survives mainly through a few brief mentions by Latin and Greek authors.

The myth

According to tradition, Smilax and Crocus loved each other, but the details of their story are sparse and vague. Pliny writes that Smilax was turned into a bindweed shrub for loving the young Crocus.

Ovid tells the story of Smilax and Crocus as a love story in which both are central figures. Nonnus also mentions Crocus's love for Smilax, whom he calls the air-garlanded girl.

Ancient sources: Pliny, Ovid and Nonnus mention her in their works.

Trivia

Origin of the name. According to Robert Beekes, variants of the name point to a pre-Greek origin for the word.
