Epeius of Elis

Epeius is a mythical hero and king of ancient Elis, in the northwest of the Peloponnese, and the third ruler of that land in Greek tradition. He gave his name to the Epeans, one of the peoples who lived in the region.

The myth

Epeius was a son of Endymion, who decided his successor through a footrace at Olympia in which his three sons competed. Epeius won, inherited the throne and gave his name to his subjects. His defeated brother Paeon left for a distant land beyond the River Axius, which came to be called Paeonia; the other brother, Aetolus, stayed in Elis and succeeded Epeius when he died without a male heir.

Epeius married Anaxiroe, daughter of Coronus, and they had a daughter, Hyrmine. Diodorus adds a son, Alector. Ancient traditions disagreed about his mother, whose name Pausanias gives as Asterodia, Chromia or Hyperippe. During his reign, Pelops the Lydian defeated Oenomaus in Pisa and took those lands, along with Olympia, separating them from Epeius' realm.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Diodorus Siculus and Hellanicus mention Epeius, and Stephanus of Byzantium also refers to his siblings.

Trivia

Origin of Paeonia. According to tradition, Epeius' defeated brother Paeon settled beyond the River Axius, and that land took its name from him as Paeonia.
A different tradition. Hellanicus imagined Epeius as a son of the eponymous king Eleus, while Pausanias holds that Eleus was a son of Poseidon and Eurycyda.
