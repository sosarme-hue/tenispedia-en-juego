Japanese kami of thunder and agriculture

Ajisukitakahikone

Ajisukitakahikone is a kami in Japanese mythology linked to thunder, agriculture and land. A son of Ōkuninushi, he appears in the Kojiki as a fiery deity whose crying as an infant was famously loud.

The myth

According to myth, Ajisukitakahikone was the son of Ōkuninushi and Takiribime, one of the Munakata goddesses, and the older brother of Shitateruhime. As a baby he cried so much that he was set adrift in a boat and sailed around the islands of Japan until he calmed down; the thunder is explained by the ladder his mother carried him up and down to lull him to sleep.

As an adult he married Ame-no-mikajime and became the father of Takitsuhiko, a rain god. In another tale, when Ame-no-Wakahiko died, Ajisukitakahikone came to the funeral, but his resemblance to the dead man led the family to take him for him. Insulted, he hacked apart the funeral hut and kicked it away; its remains formed Mount Moyama, the «mourning mountain».

Worship and religion

He is the tutelary deity of Kamo and is worshipped at Takakamo Shrine in Gose (Nara) and Tsutsukowake Shrine in Tanagura (Fukushima).

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, Nihon Shoki, Izumo Fudoki and Harima Fudoki, which record him under variant forms of his name.

Trivia

A disputed name. Scholars disagree on the meaning of his name. Some readings take it as “excellent” or as “many ploughs.”
A mountain born from a funeral. The remains of the funeral hut, thrown into Mino, became Mount Moyama, whose name means “mourning mountain.”
