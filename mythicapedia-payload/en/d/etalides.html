Aethalides

Aethalides is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Hermes and Eupolemeia, who sailed with the Argonauts. He matters for his prodigious memory and for the reincarnation story that ancient writers attached to him.

The myth

According to the tradition, Aethalides was the son of Hermes and Eupolemeia, a daughter of King Myrmidon of Phthia. He was born near the streams of the Amphrysus. His father granted him the faculty of remembering everything, even in Hades, and a staff helped preserve that memory.

He joined the Argonauts in their quest for the Golden Fleece and served as their herald, a role his memory made possible. At the end of his life he was allowed to dwell alternately in the upper and the lower world.

According to Heraclides Ponticus, Pythagoras claimed to remember his former lives: he had been Aethalides, then Euphorbus, who fought at Troy, then Hermotimus and Pyrrhus, a fisherman of Delos, before being born as Pythagoras.

Legacy

The account of his successive reincarnations makes him part of the ancient discussion of the transmigration of souls linked to Pythagorean doctrine.

Ancient sources: Heraclides Ponticus reports his place in the tradition about Pythagoras, and lists of Argonauts place him aboard the Argo.

Trivia

A memory without forgetting. According to the account, his soul forgot nothing even after death, which is why it remembered its earlier lives.
