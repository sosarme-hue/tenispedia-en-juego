Nereid of the calm sea in Greek myth

Galene

Galene is a Nereid in Greek mythology, personifying the calm of the sea. She matters because she shows how the Greeks imagined the peace of the waters as a divine figure.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Galene was one of the fifty Nereids, daughters of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and the Oceanid Doris. Hesiod lists them, but says little about the deeds of each one.

Other sources offer variants: Euripides calls her a daughter of Pontus, that is, of the sea, and Callimachus calls her Galenaia. She may be identical with her sister Galatea, a name that became popular in the eighteenth century.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes a statue of her, placed beside one of Thalassa, as an offering in the temple of Poseidon at Corinth. There is no evidence of a separate cult with its own festivals or sanctuaries.

Legacy

Her name was given to the main belt asteroid 427 Galene.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Euripides, Callimachus and Pausanias, who mention her as a Nereid, a daughter of the sea, or an offering at Corinth.

Trivia

Asteroid named after her. The main belt asteroid 427 Galene takes its name from her.
A maenad on a vase. A vase painting shows her as a maenad, a follower of Dionysus.
