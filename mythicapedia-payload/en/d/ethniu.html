goddess in Irish mythology, daughter of Balor and mother of Lugh

Ethniu

Ethniu is a female figure in Irish mythology, daughter of the Fomorian leader Balor and mother of the hero Lugh. Her story of a risky birth of triplets links her to the tale of Perseus in Greek myth.

The myth

In the early texts, Ethniu is the daughter of Balor, leader of the Fomorians, and the mother of Lugh. Her union with Cian of the Tuatha Dé Danann is presented as a simple dynastic marriage, but later folklore preserves a more elaborate tale.

In a folktale recorded by John O'Donovan in 1835, Balor tried to escape a druid's prophecy that his own grandson would kill him, so he imprisoned Ethniu in a tower on Tory Island, away from all men. A man named Mac Cinnfhaelaidh, helped by the leanan sídhe Biróg, reached her and seduced her. Ethniu gave birth to triplets, but Balor ordered them drowned. The messenger drowned two, but the third fell into the harbour and Biróg rescued him. The boy was fostered by Goibniu the smith and eventually killed Balor. Comparison with the Cath Maige Tuired and the Lebor Gabála Érenn identifies the boy as Lugh.

The genealogies vary. In some traditions Ethniu is the daughter of Delbaeth, mother of the Dagda and Ogma, and wife of Nuada; in a variant of the birth of Aengus she is the wife of Elcmar, seduced by the Dagda, and may be a double of Boann. The Baile in Scáil names her as Ethliu, and the Banshenchas gives her true name as Feada.

Legacy

Her name has many spellings, and the modern Irish name Eithne and forms such as Aine, Enya and Eithna, still used as personal names, derive from it.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Cath Maige Tuired, the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Baile in Scáil and the Banshenchas.

Trivia

A name with many forms. Her name is spelled in dozens of different ways. Some variants arose from confusion with the genitive, which was often taken for a nominative.
A male name in some texts. In some manuscripts, Ethniu appears as a male name, in the form Ethliu, son of Tigernmas.
