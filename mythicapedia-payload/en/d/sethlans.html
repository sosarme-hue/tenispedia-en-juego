Etruscan god of fire and the forge

Sethlans

Sethlans is the Etruscan god of fire, the forge and metalworking, and by extension of craftsmanship in general. He is considered one of the principal indigenous Etruscan deities and corresponds to the Greek Hephaestus and the Roman Vulcan, although their names share no etymology.

The myth

Few narratives about Sethlans have survived. He is depicted mainly as a divine craftsman, with the blacksmith's hammer and tongs, and sometimes wearing the pileus or the petasus. On several engraved bronze mirrors he appears working at his forge or in scenes with other gods, such as one in which he is assisted by Dionysus (Fufluns).

Worship and religion

There is no direct archaeological evidence of a cult of Sethlans, although he may have been worshipped in Populonia, a city whose wealth depended on metalworking and which issued coins bearing his image. He is also thought to have been venerated in Perugia.

Legacy

His figure appears on Etruscan bronze mirrors, where he is shown at his forge or alongside other deities, reflecting the importance of craftsmanship in Etruscan religion.

Trivia

A striking omission. His name does not appear on the bronze liver of Piacenza, the object used by haruspices for divination, where other deities do have their sectors.
A possible double. On square 34 of the Piacenza liver appears the god Velchans, which some have considered a double of Sethlans, although there is no formal proof.
