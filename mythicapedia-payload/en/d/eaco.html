king of Aegina and judge of the dead in Greek myth

Aeacus

Aeacus was the king of the island of Aegina, a son of Zeus and the nymph Aegina, renowned for his justice and piety. According to legend, after his death he became one of the three judges of the underworld, and he was the ancestor of the Aeacidae, the line that included Achilles and Ajax.

The myth

According to the myth, Aeacus was born on the island of Oenone, which later took the name Aegina in honour of his mother, whom Zeus had carried there to shield her from her parents' anger. When the island was still uninhabited and Aeacus felt lonely, he begged Zeus for companions, and the god turned the island's ants into men. These people were called Myrmidons, after the Greek word for ant, and later followed Peleus and fought at Troy alongside Achilles.

Other accounts tell that Hera, jealous, punished the island with a plague of serpents and a drought that killed nearly all its people, until Zeus repopulated it with the transformed ants. During a drought that afflicted Greece, the Delphic oracle declared that only Aeacus could ask Zeus for rain; he made sacrifices and the rain came.

With Endeis he had Peleus and Telamon, and with the Nereid Psamathe he had Phocus. His sons killed Phocus out of jealousy during a contest, and Aeacus exiled them both. Some versions say he also helped Apollo and Poseidon build the walls of Troy, and an oracle foretold that the city would fall to his descendants. After death he served as a judge in the underworld alongside Minos and Rhadamanthus, and according to Plato he was concerned with the souls of Europeans.

Worship and religion

Aeacus had sanctuaries in Aegina and Athens. The Aeginetans regarded him as the guardian deity of their island and celebrated the Aeacea in his honour. In Aegina a square temple enclosed by white marble walls, the Aeaceum, was built, and his tomb was believed to lie beneath its altar. The Aeginetans also erected a temple to Zeus Panhellenius in gratitude for the rain.

Legacy

The name Aeacidae was given to the descendants of Aeacus, including Achilles, whom Homer calls Aiakides. The kings of Epirus and Olympias, mother of Alexander the Great, claimed descent from this lineage.

Ancient sources: Pindar recounts the story of the walls of Troy, Ovid narrates the plague of Aegina, Aristophanes presents him in The Frogs, and Homer refers to Achilles as Aiakides.

Trivia

The ants of Aegina. According to myth, the Myrmidons were born from ants transformed by Zeus, and their name comes from the Greek word myrmex, meaning ant.
A judge in the underworld. Aristophanes makes him a character in The Frogs, where he sentences Dionysus, disguised as Heracles, to torture by various monsters.
