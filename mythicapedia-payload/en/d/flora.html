Roman goddess of flowers and spring

Flora

Flora is an ancient Italic goddess of flowers, fruit trees and spring, honoured by the Romans with her own festival and a dedicated priest. She matters because she embodies the yearly renewal of life in Roman religion, and because ancient writers already identified her with the Greek nymph Chloris.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Flora is the "Mother of Flowers", a deity linked to the cycle of vegetation. Her name comes from Latin flos, "flower", and the same root appears in the Oscan goddess of flowers, showing that her cult spread among the Italic peoples.

Latin poets considered her the Roman version of the Greek nymph Chloris, a process scholars call interpretatio graeca. According to Ovid, the Hellenized Flora was married to Favonius, the west wind also called Zephyr, and her companion was Hercules. The legend says Flora fled from Favonius, but he caught her, married her and gave her dominion over the flowers. In the Fasti, Ovid presents her as a nymph named Chloris who, once seized by Zephyr, received power over plants as her dowry.

Lactantius Placidus tells a far less solemn version: a former courtesan named Flora left her fortune to the Roman people, and the games in her honour arose from that gift. The Senate, he says, later gave the story a more dignified form, presenting her as a goddess who protected flowers.

Worship and religion

Flora had her own flamen, the Floralis, one of the flamines minores. Her festival, the Floralia, was held from 28 April to 3 May, with drinking, flowers and stage entertainments: for five days farces and mimes were performed, and a sixth day was given over to hunting goats and hares. According to the sources, the festival was instituted in 240 BCE and her temple was built in 238 BCE on the advice of the Sibylline books. On 23 May the Rosalia, another flower festival, was also held. Varro attributes to King Titus Tatius the dedication of an altar to her, which suggests a very old cult.

Legacy

Flora's name has passed into language to mean the plants of a region, and her figure inspired a long artistic tradition, with works by Botticelli, Titian and Rembrandt. Tchaikovsky did not choose her, but the 1894 ballet The Awakening of Flora made her a stage protagonist.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions her in the Fasti, and Varro, Ennius and Lucretius cite her as a goddess of spring.

Trivia

Festival of farces. During the Floralia, farces and mimes were performed, and women wore brightly coloured clothing that was normally forbidden to them.
An almost courtesan goddess. Lactantius Placidus says the story behind her cult began with a former courtesan who left her fortune to the Roman people.
