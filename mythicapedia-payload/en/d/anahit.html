Armenian goddess of fertility and water

Anahit

Anahit is the Armenian goddess of fertility, birth, water and wisdom, and in earlier times also of war. Her cult, probably borrowed from Iranian and Near Eastern traditions, was among the most important in the Armenian pantheon before the country's conversion to Christianity.

The myth

According to Armenian tradition, Anahit was a daughter of Aramazd, the great supreme god, and sister of Mher, Astghik and Nane. Ancient texts present her as a chaste and fertile figure, benefactress of the whole human race and mother of all knowledge, as King Trdat invokes her in the account of Agathangelos.

Sources do not agree on her links with other deities: the historian Berossus identified her with Aphrodite, while medieval Armenian scribes linked her with Artemis. Some modern scholars also relate her to the Mesopotamian Inanna and to the Iranian Anahita, although these connections rest on interpretation rather than direct testimony.

Worship and religion

Her main sanctuary stood at Erez in Akilisene, whose temple, according to Plutarch, was the wealthiest and noblest in Armenia. It was staffed by priestesses from eminent families who served there before marrying. Kings were expected to make an annual pilgrimage for the festival of the month Navasard, marked by dance, music and competitions, and the sick came seeking recovery. Strabo also mentions rites of sacred prostitution, a custom not recorded by later Christian writers. Her worship disappeared after Armenia's conversion in the early 4th century.

Legacy

After Christianity, her image as a chaste and fertile mother became associated with the Virgin Mary, and her name remains very popular among Armenian women. A corona on Venus bears her name, and her image appears on Armenian coins and stamps.

Ancient sources: Agathangelos, in his History of the Armenians, and the Greco-Roman authors Strabo, Plutarch, Pliny the Elder and Dio Cassius, who mention her in their accounts.

In art

Trivia

Golden statue. Pliny the Elder reports that Mark Antony's soldiers smashed a huge solid gold statue of the goddess, and the Armenians later erected a new golden statue at Erez.
A region named after her. According to Pliny and Dio Cassius, the region of Akilisene came to be known as Anaïtica, after the goddess.
