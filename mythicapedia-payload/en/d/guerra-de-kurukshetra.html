battle in the Indian epic Mahabharata

Kurukshetra War

The Kurukshetra War is the great final battle of the Mahabharata, the Hindu epic of India. It pits the Kauravas against the Pandavas, two groups of cousins from the same family, and provides the setting for the dialogues of the Bhagavad Gita.

The myth

The Mahabharata follows several generations of the Kuru clan. Its central conflict is between the five Pandava brothers and their hundred cousins, the Kauravas, over the throne of Hastinapura, near present-day New Delhi. After negotiations to divide the kingdom fail, both sides gather allies from across India: Drupada and Shikhandi support the Pandavas, while Bhishma, Drona, Karna and Shalya side with the Kauravas.

Krishna plays a pivotal role as mediator and as Arjuna's charioteer. Before the fighting begins, he makes a final peace mission to Hastinapura, but Duryodhana humiliates him in the royal court, which amounts to an open declaration of war. On the battlefield, Arjuna hesitates to fight his relatives and teachers, and Krishna offers him a teaching on duty and existence, later known as the Bhagavad Gita.

The battle took place at Kurukshetra, the field of the Kurus, which according to the text was chosen because sins committed there would be absolved by the sanctity of the land. It lasted eighteen days. The Pandava army, led by Dhristadyumna, had seven divisions with about 1,530,900 men, while the Kaurava army, under Bhishma, had eleven divisions with 2,405,700. According to the account, the war was devastating, and only eight Pandavas and four Kauravas survived.

Legacy

A huge bas-relief 94 metres long depicting the battle decorates the walls of the Angkor Wat temple in Cambodia. Its historicity remains debated: the Mahabharata greatly expanded and reshaped the war, and while popular tradition dates it to 3102 BCE, marking the start of the Kali Yuga, scholars propose very different dates, from the ninth century BCE onward.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata itself, whose sixth to tenth books (parvas) narrate the battle, and the Rigveda, which mentions the Battle of the Ten Kings as a possible core of the story.

Trivia
