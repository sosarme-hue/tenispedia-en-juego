Aztec god of nocturnal roads

Tlacotzontli

Tlacotzontli is an Aztec god of the roads and protector of those who travelled at night, honoured by those who crossed the paths of Mexica territory. He is said to be the son of Coatlicue, the mother goddess of Aztec tradition.

The myth

The reference texts offer few details about his deeds, and no full account of his origin survives.

What is recorded is his role as guardian of the roads, the one to whom travellers entrusted their journeys. In the sources he appears mainly linked to the night and to the paths.

His name is also written in several ways, such as Tlacotontli or Tlaco, reflecting the variety of transcriptions from Nahuatl.

Worship and religion

To secure a safe journey, travellers would draw blood by cutting themselves with thorn branches as a sign of devotion. Paper banners stained with blood and rubber were also made and offered to this god.
