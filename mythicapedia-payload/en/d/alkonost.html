woman-headed bird of Russian folklore

Alkonost

The Alkonost is a legendary bird of Russian folklore with the head of a beautiful woman, whose song makes listeners forget everything they know. It is usually paired with its counterpart, the Sirin, and symbolizes joy and paradise.

The myth

In Russian folklore, the Alkonost has the body of a bird and the face of a woman. Its song is so lovely that those who hear it forget all they know and no longer want anything, much like the sirens of Greek myth.

Tradition holds that she lays her eggs on a beach and lets them roll into the sea. While the eggs are in the ocean there are no storms or disasters, but when they hatch, violent storms break out. The Alkonost lives in Rai, where the dead dwell. In popular tales, on the morning of the Apple Feast of the Saviour the Sirin weeps sadly in the orchard, and in the afternoon the Alkonost arrives, rejoices and laughs, and shakes dew from her wings, giving healing powers to the fruit of the tree she sits on.

The name is linked to Alcyone, a demigoddess of Greek myth who was turned into a kingfisher. Some accounts trace her origin to the Xrafstar of Persian folklore.

Legacy

When Christianity reached Russia, the Alkonost became popular as a messenger from God who eased the pain of hardship, and also as an image of the soul ascending to Heaven. Viktor Vasnetsov painted her alongside the Sirin in 1896. The name has also been given to an early twentieth-century Russian publishing house of the Silver Age of poetry, to a minor character in the opera The Legend of the Invisible City of Kitezh, to a rare minion in the card game Mythgard, and to a Russian epic folk metal band formed in Tatarstan in 1995.

Trivia

A pair with the Sirin. The Alkonost almost always appears alongside the Sirin, a woman-headed bird of opposite character: the Sirin is linked to misfortune and the Alkonost to joy.
Storms at hatching. While her eggs drift in the sea there are no storms, but when they hatch, violent tempests break out.
