mythical Himalayan utopia from the novel Lost Horizon

Shangri-La

Shangri-La is a fictional valley created by British writer James Hilton in his 1933 novel Lost Horizon, a hidden refuge in the mountains of Tibet where people live for centuries. Today the name refers to any earthly paradise, especially a Himalayan utopia.

The myth

In the novel, a group of survivors of a plane crash reaches a lamasery, a Tibetan Buddhist monastery, at the western end of the Kunlun Mountains. There time seems to stand still: the inhabitants age very slowly and live hundreds of years longer than other human beings. The valley, cut off from the outside world, is gently guided from the monastery.

Hilton drew, as he said in 1936, on the travel diary of the French missionaries Évariste Huc and Joseph Gabet, who journeyed between Peking and Lhasa in 1844-1846, and on the legend of Shambhala, a hidden paradise that Buddhist scriptures mention as a source of wisdom, to be revealed only when humanity is ready. Ancient Tibetan scriptures also name Khembalung, one of the beyul, hidden lands that tradition credits Padmasambhava with creating as sacred refuges for Buddhists in times of strife.

The name seems to combine Tibetan words: Shang, a district of Ü-Tsang; ri, meaning «mountain»; and la, «mountain pass». Before 1933 there is no clear record of a place with that name, so many regard it as a Western invention.

Worship and religion

Shangri-La has no cult of its own, as it is a literary creation. Shambhala and the beyul, however, belong to Tibetan Buddhist tradition, where they are regarded as sacred places and spiritual refuges.

Legacy

The novel was a huge success, translated into 34 languages, and in 1939 it appeared as a pocket book, a development that transformed the book trade. Two films were called Lost Horizon, Frank Capra's 1937 version, which won an Oscar, and Charles Jarrott's 1973 version. In 1942 President Franklin Roosevelt named his retreat Shangri-La, a name Eisenhower changed to Camp David in 1953. In 2001 Zhongdian county in Yunnan was officially renamed Shangri-La to attract tourism. The name appears on luxury hotels, the Shangri-La Dialogue security conference, a 1956 Broadway musical and the Japanese web novel series Shangri-La Frontier.

Ancient sources: Ancient Buddhist scriptures mention Shambhala as a source of wisdom, and ancient Tibetan scriptures name Khembalung among the beyul.

Trivia

A presidential name. In 1942 President Franklin Roosevelt named his retreat Shangri-La, and Eisenhower renamed it Camp David in 1953.
A town with a borrowed name. In 2001 Zhongdian county was renamed Shangri-La, and in 2014 it was upgraded to a county-level city.
