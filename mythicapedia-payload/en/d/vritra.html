serpent demon of Vedic myth, enemy of Indra

Vritra

Vritra is an asura who takes the form of a serpent or dragon and personifies drought. In the Vedas he held back the waters of the world until Indra slew him, a battle that became one of the founding myths of Vedic religion, which predates Hinduism.

The myth

According to the Rig Veda, Vritra kept the world's waters captive behind ninety-nine fortresses. The combat began soon after Indra's birth, when he had drunk a great quantity of soma at the house of the sage Tvashtri, who forged the thunderbolt for him. Vritra broke both of Indra's jaws, but Indra eventually knocked him down, and as he fell he crushed his own fortresses. For this feat Indra became known as Vritrahan, 'slayer of Vritra'. Indra then attacked Danu, Vritra's mother.

Later Puranic versions tell that Tvashtri created Vritra to avenge his son, killed by Indra. After a truce brokered by Vishnu, Indra killed him with sea-foam, which was neither wet nor dry. In the Bhagavata Purana, by contrast, Vritra is a devotee of Vishnu, and he is slain with a weapon made from the bones of the sage Dadhichi.

Legacy

His name survives in Indra's epithet Vritrahan. His role as a serpent who holds back water links him to Norse Jörmungandr and Greek Typhon.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda, the Taittiriya Samhita, the Mahabharata and the Puranas, especially the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A name meaning obstruction. Vritra literally means 'cover' or 'obstacle', a reference to his holding back the waters.
Victory with allies. In one version of the story, Indra persuaded the devas Varuna, Soma and Agni, who had previously sided with Vritra, to fight against him.
