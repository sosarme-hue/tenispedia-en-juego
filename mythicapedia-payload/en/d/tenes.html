eponymous hero of the island of Tenedos

Tenes

Tenes is the eponymous hero of the island of Tenedos, in the Aegean. Tradition makes him a son of Apollo or of King Cycnus, and his story, shaped by false accusations and death, explains a local sanctuary and a Greek proverb.

The myth

According to the myth, Cycnus's second wife, Philonome, falsely accused Tenes with the help of the flute player Eumolpus. Cycnus sealed Tenes and his sister Hemithea in a chest and cast it into the sea, but it washed up on Leucophrys, an island later renamed Tenedos, where the islanders made Tenes their king.

When Cycnus later sought reconciliation, Tenes cut his ship's moorings with an axe. Afterwards, Achilles killed him without knowing that Apollo protected him, while Tenes was defending his sister, and buried him where his sanctuary was later built.

Worship and religion

According to Plutarch, at Tenedos the people barred flute players from entering Tenes's sanctuary and forbade mention of Achilles's name within it.

Legacy

Agamemnon allowed the Trojan prisoners of war to found a city named Tenea after him. Greeks also used the phrase "Tenedian human" for people with a frightening appearance.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, Diodorus Siculus, Pseudo-Apollodorus, Pausanias and Tzetzes each recount different versions of his story.

Trivia

A Greek proverb. Greeks said "Tenedian human" of people with a frightening look. The saying refers to a law of Tenes that placed a man with an axe behind the judge to strike the convicted man after his plea.
A forbidden name. In Tenes's sanctuary on Tenedos the name of Achilles could not be spoken, because he had killed Tenes.
