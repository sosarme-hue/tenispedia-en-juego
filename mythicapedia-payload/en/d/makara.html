hybrid sea creature of Hindu mythology

Makara

The makara is a hybrid sea creature found in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain art. It serves as the vehicle of Varuna, god of the waters, and as the emblem of Kama, god of love, and it corresponds to the sign of Capricorn in Hindu astrology.

The myth

According to Vedic texts, when Indra ruled the heavens, Varuna became lord of the seas and was pictured riding the makara, which was called the water monster vehicle.

Tradition also links the makara to Pradyumna, son of Krishna. One of his epithets in the Harivamsa is Makaradhvaja, meaning he whose banner is the crocodile, and in the Mahabharata the makara is associated with both Pradyumna and Kama, suggesting they are the same figure. The river goddess Ganga also has it as her vehicle.

Worship and religion

The makara is one of the most common motifs in Hindu and Buddhist temples. It runs along walls in rows, forms handrails on staircases or arches over doorways, and in the toranas framing a deity its jaws meet around the kirtimukha, the Face of Glory. It guards thresholds and royal thrones, and as an aquatic creature it is linked to water, the source of life and fertility. Deities such as Shiva and Vishnu wear makara-shaped earrings, called makarakundalas. In some regions a bride was once given a makara-shaped bracelet as a wedding gift.

Legacy

Its image remains part of the architecture and religious art of South Asia. In Java it appears combined with the kala head in the kala-makara reliefs of ancient temples. Its name gives the sign of Capricorn in Indian astrology. Some researchers have suggested that it was inspired by the dugong, the Ganges river dolphin or the Greek ketos, though these remain unproven hypotheses.

Ancient sources: The Harivamsa, which mentions it in an epithet of Pradyumna, the Mahabharata, and the Jain Sūryaprajñapti, cited by scholars in discussing its possible origins.

Trivia

A monster of many parts. Old Sinhalese illustrations describe it with parts of six or seven animals, such as an elephant's trunk, crocodile jaws and lion's feet.
A treasure on Kubera's list. In Sanskrit texts, the makara also appears as one of the nine treasures of Kubera, treasurer of the gods.
