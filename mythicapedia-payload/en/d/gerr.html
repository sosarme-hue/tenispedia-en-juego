Norse giantess and goddess, wife of Freyr

Gerðr

Gerðr is a giantess and goddess in Norse mythology, renowned for her beauty and married to the god Freyr. Her story, told mainly in the poem Skírnismál of the Poetic Edda, explains how she came to wed Freyr after a tense negotiation, and her name is linked to the idea of an enclosed space.

The myth

According to the myth, Freyr once sat on Hlidskjálf, Odin's high seat from which all worlds could be seen. Looking toward Jötunheimr, the land of the giants, he spotted a great hall and a young woman walking toward it. Her skin shone so brightly that it lit up all the worlds, and Freyr fell deeply in love. From then on he could neither eat nor sleep, and he barely spoke to the other gods.

His parents, Njörðr and Skaði, sent his servant Skírnir to find out what was troubling him. Skírnir rode to the hall of Gymir, Gerðr's father, and after getting past the fierce dogs at the gate, he managed to speak with her. Gerðr turned down the offers of eleven golden apples and the ring Draupnir. Skírnir then threatened her with his sword, with curses, and with a future of loneliness and misery. Only when he carved runes and chanted a spell did Gerðr agree to meet Freyr nine nights later at a place called Barri.

For this service Freyr gave Skírnir his magic sword, which fought giants by itself. Tradition holds that this loss would matter at Ragnarök, when Freyr, unarmed, would face the fire giant Surtr. In other accounts Gerðr is the daughter of Gymir and the giantess Aurboða, and she is named among the lovers of Odin, though she is probably confused with another giantess here.

Legacy

Her name, linked to an enclosure, has inspired modern readings that connect her with the earth and fertility.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Poetic Edda (Skírnismál), in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and in his Heimskringla.

Trivia

A sword for a bride. To win Gerðr, Freyr had to give up his magic sword, leaving him defenseless against his enemies. Some accounts say he later fought with deer antlers.
