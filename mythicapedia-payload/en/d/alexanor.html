Greek hero, son of Machaon and grandson of Asclepius

Alexanor

Alexanor is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Machaon and grandson of Asclepius. He was honored at Titane in Sicyonia, where he shared a cult with his grandfather, the god of healing.

The myth

According to the tradition, Alexanor deeply respected his grandfather and took great pride in his heritage. To honor him, he built a temple on the summit of Titane, an Asclepieion set within a grove of cypresses, with dwellings for those who came seeking the god's help.

Inside stood wooden statues (xoana) of Asclepius and Hygieia, and many believed the statue of the god had been carved by Alexanor himself. In time he too was worshipped there, and a statue of him stood inside the building. His brothers included Sphyros, who founded another Asclepieion at Argos, Polemocrates, Alcon, and others whose names are lost.

Worship and religion

Alexanor was worshipped as a hero at Titane, where sacrifices were offered to him, but only after sunset.
