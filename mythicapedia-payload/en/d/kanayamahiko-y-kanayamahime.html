Japanese deities of mines and metalworking

Kanayamahiko and Kanayamahime

Kanayamahiko and Kanayamahime are sibling deities in Japanese mythology who rule the mines. They protect mining, metallurgy and the craftspeople who work with metals.

The myth

According to the tradition, the goddess Izanami was severely burned in her genitals when she gave birth to Kagutsuchi, the god of fire. Later she fell gravely ill and vomited, and from that vomit Kanayamahiko and Kanayamahime were born.

Their names come from a Japanese word that refers to mines, which is why they are regarded as the rulers of mines. It is believed that metals are mythologically linked to Izanami's vomit.

Worship and religion

They are venerated at the Minamimiya-taisha shrine in Tarui (Gifu Prefecture) and at the Koganeyama Shrine on Mount Kinkasan in Ishinomaki (Miyagi Prefecture). They are also honored at shrines named Kaneyama found throughout Japan.

Trivia

Kaneyama shrines. Shrines named Kaneyama exist throughout Japan and are dedicated to these deities of the mines.
