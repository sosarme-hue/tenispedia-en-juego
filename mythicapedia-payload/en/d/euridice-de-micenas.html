Eurydice of Mycenae

Eurydice is a figure of Greek mythology, the daughter of Pelops and wife of Electryon, king of Mycenae. She matters mainly because she was the mother of Alcmena, who in turn gave birth to Heracles.

The myth

According to the Greek tradition, Eurydice was the daughter of Pelops and married Electryon, king of Mycenae and son of Perseus. With him she had Alcmena, who would later become the mother of Heracles, one of the most celebrated heroes of Greek myth. In this way, Eurydice appears as a link in a genealogy that joins the house of Perseus with the line of Heracles.

Accounts do not entirely agree about her, since in some versions her place as Electryon's wife is taken by Anaxo, the king's niece. This kind of shift is common in mythology, where the same figures appear with different family ties depending on the source or the era. Despite her quiet role, Eurydice stands as a key piece in the origin of the hero.

Trivia

The meaning of her name. Eurydice comes from Greek words meaning something like "wide justice", formed from eurys (wide) and dike (justice).
