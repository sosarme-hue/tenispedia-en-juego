Belphegor

Belphegor (also Belfagor or Belfegor) is a demon of Jewish and Christian tradition, descended from a Moabite god called Baal Pe'or. His name moved from the Bible into medieval and Renaissance demonology, and from there into literature and modern popular culture.

The myth

According to the Book of Numbers, the Israelites, camped at Shittim on the plains of Moab, began relations with local women and were lured into worshipping Baal Pe'or. God punished the idolatry with a plague that killed thousands. Moses ordered the idolaters executed, and the priest Phinehas killed an Israelite leader who was sleeping with a Midianite woman. Later texts credit the seduction to the advice of the prophet Balaam, and the story was used to discourage marriage with foreigners.

Over time the name came to designate a demon. Jewish commentators linked this god to nakedness and latrines, through a wordplay on the root p'r, meaning opening. In Christian demonology, Peter Binsfeld made him the chief demon of sloth, while Collin de Plancy described him as Hell's ambassador to France.

Worship and religion

Little is known about the worship of Baal Pe'or, and modern descriptions rely almost entirely on the Bible. As a demon, Belphegor had no temple or rite of his own; he appears instead in lists of demons, grimoires and books against witchcraft.

Legacy

Machiavelli made him the protagonist of the novella Belfagor arcidiavolo, in which the demon comes to earth to find out whether marriage is worse than Hell. Ottorino Respighi wrote an opera based on the story, premiered at La Scala in 1923. Milton included him in Paradise Lost, and in later times he appears in French popular fiction, the TV series Supernatural and the Persona video game series.

Ancient sources: The Book of Numbers (25) and Deuteronomy (3:29), in the Greek Septuagint and the Latin Vulgate.

Trivia

A Polish aircraft. The Polish utility aeroplane PZL M-15 Belphegor, built in the 1970s, was named after the demon because of its strange looks and the noise of its jet engine.
Belphegor's prime. The palindromic prime 1000000000000066600000000000001 is known as Belphegor's prime, because it contains the number 666 surrounded by zeros.
