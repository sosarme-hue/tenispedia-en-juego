Arcadian lover of Heracles in Greek mythology

Phialo

Phialo is, in Greek mythology, an Arcadian young woman loved by Heracles and mother of his son Aechmagoras. Her story is linked to the name of a spring in Arcadia.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phialo was the daughter of the Arcadian hero Alcimedon. While Heracles was in Arcadia, he seduced her on Mount Ostracina, and she bore a son, Aechmagoras. When her father found out, he drove mother and child from their cave and left them on the mountain to die.

The baby's crying caught the attention of a jay, which imitated the sound and led Heracles to the tree where Alcimedon had tied his daughter. The hero freed her, tended her wounds and saved the child, who grew to manhood. The story is tied to the name of the nearby spring, called Cissa, from the Greek word for jay.

Legacy

The English writer Stephen Gosson titled one of his books The Ephemerides of Phialo, a work meant to resemble Hesiod's Works and Days, and opened it with a discussion of the sacrifices offered to Heracles.

Trivia

A spring named after a bird. The nearby spring was named Cissa, meaning jay. The name is tied to the story of the bird that guided Heracles.
A modern explanation. Robert Graves considers the story an invented anecdote to explain the spring's name, which may have been sacred to a clan with the jay as its totem.
