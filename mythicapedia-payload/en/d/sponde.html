Greek mythology's Hour who presided over the libations after lunch

Sponde

Sponde is one of the Horae of Greek mythology, divinities who governed the parts of the day. She was the seventh of them and presided over the hour of libations poured after the midday meal.

The myth

According to the tradition, Sponde was one of the twelve Horae, sisters who looked after the different moments of the day. Her hour came after lunch, when libations were poured in honour of the gods. Like her sisters, her role was to mark the passing of time and the rhythm of the day rather than to take part in great deeds.

The stories place the Horae as daughters of Helios, the Sun, or of Chronos, Time, depending on the version. Alongside Sponde were Anatole, who heralded the sunrise, Auge, the first light, Messembria, noon, Dysis, sunset, and Arctus, the night sky, among others.

Trivia

Twelve sisters. The Horae formed a group of twelve sisters, each linked to a part of the day, from sunrise to the starry night.
An uncertain father. Accounts disagree about their parentage: some make them daughters of Helios, the Sun, and others of Chronos, Time.
