ancestor mother of the Children of Dôn in Welsh legend

Dôn

Dôn is an ancestor figure in Welsh legend and literature, usually given as the mother of a group called the Children of Dôn, including Gwydion, Arianrhod and Gilfaethwy. Her name is linked to the earth, and the Welsh called the constellation Cassiopeia the Court of Dôn.

The myth

According to Welsh tradition, Dôn is the mother of a large lineage. Her children include Gwydion, a magician and teller of deeds; Arianrhod, associated with the stars; Gilfaethwy, Gofannon, Amaethon, Lludd Llaw Eraint and Eufydd fab Dôn. The lists vary: the text Bonedd yr Arwyr (the Gentry of the Heroes) adds further names, such as Aidden, Cynan, Digant, Elawg, Elestron, Eunydd, Hedd, Hunawg and Idwal.

Antiquarians of the early modern period generally considered Dôn a male figure, which shows that her gender was not fixed in the sources. Some scholars propose that her name comes from a Proto-Indo-European root meaning "earth", and that the Children of Dôn could be related to the Greek Titans, though this link is a modern hypothesis.

Legacy

The Welsh called the Milky Way Caer Gwydion and the constellation Corona Borealis Caer Arianrhod, so Dôn's lineage became tied to the night sky.

Ancient sources: Dôn appears in medieval Welsh tales such as the Mabinogion and in the genealogy of Bonedd yr Arwyr.

Trivia

Names in the sky. In Welsh tradition the Milky Way was called Caer Gwydion, the Castle of Gwydion.
A male figure. Some early modern antiquarians described Dôn as a male figure.
