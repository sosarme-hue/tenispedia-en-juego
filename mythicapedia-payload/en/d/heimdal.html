guardian god of Asgard in Norse mythology

Heimdall

Heimdall is the watchman god of Norse mythology, guardian of Asgard and of the rainbow bridge Bifröst. His role as sentinel, his extraordinary senses and his fated death at Ragnarök make him one of the most distinctive figures of the Norse pantheon.

The myth

According to the tradition, Heimdall is the son of Odin and nine giantess sisters, the maidens of the waves, who nursed him with the strength of the earth, the moisture of love and the warmth of the sun. He grew at astonishing speed and ran to join his father in Asgard just as the gods were admiring the rainbow bridge Bifröst, freshly built from fire, air and water.

Worried about the frost giants, the gods appointed him sentinel. His senses were so keen that he could hear grass growing and wool on the backs of sheep, see a hundred miles by day and by night, and needed less sleep than a bird. He received a gleaming sword and the horn Gjallarhorn, which he was to sound on sighting the enemy, its blast waking every being in the sky, the earth and Niflheim. He lived at Himinbjörg, at the head of the bridge, where he served the gods mead. He always appeared in gleaming white armour, which earned him the name of the bright god, and his golden teeth flashed like the sun when he smiled.

One of his best-known episodes is the theft of the necklace Brísingamen. One night Loki slipped into Freya's hall, Folkvang, first as a fly and then as a flea, reaching her bed to work open the clasp without waking her. Heimdall heard him from afar, pursued him and attacked him through a series of transformations: blue flame, polar bear, seal. In that final struggle Heimdall won back the necklace and returned it to Freya.

In the poem Lokasenna, Loki mocks him, saying his back is always muddy and that he is doomed to keep watch over the gods. In Þrymskviða, when the gods seek to recover Thor's hammer, Heimdall proposes dressing Thor as a bride; he is described as the whitest of the Æsir and as having foresight like the Vanir.

At the end of time, Heimdall's horn will announce the battle between gods and giants, Ragnarök. He will fight and die at the hands of Loki, in a clash the sources present as a fated outcome. According to a tradition preserved in the Rígsþula, under the name Rígr he walked the roads, spent the nights between couples and so gave rise to the three social classes: nobles, freemen and thralls.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Völuspá, Grímnismál, Lokasenna, Þrymskviða, Rígsþula and Hrafnagaldr Óðins), Snorri's Prose Edda, Heimskringla, and the lost poem Heimdalargaldr, of which two lines survive.

Trivia

Golden teeth. His name Gullintanni literally means the one with the golden teeth, and his smile was said to shine like the sun.
A lead spindle whorl in England. A lead spindle whorl found at Saltfleetby, in eastern England, in 2010, may name him in an Old Norse inscription dated between the 10th and 12th centuries.
