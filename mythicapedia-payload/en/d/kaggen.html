San trickster god of southern Africa

ǀKaggen

ǀKaggen is a trickster god and demiurge of the San people of southern Africa, often appearing as a praying mantis. His figure explains the origin of many animals and of the hunting that sustained his people.

The myth

ǀKaggen can transform into any animal, most often a praying mantis, though he also takes the form of a bull eland, a louse, a snake or a caterpillar. His wife, mother of the bees, and his adopted daughter complete his family.

His favourite animal was the eland, which he had hidden near a secluded cliff. When his sons Cogaz and Gewi killed it while hunting, unaware of their father's affection, ǀKaggen was angry. Gewi churned the blood in a pot, and as it splashed, snakes appeared; when scattered, it became hartebeests. Only after his wife cleaned the pot and added more blood and fat from the heart did ǀKaggen sprinkle the mixture on the ground, and a great herd of elands emerged to feed his people. That is why the San attribute the eland's wildness to its being killed before it was ready.

Trivia

Mongoose variant. A version recorded by the scholar David Lewis-Williams tells how the mongoose, ǀKaggen's grandson, spies on his grandfather and reveals to his meerkat brothers where the eland was hidden.
A wild beast by birth. According to the San, the eland is wild because ǀKaggen's sons killed it before it was ready to be hunted.
