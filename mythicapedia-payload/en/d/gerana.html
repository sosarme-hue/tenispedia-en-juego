pygmy queen turned into a crane

Gerana

Gerana is a queen of the Pygmies in Greek mythology, turned into a crane by Hera. Her story explains the rivalry between Pygmies and cranes and warns against hubris.

The myth

According to Antoninus Liberalis, who attributes the tale to the poet Boeus, Gerana was a very beautiful but graceless young woman who scorned Hera and Artemis. She married Nicodamas, a sensible man, and had a son, Mopsus. Hera, enraged, stretched her neck and turned her into a crane. To stay close to her child she flew from roof to roof, but the Pygmies drove her away with weapons, and so the enmity between Pygmies and cranes began. Athenaeus and Aelian say her people worshipped her as a goddess. Athena wove her into her tapestry as a warning to Arachne.

Worship and religion

According to the sources, the Pygmies honoured her as a goddess. No temples or specific rites survive, and there is no living cult.

Legacy

Her name comes from the Greek geranos, meaning crane, a word related to the English crane. It inspired the ancient parody of the Iliad known as the Geranomachia.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis, Athenaeus, Aelian and Ovid, who narrate or mention her story, and the Iliad, which seems to allude to it.

Trivia

A bird's name. Gerana takes her name from the Greek word for crane, the bird she was transformed into.
A tortoise in the family. Athenaeus says Gerana and Nicodamas became the parents of a tortoise.
