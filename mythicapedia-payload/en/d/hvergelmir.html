Hvergelmir

Hvergelmir is a spring of icy waters located in Niflheim, the misty realm of ice in Norse mythology. According to medieval sources, the rivers of the cosmos flow from it, and the dragon Níðhöggr dwells in its depths.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Hvergelmir is one of three great springs at the primary roots of Yggdrasil, the cosmic ash tree, alongside Urðarbrunnr and Mímisbrunnr. Its Old Norse name is usually rendered as 'bubbling boiling spring', although its water is icy.

The Poetic Edda mentions it in a single stanza of the poem Grímnismál: liquid dripping from the antlers of the stag Eikþyrnir, who feeds on the branches of the tree, flows into the spring, the source of all waters. The Prose Edda, written by Snorri Sturluson, repeats this, adds that Hvergelmir lies in Niflheim, and describes countless snakes living in it alongside Níðhöggr, who gnaws at the roots of the tree. Numerous rivers flow from it; some lead to the dwellings of the gods, while Gjöll and Leipt reach Hel.

The Prose Edda also says that Níðhöggr torments the bodies of the dead, making Hvergelmir even more dreadful than Náströnd, the shore of the wicked.

Ancient sources: It appears in the poem Grímnismál of the Poetic Edda and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which describes it in Gylfaginning and elsewhere.
