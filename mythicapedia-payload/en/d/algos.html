Greek personification of pain

Algos

Algos is the Greek personification of pain and grief, both physical and emotional. In the plural, the algea were female spirits said to bring men laments and tears.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Algos was a daughter of Eris, Strife, with no father named. Later, Hyginus imagined her as a daughter of Aether and Earth, so her parentage varies from author to author.

Tradition places her on the banks of the Cocytus, the river of the underworld. Together with some of her brothers, she helped Hephaestus forge the notorious necklace of Harmonia. She is naturally linked with Ezis, Misery, and Penthos, Mourning, and stands opposite the Graces and Hedone, Pleasure.

In two Theban tragedies, the ghost of Laius demands that Oedipus be expelled and recalls several daimones that plague the land, Algos among them. A generation later, blinded, Oedipus recalls that host of woes, Pain included. Other authors use different names: Alcman speaks of Achos, "suffering," and Anacreon of Lype, "grief."

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony makes her a daughter of Eris; Hyginus places her as a daughter of Aether and Earth, and Alcman and Anacreon use other terms for her.

Trivia

The necklace of Harmonia. Algos and some of her brothers took part in forging the necklace Hephaestus made for Harmonia, an object Greek tales link with misfortune.
Other names for pain. The poet Alcman called her Achos, "suffering," while Anacreon named Lype, "grief," as a separate figure who rests when Mete reigns.
