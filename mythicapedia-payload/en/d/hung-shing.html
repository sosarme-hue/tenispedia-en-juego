god of the southern seas in Chinese folk religion

Hung Shing

Hung Shing, also called Tai Wong, is a deity of Chinese folk religion associated with the southern seas. According to tradition he was a Tang dynasty official who looked after fishermen and traders, and his worship spread mainly through Guangdong and Hong Kong.

The myth

The most popular story tells of Hung Hei, an upright official of the Tang dynasty serving at Pun Yue, in what is now Guangdong province. He promoted the study of astronomy, geography and mathematics, and had an observatory built to track weather changes, which benefited fishermen and sea traders above all.

He died young. A Tang emperor spread his virtues across the country and bestowed a posthumous title on him. Legend adds that he continued to protect people from natural disasters and appeared during storms to rescue many of them.

Worship and religion

Many temples were built across southern China, especially in Guangdong and Hong Kong, where they are called Hung Shing Miu or Tai Wong Miu. His festival is held on the 13th day of the second month of the lunar calendar. Officials and fishermen of the region were the main promoters of his worship.

Trivia

Possible origin in Zhurong. Modern scholars believe Hung Shing derives from the worship of Zhurong, the fire deity and god of the southern seas. That worship predates the Tang dynasty.
An imperial title. The title South Sea God was conferred by Emperor Wen of Sui, and more titles were added under the Song and Yuan dynasties.
