serpent dragon of Russian folklore

Zmei Gorynich

The zmei is the dragon or serpent of Russian folktales and epic ballads, sometimes taking human form while keeping dragon-like traits. Zmei Gorynich is one of the most famous, along with Tugarin Zmeyevich, and both often face the bogatyr, the heroes of the epics.

The myth

In the stories, the zmei usually appears as an enemy of the bogatyr, such as Dobrynya Nikitich or Alyosha Popovich. Older epic ballads hardly describe its appearance; later sources picture it covered in green or red scales and with iron claws. It may be male or female: in prose folktales it is usually male, while in epic ballads it is often female. Only the males carry off or seduce princesses.

One of its signature traits is having several heads, three, six, nine or twelve. In one tale, the hero Ivan defeats creatures with three, six, nine and twelve heads on successive nights; the twelve-headed one was the hardest, and six men were needed to take its last head. Gorynich spits fire, flies and is linked to a body of water. It also takes the form of a handsome youth to seduce Ivan's sister or wife, and in another tale it appears as a golden goat. Tugarin, by contrast, keeps a human shape: he devours a whole swan, and his wings are sometimes explained in songs as paper ones fixed to his horse.

Legacy

Saint George, known in Russia as Egorii the Brave, is shown slaying a zmei rather than a Greek-style dragon. The scene appears in popular lubok prints and in many Russian icons.

Ancient sources: It appears in the byliny, oral epic ballads, and in the folktales collected by Alexander Afanasyev in the nineteenth century.

Trivia

Heads that grow back. In one variant, the severed heads of the Chudo-Iudo regrow if someone draws a line on them with the dragon's fiery finger.
A disguised intruder. In tale 204 the zmei disguises itself as a broom and other kitchen tools, but Ivan's animals detect the intruder.
