king or priest of Nemea, father of Opheltes

Lycurgus

Lycurgus is a figure of Greek myth, the priest of Nemean Zeus in the earliest versions and the king of Nemea in later accounts. His story is tied to the death of his son Opheltes and to the legendary origin of the Nemean Games.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Lycurgus was the son of Pheres and settled in Nemea after marrying Eurydice, with whom he had Opheltes. When the Seven against Thebes pass through Nemea, Hypsipyle, the child's nursemaid, sets him down on the ground to guide them to a spring, and a serpent kills him.

Lycurgus wants revenge on Hypsipyle, but the leaders, led by Amphiaraus, persuade him that the gods had decreed the death. Euripides presents him as a priest of Zeus, while Statius makes him an Argive king who stayed out of the war.

Worship and religion

In the earliest version, Lycurgus was the priest of Nemean Zeus. Pausanias describes a mound of earth at the site of the games that served as Lycurgus' tomb, near a sacred grove dedicated to Opheltes.

Ancient sources: Euripides (Hypsipyle, fragments), Statius (Thebaid), Apollodorus, Hyginus, Pausanias.

Trivia

The origin of the games. According to tradition, the funeral games held in honour of Opheltes gave rise to the Nemean Games.
A visited tomb. The geographer Pausanias, in the 2nd century, says he saw the tomb of Lycurgus, father of Opheltes, at the site of the Nemean Games.
