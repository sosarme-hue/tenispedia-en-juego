asura prince and devotee of Vishnu

Prahlada

Prahlada is an asura prince in Hindu scripture, known for his unwavering devotion to Vishnu despite his father's opposition. His story, in which Vishnu appears in several avatars, is one of the most beloved in Hinduism.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, Prahlada was born to Kayadhu and Hiranyakashipu, the asura king, who had received a boon from Brahma that made him nearly invulnerable. As a child, Prahlada kept worshipping Vishnu despite his father's threats. Hiranyakashipu tried to kill him in several ways: he poisoned him, had elephants trample him, and left him among venomous snakes, which formed a bed for him with their bodies.

His aunt Holika, immune to fire, tried to burn him while sitting on her lap, but she was the one who died in the flames. Finally, Narasimha, Vishnu's avatar with a man's body and a lion's head, emerged from a pillar in the palace and killed Hiranyakashipu at dusk on the threshold of the home. Prahlada became king of the asuras and, after his death, reached Vaikuntha, the abode of Vishnu. According to the Mahabharata, he governed one of the regions of the Patalas.

Worship and religion

The festival of Holi, which commemorates Holika's death, is linked to his story. Vaishnava traditions, which revere Vishnu, regard him as a great devotee and call him majayana. His veneration continues today, and Holi is celebrated across much of India.

Legacy

The Bhagavad Gita names him the greatest of the devotee daityas, and the Bhagavata Purana credits him with a doctrinal treatise on worshipping Vishnu. He remains a model of bhakti, loving devotion to God.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana, the Vishnu Purana, the Kurma Purana, the Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Bhagavad Gita.

Trivia

The festival of Holi. The Hindu festival of Holi is celebrated in memory of Holika, who died in the fire while Prahlada emerged unharmed.
Krishna's praise. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna says that among the daityas he is Prahlada, the great devotee.
A bed of snakes. According to the story, the venomous snakes he was thrown among formed a bed for the child with their bodies.
