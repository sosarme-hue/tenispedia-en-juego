Diomede (daughter of Lapithes)

Diomede is, in Greek mythology, the daughter of the eponymous king Lapithes and the mother of Hyacinth, the young man loved by Apollo. She appears mainly within the Lacedaemonian genealogy, where her story is bound up with that of her children.

The myth

According to the Library of Apollodorus, Diomede married Amyclas, and their children were Cynortas and Hyacinth. The story tells how Apollo loved Hyacinth and how, during a game, he killed him by accident while throwing the discus. Her son's tragedy is the episode for which Diomede is best remembered.

The Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, calls her "the one with lovely curls" and follows the story of Hyacinth. Pausanias adds a detail about the family: Hyacinth's sister was called Polybaea, and she died a virgin without leaving descendants. Diomede's myth thus centers more on the tragic fate of her children than on deeds of her own.

Ancient sources: The Library (attributed to Apollodorus), the Catalogue of Women (attributed to Hesiod) and Pausanias's Description of Greece.

Trivia

Diomede's lovely curls. The Catalogue of Women describes her as "the one with lovely curls", an epithet that highlights her beauty.
A nearly forgotten sister. Pausanias remembers Polybaea, Hyacinth's sister, who died unmarried and barely appears in other versions.
