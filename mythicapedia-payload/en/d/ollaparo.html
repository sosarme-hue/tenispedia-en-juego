Galician cyclops-like giant

Olláparo

The olláparo is a cannibal giant from Galician folklore, related to the cyclopes of classical tradition. It lives in caves and forests in the mountains of Lugo and Ourense and is known for its savagery and enormous appetite.

The myth

According to popular tales, the olláparo lives in caves in mountains and forests, alongside its women, who are described as even worse than the males. It is a wild, ravenous glutton that feeds on people. Its most distinctive feature is the single eye in the middle of its forehead, although some versions give it a second eye at the back of the head.

In appearance it is compared to the cyclopes of classical texts and to the Fomorians of Irish mythology, beings who also have two eyes arranged in the same way. The most famous among them is Balar, whose back eye was terrifying: it was said to cause the evil eye, which makes him comparable to Medusa.

Trivia

An eye that harms. The back eye of Balar, one of the Irish Fomorians, is described as able to cause the evil eye. For this reason it is often compared to Medusa.
Two traditions, one shared trait. The Galician olláparo resembles the classical cyclopes and the Irish Fomorians, all of them with a central eye or with eyes arranged in a similar way.
