Aztec god of the south wind

Huitztlampaehecatl

Huitztlampaehecatl is the god of the south wind in Mexica (Aztec) mythology. He is one of four wind personifications, each linked to a cardinal direction.

The myth

In Aztec mythology, Huitztlampaehecatl is one of four winds associated with the cardinal points. His domain is the south wind, the direction traditionally assigned to him. According to the tale, all these winds were subordinate to Ehecatl, the god of wind and an aspect of Quetzalcoatl.

Some accounts make him a brother of Cihuatecayotl, Tlalocayotl and Mictlanpachecatl, who personify the winds of the west, east and north. Another tradition describes a division of rule: Tezcatlipoca governs the north, Xipetotec the east, Quetzalcoatl the west and Huitzilopochtli the south, so the winds also answered to these lords of the quarters.
