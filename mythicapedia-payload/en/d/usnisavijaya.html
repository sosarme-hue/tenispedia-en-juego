Buddhist goddess of longevity and protection

Uṣṇīṣavijayā

Uṣṇīṣavijayā is a goddess of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, the personification of the Uṣṇīṣa Vijaya dhāraṇī, a protective chant associated with long life. Her name refers to the uṣṇīṣa, the topknot or cranial protuberance of the Buddha.

The myth

According to tradition, the dhāraṇī brings happiness to beings, prosperity, longevity, and the removal of karmic obstacles and calamities.

One widely told story says that the translator Buddhapālita, on Mount Wutai, met Mañjuśrī disguised as an old man, who urged him to bring the Sanskrit text to China.

Worship and religion

It was widely venerated in China from the Tang dynasty, and Mount Wutai became linked to her cult. In Nepal, among the Newars, her rites are still widely performed today.

Legacy

The legend of Mañjuśrī appears in Dunhuang murals, and the dhāraṇī was carved on pillars and stelae across much of China and the Far East.

Ancient sources: The Uṣṇīṣa Vijaya Dhāraṇī Sūtra, translated from Sanskrit into Chinese eight times between 679 and 988 CE.

Trivia

Long title of the sutra. One of its longer Sanskrit titles is Sarvadurgatipariśodhana Uṣṇīṣa Vijaya Dhāraṇī Sūtra, which refers to the purification of all unfortunate paths of rebirth.
Sacred mountain. In Chinese tradition, Mount Wutai is regarded as the place of enlightenment of Mañjuśrī, and it is where the legend of the text's translation is set.
