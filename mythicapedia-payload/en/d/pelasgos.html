legendary king of Argos

Pelasgus

Pelasgus was a king of Argos in Greek mythology who welcomed the Danaids, daughters of Aegyptus, when they fled from their cousins. He matters because, in Aeschylus' play, he embodies the hospitable ruler and gives his name to the legendary Pelasgians, a people said to be among Greece's earliest inhabitants.

The myth

According to tradition, Pelasgus was a king of Argos of the Inachid line, son of Sthenelas. Some accounts name Palaechthon, the autochthon born from the earth itself, as his father. He is also known as Gelanor.

He appears above all in Aeschylus' The Suppliants. When the Danaids arrive seeking asylum, they claim descent from Argos through Io, but the king doubts their origin, since they look more like women of Libya and Egypt. Pelasgus claims to rule the Pelasgians over a broad territory, from Thessaly to the Peloponnese, though the play does not specify his southern boundary. In a lost Aeschylean play, Danaan Women, he places the original Pelasgian homeland in the region around Mycenae.

Ancient sources: Aeschylus, in The Suppliants and in the lost play Danaan Women.

Trivia

A father born of the earth. In some accounts his father was Palaechthon, whose name means roughly "ancient earth". Pelasgus presents himself as a child of the earth that brought him forth.
Two names for one king. The king is also known as Gelanor, an alternative name found in other versions of the myth.
