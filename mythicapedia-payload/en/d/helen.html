eponymous hero of the Hellenes, ancestor of the Greek people

Hellen

Hellen is the eponymous hero of the Hellenes, the ancestor from whom the Greek people and the region of Hellas took their name. His figure lets Greek myth explain how the main ethnic branches of Greece came to be.

The myth

According to tradition, Hellen was the firstborn son of Deucalion and Pyrrha, the couple who survived the flood. Several authors, however, held that his real father was Zeus, with Deucalion only his nominal father; if so, Hellen would be the first of the kings descended from Zeus. The sources disagree on his parentage: some make him a son of Pronous, others of Prometheus, and John Malalas calls him a son of Picus.

By the nymph Orseis he had three famous sons: Aeolus, Xuthus and Dorus. From them descended the Dorians (through Dorus), the Ionians (through Ion, son of Xuthus), the Aeolians (through Aeolus) and the Achaeans (through Achaeus, another son of Xuthus). According to Thucydides, Hellen's descendants conquered Phthia and gradually extended their power over other cities; the peoples they subdued came to be called Hellenes. In the Iliad, Homer mentions the Hellenes as a small tribe of Phthia led by Achilles.

In a minority tradition preserved through Euripides, Hellen takes part in the story of Melanippe, whose twin sons, Aeolus and Boeotus, are abandoned by their father; Hellen persuades Aeolus to burn them.

Legacy

The name of Hellen gave rise to the words "Hellene" and "Hellas", from which the names for the Greek people and their land derive in many languages.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, in the Catalogue of Women; Homer, in the Iliad; Euripides, in his lost play Melanippe Wise.

Trivia

A shifting genealogy. Depending on the source, Hellen was the son of Deucalion, Zeus or Prometheus, and in some accounts he appears as Deucalion's brother.
A people named after a lineage. The Hellenes were originally a small tribe of Phthia, and the name later spread to all Greeks.
