minor Aztec god of drunkenness

Tlilhua

Tlilhua is a minor Aztec god of drunkenness and one of the Centzontotochtin, the four hundred gods of pulque. His name is usually translated as "one that has ink."

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Tlilhua was one of the four hundred children of Patecatl and Mayahuel, the divine pair linked to pulque, the fermented drink made from the agave. Together they formed the Centzontotochtin, the four hundred minor gods of drunkenness.

Each of these siblings was a lesser spirit of intoxication, and the surviving accounts do not single Tlilhua out from the rest. He appears as one piece of a large group, tied to pulque and to the excesses it could cause among those who drank it.

Worship and religion

Tlilhua was worshipped in the form of a rabbit. The sources do not describe his rituals or shrines.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. The Nahuatl name Tlilhua is usually interpreted as "one that has ink."
Gods of pulque. The Centzontotochtin were regarded as the gods of pulque, the fermented drink made from the agave.
