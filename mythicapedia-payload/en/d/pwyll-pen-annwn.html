Prince of Dyfed and lord of Annwn, hero of Welsh mythology

Pwyll Pen Annwn

Pwyll is a prominent figure in Welsh mythology, lord of Dyfed, husband of Rhiannon and father of the hero Pryderi. He is the title character of the first of the Four Branches of the Mabinogi.

The myth

According to the tale, Pwyll, prince of Dyfed, drives away a pack of hounds feeding on a stag and so offends Arawn, lord of the otherworldly realm of Annwn. To make amends, he swaps places with Arawn for a year and a day, kills Hafgan, Arawn's rival, in single combat, and when the term ends the two become lasting friends.

Later he meets Rhiannon, who asks to marry him, but Pwyll rashly promises her to Gwawl. A year on, he wins her back by cleverly trapping Gwawl in a bag. After her son is born, some ladies-in-waiting make the boy vanish and blame Rhiannon, who is punished by carrying travellers on her back. The boy, raised by Teyrnon, is eventually reunited with his parents as Pryderi. The tale ends with Pwyll's death.

Legacy

His stories shaped the literature of England, Ireland and Wales. Elements of his story appear in the Irish tale Fled Bricrend, which in turn greatly influenced the Gawain poet.

Ancient sources: He appears in Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed, the first branch of the Four Branches of the Mabinogi, and briefly in Culhwch ac Olwen, both within the Mabinogion.

Trivia

Gwri of the golden hair. Teyrnon named the boy Gwri Wallt Euryn, 'Gwri of the golden hair', because his hair was as yellow as gold.
A foal stolen by a claw. Each year Teyrnon's mare gave birth to a colt that vanished on the night of its birth. When he kept watch, a great claw came through the window and grabbed it.
