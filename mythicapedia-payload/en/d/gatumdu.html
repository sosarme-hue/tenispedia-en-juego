Sumerian mother goddess of Lagash

Gatumdug

Gatumdug was a Mesopotamian goddess of fertility, the tutelary deity of the city of Lagash and closely tied to its kings. She matters because she represents one of the oldest forms of divine motherhood in the region.

The myth

According to the tradition, she was a daughter of An (Anu), the sky god and sovereign of the Sumerian pantheon. Some of the early kings of Lagash, such as Enanatum I and Enmetena, named her their divine mother, although other rulers gave that role to the goddess Bau. In the hymn inscribed on the Gudea cylinders she appears as the city's divine founder: the king asks her about the meaning of his dreams, and she supplies the bricks needed to rebuild Ningirsu's temple. Her daughter Baba married Ningirsu, and they had seven daughters.

Worship and religion

She was already worshipped in Lagash in the Early Dynastic period, with offerings made in the city itself and in Nina. Gudea built her a temple in Girsu, and offerings continued during the Ur III period. The sources document no later cult.

Ancient sources: The Gudea cylinders, the Zame Hymn discovered at Abu Salabikh, and the Lament for Sumer and Ur.

Trivia

A very early statue. Ur-Nanshe had a statue of the goddess erected, and the inscription commemorating it is considered by scholars to be the oldest known text mentioning anthropomorphic statues of deities.
Both mother and father. In his inscriptions, Gudea addresses her as both his mother and his father.
