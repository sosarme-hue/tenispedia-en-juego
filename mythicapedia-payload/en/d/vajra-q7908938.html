king in the Mahabharata

Vajra

Vajra is a king who appears in the Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic of ancient India. His name means thunderbolt and links him to Indra's celestial weapon.

The myth

The Mahabharata manuscripts, attributed to Vyasa, preserve many kings and warriors, but the accounts of Vajra are scarce and their details vary from one version to another.

In tradition, the name Vajra refers to the thunderbolt or club of Indra, the god of the sky and storm. According to some accounts, that weapon was forged from the bones of the sage Dadhichi, so the term evokes force and celestial power. As with many figures in the epic, his exact role depends on the recension consulted, so any details about his lineage or deeds should be treated with caution.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, a Sanskrit epic attributed to Vyasa, which survives in many versions with notable differences.
