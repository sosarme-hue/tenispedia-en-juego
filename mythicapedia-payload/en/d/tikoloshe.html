Tokoloshe

The tokoloshe is a malevolent creature from the folklore of the Nguni peoples and southern Africa, especially South Africa. Described as a small, hairy and strong being linked to witchcraft and revenge, it remains a powerful presence in rural life.

The myth

According to tradition, a malevolent witch doctor creates the tokoloshe from a corpse, piercing its skull with a hot iron rod to strip it of independent thought. The client who commissions the spell is usually a jealous person seeking revenge, and must promise the soul of a loved one without being able to choose which one.

Descriptions vary by region: sometimes it is a hairy monkey-like being the size of a child, other times a teddy-bear-like creature with a bony ridge that lets it knock down an ox with a headbutt. It steals curds and fresh eggs, bites the toes of sleepers and is associated with child abduction and livestock deaths. Some versions link it to an older water spirit that only later became a small zombie.

Legacy

The tokoloshe appears in South African popular culture, such as the comic strip Madam & Eve, the John Kongos hit "Tokoloshe Man" and the video for Die Antwoord's "Evil Boy". The word has also given its name to a Manchester rock band and a song by rapper Jack Parow.

Trivia

Raised beds. In some rural areas, beds are raised about two metres on bricks to keep the tikoloshe away.
The witness's secret. According to Zulu legend, anyone who sees a tikoloshe must never reveal it, or the creature will return for revenge.
