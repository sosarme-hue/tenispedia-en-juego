asura blamed for eclipses in Vedic mythology

Svarbhānu

Svarbhānu is an asura of Vedic Hinduism traditionally held responsible for solar and lunar eclipses. His story explains why the Sun and Moon grow dark in the sky.

The myth

According to the Vedic texts, Svarbhānu strikes the Sun and covers it in darkness. The Rig Veda tells how Indra struck the asura down and how the sage Atri found the hidden Sun and returned it to the sky. The Brahmanas add that the gods freed the luminary through svara, the accents of sacred recitation.

Later traditions expand the tale. Svarbhānu is an assistant of Shukra (Venus) and a teacher of the asuras. According to the myth, he secretly drank the amrita that Mohini was distributing and gained immortality, but he was beheaded at once: his head became Rahu and his body became Ketu. The Mahabharata calls the sun god Surya his enemy, and the Hari-vaṃśa says he guided Kalanemi through the galaxy. One Purana describes him as a son of the goddess Siṃhikā, "Little Lioness".

Legacy

In Puranic mythology his name appears as an attribute of Rahu and Ketu, figures linked to solar and lunar eclipses.

Ancient sources: Rig Veda, Yajurveda, Brahmanas, Mahabharata, Hari-vaṃśa and a Purana.

Trivia

A mother named after a lioness. A Purana calls him a son of Siṃhikā, whose name means "little lioness", though the text also associates her with the cat.
Immortal twice over. After secretly drinking the amrita, his head and body survived as two separate beings, Rahu and Ketu.
