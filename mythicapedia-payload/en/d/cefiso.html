river god of Greek mythology

Cephissus

Cephissus is, in Greek mythology, the personification of the river of the same name, usually identified with the one in Boeotia. Son of Oceanus and Tethys, he is the father of Narcissus and several minor figures.

The myth

According to tradition, Cephissus was a son of Oceanus and Tethys, like the other river gods. His most famous child is Narcissus, conceived when the god raped the nymph Liriope after trapping her among his waves. Ovid recalls that Cephissus mourned the fate of a grandson turned by Apollo into a fat seal, though that grandson's identity has not survived.

Pausanias tells a different story, linked to Argolis: Inachus, a river rather than a man, was the father of Phoroneus. Cephissus served as a judge, alongside Asterion and Inachus, in the dispute between Poseidon and Hera over the land; when it was given to Hera, Poseidon dried up the waters of its rivers. Stephanus of Byzantium makes him the father of Evonymus, who gave his name to an Athenian deme.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Pausanias and Stephanus of Byzantium mention Cephissus, though they do not always specify whether the river meant is the one in Boeotia or the one in Attica.

Trivia

Rivers that run dry. Pausanias explains that the riverbeds of these rivers are dry in summer, except those of Lerna.
An Athenian deme. The deme of Evonymea, south of Alimos in Athens, takes its name from Evonymus, whom Stephanus makes a son of Cephissus.
