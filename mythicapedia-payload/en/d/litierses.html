son of King Midas and the violent reaper of Greek myth

Lityerses

Lityerses is a son of King Midas in Greek myth, linked to Phrygia and notorious for his violence at harvest time. His death at the hands of Heracles is one of the tales told about that hero.

The myth

According to the stories, he challenged strangers to reaping contests, beheaded the losers and hid their bodies among the sheaves. He was also a gargantuan glutton and a formidable swordsman.

Heracles, while enslaved to Omphale, beat him in the contest and killed him, throwing his body into the river Maeander. Some versions say he did it to free the shepherd Daphnis, who had been captured by Lityerses while searching for his beloved Pimplea.

Worship and religion

Phrygian reapers sang a harvest song in his memory that bore his name, perhaps a comic version of the lament sung for Bormus. Theocritus, in his tenth Idyll, gives a reaping song addressed to Demeter, also called the Song of the Divine Lityerses, though it makes no mention of the legend.

Legacy

Rick Riordan revives him in the Heroes of Olympus saga: resurrected in The Lost Hero and turned to gold, he later appears as an ally of Apollo in The Dark Prophecy.

Ancient sources: Theocritus's tenth Idyll preserves the reaping song named for him, and the Iliad is cited as naming him a son of Midas.

Trivia

An enormous appetite. One source describes him eating three asses' panniers of food and drinking a ten-amphora cask of wine at a time.
The Reaper of Men. He was known as the Reaper of Men, a nickname that sums up how he challenged strangers to harvest contests.
