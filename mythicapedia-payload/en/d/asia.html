Oceanid of Greek mythology

Asia

Asia is an Oceanid of Greek mythology, daughter of Oceanus and Tethys, whose name later came to designate the continent. She matters because the myths make her the mother or wife of key figures such as Prometheus and Atlas.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Asia was one of the Oceanids, the daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. Andron of Halicarnassus, who appears to be the first author to mention her, also made her a daughter of Oceanus, but gave her mother as Parthenope and a sister named Libye. In this version, the Oceanid lent her name to the part of the inhabited world the Greeks called Asia, meaning Anatolia or Asia Minor.

Apollodorus says Asia married the Titan Iapetus, with whom she had Atlas, Prometheus, Epimetheus and Menoetius. Hesiod, however, named another Oceanid, Clymene, as the wife of Iapetus and mother of these sons. Lycophron calls Asia the "unhappy mother of Prometheus" and the "mother of Epimetheus", while Herodotus describes her as Prometheus' wife rather than his mother, perhaps simply by mistake.

Herodotus also records a Lydian tradition that the continent was not named after this Oceanid but after Asies, son of Cotys and grandson of Manes, from whom the Asiad clan of Sardis also took its name. Tzetzes adds that the Lycians told a similar story, in which the name came from this figure.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, the Library attributed to Apollodorus, Herodotus, Lycophron, Aeschylus and Acusilaus all mention her in their accounts.

Trivia

An alternative origin for the name. Herodotus records a Lydian tradition that the continent was named after Asies, son of Cotys, rather than after the Oceanid.
Mother or wife. Herodotus calls Asia the wife of Prometheus rather than his mother, a detail that may simply be an error by the author.
