phantom reef in the South Pacific

Ernest Legouvé Reef

The Ernest Legouvé Reef is a supposed islet in the South Pacific, reportedly sighted by a French ship in 1902. It has never been found, so it is classed as a phantom island on the maps.

The myth

According to the captain's 1902 report, it was a reef about 100 metres long, with smaller islets nearby, lying between the Tuamotu archipelago and New Zealand. It was searched for without success in 1982 and 1983, and today it is regarded as a cartographic error or an island that never existed.

Its position matches that of Lincoln Island in Jules Verne's The Mysterious Island, a novel published before the supposed sighting. Some scholars speculate about a connection between the two, but no evidence confirms it.

Legacy

It still appears on some atlases, such as the 2015 National Geographic Atlas of the World, and is cited alongside other phantom reefs like Wachusett and Jupiter. The ship bore the name of Ernest Legouvé, a friend of Verne who had promised to help him gain a seat in the Académie française.

Trivia

Shown on atlases. The reef still appeared in the 2015 edition of the National Geographic Atlas of the World, even though its existence was never confirmed.
