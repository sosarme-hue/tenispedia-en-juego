Pleiad nymph, companion of Artemis

Taygete

Taygete is one of the Pleiades of Greek mythology, a nymph linked to Mount Taygetus in Laconia and a companion of Artemis. Her story combines Zeus's pursuit, her transformation into a doe, and the birth of Lacedaemon, the mythical founder of Sparta.

The myth

In the myth, Zeus pursued Taygete as he had pursued the other local nymphs, and she called on Artemis for protection. The goddess turned her into a doe with golden horns to keep her safe. Later, once she had returned to her own form, Taygete captured the Ceryneian Hind, which Artemis had tried to catch for her chariot, and dedicated it to the goddess.

Virgil places her rising in the sky, as a star, at the start of spring. Pausanias says that Taygete eventually yielded to Zeus on the banks of the Eurotas, and that Lacedaemon was their son. Pseudo-Plutarch gives a different version in which Taygete is the wife of Lacedaemon, and their son is Himerus. A rare variant even makes her a daughter of Agenor.

Worship and religion

Her most visible connection is with Artemis, and Mount Taygetus was considered a place sacred to the goddess. At Amyclae, the rape of Taygete was depicted on a throne. The sources record no independent cult devoted to her.

Legacy

Mount Taygetus in the Peloponnese bears her name. Her figure survives mainly through the Pleiades, the star cluster in which she is counted.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Virgil, Pausanias, the Bibliotheca (attributed to Apollodorus) and Pseudo-Plutarch.

Trivia

The golden-horned doe. Pindar recalls that the doe Taygete dedicated to Artemis was the same one Heracles later chased, though he did not kill it.
A mountain named for her. Mount Taygetus in the Peloponnese took its name from her and was linked to the cult of Artemis.
