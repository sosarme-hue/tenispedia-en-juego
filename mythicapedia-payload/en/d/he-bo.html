god of the Yellow River in Chinese mythology

He Bo

He Bo, also known as Bingyi, is the god of the Yellow River (Huang He), a water deity who embodies both the river's blessings and its destructive floods. He matters because he links Chinese mythology with the history of river sacrifices, ancient poetry and imperial worship. In the tradition, He Bo is a figure of human face and fish body who rides the sky in a chariot drawn by two dragons. Some accounts show him as generous at times and destructive at others. According to the Shizi, he gave Yu the Great the River Diagram, which helped him bring the Great Flood under control. In the poem Heavenly Questions from the Chu Ci, the archer Houyi shoots He Bo and takes his wife Luoshen. According to the Huainanzi, He Bo had drowned people, which is why he was punished.

The myth

The myth of He Bo centres on an unpredictable river. Ancient sources describe him as benevolent yet also greedy and dangerous, travelling across the sky in a chariot pulled by dragons.

In the tradition, the archer Houyi, sent by the Emperor to reform the people of Xia, killed He Bo and claimed his wife Luoshen. The story appears in the Chu Ci's Heavenly Questions, which poses it as a question rather than giving a settled answer.

Another tale in the Zhuangzi has He Bo journeying to the northern sea, where Ruo, the god of the sea, lives, and where he recognises his own smallness before the ocean.

Worship and religion

The Yellow River was believed to rise at the mythical Mount Kunlun, and from the ancient states of northwestern and central China the cult of He Bo spread southward. He received human, animal and object sacrifices. Oracle bone inscriptions from the Shang period document his worship, and in the Warring States period young virgin brides were set afloat on the river on bridal rafts. Ximen Bao ended this practice at Ye, though other bridal sacrifices continued until the time of Shi Huangdi. Han-era records mention offerings of jade and live horses. His sacrifices gradually lost importance over time.

Legacy

He Bo is one of the gods in the Jiu Ge, the Nine Songs of the Chu Ci, and is the most widely known figure from that group in later culture. He is a selectable god in the video game Age of Mythology, in its Tale of the Dragon expansion, and a playable mage in the MOBA SMITE.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Chu Ci (Heavenly Questions and Nine Songs), the Shizi, the Huainanzi and the Zhuangzi.

Trivia

Chariot of dragons. According to early commentary, the first illustrations showed He Bo on a chariot pulled by two dragons, riding through the clouds in every direction.
Brides to the river. During the Warring States period, virgin brides were offered to He Bo and set adrift on the river on bridal rafts.
