rainbow serpent loa of Haitian Vodou

Ayida-Wedo

Ayida-Wedo is a loa of Haitian Vodou, rooted in the Fon and Ewe traditions of Benin and Togo, and depicted as a rainbow serpent. She is the wife of Damballa and is associated with water, fertility and wisdom.

The myth

According to Fon tradition, Ayida-Wedo existed before the Earth and served the goddess Mawu-Lisa. As the goddess shaped the world, the serpent carried her in its mouth, and its body left behind canyons, rivers, valleys and mountains.

Another version says the serpent coiled itself around the earth to hold it together and give humans a place to live. It is said that its coils number 7000, supporting both earth and sky.

Worship and religion

Her offerings are white foods such as milk and eggs, traditionally presented on Thursdays, and her festival falls on 8 December. She remains alive in Haitian Vodou: during ceremonies, the medium dresses in white and slithers across the ground, imitating the serpent.

Trivia

Serpent dung. According to tradition, where the serpent spent the night a mountain formed from its excrement. For this reason, the term 'serpent dung' is used in the Fon language for minerals and stones mined from underground.
Syncretism with Saint Patrick. In Haitian Vodou, Damballa has sometimes been conflated with Saint Patrick, the Irish saint famed for driving snakes out of Ireland.
