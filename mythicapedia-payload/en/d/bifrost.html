rainbow bridge of Norse mythology

Bifröst

Bifröst is a burning, three-coloured bridge that links Midgard, the world of humans, with Asgard, the realm of the gods. It matters in Norse tradition as the route between the gods and the earth, and as the crossing that is destined to break during Ragnarök.

The myth

According to medieval Icelandic texts, the gods built the bridge with unmatched skill. It goes by several names: Bilröst in the Poetic Edda, Bifröst in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and Ásbrú, "the bridge of the Æsir", in both works.

In Grímnismál, Odin, disguised as Grímnir, tells the young Agnarr that Bilröst is the greatest of bridges and that Ásbrú burns with flames. Every day Thor wades through the rivers Körmt and Örmt to reach the well of Urd, while the other gods ride across. In the Prose Edda the bridge ends in the heavens, beside Himinbjörg, where the god Heimdall keeps watch over the giants.

Its fate is foretold. At Ragnarök the sons of Muspell ride out of the split sky and shatter the bridge as they cross, forcing the gods to swim across great rivers. In the Prose Edda, Hár excuses the gods: nothing in this world can be made secure against the sons of Muspell.

Legacy

Richard Wagner recreates it in the closing scene of Das Rheingold (1869), where the god Froh summons a rainbow bridge for the gods to enter Valhalla. J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium includes a similar bridge. In Marvel comics starring Thor, Bifröst serves as a means of transport, and in that film universe it is described as an Einstein-Rosen bridge. Popular culture also uses the Rainbow Bridge to refer to the death of a pet.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Grímnismál and Fáfnismál) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), along with skaldic poetry.

Trivia

Three colours. In the Prose Edda, Hár explains that the bridge has three colours and that its red is burning fire, which keeps the giants from climbing up to heaven.
Milky Way or aurora. Some scholars identify Bifröst with the Milky Way and others with the northern lights, in addition to the rainbow.
