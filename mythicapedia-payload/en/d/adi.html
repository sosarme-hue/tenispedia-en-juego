asura, son of the demon Andhaka

Āḍi

Āḍi is an asura in Hindu mythology, son of the demon Andhaka, whom Shiva killed. His story, told in the Matsya Purāṇa, shows a powerful being trying to avenge a god with a trick that ends up backfiring on him.

The myth

In the best-known account, Āḍi performed austerities to please Brahmā, who appeared and asked what he wanted. Āḍi asked for immortality, but Brahmā reminded him that all living beings must die. Āḍi then specified that he would die only when he changed form, and Brahmā, taking advantage of the ambiguity, granted him the power of transformation.

When Āḍi learned that Pārvatī was performing tapasya in the forest, he remembered his father's death at Shiva's hands and resolved to take revenge, though he knew he was no match for the god. He slipped into Shiva's private quarters disguised as a snake, evading Vīrabhadra, and then approached as Pārvatī. Shiva greeted him affectionately, but soon grew suspicious and, through yogic insight, saw his true form. Shiva struck him on the genitals with the vajra, and he died instantly.

Another version says Brahmā granted him the chance to learn warfare under Shiva. After some years of training, Āḍi began troubling the residents of Mount Kailasa; disguised again as Pārvatī, he asked Shiva for one more chance, but Shiva recognised him and killed him with his triśūla. Āḍi begged forgiveness, and Shiva granted him moksha. This version is not found in the texts, while the first is the well-known one.

Ancient sources: The Matsya Purāṇa, a Hindu Purāṇic text, is the source that mentions him.
