Japanese oil-licking yōkai

Aburakago

The aburakago, or «oil child», is a Japanese yōkai that takes the form of an infant and approaches oil lamps to drink their oil. It belongs to the Japanese tradition of spirits and ghosts of the Edo period.

The myth

According to the tale, the aburakago appears alongside one or more balls of fire, perhaps hitodama, and circles the lamps in search of oil, which it sucks up to move faster. A popular explanation links it to oil thieves, spirits who repeat in death the daily habits of their lives. Other accounts present it in a very different way.

Legacy

Its image comes from Toriyama Sekien's Konjaku Gazu Zoku Hyakki, and modern yōkai literature portrays it as a ball of flame that turns into a baby and licks andon lanterns. Some scholars believe its origin lies in cats licking oil lamps. The novelist Yamada Norio recorded a similar kaidan, and it is thought that everything stems from Sekien's invention.

Ancient sources: Toriyama Sekien's Konjaku Gazu Zoku Hyakki and the Shokoku Rijin Dan, both from the Edo period.

Trivia

A creation of Sekien. Scholars suspect that the aburakago Toriyama Sekien drew was inspired by a fire of stolen oil, and that Sekien himself turned it into a child.
The value of oil. According to one interpretation, these yōkai arose as a warning against wasting oil, a very valuable resource in Japan.
