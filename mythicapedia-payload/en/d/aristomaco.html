Heraclid of Greek mythology

Aristomachus

Aristomachus was one of the Heraclids, the descendants of Heracles who claimed the Peloponnese in Greek mythology. His defeat at Mycenae and his death left his sons the task of carrying out the invasion.

The myth

Aristomachus was the son of Cleodaeus, grandson of Hyllus and great-grandson of Heracles, so he belonged to the line of the Heraclids. Tradition presents these descendants of the hero as the rightful heirs to the lands of the Peloponnese, from which they considered themselves dispossessed. During the reign of Tisamenus in Mycenae, Aristomachus led the fourth attack against that city. The story says he misread the recommendations of the oracle, which led to his defeat and his death in battle.

Despite this failure, his line did not end. Aristomachus was the father of Temenus, Cresphontes and Aristodemus, who did manage to invade the Peloponnese. His story works as a link between the figure of Heracles and the conquest that, according to tradition, gave rise to new dynasties in the region. A misreading of the oracle by a secondary figure thus ends up connecting the hero's deeds with the fate of his descendants.
