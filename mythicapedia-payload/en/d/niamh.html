Land of Youth queen, wife of Oisín

Niamh

Niamh, "the golden-haired", is a figure of Irish mythology, a ruler of Tír na nÓg, the Land of Youth, and the lover and wife of Oisín, son of the hero Fionn mac Cumhaill. Her story is one of the best-known tales of the Fianna cycle.

The myth

In the best-known version, Niamh arrived from across the sea on the white horse Enbarr and asked Oisín to come with her to Tír na nÓg. Some traditions make her a daughter of Manannán mac Lir, the sea god, while others make her a daughter of Fand, a ruler of a fairy realm.

On the way, Oisín rescued a maiden from a giant who was pursuing her. The couple lived in Tír na nÓg and had children, including Oscar and Plor na mBan, meaning "Flower of Women". After three years, or three hundred according to other tellings, Oisín grew homesick and wished to see his father and the Fianna.

Niamh reluctantly agreed, lending him Enbarr on the condition that he never set foot on Irish soil. In Ireland he found that the Fianna had vanished centuries earlier. While helping men lift a stone, he leaned down, fell from the horse and, as he touched the ground, aged at once. According to the tradition, Saint Patrick cared for him until his death. Niamh went back to Ireland searching for him and learned he had died.

A medieval version, preserved in the Acallam na Senórach, makes her a mortal princess of Munster who eloped with Oisín to Ulster and, when her father's army arrived, took her own life alongside thirty women.

Ancient sources: The medieval version appears in the Acallam na Senórach; the best-known story comes from a poem of about 1750 attributed to Mícheál Coimín.
