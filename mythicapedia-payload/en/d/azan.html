Arcadian hero of Greek mythology

Azan

Azan is a hero of Greek mythology, king of Arcadia and eponym of the region of Azania and of the Azanes tribe. His name is tied to the original division of Arcadian lands among the sons of Arcas.

The myth

According to the tradition, Azan was the son of King Arcas, the eponymous ancestor of the Arcadians, and of the dryad Erato, although some accounts name Leanira as his mother. His brothers were Elatus, Apheidas and Autolaus. When they grew up, Arcas split the land into three parts: Azan received the district that would bear his name, Apheidas got Tegea, and Elatus was given Mount Cyllene, which had no name until then.

Azan founded the city of Lycosura, which became his capital, and he ruled after his father's death. His only son, Cleitor, was meant to succeed him, but since Cleitor had no children the throne passed to Aepytus, son of Elatus. Some sources also mention a daughter, Coronis, though it is unclear whether she was Azan's child or whether the term simply refers to a young woman of his lineage.

After his death, the first funeral games in recorded history were held in his honour. During those games Aetolus accidentally killed Apis. Another figure with the same name appears in a different account as the husband of Hippolyte, daughter of Dexamenus, whom the centaur Eurytion threatened with violence.

Trivia

The first funeral games. According to the account, the games held after Azan's death were the first in history, and during them Aetolus accidentally killed Apis.
A namesake. Another character named Azan appears as the husband of Hippolyte, daughter of Dexamenus, in an account separate from the Arcadian one.
