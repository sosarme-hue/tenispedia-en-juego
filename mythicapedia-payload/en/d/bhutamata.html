Hindu goddess, mother of the bhutas

Bhutamata

Bhutamata is a fearsome form of the Hindu goddess Parvati, sometimes also regarded as the shakti (divine feminine energy) of the god Kartikeya. She is the mother of the bhutas, ghostly beings that Hindu tradition associates with badly performed offerings.

The myth

According to the Skanda Purana, the god Kartikeya allowed the bhutas (ghosts), pishachas and vetalas who lived along the banks of the river Sarasvati to consume Vedic ritual offerings that were performed incorrectly. Over time, these beings began to devour every offering made to the devas, who complained to Kartikeya.

In his fury, the god brought forth from between his eyebrows a twelve-eyed goddess, who offered to serve him. Kartikeya ordered her to subdue the disobedient creatures and bring them back to proper conduct. Accompanied by a host of goddesses, she attacked them, and they quickly submitted and appeased her. Pleased, she granted them a boon: they asked that she be called Bhutamata, the mother of the bhutas, and promised never again to break the laws of Kartikeya.

Ancient sources: The Skanda Purana, which narrates her origin.

Trivia

Twelve eyes. The goddess is born with twelve eyes, a feature that sets her apart from other deities in the story.
Born of anger. She emerges from Kartikeya's eyebrows in a moment of fury, and her origin is tied to punishing disobedient beings.
