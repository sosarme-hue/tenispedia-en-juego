healing white bird of Roman mythology

Caladrius

The caladrius (caladrius) is a snow-white bird that, according to tradition, lives in the houses of kings and has the power to heal. Although it is linked to Rome, its Greek name was dhalion, and its fame grew through medieval bestiaries.

The myth

According to tradition, the caladrius refuses to look at patients who will not recover: if it fixes its gaze on a sick person, that person will get well; if it looks away, the person will die. The bird is said to absorb the illness and then fly toward the sun, where the disease burns up and is destroyed, healing itself as well.

In medieval bestiaries it became a symbol of Christ, pure and free from any trace of sin, who turns away from unrepentant sinners and restores those he turns his face toward. Some texts used this moral against the Jews.

Legacy

It appears in heraldry, such as the crest of Keith William James. In popular culture it has featured in a Saturday Night Live sketch, in the video game Age of Mythology: The Titans, as a demon in the Megami Tensei series, and in novels such as Hollow Earth and the InCryptid series.

Ancient sources: The caladrius appears in the Greek dhalion and in the Physiologus, an ancient-origin bestiary that shaped its Christian reading.

Trivia

A television sketch. It appeared in a Saturday Night Live sketch titled "Theodoric of York, Medieval Barber", where a medieval barber used it to diagnose a patient.
A possible real model. The art historian Louis Réau believed that, if a real bird lay behind the myth, it was most likely a white plover.
