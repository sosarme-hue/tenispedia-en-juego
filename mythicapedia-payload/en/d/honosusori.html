Honosusori

Honosusori is a kami of Japanese mythology who appears in the Kojiki as a son of Ninigi-no-Mikoto and Konohanasakuya-hime. His name is interpreted as "rice ear advancing."

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Honosusori is the second child of Ninigi-no-Mikoto and Konohanasakuya-hime, and one of the grand-uncles of Emperor Jimmu. After a brief encounter with Ninigi, Konohanasakuya-hime became pregnant within a single night. Ninigi doubted she could conceive so quickly, and in frustration she built a long birthing hut and set it on fire. Amid the blaze she gave birth to three sons: Hoderi, Honosusori and Hoori, in that order. After this, the story barely mentions him again.

The Nihon Shoki retells the birth with variations, giving different names and between two and four children, though usually three. His name does not appear in that version.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, which names him as the second son of Ninigi; the Nihon Shoki only preserves the birth episode in variant form.

Trivia

A name with rice. His name is interpreted as "rice ear advancing." Its first character now means "flame" in modern Japanese.
Three sons in the fire. According to the Kojiki, he was born amid the flames of the birthing hut alongside his brothers Hoderi and Hoori.
