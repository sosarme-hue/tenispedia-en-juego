Greek healing hero, son of Asclepius

Machaon

Machaon is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Asclepius and older brother of Podalirius, both renowned physicians. He matters chiefly for his role as a healer in the Trojan War told in the Iliad.

The myth

According to tradition, Machaon and Podalirius were sons of Asclepius, and they were said to come from Thessaly, although some placed them in Messenia. Both were suitors of Helen and therefore joined the expedition against Troy, leading thirty ships from Thessalian cities.

During the siege, Machaon was wounded in the back by an arrow from Paris and rescued by Nestor, who tended him with care, as the Iliad recounts. He was credited with healing Menelaus, hit by an arrow from Pandarus, and Philoctetes, whose ulcerated wound came from an arrow of Heracles. According to Quintus of Smyrna, he was killed by Eurypylus, son of Telephus, in a battle before the sack of the city, while Virgil's Aeneid places him inside the Trojan Horse, a contradiction between versions.

His genealogy also varies. Hesiod gives his mother as Jante, while a scholium names Epione. Dares the Phrygian describes him as strong, prudent and merciful. Machaon had several sons, including Nicomachus, Gorgasus, Alexanor, Sphyrus and Polemocrates, and according to Diogenes Laertius, Nicomachus was the father of Aristotle.

Worship and religion

Machaon was worshipped at Gerenia in Messenia, where tradition says Nestor brought his ashes to a shrine that the sick visited seeking cure. His descendants, the Asclepiads, guarded his tomb and the sanctuary of his sons at Pherae, where they received sacrifices and offerings in return for their healing power.

Legacy

Machaon's name survives in the Old World swallowtail butterfly, Papilio machaon, in the plant genus Machaonia of the family Rubiaceae, and in the Jupiter trojan asteroid 3063 Makhaon.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Iliad, and his later story is told in Quintus of Smyrna's Posthomerica and Virgil's Aeneid.

Trivia

Butterfly named after him. The Old World swallowtail butterfly, Papilio machaon, carries his name in its scientific name.
Ancestor of Aristotle. According to Diogenes Laertius, Machaon was the grandfather of Nicomachus, father of the philosopher Aristotle.
