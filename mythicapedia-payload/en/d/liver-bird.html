mythical bird, symbol of Liverpool

Liver bird

The liver bird is a mythical creature that serves as the symbol of the English city of Liverpool. It is usually shown as a cormorant holding a sprig of laver seaweed in its beak, a pun on the city's name.

The myth

According to local tradition, the liver bird was a creature that once haunted the shoreline of Liverpool. Today the story goes that a pair of birds on the Royal Liver Building look out over the city: the female watches the sea for seamen returning home, and the male watches over the seamen's families.

Another local legend says the birds face away from each other, since if they mated and flew off the city would vanish. Others say they are chained down, because if they escaped the River Mersey would burst its banks and flood Liverpool.

Legacy

The bird appears on the city's arms and on heritage lamp standards across the centre. Liverpool F.C. adopted it for its crest and shirts, and in 2012 introduced a mascot based on it, called Mighty Red. A 1960s all-female rock group from the city took the name The Liverbirds, and a British 1970s sitcom, The Liver Birds, shares the title.

Trivia

A bird of confused origin. The bird on the old seal was probably meant to be an eagle, the symbol of John the Evangelist, but over time it came to be read as a cormorant.
A mistake in the arms. In 1611 the municipal records mention a cormorant, while in 1688 Randle Holme wrote of a 'lever', a word he linked to the spoonbill.
