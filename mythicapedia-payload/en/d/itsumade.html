monstrous bird from Japanese folklore (yōkai)

Itsumade

Itsumade is a monstrous bird from Japanese folklore, a yōkai recorded in the medieval chronicle Taiheiki and brought to wider attention by the artist Toriyama Sekien in the 18th century.

The myth

According to the Taiheiki, around 1334, during an epidemic, a strange bird perched each night on the Shishinden of the imperial palace, crying 'itsumade, itsumade' ('until when?'), and spreading fear.

The court recalled that Minamoto no Yorimasa had slain the nue, and entrusted the task to Oki Jirōzaemon Hiroari, who brought the bird down with a kaburaya arrow. It had a human face, a curved beak, saw-like teeth, a serpent's body and sword-like talons. The text gives it no name; the name 'Itsumade' was coined by Sekien from its cry.

Legacy

The bird appears in Toriyama Sekien's yōkai collection (1779) and in popular culture, in works such as GeGeGe no Kitarō, Nioh, Touhou Project, Fate/Grand Order and Onmyōji. In One Piece, the character St. Marcus Mars can take its form.

Ancient sources: The Taiheiki, a Japanese chronicle of the 14th century (volume 12), where the bird appears without a proper name.

Trivia

An invented name. The Taiheiki does not name the bird. The name 'Itsumade' was taken by Sekien from the cry the bird repeats in the story.
A vengeful spirit. Shōwa-era texts sometimes present it as the onryō of those who died in battle or of hunger, transformed into a bird.
