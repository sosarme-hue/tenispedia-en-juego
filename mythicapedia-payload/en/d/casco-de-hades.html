Cap of Hades

The Cap of Hades was, in Greek mythology, a helmet or cap that made its wearer invisible. Its Greek name, Aïdos kynéē, means literally "dog-skin of Hades", and the object appears in several stories about gods and heroes.

The myth

According to tradition, the cap belonged to Hades, god of the underworld, who received it during the Titanomachy. The Cyclopes, freed by Zeus from Tartarus and strengthened with divine food, forged three weapons: the lightning bolt for Zeus, the trident for Poseidon and the helmet for Hades. With these arms the gods defeated the Titans.

In the story of Perseus, the cap came to him through nymphs who guarded other divine objects, such as the winged sandals. Its power let the hero approach the Gorgons and escape from them. Other accounts show it in the hands of Hermes, who wore it in his fight against the giant Hippolytus, and of Athena, who covered herself with it so that Ares would not recognize her during the Trojan War.

Legacy

The cap inspired proverbial expressions in European literature, such as the saying that links it to the crafty politician who works in secret. In modern literature it appears in Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson series and in Dan Simmons's novel Ilium.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Bibliotheca, attributed to Apollodorus, and in Homer's Iliad.

Trivia

A proverbial helmet. Rabelais called it the Helmet of Pluto and Erasmus the Helmet of Orcus. Over time it became a saying about the politician who acts unseen.
A modern baseball cap. In the Percy Jackson series, Annabeth Chase receives from her mother, Athena, a Yankees cap that works as an invisibility cap.
