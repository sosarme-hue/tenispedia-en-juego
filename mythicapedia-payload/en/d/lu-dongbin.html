Chinese Taoist immortal, one of the Eight Immortals

Lü Dongbin

Lü Dongbin is the best known of the Eight Immortals of Chinese Taoism, a group of figures revered as scholars and poets. He is also considered one of the earliest masters of neidan, or internal alchemy. He is shown dressed as a scholar, carrying a sword to ward off evil spirits.

The myth

Legend says that a fragrance filled the room when Lü Dongbin was born. After failing the imperial examination twice, he dozed off one night while cooking yellow millet and dreamed an entire life: he passed the exam with distinction, rose to prime minister, married and had children. Then envy ruined him, his wife betrayed him, bandits killed his children, and he lay dying in the street. When he woke, he found that all of this had passed while the millet cooked. This is the famous 'Yellow Millet Dream', recorded in a compilation by Ma Zhiyuan of the Yuan dynasty.

Zhongli Quan had played the characters of the dream to show him how empty worldly glory was, and Lü set off with him in search of the Tao. After passing ten trials, he received a sword with supernatural powers, with which he drove dragons and monsters out of China. An earlier version, in volume 82 of Li Fang's Extensive Records of the Taiping Era, replaces Lü with a student named Lu and Zhongli Quan with an Elder Lü.

Worship and religion

Taoists venerate him as an immortal and call him Lu zu, 'the Original Lü'. He is invoked by the sick and honored as a scholar. The sources note that the Quanzhen School holds him in particular devotion.

Legacy

His name gave rise to two proverbs: 'the Yellow Millet Dream', for someone who craves important posts, and 'the dog bites Lü Dongbin', for someone who is ungrateful. His poems were collected in the Complete Tang Poetry, the great anthology of Tang verse.

Ancient sources: He appears in Ma Zhiyuan's Yuan-dynasty compilation and in Li Fang's Extensive Records of the Taiping Era; he is also mentioned in the History of Song.

Trivia

A hot temper as a young immortal. One story says that soon after becoming immortal, in a fit of anger he deformed the bank of a river.
An unlikely patron of love. He has a reputation as a ladies' man, even after becoming immortal, so people with romantic troubles generally do not invoke him.
