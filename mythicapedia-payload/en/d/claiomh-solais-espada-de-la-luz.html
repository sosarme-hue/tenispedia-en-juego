sword in Irish and Scottish Gaelic folklore

Claíomh Solais (Sword of Light)

The Sword of Light (in Irish, Claíomh Solais) is an object that recurs in Irish and Scottish Gaelic folktales. It is often the goal of a quest that, in many versions, is tied to the story of the unfaithful wife who turns her husband into a wolf.

The myth

In the typical tale, a hero wins a beautiful bride and riches by wager against a gruagach, a wizard champion, but loses and is bound by a geis, a magical prohibition, that keeps him from returning home until the quest is complete. The sword's keeper is usually a giant, a cailleach (hag) or the wizard's sibling, and can be killed only by secret means, often an external soul hidden in animals.

The quest is intertwined with the search for the "One Story" about women: the tale of the wolf, whose husband was transformed by an unfaithful wife. Helpers may be grateful animals or the bride herself.

Legacy

According to T. F. O'Rahilly, it relates to the divine weapons of Irish mythology. Motif H1337 appears in Stith Thompson's Motif-Index.

Trivia

Several spellings. In Irish it is written in several forms, such as Claidheamh Soluis or cloidheamh solais, and in Scottish Gaelic it is rendered as "glaive of light".
A werewolf tale. In 1903, George Lyman Kittredge studied how the sword quest frames the Irish version of the medieval tale of a man turned into a wolf.
