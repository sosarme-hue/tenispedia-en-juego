Greek hero, eponym of Cape Taenarum

Taenarus

Taenarus is the eponymous hero of Cape Taenarum, the mountain and the city of the same name at the southern tip of the Peloponnese. His genealogy varies depending on which ancient author tells the story.

The myth

In one version, he was a son of Zeus and brother of Calabrus and Geraestus. The three brothers were said to have sailed to the Peloponnese and seized a stretch of land, where Taenarus founded a sanctuary of Poseidon known as Taenarum.

Other accounts make him a son of Poseidon, or a son of Elatus, himself a son of Icarius, and credit him with giving his name to the city, the mountain and the harbor.

Stephanus of Byzantium treats him as a figure distinct from the son of Zeus.

Worship and religion

According to one version, he founded a sanctuary of Poseidon on the site, known as Taenarum, around which local worship was organized.

Ancient sources: Stephanus of Byzantium mentions him as a figure distinct from the son of Zeus.

Trivia

Gateway to the underworld. Cape Taenarum was regarded as a conventional entrance to the underworld, so the place became associated with the dead.
