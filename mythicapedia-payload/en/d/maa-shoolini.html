Hindu goddess of victory, form of Durga

Maa Shoolini

Shoolini is a form of the goddess Durga or Parvati, revered in Hinduism as Shakti, the power of Shiva. She is the patron goddess of the city of Solan and the kula devi, or family deity, of its people.

The myth

According to the tradition, Vishnu took the form of Narasimha, half man and half lion, to save his devotee Prahlada, and killed the demon Hiranyakashipu. Afterwards, Narasimha's rage could not be calmed, and he threatened the entire universe.

To subdue him, Shiva assumed the form of Sharabha, half bird and half lion. From its right wing emerged Parvati as Shoolini, dark in colour (hence the name Saloni), who carries a shool, a spear.

Worship and religion

Her main shrine is the Shoolini Devi temple in Solan. Every last week of June the Shoolini Mela is held, commemorating the goddess's three-day visit to her elder sister Durga. The image is carried in an ornate palanquin in procession to the Ganj Bazar temple and returns at the festival's end. The worship remains alive today.

Trivia

Eight legs. Sharabha, the form Shiva took, had eight legs and was mightier than a lion or an elephant.
The mushroom city. Solan, known as India's mushroom city, takes its name from this sacred shrine.
