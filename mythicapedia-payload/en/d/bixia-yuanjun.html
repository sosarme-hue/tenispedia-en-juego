Taoist goddess of Mount Tai, protector of childbirth and destiny

Bixia Yuanjun

Bixia Yuanjun, also known as Taishan Niangniang ('The Lady of Mount Tai'), is the Taoist goddess of Mount Tai, patron of childbirth, the dawn and destiny. She became one of the most popular deities in northern China during the Ming and Qing dynasties.

The myth

Accounts present her as a daughter of Dongyue Dadi, the Grand Emperor of Mount Tai, or of the judge Yanluo, who in the popular scheme serves under him. Other versions describe her as the daughter of a commoner, and she is regarded as a composite figure drawn from several historical personalities.

She is said to govern the length of human life, judge the dead and answer those who pray for children, especially sons. According to a story promoted by a 1635 guidebook, her derelict statue was found on the mountain in 1008 by Emperor Zhenzong of the Song dynasty.

Worship and religion

Her main shrine, the Palace of the Clouds of Dawn, was built at the summit of Mount Tai in the Ming period and remains a pilgrimage site. Women who wish to conceive come there to ask for her help. Today temples dedicated to her can be found in many Chinese cities.

Legacy

In northern China she took a role equivalent to that of the bodhisattva Guanyin, whose cult was stronger in central and southern China, and she merged with other matron goddesses.

Ancient sources: The History of Mount Tai, compiled by Zha Zhilong (1554–1586) and included in the 1607 supplement to the Taoist Canon, contains her official hagiography.

Trivia

Phoenix headdress. She is usually depicted wearing a headdress of three phoenixes, or more.
Nine attendant goddesses. In Taoist painting and sculpture she is often accompanied by nine other goddesses, including Zhusheng Niangniang, goddess of fertility, and Yanguang Niangniang, goddess of eyesight.
