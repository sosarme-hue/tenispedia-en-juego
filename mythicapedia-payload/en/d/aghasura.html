demon of Hindu mythology, general of King Kamsa

Aghasura

Aghasura is an asura, or demon, in Hindu mythology, one of King Kamsa's generals and the elder brother of the demoness Putana and the crane Bakasura. His story, told in the Bhagavata Purana, casts him as a defeated enemy of the god Krishna.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, Kamsa, warned that the child Krishna would kill him, sent Aghasura after his own failed attempts. The demon took the form of a giant serpent and hid its open jaws against a mountain. Cowherd boys, Krishna's companions, mistook the mouth for a cave and walked in, and were swallowed. Krishna followed them inside and enlarged his body. The serpent tried to grow too, but it suffocated. Its life force burst out through a hole in its head, and Krishna revived the boys and the calves.

Legacy

In the nineteenth century, the Bengali writer Bhaktivinoda read Aghasura as the personification of cruelty and violence.

Ancient sources: He first appears in the Vishnu Purana (around the 4th century CE), and his story is expanded in the Bhagavata Purana (11th century CE).

Trivia

Disputed measurements. The Bhagavata Purana gives the serpent a length of one yojana, about 13 kilometres, while the English version of the same story puts it at about 8 miles.
A legend later than the Vedas. The story does not appear in the Rig Veda, India's oldest text. Its first mention is in the Vishnu Purana, written much later.
