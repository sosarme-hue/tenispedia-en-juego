Mesopotamian goddess of the sea, divination and social justice

Nanshe

Nanshe was a Mesopotamian goddess linked to the sea, the marshlands, and the birds and fish that inhabit them, as well as to divination, dream interpretation and the protection of the vulnerable. Her main cult center was Nina, today Tell Zurghul, and she matters because she combines several very different roles within one local pantheon.

The myth

According to tradition, Nanshe was a daughter of Enki and a sister of Ningirsu, although the texts do not fully agree on her family, and her husband is named as Nindara, a little-known figure. Her bond with water is the most constant trait: she was imagined in the open sea, playing with the waves and foam, and the poems present her as a lover of fish and birds. Her symbolic bird, the u5, is still debated, with identifications ranging from goose and swan to cormorant, gull and pelican.

As a dream interpreter and prophetess, she was believed to know what lay ahead. Gudea called her the «dream interpreter of the gods», though Niek Veldhuis argues that this role may apply only to Nanshe as the divine mother of the kings of Lagash. In the myth of Enki and Ninhursag she appears among the deities created to heal Enki. She was also linked to weighing, measuring and marking boundaries, and one hymn calls her «lady of the boundary stone». As protector of orphans, widows and indebted households, she is presented as a deity of justice and social welfare.

Worship and religion

Her oldest worship is attested in the Uruk period. Her cult center was Nina, with a sacred district called Sirara, and she had sanctuaries in Lagash, Girsu and Gu'abba. Her cult declined after the Ur III period, but later the kings of the Sealand adopted her as a dynastic tutelary deity, and she was worshiped in the Esagil temple complex in Babylon. She was still venerated in the sixth century BCE. No living cult of hers survives today.

Ancient sources: The literary composition Nanshe and the Birds, the myth Enki and Ninhursag, the Gudea cylinders, and several Mesopotamian god lists, such as the one from Nippur.

Trivia

One bird, many guesses. The species of the u5 bird linked to Nanshe remains unresolved, with proposals ranging from swan to pelican.
A name with fish in it. Her name was written with signs combining a shrine and a fish, although its meaning and origin remain unclear.
Fish with names. Two hymns mention fish such as the «scepter fish», the «sandal fish» and the «fire fish», though scholars disagree on whether they were real animals.
