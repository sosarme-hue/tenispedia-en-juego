nymph of Greek Arcadia

Nomia

Nomia is a nymph of Greek Arcadia, where locals believed the Nomian Mountains took their name from her. She is best known as a companion of Callisto, daughter of Lycaon.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the sources, Nomia was a companion of Callisto, daughter of Lycaon. Pausanias describes a painting showing the two together: Callisto sits on a bearskin with her feet resting on Nomia's knees, and Pero is also portrayed beside them.

Some believe Nomia began as nothing more than a nymph, until the day Callisto chose to trade her love for the status of a goddess, and Nomia became a water goddess. A less certain tradition identifies her with a Sicilian nymph who loved Daphnis; abandoned by him, she blinded the young man in revenge and turned him into a rock.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions her in his description of a painting of Callisto and Nomia in his Description of Greece.

Trivia

Mountains named after her. The people of Arcadia believed the Nomian Mountains had been named after the nymph Nomia.
An ancient painting. Pausanias describes a painting in which Callisto sits on a bearskin, her feet resting on Nomia's knees.
