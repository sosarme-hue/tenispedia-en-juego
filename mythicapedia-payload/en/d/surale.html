forest spirit of Tatar and Bashkir folklore

Şüräle

Şüräle, also known as Shurale, is a forest spirit from Turkic mythology, especially among the Tatars and Bashkirs. He lures his victims into the thickets and can tickle them to death.

The myth

According to legend, Şüräle lives in the forests. He has long fingers, a horn on his forehead and a woolly body, and he lures travellers into the undergrowth to tickle them to death.

He can take many shapes. As a man he looks like a peasant with glowing eyes and his shoes on backwards. His tricks include leading peasants astray, making them ill, hiding woodcutters' axes and leaving people lost in the woods. To find the way out, one must turn one's clothes inside out and put the shoes on the opposite feet. Whoever befriends Şüräle may learn the secrets of magic.

Legacy

The Tatar poet Ghabdulla Tuqay drew on this figure for a poem, which became the basis of the first Tatar ballet, by Farit Yarullin. Soyuzmultfilm adapted the theme into a 1987 animated film about a superstitious lad who only imagines the creature, and Tatarmultfilm (2014) and ShayanTV (2020) made animated films in Tatar. An amusement park was originally named "Shurale".

Trivia

A Tatar ballet. The first Tatar ballet was based on a poem by Ghabdulla Tuqay inspired by this folklore figure.
Relatives among other peoples. Similar figures appear in the folklore of the Chuvash, the Siberian Tatars and the Ural Tatars.
