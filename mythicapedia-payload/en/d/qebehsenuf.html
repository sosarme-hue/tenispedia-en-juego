Egyptian god, son of Horus and guardian of the intestines

Qebehsenuef

Qebehsenuef is one of the four sons of Horus, Egyptian deities who protected the dead in the afterlife. He was linked to the west and to the intestines, one of the organs removed during mummification, and was depicted with a falcon's head.

The myth

According to the funerary texts, Qebehsenuef was one of four brothers alongside Imsety, Hapy and Duamutef. The Pyramid Texts present them as children of Horus, though some passages call them children of Atum, Geb or Nut, and the Coffin Texts make them offspring of Isis and an older form of Horus. His name is usually rendered as "he who purifies his brother by means of libation".

In the Pyramid Texts they help the dead king climb into the sky and shield him from hunger and thirst. Over time they became chiefly guardians of Osiris. In the Book of the Dead, Qebehsenuef speaks to the deceased as though he has gathered his bones, reassembled his limbs and returned his heart to his body. A Middle Kingdom ritual credits the sons of Horus with fighting Set's followers and restoring the Eye of Horus to their father.

Worship and religion

His worship was almost entirely confined to funerary practice. He took part in protecting the body of Osiris alongside his brothers and other deities known as "the seven shining ones". He was under the protection of the goddess Serket, although in the Middle Kingdom that role could fall to Renenutet. His cult disappeared with the ancient Egyptian funerary tradition, which ended in the fourth century AD.

Legacy

For centuries his image decorated canopic jars, chests, coffins and sarcophagi, and in the New Kingdom he took on the falcon form that made him recognisable.

Ancient sources: Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead (Papyrus of Ani), Book of Gates and the Dramatic Ramesseum Papyrus.

Trivia

Falcon-headed lid. The lids of the canopic jars took the form of the sons of Horus' heads. In the New Kingdom, Qebehsenuef appeared as a falcon.
Stars and compass points. The sons of Horus were linked with stars, regions of Egypt and the cardinal directions. Bernard Mathieu suggested equating them with stars of Orion and Ursa Major.
