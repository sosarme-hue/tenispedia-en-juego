Tarakasura

Tarakasura is a powerful asura in Hindu mythology, son of Vajranga and Vajrangi. He matters because his defeat at the hands of Kartikeya, son of Shiva, is the core of his story.

The myth

According to the legend, Diti wanted a son able to defeat the devas, the sons of Aditi, and Kashyapa granted her Vajranga, whose limbs were vajra. After he captured Indra and the devas, Brahma asked him to release them, and Vajrangi, the wife Brahma created for him, asked for a son who would rule the three worlds. Thus Taraka was born, and he obtained two boons: that no one would equal him in the three worlds, and that only a son of Shiva could slay him, a crafty wish since Shiva was an ascetic. Taraka drove the devas out of Svarga. To stir Shiva's desire for Parvati, Kamadeva was burnt to ash, but Parvati won Shiva's hand and Kartikeya was born.

In the war, Kartikeya defeated Taraka by piercing his chest with his spear, the Vel.

Legacy

Kalidasa retold the story in the epic poem Kumarasambhava, and Tagore wrote a poem about Kamadeva's love reduced to ashes.

Ancient sources: The Skanda Purana narrates the battle between Kartikeya and Taraka.

Trivia

A crafty wish. Taraka asked to be slain only by a son of Shiva, a wish considered clever because Shiva was an ascetic unlikely to have children.
Vishnu yields the duel. Vishnu stepped back from the final combat after Brahma reminded the gods of Taraka's boon, letting Skanda fulfill his destiny.
