king of Thebes, son of Eteocles

Laodamas

Laodamas is, in Greek myth, the king of Thebes who succeeded his father Eteocles. His story belongs to the Theban cycle and explains how the city fared after the second war against Argos.

The myth

According to the tradition, Laodamas was the son of Eteocles and was still a child when his father died. During his minority, the guardianship fell to Creon, who ruled in his name until the young man grew up.

As an adult, Laodamas took part in the second war of Thebes, in which the Thebans faced the Epigoni, who came from Argos. In the fighting near the city of Glisas, Laodamas killed Aegialeus, leader of the attackers. But the Argives ended up prevailing.

After the defeat, the story tells that Laodamas left Thebes at night, accompanied by all the inhabitants who chose to follow him, and set off towards Illyria. Another tradition, however, holds that he did not survive and was killed by Alcmaeon. Either way, according to the myth, the Thebans took advantage of a truce, while a herald negotiated with the Argives the end of hostilities, to abandon the city on the advice of the seer Tiresias.
