mistress of Menelaus, mother of Xenodamus

Cnossia

Cnossia is a minor figure in Greek myth, described as either a nymph or a slave, who bore a son, Xenodamus, to Menelaus, king of Sparta. She matters mainly for her name, which links her to the Cretan city of Cnossus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cnossia was the mistress of Menelaus, king of Sparta, and from their relationship came her son Xenodamus. Accounts present her either as a nymph or as a slave, and it is not clear which status applied to her.

The profile lists her father as Amnisus, but the reference texts give no further detail about her story, her deeds or what became of her son beyond his birth.

Legacy

Cnossia barely appears outside this account. The name is rare as a proper name, and it is often suggested that enslaved women were sometimes given names referring to their place of origin, which would point to a woman from Cnossus.

Trivia

A city name. Her name points to Cnossus, the city on Crete, although the sources do not explain whether she had any direct connection to that place.
A rarely attested name. Cnossia is not attested as a proper name elsewhere in ancient texts, which makes her case quite unusual.
