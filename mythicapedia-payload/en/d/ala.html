Igbo goddess of the earth

Ala

Ala is the goddess of the earth, fertility, morality and creativity in Igbo belief in south-eastern Nigeria. She is regarded as the highest deity in the pantheon and the guardian of Igbo law and custom.

The myth

In Igbo tradition, Ala is said to be the wife or daughter of Chukwu, the supreme god. Her name literally means "earth", and she is the ground itself, which is why all land is considered sacred.

In the myth, Ala rules the underworld and holds the souls of the dead in her sacred womb. She is credited with human fertility and the productivity of the fields. She judges human actions and watches over the laws and customs known as omenala. Taboos and crimes against her order are called nsọ Ala, and whoever commits them offends the goddess herself. Tradition also holds that Ala could swallow people into the underground. She protects women and children and is often shown holding a small child.

Worship and religion

Ala is still worshipped by the Igbo of Nigeria, who pay her homage every year during the yam festival. In the past, life-size clay sculptures were made for her in festive shrines called mbari. Her messenger and living agent on earth is the python, éké, which is especially revered in many Igbo communities.

Legacy

Her symbols are the crescent moon and the serpent. Her artistic image, seated on a throne surrounded by her family, forms part of the Igbo sculptural heritage.
