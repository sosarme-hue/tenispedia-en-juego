Egyptian goddess of the star Sirius

Sopdet (Sothis)

Sopdet, known to the Greeks as Sothis, is the Ancient Egyptian personification of the star Sirius. Her yearly reappearance heralded the Nile flood and the New Year, which made her central to the calendar and to farming life.

The myth

According to tradition, Sopdet is the consort of Sah, the personified constellation of Orion, and mother of Sopdu, the hawk god "Lord of the East". Other sources present her as the wife of Hapy, showing that genealogies varied by period and place. From an early date she was associated with Osiris, as bringer of the New Year and the Nile flood.

In the Old Kingdom she was an important goddess of the flood and a psychopomp who guided deceased pharaohs through the underworld. In some accounts she appears as the mother and sister of the pharaoh, whom she leads through the Fields of Aaru so he can become an eternal star. In the Middle Kingdom she was mainly a mother and nurse, and in the Ptolemaic period she was almost entirely absorbed into Isis.

Worship and religion

Her heliacal rising marked the start of the year, and the Egyptian New Year, Wep Renpet, was celebrated around her. She was also venerated as a goddess of the fertility that the flood brought to the soil. The sources give no evidence that her cult survives today.

Legacy

Her Greek name, Sothis, eventually became the name of the star Sirius itself. The Greeks called the hottest part of summer the "Dog Days", linked to the Dog Star. In tombs she was depicted on the so-called astronomical ceilings.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, who explains that the Greeks called the soul of Isis "Dog", and numerous Egyptian texts that identify Sopdet with Isis.

Trivia

The Dog Days. The Greeks called the hottest days of summer the Sirian period and linked them to certain illnesses. The Egyptians also connected this period with sicknesses.
A disputed tablet. For a long time an ivory tablet from the reign of Djer was thought to show Sopdet as a cow, but most Egyptologists no longer accept this identification.
