blind goddess of disease in Finnish mythology

Loviatar

Loviatar is a blind goddess of disease and plague in Finnish mythology, daughter of Tuoni, lord of the dead, and of Tuonetar. She matters because she explains the origin of the ailments that afflict people.

The myth

According to the Finnish runic songs, Loviatar was impregnated by the wind and gave birth to nine sons, who represent the diseases of the world. In other versions she is a maiden who conceives in water after being fertilised by a sea giant, and she herself names her children, giving them the names of diseases and disasters.

In the Kalevala, the collection by Elias Lönnrot, she appears separate from Louhi, the mistress of Pohjola, whom he portrays as a wicked queen. Some scholars connect her name with the word lovi, which refers to trance.

Legacy

In the Kalevala, Elias Lönnrot separated her from Louhi and turned her into a distinct character: a blind daughter of Tuoni.

Ancient sources: The Finnish runic songs (runot), collected by folklorists and compiled in Elias Lönnrot's Kalevala.

Trivia

Twelve children in Italy. In Italy it was said that Loviatar had twelve children rather than nine.
A name tied to trance. Her name is linked to lovi, and the phrase langeta loveen means to fall into a trance.
