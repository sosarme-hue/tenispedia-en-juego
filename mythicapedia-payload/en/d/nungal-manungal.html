Mesopotamian goddess of prisons

Nungal (Manungal)

Nungal, also known as Manungal, was the Mesopotamian goddess of prisons, sometimes linked to the underworld. She matters because she shows how Sumerian and Babylonian culture understood punishment, justice and the possibility of redemption.

The myth

According to the Sumerian hymn Nungal in the Ekur, the goddess is the daughter of Ereshkigal and Anu, although the text suggests the latter claim may not be meant literally. As daughter-in-law of Enlil, she held a place in the divine court, and her husband was Birtum, whose name means "fetter" or "shackle".

Her prison separated the guilty from the innocent and gave the former a chance at redemption, an idea compared to refining silver or being born again. Despite her role, she was regarded as compassionate, able to soften the most severe punishments.

Worship and religion

She was worshiped mainly in the Ur III period, in Nippur, Lagash and Ur, and in the Old Babylonian period also in Sippar. In the first millennium BCE she continued to receive worship in Nippur under the name Bēlet-balāṭi. There is no evidence that her cult survives today.

Legacy

Her hymn was part of the scribal school curriculum in the Old Babylonian period, which explains the large number of surviving copies.

Ancient sources: The Hymn to Nungal in the Ekur, a Sumerian hymn preserved in many Old Babylonian copies, and god lists such as the Fara list and An = Anum.

Trivia

A mother's name. The name Manungal may be a contraction of ama Nungal, meaning "mother Nungal".
A male god with a feminine name. Birtum means "shackle" in Akkadian and is grammatically feminine, yet the deity was regarded as male.
