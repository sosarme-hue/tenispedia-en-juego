Athenian hero, son of Hermes and Herse

Cephalus

Cephalus is an Athenian hero of Greek myth, son of Hermes and Herse, and lover of Eos, goddess of dawn. His story is tied to the origin of the kings of Cyprus and to several myths about the stars.

The myth

According to the tales, Eos fell in love with Cephalus, carried him off and had with him Tithonus, father of Phaethon. Thus Cephalus appears as an ancestor of the kings of Cyprus. In the Theogony, Cephalus and Eos are the direct parents of Phaethon, and in other versions this boy is abducted by Aphrodite, who makes him guardian of her temples.

Tradition also links him to the stars: the morning star, called Lucifer, Hesperus or Phaethon, was imagined as the son of Cephalus and Eos. According to Pausanias, in Athens a portico showed Hemera carrying off Cephalus.

Legacy

The morning star bears the name Phaethon in some astronomical traditions, and his descendants head the list of Cypriot kings in some accounts.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Apollodorus' Library and Pausanias' Description of Greece.
