Argive hero, son of Capaneus

Sthenelus

Sthenelus was a king of Argos, son of Capaneus and Evadne, and one of the most prominent Greek warriors in the Trojan War alongside Diomedes. He matters mainly for his role in the saga of Thebes and the Epigoni, the sons of those who attacked Thebes.

The myth

According to tradition, his father Capaneus died after defying Zeus at the walls of Thebes: while climbing a ladder, he was struck down by a thunderbolt. Sthenelus watched as his mother Evadne threw herself onto her husband's funeral pyre. The sons of the Seven Against Thebes swore to avenge their fathers and became known as the Epigoni; ten years later they took the city.

In the Iliad, Sthenelus fights beside Diomedes, driving his chariot and advising him in battle. When Agamemnon compares the two to their fathers, Sthenelus flares up and boasts that they achieved what their fathers could not, but he heeds Diomedes' advice and holds his tongue. According to Philostratus, it was he who killed Pandarus. He also hid inside the Trojan Horse.

After the war, he ruled with Diomedes the half of Argos that had belonged to Iphis. His son Cometes had an affair with Aegiale, Diomedes' wife, and the two plotted to kill Diomedes, who escaped to Italy. It is unknown whether Sthenelus joined him. He was succeeded by his son Cylarabes, during whose reign Argos was reunited.

Legacy

The asteroid 3794 Sthenelos is named after him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him alongside Diomedes and Agamemnon; Philostratus credits him with killing Pandarus.

Trivia

An asteroid bears his name. The asteroid 3794 Sthenelos is named after the hero, one of the few modern traces of his figure.
A fiery hero. In the Iliad, Sthenelus gets angry at Agamemnon, but he takes Diomedes' advice and falls silent.
