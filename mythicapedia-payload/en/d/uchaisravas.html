seven-headed flying horse of Hindu mythology

Uchchaihshravas

Uchchaihshravas is a seven-headed flying horse of Hindu mythology, born from the churning of the ocean of milk and regarded as the finest of horses and their king. Its story appears in the Mahabharata and many later texts, and it remains one of the best-known mythical horses of India.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Uchchaihshravas rose from the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean of milk, alongside treasures such as the goddess Lakshmi, whom Vishnu chose as his consort, and the amrita, the elixir of life. King of the gods Indra seized the horse and made it his vahana, or mount. Other accounts describe it as the mount of Bali, king of the asuras, who used it to achieve impossible feats.

The Mahabharata also tells of a wager between Vinata and Kadru, wives of the sage Kashyapa, over the colour of the horse's tail. Vinata said it was white, while Kadru said it was black. Kadru ordered her naga serpent sons to cover the tail, and she won the bet. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna identifies himself with Uchchaihshravas among horses. Other tellings say the demon Tarakasura stole it from heaven, or that the king Prithu named it king of horses.

Legacy

The logo of Dark Horse Records, the music label founded by former Beatle George Harrison, is inspired by this horse. It also appears as a purchasable horse in the video game Kingdom Come: Deliverance.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Bhagavad Gita, the Ramayana and several Puranas, such as the Vishnu Purana, mention Uchchaihshravas.

Trivia

A horse with a disputed tail. In the Mahabharata, the bet over the colour of the tail ends with the loser becoming the winner's servant.
A horse in the Bhagavad Gita. Krishna declares that among horses he is Uchchaihshravas, the one born from the amrita.
