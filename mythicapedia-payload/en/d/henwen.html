magical sow of Welsh legend

Henwen

Henwen, meaning "Old White", is a sow in Welsh legend who, according to the Welsh Triads, gave birth to a host of creatures, among them the monstrous cat Cath Palug. Her story links Welsh tradition with the Arthurian tales.

The myth

According to the tradition, the sow was kept by Coll, son of Collfrewy, a swineherd for Dallwyr Dallben. As she was about to give birth, a prophecy warned that this would bode ill for the Isle of Britain, so she was chased until she plunged into the sea at Penrhyn Awstin, in Cornwall. She later re-emerged on land at Aber Tarogi, in Gwent Is-coed.

Along her wanderings she bore various creatures: at Wheat Field in Gwent, a grain of wheat and a bee; at Llonion, a grain of barley and another bee; at Lleyn, a grain of rye; on the Hill of Cyferthwch in Snowdonia, a wolf-cub and an eaglet; and at Llanfair, under the Black Rock, a kitten. The wolf and the eagle were adopted by eminent men, but both came to harm. The swineherd threw the kitten into the Menai Strait, and the sons of Palug raised it on Anglesey until it became Cath Palug.

Legacy

In Lloyd Alexander's The Chronicles of Prydain, Hen Wen is a clairvoyant pig kept by Dallben and Coll and looked after by Taran, the young protagonist. The character also appears in Disney's The Black Cauldron (1985), an adaptation of the first two books. In the manga and anime The Seven Deadly Sins, a character called Hawk Mama is based on Henwen.

Ancient sources: The Welsh Triads, in the versions of the Red Book of Hergest and the White Book of Rhydderch, recount the tale of the sow and her offspring.

Trivia

A revealing name. Henwen means "Old White", a name that suggests an ancient, respected figure rather than an ordinary farm animal.
A historical reading. Don Carleton suggests the tale may be an allegory of Arthur's pursuit of a female religious leader across south-west Britain.
