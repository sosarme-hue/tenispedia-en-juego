Hindu goddess of the Ganges river

Ganga

Ganga is the goddess who personifies the Ganges river in Hinduism, revered as a source of purification and forgiveness. Her best-known story tells how she descended from heaven to earth to purify the souls of a king's ancestors.

The myth

According to the Rigveda, the oldest Vedic text, the river was already revered in the Vedic period, although its hymns do not always make clear whether they refer to Ganga. Later accounts vary about her origin. The Bhagavata Purana says Vishnu brought her into being when he extended his foot, while the Ramayana presents her as the eldest daughter of the Himalayas and elder sister of Parvati.

Her most famous story involves King Bhagiratha. The sixty thousand sons of King Sagara had been burned to death by the gaze of a penitent sage and wandered as spirits until the ritual their father had begun was completed. To save them, Bhagiratha asked that Ganga be brought down from heaven. Fearing the impact on the earth, the goddess was caught in Shiva's tangled hair and gently released. Upon reaching the ocean, according to the tale, she fell into the underworld and freed the souls of Sagara's sons.

In the Mahabharata, Ganga becomes the wife of King Shantanu on condition that he never question her actions. She drowns the first children they have together until the king objects, and she leaves him. The eighth son, Bhishma, survives and becomes one of the most respected figures of the epic.

Worship and religion

Hindus regard the Ganges as the holiest river since the Vedic period. Cities such as Varanasi (Benares), Haridwar, Gangotri and Prayagraj are pilgrimage destinations, where people bathe or meditate on the banks, believing each immersion atones for a sin. Placing the ashes of the dead in its waters is believed to help the soul reach moksha, liberation from the cycle of life and death. Festivals in her honour include Ganga Dussehra and Ganga Jayanti. She is depicted with fair skin and a white crown, seated on a makara, and in Thailand she is worshipped during the Loy Krathong festival alongside Buddha. Her worship remains alive today.

Legacy

The river she personifies remains central to India's religious identity. One of her names, Vishnupadi, meaning 'emanating from the feet of Vishnu', recalls the divine origin the tradition attributes to her.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda mentions her in uncertain terms, while her story is narrated in the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and various Puranas, such as the Skanda Purana and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Two mothers for Ganesha. According to the Skanda Purana, Ganesha has two mothers, Parvati and Ganga, which is why he is called Gangueia, 'son of Ganga'.
A name tied to Vishnu. Her name Vishnupadi means 'born from the feet of Vishnu', alluding to her origin in the god's foot.
