horse-like sea demon of Orcadian folklore

Nuckelavee

The nuckelavee is a horse-like demon from Orcadian folklore, the tradition of the Orkney Islands off northern Scotland. It is considered the most fearsome of the demons of Scotland's Northern Isles, and its stories reflect islanders' fears of drought, epidemics and the sea.

The myth

According to tradition, the nuckelavee lives mostly in the sea, but it comes ashore, where its breath withers crops, sickens livestock and brings drought and epidemics. Its appearance is that of a skinless horse, with a human head and torso that seem to sprout from its back, and a single eye that burns red like a flame. Black blood runs through its yellow veins, and its pale sinews and muscles can be seen pulsating beneath the flesh.

Only the Mither o' the Sea, an ancient spirit, can control it, and she keeps it confined during the summer months. Like other sea monsters, it cannot tolerate fresh water, so crossing a river or stream is enough to escape it. A islander named Tammas claimed to have survived an encounter with the creature, and he got away by splashing it with water from a loch. His account, recorded by folklorist Walter Traill Dennison, is the only known first-hand description, though details vary between writers.

In some versions, the monster punished those who burned seaweed to make kelp, whose smoke enraged it and brought plague and the death of cattle.

Legacy

The nuckelavee appears in the television series Grimm, where it is a horse-type wesen, and in the Japanese manga Mahō Tsukai no Yome. It was also a figure in the Monster in My Pocket collection and was adapted in the SCP Foundation as SCP-3456, nicknamed "The Riders of Horcada".

Ancient sources: The Orcadian descriptions attributed to Jo Ben, from the 16th century, record tales of Orcadian demons, though it is unclear whether they mention the nuckelavee.

Trivia

Origin of its name. According to folklorist Walter Traill Dennison, its name comes from the Orcadian dialect word knoggelvi and means "Devil of the Sea".
A punishment for kelp. Burning seaweed to make soda ash, known locally as kelp, is linked to the nuckelavee's fury.
