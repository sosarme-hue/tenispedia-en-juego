minor funerary deity of ancient Egypt

Isdes

Isdes is a minor funerary deity of ancient Egypt, linked to the judgment of the dead in the underworld. As an assistant to Thoth, he serves as a discreet but important witness to the fate of souls.

The myth

In Egyptian tradition, Isdes assists Thoth as the recorder of the weighing of the heart, the judgment presided over by Osiris. Some accounts also make him one of the four hamadryas baboons who guard the lake of fire in the Duat, the underworld, and he is associated with defending and justifying the dead before the tribunal.

He is depicted with a human body and the head of a baboon or an ibis, like Thoth, though he also appears with a jackal's head. A predynastic precursor was Babi or Baba, a bloodthirsty god said to devour the entrails of wicked dead, who, according to belief, granted lasting vigour to the righteous and carried them to paradise using his penis as a mast.

Ancient sources: The Book of the Dead mentions him in chapters 18 and 145, and a different Isdes, Lord of the West, in chapter 17.

Trivia

A god with a mast. His predecessor Babi was said to use his penis as the mast of a boat to carry the dead to the Egyptian paradise.
Four guardians of fire. Isdes is counted among the four hamadryas baboons who guard the lake of fire in the Duat.
