sixth Jain tirthankara

Padmaprabha

Padmaprabha is the sixth tirthankara of the current cosmic age in Jainism, a teacher who, according to Jain belief, became a siddha, a liberated soul, after destroying all his karma. His name means 'bright as a red lotus' in Sanskrit.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, he was born into the Ikshvaku dynasty, son of King Sridhara and Queen Susima, in Kosambi. While he was in the womb, his mother craved to sleep on a bed of red lotuses, which gave him his name.

He renounced his kingdom to become an ascetic, attained omniscience while meditating beneath a banyan tree, and achieved liberation on Mount Shikharji.

Worship and religion

His veneration continues today. Pilgrims visit Mount Shikharji, where a shrine houses his footprints, as well as temples such as the Laxmani Jain Tirth in Madhya Pradesh, the Padampura Jain Temple near Jaipur, and the Mahudi Jain Temple in Gujarat.

Legacy

In Jain art he is shown meditating, with a reddish complexion, and the red lotus is carved on the pedestal beneath his idols as his emblem.

Trivia

Distinctive colour. In art he is shown with a reddish complexion, which sets him apart in his statues.
A symbolically long life. Jain cosmology gives him a symbolic lifespan of 3,000,000 years and a height of 250 bows, about 750 metres.
