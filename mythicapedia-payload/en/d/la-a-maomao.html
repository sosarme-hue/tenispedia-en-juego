Hawaiian god of the wind

La'a Maomao

In Hawaiian mythology, La'a Maomao is the god of the wind, usually remembered as a largely benevolent being and the god of forgiveness. In his male form he matches the wind gods of other Polynesian traditions, although some Hawaiian stories portray the wind deity as a woman.

The myth

According to tradition, he was created in the midst of chaos by his father, the sun god. One account has him accompany Moikeha from Kahiki to Hawaiʻi, where he settled at Hale-o-Lono on the island of Molokaʻi.

In the story of Pakaʻa, the wind deity appears as a woman, also named Laʻamaomao, a name often rendered as "distant sacredness." She guarded a gourd holding all the winds of Hawaiʻi, which could be summoned by chanting their names. The gourd passed from her to her granddaughter, then to that granddaughter's son Pakaʻa, and from him to his son, Kuʻa-Pakaʻa.

Worship and religion

He was worshiped as an ʻaumakua, a family or guardian deity, of the winds at Hale-o-Lono on Molokaʻi.

Trivia

The winds in a gourd. The winds of Hawaiʻi were kept in a gourd and called forth by chanting their names.
God or goddess. The same wind deity appears as a god in one account and as a goddess in another, a variation that seems to be Hawaiian.
