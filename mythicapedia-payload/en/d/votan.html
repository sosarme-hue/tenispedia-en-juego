Tzeltal culture hero of Chiapas

Votan

Votan is a legendary figure of the Tzeltal Maya of Chiapas, presented as a culture hero who supposedly founded Palenque. His story comes from colonial-era sources of the 17th and 18th centuries, not from ancient Maya texts.

The myth

According to an account recorded by Bishop Francisco Núñez de la Vega in 1702, Votan was present at a great construction that his uncle ordered built to reach heaven, after which God gave each people its language and charged Votan with dividing the lands of Anahuac.

Later writers turned him into a noble who arrived from the Old World, founded the kingdom of Na Chan on the Usumacinta River, and left descendants who spread across Chiapas.

Worship and religion

Ritual objects linked to Votan were kept in a sanctuary until Bishop Núñez de la Vega had them burned in the main square of Huehuetlán in 1691. His name was also one of the twenty day names of the Tzeltal calendar.

Legacy

Humboldt and Donnelly linked him to Odin and Atlantis, and New Age leader José Argüelles reinterpreted him as 'Pacal Votan'. Today he lives on as Votan Zapata, a legendary spirit of Emiliano Zapata honored by the Zapatista Army (EZLN).

Ancient sources: A Chiapanese manuscript cited by Núñez de la Vega in 1702 and the Probanza de Votan, attributed to Ramón de Ordóñez y Aguilar.

Trivia

Resemblance to Wotan. Alexander von Humboldt compared the name Votan with Wodan or Odin and with the names of a day of the week in different regions.
A day in the calendar. Votan is one of the twenty day names of the Tzeltal calendar, linking him to that culture's sacred timekeeping.
