kami of jewelsmithing in Japanese mythology

Tamanooya-no-Mikoto

Tamanooya-no-Mikoto is a kami of Japanese mythology, credited with creating the Yasakani no Magatama, one of the three imperial regalia of Japan. He matters for his role in the ancient accounts and for his link to the Shinabe clan.

The myth

In the myths, Tamanooya was one of the gods who devised the plan to lure Amaterasu out of the cave where she had hidden herself. To tempt her out, the jewel he had made was hung outside the entrance.

The sources disagree about his lineage. The Nihon Shoki makes him a son of Ninigi, while the Shinsen Shōjiroku calls him a grandson of Takamimusubi. He also goes by many names, among them Toyotama and Tama no oya.

Worship and religion

He is venerated at Tamanooya-jinja in Yamaguchi Prefecture, and he is regarded as the ancestral kami of the Shinabe clan. Tradition holds that his grave is the Tama-no-iwaya cave. He is not venerated at any kampeisha, the officially ranked shrines.

Legacy

His name is tied to the Yasakani no Magatama jewel, one of the three imperial regalia, popularly known as the jewel, the sword and the mirror.

Ancient sources: The Nihon Shoki and the Shinsen Shōjiroku record his genealogy and his various names.

Trivia

A tomb in a cave. Tradition identifies the Tama-no-iwaya cave as his grave, linking him to his role in the story of the sun goddess.
Many names. He is known by several names, such as Amenoakarutama, Haakaru tama or Toyotama, which reflect his connection with jewels.
