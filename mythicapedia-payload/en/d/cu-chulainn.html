Irish hero of the Ulster Cycle

Cú Chulainn

Cú Chulainn is the best-known hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, also found in Scottish and Manx folklore. Son of the god Lugh and the mortal Deichtine, he was famed for his strength and battle fury, and is often compared to the Greek hero Achilles.

The myth

According to the myth, as a child he was called Sétanta, and his birth has several versions. In the earliest, his mother Deichtine, daughter and charioteer of King Conchobar mac Nessa, attends the birth of a boy during a hunt, in a house that vanishes by morning, while a mare gives birth to twin colts. The child falls ill and dies, and the god Lugh appears to Deichtine to tell her that he has placed his son in her womb, who must be called Sétanta. In a later version, Deichtine is Conchobar's sister and Lugh is again the host that night.

Raised by several Ulster nobles, Sétanta killed the fierce guard dog of the smith Culann in self-defence as a boy, after arriving late to a feast. He promised to guard the house until a replacement dog was reared, and the druid Cathbad gave him the name Cú Chulainn, the Hound of Culann. Later, when he received his arms, he broke spear after spear and chariot after chariot until the king gave him his own. He was trained by Scáthach, who taught him the spear Gáe Bolg, and at seventeen he defended Ulster alone against Queen Medb's armies in the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

His best-known trait was the ríastrad, a battle frenzy that turned him into an unrecognisable monster who knew neither friend nor foe. According to the most famous legends, he died young, at 27, after being deceived by his enemies. It is said that to die on his feet he bound himself to a standing stone, and his enemies dared not approach until a raven landed on his shoulder to drink his blood.

Legacy

Cú Chulainn has inspired art and literature. Lady Gregory retold many of his legends in Cuchulain of Muirthemne (1902), softening the violence of the originals. The sculptor Oliver Sheppard made a bronze of the dying hero that stands in the General Post Office in Dublin, commemorating the Easter Rising of 1916, and his image also appears in a stained glass panel at St. Enda's School. The town of Dundalk takes as its motto 'Mé do rug Cú Chulainn cróga', 'I gave birth to brave Cú Chulainn'.

Ancient sources: The Táin Bó Cúailnge ('Cattle Raid of Cooley') of the Ulster Cycle, which recounts his childhood in a flashback, and the tale Compert Con Culainn, which tells of his conception.

Trivia

Superhuman features. According to the tale, he had seven fingers on each hand and toe and seven pupils in each eye, features said to enhance his feats.
Another childhood name. In another version of the birth, the boy is named Sédana, a name given by Ceat mac Mágach before he is placed with foster parents.
