Auðr

Auðr is a figure of Norse mythology, son of Nótt, the personified night, and Naglfari. He has few stories of his own and appears mainly as Thor's uncle in the Prose Edda.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Auðr was born to Nótt and Naglfari. His name means "prosperity" in Old Norse. Hár mentions him in Gylfaginning when listing the family of the night, but no deeds or episodes are attributed to him.

He appears again in Skáldskaparmál, where Jörð, the earth, is called "sister of Auðr" in a poetic periphrasis. A verse by the skald Hallfreðr vandræðaskáld also refers to "Auðr's splendid sister". His role is mainly that of a link in a genealogy.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál) and skaldic poetry, including a verse by Hallfreðr vandræðaskáld.

Trivia

A name meaning prosperity. Auðr means "prosperity" in Old Norse, a name that contrasts with how little is told about him.
Perhaps an invention. The scholar Rudolf Simek believes Snorri Sturluson may have created Auðr, though he does not know why.
