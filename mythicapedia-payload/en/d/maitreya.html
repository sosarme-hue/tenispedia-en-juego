bodhisattva and future Buddha in Buddhism

Maitreya

Maitreya is the bodhisattva whom Buddhism regards as the next Buddha of this world, the successor to Gautama Buddha. Recognized by all Buddhist schools, he embodies the promise of a future age in which the Dharma will be taught again.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, Maitreya is a bodhisattva who waits in the Tushita heaven, where he currently dwells. Mahayana sutras describe him as a worthy monk who, over many lives, grew in wisdom and taught the Dharma before being reborn in Tushita.

Tradition holds that Gautama Buddha announced Maitreya as his successor. When Gautama's teachings have been forgotten, Maitreya will descend to Earth, attain complete enlightenment and teach a Dharma similar to that of his predecessor, re-establishing it in the world. He will be the fifth and final Buddha of the current kalpa.

Although he resides in Tushita, the accounts say Maitreya is not out of reach. Various Buddhists throughout history have claimed to have had visions of him or received teachings from him. Mahayana Buddhists also regard him as the revealer of key scriptures of the Yogacara school.

Worship and religion

His devotional cult emerged early in India and became especially popular in Central Asia and China between the 4th and 8th centuries. Devotees sought rebirth in Tushita, first to hear his teachings and later to join him as the coming Buddha. In East Asia the cult of Amitabha largely eclipsed it over time, yet hopes for Maitreya's golden age periodically resurfaced in millenarian movements. He is also the only bodhisattva generally venerated in the Theravada tradition.

Legacy

His image, often seated with legs pendant and a small stupa on his head, is among the most recognizable in Buddhist art. His name has also been adopted by non-Buddhist religions and new religious movements, such as Yiguandao and Falun Gong, and by many leaders who have claimed to be Maitreya, a claim Buddhist schools reject.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Lotus Sutra, the Amitabha Sutra and the Cakkavatti-sihanada Sutta of the Pali Canon, as well as in the Buddhavamsa.

Trivia

Name linked to Mitra. His name is related to Mitra, an ancient Vedic and Iranian god, and to the Sanskrit word maitri, meaning friendship.
Images in Gandhara. In Greco-Buddhist art of Gandhara, in northern India, Maitreya was the most popular figure depicted alongside Gautama.
