Tilottama

Tilottama is an apsara, a celestial nymph in Hindu mythology, whose name is usually read as the one whose smallest particle is the finest. Her story, told mainly in the Mahabharata, shows her as an enchantress created to bring about the downfall of two demons and to stir rivalry among the gods.

The myth

In the Mahabharata, Brahma asked the divine architect Vishvakarma to create a beautiful woman. Vishvakarma gathered the finest of the three worlds and all the gems of the world to shape Tilottama, whom Brahma named for being formed bit by bit. Her task was to seduce the asuras Sunda and Upasunda, sons of Nikumbha, who had grown so powerful that they drove the gods out of heaven. The brothers, until then inseparable, quarrelled over her beside a river in the Vindhya mountains, struck each other with their clubs and died.

The gods praised her, and Brahma granted her the freedom to roam the universe. Other accounts show her power over the gods: when she circled Shiva, he grew several faces to see her, while Indra sprouted a thousand eyes. According to the Padma Purana, she had been an ugly widow named Kubja in an earlier life, and was reborn as an apsara after a long ritual. Tilottama also appears as an agent of curses, most notably against King Sahasranika.

Legacy

Her story was adapted into a 1954 film directed by Babubhai Mistry and produced by Homi Wadia.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, especially Books 1 and 13; the Padma Purana, the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, and the Kathasaritsagara, an 11th-century Sanskrit version of the Brihatkatha.

Trivia

A thousand eyes. According to the Mahabharata, Indra grew a thousand eyes across his body when he saw her, a striking image of his desire.
Shiva's faces. As she circled Shiva, he grew several faces to watch her, and the Mahabharata explains his five faces in different ways.
