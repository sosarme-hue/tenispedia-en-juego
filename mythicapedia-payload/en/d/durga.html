warrior goddess of Hinduism

Durga

Durga is one of the most important goddesses in Hinduism, revered as the principal aspect of the Great Goddess Mahadevi and associated with protection, strength, motherhood and the fight against evil. She is central to Shaktism, though she also matters in Shaivism and Vaishnavism.

The myth

According to the tradition, Durga emerges to defeat the demons that threaten peace, prosperity and dharma. Her best-known deed is the slaying of Mahishasura, the buffalo demon, in battle. Other stories show her fighting demons such as Shumbha and Nishumbha, calling on other warrior goddesses, the Matrikas, and even Kali to help her.

The Shakta scriptures, such as the Devi Mahatmya and the Devi Bhagavata Purana, present her as the primordial creator of the universe and as the ultimate reality, Brahman. In Vaishnava traditions she is seen as a form of the goddess Lakshmi, or even as Vishnu's sister, while later traditions link her with Shiva, though she is not usually regarded as his consort. In Bengali regional traditions she is the mother of Ganesha, Kartikeya, Lakshmi and Sarasvati.

Worship and religion

Durga is widely worshipped in India, Nepal, Bangladesh and elsewhere, especially after the spring and autumn harvests. Her major festivals include Durga Puja, Durga Ashtami, Vijayadashami, Deepavali and Navaratri. She is usually shown as a warrior riding a lion or tiger, with many arms holding weapons, though traditions also use geometric diagrams called yantras to honour her. Her weapons are read as symbols of self-discipline, devotion and meditation, and warriors have long invoked her before battle.

Legacy

Her name is usually rendered as "the inaccessible" or "the invincible", and it is linked to the word durg, meaning a fortress. Her iconography, with its calm face amid violent action, has shaped Indian religious art for centuries.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda contains a hymn to the Goddess, the Devi Sukta, and the Mahabharata and the Ramayana mention the name Durga. The main texts devoted to her are the Devi Mahatmya, the Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Markandeya Purana.

Trivia

Nine forms. Her nine appellations, known as Navadurga, are honoured during Navaratri.
A goddess of the pen. Some intellectuals place a pen in her hand, considering her stylus to be her weapon.
