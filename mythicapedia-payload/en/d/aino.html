young heroine of Finnish mythology

Aino

Aino is a young woman in the Kalevala, the Finnish national epic compiled by Elias Lönnrot from oral poetry. Her tragic story made her one of the most beloved female figures in Finland.

The myth

According to the tale, Aino is the only daughter and the only sister of Joukahainen. Her brother loses a singing contest to Väinämöinen, the most famous singer and sage of the poem, and promises Aino's hands and feet to the winner. Her mother is delighted by a match with such a renowned and well-born man, but Aino has no wish to marry someone so old.

The tradition offers two endings. In one version, Aino drowns herself; in another, she is transformed into a water nymph. Afterwards she returns as a perch to taunt the grieving Väinämöinen.

Legacy

The name Aino does not appear in the original poems, which call her only the "only daughter" or "only sister". Lönnrot invented it, giving it the sense of "the only one". During the national romantic period at the end of the nineteenth century, Fennoman activists adopted it as a Christian name. Among the first to bear it were Aino Järnefelt, later Aino Sibelius, and Aino Krohn, later Aino Kallas. According to the Finnish Population Register Centre, over 60,000 women have received the name. It was especially popular in the early twentieth century, the most common women's name in the 1920s, and it became the most chosen name for girls again in 2006 and 2007.

Ancient sources: The original oral poems, collected by Lönnrot for the Kalevala, call her only the only daughter or only sister, without a personal name.

Trivia

An invented name. Lönnrot created the name Aino for the Kalevala. The original oral poems do not use it.
From myth to real life. Many Finnish women were given the name in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, encouraged by the nationalist movement.
A name that returns. After falling out of favour, the name became the most popular for girls in Finland again in 2006 and 2007.
