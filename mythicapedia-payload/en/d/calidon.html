Greek hero, eponymous ruler of Calydon in Aetolia

Calydon

Calydon is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Aetolus, traditionally credited with giving his name to the city of Calydon in Aetolia. His name is also linked to a nearby mountain by the Achelous river.

The myth

According to tradition, Calydon was the son of King Aetolus and Pronoe, daughter of Phorbus, and the brother of Pleuron. He married Aeolia, daughter of Amythaon, and had two daughters, Epicaste and Protogeneia.

A later version gives a different origin for the name of a mountain near the Achelous, which was once called Gyrus and took the name Calydon from a Calydon said to be the son of Ares and Astynome. This Calydon, having seen Artemis bathing, was turned into rock by the goddess. On that mountain grows the herb myops: whoever washes their face with it loses their sight, but if they atone for their guilt in honour of Artemis, she restores it.

The genealogies do not fully agree: according to another account, Protogeneia consorted with Ares, while Epicaste married her cousin Agenor.
