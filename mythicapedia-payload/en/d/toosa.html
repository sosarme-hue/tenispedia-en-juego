Greek sea nymph, mother of Polyphemus

Thoosa

Thoosa is a sea nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of Phorcys and, according to Homer, mother of the Cyclops Polyphemus by Poseidon. She matters mainly as one of the few female figures who give rise to a giant lineage in the Homeric tradition.

The myth

According to the ancient texts, Thoosa was the daughter of Phorcys, a primordial sea god. Her Greek name, Θόωσα, means "swift", and the scholar Eustathius linked it to the impetuosity of anger, a fitting echo of her son's vengeful nature.

Tradition makes her a lover of Poseidon, by whom she bore Polyphemus, the Cyclops who appears in the Odyssey. Homer does not say whether she bore other Cyclopes. Theocritus names her companion as the Nereid Galatea, whom Polyphemus loved.

The genealogies vary. A single source makes her the consort of Apollo and mother of the poet Linus, and Servius states that Phorcys was the son of Poseidon and Thoosa, which conflicts with the Homeric version. Another figure, also called Thoosa, was the mother of the naiad Strymo and wife of the river god Scamander.

Ancient sources: Homer names her in the Odyssey as the mother of Polyphemus; Theocritus, Eustathius and Servius record later variants.
