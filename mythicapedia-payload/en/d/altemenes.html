Cretan prince, son of Catreus

Althaemenes

Althaemenes is a Cretan prince of Greek myth, son of King Catreus, whose story of an oracle fulfilled by accident illustrates the power of fate. His tale survives mainly in Diodorus Siculus and Apollodorus.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus, Althaemenes received an oracle foretelling that he would kill his father. To avoid this, he fled Crete for Rhodes with many followers and set up an altar to Zeus Atabyrius on Mount Atabyrus, today Attavyros.

Years later, Catreus sailed in search of his son, his heir. One night his ship stopped at Rhodes, a fight broke out, and Althaemenes unknowingly killed his father with his spear. When he learned what he had done, he wandered from place to place until he died of grief. By the command of an oracle, the Rhodians honoured him as a hero.

Apollodorus tells a similar story with variations. Catreus also received an oracle, that he would be killed by one of his children, and though he hid it, Althaemenes learned of it and fled with his sister Apemosyne to Rhodes. There Apemosyne was raped by Hermes; when she told her brother, he did not believe her and kicked her to death. Later Catreus arrived searching for Althaemenes, and when his crew was mistaken for pirates, the prince again killed his father unknowingly. This time, realizing what had happened, Althaemenes prayed to the gods and fell into a chasm.

Worship and religion

According to Diodorus, the Rhodians honoured him as a hero by oracular command, and the altar to Zeus Atabyrius on Mount Atabyrus recalls the prince's link with the island.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus and Apollodorus preserve his story, with versions that agree on the accidental patricide but differ in details.
