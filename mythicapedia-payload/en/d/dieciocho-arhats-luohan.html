Buddha's disciples in Chinese Buddhism

Eighteen Arhats (Luohan)

The Eighteen Arhats, known in China as Luohan, are disciples of Gautama Buddha who reached nirvana and were charged with protecting the Buddhist faith until the coming of Maitreya. They are a popular subject in Chinese Buddhist art.

The myth

According to tradition, the arhats were originally ten disciples of Gautama Buddha, although the earliest Indian sutras mention only four, such as Pindola, Kundadhana, Panthaka and Nakula, who were told to await Maitreya. The group grew first to sixteen, adding patriarchs and other masters, and finally to eighteen after two more were added towards the end of the Tang dynasty.

Worship and religion

In China, devotion to the Luohan gained momentum at the end of the ninth century, after a great persecution of Buddhists, and they were revered as guardians of the faith. Guanxiu's portraits, painted in 891, are carefully preserved at the Shengyin Temple in Hangzhou.

Legacy

Guanxiu's portraits became the definitive iconography of the Luohan. Emperor Qianlong admired them in 1757, wrote a eulogy for each one and in 1764 ordered copies engraved on stone tablets.

Ancient sources: The Nandimitrāvadāna, translated into Chinese by Xuanzang in 654, records the names of these disciples.

Trivia

Luohan in a dream. Guanxiu explained that he had painted what he saw in dreams, so he called these figures 'Luohan in a dream'.
Dragon and tiger. The last two added Luohan, the Taming Dragon and the Taming Tiger, are seen as a veiled criticism of Taoism.
