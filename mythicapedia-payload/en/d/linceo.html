Argonaut hero of Greek mythology

Lynceus

Lynceus is a Messenian prince in Greek mythology, brother of Idas, famous for a sight that could see through walls, trees and the earth itself. His story is told mainly in connection with the voyage of the Argonauts and his violent death at the hands of the Dioscuri.

The myth

According to the most common genealogy, Lynceus was a son of Aphareus and Arene, daughter of Ebalus, and brother of Idas and Peisus. Other accounts give his mother as Polydora or Laocoosa. His name, from the Greek for "lynx-sight", reflects his most famous gift: he was said to see through walls, trees and even skin, and to perceive what lay beneath the ground. Some stories add that he could also see in the dark. He took part in the hunt for the Calydonian boar and, in some accounts, in the voyage of the Argonauts, where he served as the lookout of the ship.

His end came from a quarrel with his cousins Castor and Polydeuces, the Dioscuri, over the spoils of a joint cattle raid in Arcadia. The Dioscuri attacked Messenia, seized the booty and set an ambush for the sons of Aphareus. Lynceus spotted Castor from afar and warned Idas, who killed him. Polydeuces pursued Lynceus and killed him with a spear, but when he went after Idas he was struck on the head by a rock and fell unconscious.

Another version places the cause in the abduction of Leucippus's daughters, who had been promised to Lynceus and Idas, by the Dioscuri. When the brothers fought back, the outcome was much the same, though this time it was Castor who killed Lynceus in battle.

Legacy

Lynceus's keen sight has been compared to the real technique of X-ray photography and to Superman's X-ray vision. A rationalist explanation holds that the rumor of his power to see underground arose from his knowledge of geology and gold mining, which made him seem to uncover gold by magic when he went down into the mines.
