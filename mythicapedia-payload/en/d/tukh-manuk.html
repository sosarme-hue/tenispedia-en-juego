rural shrines and folk figure of Armenia

Tukh Manuk

Tukh Manuk, meaning "dark-skinned youth", names a group of rural shrines in Armenia and also the character from folk tales linked to them. The shrines likely began before Christianity and are now part of the folk traditions within the Armenian Church.

The myth

Folk tales portray Tukh Manuk as the main character of many medieval and modern folk poems. His stories are partly based on oral traditions from the 8th to 10th centuries and on the struggles of local Christian kings against Arab rivals.

He also appears in the Armenian national epic Sasna Tsrer (Daredevils of Sassoun).

Worship and religion

The shrines are often found on hilltops, beside springs or just outside villages, frequently among church ruins or in simple enclosures. Pilgrims, traditionally more often women, make offerings and sacrifices and light candles to seek the cure of illnesses. Yazidis also visit them.

Legacy

His figure remains alive in Armenian folk poetry and epic, passed down for centuries.

Ancient sources: The Armenian national epic Sasna Tsrer (Daredevils of Sassoun) mentions him, and his stories draw on oral traditions from the 8th to 10th centuries.

Trivia

Ancient origins. Some of these shrines are thought to date back to the 5th century or even to the pre-Christian era.
Visitors of another faith. Although tied to Armenian Christian tradition, the shrines are also visited by Yazidis.
