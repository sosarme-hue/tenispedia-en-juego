Astyoche

Astyoche is a Minyan princess in Greek mythology, mother of the heroes Ascalaphus and Ialmenus. Her story matters because it links the Minyans of Orchomenus with the god Ares and with the expedition against Troy.

The myth

According to tradition, Astyoche was a Minyan princess connected to the throne of Orchomenus. Homer's Iliad tells that she bore Ascalaphus and Ialmenus in the palace of her father Actor, after Ares had slipped into her bed in secret. She was a young, blushing maiden who had gone up to the upper floor of the house when the god came to her.

Pausanias gives a prose version. In his account, the two brothers inherited the throne of the Orchomenians and were considered sons of Ares. Under their leadership, the Minyans marched against Troy.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad names her as the mother of Ascalaphus and Ialmenus and as the daughter of Actor; Pausanias records her lineage in his account of Orchomenus.

Trivia

A secret union. In the Iliad, Ares couples with Astyoche secretly inside her father's house, which explains the divine paternity of her sons.
Sons with a throne in Orchomenus. Pausanias says that Ascalaphus and Ialmenus inherited the throne of the Orchomenians, and that the Minyans set out for Troy under them.
