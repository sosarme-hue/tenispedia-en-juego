Apollo's favorite, healer of Aeneas

Iapyx

Iapyx (also Iapux or Iapis) is a hero of Greek and Roman mythology, son of Iasus and a favorite of the god Apollo. He is best known as the physician who treated Aeneas.

The myth

According to the tradition, Apollo offered him the gift of prophecy, the lyre and other arts. Iapyx chose healing instead, because he wanted to prolong his father's life. Later, during the Trojan War, he cured Aeneas after he was wounded in the fighting against Latinus.

According to Virgil, after the war he escaped to Italy and founded Apulia. His descent is unclear, however: other accounts make him a son of Daedalus, a brother of Daunius and Peucetius, or a Cretan from whom the Iapygians took their name.

Legacy

Iapyx gave his name to a north-west or west-north-west wind, which Virgil linked to the flight of Cleopatra back to Egypt after the battle of Actium. Horace asked this wind to carry his friend Virgil's ship safely to Greece. According to Strabo, the Iapygians were named after him.

Ancient sources: Virgil presents him as Aeneas's healer in the Aeneid (Book XII); Horace and Strabo also mention him.

Trivia

A wind with his name. Iapyx also names a minor north-west wind, which the ancients associated with sea voyages.
The Iapygians. According to Strabo, the people of the Iapygians took their name from Iapyx.
