protective genie of Egyptian mythology

Aha

Aha was a benevolent genie in Egyptian mythology who protected pregnant women and newborn children. His name means "fighter", and he is regarded as an ancestor of the god Bes.

The myth

Egyptian sources present him as a small, sturdy being with a round face, a mane, feline ears, long legs and a broad tail. He is shown strangling two snakes or two scorpions, or holding a gazelle, images that stress his role as a defender against danger.

During the Middle Kingdom he was the main deity of Jemenu, today Ashmunein. The Pyramid Texts call him the "Pygmy of the Dance of the God".

Worship and religion

Although he had no dedicated temples, families venerated him in the home. His image appeared on amulets and on magical ivory wands placed beside the newborn, and he was often shown alongside other protective deities such as Taweret and Heket.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts mention him with the epithet "Pygmy of the Dance of the God".

Trivia

Ivory wands. Magical ivory wands bearing his image and that of other protective deities were placed beside newborns.
Ancestor of Bes. His dwarf-like figure with feline traits is considered a precursor of the god Bes, who was very popular in Egyptian homes.
