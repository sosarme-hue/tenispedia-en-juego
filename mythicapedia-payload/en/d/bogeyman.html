folklore creature used to frighten children

Bogeyman

The bogeyman is a creature of folklore, mainly English, that adults invoke to get children to behave. Its form changes from culture to culture, and many traditions around the world have a similar monster, which makes its origin hard to trace.

The myth

It has no fixed appearance: it is usually described as a dark, formless figure able to shift its shape, sometimes with claws, sharp teeth, or animal traits such as horns or hooves. In many versions it is male, in others female, and tradition presents it as a spirit that punishes children for misbehaving.

In the most widespread telling, it lurks in bedrooms, hiding behind the door, inside the wardrobe or under the bed, waiting for the child to fall asleep. In parts of the United States, by contrast, it scratches at the windows from outside. Sometimes it takes the form of whatever frightens its victim most.

Adults use it as a warning against a specific naughtiness, such as thumb-sucking or refusing to sleep, though it can also stand for terror in general or, in some cases, the Devil.

Legacy

The word has entered the language as a synonym for irrational fear, and it is used metaphorically for a person or thing that inspires such dread. In Spanish-speaking countries it is known as el viejo del costal or el viejo del saco, the old man with the sack. In Brazil, Cuca appears in Monteiro Lobato's children's series Sítio do Picapau Amarelo. In Germany, the figure of the black man appears in the children's game Who's Afraid of the Black Man?

Trivia

Origin of coconuts. Coconuts got their name from the three indentations on the shell, which look like a hairy, dark face. Portuguese sailors linked them to the coco, a ghost of the same kind.
Protection against the Madar-i-Al. In Afghanistan, the Madar-i-Al is said to kill infants in their cribs. Burning wild rue seeds around the baby is believed to protect against her.
