supreme trinity of the Chinese Taoist pantheon

The Three Pure Ones

The Three Pure Ones (Chinese 三清, Sān Qīng) are the three highest gods of Taoism, embodiments of the Primordial Celestial Energy of the Tao and the source of all beings. They belong to Chinese tradition and are usually shown as three elderly figures representing the creation of the universe.

The myth

In Taoist mythology, creation began when the Tao manifested as One, then as Two, and finally as Three. The first, Yuanshi Tianzun, or Jade Purity, oversees the earliest phase of creation and is known as the Treasure of the Tao. Lingbao Tianzun, or Upper Purity, separated yang from yin and ordered the elements, and is therefore called the Treasure of the Law. Daode Tianzun, or Grand Purity, emerges to bring civilization and teach the Law of the Tao; he is identified with the sage Laozi in his divinized form.

The Three Pure Ones dwell in the three highest heavens, guardians of the scriptures and sacred teachings. According to tradition, those whose qi is pure and light ascend to these heavens. They are depicted as elders robed in red, blue and yellow or green, holding symbolic objects: the Pearl of Creation, a ruyi and a fan.
