Eggthér

Eggthér is a giant in Norse mythology, shown in the Völuspá sitting on a mound and joyfully playing his harp as the red rooster Fjalar crows to herald Ragnarök. He matters mainly as a figure who signals the final battle.

The myth

According to the poem, Eggthér herds a female giant who lives in the forest Járnviðr and raises the wolves descended from Fenrir. Many identify her with Angrboða. Some scholars argue he is less a shepherd than a wolf-herder, and that his cheerful harping rouses his charges to the ferocious deeds that will bring about the downfall of the gods.

His identity remains uncertain. John Lindow linked his name to Ecgþéow, the father of Beowulf in the Old English poem, but Andy Orchard regards that parallel as a likely red herring.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons is named Eggther after him.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá in the Poetic Edda, where he appears in stanza 42.

Trivia

An echo in Njáls saga. In the Icelandic saga, the hero Gunnar sings joyfully inside his own burial mound, a scene scholars compare with Eggthér's.
A related name. His name is cognate with the Old English Ecgþéow, father of Beowulf, though scholars debate whether the two figures share a mythological role.
