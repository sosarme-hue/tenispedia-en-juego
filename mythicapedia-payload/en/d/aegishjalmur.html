Norse helm of terror

Aegishjalmur

The aegishjalmur, or Helm of Awe, is an object in Norse mythology and also a modern Icelandic magical stave of protection. Medieval sources connect it with the hoard guarded by the dragon Fáfnir, and today it is a widely known magical symbol.

The myth

In the Völsunga saga and the Fáfnismál of the Poetic Edda, the helm belongs to Fáfnir, the dragon who guarded a hoard. According to the Reginsmál, every living creature feared it.

After Sigurd kills Fáfnir, he takes the helm from the hoard, and in the following stanzas of the poem he mentions it again.

Worship and religion

Viking warriors painted this protective sign between their eyes before battle, a gesture known as herkumbl, or 'war sign'. It was believed to make the bearer invincible and to frighten enemies. Today it appears in Ásatrú neopaganism, where it is tattooed as a protective charm.

Legacy

It is the official emblem on the coat of arms of the Icelandic county of Strandasýsla. Its name and protective role have carried over into modern magical staves, and the Eyrbyggja, Reykdæla, Njál and Vatnsdœla sagas also mention it under other names, such as 'semblance of terror'.

Ancient sources: The Völsunga saga, the Reginsmál and the Fáfnismál of the Poetic Edda, and the Icelandic sagas Eyrbyggja, Reykdæla, Njál and Vatnsdœla.

Trivia

A county's coat of arms. The aegishjalmur is the official symbol on the coat of arms of the Icelandic county of Strandasýsla.
A helm everyone feared. According to the Reginsmál, Fáfnir owned the helm, and every living creature was afraid of it.
