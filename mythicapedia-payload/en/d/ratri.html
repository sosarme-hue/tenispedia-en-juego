Vedic goddess of the night

Ratri

Ratri is the Vedic goddess of the night in Hinduism, the personification of the darkness that follows the day. She is the sister of Ushas, the dawn, and appears mainly in the hymns of the Rigveda.

The myth

According to the Vedic texts, Ratri is the sister of Ushas, the dawn, and the two form a complementary pair: night and dawn. The hymns of the Rigveda describe her as a beautiful maiden, although they say little about her physical appearance. Together they are invoked as powerful mothers and as sources of vital strength, which suggests that her role went beyond marking the hours of the day.

Her most notable trait is the cyclical rhythm she represents: the regular succession of night and day forms part of the cosmic order. For this reason she is linked to concepts such as Rta, the order that sustains the cosmos, and Satya, truth or steady reality, as well as to Indra in the Rigveda. The Atharvaveda associates her instead with the solar god Surya, showing that relations between deities varied from text to text.

Over time her figure remained present in the Brahmanas and the Sutra literature, where she is often mentioned, and later Tantric texts gave her an important place. The sources do not offer an extended narrative about her: her profile is built mainly from hymns and from the relations she shares with other deities.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Rigveda, which contains one hymn dedicated to her, and in the Atharvaveda, which contains five hymns dedicated to her; the Brahmanas and the Sutras also mention her.
