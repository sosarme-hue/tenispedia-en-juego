Celtic god associated with Mars

Ocelus

Ocelus is a Celtic god known from three inscriptions in Roman Britain, where he is identified with Mars. His role suggests a local deity of protection, also linked to the imperial cult.

The myth

No literary source records stories about Ocelus. He is known mainly from inscribed dedications. At Caerwent, one stone is the base of a statue of which only a pair of human feet and a pair of goose feet survive. The inscription invokes Mars Lenus or Ocelus Vellaunus and the divine spirit of the emperor, and was dedicated on 23 August AD 152.

Another Caerwent dedication is an altar to Mars Ocelus. At Carlisle he was again equated with Mars and linked to the imperial cult. For this reason he is thought to have been a British god, perhaps Silurian, whose warlike Mars side was oriented toward protection.

Worship and religion

He was venerated at Caerwent and Carlisle, where inscriptions and altars were dedicated to him using familiar votive formulas such as uotum soluit libens merito. At Caerwent he appears alongside Lenus, a Treveran healing deity, and Vellaunus, whose name has been glossed as 'chief' or 'commander'. The sources do not say whether his cult survived beyond the Roman period.

Ancient sources: He does not appear in ancient literary texts of his own tradition; he is known from three Roman inscriptions from Britain, including a dedication dated AD 152.

Trivia

Goose feet. One of the statues dedicated at Caerwent survives only as a pair of human feet and a pair of goose feet, so the full figure cannot be known for certain.
A name meaning command. The name Vellaunus has been glossed as 'chief' or 'commander', a detail often used to think about his protective role.
