commander of Ravana's army in the Ramayana

Prahasta

Prahasta is a rakshasa, a powerful warrior who served as chief commander of Ravana's army in Lanka, in the Hindu epic the Ramayana. He matters because his military role shows how serious the conflict with Rama became.

The myth

According to the tradition, Prahasta was one of the ten sons of Sumali and Ketumati, and brother of Kaikasi, Ravana's mother, which made him the king's maternal uncle. As commander-in-chief he led the wars against Yama, Kubera, the Devas, the Asuras and the Daityas, through which Ravana established his rule over the three worlds.

When Rama and his allies attacked Lanka, Prahasta led the first Lankan response and killed several of Sugriva's warriors. In one version of the Ramayana he fell to Lakshmana; in another, Nila hurled a rock that broke his neck.

Legacy

In Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas he appears as a virtuous son of Ravana and Mandodari who withdraws from the war and asks that Sita be returned to Rama, for which he is rebuked. In the Mahabharata he is reborn as Purochana, an aide to Duryodhana.

Ancient sources: Valmiki's Ramayana (book 6, canto 58), which recounts his death, and the Mahabharata, which recalls him in a later birth.

Trivia

Ravana's uncle. His sister Kaikasi was Ravana's mother, making him the king's maternal uncle.
A death by rock. One version of the Ramayana says Nila hurled a rock that broke his neck.
