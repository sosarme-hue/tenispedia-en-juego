primordial earth goddess of Mesopotamian mythology

Kishar

Kishar is a primordial goddess of Mesopotamian mythology, linked to the earth and regarded as a divine mother. Her name is usually understood as "Whole Earth", and she matters chiefly for her role in the Babylonian account of the cosmos.

The myth

In the Babylonian epic Enuma Elish, Kishar appears only in the opening lines and then vanishes from the story. According to this text, she is the daughter of Lahmu and Lahamu, the first children of Tiamat and Abzu, and she is paired with her brother Anshar, the male principle associated with the sky. Their union produces Anu, who will become the god of the heavens.

Other texts from the first millennium BCE mention her only occasionally, and in them Kishar can be equated with the goddess Antu. Some traditions make her the mother of Ki or Ninhursag, although in other accounts that goddess is the daughter of Nammu, so her genealogy varies from source to source.

Because of her partnership with Anshar, Kishar is often read as the earthly counterpart of the sky, a pair that modern scholars interpret as a picture of the ordered world. The text, however, presents her more as a name within the divine family tree than as a character with deeds of her own.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Enuma Elish, a Babylonian epic of the first millennium BCE, and in other texts of the same period where she is equated with Antu.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Kishar's name is usually translated as "Whole Earth", which reinforces her link to the material world set against the sky.
A brief appearance. In the Enuma Elish she appears only at the start of the poem and takes no further part in the story.
