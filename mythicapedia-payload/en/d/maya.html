Greek goddess, eldest of the Pleiades and mother of Hermes

Maia

Maia is one of the seven Pleiades, mountain nymphs and daughters of the Titan Atlas, and the mother of Hermes, the messenger of the gods, whom she bore to Zeus. In Greek myth she is a mountain nymph, while in Rome she gave her name to the month of May and in Hindu tradition the name Maya came to stand for illusion and creative power.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Maia was born on Mount Cyllene in Arcadia and was the youngest of the Pleiades, who were called Atlantides after their father Atlas and their mother Pleione. The Homeric Hymn to Hermes tells how Zeus secretly lay with her on a moonless night, unknown to Hera, and how Hermes was born from that union.

The infant grew with astonishing speed and crawled off to Thessaly, where by nightfall of his first day he stole some of his half-brother Apollo's cattle and invented the lyre from a tortoise shell. Maia refused to believe that Hermes was the thief, and Zeus sided with Apollo, who in the end received the lyre in exchange for the herd. In another episode, Hermes stole the clothes of the Pleiades while they bathed and left them standing naked before returning their garments. In Sophocles' lost satyr play, however, the nymph Cyllene is the one who nursed and raised Hermes.

Maia also raised Arcas, the son of Callisto and Zeus, whom Hera had turned into a bear. The story of Callisto and Arcas, together with that of the Pleiades, is told as the origin of the constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

Legacy

In Rome, Maia was a goddess of spring and growth, and the month of May is traditionally linked to her name, although ancient explanations of the name disagree. Macrobius reports that some connected it to a Maia who was the wife of Vulcan, others to the mother of Mercury, and others to the Earth or to the Bona Dea. The Pleiades, of which Maia is one, are also a star cluster visible to the naked eye.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Hermes recounts his birth and early mischief, Simonides called her "mountain Maia", and Sophocles mentioned her in his lost satyr play Ichneutae.

Trivia

Mother of a traveling god. Hermes, Maia's son, was the messenger of the gods and also a protector of travelers, merchants and thieves, traits already visible in his early mischief.
A name with several roots. The Romans linked her name to the adjective maius, meaning "greater", which fit her role as a force of growth.
