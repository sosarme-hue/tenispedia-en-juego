Japanese sea deity and protector of merchant sailors

Konpira Gongen

Konpira Gongen is a Japanese deity of the Shugendō sect, protector of merchant sailors and venerated mainly at the Kotohira shrines in Kagawa Prefecture. His figure shows how Buddhism, Shinto and mountain beliefs merged in Japan.

The myth

According to tradition, Konpira came into being when a priest at Matsuo Temple summoned Kumbhīra, the Ganges deity, a crocodile god said to have been present at the preaching of the Mahāsamaya Sutta. At first he was seen as a hostile sea monster, but he was placated and became a god of navigation.

He is identified with the first of the twelve spirit messengers emanating from Bhaisajyaguru. Other texts link him to Susanoo, the god of sea and storms, and he is said to be the divine child of Susanoo no Mikoto. One legend holds that the monk Saichō returned safely from China because he prayed to Susanoo.

Worship and religion

His main shrine was set on Mount Zōzu in Kotohira, important to Shugendō followers from the late Heian period. The Kotohira shrines, dedicated to seafarers, spread across Japan. After the Meiji separation of Shinto and Buddhism they were rededicated to Ōmononushi, though the veneration continues.

Ancient sources: The Buddhist text Sange yōryakki lists him among the twelve gods placed on Eagle Peak, and later works such as the Jindai no maki kaden kikigaki record his stories.

Trivia

Crocodile god. He is described as a crocodile god of the Ganges, an origin far removed from the Japanese coast.
Sea serpent. He may be a deification of a sea serpent called wani.
