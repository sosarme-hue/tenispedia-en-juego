disembodied voice from Japanese folklore

Uwan

The uwan is a disembodied voice from Japanese folklore, linked to old temples and abandoned houses. Its best-known trait is a piercing shout that only people inside the building can hear.

The myth

According to legends from Aomori Prefecture, anyone who enters one of these buildings may hear a shriek that pierces their ears, while those outside hear nothing. The uwan has no physical body: it is only sound, and poses no physical danger.

Some versions describe it as a black-toothed creature with threatening hands and voice, though no explanatory text confirms its identity. Another tradition says it lurks near old temples, startles travellers and takes their lives while they are distracted; if the victim repeats back everything the uwan says, it flees.

Legacy

It appears in two Edo-period picture books, Sawaki Suushi's Hyakki Zukan and Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō, which gave it a monstrous look. Modern yōkai books by Arifumi Satō and Sanpei Kasu also record its legend.

Ancient sources: Sawaki Suushi's Hyakki Zukan and Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō, both from the Edo period; no ancient texts describe it directly.

Trivia

Three fingers. In the illustrations, the uwan has only three fingers on each hand. It has been suggested that this refers to oni, which also have three fingers.
A trick to escape. According to a children's book by Arifumi Satō, repeating everything the uwan says makes it flee. The original source of this detail is unknown.
A doubtful story. The tale of Kasuke appears in an essay by Norio Yamada, but it has no primary sources and may be the author's invention.
