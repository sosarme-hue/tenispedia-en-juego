Kripa

Kripa is a warrior, sage and teacher of arms in the Mahabharata, one of the great epics of Hindu tradition. He served as councillor of the Kuru kingdom and preceptor to both the Pandava and Kaurava princes, and is counted among the chiranjivi, the immortals.

The myth

According to the Adi Parva of the Mahabharata, Kripa was born in an extraordinary way. His father was the warrior-sage Sharadvan, who had performed penance to become invincible. To distract him, Indra sent the apsara Janapadi from heaven, and when Sharadvan lost his self-control, his semen fell onto some weeds, which split in two. From them were born a boy and a girl, Kripa and Kripi.

King Shantanu of the Kurus found the twins while hunting and adopted them out of compassion, which is why they were named after pity (kripa). When Sharadvan learned of this, he revealed their identity and taught Kripa the Dhanurveda, the art of archery. Kripa became a skilled archer and trained Pandavas, Kauravas and other warriors, Karna among them.

In the Kurukshetra War he fought for the Kauravas. After Duryodhana fell, Kripa and Ashwatthama slaughtered the Pandava army at night. He was one of three survivors from the Kaurava side. Tradition holds that he later withdrew to the forest to do penance. Hindu texts maintain that he will become one of the Saptarishi, the seven sages, in the next Manvantara.

Legacy

In Hinduism, Kripa is one of the chiranjivi, immortals who will remain alive until the end of the Kali Yuga. According to some scriptures, he earned this status through his discipline, loyalty and impartial nature. The Sapta Chiranjivi Stotram, a mantra, invokes eight immortals, Kripa among them, seeking protection from illness and a long life.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata recounts him across several of its books, while the Vishnu Purana and the Agni Purana mention his lineage and future destiny.

Trivia

Name. Kripa means "pity" or "mercy" in Sanskrit, and the name refers to the compassion with which he was adopted.
Title of master. He received the title "acharya" (master) for his skill with the bow.
