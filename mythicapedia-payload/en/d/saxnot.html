national god of the Saxons

Saxnot

Seaxnēat, also spelled Saxnot, was the national god of the Saxons and, according to Anglo-Saxon sources, the founder and chief ancestor of that people. He matters because he is one of the few Germanic gods known almost only through genealogies and a baptismal vow.

The myth

The genealogies of the kings of Essex list him as the first ancestor, with the first king, Æscwine, appearing seven generations later. In that earliest form he has no divine father. A later version, preserved in a twelfth-century chronicle, makes him a son of Woden (Odin). Some sources also name him as a brother of several less familiar figures and as an ancestor of the royal house of Essex.

On the Continent his name appears in the Old Saxon Baptismal Vow, where the convert renounces Thunaer, Uuöden and Saxnote along with the demons who serve them. Jacob Grimm thought he must have ranked alongside Odin and Thor. William Chaney identified him with Tiwaz/Týr, while Rudolf Simek, following Dumézil's three-function theory, preferred Freyr.

Worship and religion

No temples or festivals dedicated to him are recorded. His religious role is known mainly from the baptismal vow, which shows that he was one of the gods that new Christians had to renounce.

Ancient sources: The royal genealogies of Essex, the twelfth-century Chronicon ex chronicis, and the Old Saxon Baptismal Vow.

Trivia

Sword companion. The name is often explained by seax, the long knife of the Saxons, and by an element meaning companion, related to German Genosse.
A name without place names. Unlike many other gods, his name has not survived in any place name.
