Hindu god of Maharashtra, a form of Vishnu and avatar of Krishna

Vithoba

Vithoba is a Hindu god worshipped mainly in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh, regarded as a manifestation of Vishnu or an avatar of Krishna. His main temple at Pandharpur is one of the most important centres of devotion in western India, and his worship remains alive among his followers.

The myth

The legends revolve around the devotee Pundalik, who is credited with bringing the deity to Pandharpur. According to the story, Vithoba also comes to the aid of the Varkari poet-saints. He is shown as a dark-complexioned young man standing on a brick with his arms akimbo, sometimes beside his consort Rakhumai.

His name has several explanations. Varkari tradition links it to the brick, so that Vitthala would mean one who stands on a brick. The saint Tukaram proposed another reading, in which the god accepts innocent people who lack knowledge. Other scholars believe the name derives from Vishnu, and that the epithet Panduranga, meaning whitish, may have come from Shiva.

Worship and religion

His main festivals fall on the ekadashi, the eleventh day of the lunar month: Shayani Ekadashi in the month of Ashadha and Prabodhini Ekadashi in Kartika. In worship, light is offered to him with aarti songs, and the Haridasa sect of Karnataka composed hymns in Kannada in his honour.

Ancient sources: The Pandurangashtakam hymn, attributed to Adi Shankara, and the abhangas of the Varkari poet-saints, composed in Marathi.

Trivia

A dark god called white. Although he is shown with dark skin, his epithet Panduranga means whitish. Scholars explain this paradox by linking it to a form of Shiva worshipped at Pandharpur.
A name with a brick. Varkari tradition explains that Vitthala means one who stands on a brick, in connection with the legend of the devotee Pundalik.
