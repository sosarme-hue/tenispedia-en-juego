Damballa

Damballa is a serpent loa of Haitian Vodou and other African diaspora religions, regarded as the sky father and primordial creator. His worship traces back to Ouidah, in present-day Benin.

The myth

In some traditions, Damballa created the cosmos with his seven thousand coils, forming the stars, planets, hills and valleys. By shedding his skin he produced all the waters, and as a serpent he links the earth with the waters below, generating life. He is wise, patient and benevolent, yet distant from human affairs. His wife is Ayida-Wedo, shown as a serpent and as a rainbow.

Worship and religion

When a devotee is possessed by Damballa, they move across the floor like a serpent, and are cooled with white sheets. Offerings include milk, white foods and rice, and the most important is a raw white egg on a mound of white flour. His sacred day is Thursday.

Legacy

His name appears in a 1964 poem by Amiri Baraka, in Exuma's song 'Dambala' later covered by Nina Simone, and in John Edgar Wideman's 1981 story collection Damballah.

Trivia

Chucky. In the Child's Play franchise, the villain Charles Lee Ray uses Damballa's name in a voodoo incantation to transfer his soul into a Good Guy doll. Damballa also appears in the third season of the Chucky series as Chucky's heavenly judge.
Possible origin of 'zombie'. The word 'zombie' may derive from 'nzambi', meaning god, in reference to Damballa.
