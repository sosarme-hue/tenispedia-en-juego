warrior prince, son of Arjuna in Indian epic

Babhruvahana

Babhruvahana is a prince in the Indian epic tradition, the son of the Pandava hero Arjuna and the princess Chitrangada. His story, especially in the Jaimini version, is one of the most popular episodes linked to the Mahabharata.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Arjuna spent a year of exile in Manalura, the kingdom of King Chitravahana. There he met the king's daughter Chitrangada, raised as a putrika, and their marriage was agreed on the condition that their children would stay in the maternal kingdom. Babhruvahana was born and inherited the throne of Manalura, and he took no part in the Kurukshetra War.

During the Ashvamedha, the horse sacrifice by which Arjuna asserted Yudhishthira's sovereignty, Babhruvahana greeted his father respectfully, but Arjuna provoked him by accusing him of neglecting his warrior duty. Ulupi, a Naga princess and Arjuna's wife, urged him to fight, and Babhruvahana struck his father down. Ulupi later explained that she had acted to free Arjuna from a curse for having killed his grandfather Bhishma. Arjuna was brought back to life with the Mritasanjivani gem.

In the twelfth-century Jaimini version the story expands: Babhruvahana invades the Naga realm with an army of mongooses, beheads Arjuna, and only Krishna manages to recover the head and restore him to life.

Legacy

The story has inspired plays and films, including Telugu versions from 1942 and 1964, the Hindi films Veer Babruvahan (1951) and Veer Arjun (1952), and a Kannada film from 1977. It was adapted into languages such as Bengali, Marathi and Odia, and the sixteenth-century Persian Razmnama included the Jaimini version.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, in the Adi Parva and Ashvamedhika Parva books, and the twelfth-century Jaiminibharata, in its Ashvamedhaparva.

Trivia

A mother who changes the story. In the Jaimini version, Chitrangada is the daughter of a Gandharva king and leaves her son to join Yudhishthira, so Ulupi raises him.
An army of mongooses. To invade the Naga kingdom in the underworld, Babhruvahana gathers an army of mongooses.
