heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of Sthenelus

Alcyone

Alcyone is a heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of King Sthenelus and sister of Eurystheus. She is best known for a brief episode linking her to Heracles, although she barely appears in ancient texts.

The myth

According to the genealogy handed down by the sources, Alcyone was a daughter of Sthenelus, son of Perseus, and of Nicippe, daughter of Pelops. Her brother Eurystheus is the well-known king who set Heracles his famous labours, which places the family among the great heroes of Argos.

The only episode the tradition records is violent. According to Diodorus, the centaur Homadus tried to assault Alcyone in Arcadia, and Heracles killed him there. The account is brief and gives no further details about the heroine's life.

The Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, might identify her with the wife of Perieres, mother of Halirrhothius, although this identification is uncertain.

Ancient sources: Diodorus of Sicily is the main source that mentions her; the Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, may refer to her.
