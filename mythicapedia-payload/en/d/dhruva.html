devoted prince of Vishnu turned Pole Star

Dhruva

Dhruva is a devoted prince of Vishnu in Hinduism who, according to tradition, became the Pole Star. Hindu children are often told his story as an example of perseverance.

The myth

According to tradition, Dhruva was the son of King Uttanapada and his wife Suniti. When he was five years old he wanted to sit on his father's lap, but his stepmother Suruchi, mother of his half-brother Uttama, mocked him and said he could only earn that place by asking Vishnu to be born from her womb.

Hurt, the boy withdrew into the forest to meditate. The sage Narada tried to dissuade him but ended up teaching him a mantra, Om namo bhagavate vasudevaya. Dhruva remained for six months without food or water, until Vishnu appeared to him. Before the god he improvised a hymn of twelve verses, the Dhruva stuti. In the Vishnu Purana, Vishnu offers him a boon and he asks only for the knowledge to compose praise; in the Bhagavata Purana he forgets his purpose and asks only to remember God forever.

Vishnu granted him the position of the Pole Star, a body that not even the pralaya, the final cataclysm, could touch. Dhruva returned to his kingdom, ruled fairly for decades and, at the end of his life, became the Pole Star.

Legacy

The word dhruva, meaning "fixed", gave rise to the term dhruva nakshatra for the Pole Star, which appears in the Mahabharata and the Surya Siddhanta. The grammarian Panini used the word for a fixed point of departure, and the idea of dhruva-pada, a firm position, remains present in Hindu spiritual thought. His story is still told to children as an example of perseverance.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda mentions dhruva as both "pole star" and "fixed"; the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana recount his story, and the Mahabharata, the Grihya Sutra and the Surya Siddhanta name him as the Pole Star.

Trivia

A war against the Yakshas. According to the Srimad Bhagavata, Dhruva declared war on the Yakshas after his half-brother Uttama was killed. When he began killing innocent Yakshas, his grandfather Manu stopped him, and Dhruva later apologised to the Yaksha king Vaishravana.
A star named in the Mahabharata. The Mahabharata explicitly names him as the Pole Star, although at the likely time of its composition Polaris was still several degrees away from the celestial pole.
