Guanche goddess of Tenerife

Chaxiraxi

Chaxiraxi is a goddess of the Guanches, the first inhabitants of Tenerife in the Canary Islands, often called the Sun Mother. She matters because her image merged with that of the Virgin of Candelaria and she remains the main deity of a modern neo-pagan religion.

The myth

Stories about Chaxiraxi are scarce, and interpretations differ. Her name, of Guanche origin, probably comes from an older Tamazight form, often rendered as something like "she who holds up the heavens". Another of her names, Achmayex Guayaxerax, is read as "behold the mother of him, the Spirit who sustains the universe".

Scholars disagree about her nature. For some she was a goddess tied to the moon and fertility; for others, to the sun. She has been compared with Juno and with the star Canopus, and it has been suggested that she may derive from the Punic-Berber goddess Tanit, though this remains a conjecture.

According to tradition, the Guanches linked her to a carved image of the Virgin found on a stone on the beaches of Güímar between 1390 and 1401. Over time her worship merged with that of the Virgin Mary through Christian missionaries, and the child the Virgin carries was identified with Guayaxerax.

Worship and religion

Before the European conquest, the veneration of Chaxiraxi was part of Guanche religion, though the sources offer few details about temples or rites. After the arrival of Europeans, her figure became part of Marian devotion. Today she is the main goddess of the Church of the Guanche People, a modern neo-pagan religion that honours her as its central deity.

Trivia

Discovery at Güímar. According to tradition, an image of the Virgin was found on a stone on the beaches of Güímar between 1390 and 1401.
Variants of the name. Her name appears in records in several forms, such as Chaciraxi, Chijoragi or Chirijoraji.
