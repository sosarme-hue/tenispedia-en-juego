ancient Egyptian sun god and patron of Thebes

Amun-Ra

Amun-Ra is the fusion of Amun, patron deity of Thebes, with Ra, the sun god, and became the chief deity of the Egyptian pantheon during the New Kingdom. His worship spread beyond Egypt, and the Greeks and Romans identified him with Zeus and Jupiter.

The myth

Amun is attested in the Pyramid Texts alongside his consort Amunet, as one of the Hermopolitan Ogdoad. His name seems to mean something like "the hidden one" or "the invisible", and the texts address him as a god whose name is unknown.

When Thebes rose to prominence, Amun became its patron, displacing Montu, and formed with Mut and the moon god Khonsu the Theban Triad. After the expulsion of the Hyksos, the pharaohs of the Eighteenth Dynasty credited their victories to Amun, which led to his merger with Ra. He also absorbed traits of fertility from Min, and through his association with the ram he was identified with the ram deity of Kush.

In the New Kingdom he was seen as a self-created creator and a champion of the poor and of travelers. During the reign of Akhenaten, the cult of the sun disk Aten challenged his worship, and after the reform his priests lost much of their power until the traditional cult was restored.

Worship and religion

His main cult center was the Karnak complex in Thebes, expanded by many pharaohs, with temples, pylons and a hypostyle hall. His oracle in the Siwa Oasis, in western Egypt, remained the only oracle of Amun throughout. Worshippers were required to confess their sins before praying to him.

Legacy

The term "Horns of Ammon" describes the small ram's horns in his depictions, and the name of ammonia salts derives from him.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts mention Amun and Amunet, and ancient Greek historians described his worship in Libya and Nubia.
