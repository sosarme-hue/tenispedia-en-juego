Norse heavenly hall after Ragnarök

Gimlé

Gimlé is a golden-roofed hall in Norse mythology where the worthy survivors of Ragnarök are foretold to live. It is described as more beautiful than the sun, and its name is often linked to a place protected from fire.

The myth

According to the myths, Gimlé lies in Asgard, the realm of the gods, and it is where righteous people go when they die. Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda places it in Víðbláinn, which he calls the third heaven, then inhabited only by light elves. The poem Völuspá, by contrast, puts the hall on a mountain called Gimlé rather than naming the hall itself. Both traditions agree that it is a place spared from final destruction.

At Ragnarök, the great battle in which most gods and beings of the world die, the fire of Surtr consumes the earth. Afterwards, two humans and some Æsir survive and repopulate a new world. Gimlé is one of the places that endure, and it is described as the last to stand, where the gods and the righteous people of all ages will live.

Modern scholars read the name in different ways. Ursula Dronke suggested it was chosen to protect the blessed from the fires of Surtr and of the Christian Hell, while Lee Hollander and Rudolf Simek have seen the influence of the Heavenly Jerusalem in its description.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and the Eddic poem Völuspá.

Trivia

A name protected from fire. Gimlé is linked to gimr, fire, and hlé, protected place. Hollander proposed another possible reading: gem-roof.
A pagan heaven with a Christian echo. Snorri presents it as a pagan heaven, but several scholars see in its description an echo of the Christian Heavenly Jerusalem.
