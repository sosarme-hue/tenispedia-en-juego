Plato (son of Lycaon)

Plato is, in Greek mythology, one of the fifty sons of the Arcadian king Lycaon, punished along with his brothers for their impiety. His story illustrates the sin of hubris and the divine punishment that fell upon the whole family.

The myth

According to the myth, Lycaon was so proud and impious that he surpassed all men in cruelty. When Zeus, wanting to check the rumours, visited him disguised as a peasant, they served him a dish prepared with human flesh. Enraged, Zeus overturned the table and struck the king and nearly all his sons with a thunderbolt.

Plato died along with his brothers. Only the youngest, Nyctimus, was saved through the intervention of the goddess Gaia, and later succeeded his father on the throne. Accounts differ about his mother: the naiad Cyllene, Nonacris, or an unknown woman.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Plato comes from the Greek Plátōn, meaning «broad-shouldered». The same name was later borne by the famous philosopher, although the mythological figure has no connection with him.
A survivor. Among Lycaon's sons, only the youngest, Nyctimus, escaped Zeus's thunderbolt thanks to the goddess Gaia, and ended up ruling in his father's place.
