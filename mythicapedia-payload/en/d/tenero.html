Theban hero, son and prophet of Apollo

Tenerus

Tenerus is a Theban hero of Greek myth, son of Apollo and the nymph Melia, and a priest and prophet of the god. His story links the city of Thebes with Apollo's oracular worship.

The myth

According to Pausanias, Apollo abducted Melia, a daughter of the Titan Oceanus, and she gave birth to Tenerus and his brother Ismenus, who gave his name to the river of Thebes.

The tradition holds that Apollo gave Tenerus the art of divination, and that he became a priest and prophet of the god. Pindar calls him the seer who tends the temple and says Apollo entrusted Thebes to him for his wise courage, while Poseidon honored him above all mortals. The Teneric plain, northwest of Thebes, was named after him.

Worship and religion

Tenerus had an oracle at the Ismenion, the Temple of Apollo in Thebes. A fragmentary Pindaric paean, perhaps addressed to him, mentions bulls and singing before the altar. Lycophron refers to Thebes, or perhaps Boeotia, as the land and temples of Teneros. He may also have been connected with the oracular sanctuary of Ptoion at the foot of Mount Ptoion.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Pindar (5th century BC) and Lycophron, who mention him in their works on Thebes and Boeotia.

Trivia

A plain named after him. The Teneric plain, northwest of Thebes, was named after him, according to Pausanias.
Praised by Poseidon. Pindar says Poseidon honored him above all mortals.
