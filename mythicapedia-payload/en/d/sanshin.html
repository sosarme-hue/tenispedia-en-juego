mountain deity of Korean shamanism

Sanshin

Sanshin (also Sansin) is a mountain deity in Korean shamanism, regarded as the highest-ranking of the spirits. Its worship was central in Korea, a peninsula dominated by mountains.

The myth

According to Korean tradition, Sanshin is the local god of each mountain. In the myth of Dangun, founder of Gojoseon, he is said to have turned into Sanshin after abdicating the throne.

One tale says that eight Sanshin appeared at Poseokjeong, where the king and nobles of Silla held great ceremonies as the kingdom was falling.

Worship and religion

For centuries it was an important object of worship, and the Joseon government held rituals in its honour. With the arrival of Buddhism it was absorbed into temples, where each one has a shrine called sanshingak. In villages, the sanshinje, a ceremony for the local Sansin who protects the community, is still held at a mountain altar.

Legacy

He is usually shown as an old man accompanied by tigers, and his paintings still appear in mountain temples. In the Joseon period, Grandmother Samshin protected mothers and babies.
