Hreidmar

Hreidmar is a dwarf in Norse mythology, father of Fafnir, Regin and Ótr, and owner of a hoard of gold whose curse set off a long chain of deaths. His story appears in the Völsunga saga and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and it is a key piece of the cycle of Andvari's treasure.

The myth

According to the story, Hreidmar lived in a house of glittering gold and gems built by his son Regin and guarded by Fafnir. He had five children: Regin, Fafnir, Ótr, Lyngheiðr and Lofnheiðr. Ótr was accidentally killed by Loki, and as compensation the Æsir gave Hreidmar Andvari's gold and the ring Andvaranaut, a magical object that could create gold.

However, Andvari had cursed the ring to bring misfortune and destruction to whoever possessed it. Hreidmar ignored Loki's warnings and kept the ring, but he was killed by Fafnir and Regin for its sake.

Fafnir wanted the ring for himself, turned into a dragon and drove Regin away. He guarded the treasure until Sigurd, at Regin's urging, arrived and struck him a fatal blow. Regin then tried to murder Sigurd to get the ring, but Sigurd killed him too. In the end, all of Hreidmar's family was dead.

Ancient sources: The Völsunga saga and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A cursed ring. The ring Andvaranaut passed from hand to hand, leaving death in its wake. According to the story, Andvari cursed it before handing it over.
A destroyed family. By the end of the story, all of Hreidmar's children are dead, some at each other's hands and others over the treasure their father accepted.
