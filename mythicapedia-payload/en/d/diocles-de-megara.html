Megarian hero honoured in Megara

Diocles of Megara

Diocles is a hero of Megarian tradition, an Athenian warrior who died defending his beloved and was honoured as a hero in Megara. His figure is linked to one of the most unusual festivals of the Greek world.

The myth

According to the story, Diocles was exiled from Athens for a reason that is unknown, and he took refuge in Megara. There he met a young man and fell in love with him. During a battle he died protecting his beloved with his shield. Moved by his sacrifice, the Megarians buried him with heroic honours.

According to Megarian belief, Diocles had also been a ruler of Eleusis.

Worship and religion

Every spring the Diocleia were held at his tomb. Boys and young men competed in naked gymnastic contests and other trials, including a kissing contest: a chosen referee prayed to the hero Ganymede, beloved of Zeus, and the sweetest kiss won a garland of flowers.

Legacy

In Aristophanes' Acharnians (425 BCE), a character identified as a Megarian swears 'By Diocles!', a sign of the great honour in which he was held.

Ancient sources: Aristophanes' Acharnians (425 BCE) is the ancient reference that mentions him.

Trivia

A kissing contest. Among the trials of the Diocleia was a contest that rewarded the sweetest kiss. The referee had to pray that his lips could tell the best one apart.
A comic oath. In Aristophanes' comedy, a Megarian swears by Diocles, showing that his name was known even beyond the city.
