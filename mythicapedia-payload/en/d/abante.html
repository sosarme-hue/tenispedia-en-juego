legendary king of Euboea, son of Poseidon

Abas

Abas is a legendary king of Euboea in Greek tradition, son of the god Poseidon and Arethusa. His family line and his accidental death at the hands of his grandson make him a minor but memorable figure in the myth.

The myth

According to tradition, Abas ruled the Abantes of Euboea. His mother was Arethusa, whom some authors describe as a Nereid and others as the daughter of Hyperenor. He married Melanippe and had two sons, Canethus and Chalcodon, the latter succeeding him on the throne. Canethus fathered a grandson, Canthus, who was killed by the Libyan Cafaurus when he tried to steal his flocks.

Chalcodon, married to Alcyone, had two grandchildren, Chalciope and Elephenor. Abas died by accident at Elephenor's hand. Seeing a servant mistreat his grandfather, Elephenor raised his staff to punish him, slipped, and struck the old king fatally. Afterwards he had to leave the island of Euboea.

Trivia

An easily confused name. Melanippe, Abas's wife, is a little-known figure and should not be confused with other women of the same name in Greek myth, such as Poseidon's lover.
A death by accident. Abas, who was trying to protect his grandfather, died unintentionally. His killer was his own grandson, who meant only to punish a servant.
