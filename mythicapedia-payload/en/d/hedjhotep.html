minor Egyptian god of fabrics and clothing

Hedjhotep

Hedjhotep was a minor Egyptian deity, lord of fabrics and clothes and, to a lesser extent, of weaving and the dead. Although he is sometimes depicted as a goddess, his main role was to provide garments for kings, gods and the deceased, which linked him to resurrection.

The myth

The funerary texts of the Middle Kingdom, such as the Coffin Texts, mention him in several spells. In the New Kingdom his character became ambiguous: he was given care over medicines and amulets, yet a papyrus of the same period portrays him as a god who committed an offence against Montu, perhaps running away with one of his divine wives or forcing a sexual relation on him, much like the events in The Contendings of Horus and Seth.

In later periods Hedjhotep is often linked with the weaving goddess Tayt and with Renenutet. During the Ptolemaic and Roman periods he replaces Horus as the son of Isis in scenes of cloth offerings. In the final period he was identified with the god Shu and made a son of Ra, the first to clothe the naked.

Worship and religion

A possible cult centre lay east of the Faiyum, at El Lahun, and at Harageh archaeologists found the only known stele dedicated explicitly to him, from the tomb of an official named Nebipu. In the region of Heracleopolis Magna he had a growing cult and dedicated priesthood during the Middle Kingdom, but later he was honoured only sporadically, appearing on sarcophagi and royal rituals.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Middle Kingdom Coffin Texts and in New Kingdom papyri recounting his offence against Montu.
