sun goddess of Japanese Shinto

Amaterasu

Amaterasu is the sun goddess of Japanese mythology and one of the chief deities (kami) of Shinto. According to the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, she rules the heavenly realm of Takamagahara and is the mythical ancestress of Japan's imperial family.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Amaterasu was born from the left eye of Izanagi as he purified himself after his failed attempt to rescue his wife Izanami from Yomi, the land of the dead. She and her brothers Tsukuyomi, the moon deity, and Susanoo, the impetuous storm god, form the group known as the "Three Precious Children." Izanagi assigned her the rule of Takamagahara, the Plain of High Heaven.

The best-known episode tells how Susanoo, after wrecking his sister's rice fields and defiling her palace, caused Amaterasu to shut herself in the Heavenly Rock Cave and seal it with a boulder. The world fell into darkness. The kami gathered outside, and following the plan of the god Omoikane, had Ama no Uzume dance on an overturned tub amid laughter and noise. Curious, Amaterasu peeked out and saw her own reflection in a mirror. Startled, the kami seized the moment to draw her out and restore light to the world.

Later, Amaterasu sent her grandson Ninigi to pacify Japan, giving him the sword Kusanagi, the Yata no Kagami mirror and the Yasakani no magatama jewels. Her great-grandson Jimmu was, according to tradition, the first emperor.

Worship and religion

Her chief shrine is the Grand Shrine of Ise in Mie Prefecture, one of Shinto's holiest sites and a major pilgrimage destination. She is also enshrined in many shrines across Japan. Her sacred mirror, the Yata no Kagami, is believed to be kept at the Naiku shrine in Ise as one of the imperial regalia. Her worship remains alive among Shinto practitioners today.

Legacy

Her link to the imperial family and to Japanese nationalism was strengthened during the Meiji Restoration of 1868.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (c. 712 CE) and the Nihon Shoki (720 CE) describe her as ruler of Takamagahara and ancestress of the emperors.

Trivia

A transparent name. Her epithet Amaterasu Ōmikami literally means "The Great August Goddess Who Shines in Heaven," with none of the etymological opacity of many names in the ancient texts.
No iconography of her own. Amaterasu has no iconography of her own. She is linked mainly to the sacred mirror from the cave myth.
