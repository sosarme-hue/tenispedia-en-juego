Aztec creator and fertility god

Tōnacātēcuhtli

Tōnacātēcuhtli is the Mexica creator and fertility god, an aged deity who dwelt in the highest heaven. His name is usually rendered as "Lord of Our Sustenance", and he is the Central Mexican form of the aged creator god found across Mesoamerican religion.

The myth

According to the stories, Tōnacātēcuhtli lived with his consort Tōnacācihuātl, "the lady of sustenance", in Omeyocan, the thirteenth and highest heaven, from which human souls descended to earth. Tradition credits him with dividing the world into land and ocean at the time of creation.

With Xiuhtecuhtli, he called on Nanahuatzin, a sick and deformed god, to sacrifice himself by throwing himself into a great fire and become the sun. When he reached the sky, the divine couple placed him on a throne of heron feathers.

Some colonial manuscripts equate him with Ōmetēcuhtli, the creator of life alongside Ōmecihuātl, and he is sometimes confused with the goddesses Citlalicue or Xochiquétzal. His children are commonly named as Quetzalcoatl, Huitzilopochtli, Xipe Totec and Tezcatlipoca, though accounts of his genealogy vary between authors.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples, festivals or offerings dedicated specifically to this god. They do record a link to everyday life: after bathing newborns, Aztec midwives told them they had been created in the place of duality, above the nine heavens, by their parents Ōmetēuctli and Ōmecihuātl. In the calendar he was the patron of Cipactli, the first day of the month, and presided over the trecena 1 Cipactli, the first of the thirteen-day ritual weeks. In pre-Columbian art he appears near copulating humans, alluding to procreation.

Ancient sources: The Codex Ríos, the Histoyre du Mechique, the Florentine Codex of Sahagún and the Codex Chimalpopoca present him as creator; the Codex Borgia depicts him.

Trivia

A name with several readings. The name is usually translated as "Lord of Our Sustenance", but some read its root as "human flesh" or "food", and others as "abundance".
A god linked to procreation. In pre-Columbian art he appears beside human couples in scenes of procreation, and midwives told newborns that his parents had formed them in the heavens.
