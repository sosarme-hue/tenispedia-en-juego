sea maiden of Māori mythology, linked to Napier

Pania

Pania, often called "Pania of the Reef", is a figure of Māori mythology from New Zealand and a symbol of the city of Napier. Her legend, tied to the east coast of the North Island, explains the origin of the taniwha who guards those waters.

The myth

According to the tradition, Pania was a maiden who swam by day with the creatures of the reef and at nightfall followed a stream to the bay where Napier now stands. The handsome Karitoki, son of a Māori chief, drank there every evening until she whispered a spell that the wind carried to him. They fell in love and married in secret.

At sunrise Pania had to return to the sea, called by the sirens, and promised to come back each night. Karitoki, doubted by his friends, sought advice from an elder, who warned him that a sea creature could not return to the ocean if she had eaten cooked food. While she slept, he placed a morsel in her mouth, but the warning call of Ruru, the morepork owl, woke her. She fled to the sea and never saw him again.

Her son Moremore now protects those waters as a taniwha, sometimes disguised as a shark, a stingray or an octopus.

Legacy

A statue of Pania on Marine Parade in Napier has made her a symbol of the city.

Trivia

Statue on the seafront. A statue of Pania stands on Marine Parade in Napier, and the city has adopted her as an emblem.
Ruru's warning. In the story, it is the morepork owl Ruru who gives the alarm call that wakes Pania and ruins Karitoki's plan.
