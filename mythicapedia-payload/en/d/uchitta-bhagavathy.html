regional Hindu goddess of North Malabar

Uchitta Bhagavathy

Uchitta Bhagavathy is a regional Hindu goddess worshipped mainly in North Malabar, in the Indian state of Kerala. Her cult takes shape through the theyyam, a ritual dance in which the deity is embodied by a performer.

The myth

Accounts of her origin vary, and each theyyam has its own legends. According to one version, she was about to be killed by Kamsa in place of Krishna, but when he tried, she cried out that his future killer had been born on earth.

Another tradition makes her a daughter of Agni, the fire god. An ember that fell from his body landed on the lotus where Brahma sits, and from it emerged a radiant goddess, whom Brahma offered to Shiva and who came down to earth at the request of the goddess Bhumi. Because she is Agni's daughter, in the theyyam she sits on the flames and plays with the embers.

Worship and religion

Women are especially devoted to her and pray to her for a smooth delivery. Her shrines are spread across the Kannur and Kasaragod districts, and the theyyam is performed mainly by members of the Malaya community, with the Velar community also taking part. She is also honoured in the Perumkaliyatta Mahotsavam, the major theyyam festival held at many temples in North Malabar, and the rite remains alive today.

Legacy

Her most vibrant legacy is the theyyam itself, which continues to be performed at temples and festivals in Kerala.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Her name is said to come from her loud way of speaking, expressed in Malayalam as uchathil.
Other names. She is also known as Adiyeri madathil Uchitta Bhagavathy, after the Adiyeri matha, and as Vadakkinakathachi.
