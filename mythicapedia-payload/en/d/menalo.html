Arcadian hero of Greek mythology, son of Lycaon

Maenalus

Maenalus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of King Lycaon of Arcadia. His story, tied to the punishment Zeus imposed on the Lycaonids, explains how a city and a mountain in that region got their name.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Maenalus was known for his pride and impiety, like most of the Lycaonids, the sons of Lycaon. After Zeus turned their father into a wolf for offering human sacrifices, the brothers divided the kingdom of Arcadia between them.

Reports of their wrongdoing reached Zeus, who came down to earth disguised as an old beggar or a farmhand. In the Lycaonids' house, several signs revealed the guest's divinity, so Maenalus proposed killing a child, or according to other versions his brother Nyctimus, and serving him to the visitor as a test. Zeus saw through the crime, hurled the meal aside, set the palace on fire and struck Maenalus and his brothers with his thunderbolt, or according to other versions turned them into wolves. He then brought Nyctimus back to life and handed him his father's throne.

Legacy

Maenalus's name stayed attached to the region: it gave its name to a city and to a mountain in Arcadia, Mount Maenalus.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Library, records his story alongside that of the Lycaonids.

Trivia

Nyctimus, the brother saved. According to some versions, the child Maenalus proposed sacrificing was his brother Nyctimus. Zeus brought him back to life and gave him the throne of Arcadia.
Two possible endings. The sources give two outcomes for Maenalus's brothers: Zeus's thunderbolt or their transformation into wolves.
