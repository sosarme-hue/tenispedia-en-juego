ancient king of Laconia in Greek mythology

Myles

Myles is an ancient king of Laconia in Greek mythology and the father of Eurotas, the figure whose line is linked by tradition to the naming of the city of Sparta. He appears in only a few accounts, but he serves as a link between the genealogy of the Laconian kings and the origins of the city, which makes him a useful figure for understanding how the past of Sparta was told.

The myth

According to the tradition, Myles was the son of King Lelex and the brother of Polycaon. After his father's death he ruled Laconia, and when he died his son Eurotas succeeded him. Among the traits attributed to him is one quite different from the usual heroic deeds: according to the account, he was the first mortal to invent the mill and grind corn at Alesiae.

The sources do not agree on his wife. According to one version, Eurotas's mother was called Peridia and had among her brothers Polyclon, Bomolochus and Therapne. Another tradition calls her Teledice or Cleocharia and gives the couple two children, Eurotas and Kepedia or Pelias. In every variant, Eurotas is Myles's heir and the father of the figure from whom the city took its name.

The myth therefore places Myles between the generation of Lelex and that of Eurotas, with few episodes of his own. His story works more as a piece of genealogy than as a tale of adventure.

Trivia

An inventor of mills. According to tradition, Myles was the first mortal to invent the mill and grind corn, at the place called Alesiae.
Two mothers for one king. The ancient versions give different names to Myles's wife, Peridia in one and Teledice or Cleocharia in another, showing that the genealogy was not fixed.
