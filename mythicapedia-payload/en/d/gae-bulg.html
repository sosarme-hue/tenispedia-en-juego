Cú Chulainn's spear in Irish mythology

Gáe Bulg

The spear of Cú Chulainn, hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. Its name, Gáe Bulg, is usually rendered as "spear of mortal pain", and it is one of the most feared weapons in Celtic tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition, the spear was made from the bone of a sea monster, the Curruid, which had died fighting another sea monster, the Coinchenn. Cú Chulainn received it from Scáthach, the warrior woman who trained him in Alba alongside his foster-brother Ferdiad, though she taught the Gáe Bulg technique only to him.

In the Táin Bó Cuailnge, the two heroes meet in single combat at a ford. When Ferdiad gets the upper hand, Láeg, Cú Chulainn's charioteer, sends the spear floating downstream to his master, who hurls it into Ferdiad and kills him. Other versions describe a spear with seven heads, each bearing seven barbs, and the Book of Leinster claims it could only be used under very specific ritual conditions. Cú Chulainn also used it against his own son, Connla. Once thrown, it was invariably fatal.

Ancient sources: The Táin Bó Cuailnge and the Book of Leinster, which describes its ritual conditions of use.

Trivia

A disputed name. Traditionally the name is translated as "belly spear", but Joseph Loth and Kuno Meyer linked it to Old Irish bolc, "notch", which would connect it with Fergus's sword Caladbolg and, in their view, with King Arthur's Caledfwlch.
A redundant compound. According to linguist Eric Hamp, once Irish speakers no longer recognized the second element, gaisos "spear", the word gáe was added at the beginning to clarify it, forming a tautological compound.
