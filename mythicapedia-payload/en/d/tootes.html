Achaean herald in the Iliad

Thootes

Thootes (Ancient Greek Θοώτης) is the Achaean herald of the Athenian leader Menestheus during the Trojan War, as told in Homer's Iliad. His role is minor, but he serves as the messenger who brings help at a moment of crisis for the Greeks.

The myth

In the Iliad, Book XII, when the Trojans and their allies press against the Achaean wall near the ships, Menestheus sends Thootes to fetch help. The herald must call the two Aiantes, Ajax son of Telamon and Ajax son of Oileus, and ask them to reinforce the front, since the Lycian leaders are attacking fiercely.

Thootes runs along the wall and finds the Aiantes. He delivers Menestheus's message in the sender's own words, asking that Ajax son of Telamon come alone and that Teucer, skilled with the bow, follow him. The poem stresses that the herald carries out his errand without delay, and that great Telamonian Ajax obeys.

Legacy

The Trojan asteroid 5041 Theotes is named after Thootes. The spelling with an 'e' comes from a spelling error in a German translation of the Iliad.

Ancient sources: Appears in Homer's Iliad, Book XII.

Trivia

A translation error. The Trojan asteroid 5041 is named after the herald, but with an extra 'e' caused by an error in a German translation of the Iliad.
