Greek goddess of love and beauty

Aphrodite

Aphrodite is the Greek goddess of love, beauty and desire, one of the Twelve Olympian gods. Poets regarded her as the most beautiful of the goddesses, and her cult, with roots in the Near East, spread across the Greek world and later the Roman one, where she was identified with Venus.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Aphrodite was born from the sea foam formed by the severed genitals of Uranus, the ancient sky god. Wherever she stepped, grass and flowers sprang up. Homer, by contrast, makes her the daughter of Zeus and Dione, showing that ancient accounts did not agree on her parentage.

The tradition has her married to Hephaestus, the smith god, yet she was unfaithful with Ares, god of war, and her husband caught them in a metal trap. In the Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite, Zeus makes her fall in love with the mortal shepherd Anchises, with whom she has Aeneas. Aphrodite was also the lover and surrogate mother of Adonis, a youth killed by a wild boar. Her children include Eros, Phobos, Deimos, Hymenaios and Priapus, among others.

Her most famous possession was the embroidered girdle, an amulet that made its wearer irresistible. Aphrodite was also one of the three goddesses whose rivalry with Athena and Hera set off the judgment of Paris and the Trojan War.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Paphos, on the southwest coast of Cyprus, and Cythera, Corinth and Athens were also important. In Athens and elsewhere the Aphrodisia festival was celebrated in her honour. Modern scholars doubt that sacred prostitution took place at her Corinthian temple, though ancient writers mention it.

In today's Dodekatheism, Aphrodite is still one of the deities worshipped, with offerings of pomegranates, apples and limes. A popular custom on Cyprus involves swimming around the Rock of Aphrodite, between Limassol and Paphos, to gain eternal beauty or good luck in love.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the words "aphrodisiac" and "hermaphrodite". In Western art she is the standard image of female beauty, usually shown nude and accompanied by Eros, and she inspired many works of literature and painting. Her emblems include the dove, myrtle, apple, scallop shell and rose.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony describes her as born from sea foam, while the Homeric Hymns present her as a goddess of universal power who makes every living species conceive.
