queen of Argos, wife of Acrisius

Eurydice of Argos

Eurydice is, in Greek mythology, the queen of Argos married to Acrisius and mother of Danaë, grandmother of the hero Perseus. She matters for her place in the Argive line that leads to Perseus, especially in Euripides' fragmentary tragedy Danaë.

The myth

According to tradition, Eurydice was the daughter of Lacedaemon and Sparta, the legendary founders of the city that would bear her mother's name. Through this descent she belonged to the Lacedaemonian line and was the sister of Amyclas. She married Acrisius, king of Argos, and became the mother of Danaë. Some late sources call her Aganippe, and she may also have had another daughter, Evarete, who married Oenomaus, king of Pisa in Elis.

In Euripides' fragmentary tragedy Danaë, Eurydice has her largest role. Acrisius, frightened by a prophecy that a son of his daughter would kill him, locked Danaë in a chamber. Zeus visited her in the form of gold and made her pregnant. An ancient summary published in 2016 suggests that Eurydice discovered what had happened, faked a pregnancy and, when the child was born, presented Perseus as the son of her and Acrisius. Her motive is unclear: it may have been love for her daughter or a wish to avoid the scandal of an unmarried girl with a child outside marriage.

When Acrisius rejoiced at finally having a male heir, Eurydice asked him to free Danaë, and he agreed. However, the servant who led Danaë out of the chamber found signs of a birth and told the king. Acrisius shut mother and son in a chest and threw it into the sea, as in the other versions of the tale. It is not known whether he punished Eurydice; perhaps he felt the fate of Danaë and Perseus was punishment enough.

Ancient sources: Euripides' Danaë, a tragedy surviving only in fragments, is her largest appearance; some late sources call her Aganippe.

Trivia

The meaning of her name. Eurydice comes from Greek eurys, "wide", and dike, "justice", so her name means "wide justice".
A summary published in 2016. An ancient summary of Euripides' Danaë was published for the first time in 2016 and supported the theory that Eurydice faked her daughter's pregnancy.
