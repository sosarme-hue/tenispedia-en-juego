Carian hero of Greek mythology, founder of Alabanda

Alabandus

Alabandus is a hero of Greek mythology from Caria, in Asia Minor, credited with founding the town of Alabanda. After his death, its inhabitants worshipped him as a divinity, and his figure became a source of local pride.

The myth

According to tradition, Alabandus was the son of Euippus and Callirhoe, although the sources disagree: some versions give his mother's name as Car, or Cares. His original name is unknown.

The legend tells that, after winning a prize in a horse race, the people named him Alabandus, because in the Carian language ala meant "horse" and banda "victory". For this reason he was sometimes also called Hipponicus. He later founded the town that bears his name, and tradition presents him as the great founder of his people.

Worship and religion

After his death, the inhabitants of Alabanda worshipped him as a divinity. The sources offer no information on whether this cult continued in later periods.

Ancient sources: Cicero mentions him and recounts an anecdote about the Athenian musician Stratonicus, who grew tired of the constant praise the Carians gave to their founder.

Trivia

A name won on the track. According to the legend, the name Alabandus came from a victory in a horse race, combining the Carian words for "horse" and "victory".
A musician tired of praise. Cicero tells how the musician Stratonicus, weary of hearing Alabandus praised, said he would make Alabandus his enemy but Hercules his own.
