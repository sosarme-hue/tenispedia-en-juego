Hindu deity of the descending lunar node

Ketu

Ketu is, in Hindu astrology, the descending lunar node, the point where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic heading south. Personified as a deity, he forms one half of an immortal demon together with Rahu, and is seen as a force that brings detachment, losses and, at the same time, a path toward spiritual liberation.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Ketu and Rahu are the two halves of Svarbhanu, an immortal asura who had disguised himself as a god to drink the amrita. Vishnu discovered him and beheaded him, but because he had already swallowed the nectar, both head and body kept living. The head became Rahu and the torso became Ketu.

Astrological texts explain that the two are always 180 degrees apart and complete their cycle in about 18.6 years. They are blamed for eclipses, since their passage over the Sun or the Moon gave rise to the story that they swallow them.

Worship and religion

Ketu is revered mainly within Vedic astrology, where mantras of Ganesha and Matsya are recommended to calm his effects. His role is more astrological than devotional, and the sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to him.

Legacy

Ketu is one of the nine grahas of Hindu astrology and gives his name to an astronomical node. His headless figure and his association with renunciation and enlightenment have made him present in spiritual reflection and in popular astrology.

Ancient sources: Hindu astrological texts such as the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra list him as a graha; the story of his origin appears in the Puranic tradition.
