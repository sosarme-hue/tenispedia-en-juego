sacred cave in Japanese mythology

Ama-no-Iwato

Ama-no-Iwato, the "Heavenly Rock Cave", is where, in Japanese mythology, the sun goddess Amaterasu shut herself away after a quarrel with her brother Susano'o. Her seclusion plunged the world into darkness, and her coaxed return is one of the best-known stories of Shinto.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Susano'o, god of the sea and storms, repeatedly wronged his sister Amaterasu, until he wrecked her sacred weaving hall. Angered, the goddess hid in the Ama-no-Iwato cave and sealed its entrance, leaving the world in darkness.

The other gods gathered outside. After failed attempts, such as trying to make roosters crow, they followed a plan devised by Omoikane: Ame-no-Uzume danced at the entrance until the crowd burst into laughter. Curious, Amaterasu opened the door slightly and glimpsed her reflection in the mirror Yata no Kagami, which held her attention long enough for Ame-no-Tajikarao to pull her out. A sacred rope, the shimenawa, was then stretched across the entrance to stop her hiding again.

The Nihonshoki tells a similar story, with differences: the flayed horse falls onto the goddess herself, wounding her, and one of her daughters dies in the attack.

Worship and religion

A shrine at Takachiho, in Miyazaki Prefecture, claims to be the site of the event. According to the Nihonshoki, the imperial court chose this area for the goddess's official shrine, which was rebuilt in 720 and again in 1707. Other places, such as Kyoto and Tokushima, also keep their own local traditions about the cave.

Legacy

The cave story inspires festivals and purification rites held at the Higashihongu and Nishihongu halls, which face each other across the Iwato River gorge.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Japanese chronicles from the 8th century, recount Amaterasu's seclusion in this cave.

Trivia

Sacred trees. The grounds hold rare ancient ginkgo and Magnolia compressa trees, considered sacred in Japan since ancient times.
View from the shrine. After a Shinto purification ritual, visitors at Nishihongu can see the cave on the other side of the Iwato River.
