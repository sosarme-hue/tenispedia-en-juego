figure of Greek myth, father of Eurydice

Adrastus

Adrastus is a minor figure in Greek mythology, known mainly as the father of Eurydice and the father-in-law of King Ilus. He is named in only one ancient source, so his story is brief.

The myth

The best-known account places him in the founding of Ilion. According to Apollodorus, Ilus asked Zeus for a sign to show where he should build his city, and the next day he found the Palladium, a sacred image that had fallen from the sky, in front of his tent. Ilus then married Eurydice, daughter of Adrastus, and fathered Laomedon.

Adrastus may also have been the grandfather of Themiste, mother of Anchises, although the text does not specify whether Eurydice was her mother. The Hellespontine city of Adrasteia may also have been named in his honour.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Library, is the only ancient source that names him as Ilus's father-in-law.
