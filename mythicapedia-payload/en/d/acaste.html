Oceanid of Greek mythology

Acaste

Acaste is one of the Oceanids of Greek mythology, the nymph daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. Her name appears in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter, which places her among the companions of Persephone when Hades abducted her.

The myth

According to tradition, the Oceanids were some three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, water deities of the rivers and seas that encircled the earth. Acaste belongs to this vast group and rarely appears with distinct traits in the ancient texts.

Her most notable moment comes in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter. There she is gathering flowers alongside Persephone and other Oceanids in a quiet meadow when Hades bursts out of the earth and carries the young goddess off to the underworld. The story shows her as part of the retinue accompanying Demeter's daughter in the episode that gives rise to the cycle of the seasons.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Demeter names her among Persephone's companions at the moment of the abduction.

Trivia

Three thousand sisters. The Oceanids were said to be some three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, which makes Acaste one of a vast family of nymphs.
Persephone's companion. The Homeric Hymn to Demeter names Acaste among the young women gathering flowers with Persephone before the abduction.
