summer spirit of Orcadian folklore

Sea Mither

Sea Mither, or Mither of the Sea, is a spirit of Orcadian folklore from the north of Scotland who rules the sea during summer. Her eternal rival, Teran, holds sway in winter, and their struggle is among the oldest legends of the islands.

The myth

According to tradition, Sea Mither lives in the sea during the summer days. She keeps the demonic nuckelavee confined to the ocean depths, empowers sea creatures to reproduce, calms the waters and gives the summer breeze a soft, song-like quality.

Each spring she battles Teran, the winter spirit capable of unleashing storms, until she drives him down to the bottom of the ocean. But the effort wears her out. In autumn Teran takes advantage of her weakness, seizes control of the seas again and brings freezing weather and tempests, and Sea Mither has to withdraw. Fishermen took comfort in the belief that she would return refreshed in spring.

Worship and religion

No formal temples or official rites are documented. In Shetland, fishermen in particular sought her protection from the Devil.

Trivia

A name with character. In local Orkney dialect, Teran means "furious anger". It may derive from the Norse word for "angry".
Two battles with their own names. The spring struggle is called vore tullye or "spring struggle", and the autumn one gore vellye.
