Oceanid nymph of Greek mythology

Theia

Theia is an Oceanid nymph of Greek mythology, one of the three thousand daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, and she is best known as the mother of the Cercopes. She should not be confused with the Titaness Theia, sister of Oceanus and Tethys and mother of Helios, Selene and Eos.

The myth

According to the myth, Theia bore the Cercopes to her own father, Oceanus. The two sons were mischievous, impish thieves. Their mother warned them not to provoke the brave Heracles, but when they stole from the hero, he seized and bound them and was about to kill them.

Theia begged him to let her sons go. In some versions they were transformed into monkeys, in others into stone, and in some accounts the punishment came for trying to deceive Zeus.

Trivia

Other names. Her name is also rendered as Thea or Thia, and she is sometimes called Memnonis.
Two different Theias. The Oceanid nymph is not the Titaness Theia, mother of Helios, Selene and Eos, although both share the name.
