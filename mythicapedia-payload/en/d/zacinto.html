eponymous hero of the island of Zacynthus

Zacynthus

In Greek myth, Zacynthus is the hero who gave his name to the island of Zacynthus, also known as Zante. He matters because he links the island's origins to the lineage of Dardanus, the ancestor of Aeneas.

The myth

According to tradition, Zacynthus was the son of Dardanus and Bateia, and the brother of Erichthonius. Dionysius of Halicarnassus presents him as the settler of the island, reached after the Trojan War by the Iliotes, who were kindly received by the Zacynthians thanks to their kinship.

Clement of Alexandria records that Apollo loved him. Pausanias gives an Arcadian version: the first man to cross to the island by ship was Zacynthus of Psophis, and the island's acropolis was therefore also called Psophis.

Legacy

The island's name, and with it its hero's, survives today in the form Zakynthos, and the figure appears on the island's flag.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Clement of Alexandria and Pausanias.
