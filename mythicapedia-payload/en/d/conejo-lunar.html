Moon rabbit

The Moon rabbit is a creature of East Asian folklore that lives on the Moon, where, according to tradition, it pounds herbs or the elixir of immortality. Its shape is seen in the dark markings on the Moon's near side, which makes it one of the most beloved figures in Asian popular imagination.

The myth

According to tradition, the dark markings on the Moon form the figure of a rabbit or hare pounding with a mortar and pestle. The earliest Chinese source is the Chu Ci, an anthology of poems from the Western Han dynasty, which describes a hare that constantly pounds herbs for the immortals. Since rabbits had not yet reached China, the original figure was a hare, and Han poets called it the "Jade Hare" or "Gold Hare". In the most widespread Chinese version, the rabbit Yutu keeps company with the goddess Chang'e and prepares the elixir of life; in Japan and Korea it pounds rice for mochi or tteok.

In a Buddhist Jataka tale, a monkey, an otter, a jackal and a rabbit resolve to practise charity on the full moon. When an old man asks them for food, the rabbit, who knows only how to gather grass, offers itself by throwing itself into a fire. The old man was really Śakra, who was moved and drew the rabbit's likeness on the Moon. It is said that the smoke from that fire still drapes the image.

Worship and religion

It has no cult of its own, but the myth gave rise to lunar festivals that are still celebrated: the Mid-Autumn Festival in China, Tết Trung Thu in Vietnam, Tsukimi in Japan, Chuseok in Korea and Bon Om Touk in Cambodia.

Legacy

The Jade Rabbit appears in Journey to the West as a guardian of an elixir in the Guanghan Palace. China's Yutu rover, which landed on the Moon in 2013, was named after the Jade Rabbit following an online poll, and its successor Yutu-2 operated on the far side in 2019. During Apollo 11, NASA mission control joked with the crew about the rabbit that keeps Chang'e company.

Ancient sources: The Chu Ci from the Western Han dynasty; the Taiping Yulan from the Song era; the Buddhist Jataka tales; and the Japanese Konjaku Monogatarishū.

Trivia

A hare before a rabbit. In the earliest sources the lunar figure is a hare, because rabbits had not yet arrived in China during the Western Han dynasty.
A name chosen online. The name of China's Yutu lunar rover was chosen through an online poll.
