Hindu goddess of Virar, Maharashtra

Jivdani Mata

Jivdani Mata is a Hindu goddess worshipped at a temple on a hilltop in Virar, in the Indian state of Maharashtra, on the northern edge of the Mumbai metropolitan area. Her cult centres on the idea of life's true wealth, which is what her name means.

The myth

According to local tradition, the Pandavas, during their forest journey, reached Shurparaka and worshipped the goddess Ekavira on the banks of the Viraar Tirtha. Struck by the calm of the place, they carved caves into the nearby hills and installed the Yoga Linga of the goddess in one of them, calling her Bhagavati Jeevadhani.

Centuries later, a man from the Mahar community was herding the village cattle. After following a cow to the top of the hill, he met a woman of divine appearance who turned out to be his family goddess. She wanted to pay him for his work, but he asked instead for moksha, liberation. As she was about to vanish into the cave, a barren woman who had witnessed the scene begged for children for all women who prayed to the goddess, and the goddess agreed, saying she would dwell in a hollow in the cave.

Worship and religion

The focus of worship is the hollow in the cave, regarded as the original sanctum; the white marble image in the temple today is recent. Barren women offer betel nuts into this hollow, as is done at the shrine of Mahurgad, hoping to conceive. Many devotees come during the nine nights of Navratri, and a fair held on Dusherra day draws thousands of people.

Ancient sources: The Puranas refer to the Shurparaka pilgrimage associated with this site, but the story of the goddess comes from local legends.

Trivia

Many steps. The temple is said to be reached after climbing about 1465 steps from the foot of the hill, although other sources give a noticeably lower height above sea level.
A sacred cow. In the story, the cow that led the herder is identified with Kamadhenu, the divine cow, and her footprints are said to remain on the rock of the hill.
