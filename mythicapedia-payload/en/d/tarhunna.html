Hittite weather god

Tarḫunna

Tarḫunna was the weather god of the Hittite religion in Anatolia, lord of thunder, lightning and rain. He was regarded as the ruler of the gods and the guarantor of the king's power.

The myth

According to Hittite tradition, Tarḫunna ruled the heavens and the mountains, and he decided whether the fields would be fertile and the harvests good, or whether drought and famine would come. His name, linked to the verb meaning "to prevail", suggests a conquering god.

He was the partner of the sun goddess of Arinna, with whom he had children such as Telipinu, Kammamma, Mezulla and Inara. He was shown as a bearded man in a pointed cap, holding a three-pronged thunderbolt and, in later times, an adze.

Worship and religion

He was the chief god of the Hittites. In the rock reliefs of the Yazılıkaya sanctuary he stands at the front of a long line of gods, standing on the mountain gods Namni and Ḫazzi. The king ruled the land in his name, and royal authority was considered to rest on his support.

Legacy

His name survived in other Anatolian languages, such as Luwian and Lycian, and appeared in personal names into Roman times. He was identified with the Mesopotamian god Hadad and, in the Roman period, with Jupiter Dolichenus.

Ancient sources: Hittite and Luwian inscriptions in cuneiform and hieroglyphic script, along with the reliefs at Yazılıkaya.

Trivia

Name of victory. His name comes from a root meaning «to conquer» or «to prevail», and it was used in almost all Anatolian languages.
Indo-European origin. According to Norbert Oettinger, the functions of this god may go back to the Proto-Indo-European god Perkwunos, though the Anatolian peoples changed his name.
