Greek hero, son of Perseus and founder of Cynuria

Cynurus

Cynurus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Perseus and Andromeda, credited with founding the city of Cynuria in Laconia. He matters mainly as a mythical explanation for the origin of a community that regarded itself as descended from Argos.

The myth

According to tradition, Cynurus was the son of Perseus and had a brother named Alceus. Accounts hold that his first settlers came from Argos, to which they considered themselves related, and that the community of Cynuria grew from them.

Pausanias records a later episode: the Lacedaemonians expelled the men of Cynuria of military age, accusing them of allowing bandits from their region to raid the Argolid and of making open raids into Laconian land themselves. Pausanias reports this version as part of the tradition about the community.

A scholium also mentions a Cynosurus, son of Pelops and brother of Eelius, Corinthus and Hippasus, who is sometimes associated with Cynurus. This genealogy does not match that of Perseus, so the sources differ on his lineage.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes the expulsion of the men of Cynuria, and a scholium mentions a Cynosurus linked to Pelops.
