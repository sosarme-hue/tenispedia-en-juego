lakes of the underworld in Greek myth

Acherusia

Acherusia was the name the ancient Greeks gave to several lakes and swamps linked to the underworld. The first one associated with this belief lay in Thesprotia, beside the river Acheron.

The myth

Several lakes and swamps in the Greek world bore this name, among them those near Hermione in Argolis and Heraclea in Bithynia. An underground cavern of similar name was the passage through which Heracles dragged Cerberus during one of his Twelve Labors.

In Plato's Phaedo, neutral souls and those who repented of great crimes are cleansed in this lake before moving on, and repentant wrongdoers may hope that their victims will invite them in.

Legacy

Early Christianity took up the idea of the lake as a place of purification. The Apocalypse of Peter, from the 2nd century, describes damned souls being washed in it through the intercession of the saints. Other texts include it too, such as the Apocalypse of Paul, where the archangel Michael washes souls there.

Ancient sources: Plato's Phaedo, and later Christian texts such as the Apocalypse of Peter.

Trivia

A drained lake. The Acherusia lake in Thesprotia no longer exists: it was drained in the 1960s by the British company Boots Ltd, and today a network of irrigation canals crosses the plain.
A book attributed to Bartholomew. The Book of the Resurrection of Jesus Christ, attributed to Bartholomew, describes a narrator who is washed three times in the lake by the archangel Michael before entering heaven.
