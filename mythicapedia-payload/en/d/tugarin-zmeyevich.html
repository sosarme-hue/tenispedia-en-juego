dragon-like monster of Slavic folklore

Tugarin Zmeyevich

Tugarin Zmeyevich is a creature of Russian byliny and fairy tales who embodies evil and cruelty and appears in a giant, dragon-like form. His duel with the hero Alyosha Popovich is one of the best-known episodes of Russian epic poetry.

The myth

According to tradition, Tugarin appears as a giant with a largely human shape but dragon-like traits: he rides a horse, seems to fly with paper-like wings attached to the horse rather than himself, and is a voracious glutton with an enormous head. His name has been linked to Tugar-Khan, a Turkic Polovtsian leader, though another etymology connects it to the root tug, "grief".

The central episode is his duel with Alyosha Popovich at the Safat River, where both pitch pavilions. Tugarin roars or hisses and attacks with fire, smoke and burning brands, but the hero wins, cuts his body into pieces and scatters them across the field. His paper wings fail in the rain, which decides the fight. Some versions add that he had a relationship with Prince Vladimir's wife, who, upon learning of his death, reproaches Alyosha for separating her from her "dear friend".
