Norse god, son of Odin and brother of Thor

Meili

Meili is one of the Æsir in Norse mythology, a son of Odin and brother of Thor. He barely appears in the sources, but his place among Odin's children helps show how the family of gods was organised.

The myth

According to the Scandinavian sources, Meili almost always appears as the brother of other gods. His name occurs in the Poetic Edda, in the poem Hárbarðsljóð, where Thor declares that he is the son of Odin, the brother of Meili and the father of Magni. Thor also makes clear that Meili is not his half-brother, which means the two share a mother.

Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda mentions him four times in the Skáldskaparmál. In the poem Haustlöng, attributed to the tenth-century skald Þjóðólfr of Hvinir, Thor is called Meili's brother. Elsewhere a kenning refers to the god Hœnir as step-Meili, an allusion to a family tie the text never explains. In the list of names of the Æsir at the end of the work, Meili appears as a son of Odin, between Baldr and Víðarr.

Apart from these mentions, the texts tell no deeds or episodes specific to Meili.

Legacy

The origin and meaning of his name are unknown. Rudolf Simek suggested it might mean 'the dear one'. In the nineteenth century some scholars argued that he shared a mother with Thor, namely Jörð, the goddess who personifies the Earth. In the same century Viktor Rydberg proposed that Meili and Baldr were in fact the same figure.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Hárbarðsljóð) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Skáldskaparmál and the final list of names, along with the poem Haustlöng, quoted in that same work.

Trivia

Brother on the mother's side. In the Hárbarðsljóð, Thor makes clear that Meili is not his half-brother, which means the two share a mother.
