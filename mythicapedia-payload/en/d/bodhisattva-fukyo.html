Bodhisattva Never Disparaging

Never Disparaging, or Sadāparibhūta, is a mahayana bodhisattva who appears in the Lotus Sutra. His story shows that honouring others and persevering in the teaching leads to buddhahood, even under persecution.

The myth

According to the Lotus Sutra, the Buddha Shakyamuni tells how, in an age when the teaching was declining, there lived a bodhisattva called Never Disparaging. He did not study sutras or preach, but he paid homage to every monk, nun and lay follower he met, predicting that they would all become buddhas. Some arrogant clerics attacked him with sticks and stones, and from a distance he kept calling out that he did not despise them.

Before he died he heard the Lotus Sutra and purified his six senses. After countless rebirths he attained enlightenment. His former attackers suffered for a long time in the Avici hell, until they met him again and became his disciples.

Ancient sources: He appears in chapter twenty of the Lotus Sutra, where Shakyamuni presents him as one of his past lives.

Trivia

A disputed name. The name sadāparibhūta can be understood as «never despising» or as «always despised». According to some scholars, the first reading was popularised by Kumārajīva.
