hall of the gods in Norse mythology

Vingólf

Vingólf is one of the dwellings of the gods in Norse mythology. It is described as the hall of the goddesses and also as the place where righteous men and those slain in battle go after death.

The myth

Ancient sources mention it only a few times. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, it appears three times in the Gylfaginning section, and the three mentions are hard to reconcile. First, it is an alternative name for Gimlé, the paradise where righteous people go after death. Second, it is the hall or shrine (hörgr) of the goddesses. Third, it is the residence of the warriors slain in battle, the einherjar.

It also appears in the enigmatic Hrafnagaldur Óðins, a late poem composed in the Eddic style, where it seems to be a place where the Æsir gather for an ale feast. The meaning of this mention depends on how the poem is interpreted as a whole.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) and the poem Hrafnagaldur Óðins.

Trivia

A name with two readings. Vingólf can be read as 'pleasant hall' or, with a different accent, as 'wine hall'.
A variant in Uppsalabók. Uppsalabók, one of the four main manuscripts of the Prose Edda, has the variant Vindglóð, which is thought to be probably corrupt.
