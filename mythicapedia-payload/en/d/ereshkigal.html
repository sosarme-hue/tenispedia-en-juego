goddess of the underworld in Mesopotamian mythology

Ereshkigal

Ereshkigal is the sovereign goddess of the underworld in Sumerian, Akkadian and Babylonian mythology, queen of Kur or Irkalla and wife of Nergal. Her stories, among the great Mesopotamian accounts of death, show how people imagined the fate of the dead.

The myth

In Mesopotamian tradition, Ereshkigal rules Kur, or Irkalla, the land of the dead, alongside her husband Nergal. Sources do not agree on her origins. In the Sumerian poem Inanna's Descent into the Underworld she is described as Inanna's older sister, though this kinship is better understood as a way of placing the two goddesses on the same rank than as a true family tie.

The best-known myth tells how she came to marry Nergal. The gods hold a banquet that Ereshkigal cannot attend, so she sends her vizier Namtar in her place. Everyone greets him with respect except Nergal, who remains seated. As punishment, Nergal must descend to the underworld to ask for forgiveness, advised by Ea not to eat, drink or join with the goddess. He gives in to temptation and spends six days with her. When he leaves, Ereshkigal demands his return, and Namtar fails to recognise him because Ea has disguised him. Furious, she threatens to release the dead onto the world of the living. Nergal returns and seizes her by the hair, intending to kill her, but she offers to share her realm and he accepts.

Worship and religion

Her cult was very limited. Her main temple stood in Kutha, a city originally linked to Nergal, and no personal names built with her name are known, suggesting little popular devotion. Ritual texts mention her rarely, while she plays a prominent role in mythical literature. Beliefs about the underworld varied, and different male and female deities ruled over the realm of the dead depending on region and period. Like the rest of Mesopotamian religion, her worship disappeared with that civilization.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian poem Inanna's Descent into the Underworld and the story of her marriage to Nergal, preserved in Akkadian and Babylonian versions.

Trivia

Other names. Ereshkigal also appears as Irkalla and sometimes as Ninkigal, 'Lady of the Great Earth'. Something similar happened with the Greek name Hades, which referred both to the place and to its ruler.
Several underworld rulers. Ereshkigal was not the only ruler of the dead. Ninazu, first regarded as her husband and later as her son, and Ningishzida are also associated with her in different myths.
