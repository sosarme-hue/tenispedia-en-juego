Italic god of purification and death

Februus

Februus is an ancient Italic god of purification, death and the underworld, worshipped by both Romans and Etruscans. His name gave rise to February, the month when purification rites were held.

The myth

Few sources tell stories about Februus, so his figure is known mainly through his functions. For the Romans he was a god of the dead and of purification. The Etruscans also worshipped him as a god of the underworld, linking him with riches, gold and money, in a way similar to how the Romans regarded Pluto. His name may have meant something like 'the underground one' in Etruscan.

An old interpretation holds that Februus may have become Febris, goddess of fever. The connection lies in the sweating of fever, which was seen as a washing and cleansing process. The Romans also often considered Faunus and Februus to be the same god, since both were honoured at around the same time of year.

Worship and religion

The Romans celebrated the Februa or Februalia, festivals of washing and purification held on the 15th of February. Expiatory sacrifices were offered to cleanse sins. These rites took place at about the same time as the Lupercalia, held in honour of Faunus and the she-wolf who nursed Romulus and Remus. The sources do not say whether his cult survives today.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Februarius, the Latin name of the month that became February in English. Some scholars also link him to Febris, the Roman goddess of fever, whose name in Latin simply means fever.

Trivia

A month named after a rite. The month of February takes its name from the Februa, purification festivals held around the 15th day of the month.
Purifying sweat. In this tradition, the sweating of fever was seen as a way of purging the body.
Two gods in one. Faunus and Februus were often regarded as the same entity because their festivals fell so close together.
