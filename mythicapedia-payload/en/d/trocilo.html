Argive hero linked to the invention of the chariot

Trochilus

Trochilus is a figure from Greek mythology, a member of the Argive royal house and son of the princess Callithyia. Some traditions credit him, or his mother, with inventing the chariot, and he matters mainly because he is linked to the origins of Triptolemus.

The myth

According to tradition, Trochilus belonged to the royal house of Argos. Some versions credited him or his mother with inventing the chariot, although Tertullian notes that Erichthonius may have been the inventor and adds that, in that case, Trochilus dedicated the chariot to Hera.

Pausanias relates that, pursued by Agenor, Trochilus had to flee from Argos to Attica, where he married a woman from Eleusis and had two sons, Triptolemus and Eubuleus.

Worship and religion

Pausanias presents him as a priest of Demeter before his exile to Attica.

Legacy

Hyginus and a scholiast on Aratus associate the constellation Auriga with Trochilus, who was honored in the stars for his invention.

Ancient sources: Tertullian, Hyginus, a scholiast on Aratus and Pausanias.

Trivia

A constellation named after an inventor. Some ancient authors thought the constellation Auriga was the starry image of Trochilus, with which he was honored for his invention.
From Argos to Attica. According to Pausanias, Trochilus's fate changed when he had to leave Argos and settle in Attica, where he started his family.
