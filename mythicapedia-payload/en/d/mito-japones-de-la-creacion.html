Japanese tale of the origin of the world and the archipelago

Japanese creation myth

The Japanese creation myth tells how Heaven, Earth and the archipelago came into being, as recounted in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, the literary foundation of Japanese mythology and Shinto.

The myth

At first the universe was a thick, silent, shapeless mass. The lightest matter rose to form Heaven, while the dense part sank to become Earth. Gods appeared in Takamagahara and, finally, five pairs of deities were born. The youngest pair, Izanagi and Izanami, were tasked with consolidating the land using a heavenly spear.

When they stirred the sea with the spear, the drops that fell formed the island of Onogoro. There they raised an altar around a central pillar and, circling in opposite directions until they met, they gave birth to islands and gods. The first attempt failed because she spoke first. After repeating the rite with him speaking first, the islands of the archipelago were born. They then bore gods of wind, mountains and trees, with which the myth ends.

Legacy

Its written form in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki gives Japanese mythology and Shinto much of their literary foundation.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Nihon Shoki (720), which describe it at the beginning, with some variations between them.

Trivia

An island born from a drop. The island of Onogoro arises from the drops of salt water that fall from the spear when it is lifted out of the sea.
Unknown islands. Hokkaido and Okinawa were unknown to the Japanese when the myth was written, and they do not appear among the islands created.
