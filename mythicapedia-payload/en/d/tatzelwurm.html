Alpine creature, cat-headed semi-dragon

Tatzelwurm

The Tatzelwurm, also called Stollenwurm, is a semi-dragon from the folklore of the Alps in Bavaria, Austria and Switzerland, described as a lizard-like or serpent-like creature with a cat's head. It matters because it is one of the most enduring figures of Alpine folklore and of the lindworm family of dragons.

The myth

According to popular accounts, the Tatzelwurm is a stubby, lizard-like creature a few feet long, with a cat-like face and visible front legs; some describe four short legs, others only two. It is said to be venomous, to attack with poisonous breath that can cause headaches and dizziness, and to whistle or hiss. It is also believed to be able to leap long distances.

The stories are anecdotal. In 1660, Andreas Roduner described a four-legged, cat-faced beast that reared up as tall as a man. Later lore tells of a farmer, Hans Fuchs, who fled after seeing two of these creatures in the mountains and died of a heart attack. In the early nineteenth century, Samuel Studer described it as a short, stubby serpent with a cat-like head and clawed feet, said to appear after humid, hot weather.

Legacy

The name was adopted as an emblem by the Luftwaffe's 1/JG 3 fighter group, and later by II/JG 1. It remains part of Alpine popular culture as one of the semi-dragons alongside the lindworm, Fafnir and the wyvern.

Trivia

Scheuchzer's dragons. Copperplate drawings of supposed Alpine dragons published by Johann Jakob Scheuchzer in 1723 are known as Scheuchzer's dragons, although the original testimonies were recorded by Wagner.
A reward for a specimen. Samuel Studer offered between 3 and 4 louis d'or to anyone who could supply the remains of an authentic Stollenwurm.
