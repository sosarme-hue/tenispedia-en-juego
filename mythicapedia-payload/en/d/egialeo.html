Argive hero, one of the Epigoni

Aegialeus

Aegialeus is a hero of Greek myth, the elder son of Adrastus, king of Argos. He belonged to the Epigoni, the generation of leaders who retook Thebes years after their fathers failed to take the city, and his death in that campaign ties him to revenge and to the succession of the Argive throne.

The myth

According to tradition, Aegialeus was the eldest son of Adrastus and either Amphithea or Demonassa. His father was the only one of the Seven against Thebes to survive that failed expedition, so Aegialeus was left to avenge the defeat.

The stories place him among the Epigoni, the sons of the fallen leaders, who attacked Thebes and captured it. Pausanias and Hellanicus both identify him with this group. During the assault Aegialeus was the only Argive leader to die. Laodamas, son of Eteocles, killed him at Glisas.

His body was taken to Pagae in Megaris, where he was buried. After his death Adrastus died of grief, and the throne passed to Diomedes, his grandson through his daughter Deipyle. Later, when Diomedes was exiled, Aegialeus' son Cyanippus took the throne. Accounts differ on Laodamas's fate: some say he fled to Illyria, others that Alcmaeon killed him.

Worship and religion

Aegialeus was worshipped as a hero at Pagae in Megaris. Tradition held that his body had been carried there from Thebes and buried in that place.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and Hellanicus identify him as one of the Epigoni who took Thebes.
