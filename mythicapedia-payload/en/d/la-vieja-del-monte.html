benevolent figure of Leonese folklore

La Vieja del Monte

La Vieja del Monte is a character from the oral tradition of the mountain villages of the province of León, a kindly old woman who feeds the children of the area. Her figure, with parallels in Asturias and Navarre, reflects the life of shepherds and farmers in the region.

The myth

According to oral tradition, La Vieja del Monte lives in a rock or a cave in the mountains, where she kneads and bakes bread for the children of nearby villages. The bread reached the little ones through relatives returning from work in the fields.

The tale was most present during the seasons of heaviest farming and herding work. In some areas the bread goes by names such as pan de paxarines, pan de pajarines or pan de raposa.

The figure has parallels in other regions: in Pigüeces (Asturias) an old woman living in a hut bakes buns for children, and in Proaza, also in Asturias, the Cueva la Maruxina was where children climbed to receive rolls for their afternoon snack. In the Burunda valley in Navarre, the leftovers the men brought home were called basoko Mariren ogia, and in Améscoa the little ones were told «Toma pan de la vieja del monte» when given leftover bread.

Legacy

In early 2014 she was first mentioned as a local alternative to Christmas figures such as Papá Noel or the Three Kings, though stripped of her original attributes and meaning. From 2016 associations and groups have popularized her in the city of León; in 2017 she led her first parade and her own merchandise began to be sold.
