Titan of Greek mythology, father of Hecate

Perses

Perses is a Titan in Greek mythology, son of Crius and Eurybia, and the father of the goddess Hecate. His importance is mostly genealogical, since he serves as a link that gives origin to more prominent figures. Ancient stories say little about him beyond his marriage and offspring.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Perses was born to Crius, one of the original twelve Titans, and Eurybia, and he had two brothers, Astraeus and Pallas. His name is linked to the Ancient Greek word perthō, meaning to sack, ravage or destroy. Hesiod oddly describes him as eminent among all men in wisdom, a trait no other author gives him and one that was instead associated with his contemporaries Metis and Prometheus.

Perses married his cousin Asteria, daughter of Phoebe and Coeus, and they had Hecate, whom Zeus honoured above all others as the goddess of magic, crossroads and witchcraft. In a lesser-known tradition recorded by Musaeus, Zeus himself was Hecate's father, having kept Asteria as his mistress for a time before giving her to Perses.

A late source claims that the ancient Perses was a son of the Sky, Caelus. The Latin writer Hyginus, whose text survives in a badly corrupted state, makes him a son of Polus and Phoebe, confusing the offspring of Crius and Coeus. Some versions also make him the father of Chariclo, wife of the centaur Chiron, though not all accounts agree. He is also often confused with another Perses, the son of the sun-god Helios and the nymph Perse, who in some versions fathers Hecate.

Ancient sources: Hesiod in his Theogony, with later mentions by Musaeus, Hyginus and a late source that give varying genealogies.

Trivia

A name with warlike sense. His name comes from perthō, meaning to sack or destroy, a meaning that contrasts with the wisdom Hesiod credits him with.
Two different Perses. He is often confused with another Perses, son of the sun-god Helios and the nymph Perse, who in some versions is Hecate's father.
