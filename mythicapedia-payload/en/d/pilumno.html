Roman god of grain milling and childhood

Pilumnus

Pilumnus is a rustic nature deity of early Roman mythology, protector of children and of grain grinding. He was the brother of Picumnus, and his figure links domestic life with farming.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Pilumnus was a son of Jupiter and the brother of Picumnus. He was credited with inventing the grinding of wheat, and bakers honoured him. He took Danae as his wife and founded Ardea in Apulia, where his son Daunus was born. He was invoked alongside two protective goddesses: Intercidona, who struck the doors with an axe to drive out the demon Silvanus, and Deverra, who swept the threshold after a child was born so that the little ones would grow up healthy.

In Virgil's Aeneid, Pilumnus is the grandfather of Turnus and the father of Daunus.

Worship and religion

Ancient Romans set up an extra bed after a child was born to secure the god's help. A ceremony in his honour involved driving a stake into the ground. There is no evidence that his cult survived.

Ancient sources: Virgil mentions him in the Aeneid, where he is the grandfather of Turnus and the father of Daunus.

Trivia

Intercidona. She was the goddess of axe blows struck against doors to drive out the demon Silvanus.
An extra bed. After a child was born, Roman families prepared an additional bed to win the help of Pilumnus.
