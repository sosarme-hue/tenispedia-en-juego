Celtic princess, mother of the ancestor of the Celts

Celtine

Celtine, also called Celto, is a princess of Celtic tradition who, according to Graeco-Roman authors, was the mother of Galates or Celtus, the mythical ancestor of the Celts. Her story, passed down by Diodorus, Parthenius and others, explains the legendary origin of this people.

The myth

According to these accounts, Celtine was the daughter of a king named Bretannos in the land of Celtice. When Heracles passed through with the cattle of Geryon, the princess fell in love with him. In one version she kept the herd and refused to return it until they lay together; in another, she asked him for union directly. From that encounter a son was born, Galates or Celtus, who according to tradition was the only one able to draw Heracles' bow, and so he became king.

Diodorus of Sicily links the story to Heracles founding Alesia and makes Galates the source of the name of the Gauls. Silius Italicus records another version with the princess Pyrene, who gives birth to a serpent, an echo of an older cycle about Heracles and serpents.

Ancient sources: Diodorus of Sicily, Parthenius of Nicaea and Silius Italicus, along with a version attributed to Timagenes.

Trivia

Inheritance of the youngest. In the myth, the youngest son inherits Heracles' bow, which recalls the Celtic custom by which the youngest received the family estate, with its buildings, eight acres of land, an axe, a cauldron and a coulter.
Scythian parallel. A Scythian story has an almost identical structure: Heracles, a local woman and a son who inherits his father's bow.
