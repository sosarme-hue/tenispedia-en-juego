legendary Chinese sovereign, second of the Three Sovereigns

Dihuang

Dihuang, the "Earthly Sovereign", is the second of the legendary Chinese rulers, coming after the age of Pangu. Traditional Chinese historiography places him second among the Three Sovereigns, and his story explains how heaven and earth were put in order.

The myth

According to the "Basic Annals of the Three Sovereigns", preserved by Sima Zhen in his supplement to the Records of the Grand Historian, Dihuang had eleven heads and ruled under the influence of the fire element. After his birth the world fell into chaos: the sun and moon, born from Pangu's eyes, and the stars, born from his hair, would not move in an orderly way, causing days without sun, days of unbroken sunshine, or dangerous falling stars.

Dihuang corrected these disorders, made the sun and moon follow their proper courses, and set the length of months and years. He had eleven brothers, ruled for eleven thousand years and, according to the tradition, died at the age of eighteen thousand. He created Mount Xiong'er ("bear ear mountain") and Mount Longmen ("dragon gate mountain"). His successor was Renhuang.

Ancient sources: Sima Zhen, in his supplement to the Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji), and the encyclopedia Yiwen Leiju.

Trivia

Eleven heads. According to the annals, Dihuang had eleven heads and eleven brothers, supernatural traits that emphasize his exceptional nature.
Mountains named after animals. The mountains he created bear names meaning "bear ear" and "dragon gate", linking the landscape with the mythic story.
