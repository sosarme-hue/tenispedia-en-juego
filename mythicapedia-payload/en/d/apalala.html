nāga king of the waters in Buddhist mythology

Apalāla

Apalāla is a nāga, a serpent-like water being, from Buddhist mythology. According to the tale he lived near the Swat River, in what is now Peshawar in Pakistan, and he is best known for his conversion to Buddhism by the Buddha, one of the most popular stories in Buddhist lore and art.

The myth

According to the tradition, Apalāla was a water-dwelling nāga who lived by the Swat River and was regarded as a king of his kind. The Buddha converted him to the Buddhist faith, and the story is often told to children of Buddhist families to teach them that their happiness lies in the Buddhist faith.

Legacy

The scene of his conversion is among those depicted in the relic chamber of the Mahāthūpa in Sri Lanka, which shows that the tale was known there. It is also a frequent subject in Buddhist art.

Ancient sources: The Divyāvadāna, in Sanskrit, places his conversion shortly before the Buddha's death. The Samantapāsādikā, a Pali commentary, notes that the episode was left out of the Three Councils.

Trivia

Outside the canon. The story does not appear in the Pali Canon, although his name does appear among the beings who honor the Buddha.
