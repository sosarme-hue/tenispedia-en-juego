heroine of Indian tradition, mother of the emperor Bharata

Shakuntala

Shakuntala is a heroine of Hindu tradition, the daughter of the sage Vishvamitra and the celestial nymph Menaka, and the wife of King Dushyanta. Her story appears in the Mahabharata and became best known through Kalidasa's play, since her union with Dushyanta produced Bharata, a legendary figure linked to the origin of India.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, the sage Vishvamitra performed such intense penance that Indra, king of the gods, feared losing his power and sent the apsara Menaka to distract him. Their encounter produced a daughter, whom Menaka left near the hermitage of the sage Kanva before returning to heaven. Kanva found the infant surrounded by śakunta birds and named her Shakuntala, meaning one protected by the birds.

Years later, King Dushyanta, while hunting, found her at the hermitage during Kanva's absence. They fell in love and married in the gandharva manner, without ceremony or family consent. When the king returned to his palace, Shakuntala was pregnant. After her son was born, a boy so strong that he could overpower lions, she brought him to the court, but Dushyanta pretended not to remember her and rejected the child. A celestial voice confirmed the truth, the king accepted the boy, who was named Bharata, meaning the cherished one. Tradition holds that under his rule the land came to be called Bharatavarsha.

Legacy

Kalidasa's play Abhijnanashakuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala), written around the fourth or fifth century AD, added a curse and a lost signet ring to the story and made the heroine a central figure of Sanskrit literature. She is regarded as a symbol of Indian womanhood and has inspired many literary, artistic and visual adaptations. Modern scholars often highlight the contrast between the epic Shakuntala, frank and determined, and the more passive, naive figure of the drama.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata narrates her story in its Adi Parva; the dramatic version belongs to Kalidasa, writing in the fourth or fifth century AD.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Her name derives from śakunta, a word for a bird or a bird of prey, and is explained as one protected by the birds.
A ring that reappears. In the play, the signet ring Dushyanta gave her is lost in a river and later found in a fish's belly, which restores the king's memory.
