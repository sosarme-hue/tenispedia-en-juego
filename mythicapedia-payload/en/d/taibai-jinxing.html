Chinese god of the planet Venus

Taibai Jinxing

Taibai Jinxing is a deity of Chinese folk religion and Taoism, associated with the planet Venus. He is said to be the son of the White Emperor and a celestial messenger.

The myth

Most accounts treat him as a minor figure. In one storytellers' tale, he was charmed by two celestial weaving girls and spent forty-six days with them in a cave. Discovered during an inquiry into a long drought, he was escorted back to his post and quickly brought rain.

In the classical Taoist description he appears as a goddess, dressed in yellow and holding a lute. After the Ming dynasty, the novel Journey to the West made him an elderly god and envoy of the Jade Emperor, tasked with summoning Sun Wukong to Heaven.

Worship and religion

Chinese folk religion regards the appearance of the Taibaixing, the Great White Star, especially in daylight, as a negative omen.

Legacy

In Chinese astronomy his name designates Venus. He was considered the reincarnation of the Han scholar-official Dongfang Shuo, and the mother of the poet Li Bai dreamed of him during her pregnancy, so her son was given a name echoing Taibai.

Ancient sources: The novel Fengshen Yanyi refers to him as an aide in the court of the Jade Emperor.

Trivia

A dream before birth. The mother of the poet Li Bai dreamed of Taibai Jinxing while pregnant, and so believed her son was his reincarnation.
Forty-six days in a cave. According to the tale, the escapade with the weaving girls came to light during an inquiry into a lengthy drought.
A post that insulted Sun Wukong. Sun Wukong was angered at being appointed Protector of the Horses, which he saw as an insult, and rebelled against Heaven.
