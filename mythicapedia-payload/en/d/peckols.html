Prussian god of the underworld and the dead

Peckols

Peckols is a god of pagan Prussian mythology, lord of the underworld, darkness and the dead, worshipped by the Old Prussians. Many researchers think he was the same figure as Patollo, whose name varied from one language to another.

The myth

Sources describe him as an angry, evil spirit, similar to the Lithuanian Velnias. The Sudovian Book, from the 1520s, names him as god of hell and darkness, alongside Pockols, a flying spirit or devil. The chronicler Simon Grunau, in 1529, portrays him as an old man with a white beard and turban-like headdress, a frightening and ruthless figure who punished those who disobeyed their priests or buried their dead without proper sacrifices. Grunau's details come late and are doubtful, so they should be treated with caution.

Worship and religion

The texts say little about his worship. Grunau mentions a temple called Rickoyoto, but its existence is uncertain, and no living religious practice survives.

Legacy

His name drifted toward meanings of devil and hell in Lithuanian, shaped by Christianity. In the 16th century, Mikalojus Daukša used the root pik- as a synonym for hell, although these words have since fallen out of use in favour of demonas and pragaras.

Ancient sources: The Sudovian Book (1520s) and the Constitutiones Synodales (1530) are the earliest sources that mention him, alongside the later chronicle of Simon Grunau.

Trivia

A name with several etymologies. Christoph Hartknoch linked it to a Sarmatian word for the underworld, while other scholars proposed Lithuanian roots tied to evil or hostility.
A possible link to Veles. Some researchers believe Peckols and the Baltic deity Veles could be different names for the same divinity.
