Odysseus's home island in the Odyssey

Homeric Ithaca

In Greek mythology, Ithaca is the island home of the hero Odysseus and the main setting of Homer's Odyssey. Its exact location has been debated for centuries, because the epic describes it in remarkable geographic detail.

The myth

According to tradition, Ithaca is the home Odysseus tries to reach after the Trojan War. His journey home stretches on for years, while on the island his wife and son resist the pressure of other men who want to take over the palace.

The story ends with the hero's return to his homeland, where he reclaims his house and his place. Ancient texts describe the island in such detail that many scholars have tried to identify it with a real place, though no proposal has been conclusive.

Legacy

The location of Ithaca has inspired very different theories, from Eratosthenes, who regarded it as an invention of Homer, to proposals placing it on modern Ithaca, on Kefalonia, on Paxos, in the Baltic or in the Azores. Samuel Butler argued that the Odyssey was written by a young Sicilian woman, an idea Robert Graves turned into his novel Homer's Daughter.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey is the main source. Strabo, in his Geography, records some of the earliest debates about where the island lay.

Trivia

A Sicilian authoress. Samuel Butler argued that the Odyssey was written by a young Sicilian woman who presents herself in the poem as Nausicaa. He proposed that the coast of Sicily, especially the Trapani area, is reflected in the poem's settings.
An island that may never have existed. Eratosthenes, as recorded by Strabo, believed that Ithaca did not exist and was an invention of Homer. It is one of the oldest positions on the question.
