Greek god, son of Poseidon and eponym of the island of Chios

Chios

Chios is a figure of Greek mythology to whom the island of Chios, in the Aegean Sea, owes its name. Sources usually present him as a son of Poseidon, god of the sea, although another version makes him one of the Oceanids.

The myth

In the best-known version, Chios was the son of Poseidon and an unnamed nymph of the island. His name was said to come from the heavy snowfall that fell while his mother was in labour, since the Greek word chiōn means "snow".

Another tradition makes him one of the Oceanids, daughter of the Titan Oceanus and possibly of his sister and wife Tethys. In this account the island's name would have a different origin, since it would no longer be explained by the birth.

The island may instead have taken its name from the nymph Chione, or simply from its snowy weather.

Trivia

Two genealogies. The same name can refer to a son of Poseidon or to one of the Oceanids, daughters of the Titan Oceanus. Both figures serve to explain the island's name.
A name linked to snow. The Greek name Chios is related to chiōn, meaning "snow", the word the stories use to explain the birth legend.
