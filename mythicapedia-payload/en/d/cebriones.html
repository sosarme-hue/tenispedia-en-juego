Trojan hero, Hector's charioteer

Cebriones

Cebriones is a Trojan hero in Greek mythology, the illegitimate son of King Priam and a slave, and half-brother of Hector. He matters mainly for his role in the Iliad, where he dies during the assault on the Achaean wall.

The myth

According to Homer's Iliad, Cebriones served as Hector's charioteer during the Trojan War and fought beside him and Paris in the division that finally breached the Argive wall. His end comes when Patroclus strikes him with a stone that hits him in the forehead; the blow knocks his eyes out and flings him from Hector's chariot.

As he falls, Patroclus mocks his diving skill, remarking that with such ability he could have fed many by diving for oysters in the storming sea.

Legacy

Strabo suggests that Cebriones may have given his name to the region of Cebrenia, its city and the Cebreni people, though the link is uncertain. He also appears as a giant in the Gigantomachy and is mentioned in Aristophanes' comedy The Birds.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad presents him as Hector's half-brother and charioteer during the Trojan War.

Trivia

A name on the map. Strabo suggests that Cebriones gave his name to the region of Cebrenia, its city and the Cebreni people.
Patroclus's mockery. Patroclus jokes about Cebriones's diving skill, comparing it to that of an oyster diver.
