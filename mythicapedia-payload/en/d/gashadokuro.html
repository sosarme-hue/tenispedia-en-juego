giant skeleton yōkai of Japanese folklore

Gashadokuro

The gashadokuro is a yōkai from Japanese folklore: a giant skeleton built from the bones of people who died of starvation or in battle without proper burial. Absent from ancient tradition, its image took shape in twentieth-century popular culture and became one of the most recognisable figures in the yōkai bestiary.

The myth

According to modern tradition, the gashadokuro arises from the resentment of those who died in anger and pain, whose spirits remain bound to abandoned bones. It roams after midnight and attacks lone travellers, biting their heads to drink their blood. Some accounts say the clattering of its teeth announces its approach, and that Shinto charms can ward it off. Another version gives it a small bell in its right hand, which rings without its control; hiding where it cannot see you is the only way to escape.

The link to older history runs through Taira no Masakado, a samurai from the Kantō region who was beheaded in 940 after a revolt in the tenth century. In the story, his daughter Takiyasha-hime, a powerful sorceress, summoned a colossal skeleton from the bones of the fallen to avenge him and sent it against Kyoto. This tale, however, comes from a later literary tradition rather than from an ancient text that names the gashadokuro.

Legacy

The gashadokuro became popular in the twentieth century. It appeared in magazines and yōkai encyclopedias, such as Arifumi Satō's of 1972, and Shigeru Mizuki's illustrations did much to fix its modern look. Utagawa Kuniyoshi's print of the witch Takiyasha and the skeleton spectre served as a visual reference. It also appears in the collaborative fiction SCP, where it is catalogued as SCP-2963.

Ancient sources: No ancient source names it; its image derives from a Utagawa Kuniyoshi print inspired by a Santō Kyōden yomihon of the Edo period.

Trivia

A modern origin. Although linked to ancient history, the gashadokuro first appeared in print in the second half of the twentieth century, through magazine writers and yōkai encyclopedias.
A frequent mix-up. A tale from the Nihon Ryōiki, about a skeleton with a bamboo shoot growing from its eye socket, is sometimes confused with the gashadokuro, although the two are unrelated.
