eponymous king of the Phaeacians in Greek mythology

Phaeax

Phaeax, also spelled Phaeacus, is in Greek mythology the eponymous king of the Phaeacians, the people who took their name from him. A son of Poseidon, his importance lies in being the founder of the line that ruled the island of Scheria, although ancient accounts do not fully agree on who his descendants were.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phaeax was a son of Poseidon and Korkyra, one of the daughters of the river god Asopus. He reigned on the island of Scheria and is named as the father of Alcinous and Arete, though other accounts name a son called Locrus instead.

When Phaeax died, his two sons fell into a quarrel. In the end they came to terms: Alcinous would be king of the Phaeacians, while Locrus would take the heirlooms and part of the people to found a new colony.
