honorific title of female deities in Hinduism

Bhagavatī

Bhagavatī is a Sanskrit-derived title used to honour goddesses in Hinduism, and also in Jainism and Buddhism. In Hinduism it is applied mainly to Sarasvatī, Lakshmī and Pārvatī, and it is the feminine counterpart of Bhagavān.

The myth

The term does not name one goddess with a story of her own; it is a reverent form of address applied to several female divinities. Its myths therefore depend on the goddess addressed: Hindu sources link it with Sarasvatī, Lakshmī and Pārvatī, while in Mahayana Buddhism it appears in the names of goddesses such as Cundā.

It is also treated as equivalent to other titles such as Devī and Īshvarī, so in many local traditions the word works as a general name for the great goddess.

Worship and religion

Worship of the Bhagavatīs centres on temples called Bhagavati Kshetram in Kerala and on many shrines in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh and Goa, where the goddess is often honoured as Mahishasuramardinī. Busy temples also stand in Nepal and in the Mithila region, where they are called Maharani Sthan. Their worship remains very much alive.
