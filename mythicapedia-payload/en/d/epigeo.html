Greek hero of the Iliad, Myrmidon warrior

Epeigeus

Epeigeus (Epigeus) is a hero of the Iliad, one of the finest warriors in the Myrmidon army of Achilles during the Trojan War. His death at the hands of Hector gives Homer a grim picture of the fighting over fallen bodies.

The myth

According to the Iliad, Epeigeus was the son of Agacles and once ruled Budeum, a city of old. After killing a kinsman, he fled and took refuge as a suppliant with Peleus and the sea goddess Thetis, who sent him to Troy to accompany their son Achilles.

During the siege, the Trojans pushed the Achaeans back, and Epeigeus fell in the fighting. As he tried to lift the body of a fallen man, Hector struck his head with a stone, splitting his helmet and dropping him dead across the corpse. Homer describes the death in grim detail.

Legacy

A Trojan asteroid, 5259 Epeigeus, is named after him, one of the few modern traces of this minor figure from the epic.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Iliad, where he is named as a Myrmidon hero killed by Hector.
