Theban prince, son of Cadmus

Polydorus

Polydorus is a Theban prince of Greek myth, the youngest son of Cadmus and Harmonia and father of Labdacus. He matters because he links the founding line of Thebes with the generations that followed.

The myth

According to tradition, Polydorus was the only son among the children of Cadmus and Harmonia, and his sisters were Autonoë, Ino, Agave and Semele. Accounts differ on how he came to the throne. In some, Cadmus handed him the rule of Thebes when the family moved to Illyria; in others, he went into exile with his father; in others, Pentheus, son of Echion and Agave, seized the throne once Cadmus had left the city. Pentheus never reigned as king, but his birth and his friendship with Polydorus gave him considerable influence in Thebes.

Polydorus did become king. After Pentheus' death, Cadmus and Harmonia left Thebes and settled among the Enchelii, and Polydorus ruled in his place. He married Nycteis, daughter of Nycteus, and had a son, Labdacus. Greek authors do not say how he died. Pausanias reports that, feeling his end was near, he recommended his son as successor. A rare version makes him a son of Agenor and grandson of Belus.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Diodorus Siculus and Nonnus of Panopolis preserve different versions of his story and of his place in the Theban royal house.

Trivia

A log from heaven. Along with the thunderbolt that struck Semele's bridal chamber, a log is said to have fallen from the sky. Polydorus decorated it with bronze and named it Cadmeus.
A second exile. According to Diodorus, the Thebans were exiled a second time when Polydorus returned, dissatisfied with the misfortunes that had befallen Amphion, the previous king, and his children.
