lake monster of Okanagan Lake (Canada)

Ogopogo

The Ogopogo is a lake monster said to inhabit Okanagan Lake in British Columbia, Canada, in Canadian folklore. It matters because it blends the traditions of the region's First Nations with modern interest in cryptozoology and with the tourist image of the valley.

The myth

For the Secwepemc and Syilx peoples, the creature was called Naitaka, a supernatural water being that demanded a live sacrifice from anyone who wanted to cross the lake. According to oral tradition, the chief Timbasket refused the offering, and when he entered the lake in a canoe with his family, the monster whipped the surface with its tail and sank them.

Accounts differ. Some describe it as an evil spirit, while the Syilx understand it as a protective spirit that dwelt in caves beneath Rattlesnake Island and deserved respect rather than fear. In 1855, settler John MacDougal claimed his horses had been dragged under the water. In the twentieth century the creature took on its modern form as a long, dark serpent that leaves humps moving across the surface.

Worship and religion

For centuries, people in the area offered small animals before crossing the lake as a sign of respect to Naitaka. Today, local First Nations still regard the creature as part of reality rather than a mere story.

Legacy

The Ogopogo appears in advertising and in the tourist image of the Okanagan. In 1980, a tourist filmed a few seconds of a supposed monster off Kelowna, and its sightings reached Time magazine and the television show Unsolved Mysteries.

Trivia

Music-hall name. The colonial name may come from an English music-hall song heard in Vernon in 1924, although a version of the song already appeared in a Vancouver newspaper in 1912.
Affectionate nicknames. The creature is sometimes called Oggy, and smaller creatures are called Ogopups.
2005 review. A 2005 investigation using surveyor boats concluded that the animal was much closer to shore than believed, so its size had been exaggerated. It was probably a real animal, such as a waterfowl, an otter or a beaver.
