Ephialtes

Ephialtes is a giant of Greek mythology whose name means "nightmare". He fought in the Gigantomachy, the war of the giants against Zeus and the Olympian gods, and died at the hands of Apollo and Heracles.

The myth

According to Pseudo-Apollodorus, Ephialtes was born from Gaia and from the blood of Uranus spilled when Cronus castrated him. Hyginus, by contrast, makes him a son of Gaia and Tartarus. Like the other giants, he was enormously large and strong, with a shaggy head of hair and beard, human in appearance except for his legs, which ended in serpent tails. He was immortal unless a god and a mortal worked together to kill him.

In the Gigantomachy, Ephialtes fell to Apollo, who shot an arrow into his left eye, and to Heracles, who finished him with another arrow in his right eye. He should not be confused with another giant of the same name, one of the Aloadae.

Legacy

Dante includes him in his Divine Comedy, where Virgil and the poet meet this giant in the underworld.

Ancient sources: Pseudo-Apollodorus's Library and Hyginus's Preface, which both name him as a son of Gaia, though they differ about his father.

Trivia

Early archaeology. The oldest archaeological remains naming Ephialtes are Attic black-figure vases, dated to around 575-525 BC.
Two Ephialtes. Another giant named Ephialtes, one of the Aloadae, exists, so the two characters should be kept apart.
