legendary hero and founder of Trier

Trebeta

Trebeta is a legendary hero of Germanic tradition to whom a medieval chronicle credits the founding of Trier. His story, with no historical basis, places the city's origin independently of Rome.

The myth

According to the Gesta Treverorum, written around 1105, Trebeta was the son of the Assyrian king Ninus and of a Chaldean princess who had been Ninus's wife before he met Semiramis. After his father's death, Semiramis seized the throne and forced him into exile in Europe.

With a band of colonists he settled on the banks of the Moselle and founded a city that bears his name, 1300 years before Rome. When he died, his body was cremated on the Petrisberg by the people of Trier.

Legacy

The German historian Johannes Aventinus rejected his parentage by Ninus. He considered Trebeta a son of Mannus, a legendary second king of Germany, and credited him with founding Metz, Mainz, Basel, Strasbourg, Speyer and Worms. Another legend, upheld by chroniclers of the 15th and 16th centuries, also credits him with founding Strasbourg.

Ancient sources: Gesta Treverorum, a medieval chronicle of the 12th century; no ancient texts mention him.

Trivia

An inscription in Trier. On the Rotes Haus, in the central market square of Trier, an inscription commemorating the city's founding by Trebeta has been preserved.
A king invented by the Greeks. According to the tradition, King Ninus was a legendary Assyrian monarch invented by the ancient Greeks, and his first mention is attributed to Ctesias.
