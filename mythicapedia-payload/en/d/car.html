eponymous hero of Caria

Car

Car is the eponymous hero of Caria, in southwestern Anatolia, and the ancestor of the Carians. He was credited with inventing augury from the flight of birds.

The myth

According to tradition, Car was the son of Zeus and Crete and the brother of Lydus and Mysus, the eponymous heroes of the Lydians and Mysians, as Herodotus recounts. Pliny the Elder credited him with inventing the auspicia, the practice of reading omens from birds.

Car founded the city of Alabanda, which took its name from his son Alabandus, born to him and Callirhoe, daughter of the river god Maeander. That name supposedly commemorated a victory Car won in a horse race: according to the scholar Stephanus of Byzantium, the Carian word alabandos meant "winner in a horse fight". Another son, Idrieus, gave his name to the city of Idrias. When Car died, his tomb stood in the Carian city of Souangela, which owed its name to it, since in Carian the word meant "tomb of the king".

Ancient sources: Herodotus, Pliny the Elder and Aelian, plus the scholar Stephanus of Byzantium for the Carian place names.

Trivia

A city named after a son. Idrias was named after Idrieus, another son of Car. Carian cities often took the names of their legendary founders.
A tomb that named a city. Souangela, according to Stephanus of Byzantium, meant "tomb of the king" in Carian, after Car's burial place.
