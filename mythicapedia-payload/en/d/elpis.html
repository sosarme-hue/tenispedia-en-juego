minor goddess of hope in Greek mythology

Elpis

Elpis is the personification of hope in Greek mythology, a minor, allegorical divinity. She matters chiefly because of the myth of Pandora, in which she is the only good thing left inside the jar of evils.

The myth

In Hesiod's account, Pandora brought a large jar as a wedding gift, and when she opened it out of curiosity, it released a host of human ills. Before the lid could be secured again, only Elpis remained inside, staying under the rim of the jar and not flying away, by the will of Zeus.

The meaning of that ending has been debated. Was hope a comfort left to people in their misery, or an idle expectation that kept the lazy from working? The classical commentator Willem Jacob Verdenius framed the question and concluded that one cannot escape the mind of Zeus.

Theognis tells the reverse story: the good spirits, Elpis among them, fled from the jar to heaven and left humans to their fate. In a later fable, Babrius says the jar held blessings meant for humanity that then returned to the heavens; in that version Elpis is a divine gift kept on earth.

Worship and religion

Elpis was never the centre of a cult of her own, unlike Spes, her Roman equivalent, who received worship and appears on coins and in statues. In Greece her presence is mainly literary and tied to origin myths.

Legacy

The phrase "Pandora's box" comes from this myth and is used for something that, once opened, sets off many problems. The Latin formula Spes Ultima Dea, "hope is the last goddess", is often seen as an echo of the story, since Elpis is the last thing left in the jar.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Works and Days is the main source; Theognis, Sophocles in Oedipus Rex, Euripides in The Suppliants, Pindar and Babrius also mention her.

Trivia

Sweet hope. Pindar gives Elpis the adjective "sweet", showing that the Greek view of her was not only negative.
A Roman goddess with cult. Spes, her Roman equivalent, was shown as a young maiden holding a flower in her right hand and lifting the hem of her skirt with her left.
Pandora in reverse. In Babrius's fable, the jar held blessings for humans that flew back to heaven, so Elpis stays on earth as a divine gift.
