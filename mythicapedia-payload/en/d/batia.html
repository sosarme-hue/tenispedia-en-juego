wife of King Dardanus in Greek mythology

Batea

Batea is a figure of Greek mythology, the wife of King Dardanus and mother of several sons who gave their names to lineages of the Troad. Her story links the Teucrians, the people of her father, with the royal line that settled in the region of Troy.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Batea, in Greek Bateia, was the daughter of King Teucer, who ruled the Teucrians, a people who lived in the northwest of Asia Minor, an area later called the Troad. Some accounts make her Teucer's aunt rather than his daughter, and another makes her the daughter of Tros. Teucer named Dardanus, son of Zeus and Electra, as his heir, and Batea married him.

With Dardanus she had four children: Ilus, Erichthonius, Zacynthus and Ideus. Some myths give Dardanus's wife the name Arisbe, a daughter of Teucer, although most writers assume they are the same person. Some versions add that Batea may be identified with the Amazon queen Myrina, a reading not all sources accept.

Batea gave her name to a hill in the Troad mentioned in the Iliad, and also to the town of Bateia.

Legacy

Her name remained attached to the geography of the Troad, where a hill and a town were named after her. She is an example of how ancient stories tied mythical figures to specific places in the landscape and in local memory.

Ancient sources: The Iliad mentions the hill in the Troad that bears her name.

Trivia

One woman, several names. Some accounts call Dardanus's wife Arisbe, daughter of Teucer, and most writers consider her the same person as Batea.
An Amazon queen. Some versions identify Batea with the Amazon queen Myrina, although not all sources accept this.
