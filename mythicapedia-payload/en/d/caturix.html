Celtic war god of the Helvetii

Caturix

Caturix was the war god of the Helvetii, a Celtic people who lived in what is now western Switzerland. His name, meaning "battle king", led to his identification with Mars under the name Mars Caturix in Gallo-Roman religion.

The myth

The surviving texts about Caturix are scarce, and they do not narrate any real stories. He is known mainly through his name and the place he held in the worship of his people, so there is no detailed account of his deeds.

The name comes from the Gaulish catu-rix, built from the root catu- ("battle") and rix ("king"). The same root appears in later Celtic words, such as Old Irish cath ("battle"). Scholars think Caturix may have begun as an epithet of Toutatis, another Celtic god, before gaining an identity of his own.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped in Helvetian territory, with a temple to Mars Caturix at Aventicum, capital of Roman Helvetia, and another at Nonfoux, near Yverdon. Five dedicatory inscriptions found near Avenches and Yverdon attest to this devotion, and a sixth, found at Böckingen in Germany, suggests that his cult spread beyond the region. Other epithets of his were Cicollus and Caisivus.

Legacy

The name of the Gallic tribe of the Caturiges, who settled in the area of modern Chorges, is apparently derived from that of this god, suggesting a close relationship between the two peoples.

Ancient sources: Caturix does not appear in the great literary texts of antiquity; he is known mainly from Gallo-Roman epigraphic inscriptions.

Trivia

A name that is almost a sentence. Caturix means "battle king", and the same meaning appears in the name of the Caturiges, the tribe that gave its name to the Chorges area.
Two cities with the same name. The capital of the Caturiges was called Eburodunum, modern Embrun, the same name as Yverdon, which points to a close relationship between the two peoples.
