legendary lost city of Bedouin lore in the Arabian desert

Atlantis of the Sands

Atlantis of the Sands is a legendary lost city of Bedouin tradition, said to lie in the southern deserts of Arabia and known also as Ubar, Wabar or Iram. It matters for the fascination it has held over explorers and archaeologists and for the debate over whether it ever existed.

The myth

According to Bedouin accounts, Ubar was a wealthy city with date gardens and a fort of red silver, whose wicked people drew the wrath of God, and it was destroyed and buried beneath the sands.

Some identify it with Iram, the city of lofty pillars mentioned in the Quran in the chapter of the Dawn, although there is no agreement that both names refer to the same place.

Legacy

The story was popularised by Ranulph Fiennes's book, and the mystery has inspired books, films and websites. The English name is commonly credited to T. E. Lawrence, though it never appears in his works. In 1992, a team led by Nicholas Clapp announced a find at Shisr in Oman, a claim still debated today.

Ancient sources: The Quran mentions Iram, the city of the pillars, but does not clearly identify it with Ubar.

Trivia

A name without a clear author. Although the English name is credited to T. E. Lawrence, it never appears in his published writings.
A route never followed. Bertram Thomas marked on a map a track said to lead to Ubar, but he was never able to return and follow it.
