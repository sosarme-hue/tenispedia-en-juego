second tirthankara of Jainism

Ajitanatha

Ajitanatha is, in Jainism, the second tirthankara of the present cosmic age, a liberated teacher who showed the path out of the cycle of rebirth. He remains a central figure in Jain theology, and his veneration spans much of the Indian subcontinent.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, Ajitanatha was born in Ayodhya to King Jitashatru and Queen Vijaya, within the ancient Ikshvaku dynasty. His name means "invincible": it is said that while he was in his mother's womb, the queen became so powerful that she always won against her husband at dice.

After ruling his kingdom, he renounced worldly attachments and became an ascetic. He attained omniscience while meditating beneath a sal tree and founded a great four-part monastic order with 90 chief disciples, Simhasena being the principal leader. After a long period of teaching, he attained liberation on the peaks of Mount Shikharji, together with a thousand other monks.

Worship and religion

Ajitanatha is traditionally shown in a meditative pose with a golden complexion, and is identified by the elephant carved on the pedestal of his images. His main pilgrimage site is Shikharji, where his footprints are venerated by both Digambara and Svetambara followers. Notable centres of worship include the Mathura Chaurasi complex in Uttar Pradesh and the Taranga temple in Gujarat.

Legacy

His life and teachings inspired the poet Ranna, who composed a Kannada epic about him in 993 CE. The Ajitashanti, attributed to Nandisena, remains a fundamental devotional text in Jainism.

Ancient sources: Medieval Jain sources, such as Ranna's Kannada epic and Nandisena's Ajitashanti, are his main references; the name also appears in the Vedas.

Trivia

An inherited name. The name Ajita appears in the Vedas, although modern Indologists attribute it to shared ancient Indian naming rather than to historical proof of his existence.
An elephant emblem. He is identified by the elephant carved on the pedestal of his images, a feature that distinguishes him from other tirthankaras.
