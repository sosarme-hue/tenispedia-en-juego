Zoroastrian yazata of the Radiant Sun

Hvare-khshaeta

Hvare-khshaeta is the Zoroastrian yazata (divinity) of the «Radiant Sun», whose Avestan name survives in modern Persian as Khorshīd. He matters because he is the original solar figure of the Avestan tradition, before Mithra took over much of that role.

The myth

The surviving texts offer more epithets and mentions than extended stories. The sixth Yasht, a short hymn of seven verses, is dedicated to him, and the Avesta also includes a litany to the Sun. Tradition holds that Mithra eventually eclipsed him as the god of the Sun; scholars attribute this shift to late syncretic influences, perhaps a conflation with the Akkadian god Shamash. Even so, in scripture the Sun remains unmistakably his domain, distinct from the divinity of the «Covenant».

Worship and religion

He is dedicated the eleventh day of the Zoroastrian calendar, which falls under his protection.

Legacy

His name lives on in modern Persian as Khorshīd. He has been linked etymologically to Hors, a Slavic solar deity, though that connection has been questioned.

Ancient sources: The Avesta, notably the sixth Yasht and the litany to the Sun.
