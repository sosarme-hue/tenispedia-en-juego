pair of Taoist immortals of harmony and union

He He Er Xian

The He He Er Xian, or Immortals of Harmony and Union, are a pair of Chinese immortals linked to happy marriages. They are usually shown as two boys holding a lotus flower and a box, two objects whose Chinese names sound like the word for harmony.

The myth

According to legend, the two celestial beings are linked to two monks who lived in seclusion on Tiantai Mountain during the Tang dynasty, Hanshan and Shide, whose later whereabouts remain unknown. The story draws on poems attributed to both men.

Over time, the figures of those monks were transformed into two celestial spirits who bless love between husband and wife.

Worship and religion

In the Qing dynasty they were officially recognized as the gods of harmony and good union, in the first year of the Yongzheng reign. They are worshipped in Taoist temples, where statues of them stand, such as the one at the Changchun temple in Wuhan.

Legacy

Their image has also been captured in painting, as in the work of Wang Wen from the Ming dynasty.

Trivia

A play on words. The lotus and the box are pronounced the same as the Chinese word for harmony, which is why they serve as symbols of union.
