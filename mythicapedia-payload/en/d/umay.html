mother goddess of fertility in Turkic mythology

Umay

Umay is the goddess of fertility in Turkic mythology and Tengrism, protector of women, mothers and children. Her name, linked to the Turkic word for placenta, places her at the heart of family life and of khagan power.

The myth

According to tradition, Umay lives in heaven and is invisible to ordinary people. There she keeps the souls of unborn babies, in a sacred place associated with Mount Ymay-tas or Amay. She protects and educates infants, but she may also separate the souls of the dead from their bodies, especially those of small children.

She is almost always shown with a child and with sixty golden tresses that resemble the rays of the sun. That is why she is called Sarı Kız, the Yellow Maiden, and yellow is her colour. Some accounts identify her with Od iyesi, and from her comes the essence of fire, Od Ana. If she leaves a child alone for too long, the child falls ill and shamans must call her back. A sleeping baby's smile shows she is near; crying means she has gone.

Worship and religion

In the Turkic empire, the katuns, the wives of the khagans, were regarded as embodiments of Umay, and the continuation of the dynasty was thought to depend on her favour. According to the Divanü Lügat'it-Türk, women who worshipped her had sons. Turkic women tied strings with small cradles to attract a baby. The sources do not describe how her cult is practised today.

Legacy

Umay and Ece are common female given names in the Republic of Turkey. At the Teos archaeological site stands an olive tree believed to be about 1,800 years old, named Umay Nine or Nana Umay, a symbol of birth, growth and the cycle of life.

Ancient sources: The eighth-century Kul Tigin inscription and the Orkhon inscriptions present her as a protective mother, and the Divanü Lügat'it-Türk records her rites.

Trivia

Yellow colour. Umay is associated with the sun, so yellow is her colour and she is called the Yellow Maiden.
A sacred olive tree. At the Teos site, an olive tree of about 1,800 years bears the name Umay Nine.
