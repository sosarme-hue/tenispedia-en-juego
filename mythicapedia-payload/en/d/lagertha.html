Norse shieldmaiden and legendary ruler

Lagertha

Lagertha is a skjaldmö (shieldmaiden) of Norse tradition, a legendary figure recorded mainly by Saxo Grammaticus. She matters because she is one of the best-known examples of a woman warrior in Scandinavian culture and the first wife of Ragnar Lothbrok.

The myth

According to the tale recorded by Saxo Grammaticus in Book IX of his Gesta Danorum, Lagertha's career as a warrior began when a king named Frø invaded Norway and killed King Siward. Frø sent the women of the dead king's family to a brothel to humiliate them. Hearing of this, Ragnar Lothbrok came with an army to avenge his grandfather Siward. Many of those women dressed in men's clothing and fought at his side, and Lagertha stood out, her courage key to the victory.

Impressed, Ragnar courted her from afar. She feigned interest, and when he came to ask for her hand, he had to overcome a bear and a huge hound that she had set to guard her home. They married and had a son, Fridleif, and two daughters. Later Ragnar divorced her to marry Þóra borgarhjǫrtr. When he returned to Denmark amid civil war, he asked Norway for help, and Lagertha came with 120 ships. Later, after a quarrel, she killed him with a spearhead hidden in her gown. According to Saxo, she then ruled alone, preferring to reign without a husband rather than share the throne.

Legacy

Lagertha's story inspired the historical drama Lagertha (1789) by Christen Pram and the ballet of the same name by Vincenzo Galeotti (1801), the first ballet with a Nordic theme. In the twenty-first century, actress Katheryn Winnick played her in the TV series Vikings (2013), where she starts as a shieldmaiden and later rules as a jarl and queen. She also appears in the Final Fantasy XIV expansion Evercold, as second-in-command of the Realmship Naglfar.

Ancient sources: Saxo Grammaticus, Gesta Danorum, Book IX, written in the late twelfth century. Other Norse texts, such as the Flateyjarbók, mention the goddess Thorgerd, with whom some scholars link her.

Trivia

Flight like a valkyrie. Saxo says Lagertha flew around to the rear of the enemy. According to Judith Jesch, this trait links her to the valkyries.
Possible divine origin. Hilda Ellis Davidson considered it possible that Lagertha is the same figure as the goddess Þorgerðr Hǫlgabúðr, venerated near Lade in Norway.
