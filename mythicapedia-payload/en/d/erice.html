king of the Elymians in Sicily, son of Butes and Aphrodite

Eryx

Eryx (in ancient Greek Éryx) was a king of the Elymians, a people of Sicily, in Greek mythology. He is remembered for his strength as a boxer and for his defeat by Heracles, and his name gave rise to the mountain and the town of Erice, in the northwestern corner of the island.

The myth

According to tradition, Eryx was the son of Butes and Aphrodite, although some accounts make him a son of Venus and Neptune, the Latin names of the same gods. Confident in his strength, he challenged everyone who reached his kingdom and killed those he defeated.

One day he dared to face Heracles, who was passing through Sicily with the cattle of Geryon. They wagered the hero's herd against Eryx's kingdom. He accepted the very lopsided bet after learning that if Heracles lost the cattle, he would also lose his hope of immortality. But he was defeated and buried in the temple of his mother Aphrodite, on the mountain that bears his name. Before leaving, Heracles left the kingdom to its inhabitants and announced that one of his descendants, a Heraclid, would return to claim it. He is also credited with a son, Psophis.

Worship and religion

On the summit of the mountain stood a sanctuary of Aphrodite, who took the place of the Phoenician god Melqart, whom the Greeks identified with Heracles. This shift shows how the deities of different peoples overlapped and adapted to one another on the island.

Legacy

The mountain and the town of Erice still keep his name, and that is his most lasting mark.

Trivia

A mountain with his name. The mountain where he was buried keeps his name, and the town of Erice stands on it.
A promise of return. Heracles announced that a Heraclid would come to claim the kingdom, linking the hero to the island's story.
