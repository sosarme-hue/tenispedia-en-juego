Chinese god of war

Chiyou

Chiyou is a figure of Chinese mythology, leader of the Nine Li tribe (Jiuli), who lost a decisive struggle against the Yellow Emperor. Once a defeated king, he became a war god and a metalworking hero, and today several peoples claim him as an ancestor.

The myth

According to tradition, Chiyou was a fearsome chief with four eyes, six arms and a head of bronze with an iron forehead; he was said to have eighty-one brothers and to have made the first weapons, forging swords of bronze and copper.

In the Miao version, he fought the Yellow Emperor at the Battle of Zhuolu. Chiyou shrouded the battlefield in thick fog, but the Emperor's troops found their way with a south-pointing chariot. Once captured, he was killed by the winged dragon Yinglong, and his chains turned into oak trees.

Chinese accounts agree on the defeat but differ in details. Afterwards his people scattered, some southwest and some southeast, and the Miao and Li peoples are said to descend from them.

Worship and religion

In East Asia he was worshipped as a guardian god of war. According to Sima Qian, the Han emperor Gaozu prayed at his tomb before defeating Xiang Yu. His veneration survives in some popular circles in modern China.

Legacy

He appears in Taoist lore as a demon, and as a protector of soldiers. He is also a motif on ritual bronzes and in modern popular culture, including video games and comics.

Ancient sources: Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian and the Lushi recount his story and his battle against the Yellow Emperor.

Trivia

Father of metal. Some sources credit him with inventing metal weapons and forging the first swords of bronze and copper.
Rain of blood. According to some versions, blood rained for a time after his death.
