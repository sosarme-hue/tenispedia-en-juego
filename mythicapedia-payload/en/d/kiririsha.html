Elamite mother goddess, lady of Liyan

Kiririsha

Kiririsha, whose name means «great goddess», was one of the leading deities of Elamite religion and the «Lady of Liyan». She formed triads with Napirisha and Inshushinak, and was called mother of the gods, a protector of fertility, war and kings.

The myth

Few extended myths survive about her. Texts present her as the wife of Napirisha and the mother of Hutran, «the mighty», forming a divine family.

She was associated with protecting water and earth, according to an inscription of Hanni of Ayapir. Her link to death is debated: the epithet «lady of death in Liyan» has been translated in different ways, and some scholars prefer «hidden».

Worship and religion

She was the tutelary goddess of Liyan, near modern Bushehr, where a great temple stood, and she had shrines in Susa, Anshan and Chogha Zanbil. Kings dedicated temples and statues to her, and offerings included weapons, animal figurines and jewelry. Temples were also dedicated to her in groves. Her cult disappeared with Elamite civilization.

Ancient sources: Proto-Elamite texts from the 19th-18th centuries BCE, votive inscriptions, the stele of Shilhak-Inshushinak and the inscriptions of Untash-Napirisha.

Trivia

Buried palm trees. Excavations near Bushehr uncovered a custom of «burying» palm trees, which researchers tentatively link to her cult.
A royal curse. She appears in a curse carved on the statue of Napir-Asu, the king's principal wife, alongside Napirisha and Inshushinak.
