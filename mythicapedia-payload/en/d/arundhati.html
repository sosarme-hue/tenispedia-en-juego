wife of the sage Vasishtha in Hindu mythology

Arundhati

Arundhati is, in Hindu mythology, the wife of the sage Vasishtha, one of the seven sages (saptarishi). She is a model of marital devotion, and her name is linked to the star Alcor, which forms a double star with Mizar in the Big Dipper.

The myth

According to the Shiva Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, Arundhati was one of the nine daughters of the sage Kardama and Devahuti, and her father gave her in marriage to Vasishtha. Other Puranic traditions make her a daughter of Kashyapa and sister of Narada and Parvata, and say that Narada offered her to the sage. In an earlier life she had been Sandhya, a daughter born from Brahma's mind; to purify herself of passion she performed penance, and Shiva told her to leap into the fire of the sage Medhatithi, from which she was reborn as his daughter.

The Mahabharata portrays her as an ascetic who even taught the seven sages, and it notes that Svaha, the wife of Agni, could take the form of the wives of the other six, but not Arundhati's. In Valmiki's Ramayana she bore a hundred sons, all cursed to die by Vishvamitra in his rivalry with Vasishtha; she later had Shakti and then Suyagya. Some sources speak of eight sons instead. The same epic recounts that, when twelve years without rain left the seven sages without roots or fruit, she won Shiva's favour.

Worship and religion

Although she is the wife of a sage, she is worshipped together with the seven sages and accorded the same status. In the Hindu wedding, after the seven steps (saptapadi), the groom shows the bride the double stars of Vasishtha and Arundhati as a model of a couple. In some regions of India, wives whose husbands are alive observe a fast in her honour on the second bright day of the lunar month of Chaitra, believing it will spare them from widowhood.

Legacy

The maxim Arundhati darshana nyaya ('seeing Arundhati by inference') teaches moving from the known to the unknown, and it is named after her. Kalidasa mentions her in the sixth canto of the Kumarasambhava, where she pleads with Shiva to marry Parvati, and the Hindi epic poem Arundhati by Jagadguru Rambhadracharya (1994) tells her life.

Ancient sources: Atharva Veda, Valmiki's Ramayana, Mahabharata, Shiva Purana, Bhagavata Purana and Kalidasa's Kumarasambhava.

Trivia

A hard-to-see star. Alcor is so faint that, according to a belief, someone near death can no longer make it out.
Showing her in steps. To locate her, brighter stars are pointed out first, and from them the gaze is guided toward the faint Arundhati.
