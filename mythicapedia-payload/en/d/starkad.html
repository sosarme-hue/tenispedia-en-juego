legendary hero of Norse mythology

Starkad

Starkad is a legendary hero of Norse mythology, a formidable warrior of dubious conduct who, in some versions, was an eight-armed giant. He appears in the Gesta Danorum and several Icelandic sagas, which blend his heroic deeds with many crimes.

The myth

According to Hervarar saga, Starkad Ala-Warrior, his ancestor, lived in Norway by the waterfalls of Alufoss. He was an eight-armed giant who kidnapped Álfhildr, daughter of King Álfr, and Thor killed him to rescue her.

His grandson Starkad was raised at the court of Harald of Agder alongside the prince Vikar, whom he helped avenge his father. According to Gautrek's saga, Odin and Thor bestowed conflicting blessings and curses on him: Thor condemned him to crime and childlessness, while Odin gave him riches, victory and the gift of poetry. In the end, Starkad delivered Vikar to Odin, who sacrificed him with a spear disguised as a reed.

Legacy

His story appears in the Anglo-Saxon poem Beowulf, the Gesta Danorum and the Icelandic sagas, where he remained a figure of the wandering warrior with a grim fate.

Ancient sources: Gesta Danorum, the Hervarar, Gautrek, Ynglinga and Sögubrot sagas, Skáldskaparmál and the poem Beowulf.

Trivia

Eight arms. Some versions describe him as a giant with eight arms, and in Hervarar saga he uses four swords at once in a duel.
Origin in Beowulf. His legend has a precedent in the Anglo-Saxon poem Beowulf, where an unnamed warrior urges the Heathobards to take revenge.
