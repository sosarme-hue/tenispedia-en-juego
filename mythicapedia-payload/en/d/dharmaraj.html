Hindu god of death and justice in Bengal

Dharmaraj

Dharmaraj, also called Dharma, is a Hindu deity of death and justice worshipped by villagers in the Rarh region of what is now the Indian state of West Bengal, as one of their local village gods (gram devata).

The myth

There is no single account of his origin. According to tradition, some texts present Dharma as the supreme deity, creator and ordainer of the universe, above Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, though he is sometimes identified with them. He has also been linked with Surya, Varuna, Yama and Buddhism. Some scholars believe his roots lie in ancient harvest-related beliefs that predate Aryan traditions.

Worship and religion

He is represented by a shapeless stone smeared with vermilion, placed under a tree or in the open, though sometimes kept in a temple. He is worshipped mainly in the months of Baisakh, Jaistha and Asarh, on full moon days. His festival, the gajan, features terracotta horses as symbolic offerings and a skull dance. His worship remains alive in rural communities of Bengal.

Ancient sources: Works by Brahmin authors, such as the Dharma Purana and the Dharmamangal of Mayura Bhatta, were written as devotional texts in his honour.

Trivia

Terracotta horses. In the Rarh region countless terracotta and wooden horses are used as symbolic offerings, something scholars consider peculiar to Dharmaraj.
A culture of many layers. Anthropologist Frank Korom of Boston University wrote his PhD dissertation on Dharmaraj and found that he is perceived and worshipped differently from district to district.
