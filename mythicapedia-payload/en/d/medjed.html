minor god of Egyptian religion

Medjed

Medjed, also known as Medjedu, is a minor deity of ancient Egypt mentioned in some copies of the Book of the Dead. Almost nothing is known about him beyond one brief passage, but his ghostly image has made him a well-known figure in Japanese popular culture.

The myth

Medjed appears in spell 17 of the Book of the Dead, a collection of funerary texts with magic spells meant to help the deceased pass the judgment of Osiris and travel through the Duat, the underworld, to the paradise of Aaru. The spell names him among other obscure deities of the House of Osiris. His name means roughly "The Smiter", and he is described as one who shoots with his eye yet cannot be seen.

No other stories about him survive. Hermann Grapow suggested he might be a star, since he is said to radiate light and to be linked to the Nile's cyclical flooding, though the name is never written with a star determinative.

Visual depictions are even rarer: they appear on only nine papyrus scrolls, dating to around the Twenty-first Dynasty. There he is a dome with eyes, supported by two human-like feet, sometimes with a red knotted belt. Some scholars read the dome as a shroud or a shapeless body expressing his invisible nature, while others, such as Bernard Bruyère and Terence DuQuesne, see a personified oil jar.

Legacy

Medjed became a sensation in Japan in 2012, when illustrations from the Greenfield Papyrus were exhibited in Tokyo and Fukuoka and spread as an internet meme thanks to his cartoon-ghost look. He has since appeared in video games such as Fate/Grand Order and Persona 5, where he is a hacktivist group, and in anime such as Kamigami no Ki and Oh, Suddenly Egyptian God.

Ancient sources: He appears in spell 17b of the Egyptian Book of the Dead, preserved in funerary papyrus copies.

Trivia

A Japanese meme. His ghost-like image spread across Japanese social media after the 2012 exhibitions.
A hacktivist group. In the video game Persona 5, Medjed is the name of a hacktivist group.
