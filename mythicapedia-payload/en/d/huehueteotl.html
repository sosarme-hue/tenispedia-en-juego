Aztec god of fire

Huehueteotl

Huehueteotl, 'old god' in Nahuatl, is the Aztec and Mesoamerican deity of fire, usually shown as a bent old man carrying a brazier on his back. One of the oldest and most revered gods of Mesoamerica, he was tied to the household hearth, the centre of the world and the renewal of life.

The myth

According to an Aztec legend, a man and a woman discovered fire and roasted fish to feed themselves. The gods, outraged by their audacity, cut off their heads and turned them into dogs. Since then, people must survive by their own means, and fire symbolises the knowledge that separates humans from the divine.

Tradition holds that he was the last to arrive at the gathering of the gods during the Teotleco feast, while Tezcatlipoca came first. He could take the form of the fire serpent Xiuhcoatl, though sources differ: some identify him with Xiuhtecuhtli, others see him as a separate god. His dual nature links him to the creative pair Ometeotl, and he was invoked as 'mother and father of the gods'.

Worship and religion

His cult is one of the oldest in Mesoamerica, with effigies found at Cuicuilco and Monte Alban. During the monthly Izcalli feast, boys hunted small water creatures and handed them to the elders who guarded the fire, who rewarded them with tamales. In the most dramatic ceremony, the Aztecs burned the hearts of sacrificial victims on coals to win back the god's favour, through his elements of fire and blood. For the Mexica, fire was the regenerator of the world, and its kindling at the end of the fifty-two-year cycle renewed life.

Ancient sources: The Florentine Codex, compiled by Sahagun, names him as an epithet of Xiuhtecuhtli, and incantations recorded by Ruiz de Alarcon and Jacinto de la Serna invoke him as father and mother of the gods.

Trivia

The last to arrive. At the Teotleco feast, Huehueteotl is the last god to appear at the gathering, while Tezcatlipoca arrives first.
A cross of four directions. One of his symbols is the quincunx, a cross radiating from a central point, where the god resides, toward the four directions of the universe.
