two-headed dog of Greek mythology

Orthrus

Orthrus, also spelled Orthus, was a two-headed dog in Greek mythology who guarded Geryon's cattle. He was killed by Heracles during the tenth labour of the hero, and he was the brother of Cerberus and the Lernaean Hydra.

The myth

According to the tradition, Orthrus was the son of Typhon and Echidna. Hesiod, the oldest source, places him among the descendants of Phorcys and Ceto, and says that, together with the Chimera, he fathered the Sphinx of Thebes and the Nemean Lion. His first owner was the Titan Atlas, who gave him to the giant Geryon.

Geryon kept a herd of red cattle on Erytheia, a distant island near the Ocean, guarded by the herdsman Eurytion and the dog. Heracles was sent to steal the cattle. While he spent the night on Mount Abas, the dog attacked him, but the hero struck it with his club and also killed Eurytion, who had come to help Orthrus. Geryon later caught up with Heracles by the river Anthemus.

The number of heads varies. Apollodorus gives him two, as is usually shown, but Hesiod does not specify. Pindar speaks of hounds in the plural, which is unique among ancient authors, and it is unclear whether he meant the heads or a different tradition.

Legacy

Orthrus rarely appears in art, and always in connection with the theft of Geryon's cattle. The oldest piece is a late seventh-century BC bronze horse pectoral from Samos, which shows him at Geryon's feet with an arrow in one head. A red-figure cup by Euphronios shows him lying on his back, his snake tail still writhing, while an Attic black-figure amphora depicts him with a dog's tail.

Ancient sources: Hesiod in the Theogony, Apollodorus in the Library, Pindar, and Quintus of Smyrna, who places him on the shield of Eurypylus alongside Cerberus.

Trivia

A dog with many versions. Orthrus appears with one, two or three heads, and even with a snake tail, depending on the author or the artwork.
Two forms of the name. Hesiod calls him Orthus, while Apollodorus calls him Orthrus.
