Greek hero, son of Sthenelus

Cometes

Cometes is a hero of Greek myth, son of Sthenelus, king of Argos. His story, tied to Diomedes' return from the Trojan War, shows how betrayal and jealousy drove one of the Achaean heroes into exile.

The myth

According to the tradition, Diomedes went off to the Trojan War and left Cometes in charge of his household and kingdom. Cometes, however, began an affair with Aegialeia, Diomedes' wife. The accounts offer several reasons. In one version, Aphrodite, angry because Diomedes had wounded her at Troy, stirred up the adultery and filled Cometes with desire for the queen. Another version holds that the goddess made Aegialeia fall in love with Hippolytus, Cometes' son, rather than with his father.

One tradition links the affair to Nauplius, father of Palamedes, who sought revenge on the Greek leaders after his son was falsely accused and killed by Odysseus, and arranged for their wives to be unfaithful. When Diomedes came home from Troy, he found Aegialeia with Cometes. Cometes chased him with his bodyguard, intending to kill him, and Diomedes fled to Italy. According to the Chiliades, the outcome was the same: the hero abandoned his homeland and emigrated to Italy.

Ancient sources: The Chiliades of Tzetzes record that Diomedes, after discovering his wife's adultery with Cometes, chose to flee to Italy rather than remain in his homeland.
