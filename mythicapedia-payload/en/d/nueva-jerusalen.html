holy city of Judaism and Christianity

New Jerusalem

New Jerusalem is a holy city that, according to tradition, may be literal or figurative, representing the physical or spiritual restoration of Jerusalem. It appears in the prophet Ezekiel's vision and in the Book of Revelation, and it is a central theme in Jewish and Christian eschatology.

The myth

In the Book of Ezekiel, the prophet receives a vision of a city centred on a rebuilt Temple, with twelve gates, one for each tribe of Israel. Zechariah and Isaiah expand the image: in Zechariah the rebuilding depends on God's intervention, and in Isaiah the city is adorned with precious stones and free from terror and oppression. The Book of Enoch, written after the persecutions of Antiochus IV, describes a new house, larger and loftier than the former, replacing the defiled Jerusalem.

The Book of Revelation describes the heavenly city descending from heaven, measuring 12,000 stadia on each side with walls of 144 cubits. These measurements amount to a cube about 2,222 km along each edge. In Jewish mysticism, the heavenly Jerusalem already exists in heaven as the model of the earthly one.

Worship and religion

Orthodox Judaism expects the city to be literally rebuilt in Jerusalem, with the Temple, when the Messiah arrives. Some Christian traditions share this expectation, while many Protestant denominations and modernist branches understand it figuratively. Mormons believe it will be established in Missouri, United States.

Legacy

New Jerusalem has shaped Christian theology, philosophy and mysticism, and it is an important theme in the Baha'i faith. Its image of a perfect city free from oppression continues to inspire religious hope in several communities.

Ancient sources: Ezekiel, Zechariah, Isaiah, the Book of Enoch, the Dead Sea Scrolls and the Book of Revelation.

Trivia

A city of thousands of kilometres. The measurements in Revelation, multiplied by the length of the ancient stadion, give a city about 2,222 km along each side.
A name meaning presence. In Ezekiel the city is called "YHWH Shamma", which can be translated as "YHWH is there".
