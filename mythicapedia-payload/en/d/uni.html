Etruscan supreme goddess of marriage and love

Uni

Uni is the supreme goddess of the Etruscan pantheon, patron of marriage, fertility and family. The Romans identified her with Juno and the Greeks with Hera, and she formed a triad with Tinia and Menrva, a precursor of the Capitoline Triad.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Uni is the wife of Tinia, god of the sky, and mother of Hercle and Menrva. Most of what survives about her comes from engraved bronze mirrors, as little Etruscan literature has come down to us.

The best-known scene shows Uni nursing an adult Hercle while Tinia points to an inscription stating that Hercle has become Uni's son. Many scholars read it as the demigod's initiation into godhood, or as a reconciliation. In the Greek myth, Hera was tricked by Zeus into nursing an infant Heracles; the Etruscan version features an adult Hercle and a willing Uni.

Another motif is the Judgement of Elcsntre, the Etruscan version of the Judgement of Paris. Here Uni appears differently from her Greek counterpart: nude or clothed, holding a pomegranate branch with three fruits, which links her to fertility rather than power.

Worship and religion

Uni was worshipped both in dedicated shrines and alongside other deities in large public sanctuaries across Etruria. At Pyrgi, a shrine built around 500 BC held hundreds of votive objects and animal bones suggesting sacrifice; three gold plaques found in 1964, two in Etruscan and one in Phoenician, identify her with Astarte under the name Unialastres. At Gravisca, a 6th-century BC port sanctuary, a room was dedicated to her with statuettes of swaddled babies and many anatomical votives such as wombs and breasts, linked to fertility.

Ancient sources: Livy, in his Ab Urbe Condita (Book V), says that Juno, an Etruscan goddess of the Veientes, was ceremonially adopted by Rome after the sack of Veii in 396 BC, a passage usually connected with Uni. She also appears on the Liver of Piacenza.

Trivia

A name fused with Astarte. At Pyrgi, a Phoenician and an Etruscan inscription name her as Unialastres, a blend of Uni and Astarte.
Votive wombs. At the Gravisca sanctuary, 145 votive wombs were found dedicated to Uni, compared with 74 for Turan.
