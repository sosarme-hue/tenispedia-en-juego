Arcadian prince in Greek mythology

Pereus

Pereus is an Arcadian prince in Greek mythology, the son of King Elatus and Laodice. His role is minor, but his lineage places him within the Arcasid family, one of the heroic lines most closely tied to that region.

The myth

Pereus appears almost always in genealogical lists and has few stories of his own. According to tradition, he was the son of King Elatus and Laodice, daughter of Cinyras. Pausanias states that he had no sons, only a single daughter, Neaera, whom Autolycus took as his wife. Autolycus claimed to be a son of Hermes, although Pausanias says he was really the son of Daedalion. Other authors hold that Neaera married Aleus, son of Aphidas, and the name of Pereus's wife is never recorded.

Apollodorus lists him among the brothers of Stymphalus and Polyphemus, while Pausanias adds Aepytus, Cyllen and Ischys. All of them are famous heroes of the Arcasid line, and Pereus is best understood as part of that family network.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and Apollodorus mention him within the genealogies of the Arcadian heroes.
