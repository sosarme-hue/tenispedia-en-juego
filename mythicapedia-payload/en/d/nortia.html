Etruscan goddess of fate and fortune

Nortia

Nortia was an Etruscan goddess linked to fate, time and fortune, whose cult centred on the city of Volsinii. The Romans identified her with Fortuna, and her story shows how the passing of the year was understood in ancient Italy.

The myth

Almost no Etruscan texts about Nortia survive, so most of what we know comes from Latin sources and inscriptions. Her Latinized name appears in several variant spellings in the manuscripts, and her sphere covered time, destiny and chance.

Her attribute was the nail. According to Livy, at Volsinii nails were fixed in a wall of her temple to mark the number of years passing, a rite tied to the start of the new year. The nails of her temple therefore served to count time.

Worship and religion

Her cult took place at her temple in Volsinii, where the annual driving of a nail marked the New Year. Versnel suggested that the ritual may have been linked to the yearly meeting of the Etruscan league, with Voltumna possibly as her consort. Nothing indicates that this cult survived beyond antiquity.

Legacy

A ruin at Bolsena is known locally as the Tempio di Norzia, though this identification is doubtful and its architecture is Roman. Her nail imagery recalls Necessitas, personified at Rome with nails that Horace describes as unbreakable.

Ancient sources: Livy mentions her in his history of Rome, and the poet Juvenal and the writer Martianus Capella cite her among goddesses of fate and chance.

Trivia

Name with variants. The manuscripts record several spellings of her name, such as Norcia or Nyrtia, which makes the exact form hard to fix.
Nails with dedications. At Ateste, bronze nails bearing dedications were found in a precinct thought to belong to Rehtia, a goddess some compare with Nortia.
