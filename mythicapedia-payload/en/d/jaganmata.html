epithet of the goddess Lakshmi, mother of the universe

Jaganmata

Jaganmata, also called Lokamata, is primarily an epithet of Lakshmi, the supreme goddess of Vaishnavism. The title presents her as the mother of the universe.

The myth

According to tradition, Lakshmi is the divine shakti of Vishnu. Since Vishnu is present everywhere, she serves him as the mother of the universe under his protection. A hymn from the Atharva Veda dedicated to Lakshmi asks that a portion of the Jaganmata dwell upon the devotee's tongue.

The Bhagavata Purana includes a prayer describing Vishnu's form. In it, Lakshmi, the universal mother, tends to the feet of her eternal consort.

In the Lakshmi Tantra, Indra performs a penance lasting two thousand years to meet her. Lakshmi appears to him upon a lotus and is described as the supreme mother of the universe.

Ancient sources: The Atharva Veda, the Bhagavata Purana and the Lakshmi Tantra, which present her as the mother of the universe.

Trivia

A two-thousand-year penance. In the Lakshmi Tantra, Indra needs two thousand years of penance before the goddess appears to him.
An appearance on a lotus. Lakshmi appears before Indra seated on a lotus, a flower often associated with her.
