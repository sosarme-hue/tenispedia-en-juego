Gallo-Roman goddess of the Ardennes forest

Arduinna

Arduinna was the tutelary goddess of the Ardennes forest and region in Gallo-Roman religion, shown as a huntress riding a boar. She was identified with the Roman goddess Diana, and her name still lives on in the geography and culture of the area.

The myth

Arduinna was the eponymous guardian goddess of the Ardennes forest and its territory, which today covers parts of Belgium, Luxembourg and France. She was imagined as a huntress riding a boar, an image that linked her to the dense woodland and to the hunt.

No extended stories about her survive, so her figure is known mainly through her depictions and a handful of inscriptions. Two dedications name her directly: an altar found at Düren in Germany, and a relief in Rome where her name has also been read as Saturn.

A bronze statuette of a woman in a short tunic, riding sidesaddle on a boar and holding a knife, was attributed to Arduinna even though it bears no inscription. Both bronze statuettes of this type are now headless.

Worship and religion

Her cult originated in the Ardennes region and she was identified with Diana, who was worshipped in the same territory. According to the sixth-century historian Gregory of Tours, a preacher named Walfroy tried to persuade the people of Villers-devant-Orval to abandon the worship of Diana, whose large stone statue on a nearby hill drew chants and feasting. After some difficulty, they pulled the statue down with hammers.

Legacy

Her name survives in place names: the Latin Arduenna silva, "wooded heights", gave rise to the Ardennes and appears elsewhere in France. The asteroid 394 Arduina, discovered in 1894, is named after her. In the French-Belgian television series Black Spot, set in the Ardennes, a group of eco warriors calls itself the Children of Arduinna.

Ancient sources: The two inscriptions that name her directly are the altar at Düren and the Roman relief; the account of the cult of Diana in the Ardennes comes from Gregory of Tours, a medieval source.

Trivia

Etymology. The name derives from the Gaulish arduo-, "height", and recurs in the names of several forested mountains in France, not only the modern Ardennes.
A boar as a symbol. The boar is today the modern symbol of the Ardennes region, which may have influenced the attribution of some statuettes to the goddess.
