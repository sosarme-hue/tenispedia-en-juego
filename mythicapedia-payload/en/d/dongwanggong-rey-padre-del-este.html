Taoist god, guardian of the immortals

Dongwanggong (King Father of the East)

Dongwanggong, the King Father of the East, is the tutelary deity of the Taoist immortals and the consort of Xiwangmu, the Queen Mother of the West. He embodies yang energy.

The myth

According to legend, Xiwangmu was a mountain demon who fell in love with him; she ruled the west and he ruled the east. In some versions of the Chinese creation myth, the couple create humanity through their union.

The Shanhaijing describes a bronze pillar on Mount Kunlun, topped by a huge bird named Xiyou, which held them under its wings; she had to climb onto the wing to reach him. Another account describes him with a bird's face, a tiger's tail and white hair, riding a black bear.

Worship and religion

Many Taoist texts present him as born of the Pure and Holy Mother of Origins, and his younger brother is said to be Dongyue Dadi, god of Mount Tai. In the alchemical tradition of the Quanzhen School, Wang Xuanfu is considered his avatar, according to Lü Dongbin.

Legacy

An inscription on a bronze mirror cast in 106 CE pairs him with Xiwangmu, wishing fortune and longevity.

Ancient sources: The Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas) and the Shenyijing (Book of Gods and Wonders) describe him, alongside various Taoist texts.

Trivia

An unusual appearance. Descriptions show him with a bird's face and a tiger's tail, an unusual combination among Chinese divine figures.
A heavenly father. Another legend honors him as the Heavenly Father, with Xiwangmu as the Heavenly Mother; he transforms essence and she transforms blood.
