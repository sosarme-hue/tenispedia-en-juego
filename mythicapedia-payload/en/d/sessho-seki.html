Killing Stone of Japanese mythology

Sessho-seki

The Sessho-seki, or "Killing Stone", is a mythical object in the volcanic mountains of Nasu, Japan. Tradition holds that it kills anyone who touches it, and that it is the transformed corpse of Tamamo-no-Mae, a woman revealed to be a nine-tailed fox.

The myth

According to the legend, Tamamo-no-Mae was a beautiful woman serving an evil daimyō who plotted to kill Emperor Konoe and seize the throne. She was exposed as a nine-tailed fox, and when the warrior Miura-no-suke killed her, her body became the Killing Stone, as the Otogi-zōshi recounts.

Later, the Buddhist priest Genno rested beside the stone and was threatened by the fox's spirit. He performed exorcism rituals and begged her to consider her salvation. Tamamo-no-Mae relented and promised never to haunt the stone again.

Worship and religion

At Nasu, an area of sulphurous hot springs, the legend is commemorated, and many jizō stone statues stand in front of the rock. After the stone split in two on 5 March 2022, local priests held a ceremony on 26 March with prayers, offerings and waving haraegushi to appease the spirit.

Legacy

The poet Matsuo Bashō visited the stone and described it in Oku no Hosomichi (The Narrow Road to the Deep North). The legend inspired a Noh play attributed to Hiyoshi Sa'ami, a novel by Kido Okamoto and a 1967 animated film.

Ancient sources: The Otogi-zōshi tell of the fox's death and the transformation of her body into the stone.

Trivia

Stones with the same name. In Japan, rocks in areas with toxic volcanic gases are often called Sessho-seki.
A split stone. In March 2022 the stone split in two, probably through natural weathering.
