king of Mycenae and commander of the Achaeans in the Trojan War

Agamemnon

Agamemnon is the king of Mycenae who, according to Greek tradition as told by Homer, led the Achaeans against Troy. He matters because his quarrel with Achilles and the sacrifice of Iphigenia are among the best-known episodes of Greek epic and tragedy.

The myth

According to tradition, Agamemnon was the son of King Atreus of Mycenae and Aerope, and the brother of Menelaus. Accounts of his lineage differ: in the Homeric version he is the son of Atreus and grandson of Pelops, while another tradition makes him the son of Pleisthenes, who died young, and raised by Atreus. His family was marked by crime, including Atreus's murder of Thyestes's sons, and by a curse on the house of Pelops.

Agamemnon and Menelaus were driven from Mycenae by Aegisthus, son of Thyestes, and found refuge in Sparta. There Agamemnon married Clytemnestra, daughter of Tyndareus, after killing, according to some versions, her first husband and their infant son. With Clytemnestra he had Orestes, Iphigenia, Electra and others, though the number of children varies by author. He later regained the throne of Mycenae and became the most powerful prince in Greece.

When Paris took Helen, the Greek leaders chose Agamemnon as supreme commander. While the fleet waited at Aulis, the goddess Artemis, offended by his killing of a sacred deer, sent a dead calm. The seer Calchas declared that only the death of Iphigenia would appease her. In Aeschylus and Euripides, Agamemnon sacrifices her, though some versions say Artemis substituted a deer and carried her off to Tauris.

In the Iliad, in the tenth year of the war, Agamemnon quarrels with Achilles by taking Briseis, the captive who had been given to him. His pride drives Achilles from battle, and the Achaeans suffer heavy defeats. Agamemnon fights bravely but also wavers and proposes sailing home. Only when Patroclus dies at the hands of Hector does Achilles return and make peace with him. Although not the poem's central hero, Agamemnon embodies the authority of the warrior king.

In Aeschylus's tragedy, Agamemnon returns victorious from Troy and is killed by Clytemnestra, with Aegisthus as her partner in some versions. His son Orestes later avenges him.

Ancient sources: He is a central figure in Homer's Iliad, and his death at the hands of Clytemnestra is the subject of Aeschylus's Oresteia.
