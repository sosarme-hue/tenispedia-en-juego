second sign of the Chinese zodiac

Ox

The Ox is the second animal in the Chinese twelve-year zodiac cycle, linked to prosperity earned through strength and hard work. In Chinese astrology it stands for diligence and steadiness, and its year is also denoted by the Earthly Branch chǒu (丑).

The myth

According to some old mythological traditions, a great deity held a race to decide which animals would give their names to the years of the twelve-year cycle, and in what order. The course ran over land and through water, with the finish line on the far side of a great river. The Ox crossed easily, being large, powerful and at home both on land and in water. The Rat asked the good-natured Ox for a ride on its back, then at the last moment jumped off and crossed the line first. It won the first place in the cycle, and the Ox finished second.

The ox also appears in other Chinese myths, where oxen or ox-like beings are main characters or supporting figures in stories about plowing, farming and ox-drawn carriages. These tales present the animal as the farmer's partner in labour and, in some cases, as a figure of strength and patience.

Worship and religion

The reference sources do not describe a specific cult of the zodiacal ox. They do note that in Chinese mythology cattle had a religious role as sacrificial offerings. The sign is also linked to the Hour of the Ox, from 1:00 to 3:00 a.m., and to the Month of the Ox in the traditional calendar.

Legacy

The Chinese zodiac has spread to many East Asian countries, including Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, Cambodia and Thailand. In Vietnam the water buffalo takes the Ox's place. In Nepal, the Tamu and Gurung people celebrate the Year of the Cow. The word 'zodiac' comes from an Ancient Greek term meaning 'circle of little animals'. The twelve-year cycle approximates the 11.85-year orbital period of Jupiter. Unlike the Western zodiac, the Chinese animals are not associated with constellations along the ecliptic.

Trivia

A buffalo in Vietnam. In the Vietnamese zodiac, the water buffalo takes the place of the Ox.
The Hour of the Ox. In the traditional Chinese system, the Hour of the Ox runs from 1:00 to 3:00 a.m., corresponding to the double-hour chǒu.
