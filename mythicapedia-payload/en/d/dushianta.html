lunar king of Hindu mythology, husband of Shakuntala

Dushyanta

Dushyanta is a king of the Lunar dynasty and the central figure of a famous story of love and forgetting in Hindu epic and literature. He matters above all as the father of the legendary emperor Bharata, after whom India is named in some traditions.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Dushyanta was the son of King Ilina and Rathantara, and inherited the throne of Hastinapura. His realm stretched from Gandhara, modern Kandahar, to the Vindhya mountains, and from the Sindhu to Vanga, in Bengal.

While hunting, he arrived at the hermitage of the sage Kanva, where he met Shakuntala, daughter of Vishvamitra and Menaka, raised by the sage. They fell in love and married in the gandharva manner. Dushyanta promised to send for her, but affairs of state kept him busy for years. One day the sage Durvasa visited the hermitage, and Shakuntala, absorbed in her love, failed to attend to him; he cursed her so that her beloved would forget her until he saw a token of their union. On the way, Shakuntala lost the royal ring, which a fish swallowed. A fisherman found it and brought it to the court, and on seeing it the king recovered his memory, though he had already denied his wife. They were finally reunited, and Shakuntala became his queen.

In another version, the forgetting was a stratagem devised by the sages to make Vishvamitra bow before the king for the sake of his daughter.

Legacy

His story was reworked by the Sanskrit poet Kalidasa in the drama Abhijnanashakuntalam, which recreates the episode of the ring and the curse and presents Dushyanta as a noble, emotionally refined hero.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata and several Puranas, and is the protagonist of Kalidasa's drama The Recognition of Shakuntala.

Trivia

The lost ring. The royal ring was swallowed by a fish in the river, and it was a fisherman who found it when he cut open his catch, allowing the king to remember Shakuntala.
