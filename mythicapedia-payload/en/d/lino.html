legendary musician and poet of Greek mythology

Linus

Linus is a musician and poet of Greek mythology, regarded by tradition as the first leader of lyric song. He is linked to the dirge and to the origins of music, and his name gave rise to the funeral song genre known as linos.

The myth

Ancient sources disagree about his parentage. In some versions he is a son of Apollo and a Muse, whether Calliope, Urania, Terpsichore or Clio; others make his mother Aethusa or Chalciope. He is usually considered the brother of the musician Orpheus.

Tradition places him in Thebes, in the time of Cadmus, alongside Amphion and Zethus. He is credited with inventing melody, rhythm and dirges, and with being the first to play the harp or to sing accompanied by it. Hesiod called him wise in every art, and later writers attributed verses and apocryphal works about Dionysus to him. Diodorus Siculus also says he brought the Phoenician alphabet to the Greeks.

In a Boeotian version, Apollo killed him with arrows after he defeated him in a musical contest. Thebes and Argos both claimed his tomb. In another tradition he was the teacher of Heracles, who, enraged by his reproaches, struck him with his own lyre and killed him; the hero was acquitted under a law of Rhadamanthys permitting self-defence against an aggressor.

Worship and religion

Every year, before sacrifices to the Muses, a funeral offering was made in his honour and dirges (linoi) were sung. He was honoured near Mount Helicon, and Chalcis in Euboea claimed to hold his tomb.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the funeral genre linos, which Greek tradition linked to his laments. The Alexandrian scholars treated him almost as a historical figure, and Hellenistic writers attributed poems to him.

Ancient sources: Hesiod praises him as wise in every art; Diodorus Siculus, Pausanias, Hyginus, Athenaeus and Diogenes Laertius record his versions of his death, his teaching and his tomb.

Trivia

A lament named after him. The funeral genre linos took its name from the musician, and some authors think Linus himself may have been a personification of the lament.
Heracles' teacher. According to Athenaeus, the poet Alexis wrote a play titled Linus in which Heracles was educated in his house.
