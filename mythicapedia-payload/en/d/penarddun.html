Welsh goddess, wife of Llŷr

Penarddun

Penarddun is a figure in Welsh mythology, wife of Llŷr and mother of Bran, Branwen and Manawydan, as well as Nisien and Efnysien, born of her union with Euroswydd. She helps illustrate the tangled family trees of Welsh tradition.

The myth

According to the Second Branch of the Mabinogi, Penarddun had Bran, Branwen and Manawydan with Llŷr. She later bore two more sons, Nisien, a good man, and Efnysien, a scheming troublemaker, whom the story attributes to Euroswydd. The Welsh Triads remember Llŷr as one of the Three Exalted Prisoners of Britain for his captivity at Euroswydd's hands, which may point to a lost tradition about the birth of her younger sons.

The genealogy is confused: the Mabinogi makes her a daughter of the ancestor Beli Mawr, though she may have been meant as his sister.

Ancient sources: The Second Branch of the Mabinogi and the Welsh Triads.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Her name can be translated as "the most fair". It combines pen, meaning "head" or "chief", with arddun, used of a beautiful girl.
The Three Exalted Prisoners. The Welsh Triads count Llŷr among the Three Exalted Prisoners of Britain, because of his captivity at Euroswydd's hands.
