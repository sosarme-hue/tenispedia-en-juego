Greek god of fear, son of Ares

Phobos

Phobos is the god and personification of fear and panic in Greek mythology. Son of Ares and Aphrodite and brother of Deimos, he attended his father in war, and his name gave rise to the word "phobia".

The myth

According to the tradition, Phobos was the son of Ares, god of war, and Aphrodite, goddess of love. His brother Deimos, Dread, and the goddess Enyo completed his father's warlike retinue. In Hesiod's Theogony he is the brother of Deimos and Harmonia, and his role is almost always that of an assistant to Ares, sowing disorder in battle.

In the Iliad, Phobos and Deimos harness their father's horses and accompany him into combat alongside Eris, discord. In Hesiod's Shield of Heracles, the two brothers escort Ares and carry him from the field when Heracles wounds him. Aeschylus describes the seven warriors of Thebes swearing an oath by Ares, Enyo and Phobos over the blood of a bull. Stesichorus tells how Kyknos, son of Ares, beheaded strangers to build a temple to Phobos from their skulls.

He was often pictured with a lion's head, and the shield of Agamemnon showed him at its centre beside the face of the Gorgon. Phobos never fully left the stage of personified abstraction and has no legends of his own.

Worship and religion

Plutarch mentions a shrine to Phobos at Sparta, alongside shrines to Thanatos and Gelos, Death and Laughter. He reports that the Spartans regarded fear as the force that held the state together, since it produced discipline and obedience to the laws. Pausanias, writing under Imperial Rome, places his temple outside the city, in the area used for military training. Plutarch also says Alexander the Great sacrificed to Phobos on the eve of Gaugamela, probably to fill Darius with fear.

Legacy

Asaph Hall, who discovered Mars's two moons in 1877, named the larger one Phobos and the smaller Deimos. The Soviet and later Russian Phobos Programme studied the satellite. The word "phobia", from the Greek phobos, denotes an irrational fear. Phobos also appears in Sailor Moon, in Marvel comics and in the video game Darkstalkers.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Homer (Iliad), Aeschylus (Seven Against Thebes), Stesichorus and Pausanias.

Trivia

A moon of Mars. Phobos, the larger of Mars's two moons, was named after the god in 1877.
Origin of a word. The word "phobia" derives from the Greek phobos, though today it means an irrational fear.
