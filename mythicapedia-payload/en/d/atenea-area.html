epithet of the Greek goddess Athena

Athena Areia

Areia was a cultic epithet of the Greek goddess Athena, meaning roughly "good, sturdy, effective." Under this name she was worshipped at Athens and Plataea.

The myth

According to tradition, Orestes instituted this cult after the Areopagus acquitted him of murdering his mother. When the judges were tied, Athena Areia gave the decisive vote.

The name is often linked to Ares, but some nineteenth-century scholars derived it from words meaning "prayer" or "to propitiate"; modern scholars consider this unlikely. Another reading holds that the goddess took Ares' name and role to assert her dominance over him.

Worship and religion

In Athens, her statue stood in the temple of Ares, alongside those of Ares, Aphrodite Areia and Enyo. At Plataea, Pausanias describes a temple housing a colossal gilded wooden statue with marble face, feet and hands, built from spoils of Marathon and supposedly by Pheidias.

Legacy

Some modern scholars have proposed identifying her with the sculptural type known as the Medici Athena, though this attribution remains disputed.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and Plutarch, who describe the statue at Plataea.

Trivia

Smaller than its sister. The Plataea statue was somewhat smaller than the Athena Promachos on the Athenian Acropolis, also attributed to Pheidias.
