Celtic goddess of sacred groves

Nemetona

Nemetona is a Celtic goddess whose name means 'she of the sacred grove'. She was venerated in northeastern Gaul and along the Middle Rhine, and she is known mainly through inscriptions set up in places as far apart as Trier and Bath.

The myth

No narrative myth about Nemetona survives. What we know comes from inscriptions and a few objects, such as a terra cotta figurine found at Altrip.

Dedications usually pair her with Mars, sometimes under the Celtic name Loucetius, as at Bath, where she appears beside 'Loucetius Mars'. At Eisenberg, one inscription seems to identify her with Victoria, the Roman goddess of victory.

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on open-air sacred spaces. Her name comes from the root nemeto-, which refers to consecrated places, especially sacred groves, so she is considered a guardian of such sanctuaries. Votive dedications, such as the urn with lots from Eisenberg, show a religious practice tied to these spaces in the Rhine region and at Trier. There is no evidence on whether her cult survives today.

Legacy

No later traces of her figure in art or language have been recorded. Her root does survive in the names of the Romano-British goddess Arnemetia and the Matres Nemetiales, known from an inscription in Grenoble.

Ancient sources: She does not appear in ancient literary texts; she is known from inscriptions, such as those at Bath, Trier, Altrip and Eisenberg.

Trivia

A people with a related name. The Nemetes, a Germano-Celtic people of the Middle Rhine, bore a name related to the goddess's, and they are believed to have been linked to her.
A disputed identification. The scholar Noémie Beck considers the identification of Nemetona with Nemain 'inaccurate and irrelevant'.
