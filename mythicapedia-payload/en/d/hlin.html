Norse goddess of protection

Hlín

Hlín is a goddess of Norse mythology whose name means «protector» in Old Norse. The sources mention her only briefly and ambiguously, and many scholars think she may be another name for Frigg.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Hlín is the twelfth of the goddesses, and her role is to protect people whom Frigg wishes to save from some danger. The text says this gave rise to the saying that someone who escapes finds refuge, or hleinir. The work links her name to a verb, hleina, which appears only in this passage and whose meaning is unclear. Scholars often connect it to Old English hlinian, the ancestor of the English verb lean.

In the Poetic Edda poem Völuspá, Hlín is mentioned when the death of Odin at Ragnarök is foretold, which the stanza calls her «second sorrow». Most scholars read the first sorrow as the death of Baldr, son of Frigg and Odin. Some translators replace Hlín with Frigg in this verse, while others argue that the goddess grieves because she cannot protect those close to Frigg.

Worship and religion

Ancient sources do not describe temples or rituals dedicated to Hlín. Today she receives veneration within modern Germanic paganism, known as Heathenry.

Legacy

Hlín is a common female given name in Iceland, where Norse divine names are often used for people. In skaldic poetry, her name was used to refer to women in kennings such as «Hlín of rings», a usage already found in the 10th century.

Ancient sources: Völuspá and the Poetic Edda, Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), skaldic poetry, and Hávarðar saga.

Trivia

A nearly unknown verb. The verb hleina, from which the Prose Edda derives the goddess's name, appears only in that passage, so its exact meaning is still debated.
Hlín in kennings. Skaldic poets used her name to refer to women, as in «Hlín of velvet», and the practice continued in poetry for centuries.
