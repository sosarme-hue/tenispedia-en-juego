Hindu goddess of spiritual attainments

Siddhidhatri

Siddhidhatri is a form of the Hindu goddess Mahadevi, venerated as the giver of siddhis, spiritual attainments and powers. She is the ninth and last of the Navadurga and is worshipped on the ninth day of Navaratri.

The myth

According to one legend, at the beginning of the universe Shiva worshipped the unmanifest form of the great goddess Mahadevi so that creation could take place. The goddess is said to have emerged from the left half of Shiva as Siddhidhatri.

The tradition adds that she bestowed the eight siddhis, the eight spiritual attainments, upon Shiva, which is why she is linked to that left half. For this reason the form is often associated with the composite deity Ardhanarishvara, in which the left side represents Parvati.

Worship and religion

She is honoured on the ninth day of Navaratri, the nine-night festival dedicated to the goddess, and belongs to the cycle of the nine Durgas. Her iconography shows four hands holding a chakram, a shankha conch, a mace and a lotus, seated on a full-blown lotus or on a lion. In some images she is attended by gandharvas, yakshas, siddhas, asuras and devas, who are shown paying her homage.
