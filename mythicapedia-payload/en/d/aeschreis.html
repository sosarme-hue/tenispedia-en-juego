Thespian princess, mother of Leucones by Heracles

Aeschreis

Aeschreis was a Thespian princess, one of the fifty daughters of King Thespius. She bore Leucones to the hero Heracles, and her story is tied to the hunt for the Cithaeronian lion.

The myth

In the myth, King Thespius asked Heracles to kill the Cithaeronian lion, which was preying on his herds. The hero hunted it for fifty days and, meanwhile, was the king's guest. Heracles, drunk, unknowingly slept with each of Thespius's fifty daughters, always believing he was with the same woman. The king had arranged this so that all of them would have children by the hero.

Other versions speak of a single week of unions, with seven daughters each night. In some, one of the women refused, and Heracles, insulted, condemned her to remain a virgin all her life and serve him as his priest.

Trivia

Differing versions. The accounts disagree on the timing: some mention fifty days of hunting, others a single week of unions or just one night.
A virgin priestess. In some versions, the daughter who refused was condemned to virginity and had to serve Heracles as a priestess.
