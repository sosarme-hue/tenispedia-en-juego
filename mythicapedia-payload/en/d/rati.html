Hindu goddess of love and desire

Rati

Rati is the goddess of desire, passion and sexual pleasure in Hinduism. She is the companion and assistant of Kama, the god of love, and her story revolves around her husband's death and resurrection.

The myth

According to the Kalika Purana, Rati was born from the sweat of Daksha, one of the Prajapatis created by Brahma. Kama was to spread love through the world, and Daksha was to provide him with a wife, so Rati was presented to him. Other accounts make her born of Sandhya, Brahma's daughter, who killed herself after being desired by her father and was revived by Vishnu as Rati.

The central episode is Kama's death. To awaken Shiva, who had turned to ascetic life after Sati's death, so that he would father the son who would slay the demon Tarakasura, Kama shot his arrows at Shiva. Shiva was stirred to desire, but in anger he burned Kama to ashes with a glance of his third eye. The grief-stricken Rati performed penance and begged Parvati, who promised that Kama would be reborn as Pradyumna, son of Krishna. In other versions, Shiva himself grants the resurrection.

Rati waited in the house of the demon Sambara, where she passed as Mayavati, the cook. Pradyumna, raised by her without knowing her identity, grew up, recognized her and, after the warning of the sage Narada, killed Sambara. He then accepted her as his wife.

Worship and religion

Rati is worshipped alongside Kama during festivals dedicated to the god of love. In Tantra she appears in the iconography of Chhinnamasta, who stands above the copulating couple, and their union symbolizes the ritual of maithuna.

Legacy

The name Rati, from the Sanskrit root ram, meaning "to enjoy", gave rise to numerous Sanskrit words related to love and to the names of sexual techniques and positions.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Kalika Purana, the Bhagavata Purana, the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, the Harivamsa, and in the epics Mahabharata and Ramayana, and Kalidasa treats her in his Kumarasambhava.

Trivia

A sexual position. Rati-pasha, "the noose of Rati", is the Sanskrit name of a position in which the woman crosses her legs behind her lover's back.
A dominant woman. Chhinnamasta's position above the couple, with Rati on top (viparita-rati), sometimes suggests the woman's dominance over the man.
