legendary Swedish king of the Ynglings

Jorund

Jorund was, according to Norse tradition, a Swedish king of the Yngling dynasty, son of Yngvi and a legendary figure of the Vendel Era. His story of raids and warfare appears in Ynglingatal, the Historia Norwegiæ and several sagas.

The myth

According to the myth, Jorund and his brother Eirík spent their summers raiding the coasts of Denmark. There they captured King Gudlög of Hålogaland and hanged him, and his surviving companions raised a mound over him.

Later, taking advantage of the weakness of the usurper Haki, they attacked Uppsala. Eirík died in the battle of Fyrisvellir, and Haki, mortally wounded, fell after winning. Jorund regained the throne but kept raiding. Some sources also place him against the Danish king Fróði, whom he eventually killed during a blót.

His end came at Oddesund, in Limfjorden, where he was defeated by Gyllaug, son of Gudlög, and hanged in the same way he had hanged his father.

Ancient sources: Ynglingatal, Íslendingabók, Historia Norwegiæ, the Skjöldunga saga, Bjarkarímur and Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga all mention him as a king of the Swedes.

Trivia

A mound for an enemy. The surviving companions of Gudlög raised a mound over the place where he was hanged, according to Snorri's account.
Kennings from the Hagbard legend. When telling Jorund's story, Snorri cites two kennings from the legend of Hagbard and Signy: "Sigar's steed" and "Hagard's fell noose".
