Chinese god of the north, water and winter

Hēidì

Hēidì, also called Hēishén, is a deity of Chinese religion and one of the "Five Forms of the Highest Deity" in Taoist cosmology. He is linked to the north, water and winter, and is often identified with Zhuānxū and today widely worshipped as Xuánwǔ.

The myth

In Taoist tradition, when the Shang dynasty fell, a demon king ravaged the world. Yuanshi Tianzun instructed the Jade Emperor to appoint Hēidì as commander of twelve heavenly legions to fight this evil.

Hēidì defeated the demon king and was granted the title Mysterious Heavenly Highest Deity. In his temples, the bronze tortoise and serpent beneath his image stand for the triumph of good over evil.

Worship and religion

His birthday, the twenty-first day of the fourth lunar month, is celebrated across China. On Taipa Island in Macau, the festival at the Pak Tai Temple includes an opera-style performance. On Cheung Chau Island in Hong Kong, the Bun Festival is held in front of the Pak Tai Temple.
