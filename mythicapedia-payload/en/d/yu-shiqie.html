Chinese goddess of rain

Yu Shiqie

Yu Shiqie is a Chinese goddess of rain who appears in the Shanhai jing (Classic of Mountains and Seas) as the consort of the Rain Master. Her image, with snakes in her hands and ears, links her to water and dragons, and she holds a place in Taoism and Chinese folk religion.

The myth

According to the Shanhai jing, the consort of the Rain Master lives in the north. She has a dark complexion and holds a snake in each hand; a green snake hangs from her left ear and a red one from her right, and she also holds a turtle in each hand.

Another tradition presents her as a former ruler of the Yushi Kingdom, located in the East Sea, and calls her the «Dragon Maiden» because of her ability to control dragons. Some believe her name does not refer to a single person but to a people or a group of people. Haoyi's commentary explains that Yu Shique is also the name of a country, Yushi, whose ruler was highly skilled at controlling dragons.

Ancient sources: The Shanhai jing (Classic of Mountains and Seas), with Haoyi's commentary.

Trivia

Snakes in the ears. The ancient text describes a green snake in her left ear and a red one in her right, an unusual detail among rain figures.
Dragon Maiden. Her nickname comes from her control over dragons, a skill Haoyi's commentary also attributes to the ruler of Yushi.
