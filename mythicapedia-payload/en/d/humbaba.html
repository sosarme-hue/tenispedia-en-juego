guardian giant of the cedar forest in Mesopotamian mythology

Humbaba

Humbaba is a giant from Mesopotamian mythology, guardian of the cedar forest in the Land of the Living and the foe of Gilgamesh. His story, known mainly from Sumerian and Akkadian poems about the hero, is thought to have shaped the image of the Greek Gorgons.

The myth

According to the Sumerian and Akkadian tales, Humbaba had a lion's face, dragon's teeth, a thick mane and enormous feet, and his roar sounded like a flood. He guarded a cedar forest, where Gilgamesh and his companion Enkidu went to cut down a tree. Humbaba surprised them and stunned them with one of his seven « auras », the source of his invulnerability.

Once awake, Gilgamesh resolved to face him. He captured him by fixing a ring in his nose and binding his arms. When Humbaba begged for mercy, Gilgamesh considered setting him free, but Enkidu objected and they finally beheaded him. The guardian's death angered Enlil, the god who had placed him in charge of the forest, and he redistributed the seven auras he had once given him.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to Humbaba. Clay heads bearing his face, used as protective amulets against evil, are known instead, which suggests an apotropaic function for his image.

Legacy

His face seems to have inspired the Gorgons of ancient Greece, especially scenes of Perseus slaying Medusa with the help of Athena. A late form of his name appears in the Book of Giants, in Jewish and Manichaean versions, and traces of this figure have been identified in later works of Islamic tradition. His name also gave rise to those of a stone and a lizard.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian poems « Gilgamesh and Huwawa » (versions A and B) and the Akkadian Epic of Gilgamesh, along with Hurrian and Hittite adaptations.

Trivia

An ancient name. His name first appears as an ordinary personal name in documents from the Ur III period.
Amulets. His clay heads were used as protection, much like the amulets of the demon Pazuzu.
