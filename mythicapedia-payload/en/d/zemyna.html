Lithuanian goddess of the earth

Žemyna

Žemyna is the goddess of the earth in Lithuanian religion, regarded as a mother goddess who nourishes all life, human, plant and animal. Her figure shows how the Baltic peoples understood soil fertility and the link between the earth and death.

The myth

According to Lithuanian tradition, Žemyna personifies the fertile earth and feeds everything born from it: people, plants and animals. What is born of the earth returns to it, so her cult is also tied to death. Historical sources on Baltic mythology give her a dual role: besides presiding over the fertility of the land, she receives the dead and acts as their ruler and guardian.

She is said to be married either to Perkūnas, the thunder god, or to Praamžius, a manifestation of the chief sky god Dievas. The pair forms the typical Indo-European couple of mother earth and father sky. The earth needed fertilising by rain and thunder, so people believed ploughing or sowing before the first thunder would leave the soil barren.

Worship and religion

Jan Łasicki mentioned her in 1582, and later chroniclers described her cult. Mikalojus Daukša, Daniel Klein and Matthäus Prätorius recorded a rite called žemyneliauti, held at major celebrations such as weddings or farm work such as the harvest. The head of the household poured some beer onto the ground, said a short prayer and killed a rooster or hen, which the family ate, offering the bones to the goddess. Other rites included burying bread from the last harvest before sowing and sacrificing a black piglet. After Lithuania's baptism her cult declined, and her image merged with that of the Virgin Mary.

Legacy

The Lithuanian folk group Kūlgrinda, together with Donis, released the 2013 album Giesmės Žemynai ("Hymns to Žemyna").

Ancient sources: Jan Łasicki (1582), Mikalojus Daukša (1595), Daniel Klein (1653), Matthäus Prätorius and Jacob Brodowski (1740), plus folk legends and prayers.

Trivia

Related name. Her name comes from a Proto-Indo-European earth goddess and is related to Thracian Zemele, meaning "mother earth".
Related male deities. Žemėpatis ("Earth Spouse") and Žemininkas were male deities linked to cattle, agriculture and soil fertility.
