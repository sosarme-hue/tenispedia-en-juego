wife of Aeneas in Roman mythology

Lavinia

Lavinia is, in Roman mythology, the daughter of the Latin king Latinus and Amata, and the last wife of Aeneas. Tradition presents her as the bond that seals the alliance between the Trojans and Latium, and her name was given to the city of Lavinium.

The myth

After the Trojan War, Aeneas arrives in Latium and forms an alliance with King Latinus. Lavinia had been courted by many men, among them Turnus, ruler of the Rutuli, who enjoyed the favor of Queen Amata. In Virgil's account, a omen marked her as destined for a foreigner: her hair caught fire during a sacrifice, promising glorious days for her and war for the Latins.

When Lavinia does not marry Turnus, he goes to war against Aeneas. According to Virgil, Aeneas wins with the help of his mother, the goddess Venus, and with weapons she had given him. Aeneas and Lavinia marry and have a son, Silvius. Other accounts, recorded by Livy, make Ascanius their son and present Lavinia as ruling the Latins while he was too young to reign. Some versions make her the daughter of King Anius of Delos.

Legacy

Dante places her with her father in Canto IV of the Inferno in the Divine Comedy. Boccaccio included her in his book De mulieribus claris. In 2008, Ursula K. Le Guin gave her a voice of her own in the novel Lavinia, narrated in the first person.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, which mentions her about eleven times without her ever speaking, and Livy's History of Rome.

Trivia

Hair on fire. In Book VII of the Aeneid, while Lavinia attends a sacrifice, her hair catches fire, an omen of glorious days for her and of war for the Latins.
A silent heroine. Virgil mentions her around eleven times in his poem, but she never speaks in any of them.
