alleged Polish goddess of love and marriage

Dzidzilela

Dzidzilela is an alleged Polish goddess whom the chronicler Jan Długosz compared to Venus, the Roman goddess of love and marriage. Most researchers now reject that she was ever a real deity and think her name came from fragments of folk songs.

The myth

No sources tell a story about Dzidzilela. Her only known appearance is in the Annals of Jan Długosz, written in the 15th century, where she is presented as the Polish equivalent of Venus. Later writers such as Maciej Miechowita and Marcin Kromer repeated the claim with spelling variants like Dzidzilia or Zizilia.

For centuries she was accepted without question, until Aleksander Brückner criticised the sources. His explanations kept changing: first he linked the name to a phrase meaning the one who lulls children, then to dziedziły, and finally he saw it as an echo of folk song refrains. Other scholars suggest a ritual song formula such as *didi lelo, sung in Slavic songs, which Długosz may have mistaken for a proper name.

Legacy

She has left few traces in general culture. Roman Jakobson and Aleksander Gieysztor linked her sound to Dodola, a South Slavic rain-making ritual, although that connection is also only a hypothesis.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Annals of Jan Długosz, written in the 15th century, and later in chroniclers such as Maciej Miechowita, Marcin Kromer and Alexander Guagnini.

Trivia

Shifting spellings. After Długosz, several writers repeated the name with different spellings: Dzidzilia, Zizilia, Zyzylia or Zezylia.
A ruled-out surname. The Polish surname Dzidziula has been ruled out as a link, because it is a Polonised form of a Lithuanian surname.
