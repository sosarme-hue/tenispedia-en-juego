Celtic bear goddess of hunting and the forest

Artio

Artio is a Celtic goddess linked to bears, hunting and the forest, worshipped above all by the Helvetii. Her name means 'bear' in Gaulish, so she is a deity almost identified with the animal itself.

The myth

Ancient texts say very little about Artio, so her myth is largely reconstructed from her name and her images. She is regarded as a protector of bears and, in some readings, also of the hunters and travellers who ventured into the forest, whom she could help secure a successful hunt while keeping the species alive.

This dual role made her a mediator between people and wild animals. She is sometimes identified with Diana the huntress, the Roman counterpart of Artemis, although her character seems more closely tied to the bear than to the Graeco-Roman goddess.

Worship and religion

Artio is known from two Latin inscriptions. One is on the base of the bronze group found at Muri, near Bern, dedicated by a woman with a Roman name, Licinia Sabinilla, reflecting the coexistence of Celtic and Roman traditions. The other is cut into a rock face in the gorge near Ernzen, in the territory of the Treveri. No information survives about festivals or temples.

Legacy

Her name survives in the root art of Old Irish, arth in Welsh and arz in Breton. From the same root come the word Arctic, the region of the she-bears, and Antarctic, its opposite, as well as the name Arturo, whose brightest star in the constellation Boötes means something like 'the one who guides the bears'. The place name Artieda, in the Aragonese Pyrenees, may derive from the goddess or from the animal.

Trivia

A statuette in Bern. Artio's best-known image is a small bronze group found at Muri, near Bern, which is now held in the Bern Historical Museum.
From bears to stars. The same root behind Artio's name lies behind the word Arctic and the name Arturo, whose brightest star is associated with the bears of the sky.
