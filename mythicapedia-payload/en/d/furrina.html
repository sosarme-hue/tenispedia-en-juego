Roman goddess of springs

Furrina

Furrina was an ancient Roman goddess connected with water, especially springs. Her cult dated back to the earliest period of Roman religion, since she had her own flamen, which places her among the oldest deities of the pantheon.

The myth

Very few stories about Furrina survive, and by the 1st century BC her function had already become obscure. Ancient authors barely describe her. Varro regarded her as a goddess of springs, while Marciano Capella described her as a minor deity whose seat lay just above the mountain peaks.

The modern interpretation most often cited is that of Georges Dumézil, who linked her name to an Indo-European root meaning the moving or bubbling of water. It is cognate with Gothic brunna ("spring") and Latin fervere ("to bubble" or "to boil"), which also gives English words such as "fervent" and "effervescent" and the Latin defruutum ("boiled wine"). This is a scholarly hypothesis, not something the ancient tradition reports.

Worship and religion

Furrina had her own flamen, the Furrinalis, one of the flamines minores. Her sanctuary lay in Rome on the southwestern slopes of the Janiculum, on the right bank of the Tiber, where a sacred spring was located. Today the site survives as a grove within the gardens of Villa Sciarra. Excavations in 1910 uncovered a well, a system of underground channels and inscriptions dedicated to the nymphae furrinae, but these finds appear to date from around the 2nd century CE, and the well may not be the original spring.

Her festival, the Furrinalia, was held on 25 July, within a group of festivals in the second half of July devoted to woods and running waters, offering relief from the heat of the canicula. According to Cicero, another of her sanctuaries stood near Satricum. There is no evidence that her cult survives today.

Legacy

Furrina's name has entered modern popular culture through Furina de Fontaine, a character in the Chinese gacha role-playing game Genshin Impact. In the game she is a flamboyant, overconfident Hydro Archon whose public persona is eventually revealed to be a front.

Ancient sources: Varro, Cicero and Marcianus Capella mention her in their works.

Trivia

A death in her grove. Gaius Gracchus was killed in the Grove of Furrina, showing that the site was significant in late Republican Rome.
A video game archon. Furina de Fontaine, a playable five-star character in Genshin Impact, takes her name from this goddess.
