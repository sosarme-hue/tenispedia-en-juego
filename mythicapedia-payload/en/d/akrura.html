Yadava prince, uncle of Krishna

Akrura

Akrura is a Yadava prince in Hinduism, best known as the uncle of the god Krishna. His story links him to some of the most famous episodes of Krishna and Balarama's lives, as well as to the dispute over the Syamantaka jewel.

The myth

In Hindu tradition, Akrura was the son of Shvaphalka and Gandini, a daughter of the king of Kashi. His cousin Kamsa, ruler of the Yadus from Mathura, feared being overthrown by his nephew Krishna and asked Akrura to bring Krishna and Balarama to a festival of arms, the Dhanuryaga, where they were to be killed. On the journey, Akrura witnessed Krishna's cosmic form (vishvarupa), and while bathing in the Yamuna he saw Balarama as Shesha and Krishna as Vishnu and praised them.

Later, Akrura came into possession of the Syamantaka jewel, which Shatadhanva gave him after killing its previous owner, Satrajit. Krishna wanted it back, and Akrura returned it, offering also the hand of his sister Sushila; Krishna let him keep the jewel. According to the Harivamsha, Akrura married Ugraseni, daughter of Ugrasena, and had two sons. The Bhagavata Purana tells how Krishna sent him to Hastinapura to learn whether King Dhritarashtra was swayed by his son Duryodhana. Akrura died in the fratricidal massacre of the Yadus at Prabhasa.

Worship and religion

At Vrindavan lies Akrura Ghat, one of the bathing ghats of the Mathura region, where according to tradition Krishna and Balarama revealed their forms as Vishnu and Shesha. It is believed that bathing there on a full moon erases all sins. Nearby stands the ancient Gopi Nath temple, dedicated to Krishna and Balarama, with Akrura depicted between them.

Ancient sources: The Harivamsha, the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana narrate his story; the Adi Varaha Purana mentions the ghat bearing his name.

Trivia

A ghat named after him. Akrura Ghat, in Vrindavan, is one of the region's sacred bathing sites and is linked to the revelation of Krishna and Balarama's divine forms.
