naiad of Greek mythology, daughter of the Nile

Anchinoe

Anchinoe is a Greek naiad, daughter of the river god Nilus, and wife of the Egyptian king Belus. She appears only briefly in ancient sources, yet she matters because tradition makes her the mother of Aegyptus and Danaus, two figures central to the Argive genealogy.

The myth

According to the myth, Belus remained in Egypt, ruled the land and married Anchinoe, daughter of the Nile. Apollodorus, in his Bibliotheca, is the only ancient author who names her, and from this union came the twins Aegyptus and Danaus.

According to Euripides, quoted by the same Apollodorus, the marriage also produced Cepheus and Phineus. Phineus is later known in other stories as the uncle of Andromeda, although the texts do not always agree on the details of his kinship.

Her name appears in distorted forms in some local variants, and it is unclear whether these refer to the same figure or to different characters. Some texts call her Achiroe and tell how she was seduced by Ares, with whom she had a son named Sithon. According to Hegesipus, a figure called Anchirrhoe bore the twin nymphs Palene and Retea, who gave their names to two cities.

Another tradition holds that the mother of these children and wife of Belus was called Side, a name linked to the Phoenician city of Sidon. The versions are not uniform, and Anchinoe's identity remains wrapped in several possible genealogies.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Bibliotheca, names her as the wife of Belus and quotes Euripides on her sons.

Trivia
