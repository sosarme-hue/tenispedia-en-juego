Japanese god of fortune, fishermen and merchants

Ebisu

Ebisu is a Japanese deity linked to good luck, fishing and trade, and he is the only one of the Seven Gods of Fortune who comes purely from Japan, with no Buddhist, Taoist or Hindu influence. His popularity made him one of the most venerated gods in Japanese homes.

The myth

In a tradition that took shape in the Muromachi period, Ebisu was identified with Hiruko, the first child of Izanagi and Izanami. Born without bones, or in some stories without limbs, because of a ritual mistake during his parents' marriage, he was set adrift in a reed boat before his third birthday. He reached the land of Ezo, today Hokkaidō, where the Ainu looked after him, and he learned to fish under an adoptive father named Ebisu Saburō. At the age of three his limbs grew. He stayed slightly deaf and lame, yet kept his cheerful and helpful nature.

Scholars believe, however, that Ebisu first appeared as a god of fishermen and that his identification with Hiruko came much later. Another theory links him with Kotoshironushi no Mikoto, son of Ōkuninushi. In the Kojiki he is shown fishing when summoned, an image later tied to his iconography. Because Ōkuninushi was syncretized with Daikokuten, he is sometimes considered Daikokuten's son. For some communities, Ebisu also appears in objects that drift in from the sea, such as logs or even corpses.

Worship and religion

His main festival, Ebisu-kō, falls on the twentieth day of the tenth lunar month, called Kannazuki or "the month without gods". While the other deities travel to the Grand Shrine of Izumo, Ebisu stays behind and remains available to worshippers, who ask him for good fortune, abundant harvests and commercial success. The Toka Ebisu, also called Ebisu Matsuri, takes place at the Imamiya-Ebisu shrine in Osaka on 9, 10 and 11 January. Because he is thought to be somewhat deaf, devotees walk around the shrine and strike a drum so that he can hear them. Tokyo has three traditional temple groups dedicated to the Seven Gods of Fortune. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

His image, with rod and sea bream, appears in shops, restaurants and homes. The Yebisu beer logo, first brewed in 1890, is based on him, and the clothing brand Evisu takes its name from him. The B.League basketball team Osaka Evessa is named after the local pronunciation of Ebisu-sama.

Ancient sources: The ancient texts that mention him include the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki.

Trivia

A god who does not answer the summons. While the other Japanese gods gather at the Grand Shrine of Izumo in the tenth month, Ebisu does not respond to the call and stays with his worshippers.
Protector of whales. Some fishermen in northeast Japan opposed whaling because they believed whales were an incarnation of Ebisu.
