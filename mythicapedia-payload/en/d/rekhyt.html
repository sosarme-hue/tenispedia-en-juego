Egyptian people and deity linked to the lapwing

Rekhyt

Rekhyt was the name of a people living in the northern Nile Delta during the Early Dynastic Period and, from the Middle Kingdom onward, of an Egyptian deity. The term is connected to the lapwing, a bird the Egyptians saw as clumsy and mournful.

The myth

The oldest texts present the Rekhyt as mythical inhabitants of the Delta, grouped among the northern enemies of Upper Egypt. After the collapse of the Old Kingdom, the name changed meaning: it was given to a new deity at the start of the Middle Kingdom, and in the New Kingdom the Egyptians linked the older Rekhyt with Horus.

In everyday use the word came to mean "the common people", a generic term. In temple scenes the Rekhyt appear alongside the Pat and Henmemet as a three-part social order tied to Maat, while the pharaoh is shown dominating them as a sign of his rule over Egypt.

Worship and religion

Sources say little about its worship. A relief in the Temple of Seti I at Abydos shows the king holding a Rekhyt after receiving the crown from Amun-Ra, and a statue in Djoser's pyramid complex shows three Rekhyt birds beneath his feet.

Ancient sources: The Royal Annals of the Old Kingdom, including the Palermo Stone, and the story of the Henmemet.

Trivia

The Lapwing Palette. A slate palette fragment found near Sais shows a barque with a lapwing on its bow. Its exact place of origin is unknown.
A name that changed meaning. At the start of the Old Kingdom the term widened to mean peasants and craftsmen, and eventually came to mean ordinary people.
