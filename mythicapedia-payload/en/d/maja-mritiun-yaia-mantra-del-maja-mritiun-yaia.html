Hindu mantra for conquering death, rooted in the Vedas

Mahamrityunjaya Mantra

The Mahamrityunjaya Mantra, also known as the Rudra or Tryambakam Mantra, is one of the best-known prayers in Hinduism. It comes from a verse of the Rig Veda addressed to Tryambaka, an epithet of Rudra, who is identified with Shiva in Shaivism, which explains its importance in devotional life.

The myth

The verse appears in the seventh book of the Rig Veda, a hymn attributed to the tradition of Vasishtha Maitravaruni, and recurs in the Yajur Veda. According to the texts, its final four verses are late additions connected with the closing rite of the sacrifice, which ends with an offering to Rudra Tryambaka.

The name refers to the god's three eyes. Tradition links the word tryambaka to the three mothers Amba, Ambika and Ambalika. The verse compares the devotee to a cucumber or gourd that drops from its stem when ripe, and asks to be freed from death to attain immortality.

Worship and religion

Believers recite the mantra to seek long life, inner strength, protection and physical and mental health. It is chanted during the application of sacred ash and during rituals, and is considered a path towards moksha, liberation, through the understanding that the soul is eternal.

Legacy

It is one of the most widespread mantras in contemporary Hinduism, present in liturgy, devotional music and meditation practice. Its text is still recited regularly in Shaiva temples and homes.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Rig Veda (7.59.12), dating from around the mid-second millennium BCE, and in the Yajur Veda (Taittiriya Samhita 1.8.6 and Vajasaneyi Samhita 3.60).

Trivia

Another name. It is also called the Tryambakam Mantra, after the epithet of Rudra that appears in the verse.
A harvest image. The central image compares the devotee to a cucumber or gourd that separates from its stem when ripe.
