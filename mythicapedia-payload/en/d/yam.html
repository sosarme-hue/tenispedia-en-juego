Canaanite god of the sea and rivers

Yam

Yam is the Canaanite god of the sea and rivers, worshipped along the eastern Mediterranean coast and known mainly from the texts of Ugarit. He matters because he is the central rival of Baal in the Baal Cycle, one of the great narratives of Levantine mythology.

The myth

According to the Baal Cycle, Yam competed with his brother Baal for the rank of king of the gods. The senior god El seemed to favour Yam as candidate, but in the end Baal emerged victorious. Yam bears titles such as "Judge River" (ṯpṭ nhr) and "prince", and his secondary name, Nahar, means "river", reflecting his mastery over fresh and salt waters.

The story keeps mentioning Yam after his defeat. In Egypt, in the Astarte Papyrus, he appears as an enemy of the Ennead, who demands tribute from the other gods; there Set defeats him, as is known from incantations, since the papyrus ends are lost. In the Hebrew Bible he appears as an enemy of Yahweh.

Worship and religion

He appears in Ugaritic offering lists, where he receives rams and a cow, and he holds a low rank in the pantheon. Several people bore names invoking him, such as Yammu'ilu ("Yam is god"). In Emar he was venerated during the zukru festival, held every seven years. There is no evidence that his cult survived.

Legacy

His name is identical to the Ugaritic noun ym, "sea", and related words appear in Phoenician, Aramaic, Hebrew and Arabic. The Egyptians borrowed the word yammu to designate the Mediterranean.

Ancient sources: The Ugaritic texts of the Baal Cycle, the Astarte Papyrus and the Hebrew Bible.

Trivia

A name for the Mediterranean. The Egyptians borrowed the word yammu and used it for the Mediterranean Sea, although it could also function as a divine name.
Set in Baal's place. In the Astarte Papyrus, Set takes the role that Baal plays at Ugarit and is the one who defeats Yam.
