Phlegyan prince and hero of Elis

Phorbas

Phorbas is a hero of Greek mythology, a prince of the Thessalian Phlegyes who emigrated to Elis in the Peloponnese. His alliance with Alector and his descendants, several of whom sailed with the Argonauts, make him a notable figure in the region's traditions.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phorbas was a prince of the Phlegyes, a people of Thessaly, who emigrated to the Peloponnese and settled in Elis. He was the son of Lapithes and Orsinome, and brother of Periphas. When Pelops made war on Alector, king of Elis, Phorbas came to his aid and, after the victory, shared the kingdom with him.

To seal their alliance, the two agreed to marry into the families of their other ally. Phorbas wed Hyrmine, Alector's sister, while Alector took Diogeneia, Phorbas's daughter, as his wife. Hyrmine bore Augeas, Actor and Tiphys, who in various versions sailed with the Argonauts. Another tradition also presents him as a lover of Apollo.

Trivia

Lover of Apollo. A tradition noted in the English sources presents him as a lover of the god Apollo.
Father of Argonauts. His sons Augeas, Actor and Tiphys appear, in different versions, among the crew of the Argo.
