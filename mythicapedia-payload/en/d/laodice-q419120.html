Laodice

Laodice is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of Cinyras, wife of Elatus and mother of Stymphalus and Pereus. Her name means 'justice of the people'.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Laodice married Elatus and became the mother of Stymphalus and Pereus; that is all he records about her.

Pausanias places her in a different version, as a descendant of Agapenor, and says she sent a robe, a peplos, to Athena Alea at Tegea. The inscription on the offering named both the gift and her own lineage, from divine Cyprus. She may be the same Laodice named by Apollodorus, though the sources do not confirm it.

Worship and religion

The peplos offered to Athena Alea at Tegea is her only link to worship: a votive gift whose inscription recorded her ancestry. For J. Roy, the epigram was probably connected with Agapenor in the Hellenistic period.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus and Pausanias.

Trivia

An offering with a signature. The inscription on the peplos at Tegea recorded both the gift and Laodice's lineage, as quoted by Pausanias.
