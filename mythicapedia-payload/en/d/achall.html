daughter of King Cairbre Nia Fer in Irish mythology

Achall

Achall is a minor figure of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, daughter of King Cairbre Nia Fer of Tara. Her name became attached to a hill near Tara, now known as Skryne, where tradition places key episodes in the succession to the High Kingship of Ireland.

The myth

According to the story, Achall was the daughter of Cairbre Nia Fer, king of Tara, and his wife Fedelm Noíchrothach. When her brother Erc was killed by Conall Cernach, she died of grief on a hill near Tara, which was later named after her.

The hill also appears in tales about the High Kings of Ireland. The legendary Túathal Techtmar is said to have taken power after defeating the previous High King, Elim mac Conrach, in battle on that same hill. Later, according to The Expulsion of the Déisi, Cormac mac Airt lived there after losing an eye, as this physical imperfection prevented him from ruling from Tara.

Ancient sources: The Ulster Cycle and the tale The Expulsion of the Déisi preserve her story, and the Dindshenchas Érenn, which explains the origins of place names, mentions the hill that bears her name.
