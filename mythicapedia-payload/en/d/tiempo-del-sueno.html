sacred concept in Australian Aboriginal mythology

Dreamtime

The Dreamtime, also called The Dreaming or Altjeringa, is the sacred era of Australian Aboriginal mythology in which ancestral beings shaped the land, living things and laws. It matters because it lays down the patterns of life, the bond with the land and much of the identity of Aboriginal peoples.

The myth

According to the traditional story, before the present world there was a formless time when culture heroes and ancestral beings travelled across an unshaped land. Their journeys created rivers, hills, springs and sacred sites, and their deeds left marks still visible in the landscape. The serpent Wagyl, for instance, is said to have carved the watercourses near Perth, and the python Walujapi to have cut a serpent-shaped path into a cliff in the Victoria River valley.

Another account tells that, before taking form, the souls of plants, animals and people knew they would exist but not when. When the moment was right, they took shape one by one, and the last became human. That is why each person is said to exist eternally in the Dreaming, and that eternal part continues after death.

Versions differ from region to region. In some tellings Altjira appears as an eternal being who made the land and then withdrew, although scholars debate whether the word means a supreme god or simply something eternal and older than time.

Worship and religion

These stories are passed on through songs, dances and ceremonies performed at large gatherings during the most favourable season. Each group looks after its stretch of country and its song lines, and its bond with a particular place shapes its ritual duties. Many Aboriginal Australians continue to practise these beliefs today.

Legacy

The term entered popular culture and appears in works such as Tad Williams's science fiction saga Otherland, in the role-playing game Werewolf: The Apocalypse and in the 2006 video game Dreamfall. Werner Herzog's film Where the Green Ants Dream (1984), centred on a dispute over a sacred site, also explores the theme.

Trivia

Songs that crossed the continent. Some song lines travelled across Australia, passing through six to ten different language groups.
A mark in the rock. The Yarralin people believe the spirit Walujapi left the impression of her body in the ground when she sat down to camp.
