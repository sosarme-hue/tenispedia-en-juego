Chinese god of metal and the west, the White Emperor

Baidi

Baidi, the White Emperor, is one of the five manifestations of the supreme deity Shangdi in Chinese religion. He is associated with metal, the west, autumn and the tiger as his stellar animal.

The myth

Tradition links him with the White Dragon, his animal form, and with the planet Venus, which is his astral body. His son is Taibai Jinxing.

In the legend of the Uprising of the White Serpent, a gigantic white serpent killed some outlaws with its poisonous breath, until a drunk Liu slew it during the night. The next morning, a weeping old woman told them that the son of the White Emperor had been slain by the son of the Red Emperor, then vanished. Liu, whose rise to rule had been prophesied, saw his reputation grow among followers who became convinced of his destiny.

Worship and religion

Baidi's worship is linked to Xiyue Dadi, god of Mount Hua, who is identified with him. Emperor Wudi of the Han dynasty built the first temple in his honour at the foot of the mountain, near Chang'an, the capital. The shrine was later renamed the Xiyue Temple.

Trivia

A white serpent. In the legend, the serpent that kills the outlaws with its breath is white, the colour that symbolises the White Emperor.
An imperial temple. Emperor Wudi was the one who ordered the first temple dedicated to this god built at the foot of Mount Hua.
