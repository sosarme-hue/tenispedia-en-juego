female centaur of Greek mythology

Hylonome

Hylonome is a female centaur in Greek mythology, known mainly for her love for the centaur Cyllarus. Her story matters because Ovid includes it in the Metamorphoses as an episode that reflects on the blend of nature and culture, and of the human and the animal.

The myth

Hylonome was a female centaur in Greek mythology. According to the story, she was present at the battle between the Lapiths and the centaurs, where she lost her husband, the centaur Cyllarus, whom she loved deeply.

Heartbroken, she took her own life to join him. Ovid places this episode within Nestor's account of the battle in Book XII of the Metamorphoses, as a digression about love and war. For the poet, the couple illustrates several conceptual opposites: nature and culture, human and animal, male and female, love and war.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions her in Book XII of the Metamorphoses, within Nestor's account of the battle between the Lapiths and the centaurs.

Trivia

Told by Nestor. Ovid does not tell the episode directly, but within the words of Nestor, who recounts the battle between the Lapiths and the centaurs.
Allusions to other poets. Ovid alludes to two didactic works: Lucretius' De rerum natura and his own Ars amatoria III.
