guardian spirit of the Sundarbans forests

Bonbibi

Bonbibi is a lady of the forest, a guardian spirit venerated by both Hindus and Muslims in the Sundarbans of Bengal. People call on her before entering the forest for protection against tigers, especially honey-collectors and woodcutters.

The myth

According to tradition, Bonbibi is the daughter of Berahim, a fakir from Mecca, and she is born in the forest after her mother Golalbibi abandons her with her brother Shah Jangali. A doe raises her. Later she reaches the Sundarbans and confronts the demon king Dakkhin Rai, lord of the south, who appears disguised as a tiger and attacks humans.

The main narrative recounts that battle and the story of Dukhe.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped at small earthen mounds called thān and later through images called chhalan. Hindus make clay statues, while Muslims raise red flags and garlands on the mounds. Her major festival, the Jatal Puja, is held in the Bengali month of Magh, with offerings of batasa and shirni.

Legacy

Amitav Ghosh features her story in his novel The Hungry Tide (2004), and Qurratulain Hyder mentions her in River of Fire.

Ancient sources: The Bonbibir Johuranama, a Bengali verse text attributed to early poets such as Bayanuddin and Munshi Mohammad Khater, is her principal text.

Trivia

Festival in Holdia. In the village of Holdia in Joynagar, the Mandal family has organized a three-day fair in her honour for about 250 years.
Protection after Cyclone Aila. After Cyclone Aila in 2009, many locals credited Bonbibi with the destruction not being worse.
