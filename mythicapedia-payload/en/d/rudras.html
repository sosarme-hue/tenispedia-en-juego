Rudras

The Rudras are a group of gods and followers of the god Rudra, the Vedic forerunner of Shiva. According to the Ramayana, they are eleven of the thirty-three gods of the Vedic pantheon, and they are often confused with the Maruts.

The myth

Accounts give different origins. According to the Ramayana they are sons of the sage Kashyapa and Aditi; the Matsya Purana makes Surabhi and Brahma their parents. The Vishnu Purana tells that they arose from Brahma's anger: Rudra, in the androgynous Ardhanari form, split into man and woman, and the male part divided into eleven Rudras, while eleven Rudranis were born from the female part.

In the Vedic texts they are loyal companions of Rudra and, in later literature, attendants of Shiva. In the Mahabharata they aid Vishnu against demons and, led by Kapali, slay the elephant demon Gajasura. The Bhagavata Purana makes Ashwatthama, son of the teacher Drona, an avatar of one of them.

Worship and religion

The Upanishads assign them the second stage of life, from about age 24 to 68. The Chandogya Upanishad advises propitiating them during illness, and the Bhagavata Purana prescribes their worship to gain virile power.

Ancient sources: Rig Veda, Ramayana, Mahabharata, Bhagavad Gita, the Brihadaranyaka and Chandogya Upanishads, Vishnu Purana, Matsya Purana and Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A name with meaning. According to the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad, the name Rudra means 'those who make cry', because when they leave the body they cause death.
Eleven thousand Rudras. In one passage of the Mahabharata the Rudras are said to be eleven thousand, surrounding Shiva and grouped into eleven groups of a thousand.
