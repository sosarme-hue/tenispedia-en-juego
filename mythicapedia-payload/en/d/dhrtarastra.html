eastern heavenly king in Buddhism

Dhṛtarāṣṭra

Dhṛtarāṣṭra is one of the Four Heavenly Kings of Buddhism, lord of the gandharvas and guardian of the eastern side of Mount Sumeru. His veneration spread with Buddhism across East Asia, where he is widely honoured as a protector of the Dharma.

The myth

According to the Pali texts, Dhataraṭṭha is one of the Cātummahārājāno, or 'Four Great Kings', each guarding a cardinal direction. He watches over the east of Mount Sumeru, the centre of the Buddhist universe, and leads the gandharvas, celestial musicians, and the piśācas, flesh-eating spirits. He is shown in armour and, in Mahayana tradition, holding a pipa, a Chinese lute that stands for music spreading joy and drawing beings toward the Dharma. According to the Pali canon he has many sons called 'Indra' and a daughter, Sirī.

In the Chinese novel Journey to the West he fights Sun Wukong and other rebel forces in Heaven. In Fengshen Yanyi he is identified with the general Ma Li Shou, who after his death ascends to divinity and is made Heavenly King of the East. A nāga king of the same name appears in the Bhūridatta Jātaka as the father of the bodhisattva Bhūridatta, though he is a different figure.

Worship and religion

In Chinese temples, Chíguó Tiān is enshrined in the Hall of the Heavenly Kings (天王殿), usually alongside his companions. In Vietnam, where he is called Trị Quốc Thiên Vương, he is honoured during religious festivals and regarded as guardian of the Eastern Palace of the Jade Emperor. In Japan, Jikokuten appears as an armoured guardian trampling a demon, fierce in appearance yet benevolent. His cult remains alive in Buddhist temples across these regions.

Legacy

His image passed into the religious art of East Asia, into temple guardian statues and Chinese literature. His attributes, including the pipa, the Azure Cloud Sword, the Mixed Origin Umbrella and the Divine Mouse, remain part of popular iconography.

Ancient sources: Pali Buddhist canon texts, such as the Bhūridatta Jātaka, and in China the novels Journey to the West and Fengshen Yanyi.

Trivia

An instrument with meaning. The pipa he holds is read as a symbol of music as a way to spread joy and the Dharma.
Many names. His name is rendered differently by language, such as Chíguó Tiān in Chinese or Jikokuten in Japanese.
