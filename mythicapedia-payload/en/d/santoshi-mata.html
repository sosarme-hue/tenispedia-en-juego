Hindu goddess of satisfaction and Friday fasting

Santoshi Mata

Santoshi Mata, whose name means "Mother of Satisfaction", is a Hindu goddess especially venerated by women in North India and Nepal. Her cult spread widely after the 1975 film Jai Santoshi Maa, although small shrines and fasting pamphlets honouring her existed before it.

The myth

The best-known story about the goddess is not part of the epic tradition but a short tale, the vrata katha, or legend of the fast. It tells of an old woman with seven sons, the youngest of whom was irresponsible and was given his brothers' leftovers. His wife, mistreated by the in-laws, observes the Friday fast, and the goddess appears in her husband's dream to alert him, since he had grown wealthy far away and forgotten her. After a series of misfortunes caused by an offence during the closing ceremony, the family is forgiven and blessed.

The 1975 film presents her as the daughter of the god Ganesha and links her to Raksha Bandhan, when a sister ties a protective bracelet on her brother's wrist. This parentage does not appear in the original vrata katha. Her iconography, seated or standing on a lotus and holding a sword and a trident, recalls Lakshmi and Durga.

Worship and religion

The vrata, or fast, is kept on sixteen successive Fridays or until a wish is fulfilled. The devotee offers flowers, incense and a bowl of raw sugar and roasted chickpeas, eats only one meal a day and avoids bitter or sour food. When the wish is granted, the udyapan ceremony is held, with a festive meal for eight boys. There are shrines in Jodhpur, Delhi and other cities, and the cult remains alive among women in northern India.

Legacy

The low-budget film Jai Santoshi Maa (1975) was a major box-office hit and led audiences to attend screenings barefoot and with rituals. Friday matinee screenings for women became a custom, and her image spread through posters and temples across the country.

Ancient sources: Santoshi Mata does not appear in ancient texts such as the Vedas or Puranas; her story comes from twentieth-century pamphlets and oral tradition.

Trivia

Humble offerings. Santoshi Mata receives raw sugar and roasted chickpeas, foods associated with ordinary people, which helped make her popular among the masses.
A temple before the film. A temple to her already existed in Jodhpur before the film, though it was shared with Lal Sagar ki Mata, who received animal sacrifices.
