Gaulish god of healing waters

Glanis

Glanis was a Gaulish god linked to a healing spring at Glanum, in the Alpilles mountains of Provence in southern France. The town took its name from him, which shows how central he was to local life.

The myth

No detailed myth of Glanis survives. What we know comes mainly from the archaeological remains of the site. A shrine to the god was set up at Glanum in the 4th century BC, and near the springs there are cisterns where pilgrims may have bathed.

Beside one of them stood an altar to Glanis and the Glanicae, a triad of local mother goddesses associated with the healing waters.

Worship and religion

Worshippers came to the springs in search of healing, possibly bathing in the cisterns. When Glanum became a Roman colony, the Romans absorbed Glanis into their pantheon in the form of Valetudo, goddess of health. His worship ended with the rise of Christianity and the destruction of the town in 270 AD.

Legacy

The god left his mark on the name of Glanum, the town that grew up around his shrine and springs.

Trivia

A town named after a god. Glanum took its name from the god Glanis, which was unusual among towns in Gaul.
A god who changed names. When the region entered the Roman world, Glanis was identified with Valetudo, a goddess of health, and continued to be worshipped under that form.
