rakshasa from the Mahabharata

Jatasura

Jatasura is a rakshasa, a demon of Hindu tradition, who appears in the epic Mahabharata. His story matters because it shows Bhima, the strongest of the Pandavas, facing an enemy who threatens his brothers and Draupadi.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Jatasura appeared before the Pandavas disguised as a Brahmin. His plan was to win their trust, steal their weapons and abduct Draupadi, their common wife, intending to assault her.

One day, while Bhima was away, Jatasura took on his monstrous form, seized the weapons and carried off Yudhishthira, Sahadeva, Nakula and Draupadi. Sahadeva managed to escape and ran to fetch Bhima. Meanwhile, Yudhishthira confused the demon by showering him with moral reproaches about his lack of wisdom.

Bhima arrived and resolved to face the rakshasa alone, refusing the help of Nakula and Sahadeva. They fought with huge trees, boulders and their bare arms. Finally, Bhima struck a deadly blow to the neck, lifted the demon and smashed him to the ground, and ended by beheading him with his elbow. After the victory, Yudhishthira praised his deed.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the great Indian epic in which he appears.
