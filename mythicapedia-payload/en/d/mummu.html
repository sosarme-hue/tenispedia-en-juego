vizier deity of Mesopotamian mythology

Mummu

Mummu is a deity of Mesopotamian mythology, vizier of Apsu, often associated with the mist rising from the primordial waters. He matters chiefly because of the Babylonian epic Enūma Eliš, where his brief role shows the link between power and wisdom.

The myth

In the Enūma Eliš, the primordial waters existed first: Apsu, the sweet subterranean water, and Tiamat, the salt water. Mummu appears as Apsu's vizier, and the text sometimes gives him a title that also belongs to Tiamat, which has led to debate over whether he was a separate being, their son, or the same entity as Tiamat.

When the younger gods disturb Apsu's sleep, he resolves to destroy them and asks Mummu for advice, which is to kill them. Enki, one of the younger gods, learns of the plan, casts a spell that sends Apsu into sleep, and kills him. He then subdues Mummu, who is held with a ring in his nose and made to serve him. Some scholars see this episode as explaining Enki's association with wisdom.

Worship and religion

Ritual texts treat him as a primordial deity dwelling in the underworld, kept apart from the gods still active. Some early theophoric names and a mace head from Mari may refer to him, though it is unclear whether they name the same deity. In a first-millennium BCE ritual he received silver discs, flour, dates and a sheep alongside Qingu.

Legacy

His name is linked to the Akkadian noun mummu, "creative force", and he may have been depicted with a ram or wild sheep head in Kassite art.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Babylonian creation epic Enūma Eliš and in a passage by Eudemus of Rhodes preserved by Damascius.
