Trojan hero, son of Laomedon

Bucolion

Bucolion is a hero of Greek mythology, the eldest though illegitimate son of the Trojan king Laomedon and the nymph Calybe. He matters mainly for his descendants, since his sons took part in the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the tradition, Bucolion was the firstborn of Laomedon, but he was not the son of the king's legitimate wife. His mother was the nymph Calybe. He married the naiad Abarbarea, with whom he had at least two sons, Aesepus and Pedasus. Both fought in the Trojan War, alongside their people.

A commentary by the Byzantine scholar Tzetzes adds that Bucolion and Abarbarea were also the parents of the hero Euphorbus, whom other versions make the son of Panthous and Phrontis. This disagreement shows that the family lines of these figures varied from one source to another.

Ancient sources: He appears only in a commentary by the Byzantine scholar Tzetzes, who links him to the ancestry of Euphorbus. The remaining details come from traditions about the Trojan War that are not tied to a single surviving text.

Trivia

Father of Euphorbus. Tzetzes presents him as the father of the hero Euphorbus, who in other sources is the son of Panthous and Phrontis.
Several Bucolions. The same name belongs to other figures of Greek myth, including an Arcadian prince and an Achaean soldier in the Trojan War.
