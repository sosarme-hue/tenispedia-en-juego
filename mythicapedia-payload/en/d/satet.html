Egyptian goddess of war, flooding and fertility

Satet

Satet was an Egyptian goddess of the south, linked to war, hunting, fertility and the yearly flooding of the Nile. She belonged to the Elephantine Triad with Khnum and Anuket, and guarded Egypt's southern border with Nubia.

The myth

According to tradition, Satet protected the country's southern frontier by killing the pharaoh's enemies with her arrows. Her name, which can be rendered as "She who Shoots" or "She who Pours", reflects both this warlike side and her role in the river's flood, whose current was thought to run like an arrow.

Accounts make her the wife of Khnum, guardian of the Nile's source, and the mother, or sometimes sister, of Anuket. Some sources first pair her with the Theban god Montu and say she later replaced Heket as Khnum's consort. As a fertility goddess she was believed to grant the wishes of those seeking love. In the Pyramid Texts she appears purifying a dead pharaoh's body with four jars of water from Elephantine.

Worship and religion

Her main centre of worship was on Elephantine, an island facing Aswan, where a temple, which some sources credit to Hatshepsut, stood on an older site aligned with the star Sirius. She was also honoured on the nearby island of Sehel. Epithets such as "Mistress of Elephantine" reflect this local bond.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts (Dynasty VI) show her purifying the pharaoh, and the Aramaic Elephantine papyri invoke her on a divorce document.

Trivia

A temple aligned with Sirius. According to Wells, the temple on Elephantine was aligned with the star Sirius, which the Egyptians linked to the onset of the flood.
A changed hieroglyph. Her name was first written with the shoulder knot of a linen garment. Later that sign was replaced by the animal hide pierced by an arrow used for Anuket.
