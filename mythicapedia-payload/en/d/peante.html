Greek hero, archer and Argonaut

Poeas

Poeas, also called Poias, is a Greek hero and king of Meliboea or Malis, and one of the Argonauts. He is best known as the father of Philoctetes and for his role at the end of the life of his friend Heracles.

The myth

According to the tradition, Poeas was the son of King Thaumacus of Thaumacia, and with Methone he became the father of the hero Philoctetes, which is what he is most remembered for. Some accounts name Demonassa as the mother instead, and some lists give Philoctetes as an Argonaut in his father's place.

As an Argonaut he is remembered as the greatest archer of the group. When they faced the bronze giant Talos, some versions say he shot an arrow into the giant's heel, though that victory is more often credited to Medea, who had drugged the giant.

His most famous moment came at the end of Heracles' life. Poisoned by centaur blood, Heracles asked for a pyre to be built and lit, but none of his companions would light it. Some mythographers say Poeas agreed to light it, and in return Heracles gave him his bow and poison arrows. The more common version, however, says that Philoctetes lit the fire on his father's orders and received the weapons.

Trivia

An arrow to the heel. According to one tradition, Poeas hit the heel of Talos, the bronze giant, with an arrow. The feat is more often credited to Medea, who was said to have drugged the giant.
Heracles' weapons. Heracles gave his poisoned arrows and bow to whoever lit his pyre. In the most common version, those weapons went to Philoctetes rather than Poeas.
