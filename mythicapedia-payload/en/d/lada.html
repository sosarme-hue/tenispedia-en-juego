Slavic goddess of beauty and fertility

Lada

Lada is a goddess of beauty and fertility in Slavic and Baltic mythology, usually paired with her male counterpart, Lado. She matters because she shows how agricultural rites and weddings were intertwined before Christianization, although scholars still debate whether she was ever a deity with a genuine cult.

The myth

Stories about Lada are scarce and vary from source to source. Sometimes she appears as a mother goddess, with Lado as her son or consort; at other times the two are presented as divine twins. Boris Rybakov proposed that Lada and her daughter Leila were goddesses of spring, Slavic counterparts of Leto and Artemis. Some sources link her to Loduna, the Scandinavian goddess of fire, hearth and herds, and one text makes her the mother of Lel and Polel, who are associated with the Greek Dioscuri, the sons of Leda.

Together with Kupalo, Kostroma and Iarilo, Lada and Lado form an aspect of a fertility deity linked to water, harvests and the sun, each with a male and a female component. David Leeming connects her with life, death and rebirth, and some authors see Lado as a god of the underworld and marriage.

Worship and religion

Russian monastic texts from the 15th to the 18th centuries describe girls worshipping Lada in spring, during the welcoming of water spirits. Songs for sowing, harvest and weddings call on her, and Lithuanian songs name her the Great Goddess and Mother Lada. After Christianization her veneration is said to have passed to the Virgin Mary, according to Ralston.

Legacy

The asteroid (2832) Lada is named after her. Her name has been linked to the Russian word lad, meaning harmony, peace or union.

Ancient sources: The Kievan Synopsis (1674), which places her in the time of Vladimir the Great, and a Polish postil of around 1405 condemning her cult.
