Mesopotamian goddess of beer

Siris

Siris (also Siraš) was a Mesopotamian goddess associated with beer, whose name could also refer to the drink itself in Akkadian texts. She was closely linked to Ninkasi, another beer deity, and appears in god lists, offering lists and texts from Mesopotamia and Syria.

The myth

Surviving texts tell little about Siris beyond lists of gods. According to The First Brick, an incantation, Ea created her, along with other deities, from clay taken from the Apsu. In a text known only from late copies she is called the "great cook of An", a role not attested elsewhere.

Accounts of her family differ. In one version of the Weidner god list she is Ninkasi's sister, while in other sources the two are treated as equivalents. Some copies of An = Anum make her Ninkasi's daughter, though scholars now find this unconvincing. In the Nippur god list, Siris follows Nintiḫal, who may have been her mother.

Worship and religion

Siris was worshipped in Assur, where she had her own sanctuary, in Isin, and in Babylon, in the temple of Mandanu, where her seat was called Ekurunna, "house of liquor". In Nippur she was venerated in the temple of Gula. At Ebla, where her name was written Zilaš, she was linked with wine and honey.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to words for wine in Semitic languages such as Phoenician and Hebrew, and in Ugaritic. Scholars link it to a Semitic root associated with fermentation.

Ancient sources: She appears in the god lists An = Anum, the Nippur god list and the Weidner god list, in Ebla offering lists, and in the Ballad of Early Rulers.

Trivia

A name for the drink. In Akkadian texts her name was used as a metonym for beer itself, written without the divine determinative.
A seat called "house of liquor". In Babylon, in the temple of Mandanu, her seat was named Ekurunna, meaning "house of liquor".
