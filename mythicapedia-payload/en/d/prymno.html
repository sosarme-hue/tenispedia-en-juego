Oceanid nymph of Greek mythology

Prymno

Prymno is one of the Oceanids, water nymphs in Greek mythology and daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. She was associated with the stern of a ship, the rear part of the vessel where the steering was handled.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Prymno belonged to the Oceanids, around three thousand daughters of Oceanus and Tethys. Her name, Πρυμνώ, means something like "undermost" or "root", and this meaning is linked to her association with the stern of a vessel.

Beyond her name, her lineage and that maritime association, ancient texts give her no deeds or episodes of her own. She appears as one more member of the vast chorus of sea and water nymphs, so her figure stands as an example of the many minor divinities that Greek myth names without developing their stories.

Trivia

A name that describes part of a ship. Prymnô means "undermost" or "root", and her link to the stern places her in the part of the ship where the steering was handled.
One of three thousand sisters. The Oceanids numbered around three thousand, so Prymno is one of many nymphs in this family who are rarely mentioned.
