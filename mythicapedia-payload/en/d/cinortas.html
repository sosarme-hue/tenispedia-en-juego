mythical king of Sparta, son of Amyclas

Cynortas

Cynortas is, in Greek mythology, a king of Sparta who appears mainly as a link in a famous genealogy. Son of Amyclas and brother of Hyacinthus, his significance lies in connecting the Spartan royal line with later generations.

The myth

According to tradition, Cynortas was the son of King Amyclas and Queen Diomede, making him brother to Hyacinthus and to other children of Amyclas, among them King Argalus, Polyboea, Laodamia or Leanira and, in some versions, even Daphne. The stories do not give the name of his wife.

Ancient authors disagree about his descendants. Apollodorus offers two versions of his son, who could be either Oebalus or Perieres. Either way, the son succeeded him on the throne. In epic poetry, Tyndareus, the foster father of Helen, is imagined as a son of Oebalus.

After the death of his brother Argalus, Cynortas inherited the kingdom and eventually became king of Sparta. His tomb was said to be shown near Scias, in the city.

Worship and religion

His tomb was a recognised site in Sparta, near Scias, that visitors could see.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus gives the versions about his descendants, and epic poetry makes Tyndareus his grandson through Oebalus.
