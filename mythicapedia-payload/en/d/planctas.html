wandering rocks of Greek mythology

Planctae

The Planctae, also known as the Wandering Rocks, were a group of moving rocks between which the sea raged violently. They appear in Homer's Odyssey and in Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica, and scholars still debate where they were located.

The myth

In Homer, Circe warns Odysseus that the Planctae smash ships; only the Argo, on its homeward journey, got through. Wreckage that falls between them is scattered by the sea or destroyed by flames. Hera, who favoured Jason, is said to have sped the Argo safely through.

Apollonius of Rhodes separates the Planctae from the Symplegades, or Clashing Rocks, which the Argo met on the outward voyage toward the Golden Fleece. Athena aided the crew at the Symplegades, while Thetis and the Nereids helped at the Planctae, though Hera remains the figure who orchestrated the rescue.

Legacy

Scholars have debated the rocks' location, proposing sites near Sicily and Mount Etna or in northwest Greece. Tim Severin pointed to the island of Sesola, off Lefkada, as a possible origin for the legend.

Ancient sources: They appear in Homer's Odyssey and Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica.

Trivia

Two possible routes. In the Odyssey, Circe gives Odysseus two routes to Ithaca: one leads past the Planctae and the other to Scylla and Charybdis, and Odysseus chooses the latter.
Cape Skilla. Tim Severin noted that a headland near Lefkada still bears the name Cape Skilla, which supports his theory about where the legend was set.
