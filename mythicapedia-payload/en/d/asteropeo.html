Paeonian hero of the Trojan War

Asteropaios

Asteropaios is a Paeonian leader allied with the Trojans in Homer's Iliad. The grandson of a river god, he is remembered as the only Trojan warrior able to draw blood from Achilles.

The myth

In the Iliad, Asteropaios arrives at Troy shortly before the events of the poem, leading the Paeonians. He was ambidextrous and could throw two spears at once, a rare skill among warriors.

In Book XII, during the Trojan assault on the Achaean wall, he fought in the same division as the Lycians Sarpedon and Glaucus, helping Hector breach the rampart. In Book XXI, while Achilles was slaughtering Trojans beside the river Scamander, the river god himself gave Asteropaios the courage to face the Achaean hero.

Asteropaios threw both spears at once: one struck Achilles' shield, the other wounded his right forearm and drew blood. Achilles answered with his ash spear, which pierced the Paeonian, who died while trying to pull it from the ground. Achilles then boasted of his own lineage and left the corpse on the riverbank to be eaten by eels and fish. Later, at the funeral games for Patroclus, his bronze and tin corslet and silver-studded sword were awarded as prizes.

Legacy

The asteroid 4805 Asteropaios is named after the hero.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Iliad, in Books XII, XXI and XXIII.

Trivia

The only one to wound Achilles. Asteropaios is the only Trojan in the Iliad who draws blood from Achilles, though he dies shortly afterwards at the hands of the Achaean hero.
An ambidextrous warrior. He could throw two spears at the same time, one with each hand, a skill the poem singles out as unusual.
