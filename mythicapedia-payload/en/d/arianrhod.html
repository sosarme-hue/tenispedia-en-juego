Welsh goddess of Celtic mythology

Arianrhod

Arianrhod is a figure of Welsh mythology, daughter of Dôn and sister of Gwydion and Gilfaethwy, whose most important role comes in the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi. Her story, full of curses, magical births and a chain of deceptions, makes her one of the most complex female figures of insular Celtic tradition.

The myth

In the Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi, written around the twelfth century, her uncle Math fab Mathonwy, King of Gwynedd, would die unless he kept his feet in the lap of a virgin whenever he was not at war. His nephew Gilfaethwy lusted after Goewin, the footholder, and he and his brother Gwydion started a war with King Pryderi of Dyfed that drew Math away from his court. In his absence Gilfaethwy raped Goewin. When Math returned he punished his nephews by turning them into animals and married Goewin to ease her shame. Gwydion then suggested their sister Arianrhod as the new footholder. To test her virginity, Math asked her to step over his magic wand; when she did, she gave birth to a boy, Dylan ail Don, who fled to the sea after being baptised, and to a shapeless mass that Gwydion hid in a chest and that became Lleu Llaw Gyffes.

Angry at the humiliation she had suffered at Math's court, Arianrhod placed three taboos, or tynged, on her son: he would have no name unless she gave him one, he would bear no arms unless she armed him, and he would never take a wife from any race now on earth. Gwydion got around the first two, and the third led Gwydion and Math to create Blodeuwedd from oak blossom, broom and meadowsweet, a story that ends in Lleu's tragedy.

Other sources differ. One of the Welsh Triads makes her the daughter of Beli Mawr and sister of Caswallawn, and mother of Gwenwynwyn and Gwanar, who join Caswallawn in his pursuit of Julius Caesar. Welsh poets of the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries seem to have known a tradition in which Arianrhod became Math's footholder, and some scholars suggest that in an earlier version Gwydion was the father of her sons.

Legacy

Her palace, Caer Arianrhod, is linked to a rock formation visible at low tide west of Llandwrog in north-west Wales, one of the places associated with the events of the Fourth Branch. In Welsh the name also designates the constellation Corona Borealis. Robert Graves saw her as an aspect of Ceridwen. The name appears for characters and places in Diana Wynne Jones's novel The Merlin Conspiracy, in the game Fire Emblem: Three Houses, and in Judy Chicago's artwork The Dinner Party.

Ancient sources: The Mabinogion, especially the Fourth Branch, and the Welsh Triads.
