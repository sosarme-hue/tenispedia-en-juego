mythical king of Sparta, Heracleid

Eurysthenes

Eurysthenes was, according to tradition, one of the Heracleidae and a king of Sparta alongside his twin brother Procles. He is regarded as the mythical founder of the Agiad dynasty, and his story explains the origin of Sparta's unusual dual kingship.

The myth

Eurysthenes was a son of Aristodemus and Argia, and a descendant of Heracles. After the Heracleidae invaded the Peloponnesus, he and his twin Procles received the land of Lacedaemon. According to the story, Aristodemus was murdered at Delphi by the Atreids before he could name a successor, and his wife did not know which of the twins was the elder.

The Delphic oracle settled the matter by proposing that both should rule, which is the origin of the dual monarchy. To decide who came first, the choice fell on Eurysthenes, because his mother had fed and cleaned him before his brother. He married Lathria, daughter of Thersander, and became the father of his successor, Agis I.

Legacy

Sparta's dual kingship, split into the Agiad and Eurypontid lines, lasted for centuries. Later, the Spartan government denied Eurysthenes and Procles the title of archēgetēs, or founding magistrate, because they had not founded a state, and granted the honor to their son Agis and to his grandson instead.

Trivia

A domestic tiebreaker. To decide which of the twins was the elder, the story turns to an everyday detail: their mother fed and cleaned Eurysthenes first.
A denied title. The Spartans did not grant Eurysthenes or Procles the title of archēgetēs, which they gave to their descendants instead.
