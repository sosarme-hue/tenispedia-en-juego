Plataea

Plataea is a nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of the river Asopus, to whom tradition attributes the name of the city of Plataea. Her story is tied to the origin of that town and to a famous trick played by Zeus.

The myth

According to tradition, Plataea was one of the daughters of the river Asopus, and several of her sisters were carried off by Zeus. The sources do not agree on her mother: Metope is usually named, although no source states that Plataea was her daughter. Some authors identify her with Oeroe, another daughter of Asopus, who gave her name to a stream in the region.

In one episode, Hera, angry with Zeus, withdrew to Euboea. Zeus asked the wise king Cithaeron for advice, and Cithaeron suggested a pretended wedding: Zeus had a wooden image made and announced that he was marrying Plataea, daughter of Asopus, which brought the goddess back.

Worship and religion

In the city of Plataea there was a heroon dedicated to her, placed beside the altar and the image of Zeus Eleutherios.

Legacy

The name of the city of Plataea is linked to her according to some, while others derive it from the oar blade, the platē, which gave the Plataeans their name, since they earned their living by rowing.

Trivia

A wedding with a wooden statue. To win back Hera, Zeus announced a wedding to Plataea and presented a wooden image as if it were the bride. The ruse is the best-known anecdote about this figure.
