spirit of the sky and wind in Inuit religion

Silap Inua

Silap Inua is a spirit of the sky, the wind and the weather in Inuit religion, conceived as the vital force that fills everything that exists. He is one of the oldest figures in this tradition, and he is notable for his almost impersonal character.

The myth

Silap Inua is imagined as a formless being who is never depicted, and he rarely takes part in stories, since tradition gives him few personality traits. Although he is considered male, he appears more as a principle than as a character.

According to tradition, he is also the substance from which souls are made, and he controls what happens in each person's life. He has a darker side as well: he lures children who play far from camp out onto the tundra, and they are never seen again.

Anthropologists believe the belief is very old and that, over the last thousand years, he lost prominence to Sedna, goddess of sea mammals, and to the Caribou Mother, once these animals became the main sources of food.

Worship and religion

Among the Copper Inuit, shamans were believed to draw their power from this “Wind Indweller,” so their helping spirits were also called silap inue. Among the Siberian Yupik he was pictured as a mighty hunter who watched whether people respected customs and traditions.

Trivia

Mix-up with Nanook. Christian missionaries identified Nanook, the polar bear spirit, as the supreme deity of the Inuit. According to the sources, Silap Inua is much closer to that role.
A name with many meanings. Depending on the region and language, the term can mean universe, outer space, sky or weather, showing how broad the concept is.
