Roman goddess of female fertility

Libera

Libera is a Roman goddess of female fertility, companion of Liber and part of the Aventine triad worshipped alongside Ceres. She has little mythology of her own, and she was often confused with Proserpina or Ariadne.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Libera has few stories of her own. Her origins are uncertain: she may have been an Italic goddess or one of the pre-Roman peoples of southern Italy. Her name is the feminine form of Liber and means roughly "the free one". She is presented as the companion or sister of Liber, god of male fertility and protector of the plebeians' rights.

What we know of her comes mainly from her identifications with other goddesses. Sources sometimes equate her with Proserpina and sometimes with Ariadne, so much of what is told about her is borrowed from those Greek figures. Cicero considered her a daughter of Ceres, and Hyginus equated her with Ariadne.

Worship and religion

Libera received worship in the Aventine temple, built around 493 BC and shared with Ceres and Liber, each with their own cella. She may have been honoured on March 17 during the Liberalia, or during the Cerealia in April, where she was subordinate to Ceres. Around 205 BC she was officially identified with Proserpina, when the cult was reformed in the Greek manner with priestesses of Greek origin.

Legacy

Libera has little presence of her own in art or literature. The abduction of Proserpina, by contrast, was a frequent subject for sculptors and painters of the Renaissance and later periods. In Mtarfa, Malta, a temple dedicated to Proserpina once stood, and only fragments survive today.

Ancient sources: Cicero calls her a daughter of Ceres, Hyginus equates her with Ariadne, and St. Augustine describes her as a goddess of female fertility, in sources from late antiquity.

Trivia

A temple in Malta. In Mtarfa, a suburb of ancient Melite, there was a temple dedicated to Proserpina. Its ruins were quarried away between the 17th and 18th centuries.
A free goddess. Her name is the feminine of Liber, meaning "free". For this reason she is linked to the liberty of the Roman plebeians.
