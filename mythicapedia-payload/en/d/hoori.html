Japanese god of hunting and grain

Hoori

Hoori is a god of Japanese mythology, the youngest son of Ninigi and a hunter by trade. According to tradition, he was the grandfather of Emperor Jimmu, the first emperor of Japan, which makes him an ancestor of the imperial line.

The myth

According to the account preserved in the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Hoori quarrelled with his elder brother Hoderi, a fisherman, because he had borrowed his fish-hook and lost it. Since Hoderi would not accept another hook, Hoori went down to the bottom of the sea to look for it, and there he met Toyotamahime, daughter of the sea god Ōwatatsumi. She helped him recover the hook, and he married her.

The couple lived for three years in an undersea palace, but Hoori grew homesick and returned home. Once he gave back the hook, his brother forgave him. During the birth of their child, Toyotamahime asked Hoori not to try to see her true form, but he broke his promise and discovered she was a wani. Ashamed, she went back to the sea and never returned. Their son, Ugayafukiaezu, was raised by Tamayorihime, Toyotamahime's younger sister, whom he married; their son was Jimmu.

Worship and religion

He is worshipped mainly as a god of cereals and grain. His other name, Hohodemi, means many harvests. The sources do not describe particular shrines or festivals dedicated to him.

Legacy

His place in the imperial genealogy makes him an important figure in the stories about Japan's origins. The sources do not mention other traces in art or language.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki record his legend, including the fish-hook dispute and his marriage to the sea god's daughter.

Trivia

A hook with a soul. In the story, Hoderi refused another hook because he believed every tool has its own spirit and is unique.
A name with two characters. In Japanese, the part of his name often read as fire is written with a different character that refers to crops, especially rice.
