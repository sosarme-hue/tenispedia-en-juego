Greek hero, mythical founder of Lindos on Rhodes

Lindus

Lindus is a figure of Greek mythology, the mythical founder of Lindos, one of the ancient cities on the island of Rhodes. Son of Cercaphus and Cydippe and grandson of the sun god Helios, his story explains how the territory of Rhodes was shared among three brothers.

The myth

According to tradition, Lindus was a son of Cercaphus and Cydippe, kings of Rhodes, and a grandson of the sun god Helios and the nymph Rhodos. The island had just risen from the waters after the flood Zeus sent to destroy the Telchines, and his father entrusted its rule to Lindus and his brothers, Ialysus and Camirus.

The three brothers split Rhodes into three zones and founded a capital in each, naming them after themselves. Lindus thus gave his name to Lindos, a city that still keeps his memory alive on the island.

Trivia

Three brothers, three capitals. Each of the three brothers founded a capital on the island and named it after himself, as happened with Lindos.
A newly risen island. According to the story, Rhodes had just emerged from the waters after a flood sent by Zeus against the Telchines.
