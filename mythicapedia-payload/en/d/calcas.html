seer of Greek mythology

Calchas

Calchas was the chief seer of the Greek army during the Trojan War, according to the tradition preserved in the epic tales. He matters because, as the voice of Apollo, his prophecies shaped key turning points of the conflict, from the sacrifice of Iphigenia to the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles.

The myth

According to tradition, Calchas was the son of Thestor and grandson of the seer Idmon, and his family practised prophecy as a hereditary craft. Ancient sources differ on his origin, naming either Megara or Mycenae, and he received his gift of prophecy from Apollo.

In the Iliad he appears at the very start of the poem. Trapped with the fleet at Aulis, he declared that Artemis was withholding the winds and demanded the sacrifice of Agamemnon's daughter Iphigenia. Later he revealed that Apollo's plague would end only if the captive Chryseis was returned to her father, a priest of the god. Agamemnon reacted furiously, and the dispute spilled into the quarrel with Achilles over Briseis. Some accounts credit Calchas with advising the building of the Trojan Horse, while others give that idea to Odysseus. He also foretold that Troy would fall in the tenth year of the war.

After the war he did not sail home with the fleet. Later versions place him at Colophon or at the sanctuary of Claros, where he competed with the seer Mopsus and lost. Calchas died of grief. Another account says that, after another seer predicted his death and the prophecy failed, he burst into laughter and choked to death.

Legacy

His name appears in Homer's Iliad and in Sophocles' tragedies such as Ajax. In medieval and later retellings he became a Trojan defector and the father of Chryseis, now called Cressida. William Shakespeare made him a character in his play Troilus and Cressida.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Iliad and in the lost Cypria of the Epic Cycle, and in Sophocles' tragedy Ajax.

Trivia

A disputed name. The most accepted etymology links his name to pondering or melancholy, in the sense of being deep in thought.
A death by laughter. According to one version, he died laughing when he saw that a prophecy about his own death had failed.
