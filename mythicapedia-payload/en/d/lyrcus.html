legendary Argive hero, illegitimate son of Abas

Lyrcus

Lyrcus is a figure of Greek mythology, the illegitimate son of Abas, king of Argos. His story is tied to the city of Lyrceia, which makes him a figure of local Argive tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Pausanias in his Description of Greece, the city of Lyrceia, on the road called Climax between Argos and Orneae, was once called Lynceia. That name was linked to Lynceus, one of the fifty sons of Aegyptus. After his brothers were killed by the Danaids on their wedding night, Lynceus fled Argos and reached the city. He signalled his safe arrival to his wife Hypermnestra by holding up a torch, and she answered from Larissa, the citadel of Argos.

Lyrcus was the son of Abas, who was himself a son of Lynceus and Hypermnestra. He was expelled from Argos and took over Lynceia, which was then renamed Lyrceia. Eventually the town fell into ruins, and only the statue of Lyrcus on a pillar remained. Homer does not mention Lyrceia in the Catalogue because it was already deserted at the time of the Trojan expedition.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions Lyrcus and Lyrceia in his Description of Greece.
