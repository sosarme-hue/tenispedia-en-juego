Norse giantess

Járnsaxa

Járnsaxa is a giantess, or jötunn, in Norse mythology, best known as the mother of Magni, the son of Thor. Her name is usually translated as 'armed with an iron sword', linking her to the world of the giants, which often stood in opposition to that of the gods. She appears in only a few sources, so her figure is known mainly from brief mentions.

The myth

According to the Skáldskaparmál, the second part of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Járnsaxa was Thor's lover and the mother of his son Magni. Her story appears mainly within the tale of Thor's duel with the giant Hrungnir. After that fight, Thor gives Magni the splendid horse Gullfaxi, which had belonged to the giant. Odin then rebukes him: Thor should have offered the animal to his own father rather than to the son of a giantess.

The Hyndluljóð, a poem of the Poetic Edda also known as Völuspá hin skamma, names her among the Nine Mothers of Heimdall, the watchman of the gods. Snorri, by contrast, says nothing about Móði, another son sometimes attributed to Thor, so the sources leave open whether Járnsaxa was his mother too. Snorri also uses her name in a poetic periphrasis for Sif, Thor's wife, calling her 'the rival of Járnsaxa', a phrase that suggests the two shared the same man.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál in the Prose Edda, and the Hyndluljóð in the Poetic Edda (also called Völuspá hin skamma).
