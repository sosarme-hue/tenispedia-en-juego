Wyvern

The wyvern is a winged creature of European tradition, especially medieval and English, distinguished from the dragon by having two legs, two wings and, often, a pointed tail. It matters most in British heraldry, where it is treated as a figure distinct from the dragon.

The myth

The wyvern has no large cycle of stories of its own, so its history is largely reconstructed from its name and its changing role in art. The English word wyver comes from Anglo-French guivre, which descends from Latin vipera, meaning 'viper'. This links the creature to the serpent, and to related beings such as the French vouivre and the Central European lindworm. Medievalist William Sayers proposed a different route: that the word merged with an Old English term for a 'light javelin', so the image of a flying serpent arose from that blend.

Winged serpents appear much earlier in Egypt, Mesopotamia and Greece. Herodotus describes winged serpents from Arabia that sacred Egyptian ibises stop at a mountain pass, and Euripides has them pull the chariot of Helios carrying Medea. Ovid gives them wings and fiery breath. Between the fourteenth and sixteenth centuries, English heraldic texts often used 'dragon', 'wyrm' and 'wyver' almost interchangeably, until the distinction between the four-legged dragon and the two-legged wyvern became established.

Legacy

The wyvern is a frequent charge in English heraldry and vexillology, and appears as a crest or supporter on arms such as those of the city of Leicester, the city of Kazan (with the Zilant) and Portugal. As an emblem and mascot it has been adopted by schools and sports teams, such as the Korean SK Wyverns and Sydney's Newington College. It also gave its name to a 1950s Vauxhall car and to a British strike aircraft that saw service during the Suez Crisis of 1956. In modern fantasy it appears in novels and video games such as the Monster Hunter series.

Ancient sources: No ancient text names the wyvern as such. Its winged-serpent precursors appear in Herodotus (Histories), Euripides (Medea, 431 BCE) and Ovid (Metamorphoses).

Trivia

Thyrus of Terni. The green dragon on the emblem of the ancient Umbrian city of Terni is called Thyrus by its citizens.
A wyvern in Kazan. The Zilant, a wyvern, appears on the coat of arms of Kazan, capital of the Republic of Tatarstan.
