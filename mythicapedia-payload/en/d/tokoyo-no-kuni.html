Shinto otherworld

Tokoyo no Kuni

Tokoyo no Kuni is an otherworld in Shinto mythology, inhabited by kami and ancestral spirits who keep eternal youth. Its image shifts from one account to another, and it matters because it appears in several myths of Japan's earliest chronicles.

The myth

According to the Kojiki and the Nihongi, Tajimamori travelled to Tokoyo during the reign of Emperor Suinin to gain immortality. In the myth of Ōkuninushi, this ancient ruler handed the land to the Amatsukami and withdrew into the unseen world, while his son, sent by Amaterasu, went on to run the Izumo shrine.

Motoori Norinaga distinguished three senses of the term: a world of perpetual darkness, a world where no one grew old, and a land across the sea. Some scholars identify it with Ne-no-kuni or Yomi, but there is no consensus on how these realms relate.

Worship and religion

According to the Nihongi, during the reign of Empress Kōgyoku a fanatical cult devoted to the god of Tokoyo was said to have emerged. There is no clear evidence of a lasting cult to the realm itself.

Legacy

In the tale of Urashima Tarō, Tokoyo is identified with Mount Penglai. Two fossae on the asteroid 162173 Ryugu are named after these stories.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihongi, which mention Tajimamori, the myth of Ōkuninushi and the cult in Empress Kōgyoku's reign.

Trivia

A quest for immortality. According to the old accounts, Tajimamori was sent to Tokoyo by Emperor Suinin in search of a fruit or remedy that would grant eternal life.
Three Tokoyos. The scholar Motoori Norinaga proposed three meanings for the term, showing that the realm never had a single definition.
