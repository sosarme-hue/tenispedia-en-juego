lake in Norse mythology

Ámsvartnir

Ámsvartnir ("pitch black" in Old Norse) is a lake in Norse mythology that surrounds the island of Lyngvi, where Fenrir was bound. It appears in only one ancient text, which makes it a notable case for scholars.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, the Æsir tried twice to restrain Fenrir, first with the chain Leding and then with the stronger Droma, without success. In the end they turned to Gleipnir, a light, soft, silken ribbon made from impossible things: the sound of a cat's footfall, a woman's beard, the roots of mountains, the sinews of a bear, the breath of fish and the spittle of a bird.

The god Tyr offered to put his hand in the wolf's mouth as a pledge. When Fenrir realised he had been tricked, he shut his jaws and bit off the god's hand. Snorri Sturluson places the episode at Lake Ámsvartnir, which the gods crossed on their way to Lyngvi. The German scholar Rudolf Simek, of the University of Bonn, suggests that Snorri may have invented the lake, since it appears in no other source.

Ancient sources: It is mentioned only in the Gylfaginning of the Prose Edda, written by Snorri Sturluson in the 13th century.

Trivia

Ingredients of Gleipnir. The ribbon that finally bound Fenrir was made from impossible ingredients, such as the sound of a cat's footfall or a woman's beard.
A lost hand. Tyr lost his hand after offering it as a pledge so that the wolf would accept the ribbon.
