Libertalia

Libertalia (also Libertatia) was a purported pirate colony founded in the late 17th century on the northern coast of Madagascar by Captain Misson and the priest Caraccioli. Its existence is widely doubted: it rests on a single source, and most scholars regard it as fictional, though it has become a symbol of the pirate utopia idea.

The myth

According to Charles Johnson's account, Misson was a Frenchman who lost his faith in Rome, disgusted by the decadence of the Papal Court. There he met Caraccioli, a priest who, during long voyages, persuaded him and much of the crew of the warship Victoire that everything should be held in common. The two hundred sailors seized the ship, chose Misson as captain and turned to piracy, freeing enslaved people from slave ships. They flew a white flag reading "For God and Liberty" and, after rounding the Cape of Good Hope, founded a settlement on Madagascar in a fertile bay with plenty of water.

In the colony, the "Liberi" pooled their plunder, abolished money, spoke a mixed common language and lived by farming and herding. The story also features the pirate Thomas Tew, who joined the project, and Captain Kidd, who lost part of his crew to the settlers. The decline came with an attack by Portuguese ships and an uprising of Malagasy natives, in which Caraccioli died. Misson fled with some of his men and loot, the colony was abandoned, and both captains eventually died at sea.

Legacy

Libertalia has inspired fiction and music, including Imma Tubella's novel about pirates, the video game Uncharted 4 and the film Against All Flags (1952). It also serves as a symbol of the so-called pirate utopia, a term for egalitarian communities formed outside the law in the world of piracy. A pirates museum in Antananarivo commemorates it.

Ancient sources: Its only ancient source is A General History of the Pyrates, published in London in 1724 under the name of Captain Charles Johnson, a work that scholars largely attribute to Daniel Defoe.

Trivia

Single source. Libertalia comes almost exclusively from an 1724 book that includes genuine accounts of other pirates alongside this episode, which raises doubts about its truth.
Latin name. Some accounts link the name to the Latin word liberum, meaning "free".
