Ovinnik

The ovinnik is a malevolent spirit of the threshing barn in Slavic folklore, linked to the building where grain was dried and threshed. He was feared for his habit of setting these barns on fire.

The myth

His name derives from ovin, meaning 'barn'. In popular belief, the ovinnik lived in the threshing house and was prone to setting the grain alight, ruining the harvest of the village.

To appease him, peasants offered him roosters and bliny, thin pancakes. Folklore added a distinctive twist: on New Year's Eve, an ovinnik's touch foretold the fortune of the coming year. A warm touch promised luck and prosperity, while a cold one foretold unhappiness.

Worship and religion

Rather than an organised cult, the ovinnik belonged to the household and farming customs of villages, with offerings meant to protect the harvest from misfortune.

Trivia

A touch that predicts the year. On New Year's Eve, the temperature of an ovinnik's touch revealed what the year would bring: warm meant good fortune, cold meant unhappiness.
