Great Duke of Hell in Christian demonology

Astaroth

Astaroth is a demon in Christian demonology, regarded as a Great Duke of Hell. His name derives from the Phoenician goddess Astarte, a figure of the ancient Near East who was demonized in the Middle Ages and recast as a male demon.

The myth

According to medieval and early modern demonological traditions, Astaroth was originally an angel, specifically a seraph and prince of the Order of Thrones, who was corrupted after visiting the world of men. He is thereafter described as a crowned prince of Hell, ranking in the first infernal hierarchy alongside Beelzebub and Lucifer.

His figure grows out of the goddess Astarte, called Ashtoreth in the Hebrew Bible and, in the plural, Ashtaroth. Early Greek and Latin translations transliterated that plural form, which was later read as the name of a male demon.

In the legend of Saint Bartholomew, Astaroth appears as a demon who deceives the people of India, inflicting diseases on them and offering cures in exchange for offerings. Bartholomew defeats him and frees the people, although the account ends with the saint's martyrdom at the hands of a local king.

Legacy

The name first appears as a male demon in the Book of Abramelin and recurs in later occult grimoires. In popular culture it appears in the song "Astaroth" from the album Finisterra by the Spanish folk metal band Mägo de Oz, and as the final boss of Capcom's Ghosts 'n Goblins video game series.

Ancient sources: The Hebrew Bible mentions Astarte as Ashtoreth and Ashtaroth; the male demon's name first appears in the Book of Abramelin, written around 1458.

Trivia

A name that changed gender. The name comes from a female goddess, but in the Hebrew Bible it appears in a feminine plural form that early translations did not make clear.
A black-faced devil. Ælfric of Eynsham describes Astaroth as an immense Ethiopian with a sharp face and full beard, which Benjamin Thorpe translates as a "black-faced devil".
