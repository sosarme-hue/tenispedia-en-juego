Son of Creon, fiancé of Antigone

Haemon

Haemon is, in Greek mythology, the son of Creon and Eurydice, rulers of Thebes, and the fiancé of Antigone. His story, told in differing and contradictory versions, makes him one of the tragic figures of the Theban cycle.

The myth

According to the tradition, Haemon was the son of Creon and Eurydice. After the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices in their duel for the throne of Thebes, Creon seized power and declared Polynices a traitor, forbidding under penalty of death that he receive funeral rites. Antigone defied the decree, but she was caught trying to burn her brother's corpse, and Creon ordered Haemon to bury her alive. The young man begged his father for mercy, but was not heeded. When the blind seer Tiresias warned Creon that the gods disapproved, the king revoked the sentence, but too late: Antigone had hanged herself. Haemon, finding her dead, killed himself with his own sword, and Eurydice also took her life upon hearing the news.

Ancient accounts do not agree on the ending. In one version, Haemon pretended to obey, fled with Antigone, and the two hid among shepherds; they had a son, Maeon, who came to Thebes as a grown man to take part in games, but Creon recognized him by a mark. In that version Haemon killed his wife Antigone and then took his own life. Another tradition, recorded by Hyginus, says that Creon murdered the boy, and that Haemon killed both Antigone and himself. A third version drops all of this and claims that Haemon was devoured by the Sphinx before Oedipus arrived, which is why Creon offered the throne to whoever freed Thebes from the monster.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, the Oedipodea, Sophocles (Antigone), Euripides (Antigone and Phoenician Women) and Hyginus record differing versions of his story.

Trivia

A telling name. Haemon comes from the ancient Greek Haímôn, meaning 'bloody', a name that fits the tragic fate of the character.
A mark gave him away. In one version, Creon recognized his grandson, the son of Haemon and Antigone, in Thebes by a mark he bore on his body.
