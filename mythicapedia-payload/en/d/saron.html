mythical king of Troezen, eponym of the Saronic Gulf

Saron

Saron was a king of Troezen, a city in the Greek Argolid, remembered in myth for giving his name to the Saronic Gulf. His drowning at sea is told as the origin of that place name and of the sanctuary of Artemis Saronia.

The myth

According to the myth, Saron succeeded Althepus as the third king of Troezen. A keen hunter, he once chased a doe that dashed into the sea; the animal swam farther and farther out, and Saron followed it into open water until his strength gave out and he drowned.

The waves carried his body back to the sacred grove of Artemis by the lagoon, where he was buried inside the enclosure. From then on, the stretch of sea where he died bore his name.

Worship and religion

He built a sanctuary to Artemis Saronia beside the sea, in a shallow, marshy lagoon known as the Phoebaean Lagoon. In the second century BC a festival called the Saronia was celebrated there in honour of the goddess.

Legacy

His name survives in the Saronic Gulf, in a small town called Saron near Troezen, and in Saronia, another name for Troezen itself.

Ancient sources: Aelius Aristides seems to imply that Saron became a kind of sea deity, like Glaucus.

Trivia

Phoebaean Lagoon. The sanctuary stood in a shallow, marshy lagoon that the Greeks called the Phoebaean Lagoon.
A fatal doe. What led to his death was a doe that fled into the sea and drew him after her into open waters.
From god to hero. The scholar Henning Börm speculates that Saron may originally have been a god who pursued Artemis herself, later downgraded to a hero over the centuries.
