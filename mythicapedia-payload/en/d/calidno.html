mythical king of Thebes

Calydnus

Calydnus is a mythical king of Thebes in Greek myth, mentioned only by two late authors. He is credited with building the city's first fortifications.

The myth

According to one version, Calydnus was a son of Uranus, though his mother is not named. He was the first mythical king of Thebes, and the city was thought to have been called Calydna after him.

Another account credits him with building the city's first fortifications, which is why Thebes was sometimes called 'the citadel of Calydnus'. In this version, Calydnus was succeeded by Ogygus.

Legacy

A mythical figure named Calydnus also gave his name to an island near Troy, today known as Calymnos.

Trivia

Citadel of Calydnus. Thebes was sometimes called 'the citadel of Calydnus', a reference to the fortifications attributed to him.
An island with his name. The island near Troy now known as Calymnos took its name from Calydnus.
