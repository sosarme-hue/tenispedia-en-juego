Odin's auger in the myth of the mead of poetry

Rati

Rati is the auger with which Odin gained entry to the mountain where the giant Suttung kept the mead of poetry. It belongs to Norse mythology and is one of the tools that explain how the gods obtained this sacred drink.

The myth

In Norse tradition, Odin wanted the mead of poetry, guarded by the giant Suttung inside the mountain Hnitbjörg. To reach it, he turned to Baugi, Suttung's brother, and asked him to bore through the rock with the auger Rati.

Baugi claimed the hole was finished, but when Odin blew into it, the stone bits flew back at him, showing that it had not gone all the way through. He told Baugi to drill again, and this time the bits flew inward. Odin then turned himself into a snake and slid into the hole, and Baugi tried to stab him with the auger but missed. In this way Odin reached the mead.

Ancient sources: The story appears in the Skáldskaparmál section of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which describes the auger used in the quest.

Trivia

A drill with a name. In the Prose Edda, the auger is given the name Rati, which is unusual for a tool in Norse stories.
A trick exposed by a breath. Odin uncovered Baugi's deception simply by blowing into the hole and seeing the stone fragments come back toward him.
