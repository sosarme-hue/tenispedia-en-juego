Falacer

Falacer, also called Fálacre, is an ancient Italic deity almost forgotten in Roman religion, and very little is known about him. He is notable mainly for having had his own flamen, one of the priests of Rome's minor cults.

The myth

Ancient texts say very little about his story. Varro refers to him as the divine father (dīvus pater) Falacer and states that the flamen Falacer took his name from him, though he admitted he did not know his characteristics.

Johann Adam Hartung suggested he was an epithet of Jupiter, based on Festus's report that falandum was the Etruscan word for "heaven". The archaeologist Andrea Carandini considered him the male counterpart of the ancient Roman goddess Pales. His name also appears in the Sabine town of Falacrina, where some link him to a river deity, while other sources credit him with guarding fruit trees.

Worship and religion

He had an official flamen in Rome, the second-to-last of the twelve minor flamines, which suggests his cult held some standing in the city's religious life.

Legacy

It has been suggested that Falacer was the eponymous ancestor of the Falisci and Falerii, peoples whose names are related to his.

Ancient sources: Varro is the main ancient source mentioning him, along with Festus, who provides the related Etruscan word.
