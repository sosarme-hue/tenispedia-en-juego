hero of Russian folk literature

Yeruslan Lazarevich

Yeruslan Lazarevich is a young and handsome hero of Russian folk literature, the protagonist of The Tale of Eruslane Lazarevich, which spread widely among ordinary people thanks to its many military and romantic adventures.

The myth

The tale appears under several spellings, such as Eruslan, Uruslan or the German Jeruslan Lasarewitch. Many scholars think its core comes from the exploits of Rostam, the hero of the Persian Shahnameh. Vladimir Stasov proposed this link in 1868, and the story may have reached Russia through the Tatars. Shared motifs include the combat between father and son, like Rustem and Sohrab, and Rustem's campaign in Mazandaran to free King Kay Kaus, here recast as a Persian king named Kirkous.

The tale also adds episodes with little connection to one another, such as meeting the hero Ivan, fighting a sea-king dragon, and marrying a princess of the Sunflower City. These adventures have no direct counterpart in the Shahnameh, though they resemble various Eastern tales. The story also influenced tales about Ilya of Murom.

Legacy

The tale circulated widely in illustrated prints called lubok, and its influence can be seen in some retellings of tales about Ilya of Murom.

Ancient sources: The tale survives in seventeenth-century manuscripts, among them the Undolsky collection version, considered the most valuable.
