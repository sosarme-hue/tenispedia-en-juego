wise counsellor of the Mahabharata, incarnation of Dharma

Vidura

Vidura is one of the central characters of the Mahabharata, the great Indian epic. Son of the sage Vyasa and a maidservant, he serves as adviser at the court of Hastinapura, and he is regarded as an incarnation of the god Dharma, which explains his reputation for wisdom and his defence of justice.

The myth

According to the myth, Vidura was born through niyoga: the sage Vyasa fathered the sons of King Vichitravirya with his widows Ambika and Ambalika. Ambika closed her eyes in fear and bore Dhritarashtra, who was blind; Ambalika turned pale with horror and bore Pandu, who was pale. The queens then sent their maidservant Parishrami, who showed no fear and gave birth to Vidura. Another version of the tale says Vidura was an avatar of Dharma, cursed by the sage Mandavya.

Because he was born to a servant woman he could not reign, but as counsellor he was the real ruler during the reign of his blind brother, until Duryodhana came of age. He warned the Pandavas of Duryodhana's schemes, such as the plan to burn them in the house of lac. Together with Prince Vikarna, he was the only one to protest when Draupadi was humiliated at the Kaurava court, and later he broke with the Kauravas to side with the Pandavas. When Krishna came as a peace envoy, he refused Duryodhana's palace and stayed in Vidura's humble home, where Vidura's wife Sulabha mistakenly offered him banana peels instead of the fruit.

Legacy

Vidura gives his name to the Vidura Niti, a collection of advice on good governance and wise conduct found in the Udyoga Parva. In modern Theosophy he is considered a Mahachohan.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata, the great Indian epic, especially in chapters 33 to 40 of the Udyoga Parva, where his counsel is recorded.

Trivia

The banana peels. Absorbed in Krishna, Sulabha peeled bananas and offered him the peels instead of the fruit. When Vidura came home, he found Krishna eating them.
A ruler without a throne. Although his birth as a servant's son barred him from kingship, Vidura governed Hastinapura in practice during the reign of his brother Dhritarashtra.
