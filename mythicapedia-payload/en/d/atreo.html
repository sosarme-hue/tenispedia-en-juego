Atreus

Atreus is a king of Mycenae in Greek mythology, the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus, who later led the Greeks in the Trojan War. His story, marked by a bitter rivalry with his brother Thyestes and a horrific revenge, gave its name to the House of Atreus.

The myth

According to myth, Atreus was the son of Pelops and Hippodamia. He and his twin brother Thyestes were exiled from Pisa by their father after murdering their half-brother Chrysippus in their desire for the throne of Olympia. They took refuge in Mycenae, where they seized power while King Eurystheus was fighting the Heracleidae. Eurystheus had intended the arrangement to be temporary, but it became permanent when he died in battle.

Atreus vowed to sacrifice his best lamb to Artemis, but when he searched his flock he found a golden lamb and hid it with his wife, Aerope. She passed it to her lover Thyestes, who produced the animal and claimed the throne for whoever held it. Atreus won back the kingdom with advice from Hermes: Thyestes agreed to return the throne if the sun reversed its course, and according to the tradition Zeus made it happen.

Atreus then discovered Thyestes' affair with Aerope and plotted revenge. He killed Thyestes' sons, cooked them and served them at a banquet, then showed their hands and feet to his brother. Horrified, Thyestes cursed Atreus' line. An oracle advised him to father a son by his daughter Pelopia, who would kill Atreus. That son, Aegisthus, was abandoned at birth, raised by Atreus as his own, and once grown he fulfilled the prophecy.

Homer's version gives Atreus and Aerope two sons, Agamemnon and Menelaus, and a daughter, Anaxibia, whom others call Cyndragora or Astyoche. Hesiod instead gives Atreus a son, Pleisthenes, born to Cleola, daughter of Diante, or else to Aerope; in that case Atreus would be the grandfather of Agamemnon and his brothers.

Legacy

His name lives on in the word Atreidai, used for the sons of Atreus, Agamemnon and Menelaus, and more broadly for the whole House of Atreus. Some scholars have suggested that Attarsiya, the name of an Ahhiyawan leader in Hittite records, may be a rendering of Atreus, though others think it could have been a different man with the same name.

Ancient sources: Homer and Hesiod give different accounts of his descendants, and Cicero also mentions him among the ancestors of the Dioscuri.

Trivia

A name in a Hittite text. A Hittite text called The Cause Against Madduwata mentions a leader of Ahhiyawa named Attarsiya, a name some scholars connect with Atreus.
The sun in reverse. Thyestes agreed to return the throne if the sun rose in the west and set in the east. Helios did exactly that, and the throne went back to Atreus.
