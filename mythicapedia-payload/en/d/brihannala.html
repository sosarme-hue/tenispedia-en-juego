disguise name of Arjuna in the Mahabharata

Brihannala

Brihannala is the identity Arjuna, the third of the five Pandavas, assumes during the final year of exile in the Mahabharata. Under this name he works as a dance and music teacher at the court of King Virata of Matsya.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Arjuna travels to the heavenly abode of his father Indra, where apsaras entertain him. The most beautiful of them, Urvashi, falls in love with him and asks to marry him, but Arjuna declines, saying he is mortal and that marrying someone who was once his teacher would be a sin. Insulted, Urvashi curses him to lose his masculinity. Through Indra's intervention the curse is limited to one year, which Arjuna may choose, and he accepts it since it cannot be undone.

The curse proves useful during the exile. As Brihannala, a klība (a Sanskrit term for neutral gender), he teaches dance and music to the king's daughter Uttarā. When the Kauravas steal cattle from the kingdom, Prince Uttara loses his nerve on the battlefield, and Brihannala, serving as his charioteer, reveals who he is. Arjuna retrieves his weapons and alone defeats the invading army. After the victory he shows his true form to the king, who offers him Uttarā in marriage; Arjuna refuses, since he was her teacher, and asks that she be given to his son Abhimanyu.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Vana Parva and Virata Parva of the Sanskrit Hindu epic Mahabharata.
