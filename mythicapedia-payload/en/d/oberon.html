king of the fairies in medieval and Renaissance literature

Oberon

Oberon is the king of the fairies in medieval and Renaissance literature, husband of Titania and a central figure in Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream. His figure comes from medieval French romance, and his name may be linked to Alberich, a figure of Germanic legend.

The myth

In the French heroic song Huon de Bordeaux, from the early thirteenth century, Oberon first appears as the king of the fairies, met by the knight Huon as he passes through a forest. A hermit warns Huon not to speak to the king, but his courtesy in answering the fairy's greetings wins Oberon's friendship and aid.

The king is dwarfish in height yet very handsome. According to the story, an offended fairy cursed him never to grow past three years of age, but later relented and gave him great beauty as compensation. The romance makes him the child of Morgan le Fay and Julius Caesar. Among his Celtic trappings is a magic cup that is always full, providing wine or more solid food as desired.

In A Midsummer Night's Dream, Oberon quarrels with Titania over a child she wants to raise. To take revenge, he uses the juice of an enchanted flower that makes its victim fall in love with the first living thing seen. After much confusion, he undoes the spell, reconciles with Titania, and the two bless the couples returning to Athens.

Legacy

Ben Jonson wrote the masque Oberon, the Faery Prince (1611). Carl Maria von Weber's opera Oberon premiered at Covent Garden in 1826, based on a poem by Wieland, whose epic Oberon had appeared in 1780. Goethe included Oberon and Titania in Faust, and Walter Scott mentions him in Ivanhoe. Two characters in John Crowley's novel Little, Big are named Auberon. In popular culture he appears in Neil Gaiman's The Sandman, in Gargoyles and in Fate/Grand Order. The orchid genus Oberonia and a Mexican lizard, Sceloporus oberon, are named after him.

Ancient sources: The French heroic song Huon de Bordeaux, from the early thirteenth century, is his first attested appearance under this name.

Trivia

A beautiful dwarf. According to the French romance, Oberon is stuck at the height of a three-year-old because of a curse, though his beauty makes up for it.
A name on an orchid. In 1830 the botanist John Lindley named the orchid genus Oberonia after the fairy king.
