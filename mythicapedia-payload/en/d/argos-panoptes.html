hundred-eyed giant of Greek mythology

Argus Panoptes

Argus Panoptes is a giant of Greek mythology whose body was covered in eyes and who served as watchman for the nymph Io. His story, tied to Hera and Zeus, explains why the peacock's tail bears eye-like markings.

The myth

Ancient sources disagree about his parentage. Hesiod made him the son of Mycene and Arestor, others named Agenor or Inachus as his father, and some imagined him as born from Earth itself. Some versions connect him closely with Io and make him the father of Iasus, king of Argos.

His epithet Panoptes, "all-seeing", refers to his appearance: according to Apollodorus he had eyes over his whole body, and since only some of them slept at any moment, there were always watchful eyes open. This vigilance made him Hera's most faithful servant. Earlier, he had served the Olympian gods by killing Echidna, the serpent-legged monster, while she slept in her cave.

His final assignment was to guard a white heifer, which was really Io, a nymph Zeus had been pursuing and whom he had transformed. Hera tied the heifer to an olive tree at Nemea. Zeus sent Hermes to free her, and Hermes, disguised as a shepherd, lulled Argus's eyes to sleep with his pipe and with tales. Some versions say he killed Argus with a stone; others say he beheaded him with a crescent-shaped sword. This earned Hermes the epithet Argeiphontes, "Argus-slayer". Hera, to honour her watchman, placed his hundred eyes in the tails of peacocks.

Legacy

His name survives in the scientific names of several animals with eye-spot patterns, such as the Argus monitor, Varanus panoptes, the Argus pheasant, Argusianus argus, and the fish Cephalopholis argus. The scene of Hermes killing Argus was a popular subject in European painting of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, who made him the son of Mycene and Arestor, and Apollodorus, who summarises his various parentages and his story, as well as Ovid, who describes him as having a hundred eyes.

Trivia

Peacocks. According to the myth, Hera preserved Argus's hundred eyes in the peacock's tail to honour her faithful guardian.
Argeiphontes. Hermes received the epithet Argeiphontes, "Argus-slayer", after killing the giant.
