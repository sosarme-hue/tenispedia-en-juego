Mother goddess of the Tamil language

Tamil Thai

Tamil Thai, or 'Tamil Mother', is the personification of the Tamil language as a motherly goddess. Her image emerged during the Tamil Renaissance of the nineteenth century and remains a highly visible symbol of Tamil identity in Tamil Nadu.

The myth

Tamil Thai is less an ancient myth than a modern construction. By 1879 Samuel Vedanayagam Pillai was already alluding to the Tamil mother, and the figure became popular through a song in the play Manonmaniyam (1891) by Manonmaniam Sundaram Pillai. That song, the Tamil Thai Valthu, was adopted as the state song of Tamil Nadu.

She is described as a fertile womb that gave birth to the Tamils and, according to Sundaram Pillai, all Dravidian peoples, and whose milk bestows knowledge.

Worship and religion

Her iconography borrows traits from the Hindu goddess Saraswati, especially her veena, and she is sometimes identified with Kalaimakal, a name for Saraswati. A temple dedicated to her opened in Karaikudi in 1993, and during the Anti-Hindi agitations she was shown angry and in tears.

Legacy

Statues of her include a panchaloha idol commissioned by S. Ganesan in 1940 and a statue unveiled in Madurai in 1981, which Dravidian atheists criticised as irrational. In 2013 the state announced a new tall statue for Madurai. Scholars such as Patricia Uberoi and Sumathi Ramaswamy see her as a patriarchal construction.

Trivia

A modern temple. In Karaikudi, in Sivaganga district, there is a temple dedicated to Tamil Thai, opened in 1993.
Protests and representation. During the Anti-Hindi agitations in Tamil Nadu she was portrayed as angry, tearful and distressed.
