Japanese rain yōkai

Ameonna

The ameonna is a female Japanese yōkai, a rain woman said to summon rain simply by licking her hand. Her roots lie in a goddess from Chinese literature, and the word still appears in everyday Japanese speech.

The myth

According to Japanese tradition, the ameonna is a woman who stands in the rain and licks her hand to make it fall. In the morning she takes the form of a cloud, and in the evening she turns into rain, which falls wherever she passes to water the plants. She is said to be seen on rainy nights.

Her background lies in China. In Song Yu's Gaotangfu, King Huai of Chu dreams of a woman from Mount Wu who, as she departs, says she will be cloud in the morning and rain in the evening. From this comes the proverb chaoyunmuyu, which alludes to secret lovers' meetings. Toriyama Sekien included her in his Konjaku Hyakki Shūi, linking her to those goddesses, although no earlier texts describe a rain yōkai, so she may well have been his own invention.

Some folk accounts describe her as a mother who lost a newborn on a rainy day, and who appears before crying children carrying a large sack. At other times she is regarded as a god who rescues the people from drought.

Legacy

In present-day Japan, ameonna or its male form ameotoko describes an unlucky person whom rain seems to follow, often ruining weddings or sporting events. In the videogames Shadow Warrior (2013) and Shadow Warrior 2 she is a being of the Shadow Realm whose tears bring rain. Canadian producer Deadmau5 released a single called Ameonna in 2025.

Ancient sources: Song Yu's Chinese Gaotangfu, and Toriyama Sekien's Japanese Konjaku Hyakki Shūi, where she is illustrated.

Trivia

A proverb behind her name. The Chinese phrase chaoyunmuyu, morning cloud and evening rain, is an old proverb for secret encounters between lovers.
A videogame and a song. She appears as a character in the videogames Shadow Warrior and its sequel, and in 2025 producer Deadmau5 released a single named after her.
