hero of Greek mythology, son of Neoptolemus

Molossus

Molossus is a hero of Greek mythology, the son of Neoptolemus and Andromache, whom tradition regards as the eponymous founder of the Molossians, an ancient Greek tribe of Epirus in northwestern Greece. His story matters because it links the fall of Troy to the origins of a specific Greek people.

The myth

After the fall of Troy, Andromache became a captive of Neoptolemus and bore him at least one son, Molossus. Another tradition adds Pielus and Pergamus as his brothers, the latter named after the citadel of Troy. When Molossus was born, his mother abandoned him, but he was taken in and acknowledged by Neoptolemus, who by then was married to Hermione.

Hermione was barren and feared that the child and his mother would take her place. She tried to cut their throats at the altar of Thetis, but Peleus rescued them. Thetis then ordered the survivors to leave for Epirus. There Andromache married Helenus, the king of the land. When he died, Molossus succeeded him on the throne, and his name gave rise to the Molossians.

Trivia

A brother named after a citadel. According to one version, Pergamus was named after the citadel of Troy, the city from which his mother Andromache came.
A people named after a hero. The Molossians, a people of the region of Epirus, took their name from Molossus, who was considered their founder.
