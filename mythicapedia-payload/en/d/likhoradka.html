female spirit of Slavic mythology

Likhoradka

Likhoradka is a female spirit of Slavic mythology, linked to sickness and also known as tryasavitsa. She was believed to be able to possess a person's body and bring on illness.

The myth

According to tradition, Likhoradka could take possession of a person's body and cause illness. In some tales she is considered a creation of Chernobog, a dark deity of Slavic mythology. Later Russian legends speak of twelve Likhoradkas, each with an individual name and tied to a specific ailment.

She was often pictured as a tall woman with disheveled hair, a pale face and a white dress, who passed on sickness to anyone she tried to touch or kiss. In the same mythic world she was related to Chuma, a figure associated with plague.

Legacy

In modern Russian, the word likhoradka means "fever". Its origin is traced to the ancient Russian likho, meaning harm or bad luck, and radet, meaning to endeavor. Chuma, the mythological figure she was linked to, gave its name to plague in modern Russian.

Trivia

Twelve spirits. Later Russian legends describe twelve Likhoradkas, each with an individual name associated with a particular illness.
