mythical king of the Odomanti in Thrace

Sithon

Sithon is a mythical king of the Odomanti, a people of Thrace, whose figure gave his name to the Sithonia peninsula and the Sithones tribe. His story, handed down by Greek sources, revolves around his daughter Pallene and the suitors he tried to eliminate.

The myth

According to the tradition, Sithon promised his kingdom and Pallene's hand to whoever defeated him in single combat. Many suitors died at his hands, and as he grew old he made them fight one another until Dryas and Cleitus arrived. Pallene fell in love with Cleitus, and her old tutor, seeing her feelings, bribed Dryas' charioteer to leave the wheel pins undone, so Cleitus won with ease. Enraged, Sithon meant to kill his daughter by Dryas' funeral pyre, but the tradition says Aphrodite appeared at night and a storm sent by the gods made him change his mind. Pallene married Cleitus, and after his death they inherited the kingdom.

In Nonnus' Dionysiaca the story differs: Sithon loved his own daughter, which is why he killed her suitors. Dionysus came forward as a suitor, defeated him in the contest and killed him with his thyrsus.

Legacy

The Sithonia peninsula and the Sithones tribe kept his name. Pallene gave its name to a region and a city in Thrace, and the myth of Sithon resembles those of Oenomaus, Hippodamia and Pelops.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis in the Dionysiaca, and Ovid in the Metamorphoses, which mentions him in passing.

Trivia

A Sithon of two sexes. Ovid mentions in the Metamorphoses a Sithon who became of indeterminate sex, first man and then woman, as a parallel to the fate of Hermaphroditus. That episode is now lost.
