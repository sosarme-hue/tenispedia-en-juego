Maya god of death, lord of Xibalba

Ah Puch

Ah Puch is the Maya god of death and lord of Xibalba, the underworld. Colonial sources also call him Hunhau, Yum Cimil or Kisin, and the name Ah Puch, common in books about the Maya, may not be authentically Maya at all.

The myth

According to tradition, Ah Puch appears as a skeleton or corpse with the face of a jaguar or an owl, adorned with bells. He is the husband of the goddess Ixtab or Xtabay. In the Popol Vuh there are two death gods, Hun-Came and Vucub-Came, who are in fact regarded as one figure; the Hero Twins defeated them after descending into Xibalba, and they restricted their cult.

In Yucatec sources, the bishop Diego de Landa describes Hunhau as prince of the devils and ruler of the lowest of the nine underworld realms. He is linked with war, human sacrifice and ill-omened animals such as dogs, owls and screech owls. A Lacandon tale presents him as the antipode of the upper god in the creation of the world and of human bodies.

Worship and religion

During the New Year rites, priests dressed as God A' performed bloodletting and sacrifice, while participants walked over glowing embers to ward off a year of great mortality. Today, the Maya are said to believe that Yum Cimil, the Lord of Death, lingers near the sick.

Legacy

The skeletal figure hung with bells is one of the most recognisable subjects in Maya iconography. The name Ah Puch has spread through popular writing and literature on the Maya, although specialists consider it of doubtful authenticity.

Ancient sources: The Popol Vuh mentions the death gods Hun-Came and Vucub-Came, the Maya codices depict Ah Puch many times, and the bishop Diego de Landa describes Hunhau in the sixteenth century.

Trivia

Bells in the Well of Sacrifice. Dredging the Well of Sacrifice at Chichén Itzá recovered many copper and sometimes gold bells, thought to have been thrown in with the victims.
An unconfirmed name. The name Hun Ahau appears often in the Ritual of the Bacabs, but it is never identified as a death god.
