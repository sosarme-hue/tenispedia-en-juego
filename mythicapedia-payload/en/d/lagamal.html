Elamite-Mesopotamian god of the underworld

Lagamal

Lagamal is an underworld god worshipped in Dilbat, Susa and Terqa, whose Akkadian name means «no mercy». His presence in several cities makes him an unusual figure in the Mesopotamian pantheon.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian tradition, Lagamal was the son of Urash, the protective god of Dilbat, and of Ninegal. He was linked to the realm of the dead and, in some sources, identified with Nergal.

In Susa he formed a pair with Ishmekarab and shared duties with Inshushinak, the judge of souls. The dead were brought before Inshushinak so that their fate could be decided, a scene shown on several seals. Some scholars see him as the accuser in that judgment.

Worship and religion

His main temple stood in Dilbat, where many personal names invoke him. In Terqa, on the Syrian Euphrates, his statues travelled on ritual journeys alongside those of Ikšudum, accompanied by offerings; letters from Mari link these trips to rain.

Legacy

His name appears in that of the Elamite king Kedorlaomer, mentioned in the Bible.

Ancient sources: Texts from Susa, letters from Mari, the god list An = Anum and the Epic of Zimri-Lim.

Trivia

Horns in question. An oracle from the Mari archives asked how many horns his crown should have; two, four or eight were considered possible answers.
A goddess in Terqa. In Terqa he was represented as a goddess, which is unusual, as shown by the determinatives used in the texts.
