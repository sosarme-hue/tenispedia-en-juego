dwarf of Norse mythology

Litr

Litr is a dwarf in Norse mythology whose name means 'colour' or 'appearance'. A giant bearing the same name appears in some sources, so the name may belong to two different beings.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, at Baldr's funeral, Thor hallows the pyre with his hammer Mjölnir. A dwarf named Litr runs before his feet, and the god kicks him into the fire, where he burns. Litr is also listed as a dwarf in the Völuspá. In later sagas he has other roles: in Áns saga bogsveigis the protagonist forces him to make a bow, and in Þorsteins saga Víkingssonar he steals a magic horn from Dís for Halfdan, which heals Viking.

As a giant, the name appears in a stanza by the skald Bragi Boddason, quoted by Snorri, in a kenning that describes Thor as the challenger of Litr's men. Since Thor is the enemy of giants, it is generally assumed that Litr is a giant there. John Lindow suggested there may originally have been only one Litr, a giant killed at Baldr's funeral.

Ancient sources: Appears in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and in the Völuspá of the Eddas.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Litr derives from a reconstructed Proto-Germanic form *ulituz, related to Gothic and Old English words meaning 'shape' or 'brightness'.
A dwarf in several sagas. Besides the Prose Edda, Litr appears as a dwarf in sagas such as Áns saga bogsveigis and Þorsteins saga Víkingssonar, where he plays very different roles.
