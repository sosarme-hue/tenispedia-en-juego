Japanese yōkai of the crying old man

Konaki-jiji

The konaki-jiji is a yōkai from Japanese folklore that originated in the mountains of Tokushima Prefecture, on the island of Shikoku. It is one of the most widely recognized yōkai in Japan, and its name is usually translated as "old man crying like a baby."

The myth

According to the tradition, the konaki-jiji can take the form of an old man or a baby, and it lures travelers passing through the mountains. It mimics the cry of an infant left alone in the wilderness, and the compassionate passerby who picks it up finds that the creature has suddenly become as heavy as a stone, crushing them to death.

In some versions, the spirit is that of a baby left to die in the wild. The figure can be traced to family records in Shikoku, where the term described an old man whose crying sounded like a child's.

Legacy

The manga artist Shigeru Mizuki was the first to depict the yōkai visually, and the creature has featured in Japanese popular culture for decades. In the manga Dandadan, the spirit onbusuman is based on the konaki-jiji.

Trivia

Alternate kanji form. An alternate kanji spelling, 児啼爺, uses ji (児, infant) instead of ko, emphasizing the creature's infant-like cry rather than its origin as a child spirit.
A monster in Dandadan. In the manga Dandadan, the onbusuman appears with an old man's large head, a turtle's shell, an infant's arms and legs, and a long hairy tail.
