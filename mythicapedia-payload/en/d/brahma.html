Hindu creator god

Brahma

Brahma is the creator god of Hinduism and the third member of the Trimurti, alongside Vishnu and Shiva. He is linked to the creation of the universe, knowledge and the Vedas, although over time he lost prominence to other major deities.

The myth

According to the best-known myth, of Vishnuite origin, Brahma created the material universe after emerging from a lotus growing from the navel of the sleeping Vishnu, who rests on the cosmic ocean. Other accounts speak of the Hiranyagarbha, or "golden womb," a golden egg from which the god emerged. In this version Brahma shapes the foundations of the world: time, space and individual souls, as well as the earth and the lokas, the realms where gods and other beings dwell.

The god appears in different traditions with his own identity. The Vedic texts do not treat him as an independent deity, but he is associated with Prajapati, a god of procreation. For the Advaita doctrine, Brahma is the first being created by the absolute Brahman. The Puranas give him sons born from his mind, the manasaputras, among them Manu, Atri, Daksha and Narada. His wife is Sarasvati, goddess of knowledge, though he is also named as the husband of Savitri.

The tradition also preserves an episode involving Shiva. In the Shiva Purana, Brahma and Vishnu argue over who is the greatest, and Shiva, appearing as a column of fire, tests them. Brahma falsely claims to have found the column's beginning, and Shiva cuts off one of his five heads. This is said to explain his loss of worship, while Vishnu is honored for his honesty.

Worship and religion

Brahma is revered in ancient texts, but he never received wide popular worship and no significant sect sustained him. In India very few temples are dedicated to him, the most famous being the Brahma Temple at Pushkar in Rajasthan. Outside India, the Erawan Shrine in Bangkok is a very popular place of devotion within the Thai Buddhist community. In Buddhism, the Great Brahma appears as a superior deva and protector of the faith, though not as an all-powerful creator.

Legacy

His figure has given its name to key philosophical and religious concepts. The neuter brahman designates the abstract ultimate reality of Hinduism, while the masculine brahmán names the god. The word brāhmaṇa refers to the priestly caste, and the name still appears in modern Indian culture.

Ancient sources: He appears in late Vedic texts, in the Maitrayaniya Upanishad (with the Kutsayana Hymn) and in the Puranas, such as the Brahmanda Purana and the Shiva Purana.

Trivia

Four heads. His four heads represent the four Vedas and face the four cardinal directions.
A swan as mount. His vahana is the hamsa, a swan, goose or crane, and the god is usually shown seated on a lotus.
