Gaulish-Roman god of healing and prophecy

Alaunus

Alaunus was a Gallo-Roman god of healing and prophecy, known from inscriptions found in southern France and Germany. His name also appears in many place names and river names across Europe.

The myth

Ancient texts tell no stories about Alaunus, so his genealogy and deeds are unknown. What we know are his attributes: healing and prophecy.

His feminine form, Alauna, appears in two dedications from Pantenburg, alongside the goddess Boudina.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped at Lurs, where a sanctuary is thought to have stood beside the Roman mansio of Alaunium, today the Chapel of Notre Dame des Anges. Barruol and Martel suggested the original cult site lay on the nearby height of the Pied d'Aulun and was moved to the main road when it gained importance under Augustus. At Mannheim he was dedicated as Mercurius Alaunus, a god of roads.

Legacy

His name survives in place names and river names such as Valognes, Alcester, Learchild, the River Aln in Northumberland, Alun in Wales and Allamps in France. Its etymology remains unresolved, with proposals linking it to 'feed, nurture' and to 'wander'.

Ancient sources: Only Latin inscriptions dedicated to him at Lurs, Mannheim and Pantenburg; no literary accounts of this god survive.

Trivia

A god of roads. At Mannheim he appears as Mercurius Alaunus, a form linking him to Mercury, protector of roads.
Two possible roots. One proposal links the name to 'feed'; Delamarre connects it to 'wander', which would make the Alauni 'the wanderers'.
