Mahayana Buddhist bodhisattva of medicine

Bhaiṣajyarāja

Bhaiṣajyarāja, known as the Medicine King Bodhisattva, is a figure in Mahayana Buddhism associated with healing. He appears in the Lotus Sutra and is counted among the great bodhisattvas of the tradition.

The myth

According to the Lotus Sutra, in a former life he was a bodhisattva who practised severe austerities for twelve thousand years and offered his body by burning it as the supreme offering to a buddha. Reborn as a prince, he built eighty-four thousand stupas and again offered his arms.

Another account presents him as a layman named Starlight, who offered Himalayan medicines to the monk Sun-Treasury and vowed to free beings from disease. Together with his brother, he received the title of Medicine King.

Worship and religion

He is one of the eight great bodhisattvas in the retinue of Bhaiṣajyaguru, the Medicine Buddha. In China, legendary physicians such as Shennong, Bian Que and Hua Tuo are identified with him, and some temples depict him as a middle-aged man in hanfu. His festival falls on the 28th day of the fourth lunar month.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra (chapters 23 and 27) and the sutra on visualising the bodhisattvas Bhaiṣajyarāja and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata.

Trivia

Extreme offering. In the Lotus Sutra, his former life culminates in burning his own body as an offering to a buddha.
Legendary physicians. In China he is identified with legendary figures of medicine, such as Hua Tuo or Sun Simiao.
