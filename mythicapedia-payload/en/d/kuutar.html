Moon goddess of Finnish mythology

Kuutar

Kuutar is the Moon goddess of Finnish mythology, closely linked to Päivätär. She appears mainly in runic songs, where she spins and weaves golden threads, and her figure merged with that of the Virgin Mary after Christianization.

The myth

In the runic songs, Kuutar owns the gold of the Moon, spins golden yarn and weaves clothes from it. She usually appears alongside Päivätär in incantations asking the wasps, their children, not to sting. According to Anna-Leena Siikala, the two belong to a tradition influenced by Baltic mythology. Her name shifted from region to region, and in some songs it becomes a synonym for other female figures.

In the Kalevala, a teal lays its egg on the knee of Ilmatar, daughter of the air, and the white becomes the Moon and the yolk the Sun. Young maidens ask Kuutar for some of her golden jewellery and clothes, as she is described as a great beauty. One song from Ilomantsi tells how Päivätär and Kuutar weep and the Great Oak grows from their tears.

Worship and religion

Among the Finns, the new moon was a especially lucky time: people greeted it while holding bread or money and asked it for good health. This lunar practice was tied to beliefs about eclipses, when something was imagined to be devouring the Moon.

Legacy

The image of the spinning lunar woman entered European popular imagination, where it was linked to Mary. The poet Väinö Salminen read the threads of the Moon as a nature myth, in which dew resembles glittering tears.

Ancient sources: Finnish and Karelian runic songs, as well as the Kalevala.
