guardian deity of Burmese Buddhist temples and pagodas

Bo Bo Gyi

Bo Bo Gyi is the traditional name of the nat, or guardian deity, assigned to each Buddhist temple or pagoda in Burma. He is regarded as an avatar of Indra.

The myth

According to a legend of the Shwedagon Pagoda, two Mon merchants gave the King of Yangon some hair relics of the Buddha so they could be enshrined in a pagoda. Bo Bo Gyi, an avatar of Indra, pointed his finger at the exact spot where the pagoda should rise, which is why he is usually depicted pointing.

Worship and religion

He is shown as a nearly life-sized elderly man in a curved cap, sometimes with a cane. Worshippers offer scarves and paso, and new car owners visit the Shwenyaungbin shrine to have their cars blessed. Thai devotees invoke him as thep than chai, and many believe that having the pointing finger touch their forehead brings luck.

Legacy

His reputation in Thailand has spread well beyond Burma's borders.

Trivia

A pointing statue. His outstretched finger recalls the Shwedagon Pagoda legend, which is why nearly all his images show him this way.
Blessed cars. New car owners visit the Shwenyaungbin shrine, between Yangon and Bago, to ask for protection for their vehicles.
