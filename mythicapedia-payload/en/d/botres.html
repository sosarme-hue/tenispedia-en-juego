Theban youth turned into a bird by Apollo

Botres

Botres is a young Theban, son of Eumelus and grandson of Eugnotus, whose story belongs to Greek mythology. His tale explains the origin of a bird called the aeropus, identified with the bee-eater.

The myth

According to the tradition, Eumelus was a devoted worshipper of Apollo, whom he honored with generous offerings. One day, while he was sacrificing a ram to the god, Botres, who was helping him, tasted the animal's brain before the rite was finished.

Enraged, Eumelus struck his son on the head with a burning brand, inflicting a fatal wound. When it became clear that Botres was dying, the father, his wife and the servants were overcome with grief. Moved by Eumelus's devotion, Apollo took pity on them and changed the youth into a bird called the aeropus, the bee-eater. The episode is also briefly recounted in Ovid's Metamorphoses.

Ancient sources: His story is briefly recounted in Ovid's Metamorphoses.
