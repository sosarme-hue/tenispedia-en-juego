mythical king of Arcadia in Greek myth

Clymenus

Clymenus is a mythical king of Arcadia, or a man from Argos in another version, at the centre of a tragedy in Greek myth involving incest, revenge and divine punishment. The story survives mainly in late sources such as Hyginus and Parthenius of Nicaea.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Clymenus was the son of Schoeneus and king of Arcadia. He fell in love with his daughter Harpalyce and had an incestuous relationship with her. A son was born from this union, and Harpalyce, in an act of revenge or despair, served the child's flesh to her father at a banquet. When he discovered what had happened, Clymenus killed his daughter.

Parthenius of Nicaea gives a longer version: Clymenus is the son of Teleus of Argos and husband of Epicaste. After snatching Harpalyce from her wedding to Alastor, he kept her as his wife. She killed her younger brother, cooked him and served his flesh to their father. Harpalyce begged the gods to free her from the human world and was transformed into a bird, the chalcis; Clymenus ended up taking his own life.

Ancient sources: Hyginus (Fabulae) and Parthenius of Nicaea (Love Sorrows), late authors who preserve his story.

Trivia

Two genealogies. Hyginus presents him as the son of Schoeneus and king of Arcadia, while Parthenius makes him the son of Teleus, a man from Argos.
A shared name. Clymenus is the name of several characters in Greek mythology, so it is important to tell which one each story refers to.
