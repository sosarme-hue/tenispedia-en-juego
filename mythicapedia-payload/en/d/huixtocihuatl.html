Aztec goddess of salt and salt water

Huixtocihuatl

Huixtocihuatl is a fertility goddess of the Aztec pantheon who presided over salt and salt water. Her story explains how salt came to be, and her worship centred on a festival held in her honour in the Aztec calendar.

The myth

According to the account recorded by Sahagún, Huixtocihuatl was the elder sister of the tlaloque, the rain gods. After she angered her younger brothers by mocking them, they banished her to the salt beds. There she discovered salt and how to make it, using jars and piling up earth. For this invention, she was honoured by those who worked with salt.

The sources do not agree on her family ties. Some make her the wife of Tezcatlipoca, while others place her with Chicomecoatl and Chalchiuhtlicue as one of three goddesses who provided salt, food and water. Her attributes, such as the water and cloud designs on her clothing, link her more closely to water than to salt.

Worship and religion

Her festival took place during Tecuilhuitontli, the seventh month of the Aztec calendar, which fell in June. For ten days, women of all ages, including the daughters of salt-makers, sang and danced in rows while men and elders led the dance. One woman embodied the goddess as an ixiptla. On the final day she was taken to the temple of Tlaloc on the Templo Mayor and sacrificed alongside captives, who accompanied her as her companions in death. The festival ended with banquets and drinking. The sources say nothing about whether this cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Bernardino de Sahagún's Florentine Codex and Primeros Memoriales, written in the sixteenth century, and the Codex Telleriano-Remensis describe her figure, her festival and her rites.

Trivia

A goddess dressed like maize. Sahagún compares her headdress and ornaments to a maize plant, with quetzal feathers arranged like an ear of corn.
Salt, filth and urine. The Codex Telleriano-Remensis links her with the goddess Ixcuina, who represented filth and excrement. This suggests an association with urine, seen as salty and impure.
