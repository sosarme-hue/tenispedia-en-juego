mother of Dionysus in Greek mythology

Semele

Semele was a Theban princess, daughter of King Cadmus and Harmonia, whose affair with Zeus gave birth to Dionysus. Her story, in which she is struck down by lightning and her son is rescued from the flames, is one of the foundational myths of the god of wine and ecstatic worship.

The myth

According to the tradition, Semele was a daughter of Cadmus, founder of Thebes, and of Harmonia, a daughter of Ares and Aphrodite. Zeus, attracted to her, visited her in secret, and rumours spread through the city. Hera, jealous, took the voice of Beroe, Semele's old nurse, and persuaded her to ask Zeus to appear to her as he did before the goddess. Zeus had sworn to grant any request, so despite his attempts to dissuade her, he came in all his majesty amid thunder and lightning, and Semele died in the flames.

Zeus saved the six-month foetus by sewing it into his thigh, which is why the name Dionysus means the one born twice. Other versions tell that Dionysus was torn apart by the Titans, that Zeus gave Semele the heart of the child, or that the boy was raised by Nysus. Later, Dionysus descended to the underworld and brought his mother back, and she lived in the heavens under the name Thyone. According to Hyginus and Nonnus, Semele was also the mother of Osiris in Egypt, a tale that reflects the identification of Dionysus with that god.

Worship and religion

In Athens, Semele received offerings during the Lenaia, when a yearling bull, a symbol of Dionysus, was sacrificed to her. According to Pausanias, annual rites were held at Lake Alcyonia near Lerna in memory of her rescue from Hades, although the author refused to describe them. In Thebes, her bridal chamber and tomb were still shown in Roman times.

Legacy

In literature, Aeschylus wrote a tragedy titled Semele, now lost, and Ovid identified her with the Roman goddess Stimula. Her name has prompted philological studies on a possible Thraco-Phrygian origin linked to the earth.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, Pausanias, the Homeric Hymn to Dionysus, Hyginus, Ovid and Nonnus mention her in their accounts.

Trivia

Twice born. The name of Dionysus is linked to his double birth: from the womb of Semele and from the thigh of Zeus.
