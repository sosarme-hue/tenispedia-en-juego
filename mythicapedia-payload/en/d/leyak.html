creature from Balinese folklore

Leyak

The leyak is a figure from the folklore of Bali: a practitioner of black magic who, at night, separates his head and entrails from the body and flies in search of victims. It is one of the most feared figures on the island.

The myth

According to tradition, leyak are people who practise black magic and behave like cannibals. By day they look like ordinary humans, but at night the head and entrails break loose from the body and fly, seeking the blood of newborns or pregnant women. They are said to haunt graveyards, feed on corpses and change into animals such as pigs.

Their mistress is Rangda, the widow-witch known as the queen of the leyak, and demons are said to follow her.

Worship and religion

Rangda's mask is kept in the village death temple and paraded during the festivals of the temples. When an illness or death is blamed on leyak witchcraft, a balian (traditional healer) holds a séance to identify the culprit. The healers, however, advise against revenge and leave the matter to the spirits.

Legacy

Leyak statues, showing a head with a very long tongue and sharp fangs, are sometimes hung on house walls as decoration.

Trivia

Flying head. In popular belief, the leyak flies at night with its head and entrails trailing from the body, which is left behind looking like an ordinary person.
Mask in procession. Rangda's mask is paraded during temple festivals, making her one of the most visible figures in local religious life.
