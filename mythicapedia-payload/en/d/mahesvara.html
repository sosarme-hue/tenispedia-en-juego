Buddhist deva associated with Shiva

Maheśvara

Maheśvara is a deva, a heavenly being, in Buddhist mythology, closely linked to Shiva in Hinduism. His role differs across Buddhist traditions: in Theravāda he is a powerful being still bound to the cycle of rebirth, while in Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna he appears as a worldly deity, an emanation of Avalokiteśvara or even a bodhisattva.

The myth

According to Mahāyāna sources, Maheśvara was reborn as a deva for offering a cup of honey to the Buddha Kassapa in a former life. He is said to have been born from the brow of Avalokiteśvara, and to help good people who keep the Buddhist precepts while punishing the wicked.

One well-known story tells how Vajrapāni kills him for his evil deeds. Vairocana asks Vajrapāni to establish a mandala, but he refuses, since Maheśvara deceives beings with false teachings. After a magical duel, which Vajrapāni wins, the god dies and his life passes to another realm, where he becomes a Buddha called Bhasmeśvaranirghoṣa, the "Soundless Lord of Ashes".

Worship and religion

In Thailand he is venerated as Phra Isuan, and his statues stand beside other Hindu deities in Buddhist temples as protectors of the Dhamma. He is not worshipped as a path to liberation but as a celestial figure who pays homage to the Buddha's teachings. In Sri Lanka his direct worship is less prominent, though his power is honoured through his son Skanda, identified with Kataragama.

Ancient sources: In early Pali texts he appears as Īsāna, a commander deity in the Dhajagga Sutta, subordinate to Sakka in a cosmic battle against the asuras.
