Hindu goddess, clan deity of the Modh community of Gujarat

Modheshwari

Modheshwari is an aspect of the goddess Matangi and the clan deity of the Modh community of Gujarat in western India. She is tied to the protection of this community and to the shrine at Modhera.

The myth

According to the tradition, the demon Karnat was wreaking havoc in the region by disrupting the prayers of the Brahmins and Vaishyas. Seeking protection, they turned to the goddess Parvati, who, angered by their complaints, released a flame from her mouth that gave birth to Modheshwari.

The goddess slew the demon and blessed the Modh community, promising to always protect those who worship her. She is depicted with eighteen arms, each holding a weapon, the trident being the most prominent.

Worship and religion

She is the clan deity of the Modh community, made up of several groups of Brahmins, Patidars, Vaishyas, Modis and Kshatriyas who came from the Modhera region. Her main temple stands near the ruins of the famous Sun Temple at Modhera, where an original place of worship lies below the step-well. Ancient temples are also found at Chanasama, and there are notable shrines in Gujarat, Rajasthan, Ujjain and other towns. Her worship continues today.

Legacy

She is the kuldevi of Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, which has brought her wider attention beyond her community.
