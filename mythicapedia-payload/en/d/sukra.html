preceptor of the asuras and Venus deity in Hindu mythology

Shukra

Shukra, also called Shukracharya or Ushanas, is the sage and teacher of the asuras and the deity who presides over the planet Venus in Hinduism. His portrayal shifts considerably across the Vedas, the epics and the Puranas, and his name, meaning both "bright" and "semen", points to one of his best-known legends.

The myth

In the Vedas he appears as Kavya Ushanas, a poet-priest who helps Indra defeat his enemies. According to the Jaiminiya Brahmana, he switches sides to the devas after being won over with gifts and honours, a move that decides the war. In post-Vedic texts he becomes the preceptor of the asuras, a descendant of Bhrigu, who holds the Mrtasañjīvanī, a skill that can revive the dead. According to the Shanti Parva, he entered the body of Kubera, provoking Shiva's anger. After being swallowed by the god, he emerged through the seminal passage, which gave him his name.

In the story of Vishnu as the dwarf Vamana, Shukra warns King Bali that the beggar is Vishnu in disguise and urges him to refuse the gift. When the king insists, the sage hides in the water pot to stop the flow, but Vamana pierces the spout with a blade of grass and blinds him in one eye. From then on he is known as the one-eyed sage.

Legacy

In Hindu astrology Shukra rules the planet Venus, associated with Friday, the colour white and the diamond. He is linked to artistic talent, music, dance and material comfort, and his shukra dasha, the planetary period, is the longest of the nine planets.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda as Kavya Ushanas, in the Taittiriya Samhita, the Panchavimsha Brahmana and the Jaiminiya Brahmana, and in the Mahabharata.

Trivia

The name of the sage. Shukra means "bright" and, in another sense, "semen", in reference to the way he emerged from Shiva's body in a Purana story.
Friday. In India Friday takes its name from him, shukravar, as Venus is associated with this day.
