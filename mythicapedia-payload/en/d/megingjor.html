Thor's belt of strength

Megingjörð

Megingjörð is the belt of the Norse god Thor. According to the sources, it doubled the god's already prodigious strength and was one of his three main possessions, alongside the hammer Mjölnir and the iron gloves Járngreipr.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Megingjörð, meaning roughly belt of strength in Old Norse, was one of Thor's three main possessions, together with the hammer Mjölnir and the iron gloves Járngreipr. When Thor girded it on, his already enormous strength was doubled. The name combines megin, meaning power or strength, and gjörð, meaning belt.

In the Skáldskaparmál, Loki persuades Thor to travel to Geirröðargarðar without his three treasures, a condition the giant Geirröðr had set for releasing him. On the way, Thor spends the night with a woman named Gríðr. The next morning she lends him, as a sign of gratitude, her own iron gloves, her belt of strength and the staff Gríðarvölr, so that he can defeat the giant with them.

In the Gylfaginning, Thor comes upon a huge man sleeping and snoring loudly in the forest. Believing he understands the cause of the noise, Thor fastens his belt and his strength grows. The skaldic poem Þórsdrápa, composed in the 10th century by Eilífr Goðrúnarson in the service of Earl Hákon Sigurðarson, also refers to Thor's belt.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál) and the 10th-century skaldic poem Þórsdrápa by Eilífr Goðrúnarson.

Trivia

Almost always plural. In Snorri's Edda the name always appears in the plural, megingjarðar, although the singular form megingjörð is the one that became common.
A borrowed belt. In the Skáldskaparmál, the belt is not Thor's own: he receives it on loan from Gríðr, who also gives him her iron gloves.
