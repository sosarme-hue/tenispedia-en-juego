son of the Hindu god Indra

Jayanta

Jayanta is the son of Indra, king of the devas, and his wife Shachi. He appears in several Hindu texts as a warrior of the gods, and his best-known episode shows him disguised as a crow.

The myth

In the Ramayana, Jayanta takes the form of a crow and wounds Sita's breast while Rama sleeps. Rama unleashes the divine weapon Brahmastra against him, and the crow flees across the universe until it takes refuge with Rama. Since the weapon cannot be recalled, Jayanta asks that it strike his right eye, and he is left half-blind.

In the Uttara Kanda, during a battle against Meghanada, son of Ravana, Jayanta falls unconscious, and his maternal grandfather Puloman hides him in the ocean. In another version, he steals the amrita during the Samudra Manthana and flees as a crow for twelve days.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, the Kumbha Mela, held every twelve years in Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik, commemorates the places where Jayanta stopped with the amrita.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana, the Uttara Kanda, the Padma Purana, the Harivamsa, the Skanda Purana and the Vayu Purana mention him.

Trivia

A crow with a history. Jayanta is one of the few gods told of as a crow, and his encounter with Rama explains why he loses an eye.
A bamboo curse. In the Vayu Purana, the sage Agastya turns Jayanta into a bamboo tree in the Vindhya mountains, and the curse ends when Urvashi receives a bamboo staff at her dance debut.
