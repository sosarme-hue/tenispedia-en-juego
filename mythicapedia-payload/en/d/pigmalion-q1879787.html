king of Tyre, brother of Dido

Pygmalion

According to the tradition preserved in ancient texts, Pygmalion was king of Tyre, the Phoenician city, and brother of Queen Dido, founder of Carthage. His story belongs to the Phoenician world, although the record classifies him as a hero of Greek culture, and his figure reflects Tyre's commercial ambitions in the Mediterranean.

The myth

Pygmalion was a son of King Mattan I and the brother of Dido. According to the story, he coveted the treasures that Sychaeus, the priest of Hercules and Dido's husband, kept hidden in the temple of Hercules in Tyre. He forced Dido to marry Sychaeus and to find out where the treasure was hidden.

Dido discovered that the riches were in the garden, not under the altar as she had told her brother. When Pygmalion ordered Sychaeus killed, Dido dug up the treasure and fled with her followers. According to tradition, she later founded Carthage.

Legacy

His name appears in the tradition of the founding of Carthage, linked to the story of Dido.

Ancient sources: Menander of Tyre, quoted by Josephus in Against Apion, gives the length of his life and reign.
