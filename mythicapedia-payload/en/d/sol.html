Norse goddess of the Sun

Sól

Sól is the goddess of the Sun in Norse mythology, daughter of Mundilfari and sister of Máni, the Moon. Each day she drives the solar chariot across the sky, a image that Germanic and Norse sources preserve under variants such as Sunna or Sunne.

The myth

According to the stories, Mundilfari had two children so beautiful that he named them after the Sun and the Moon. The gods were angered by such arrogance and placed both in the heavens: Sól was married to Glenr and tasked with driving the sun's chariot, pulled by the horses Árvakr and Alsviðr. The chariot was made to light the worlds with embers from Muspelheim, and two bellows beneath the horses' shoulders cooled them.

As she travels, Sól is chased by the wolf Sköll, who wants to devour her. Eclipses were understood as moments when the wolf almost catches her. A shield called Svalin protects her and the Earth from excessive heat. At Ragnarök Sköll will finally catch her, and her daughter will take her place in the sky. In some accounts the wolf is Fenrir instead of Sköll.

Worship and religion

The written sources say little about a specific cult of Sól. Some scholars note that the Sun was a focus of religious practice in older Scandinavia, and Bronze Age finds such as the Trundholm sun chariot suggest it was honoured as a source of life. There is no evidence that this cult survives today.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the rune sól, ᛋ, the letter s, and to rune poems that praise the Sun as light and as shield of the clouds. The Old English name Sunna is linked to the word for Sunday, and her name is compared with Latin Sol, Sanskrit Surya and Lithuanian Saulė.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Vafþrúðnismál, Grímnismál, Alvíssmál) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál) mention her, as does the Old High German Merseburg Incantations.

Trivia

Names of the Sun. In the Alvíssmál, the dwarf Alvíss gives different names for the Sun depending on the world: sól for humans, sunna for the gods and fagrahvél, 'lovely wheel', for the elves.
A pair of wolves. In the Gylfaginning, Sköll chases Sól while her brother, the wolf Hati Hróðvitnisson, runs ahead of her after Máni, the Moon.
