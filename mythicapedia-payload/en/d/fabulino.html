Roman god who taught children to speak

Fabulinus

Fabulinus is a god of ancient Roman popular religion who taught children to speak. His name comes from the Latin verb fabulari, to speak.

The myth

Few stories are told about him, since he does not appear in literary Roman mythology. His role belonged to the realm of household faith: he watched over small children at one of their earliest achievements, saying their first words.

Worship and religion

When a child spoke its first words, its parents made an offering to the god in gratitude. Devotion was mostly domestic, part of family life rather than of the great public temples. He was among the minor household gods that the pontiffs placed on the sacred list of the Indigitamenta, to be invoked on particular occasions. There is no record of how long this cult lasted.

Legacy

The English writer Walter Pater mentions him in his novel Marius the Epicurean (1885), among the little household gods dear to Roman homes.

Trivia

A small divine court. Pater lists him alongside Vaticanus, who makes the baby cry at birth, and Cuba, who keeps the infant calm in its cot.
A late appearance. His name reaches modern literature not through ancient myths but through a nineteenth-century English novel.
