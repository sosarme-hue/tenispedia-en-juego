commander-in-chief of Matsya's army in the Mahabharata

Kichaka

Kichaka is a character from the Mahabharata, the great Indian Sanskrit epic. He served as commander-in-chief of King Virata's army, and he is remembered above all for harassing Draupadi during the Pandavas' year in disguise, an episode that ended with his death at the hands of Bhima.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata tradition, Kichaka was the son of King Kekaya, of the suta class, and Queen Malavi. He had 105 younger brothers, known as the Upa-Kichakas, and a sister, Sudeshna. According to the Southern recension, he and his brothers were believed to be born from an aspect of Bana, the eldest of the Kalakeya asuras, a lineage known for great strength and demonic origins.

When Sudeshna married King Virata of Matsya, Kichaka moved into the palace and rose to command the army. He built his reputation as a warrior by repeatedly defeating King Susharman of Trigarta.

During the year in disguise, Draupadi served at court under the name Malini. Kichaka became infatuated with her and, after she refused him, turned to his sister, who agreed to help. He assaulted Draupadi and humiliated her before the court, and no one could act against him because of his power. A guardian spirit came to her aid and knocked Kichaka unconscious. Draupadi asked Bhima for revenge, and Bhima, disguised as her, met Kichaka at night, caught him and crushed him to death. The next day, his 105 brothers tried to burn Draupadi on his funeral pyre, but Bhima defeated them all. Kichaka and his brothers were cremated together in the presence of the king and the court.

Ancient sources: Kichaka appears in the Mahabharata, one of the great Sanskrit epics of India, whose oldest parts may date to around 400 BCE.
