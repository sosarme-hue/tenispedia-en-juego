king of Thebes, son of Oedipus

Eteocles

Eteocles was a king of Thebes, son of Oedipus, whose rivalry with his brother Polynices led to the war of the Seven against Thebes. His story is one of the great tragic tales of Greek mythology.

The myth

According to tradition, Eteocles was the son of Oedipus, who had unknowingly killed his father Laius and married his mother. When the truth came out, Oedipus was expelled from Thebes. Accounts differ on the mother's name: tragic versions call her Jocasta, while the Odyssey names her Epicaste and says she hanged herself after learning the truth. Another tradition identifies her as Euryganeia, daughter of Hyperphas. Eteocles had a brother, Polynices, and two sisters, Antigone and Ismene.

After the exile, the two brothers refused to help their father, who cursed them. They agreed to rule one year each, but when the first year ended, Eteocles would not give up the throne. Hellanicus has Eteocles offer his brother a choice between the kingship and a share of the property, while Pherecydes has him drive Polynices out by force. Polynices gathered Argive support under Adrastus and other heroes and attacked Thebes. Although Eteocles's forces won, the brothers killed each other in combat.

Another version blames Astymedusa, Oedipus's second wife, who accused her stepsons of trying to force her; Oedipus, enraged, cursed them. After both brothers died, the throne passed to their uncle Creon, who had become guardian of the orphaned Laodamas, Eteocles's young son.

Legacy

Aeschylus dramatized the story in Seven Against Thebes, and Sophocles's Oedipus at Colonus recounts Oedipus's expulsion and the quarrel of his sons over the throne.

Ancient sources: The Odyssey, the Thebaid, the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus and Diodorus Siculus, among other ancient texts, preserve differing versions of his story.

Trivia

A name meaning «truly glorious». His name comes from eteós («true») and kleos («glory»), so it can be rendered as «truly glorious».
A Mycenaean form of the name. The name appears in Mycenaean Greek in an older form, Etewoklewes, and it is thought the Hittites rendered it as Tawagalawas.
