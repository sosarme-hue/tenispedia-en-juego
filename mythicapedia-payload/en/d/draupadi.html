princess and wife of the five Pandavas in the Mahabharata

Draupadi

Draupadi is one of the central figures of the Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic of India. A princess of Panchala, she is the common wife of the five Pandava brothers and is highly revered in Hindu culture for her courage and intelligence.

The myth

According to the story, Draupadi was born from a sacrifice by fire that King Drupada performed to obtain a son able to defeat Drona. She emerged fully grown from the altar and married the five Pandavas.

In the dice game at the Kuru court, Yudhishthira wagered her and lost her to Duryodhana. When they tried to disrobe her in public, her garment grew endlessly and her honour was preserved.

She later pushed for the Kurukshetra War, in which her brothers and her five sons died. At the end of the epic, she was the first to fall on the journey toward heaven.

Worship and religion

In parts of the Indian subcontinent there is a sect that worships Draupadi as a goddess.

Legacy

Her story has inspired dance, theatre and many works of later literature in India.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, a Sanskrit epic composed between c. 400 BCE and 400 CE.

Trivia

One of the panchakanya. She is one of the panchakanya, five exemplary women whose names are believed, by tradition, to cleanse sin when recited.
A name for each trait. She has many names: Krishnā, the dark-complexioned one; Pāñcālī, from Panchala; and Yajñasenī, born from the sacrificial fire.
