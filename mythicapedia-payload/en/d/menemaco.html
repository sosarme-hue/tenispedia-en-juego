Egyptian prince, son of Aegyptus, in Greek mythology

Menemachus

Menemachus is one of the sons of Aegyptus in Greek mythology, a brother of the fifty men who were killed on their wedding night by their wives, the Danaids. His story belongs to the Greek tradition and is remembered mainly for its tragic end and his marriage to Nelo.

The myth

In the most common version, Menemachus was the son of King Aegyptus and a Phoenician woman, which made him full brother of Agaptolemus, Cercetes, Eurydamas, Argius, Aegius and Archelaus. Other accounts make him a son of Aegyptus by Eurrhoe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or by Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre.

His fate was the same as that of his brothers: all of them, except Lynceus, were killed on their wedding night by their wives, who obeyed the command of their father, King Danaus of Libya. Menemachus had married Nelo, daughter of Danaus and an Ethiopian woman.
