sage (rishi) from the Hindu epic Mahabharata

Kindama

Kindama is a sage (rishi) who appears in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He is remembered for the curse he placed on King Pandu after the king killed him by mistake while hunting.

The myth

According to the story, Kindama and his wife were mating in the form of a deer and a doe when King Pandu of Hastinapura, who was hunting in the area, shot an arrow, mistaking them for wild animals. Both were mortally wounded.

Enraged, Kindama resumed his true form and reproached the king for killing him before the act of love was finished. Before dying, he laid a curse on Pandu: the king would lose his life the very moment he touched his wife with the intention of making love to her.

The episode is presented as a punishment for an offence committed without intent, and it shows how a sage's word could carry more weight than a king's will.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic, recounts this episode.
