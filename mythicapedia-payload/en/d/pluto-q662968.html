Oceanid of Greek mythology

Pluto

Pluto (Greek Πλουτώ, meaning «wealth») is an Oceanid of Greek mythology, daughter of Oceanus and Tethys. Her role is brief, but her name is linked to the mother of Tantalus.

The myth

According to Hesiod, a poet of the late eighth or early seventh century BC, Pluto was one of the many daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, described as soft-eyed. The Homeric Hymn to Demeter, probably composed around the same period, remembers her among the «deep-bosomed daughters of Oceanus» who played with Persephone when Hades abducted her.

Pluto is also the name of the mother of Tantalus, and it is unclear whether she is the same figure. Diodorus Siculus picks up this idea and claims that Tantalus surpassed everyone in wealth and renown. However, Tantalus's mother is never said to be a daughter of Oceanus: sometimes she is simply called «a nymph», other times a daughter of Cronus or of a certain Himas. A late version makes her a daughter of Atlas and counts her among the Pleiades, although that text is badly corrupted.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony and the Homeric Hymn to Demeter.
