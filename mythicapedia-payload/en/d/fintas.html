king of Messenia in Greek mythology

Phintas

Phintas was, according to Greek tradition, a king of Messenia and father of Antiochus and Androcles. His reign is linked to the events that led to the First Messenian War between Messenians and Spartans.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phintas inherited the throne from his father Sibotas. During his reign a dispute with Sparta arose over the sanctuary of Artemis, located on a lake that separated the two kingdoms. According to the Messenians, the Spartan king Teleclus plotted to kill the city's leading men: he ordered young soldiers to dress as women and attack them during a festival in honour of the goddess, but the Messenians defended themselves and Teleclus died in the sanctuary. The Spartan version accuses the Messenians of abusing Spartan girls who had come to the festival, which supposedly provoked the king's response. Whatever the real cause, the episode led, a generation later, to the First Messenian War. Phintas was succeeded by his sons Antiochus and Androcles, who ruled together until the death of Androcles.

Worship and religion

He is also remembered for being the first to send offerings and a male choir to honour Apollo at his sanctuary on Delos.
