vampiric fairy of Scottish folklore

Baobhan sith

The baobhan sith is a female fairy from the folklore of the Scottish Highlands, related to both the vampire and the succubus. She seduces lone hunters and kills them in the wilderness.

The myth

According to Highland tales, they appear as women of unearthly beauty, dressed in green or white, who find hunters sheltering in isolated huts or caves. After inviting the men to dance, they drive their sharp claws into the neck to drink their blood or life force. Some medieval versions give them cloven hooves hidden beneath their dresses.

One motif recurs: they arrive just after someone wishes for female company without invoking God's protection. They feed only once a year and cannot bear daylight or iron, so they return to the grave before dawn.

Legacy

They appear in Mark Chadbourn's fantasy trilogies and in Raymond E. Feist's Faerie Tale, as evil fairies of the Unseelie Court. Seanan McGuire's October Daye series features one as an antagonist, and the figure is considered a loose inspiration for Scarlett Johansson's character in Under the Skin.

Trivia

Dangerous wishes. Scottish belief held that a wish made at night without invoking God's protection might be granted in some terrible way. That is why the stories have the women arrive just after the hunters speak of female company.
Giving themselves away. In one tale, a hunter notices that the women have deer hooves instead of feet and flees. The next morning he finds his companions with their throats cut.
