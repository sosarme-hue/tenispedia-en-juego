Slavic male swamp spirit

Bolotnik

The bolotnik is a male swamp spirit in Slavic folklore, an old, green-bearded figure who lures travelers into the water to drown them. His name comes from boloto, meaning 'swamp'.

The myth

According to tradition, the bolotnik appears as an old man with bulging frog-like eyes, a green beard and a body caked in mud, algae and fish scales. In some legends from the Vitebsk Governorate he is an eyeless, fat, filthy creature that sits motionless at the bottom of the swamp.

To lure his victims he quacks like a duck, moos like a cow or gurgles like a blackcock, and he creates lights on the water. Anyone who comes too close to the edge is seized by the feet and slowly dragged into the depths. Some tales say he has no wife or children, while others describe him as married to the bolotnitsa.

Worship and religion

The North Slavs regarded swamps as dangerous and unclean places inhabited by chorty and demons. A 13th-century Novgorod Kormchaia Book notes that the Eastern pagan Slavs made sacrifices to the swamps, though how the bolotnik himself was venerated is unknown.

Ancient sources: The 13th-century Novgorod Kormchaia Book (Helmsman's Book) mentions that the Eastern pagan Slavs made sacrifices to the swamps.

Trivia

A name of uncertain origin. He is known by dozens of names, such as bolotny dedko ('swamp grandpa') or tsar bolotny ('the Swamp King'). Some of these come from Baltic languages or from Czech.
Vulnerable to drainage and ice. According to popular belief, bolotniki perish when the swamp is drained or freezes in winter. Lightning, by contrast, loses its power when it touches the water's surface.
