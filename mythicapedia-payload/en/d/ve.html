Norse god of speech and the senses

Vé

Vé is a god of Norse mythology, one of the Æsir, known for giving humankind the gift of speech and the external senses. He matters mainly for his part in the creation of the first humans, a story recorded in Norse sources.

The myth

In Norse mythology, Vé was one of the Æsir, the principal group of gods in that tradition. He was the son of Bor and Bestla, and the brother of Odin and Vili. The Æsir were the family of gods to which Odin belonged, and he is a central figure in these tales. His name also appears in German as Wiho, a sign that the figure was known beyond Scandinavia.

In the Völuspá, the creation of the first human couple, Ask (the man) and Embla (the woman), is attributed to Odin, who is helped by Hœnir and Lóðurr. In the Gylfaginning, those two companions are replaced by Vili and Vé. In this version, Vé's role becomes that of the one who grants humans speech and the external senses, gifts that set them apart from other beings.

Vé is therefore more a figure of creation than the hero of great deeds. His appearances are brief and depend on the version: the same episode swaps its companions from one text to another, showing that the tradition did not always fix the same names. For that reason, readers meet him mostly in short passages rather than in a continuous narrative.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and the Gylfaginning, from the Eddas, which narrate the creation of the first humans.
