Hippolytus of Sicyon

Hippolytus is a mythical king of Sicyon, a city in the Peloponnese, in Greek mythology. He matters mainly as a link between the Sicyonian kingdom and the great expedition against Troy.

The myth

According to tradition, Hippolytus was the son of Rhopalus and grandson of Phestus. After the death of Zeuxippus he took the throne of Sicyon. His reign was shaped by attacks from Agamemnon, king of Mycenae, who forced him to accept a subordinate status.

During this period the Trojan War broke out, and most Sicyonians refused to join it. Only Leonteus took part, contributing nineteen ships to the Argive fleet. Other sources, however, claim that Leonteus did not lead the Sicyonians but the Girtonians, a people of Thessaly.
