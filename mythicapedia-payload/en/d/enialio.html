minor Greek god of war, associated with Ares

Enyalius

Enyalius is a Greek war deity who appears at times as an aspect of Ares and at others as a separate god of combat. His name is already attested in Mycenaean tablets, which makes him older than many of the stories told about him, and he was later identified with the Roman god Quirinus.

The myth

In Homer, Enyalius is almost always an epithet of Ares, with no clear personality of his own. Later authors tried to give him an independent parentage: some made him a son of Ares and Enyo, others a son of Cronus and Rhea.

A story preserved by Eustathius of Thessalonica, quoting Arrian, tells of a Thracian man named Enyalius who refused hospitality to the young Ares because he hosted only those stronger than himself in war. After a long fight, Ares killed him with his sword and took the epithet. In the Argonautica, Jason uses a huge rock, described as a 'yew of Ares Enyalius', to make the earth-born warriors turn on one another.

Worship and religion

He was called upon at the start of battle, alongside Ares, in a paean and in the war cry of the initial charge. At Argos a temple to Ares Enyalius was built to honour the women who defended the city against Cleomenes I. At Sparta a fettered statue of the god stood so that he would never leave the city, and Pausanias records that puppies were sacrificed to him. His cult was closely tied to that of Ares and does not survive as a living tradition.

Legacy

His name is mostly used as an epithet of Ares in ancient literature. The Romans identified him with Quirinus, their martial god.

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad), Alcman, Aristophanes, Xenophon, Apollonius of Rhodes, Plutarch, Pausanias and Dionysius of Halicarnassus mention him.

Trivia

Chained statue. In Sparta there was a statue of Enyalius in fetters, since it was believed that this way the god would never leave the city.
A name born from a duel. According to one story, Ares gained the epithet Enyalius after killing a man of that name in combat, much as Athena took the name Pallas.
