eponymous heroine of Attica

Attis

Attis is, in Greek mythology, the eponymous heroine of Attica, the region around Athens. Daughter of the autochthonous king Cranaus, her name gave rise to that of the land, which was formerly called Actaea.

The myth

According to the tradition, she was an Athenian princess, daughter of King Cranaus and Pedias, a Lacedaemonian woman and daughter of Mynes. She had two sisters, Cranaë and Cranaechme. She died a virgin, and in her honour Cranaus named the land Attica, which had earlier been called Actaea or Actica after King Actaeus, its former ruler.

Apollodorus says that Attis had a son, Erichthonius, by Hephaestus, although he also mentions that the father could have been Gaia or Athena. Finally, the two birds into which Philomela and her sister Procne were transformed were also called Attis, showing that the name passed to other figures in the story.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus mentions her when discussing her son Erichthonius, placing her in the genealogy of the kings of Attica.

Trivia

A shared name. The name Attis was also given to the two birds into which Philomela and Procne were transformed, according to the story.
From heroine to region. Her name passed to the land her father ruled, which from then on was called Attica.
