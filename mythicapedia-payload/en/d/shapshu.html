Canaanite solar goddess

Shapshu

Shapshu was the Canaanite sun goddess, worshipped mainly at Ugarit. She served as royal messenger of the high god El, and she appears often in the texts from the city.

The myth

In the Ugaritic texts, Shapshu is the royal messenger of El, her probable father. In the Baal Cycle she brings Atar the news that El has given the throne to Yam and, in Page's reading, acts as a voice of moderation to prevent conflict.

In a spell against snakebite, Shapshu helps a mother whose children are in danger, and she eventually reaches the god Ḥoranu, who defeats the serpents.

Worship and religion

At Ugarit she was one of the main deities receiving sacrifices, and in an offering list she ranks second only to Baal. She received offerings during the ceremony of royal accession. In Phoenicia the sun was called šmš, and a month named 'sacrifice to the sun' is attested at Pyrgi, Kition and Cyprus.

Ancient sources: Ugaritic texts from Ugarit, such as the Baal Cycle and the KTU incantations, and the Amarna letters.

Trivia

A name that changed. At Alalah, the original /m/ of the name shifted to /p/, so forms such as Shamshu and Shapshu both appear. At Ugarit only Shapshu is attested.
A goddess, not a god. Unlike Shamash or Utu in Mesopotamia, Shapshu was a female sun deity, as Shams was in Arabia.
