giant bird of Chinese mythology

Peng

The Peng, or Dapeng, is a giant bird of Chinese mythology that is said to transform from Kun, a giant fish. It is best known from the Zhuangzi, where its soaring flight stands for rising above narrow viewpoints.

The myth

According to the tradition, Kun is a fish of enormous size that lives in the depths of the sea. In time it changes into a huge bird, the Peng, whose wings are so vast they seem like clouds and which can fly thousands of kilometres in a single journey.

The Zhuangzi sets the Peng against small birds, such as the cicada and the dove, which cannot grasp such a high flight. That difference in perspective is the heart of the story.

Legacy

The Peng became a famous literary metaphor in Chinese literature. The character peng suggests greatness and bright prospects, so it appears in many personal names in mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan. Mao Zedong invoked it in a 1930 poem. Chinese palaeontologists used it to name the bird Pengornis and the pterosaur Kunpengopterus. Dapeng is also a place name, as in Shenzhen and Taiwan.

Ancient sources: The Daoist classic Zhuangzi preserves the oldest record of the Kun and Peng myth, in its first chapter.

Trivia

A popular name element. The character peng is common in personal names in China, Hong Kong and Taiwan, and several politicians from those regions carry it.
A poem by Mao. Mao Zedong evoked Kun and Peng in a 1930 poem written during a military campaign.
