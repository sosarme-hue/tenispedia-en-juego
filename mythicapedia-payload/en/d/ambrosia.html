nymph, one of the Hyades of Greek mythology

Ambrosia

Ambrosia is one of the Hyades, the nymphs of Greek mythology who cared for the infant Dionysus. Her story links the god of wine to the star cluster that bears her sisters' name.

The myth

In the best-known account, the child Dionysus was entrusted to Ambrosia and her sisters. When Lycurgus attacked him as he crossed his lands on Mount Nysa, the Hyades fled, and Lycurgus pursued Ambrosia, who died during the chase. Before dying she called on Mother Earth, who took her in and turned her into a vine whose branches trapped the murderer until the god returned.

Another version makes the Hyades daughters of Atlas and Pleione. When their only brother Hyas was killed by a lion or a boar, they wept so bitterly that the gods, moved by pity, turned them into stars placed in the constellation Taurus, while Hyas was transformed into Aquarius.

Legacy

Her name lives on in the Hyades, the star cluster in the constellation Taurus.

Trivia
