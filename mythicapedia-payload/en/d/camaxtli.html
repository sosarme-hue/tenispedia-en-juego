Tlaxcalan god of hunting, war and fire

Camaxtli

Camaxtli is the god of hunting, war, hope and fire among the Tlaxcalans, a people of Nahua tradition. He is counted among the four creator gods of the Earth and, for the Chichimecs, was their chief god.

The myth

According to tradition, Camaxtli led his people from Chicomóztoc to the promised land of Tlaxcala, where they settled. He is credited with inventing fire. His regalia include a basket for game, a bow, three arrows carried on the left shoulder, two heron feathers on the left temple, and a body painted white and striped with obsidian blades. He wears a black mask made from the ashes of the goddess Itzpapalotl, whom he had defeated.

Some modern scholars identify him with a form of the red Tezcatlipoca, with Xipe Totec or with Mixcoatl, or link him to the sun or to the night because of his black face paint.

Worship and religion

His great festival, Panquezaliztli, was held in the altépetl of Ocotelulco and Tepeticpac, with fasting, sacrifices of hunted animals, and the sacrifice of a captive warrior dressed as the god, who was called the «son of the sun». The ritual ended after eighty days. The sources do not say whether this cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Colonial-era chronicles by Diego Durán and Toribio de Motolinia describe his cult in Tlaxcala.

Trivia

Son of the sun. According to Toribio de Motolinia, the warrior sacrificed first in the ritual was called the «son of the sun».
Festival in the month Quecholli. According to Diego de Durán, Camaxtli's rituals took place during the fourteenth month, called Quecholli.
