giant from Nordic church-building legends

Fin

Fin is a giant or troll from Nordic legends about the building of churches. His stories tell of a bargain with a holy man, whose outcome depends on guessing a secret name.

The myth

In the Danish version, Esbern Snare was building a church in Kalundborg when a troll offered his help on one condition: if Esbern could not guess the troll's name by the time the church was finished, he would lose his heart and eyes. Esbern overheard the troll's wife soothing her child, promising that the next day Fin would bring him the human's eyes. Esbern shouted the name, the troll flew into a rage and hurled the last pillar into the air, which is why the church has three and a half pillars. In the Swedish version, the builder is Saint Lawrence, and the giant ends up turned to stone along with his family.

Legacy

The statues in the crypt of Lund Cathedral were linked to this tale, although today they are thought to show Samson toppling the pillars of a Philistine temple.

Ancient sources: The earliest mentions are not ancient: Erich Lassota von Steblau in 1593 and Jens Lauritzen Wolf in 1654.

Trivia

Three and a half pillars. The Danish legend explains why the church in Kalundborg has only three and a half pillars: the troll hurled the last one into the air.
Stone giants. In the crypt of Lund Cathedral there are two statues that tradition identifies with the giant and his wife, turned to stone.
A passing diplomat. The diplomat Erich Lassota von Steblau mentioned the legend in passing in 1593, after being imprisoned in Sweden.
