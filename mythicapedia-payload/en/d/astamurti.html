the eight forms of the Hindu god Shiva

Ashtamurti

Ashtamurti (“having eight forms”) is the iconographic representation of the eight manifestations of the Hindu god Shiva. Its roots lie in the many names of Rudra, the howling deity of the Rig Veda, who was later merged with Shiva.

The myth

According to tradition, the Rigveda presents Rudra with numerous attributes and titles, eight of which became central to the Shaiva tradition. Rudra was later identified with Shiva: the Shvetashvatara Upanishad records this first, followed by the Taittiriya Samhita and the Vajasaneyi Samhita, which honour Shiva with the words “tam shiva namasi”, “I bow to you, Shiva”.

The Shatapatha Brahmana lists names such as Bhava, Mahadeva, Sharva, Pashupati, Ugra and Ishana. The Kaushitaki Brahmana gives a different set of eight forms: water, fire, air, medicinal plants, the sun, the moon, food and Indra. Later, the Shiva Mahimna Stotram, attributed to the gandharva Pushpadanta, names another eight, including Bhava, Sharva, Rudra and Pashupati.

Worship and religion

The Shaiva Siddhanta and Pashupata schools recognise the Ashtamurti iconography in the Agamas, though they give greater weight to the attribute of the five brahmans.

Ancient sources: Rigveda, Shatapatha Brahmana, Kaushitaki Brahmana, Shvetashvatara Upanishad, Shiva Mahimna Stotram and Bhagavata Purana.
