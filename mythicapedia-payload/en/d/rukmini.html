Hindu goddess, chief consort of Krishna

Rukmini

Rukmini is a Hindu goddess and the first queen of Krishna in Dvaraka. She is regarded as an incarnation of Lakshmi and is widely revered in Maharashtra and southern India.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata and the Puranas, Rukmini was the daughter of Bhishmaka, king of Vidarbha, and had five elder brothers, including Rukmi. Having heard of Krishna's exploits, she fell in love with him, but her brother, an ally of Jarasandha, wanted her to marry Shishupala, Krishna's cousin. Rukmini sent a letter asking Krishna to abduct her during a visit to the temple of Ambika. Krishna, with Balarama, lifted her into his chariot while the pursuing forces were driven back. Rukmi was spared at his sister's plea, though his hair and moustache were shaved as punishment. The wedding was celebrated in Dvaraka.

The Bhagavata Purana tells how Krishna, to test her devotion, feigned indifference, and Rukmini fell to the ground in distress until he assured her he had been joking. Later, wishing for a child, Krishna asked Shiva for a blessing, and Pradyumna was born, a reincarnation of Kamadeva. Accounts of her sons differ: the Bhagavata and Vishnu Purana name ten, while the Harivamsa and the Anushasana Parva give other names.

Worship and religion

Her worship remains alive. In Maharashtra she is venerated alongside Vithoba, a regional form of Krishna, under the name Rakhumai, and in southern India she is worshipped together with Krishna and Satyabhama. Her birthday is marked each year with the festival of Rukmini Ashtami.

Legacy

Her story inspired theatre, such as the play Rukmini Horon by the fifteenth-century Assamese playwright Srimanta Sankaradeva.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana, Harivamsa, Padma Purana and Skanda Purana.

Trivia

The weight of a leaf. In a folktale from Odisha, Rukmini outweighs Satyabhama's riches by placing a single tulasi leaf on the scale. This story does not appear in the main scriptures about Krishna.
A regional name. In Maharashtra she is known as Rakhumai, meaning Mother Rukmini, and is worshipped alongside Vithoba.
