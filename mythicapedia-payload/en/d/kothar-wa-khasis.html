Ugaritic divine craftsman

Kothar-wa-Khasis

Kothar-wa-Khasis is a divine craftsman of the Ugaritic pantheon, from the Late Bronze Age city on the Syrian coast. He was regarded as a divine builder of temples and maker of weapons, which is why he is often compared to Hephaestus.

The myth

In the Baal Cycle, El calls on him to build a temple for Yam, the sea god. Later he supplies Baal with weapons, and Baal defeats Yam with them. The weather god asks for his help in preparing gifts for Athirat and later in building his own palace. In the Epic of Aqhat he makes the bow of the hero. Philo of Byblos records him as Chousor, a mortal inventor of the arts, a later reflection of this craftsman god.

Worship and religion

Ugaritic ritual texts mention him often, and in sacrificial lists he receives a cow or two rams. He was associated with temple construction and with magic. He was probably worshipped in Memphis and Caphtor, and his cult reached Egypt with few followers. No living worship of him is known today.

Ancient sources: Ugaritic texts of the Baal Cycle and the Epic of Aqhat; Philo of Byblos's Phoenician History names him as Chousor.

Trivia

A name meaning skill. His name is linked to a root meaning to be skilled or to achieve. A basic translation would be skilled and wise.
Female relatives. The Kotharat, a group of midwife goddesses known from Emar, Mari and Ugarit, have a name cognate with his.
