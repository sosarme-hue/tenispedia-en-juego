one of the Charites, Greek goddess of splendour

Aglaia

Aglaia is the youngest of the three Charites, the Graces of Greek myth, goddesses of beauty, charm and adornment. According to Hesiod she was a daughter of Zeus and the Oceanid Eurynome, and the wife of Hephaestus, the smith god.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Eurynome bore Zeus three Graces with lovely cheeks: Aglaia, Euphrosyne and Thalia. Together with her sisters, Aglaia lent her grace to everything that delights and honours gods and men, and her eyes shone with a dazzling beauty.

The same poem says that Hephaestus, the lame god, took her as his bride, she being the youngest of the Graces. Homer calls her Charis, 'of the splendid veil', and shows her welcoming Thetis in the house of Hephaestus. Other traditions give the Charites different parents: the Orphic Hymn names Eunomia as their mother, while Antimachus makes them daughters of Aigle and Helios.

According to the Orphic tradition, Hephaestus and Aglaia were the parents of a second generation of Charites: Eucleia, Euthenia, Eupheme and Philophrosyne. Sostratus tells of a contest in which Calê, one of the Charites, was chosen by Tiresias over Aphrodite, and some scholars have linked Calê with Aglaia.

Worship and religion

The Charites were worshipped together across Greece, and their cult was tied to grace, festivity and the arts. The most perfect works of art were called 'works of the Charites', and great artists were considered their favourites.

Legacy

The asteroid 3 Aglaia is named after her, and the word 'grace' in English owes much to the idea the Charites embodied.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony and Homer's Iliad, with later accounts by Sostratus and the Orphic Hymns.

Trivia

A goddess with several names. Aglaia also appears as Charis or Calê, names meaning 'grace' and 'beauty'. This is why some authors think different characters may be the same figure.
An unusual marriage. Hesiod describes her as married to Hephaestus, the lame smith god, a union that contrasts with the goddess's grace.
