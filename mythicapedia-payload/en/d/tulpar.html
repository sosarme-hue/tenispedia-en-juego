winged horse of Turkic mythology

Tulpar

The tulpar is a winged or celestial horse from the mythology of Turkic-speaking peoples of Central Asia, such as the Kazakhs, Kyrgyz, Uzbeks and Tatars. Often compared to Pegasus, it appears on the national emblem of Kazakhstan, a sign of its lasting place in the identity of these peoples.

The myth

Tradition holds that the tulpar was born from the close bond between steppe peoples and their horses. Hunters rode across the steppe accompanied by birds of prey, and in popular imagination the two animals merged into a winged horse. The wings were probably meant less for flight than to stress its extraordinary speed.

In folk tales, the tulpar is a faithful companion of the batyrs, the heroes of epic poetry. It is said to share its whole life with its master, and it is treated as an exceptional being, closer to an ally than to an ordinary animal.

The tulpar also belongs to a wider Eurasian image of the heavenly horse, found since antiquity. In some traditions such horses link earth and sky and carry their riders toward immortality or divine approval of power.

Legacy

The tulpar appears on the national emblem of Kazakhstan, where two golden examples gallop beneath a blue sky. It also features on Mongolia's emblem. Its image has spread through the art and popular culture of Turkic-speaking peoples.
