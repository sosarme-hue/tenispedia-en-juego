Japanese god born from the blood of Kagutsuchi

Iwatsutsunoo

Iwatsutsunoo is a deity of Japanese mythology who emerged during the creation of the gods, from the blood of Kagutsuchi, the god of fire. His origin is told in the Kojiki and the Nihonshoki.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Iwatsutsunoo appeared during the creation of the gods, after the birth of Kagutsuchi, the god of fire. This god inflicted the fatal burns on Izanami, who died as a result. Her husband Izanagi, overcome with grief and rage, drew a sword and cut Kagutsuchi into pieces, and from the fragments of his body other deities arose.

From the blood of the fire god, more divine beings were also born. Iwasaku and Nesaku were the first to appear, emerging from a rock, and Iwatsutsunoo followed. The Nihonshoki tells the same story, which suggests the tradition was widespread. According to tradition, his son was Futsunushi.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihonshoki recount his appearance among the gods born after the death of Izanami.
