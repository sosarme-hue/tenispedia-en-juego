Hymir

Hymir is a giant (jötunn) of Norse mythology and the owner of a huge cauldron that the gods wanted for brewing beer. His story, in which Thor fishes up the serpent Jörmungandr, is one of the best-known tales of Scandinavian tradition.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Hymir was a giant married to Hrod, who lived at the edge of the heavens, east of the Élivágar rivers. He owned an enormous cauldron that Ægir, god of the sea, needed to brew beer for a feast for the Æsir. Týr remembered that the wise giant had one, and suggested that Thor go and fetch it.

On arrival, Thor ate so much that Hymir proposed they go fishing. They used the head of the giant's best ox as bait, and Thor hooked the serpent Jörmungandr, but Hymir, terrified, cut the line and let it go. Back on dry land, the giant challenged Thor to break a magic cup, which could only be shattered by throwing it at Hymir's hard skull. After Thor smashed it, the giant gave in and handed over the cauldron.

The Poetic Edda's Hymiskviða is the only source that makes Hymir the father of Týr; elsewhere, as in Snorri's Gylfaginning, Odin is Týr's father. Snorri adds that while Thor was hauling in the monster, his feet went through the bottom of the boat. Fragments of the tale survive in the skaldic poems Húsdrápa and Ragnarsdrápa, which call Hymir a clumsy giant and 'the one who sends the breeze'. He has been compared with Hræsvelgr, the eagle-shaped giant of Vafþrúðnismál who stirs up the winds by beating his wings.

Legacy

Hymir appears on several Viking Age picture stones, including Ardre VIII, Hørdum and the Gosforth Cross, where Thor's fishing trip with the giant is shown. It was one of the most popular motifs in Norse art. The meaning of his name remains unclear, with suggestions such as 'creeper' or links to Norwegian words for 'weary'.

Ancient sources: Hymiskviða, a poem of the Poetic Edda that tells his story, with mentions in Lokasenna, Húsdrápa and Ragnarsdrápa, and in the Prose Edda's Gylfaginning.

Trivia

An unusually hard skull. Hymir's skull was so tough that the magic cup shattered when it struck him. That scene decides whether he hands over the cauldron.
An unusual bait. To catch Jörmungandr, Thor used an ox head as bait, a detail that recurs in several artistic depictions.
