wealthy Ulster cattle-owner in Irish mythology

Cruinniuc

Cruinniuc is a wealthy cattle-owner of Ulster, the protagonist of a tale from the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. His story explains the origin of the name of the province's capital and the curse that left its men helpless in the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

The myth

A widower, Cruinniuc is surprised when a beautiful woman turns up at his house, shares his bed and takes care of his children without revealing her name. Soon she is pregnant by him.

Later, at a festival, Cruinniuc boasts that his wife can run faster than the king's chariot. The king hears him and forces the heavily pregnant woman to race against his chariot. She wins, but gives birth to twins on the finish line. She then reveals that her name is Macha, daughter of Sainrith mac Imbaith, and the capital of Ulster is thereafter called Emain Macha, meaning "Macha's Twins".

Finally, Macha curses the men of Ulster to suffer her labour pains in the hour of their greatest need. That is why, in the Táin Bó Cúailnge, only Cúchulainn is able to fight.

Trivia

Origin of a name. The capital of Ulster, Emain Macha, takes its name from the twins the woman gave birth to during the race.
A curse with wartime effect. Macha's curse makes nearly all the warriors of Ulster suffer labour pains in battle, leaving only Cúchulainn to fight.
