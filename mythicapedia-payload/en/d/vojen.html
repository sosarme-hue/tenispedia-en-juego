mythical prince of Bohemia

Vojen

Vojen is a mythical prince of Bohemia, the third of seven legendary rulers who, according to tradition, stood between the founder of the Přemyslid dynasty and Bořivoj, the first historical prince. His name is linked to the Slavonic word for war.

The myth

According to Cosmas of Prague's chronicle, Vojen holds third place in a list of princes who came before recorded history, and the account says little about his deeds. His role is mainly genealogical: tradition presents him as the son of Mnata and the father of Vnislav.

Some argue that the frescoes in the rotunda at Znojmo, in Moravia, reflect this list, although Anežka Merhautová believed they depict all members of the dynasty, including the minor Moravian princes.

Legacy

Their names passed into many nineteenth-century history books, such as František Palacký's work on Bohemia and Moravia. Záviš Kalandra proposed that the seven names were old labels for the days of the week: Vojen would correspond to Tuesday, Martis dies, the day of Mars, god of war. Vladimír Karbusický, by contrast, suggested that Cosmas reconstructed a supposed Slavonic message, perhaps addressed to the Franks, linked to the Battle of Zásek around 849.

Ancient sources: Cosmas of Prague's Latin chronicle, written in the early twelfth century, is the first source to record the names of these princes.

Trivia

A name with weekdays. According to Záviš Kalandra, the names of the seven princes might be old Slavonic labels for the days of the week. Vojen would equal Tuesday.
A lost message. Vladimír Karbusický proposed that Cosmas tried to read a lost Latin transcription of an Old Slavonic message, whose reconstructed text begins with the words Krok kazi tetha.
