demon of the goetic grimoires

Volac

Volac (also Valac) is a demon found in European ceremonial magic grimoires, especially the Goetia and Johann Weyer's work. He is described as a winged boy riding a two-headed dragon, and his modern fame owes more to horror films and video games than to his original texts.

The myth

In the Renaissance grimoires, Volac is a president of Hell commanding thirty legions of demons, though some manuscripts say thirty-eight. His main power is to locate, summon and control serpents, and in some versions he can also find treasures.

The sources do not agree on his rank or number of legions. The Munich Manual calls him Volach and gives him twenty-seven legions; the Officium Spirituum names him Coolor or Doolas and makes him a prince rather than a president; and in Thomas Rudd's version he is opposed by the angel Iahhael. His look, a winged boy on a two-headed dragon, recurs in nearly every version.

Legacy

His name survives mostly in popular culture. In film, the demonic nun Valak from The Conjuring 2 and the two The Nun films takes his name, although her appearance has nothing to do with the grimoire. In the Sabrina comics a young Edward Spellman summons him, and in Hellboy he is a major antagonist. He is also the fourth boss of Bloodstained: Curse of the Moon, shown as a two-headed dragon, and appears in the series Shadowhunters and the manga Iruma-kun.

Ancient sources: The Lesser Key of Solomon (Goetia), Johann Weyer's Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, the Liber Officiorum Spirituum and the Munich Manual of Demonic Magic.

Trivia

Many spellings. According to the sources, his name is written in several forms, such as Ualac, Valak, Valax or Valic, depending on the grimoire and language.
A demon of snakes. Control over serpents is the trait that recurs most in the grimoires, and the 2016 film picked it up very loosely.
