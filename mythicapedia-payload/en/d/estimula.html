Roman goddess of stimulus and impulse

Stimula

Stimula, also spelled Stímula or Estímula, is a goddess of Roman mythology linked to stimulus and impulse. She is associated with the festivals of Bacchus and, according to another tradition, with the haste of merchants in their business.

The myth

Few extended stories about Stimula survive, so what remains is a brief description of her functions. According to one tradition, she was the goddess who stirred passion in women, especially in the Bacchants, during the festivals honouring Bacchus. Her Greek counterpart is said to be Semele, mother of Dionysus, a link that ties her to the cult of the wine god and to ecstatic frenzy.

Another version presents her as one of the di indigetes, a protector of the urges that drive men toward sexual excess. She was also given a role in commerce: she urged merchants to hurry their dealings, so that the impulse she roused reached economic life as well.

Ancient sources: She is named among the deities in the lists of indigitamenta, catalogues of gods invoked in Roman rites.

Trivia

A Greek parallel. According to the description of her role, her Greek counterpart would be Semele, the mother of Dionysus.
A goddess of trade. She was said to urge merchants to hurry their business, an unusual role for a deity of impulses.
