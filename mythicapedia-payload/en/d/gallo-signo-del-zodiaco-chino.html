Rooster (Chinese zodiac)

The Rooster is the tenth animal in the twelve-year cycle of the Chinese zodiac, linked in Chinese tradition with punctuality, courage, the dawn and good fortune. Its Chinese name, jī (鸡), sounds like jí (吉), meaning "luck", which helps explain much of its reputation.

The myth

In Chinese tradition the rooster is tied to dawn: its crow announces the sun and the retreat of night spirits. For this reason it is regarded as a ghost-hunter, and in the lower Yangtze region people pasted a picture of a rooster on their doors during the Spring Festival to keep such spirits away.

Another older belief links the rooster to the sun. Because it wakes each day at sunrise, it was thought to represent the sun, and some popular accounts connect it with the Chinese phoenix, Feng Huang, though this link belongs more to folk belief than to ancient texts.

The zodiac describes those born in its year as proud, observant, precise, loyal and ambitious, though also vain and somewhat domineering. Its keen powers of observation are said to help them carry out ambitious projects and win social recognition.

Legacy

The rooster appears in the Chinese zodiac and in popular culture across East Asia, where it features in calendars, decorations and New Year greetings. Its image has long served as a charm for good luck. In Chinese custom, a rooster could stand in for an absent bridegroom during a wedding ceremony. The Japanese Onagadori breed, known for its very long tail feathers, is called the "phoenix rooster".

Trivia

Rooster and spirits. According to popular belief, spirits can appear only at night and scatter at dawn, so the rooster's crow drives them away.
A rooster for the groom. In ancient China, when the groom could not attend the wedding, a rooster was placed beside the bride during the ceremony.
