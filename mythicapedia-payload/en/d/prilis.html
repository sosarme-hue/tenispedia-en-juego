seer of Lesbos, son of Hermes

Prylis

Prylis is a seer in Greek mythology, son of Hermes and the Lesbian nymph Issa. His name is linked to the idea of the Trojan Horse, the stratagem that opened the city to the Greeks.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the scholia to Lycophron's Alexandra, the goddess Athena revealed to Prylis that the Greeks could enter Troy only by building a wooden horse. The seer passed the revelation on to King Agamemnon after receiving bribes from Palamedes, and Agamemnon ordered Epeius to build the giant structure.

The ancient sources do not fully agree. In the Odyssey, Homer credits Epeius with building the horse with Athena's help, while Apollodorus presents Odysseus as the author of the idea, which suggests that Prylis may have been a local Lesbian tradition that reduced the role of the hero of Ithaca.

Legacy

An asteroid discovered in 1973, (7543) Prylis, is named after this seer.

Ancient sources: The scholia to Lycophron's Alexandra are the only sources that name Prylis; Homer's Odyssey and Apollodorus's Library mention the horse without him.

Trivia

An asteroid with his name. The asteroid (7543) Prylis, discovered in 1973, was named after this seer.
Who got the credit?. According to Apollodorus, Odysseus claimed all the credit for the stratagem of the horse, although other figures in the ancient tradition are also linked to its construction.
