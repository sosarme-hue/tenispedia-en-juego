Taoist deity of the Divine Treasure

Lingbao Tianzun

Lingbao Tianzun, the Venerable Celestial of Divine Treasures, is one of the Three Pure Ones of Taoism. He holds the second place in that supreme triad and is regarded as lord of the Upper Purity.

The myth

According to the Yunji Qiqian, he is one of the manifestations of the Great Dao and embodies the essence of the Jade Emperor. Another account, the Lingbao Lüeji, says he was conceived in the Western Green Jade Kingdom and carried in the womb for 3,700 years before birth.

Named Qi Du, he reached enlightenment by meditating for a hundred days beneath a withered mulberry tree, until Yuanshi Tianzun descended and gave him the ten great volumes of the Lingbao Mahayana teachings.

Worship and religion

He is invoked in Taoist funeral rituals, and it is said he can take any form to lead souls to paradise. The Dongxuan Palace in the Zhujia'ao Valley, near Mount Mian in Shanxi, is dedicated to him; his statue is encircled by a ring of light.

Ancient sources: The Yunji Qiqian (Seven Bamboo Tablets of the Cloudy Satchel) and the Lingbao Lüeji (Brief Records of Lingbao) record his story.

Trivia

A mushroom-shaped scepter. He holds a scepter that grants wishes, shaped like a mushroom. It symbolizes the second phase of Creation, when Yang separated from Yin.
Eyes full of mystery. In the main hall of the Dongxuan Palace, his eyes are believed to contain the mysteries of the universe.
