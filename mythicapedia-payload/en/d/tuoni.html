Finnish god of the land of the dead

Tuoni

Tuoni is the Finnish god of the underworld and of darkness, lord of Tuonela, the realm of the dead. A figure of Finnish mythology, he shows how that tradition pictured the afterlife as a gloomy place that was hard to pass through.

The myth

According to Finnish tradition, Tuoni rules Tuonela, the land of the dead, a realm of darkness that few travellers enter and fewer leave. He is married to Tuonetar and has several daughters, among them Loviatar, a blind goddess of illness and plague, Kivutar, linked to prayers and litanies, and Vammatar, mistress of affliction and suffering. Another account also names Kipu-Tyttö and Tuonenpoika among his children, divinities of suffering. The poems gathered in the Kalevala show him greeting the dead with a jug full of frogs and worms in his right hand.

Few heroes manage to leave Tuonela, and one of them is Väinämöinen. Once he has reached the far bank of the river of death, Tuonetar plies him with beer until he dozes off. As he nods, Tuoni's daughters stretch an iron net to block his escape, but the old hero turns into a bird, flies across the realm of the dead and reaches freedom.

Legacy

In music, the Finnish symphonic metal band Amberian Dawn released their debut album River of Tuoni in 2008. Its title track tells of Tuoni abducting a mother's son, and of her journey into the world of darkness to bring him back.

Ancient sources: The Kalevala, the Finnish epic that Elias Lönnrot compiled in the nineteenth century from oral folk songs, is the main text that records him.

Trivia

Human appearance. When he takes human form, he appears as an old man with three fingers on each hand and a hat of darkness.
A jug of frogs. He receives the dead holding a jug full of frogs and worms in his right hand.
