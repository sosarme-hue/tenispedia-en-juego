Roman goddess of parents bereaved of their children

Orbona

Orbona is a Roman goddess whom parents who had lost their children would invoke, as did parents of sick children. Her name comes from orbus, meaning bereft, and her role was protective rather than narrative.

The myth

She has almost no stories of her own. Ancient writers present her as a figure who watches over grieving parents. Cicero lists her among the harmful deities, and Arnobius, a Christian apologist of the 4th century, also mentions her. Some sources link her with Febris, goddess of fever, and Mala Fortuna, goddess of bad luck, hinting at a darker side.

According to some authors, her offerings were not meant for the dead person's soul, but for the safety of the surviving children or for the conception of new ones.

Worship and religion

She had a temple in Rome near that of the Lares, which according to Pliny the Elder was meant to relieve disease. There is no evidence that her cult survives today.

Legacy

Her name survives mainly in modern fiction: it is the name of a planet in Tony DiTerlizzi's children's novel The Search for WondLa, and it appears as "the Blessed Orbona" in the HBO series Rome.

Ancient sources: Cicero, Arnobius and Pliny the Elder mention her.

Trivia

A name rooted in orphanhood. Her name derives from orbus, meaning bereft, the same root as the Greek ὀρφανός, meaning orphan.
Protector of the grieving. Her role was to protect parents who lost children, a rare focus among Roman deities.
