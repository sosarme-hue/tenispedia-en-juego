son of Aegisthus in Greek myth

Aletes

Aletes is, in Greek myth, the son of Aegisthus and probably of Clytemnestra. He appears in Sophocles' lost tragedy named after him and in Hyginus' Fabulae, and his death at the hands of Orestes closes one of the episodes of vengeance within the house of the Atreidae.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved by Hyginus, Aletes took advantage of a false report. A messenger told Electra that her brother Orestes and Pylades had been sacrificed to Artemis in Tauris. When it became known that no one of the Atreidae line survived, Aletes seized royal power in Mycenae.

Electra went to Delphi to ask about her brother's supposed death and there met Iphigenia and Orestes, who were in fact alive. When the same messenger accused Iphigenia of being Orestes' killer, Electra nearly blinded her sister with a burning brand from the altar, until Orestes intervened and they recognised one another.

Afterwards, Orestes returned to Mycenae and killed Aletes. In the story, he also meant to kill Erigone, daughter of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, but Artemis saved her and made her her priestess in Attica. The sources do not fully agree on the exact kinship, and Aletes' mother is only inferred from the absence of any other consort of Aegisthus.

Ancient sources: Sophocles' lost tragedy Aletes, known only from references, and Hyginus' Fabulae.

Trivia

A king in between. Aletes appears in the lists of Achaean kings after Clitus, son of Temenus, and before Tisamenus, son of Orestes.
A lost tragedy. Sophocles wrote a play titled Aletes, now lost apart from references, and much of what we know of the character comes from it.
