Celtic god of youth

Maponos

Maponos is an ancient Celtic god of youth, worshipped in Britain and Gaul. His name means roughly 'divine son' or 'divine youth', and he is generally seen as the forerunner of the medieval Welsh figure Mabon.

The myth

No classical author mentions him, so much of what is known comes from inscriptions, place-names and the later insular literary tradition. His mother is the goddess Matrona, the eponym of the river Marne, and the pair reappears in medieval Welsh literature as Mabon fab Modron.

In the Welsh tale, Mabon is a prisoner who was taken from his mother when he was three nights old. Arthur's men must free him from his prison at Caer Loyw so that he can join the hunt for the boar Twrch Trwyth. Some scholars have read his story of abduction, searching and rescue as a Celtic counterpart of the Greek myth of Demeter and Persephone, though this reading remains speculative.

Worship and religion

His cult is chiefly attested in northern Britain, near Hadrian's Wall, where he was identified with Apollo. Many of his dedicants were officers of the Roman army. Altars to Apollo Maponus were found at Corbridge and Ribchester, and one shows Apollo with a lyre, which has been linked to music and poetry.

Legacy

His name survives in the village of Lochmaben in Dumfriesshire and in the Lochmaben Stone near Gretna. Some have suggested that the Mabinogi took its name from material concerning this god, though the idea is unproven.

Ancient sources: Latin and Gaulish inscriptions from Britain and Gaul, the Ravenna Cosmography, and the medieval Welsh literary tradition, such as the tale of Culhwch and Olwen.
