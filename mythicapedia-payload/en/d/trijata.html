kindly rakshasi of the Ramayana

Trijata

Trijata is a rakshasi (demoness) in the Hindu epic Ramayana, assigned to guard Sita during her captivity in Lanka. She is remembered as a loyal companion who comforts the goddess and brings her news from the outside world, making her one of the most beloved female figures in the tradition.

The myth

In Valmiki's Ramayana, Trijata is an aged rakshasi who watches over Sita in the Ashoka grove of Lanka. When the other guardians harass Sita to make her accept Ravana, Trijata intervenes and recounts a prophetic dream: she sees Rama and Lakshmana riding the celestial elephant Airavata toward Sita, while Ravana is brought low and the city of Lanka sinks into the ocean. Convinced of Rama's victory, she advises the rakshasis to seek Sita's protection.

In the battle book, Indrajit binds Rama and Lakshmana with the Nagapasha weapon and both lose consciousness. Ravana takes Sita to view the battlefield, and when she laments, believing her husband dead, Trijata reassures her that the brothers are still alive.

In later versions, especially those of Southeast Asia, Trijata becomes the daughter of Vibhishana, Ravana's brother who sides with Rama. Her role grows: she stops Sita from taking her own life and, in some works, takes over functions that belong to Kala, Sarama or Mandodari in the original. After Rama's victory, both reward her generously.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, Trijata is worshipped as a local goddess in Varanasi and Ujjain, in India.

Legacy

She appears in Ramayana versions from Laos, Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia, where she goes by names such as Punukay, Benyakai or Devi Seri Jali.

Ancient sources: She appears in Valmiki's Ramayana, in the Sundara Kanda and Yuddha Kanda books; later in the Ramopakhyana of the Mahabharata and in works such as the Kakawin Ramayana.

Trivia

Three names in Southeast Asia. In the Laotian Phra Lak Phra Lam she is called Punukay, in the Thai Ramakien Benyakai, and in the Malay Hikayat Seri Rama, Devi Seri Jali.
An unexpected daughter. In Valmiki's original she is an elderly rakshasi, yet Govindaraja's commentary makes her Vibhishana's daughter, a detail the scholar Goldman finds strange given her age.
