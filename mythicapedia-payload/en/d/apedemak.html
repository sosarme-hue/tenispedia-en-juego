Nubian lion-headed god of war

Apedemak

Apedemak was the main deity of Nubia and the Kushite kingdom of Meroe, a war god shown with a lion's head. Some sources link him to Egyptian mythology, but his cult was Nubian in origin, and he became the dynastic god of the Meroitic kings.

The myth

Few texts about Apedemak survive, so much of what we know comes from his images. He is usually shown with a human torso and a lion's head. At the Naqa temple he appears with a coiled snake's body, and in other scenes as a winged hawk bearing the sun disc, hinting at solar traits.

He carries a bow, arrows and leather armour, and appears slaying enemies or holding a captive in chains. Beyond his role as a war god, he is also credited as a creator and fertility god: a hymn at the Lion Temple calls him the one who nourishes all mankind, and at Naqa he holds grain. His consort was the goddess Amesemi, linked to the moon.

Worship and religion

His temples are concentrated in the Butana region, south of Meroe, which points to a southern origin. The two main studied sites are the Lion Temple at Musawwarat es-Sufra, built in the late 3rd century BCE, and the sanctuary at Naqa, dating from the 2nd and 1st centuries BCE. In coronation scenes he touches the new ruler's elbow to invest him. By the 3rd century BCE he was already important to the peoples of Upper Nubia, and in the Meroitic period he headed the Kushite pantheon, held on equal footing with Amun.

Legacy

He is seen as a symbol of military power, conquest and empire in the Kushite kingdom.

Ancient sources: Hymns inscribed in the Lion Temple at Musawwarat es-Sufra and references in Meroitic literary sources.

Trivia

A name with an uncertain pronunciation. His name is thought to have meant 'father king'. Because Meroitic lacked the /p/ sound, it was probably pronounced abademak.
