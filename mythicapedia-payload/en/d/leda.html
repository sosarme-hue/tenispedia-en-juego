Leda

Leda is a figure of Greek mythology, an Aetolian princess who became queen of Sparta through her marriage to King Tyndareus. She is famous for being seduced by Zeus in the form of a swan and for being the mother of Helen of Troy, Clytemnestra, Castor and Pollux.

The myth

According to tradition, Leda was a daughter of the Aetolian king Thestius, although the sources disagree about her parentage. Some accounts name Glaucus as her father, others Thestius, and her mother is given variously as Eurythemis, Leucippe, Laophonte or Deidameia. She had at least two sisters, Althaea and Hypermnestra.

Leda married Tyndareus, who had fled the violence of Hippocoon and had been received at the court of Thestius. When Heracles killed Hippocoon and his sons, Tyndareus returned home, inherited the throne, and the couple ruled Sparta together.

The best-known episode tells how, while Leda walked by the river Eurotas, Zeus appeared to her disguised as a swan, pretending to be chased by an eagle, and seduced her. That same night she also lay with Tyndareus. In the most common version, Helen and Pollux were children of Zeus, while Clytemnestra and Castor were children of Tyndareus, which is why Castor was mortal and Pollux immortal. Other tellings speak of two eggs from which the children hatched, and one variant says Helen was born of Nemesis, who laid an egg that a shepherd brought to Leda, who raised the child as her own daughter.

Worship and religion

In Sparta, an egg hung from the ceiling of the sanctuary of the Leucippides, supported by ribbons, was believed to be the egg from which Leda had given birth.

Legacy

The story of Leda and the Swan has been a recurring subject in painting, from Leonardo da Vinci to Salvador Dalí and Gustav Klimt. In literature, Rubén Darío wrote poems about her, Aldous Huxley gave the name Leda to a 1920 collection, and William Butler Yeats wrote the poem Leda and the Swan. Charly García drew on the myth for his song Un hada, un cisne.

Ancient sources: Homer, in the Odyssey, names Castor and Polydeuces as sons of Tyndareus. Hesiod, in the Catalogue of Women, records the birth of her daughters and of the Dioscuri.

Trivia

An egg in the sanctuary. In the sanctuary of the Leucippides in Sparta, an egg hung from the ceiling and was believed to be the one from which Leda had given birth.
A favourite subject in painting. Leda and the Swan was a very popular motif in art, especially during the Renaissance.
