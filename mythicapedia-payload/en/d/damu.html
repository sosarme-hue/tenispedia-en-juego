Sumerian god of healing and vegetation

Damu

Damu is a Mesopotamian god who was originally linked to vegetation and to the cycle of dying and returning, and who later became mainly a deity of healing. His figure shows how a dying god could turn into a medicine deity.

The myth

According to the different traditions, Damu was the son of the medicine goddess Ninisina, or of equivalent goddesses such as Gula or Ninkarrak. The texts disagree about his father: sometimes Enki is named, sometimes Ninurta or Pabilsag, Ninisina's husband.

Originally he was imagined as a dying god, similar to Dumuzi, but he was usually pictured as a child or an infant. Over time he took on the role of a healer: he was said to know medicinal plants and to cure diseases regarded as incurable. His attribute was a karzillu knife, and he was given epithets such as "lord of life".

Worship and religion

His main cult center was in Isin, alongside that of his mother, although the original place of his worship is still unidentified. Evidence has also been found at Ur and Larsa.

Legacy

His name appears in many personal names from Ebla, though some scholars think there it referred to a deified kinship group. It has been proposed that he was a local branch of the Babylonian god Tammuz.

Ancient sources: He appears in god lists, hymns, laments and medical texts from Mesopotamia, including an incantation to Ninisina and a lament for Nippur.

Trivia

A constellation linked to him. In Mesopotamian astronomy he was associated with the constellation Pig, which may have included stars now regarded as part of Draco.
A divine physician. He could be called asû, "physician", and was said to know medicinal plants.
