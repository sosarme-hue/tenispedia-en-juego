river god of Troy in Greek mythology

Scamander

Scamander is the river god who, according to the Iliad, flowed past Troy and whom the gods called Xanthus. Son of Oceanus and Tethys according to Hesiod, he sided with the Trojans in their war against the Achaeans.

The myth

According to the Iliad, the river had two names: the gods called it Xanthus and men called it Scamander. Tradition tells that, after a famine, a third of the Cretans set out with the prince Scamander for Phrygia, where they camped by the sea and dedicated a temple to Apollo Smintheus. With Apollo's help they defeated the Bebryces, but Scamander fell into the river during the fight, and the river took its name from him.

In the Trojan War, the god was enraged by Achilles, who had filled his channel with corpses after Patroclus died, and he tried to drown him; according to Homer, Hera and Hephaestus rescued the hero. The same river had two springs, one of warm water and one of cold. Hesiod makes him a son of Oceanus and Tethys among three thousand river gods, though others call him a son of Zeus. He was the father of King Teucer, born of the nymph Idaea.

Legacy

His name lived on in geography: according to Strabo, Aeneas named two rivers near Egesta in Sicily Scamander and Simois, and the ancient Trojan river is today identified with the Turkish Karamenderes.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad and Hesiod's Theogony, with later mentions by Ovid and Strabo.

Trivia

Two springs. According to Homer, the river rose from two springs: one gave warm water and the other cold water, whatever the season.
A river that changed its name. In a story by Pseudo-Plutarch, Scamander went mad during the mysteries of Rhea and threw himself into the river Xanthus, which was then renamed Scamander.
