Mesopotamian goddess of the underworld records

Belet-Seri

Belet-Seri is a Mesopotamian goddess who served as a scribe of the underworld, recording the lives of the dead. Her name means 'mistress of the steppe'.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian tradition, Belet-Seri kept the records of the underworld and, when someone died, drew up the list of names on which admission to the realm of Ereshkigal was based.

She appears in the Epic of Gilgamesh, where Enkidu has a vision of the underworld in a dream. In the incantation series Maqlû, Šurpu and Bīt Mēseri she is asked to bind demons and witches so they cannot leave the underworld. In the second millennium BCE she came to be linked with the goddess Gula and, by extension, with medicine.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in Nippur and Assur, in temples of Gula. In late Uruk she became one of the most celebrated deities, with her own temple apparently surrounded by an orchard. Texts note that fowl was considered an unsuitable offering for her.

Ancient sources: The Epic of Gilgamesh and the Babylonian incantations Udug-hul, Maqlû, Šurpu and Bīt Mēseri.

Trivia

A lady of the steppe. Her best-known title, 'lady of the steppe', could also refer to the land of the dead, since the word ṣēru also meant the underworld.
A palace in the steppe. According to documents from the Seleucid period, her temple in Uruk bore the name 'palace of the steppe'.
