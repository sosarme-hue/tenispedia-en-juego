Celtic god linked to the boar

Moccus

Moccus (or Moccos) is a Celtic god known from a single votive inscription at Langres, where he is identified with the Roman god Mercury. His name is linked to pigs and boars, animals associated with hunting, war and prosperity.

The myth

Little narrative survives about Moccus, and he is known mainly through his name and the boar. The most accepted etymology links him to a Gaulish root meaning pig or wild boar, seen in personal names such as Cato-mocus, war-boar. Other scholars connect him with place names near Langres or with a word meaning child. Some researchers associate him with the Euffigneix statue, a torc-wearing figure with a boar over his torso, and with the Irish myth in which Lugh obtains the pig-skin of Tuis, which could heal any wound.

Worship and religion

His only trace of worship is a votive tablet found at Langres, dated to the late 2nd or 3rd century AD, where he is identified with Mercury. The Lingones, the tribe of that area, may have been his devotees. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: He is known only from a votive inscription; he does not appear in ancient literary texts.

Trivia

A tablet found in the 17th century. The inscription turned up in the foundations of a ramp near the Grand Séminaire of Langres, in 1642 or 1645.
A boar over the torso. The Euffigneix statue shows a figure wearing a torc with a large boar over its torso, dated to around the 1st century BC.
