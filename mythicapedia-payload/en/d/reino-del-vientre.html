Womb Realm

The Womb Realm, or Taizōkai, is the metaphysical space in Vajrayana Buddhism inhabited by the Wisdom Kings. It is the compassion half of the Two Worlds Mandala, centred on the cosmic Buddha Mahāvairocana.

The myth

The Womb Realm embodies the great compassion of Mahāvairocana, the cosmic Buddha who nurtures all beings toward enlightenment. Its complement is the Vajra Realm, which embodies indestructible wisdom. Together they form a map of the cosmos, following the Mahāvairocana Sūtra, compiled in India around the mid-7th century.

Its deities are arranged symmetrically around the core and oriented to the cardinal directions, up to 414 figures, each with its own mudrās, attributes and seed syllable.

Worship and religion

Shingon and Tendai temples in Japan display these mandalas beside the central altar, and practitioners use them in meditation to identify with the deities. According to the Shingon tradition, the monk Huiguo unified them in China.

Legacy

Mandalas moved from earthen platforms to paintings and textiles in China, and the Borobudur complex in Indonesia is often seen as a mandala on an architectural scale.

Ancient sources: The Mahāvairocana Sūtra (Dainichi kyō), an Indian text of the esoteric tradition on which the mandala's structure is based.

Trivia

A mandala in stone. The Buddhist complex of Borobudur in Indonesia is considered a mandala on the scale of an entire temple.
Hundreds of figures. Some mandalas gather as many as 414 deities around their core.
