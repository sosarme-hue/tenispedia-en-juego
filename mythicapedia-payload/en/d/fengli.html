flying beast of Chinese legend

Fengli

The fengli, or 'wind leopard cat', is a flying creature from Chinese tradition, described in encyclopedias and materia medica compendiums from the 10th century onward. It later reached Japan, where it is known as fūri and appears in yokai literature.

The myth

According to the lore, the fengli has a leopard-like spotted coat but is small, about the size of a wildcat or marten. Its colour varies by source: blue-green, yellow and black. It lives in trees, eats fruit and, in some versions, spiders, and moves by flying or gliding from branch to branch on the wind.

The legends give it unusual powers. It carries a magic wand that, when pointed at prey, makes the prey fall from the tree. It feigns death when struck and revives when wind reaches it. It is said to be immune to blades and fire. It could only be killed completely by rupturing its brain and breaking its bones, or by plugging its nose with the root of sweet flag, which was considered lethal to it. Its urine was believed to cure leprosy.

Legacy

In Japan, the fūri entered yokai literature. The Wakan sansai zue describes it as absent from the country but equates it with a type of tanuki, because the character li is read tanuki in Japanese.

Ancient sources: Taiping yulan (10th century), Bencao gangmu (16th century), Baopuzi, Youyang zazu, Nanzhou yiwu zhi and Wakan sansai zue.

Trivia

A reading mix-up. In Japanese, the character li is read tanuki, so the fūri came to be considered a kind of tanuki, or raccoon dog.
Many names. The fengli has many names, such as fengmu ('mother of the wind') or pinghou ('flat monkey').
