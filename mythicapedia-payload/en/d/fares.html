Greek hero, son of Hermes and founder of Pharae

Pharis

Pharis is, in Greek mythology, a son of Hermes and the Danaid Phylodameia, and the founder of Pharae in Messene. He matters mainly for the family line that connects him to heroes of the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the tradition, Pharis was born to Hermes and Phylodameia, one of the Danaids. His only daughter was Telegone, who had a son by the river god Alpheius named Ortilochus (also spelled Orsilochus). Ortilochus became the father of Diocles, whose twin sons Crethon and Orsilochus fought at Troy and were killed by Aeneas.

The sources do not agree fully on who founded Pharae in Achaea. Pausanias leaves open whether it was this Pharis or someone else, and in that passage the name is spelled Pharés.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions his foundation of Pharae and his genealogy in his accounts of Greece.
