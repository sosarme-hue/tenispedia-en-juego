anti-hero of Welsh mythology

Efnisien

Efnisien is a sadistic anti-hero of Welsh mythology, a central figure in the second branch of the Mabinogi, the tale of Branwen daughter of Llŷr. His violence sets off the final tragedy that devastates Ireland and the Island of the Mighty.

The myth

Son of Euroswydd and Penarddun, he is the twin of Nisien and half-brother to Brân the Blessed, Manawydan and Branwen. When Brân arranges Branwen's marriage to the Irish king Matholwch without consulting him, Efnisien mutilates the groom's horses; Brân soothes the insult with a magic cauldron that can restore the dead to life. In Ireland, Efnisien kills the warriors hidden in sacks during a false truce and burns Gwern, Branwen's son, alive, which sparks a vast battle. The Irish revive their fallen in the cauldron, so Efnisien hides among the corpses, lets himself be thrown into it and destroys it from within, sacrificing himself.

Legacy

He appears as a character in the 2003 film Otherworld, where he is voiced by Welsh actor Alun Raglan. Welsh singer-songwriter Elin Fflur released the EP Ysbryd Efnisien (Efnisien's Ghost) in 2006. The Italian band Ataraxia has a song titled Evnyssien, and singer-songwriter Sharron Kraus tells his story in A Hero's Death.

Ancient sources: The second branch of the Mabinogi, the medieval Welsh tale of Branwen daughter of Llŷr, where he is a central figure.

Trivia

A famous prisoner. The Welsh Triads name Llŷr one of the Three Exalted Prisoners of Britain for his captivity at Euroswydd's hands, probably a lost tradition about the birth of Penarddun's younger sons.
Compared with Loki. Some scholars compare him with trickster figures such as the Irish hero Bricriu Nemthenga and the Norse god Loki.
