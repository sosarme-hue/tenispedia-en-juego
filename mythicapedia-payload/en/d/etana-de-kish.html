legendary Sumerian king and hero of the eagle myth

Etana of Kish

Etana of Kish is a legendary king of the first dynasty of Kish, listed thirteenth in the Sumerian King List, and the protagonist of a famous Babylonian myth about an eagle, a serpent and the plant of birth. He matters because his story is one of the oldest known tales about the search for an heir.

The myth

According to the Sumerian King List, Etana succeeded Arwium, son of Mashda, and was the father of Balih. The list describes him as a shepherd who ascended to heaven and brought all foreign lands under his control. His reign is given different lengths in different copies: 1,500 or 1,560 years in some, 635 in others.

The best-known story tells how Etana, desperate to have a child, rescued an eagle that was starving. In gratitude, the bird carried him up to the sky in search of the plant of birth. In the detailed version, a tree holds the eagle's nest at its top and a serpent lives at its base; both had promised the sun god Utu to live in peace, but the eagle devoured the serpent's young, and the serpent took revenge by trapping the eagle. Utu then sent Etana to help the bird. After saving it, Etana asked for the plant, and the eagle lifted him to the heaven of the god Anu. On the first attempt he grew afraid and fell back to earth; on the second he obtained the plant and fathered Balih.

Legacy

The myth survives in three versions: an Old Babylonian one (from Susa and Tell Harmal), a Middle Assyrian one (from Assur) and the Standard version from Nineveh. Folklorists link the tale to the ATU 537 type, the hero carried by a grateful eagle, and it has been suggested that the myth may have given rise to that folk type in later oral tradition.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian King List and the Babylonian, Middle Assyrian and Standard versions of the Etana myth, found at Susa, Tell Harmal, Assur and Nineveh.

Trivia

Historical or not. The kings in the early part of the Sumerian King List are usually not considered historical, unless they appear in contemporary documents. Etana is one of the exceptions.
A possible Nimrod. It has been suggested that Etana was one of the figures behind the biblical Nimrod, together with Enmerkar of Uruk and Narmer, the first pharaoh.
