archer hero of Greek myth, son of Apollo

Melaneus

Melaneus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo, who founded Oechalia and ruled as king of the Dryopes. He matters mainly for his fame as an archer, a skill inherited from his father, and for the stories that tie him to the cities of Epirus and Messenia.

The myth

According to the tradition, Apollo carried off Stratonice, a Calydonian princess and daughter of King Porthaon, so that she could marry his son Melaneus. Their children were Eurytus, a famous archer whose reputation overshadowed his father's, and Ambracia, who gave her name to the city of Ambracia in Epirus.

Pausanias tells how, when no descendant of Polycaon survived in Messenia, the Messenians called Perieres, son of Aeolus, to be their king. Melaneus arrived in the region, and Perieres assigned him a territory that took the name Oechalia, said to derive from the hero's wife. Antoninus Liberalis records another version, in which Apollo, Artemis and Heracles dispute the patronage of Ambracia, and Apollo claims the city as the inheritance of his son, who had conquered Epirus.

Ancient sources: The Metamorphoses of Antoninus Liberalis and Pausanias's Description of Greece.

Trivia

A region named after a wife. Pausanias explains that the territory where Perieres settled Melaneus was named Oechalia, said to derive from the hero's wife.
