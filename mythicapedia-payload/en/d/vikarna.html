Kaurava prince in the Mahabharata

Vikarna

Vikarna is one of the hundred Kaurava brothers, sons of King Dhritarashtra and Queen Gandhari, and the third most prominent after Duryodhana and Duhshasana. In the Hindu epic Mahabharata he is the brother who dares to denounce the humiliation of Draupadi, though in the end he dies fighting alongside his kin.

The myth

According to the epic, Vikarna was one of Dhritarashtra and Gandhari's hundred sons, rulers of the Kuru kingdom, and a student of Drona, in whose school he excelled at archery like Arjuna. His name can mean 'without ears' or 'large-eared', and readers have linked it to his character: stubbornness for some, a capacity to listen for others.

His best-known episode comes during the dice game. As his brothers and Karna celebrate the humiliation of Draupadi, wife of their cousins the Pandavas, Vikarna speaks out against the game and the treatment she receives. He echoes the questions Draupadi had already put to the elders of the realm, but no one answers him, not even Bhishma or Drona. Karna mocks him, and Vikarna responds by appealing to duty.

Despite his objections, Vikarna remains loyal to Duryodhana and fights in the Kurukshetra War, where he confronts Abhimanyu, Drupada and Shikhandi and covers his brothers' retreat. On the fourteenth day, Duryodhana sends him to stop Bhima, who had sworn to kill Dhritarashtra's sons. Bhima recognises him as a man of dharma and urges him to step aside; Vikarna answers that he knows the Kauravas will not win, yet he cannot abandon his king. Bhima reminds him of the dice game and kills him swiftly; in some versions, Vikarna asks Bhima to perform his funeral rites.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata epic, which recounts his role in the dice game and the Kurukshetra War; medieval commentaries by Madhvacharya offer a later reading of his protest.

Trivia

A brother who opposes. Yuyutsu, another Kaurava, defected to the Pandavas at the start of the Kurukshetra War. Vikarna is often contrasted with him for choosing a different path.
A parallel with the Ramayana. Vikarna is compared with Kumbhakarna, brother of the demon Ravana in the Ramayana. Both acknowledge that their elder brothers act against dharma, yet remain loyal to them.
