Hindu creator god, father of Sati

Daksha

Daksha is an ancient creator god in Hinduism, one of the Prajapatis, the agents of creation, and in later texts a son of the god Brahma. His name means 'skilful', and he is best known for the great sacrifice he staged, which ended with the death of his daughter Sati and the intervention of Shiva.

The myth

In the Rigveda, Daksha appears as one of the Adityas, sons of the goddess Aditi, and is associated with the skill of priests in sacrifice. Over time the Puranas, composed roughly between the 3rd and 10th centuries CE, made him a son of Brahma, born from the god's right thumb according to the Matsya Purana, and the progenitor of many creatures. According to the Bhagavata Purana, he is born twice: first as a mind-born son of Brahma, married to Prasuti, and later as a son of Prachetas, married to Asikni. The number of his daughters varies by text, given as 24, 27 or 60.

One well-known episode is the curse on the Moon. In the story, the moon god Chandra favoured Rohini over his other twenty-six wives, all of them daughters of Daksha. Daksha tried to persuade him in vain and then cursed him to lose his brightness and fall ill, and the vegetation began to die. The devas calmed the father, and the curse became a cycle: the Moon sickens each fortnight and slowly recovers, which explains its waning and waxing.

The most famous story is that of his daughter Dakshaiani, later known as Sati. She wished to marry Shiva, and Daksha forbade it at first; when he finally consented, the relationship grew cold. Daksha staged a great horse sacrifice to which he did not invite the couple, and at the entrance he placed a statue of Shiva that he mocked. Sati came anyway, and when her father ridiculed her husband, she threw herself into the sacrificial fire.

On hearing the news, the enraged Shiva tore a lock of his hair and struck it against the ground, and Virabhadra emerged from it; in other versions Bhadrakali also appeared. His hordes destroyed the sacrifice, and Virabhadra beheaded Daksha. The gods begged for mercy, and Shiva restored Daksha's life with a goat's head. Repentant, Daksha became a follower of the god. Shiva then carried the pieces of Sati's body on his trident, and the places where they fell became shrines. He later married Parvati, who was regarded as Sati's reincarnation.

Worship and religion

According to the Sthala-purana, a popular local tradition, the sacrifice took place where the Kottiyur temple stands today, in Kannur district, Kerala. Another tradition places it at Kankhal, in Uttarakhand, where the Daksheswara Mahadev temple, built in 1810 by Queen Dhankaur and rebuilt in 1962, honours Shiva under a name meaning 'the great god who is the lord of Daksha'. Both sites are tied to his story, but the sources do not describe current rites dedicated to Daksha.

Legacy

The sacrifice episode is, according to these traditions, a turning point in the formation of several Hindu sects: it explains how Parvati took Sati's place beside Shiva and leads on to the stories of Ganesha and Kartikeya. His daughter Rati, goddess of love, is also considered his child.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda as an Aditya; the Taittiriya Samhita of the Yajur Veda records elements of his sacrifice; the Mahabharata and the Ramayana mention him, and most of his stories are found in the Puranas, such as the Bhagavata, Linga and Vishnu Puranas.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Daksha means 'able', 'expert' or 'honest'. According to the Bhagavata Purana, he got the name because he was skilled at begetting children.
Two births. In the Bhagavata Purana he is born twice: as a mind-born son of Brahma and, in another age of the world, as a son of Prachetas.
