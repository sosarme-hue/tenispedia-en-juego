king of Magadha, enemy of Krishna

Jarasandha

Jarasandha is a legendary king of Magadha in Hindu tradition, remembered as a powerful enemy of Krishna and the Pandavas. His story, of a child born from two joined halves of a body, is told in the Mahabharata and related texts.

The myth

His father, King Brihadratha, split a magic fruit between his twin wives, and each gave birth to half a child. The rakshasi Jara pressed the two halves together, the infant came to life, and the king named him Jarasandha, 'joined by Jara'.

As an adult he extended his power and married his daughters to Kamsa, ruler of Mathura. After Krishna killed Kamsa, Jarasandha attacked Mathura again and again without success. Planning to sacrifice a hundred kings to gain power over the Yadavas, he had already captured ninety-five, and Krishna urged the Pandavas to end his reign.

In a wrestling duel, Bhima defeated him by following Krishna's hint: tearing his body along the seam where its halves had been joined. He came back together once, until Bhima split him again and flung the pieces far apart.

Worship and religion

Jarasandha was a devotee of Shiva, whom he worshipped daily and for whom he planned a fire sacrifice. The texts portray him as generous to brahmins, granting them whatever they asked after his prayers.

Legacy

Some tribes in India claim descent from him and add the suffix Yoria, meaning 'piece of flesh', to their children's names. He also appears in Jain tradition as the ninth pratinarayana.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata presents him as king of Magadha and son of Brihadratha, the Rig Veda mentions Brihadratha, and the Jain Harivamsa Purana lists him as the ninth pratinarayana.

Trivia

A name with a story. His name means 'joined by Yara', after the cannibal demoness who found him. According to the Vishnu Purana, Yara was a daughter of Mritu, Death.
A blade of grass. Krishna settled the fight with a simple gesture: he split a blade of grass lengthwise and cast the halves apart, showing Bhima how to defeat the king.
