creature of European folklore that replaces a human infant

Changeling

The changeling is a creature of European folklore that takes the place of a kidnapped human child, leaving a supernatural being disguised as a baby in its cradle. It is one of the most widespread themes in popular tradition, and many scholars link it to attempts to explain births of children with disabilities or developmental conditions.

The myth

According to popular belief, fairies, elves, trolls and other fantastic beings stole babies, and sometimes newly married adults or new mothers, leaving a substitute behind. The substitute could be a supernatural being in disguise or, at times, an enchanted log that seemed to sicken and die. The motives attributed to the thieves included a wish for a human servant, love for children, or simple malice.

The false child usually gave itself away through its strange appearance, insatiable appetite, unnatural wisdom or behaviour it showed when it thought it was alone. In Scandinavian lore, unbaptised children were considered especially vulnerable, and parents placed scissors or knives over the cradle. To recover the true child, people resorted to cruel methods such as whipping or heating the substitute, though in one Swedish tale the mother's kindness breaks the spell. In German tradition, the trick was to brew beer or cook in eggshells, prompting the creature to speak and reveal its great age.

Legacy

The theme inspired works such as Roger Zelazny's The Changeling (1980), H. P. Lovecraft's Pickman's Model and White Wolf's role-playing game Changeling: The Dreaming. It also appears in films and series such as Hellboy (2019), Supernatural, Merlin and Outlander.
