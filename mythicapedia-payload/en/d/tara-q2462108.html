Hindu goddess of wisdom, protection and healing

Tara

Tara is a goddess of Hinduism, often seen as a form of Durga or Parvati, and one of the ten Mahavidyas, the great wisdom goddesses. Her name means star in Sanskrit, and devotees invoke her for wisdom, protection and healing.

The myth

According to tradition, Tara saved the god Shiva from the poison he drank to protect the world. The goddess turned him into a baby, held him in her arms and nursed him, and her milk neutralised the poison.

The Rudrayamala tells a different origin. The sage Vasistha tried to worship her unsuccessfully, then met Vishnu in the form of the Buddha in Mahacina (the region of Tibet) and won her favour through kaula rites. The story is used to explain the links between Shaiva, Shakta and Buddhist traditions around her figure.

She is shown with blue skin, a blue lotus, a pair of scissors and a tiger-skin skirt. She looks very like Kali, although Kali is black, and the two are often mistaken for each other.

Worship and religion

Her most famous centre of worship is the temple of Tarapith in West Bengal, beside a cremation ground. Tantric scriptures describe her mantras, including the tara-gayatri and the mantra linked to her healing power, and several of her forms, such as Ugratara and Nila Saraswati, whom tradition associates with protection from danger. Her worship was long secretive and tantric, until in Bengal it gained a public devotional dimension.

Legacy

Her figure passed into Tibetan Buddhism, where by around the 7th century she became a female Buddha. In Bengal, the songs of the poet Ramprasad Sen address her as a daughter, and the tantric saint Bamakhepa was one of her best-known devotees in modern times.

Ancient sources: Tantric texts and Puranas such as the Rudrayamala, the Brihannilatantra, the Kalika Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana, which mention her.

Trivia

A star in Sanskrit. Her name, tara, means star in Sanskrit and is written तारा in Devanagari.
Mistaken for Kali. Tara and Kali share features such as the necklace of skulls and the pose over a corpse, so they are easy to confuse.
