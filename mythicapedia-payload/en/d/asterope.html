Greek nymph, one of the Hesperides

Asterope

Asterope is one of the Hesperides, the Greek nymphs who tend Hera's garden of golden apples. Her name appears on the Meidias Hydria, an Athenian vase, but she has no story of her own in the literary sources.

The myth

According to the tradition, the Hesperides live in a garden at the far western edge of the world, near the Atlas Mountains and the ocean that encircles the earth. The orchard was a wedding gift from Gaia to Hera, who asked to have it planted in her gardens. The nymphs tended it alongside Ladon, a hundred-headed dragon who never slept.

As his eleventh labour, Heracles travelled there to steal some of the apples. Asterope appears in the myth only as one of the figures who accompany that story.

Legacy

The Hesperides gave their name to Hesperus, the evening star, and share a root with the English word "west". Greeks named citrus species "hesperidoids", and the modern Greek word for orange, portokáli, recalls Portugal.

Ancient sources: Her name appears on the Meidias Hydria, an Athenian red-figure vase from the 5th century BCE, but not in the ancient literary texts, which give different lists of names, such as those of Apollonius of Rhodes, Apollodorus and Hesiod.

Trivia

A vase with names. On the Meidias Hydria, the figures in the garden are labelled with their names, among them Asterope, Chrysothemis, Hygieia and Lipara.
Apples that might have been oranges. In later centuries it was suggested that the "golden apples" might have been oranges, a fruit unknown in Europe and the Mediterranean before the Middle Ages.
