Theban cobra goddess of the necropolis

Meretseger

Meretseger, whose name means "She Who Loves Silence", was a cobra goddess in ancient Egyptian religion tied to the Theban necropolis and above all the Valley of the Kings. She matters because she guarded royal tombs, punished those who desecrated them, and could still forgive the repentant. Tradition placed her in the West, the realm of the afterlife, and linked her to justice, medicine and protection against snakebites. Myth: According to the sources, Meretseger was the companion of the buried Osiris and embodied the necropolises dedicated to him. She was identified with the peak of El Qurn, a natural summit of 420 metres overlooking the Valley of the Kings, which some Egyptologists think may have influenced the choice of that site for royal burials. The goddess was believed to spit venom at anyone who tried to destroy or rob royal tombs, and to punish with her bite those who committed sacrilege, such as stealing copper or breaking an oath. Yet she also mercifully forgave those who repented. A draftsman named Neferabu, according to a limestone stela now in Turin, claimed he regained his sight after pleading with her. Cult: Meretseger was patron of the artisans and workers of Deir el-Medina, who built and decorated the royal tombs. She was worshipped alongside Ptah in a small rock shrine near the village, and many stelae with prayers and requests for forgiveness survive. Her cult peaked in the Ramesside period and declined after the 21st Dynasty, when the Valley of the Kings fell out of use. No living cult remains. Legacy: Her image appears in royal tombs such as those of Ramesses VI and Queen Twosret, and on numerous stelae held in museums including the Louvre and the Egyptian Museum in Cairo.
