Chinese Taoist immortal, one of the Eight Immortals

He Xiangu

He Xiangu, born He Qiong, is one of the Eight Immortals of Chinese Taoism and the only woman in the usual group, although Lan Caihe is sometimes portrayed as a woman too. Tradition places her in the Tang dynasty, and she is remembered as a model of immortality reached through asceticism and devotion.

The myth

According to tradition, He Xiangu was the daughter of He Tai, a man from Zengcheng in Guangdong, though other versions place her in Lingling, Hunan. At birth she had six long hairs on the crown of her head, a detail the stories often mention. Around the age of fourteen or fifteen, a divine figure appeared to her in a dream and told her to eat powdered mica so that her body would become ethereal and immune to death. She obeyed, vowed to remain a virgin, and gradually stopped eating ordinary food. She spent her days drifting through the valleys as if she had wings, and at sunset she came home bringing mountain fruit for her mother.

Empress Wu Zetian sent a messenger to bring her to court, but she vanished on the way. According to the legend, she ascended to Heaven in broad daylight during the Jinglong era (707-710), in the reign of Emperor Zhongzong, and so became an immortal.

Worship and religion

Her main attribute is the lotus flower, which is believed to improve physical and mental health. She is sometimes shown with a sheng, a reed instrument similar to a panpipe, with a fenghuang, or with a wooden ladle or fly-whisk.

Legacy

In the animated series Jackie Chan Adventures she is the immortal who seals away Tso Lan, the Moon Demon. In the song cycle album Eight Immortals, written by Sam Tsui and Casey Breeves, she appears as a social media influencer addicted to crushed Tahitian moonstone supplements.

Ancient sources: The Xian Fo Qi Zong and Zeng Minxing's Duxing from the Song dynasty record her story and her prophecies.

Trivia

A successful prophecy. According to Duxing, written by Zeng Minxing in the Song dynasty, General Di Qing consulted He Xiangu before suppressing a rebellion. She predicted that the enemy would flee before any battle took place, and that is what happened.
Six hairs on the crown. At birth she had six long hairs on the crown of her head, a detail the stories highlight among the particulars of her childhood.
