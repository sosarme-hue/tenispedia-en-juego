female spirit of South and Southeast Asian folklore

Churel

The churel is a female spirit of South and Southeast Asian folklore, the ghost of a woman who died in cruel, unnatural or impure circumstances. She is feared for seducing men and draining their life-force, and she remains a familiar figure in regional popular culture.

The myth

According to tradition, a woman who dies a cruel or unnatural death may return as a churel to take revenge, especially on the men of her own family. Her true form is hideous, with her feet turned backwards, but she can shape-shift into a beautiful young woman carrying a lantern, luring men into woods or mountains.

There she drains their blood or vital force until they are withered into old men. In some tales, the victim is trapped in her lair in the graveyard. Those who see her may fall ill with a deadly disease, and her reversed footprints lead inevitably into her grasp anyone who flees.

Legacy

The churel remains present in literature, cinema, television and radio in India and elsewhere in the region, where she is often mentioned. In India and Pakistan the word is also used colloquially, sometimes mistakenly, for a witch.
