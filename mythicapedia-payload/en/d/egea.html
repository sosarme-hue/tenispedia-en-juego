queen of the Amazons in Greek myth

Egea

Egea is, according to legend, a queen of the Amazons in Greek mythology. Her name is linked to the Aegean Sea, for which she is cited as an alternative eponym, although her figure is little known.

The myth

According to the tale, Egea commanded an army of women warriors who set out from Libya for Asia Minor with the aim of fighting at Troy. During the voyage, she and her troops perished at sea after fighting bravely.

The link to the Aegean Sea is what has given the name its fame. An encyclopedia of 1911 mentions her as an alternative eponym of that sea, which Greek tradition more often attributes to a figure named Aegeus.

Legacy

Modern Italian has the adjective egea meaning relating to the Aegean Sea, and botanical Latin sometimes uses the epithet aegea with the same sense. Her mark on language is more visible than in art or literature.

Trivia

An Italian adjective. Modern Italian keeps the adjective egea, meaning 'Aegean'. Classical Latin had no equivalent.
A botanical epithet. In modern botanical Latin, the specific epithet aegea is sometimes used to mean 'of the Aegean'.
