primordial Egyptian goddess of Heliopolis

Iusaaset

Iusaaset, also known as Iusaas or, in Greek, Saosis, is a primordial goddess in Ancient Egyptian religion and the feminine counterpart of the creator god Atum. She was worshipped in Iunu, the Greek Heliopolis, where her sanctuary was surrounded by acacias, a tree she was associated with.

The myth

According to the Heliopolitan tradition, creation began when Atum produced the gods Shu and Tefnut through masturbation, or by copulating with himself, depending on the version, starting the process of creation. The hand he used in this act was personified as a goddess, the Hand of Atum.

In these accounts Iusaaset is linked with two more minor goddesses, Hathor and Nebethetepet, and was equated with them at times. The earliest texts seem to treat Iusaaset and Nebethetepet as two names for one goddess, but after the Middle Kingdom (c. 2000–1700 BC) they were regarded as separate, if similar, deities. She was depicted as a woman with a scarab beetle on her head.

Worship and religion

Iusaaset was worshipped at Iunu alongside Atum, and her sanctuary in Heliopolis was known for the acacias that stood there.

Legacy

Egyptologist Stephen Quirke suggests that Iusaaset and Nebethetepet represented two aspects of creation: growth for the former and abundance for the latter, based on the meanings of their names, roughly "she who grows as she comes" and "lady of the field of offerings".
