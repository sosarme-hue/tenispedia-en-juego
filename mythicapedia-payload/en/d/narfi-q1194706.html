son of Loki in Norse mythology

Narfi

Narfi, also called Nari, is a son of Loki and Sigyn in Norse mythology. He is best known for being killed by his brother Váli, who had been transformed into a wolf.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the Æsir punished Loki for his crimes by turning his son Váli into a wolf. Váli then tore apart his brother, "Nari or Narfi", and his entrails were used to bind their father.

The prose ending of the poem Lokasenna tells a similar story, but there Narfi himself becomes the wolf, Váli is not mentioned, and the connection between events is left unexplained. The sources do not fully agree, and it is unclear whether Nari and Narfi are the same figure. Ynglingatal and Haustlöng also name Hel as the sister of the wolf and of Narfi, and the name appears as the father of Nótt, the personification of night.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the prose ending of the Lokasenna, the poem Ynglingatal and the Haustlöng.

Trivia

Changes in modern editions. Some modern editions, such as Guðni Jónsson's 1954 edition and Henry Adams Bellows's 1923 English translation, have replaced the name Narfi with Váli to match the account in the Prose Edda.
A possible origin of the name. Rudolf Simek suggests that Narfi may originally have been a demon of the dead, and that his name could be related to the Old Norse word nár, "corpse".
