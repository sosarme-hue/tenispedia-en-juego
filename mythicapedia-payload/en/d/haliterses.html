Ithacan seer in the Odyssey

Halitherses

Halitherses is an Ithacan seer, son of Mastor, who appears in Homer's Odyssey. He tried to warn Penelope's suitors that their behavior would bring them ruin.

The myth

In the Odyssey, Halitherses reads the omens sent by Zeus, which urged the suitors to end their wickedness before Odysseus returned. The suitors ignored him, and all of them died at the hero's hands.

Afterwards, the seer warned the families of the dead that they would bring evil upon themselves if they took revenge on Odysseus, since this went against the will of the gods. Only half of them listened. The families nonetheless tried to kill Odysseus's household, but the goddess Athena stopped them.

Halitherses, like Mentor, had been a dear friend of Odysseus in Ithaca, and both had tried in vain to restrain the suitors. Penelope, meanwhile, remained faithful to her husband.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey, where he appears as an Ithacan seer.
