Greek hero who gave his name to Mount Parnassus

Parnassus

Parnassus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Poseidon and the nymph Cleodora in one version of the story, and he gave his name to the mountain range stretching between the lands of the Dorians and the Phocians. He matters mainly because of the mountain's link to Delphi and the Muses.

The myth

According to tradition, Parnassus gave his name to the mountain range that separates Dorian territory from that of the Phocians. He is credited with founding the oracle of Delphi, which Apollo would later take over.

The Muses made their home on the slopes of the mountain and came when Apollo summoned them from Mount Helicon. Accounts of his parentage disagree: some make him a son of Poseidon, others name Cleopompus as his father, while his mother remains the nymph Cleodora.

Worship and religion

On the southern slope of Parnassus lay the Castalian Spring and the Panhellenic sanctuary of Delphi, dedicated to Apollo, where the famous oracle was consulted. It was a meeting place for Greeks from many different cities.

Legacy

Through its link with the Muses, Parnassus is considered the symbolic homeland of poets. Its name has been used to title anthologies of prestigious literary and artistic writers.

Trivia

A pre-Greek name. The name seems to come from a pre-Greek substrate, based on its suffix *-ssos, common in place names such as Knossos, Korinthos and Zakynthos.
A Hittite root. For the root *Parna- a connection has been proposed with the Hittite word meaning "house".
