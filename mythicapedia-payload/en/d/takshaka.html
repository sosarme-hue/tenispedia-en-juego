king of the nagas in Hindu mythology

Takshaka

Takshaka is a king of the nagas, serpent beings found in Hindu and Buddhist traditions. He is best known from the Mahabharata and for causing the death of King Parikshit, grandson of Arjuna.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Takshaka lived in the Khandava forest near Kurukshetra and was a friend of the god Indra. When Arjuna burned that forest at the command of Agni, Takshaka was away, but his son Ashvasena was there and lost his mother in the fire. Ashvasena escaped and later attacked Arjuna during the Kurukshetra War to avenge her.

The main revenge, however, falls on Arjuna's family. A sage's son cursed King Parikshit to die from a snake bite, and Takshaka fulfilled the curse by approaching him in disguise and biting him while he meditated on Vishnu. He also bribed a priest skilled in curing snake poison so that no one could save the king.

Parikshit's son Janamejaya waged war on the nagas at Takshashila and nearly wiped them out. Takshaka fled to the realm of Indra but was captured. He was saved by the young sage Astika, the son of a Brahmin father and a Naga mother, who persuaded the king to end the massacre. According to the Bhagavata Purana, Takshaka belonged to the Ikshvaku dynasty and was a descendant of Rama.

Legacy

In Chinese and Japanese traditions, Takshaka is counted among the "eight Great Dragon Kings" (八大龍王), and he is described as the only snake able to fly and the most venomous of all. In Sanskrit and Hindi, his name means something like "gliding snake."

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana, Hindu texts that recount his story.

Trivia

Flying serpent. Takshaka is described as the only snake able to fly among the great dragon kings of Chinese and Japanese tradition.
A disguised thief. In another episode, Takshaka disguises himself as a beggar and steals the earrings of the queen of Paushya, which she had given to a Brahmin named Uttanka.
