ancient Roman goddess of honey

Mellona

Mellona, also called Mellonia, was an ancient Roman goddess who watched over honey, bees and beekeeping. She was regarded as a rural deity protecting those whose livelihood depended on honey.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Mellona was the guardian goddess of honey, the divinity who looked after the sweetness produced by bees. She appears alongside other rural deities such as Segetia, who protected the crops, Bubona, who guarded cattle, and Pomona, goddess of fruit.

Arnobius describes her as a goddess of great importance and power regarding bees, caring for and protecting the sweetness of honey. In his account, she avenged those who stole honey or attacked their hives, shielding those who relied on beekeeping for their living.

Worship and religion

Mellona was included in the indigitamenta, the list of deities kept by Roman priests so that the correct divinity would be invoked in rituals.

Legacy

Her name is linked to the Latin word mel, mellis, the root of the English word honey's classical relatives, a connection often noted by etymologists.

Ancient sources: Arnobius describes her in relation to bees, and St. Augustine says she promoted the supply of honey, as Pomona did for apples.

Trivia

Companion of Pomona. St. Augustine places her alongside Pomona, goddess of apples, as a deity who promoted the supply of their respective products.
