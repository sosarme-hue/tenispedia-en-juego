Eurydice of Troy

In Greek mythology, Eurydice is the queen of Troy as the wife of Ilus, founder of Ilium. She matters because she is the mother of King Laomedon, placing her in the royal line of Troy.

The myth

According to tradition, Eurydice was the daughter of Adrastus and married Ilus, founder of the city of Ilium. Their son was King Laomedon, and some sources also name Themiste, Telecleia and Tithonus as her possible children.

The sources disagree about Ilus's wife. Apollodorus mentions Batia, daughter of Teucer, as a possible consort, but his family tree makes her Ilus's great-grandmother, so she could hardly have been his wife. Hyginus instead calls the wife Leucippe, a name found nowhere else.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus places her in the family tree of the royal house of Troy, and Hyginus gives the name Leucippe for Ilus's wife.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Her Greek name, Eurydikē, is usually rendered as 'wide justice', combining eurys, 'wide', and dike, 'justice'.
