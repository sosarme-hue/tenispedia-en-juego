Roman goddess of concord and harmony

Concordia

Concordia is the Roman goddess of agreement, understanding and harmony, especially within marriage. Her cult and image were linked to the stability of society and, in particular, to the imperial family.

The myth

Unlike many other deities, Concordia has few narratives of her own in Roman tradition; her role is more conceptual and political than storytelling. Her Greek equivalent is Harmonia, and her opposite is Discordia, called Eris in Greek.

According to the iconographic and literary tradition, she was often grouped with other goddesses representing peace and security, such as Pax, Salus, Securitas or Fortuna. In some images she also appears alongside Hercules and Mercury, who stood for security and luck respectively.

Worship and religion

The cult of Concordia was important in Rome. Her oldest temple, built in 367 BC by Marcus Furius Camillus, stood in the Roman Forum, and other shrines grew up nearby, including a bronze aedicula of 304 BC and a temple on the Arx. Under the Empire she became part of the imperial cult as Concordia Augusta, and dedicatory inscriptions were made to her on behalf of emperors and members of the imperial family. In Pompeii, the high priestess Eumachia dedicated a building to her.

Legacy

Her image appears on imperial coins, such as those issued by Marcus Aurelius and Lucius Verus. The head of the goddess on a denarius of 62 BC was the model for the obverse of Italy's 1970 commemorative 1,000-lire coin, Roma Capitale. The asteroid 58 Concordia is named after her. In modern Discordianism, the Harmonians identify her with Aneris.
