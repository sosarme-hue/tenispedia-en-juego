Japanese bodhisattva of mercy

Kannon

Kannon is the Japanese name of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, the Buddhist embodiment of compassion. Devotion to him is among the most widespread and beloved in East Asia.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, Avalokiteśvara is a bodhisattva who hears the cries of suffering beings and comes to their aid. The Lotus Sutra devotes a chapter to him and credits him with the power to take any form, divine, human or animal, in order to teach the Dharma.

In China, where he has been depicted as a woman since about the 12th century, he is called Guanyin; in Japan, Kannon. Believers hold that he places the dead in a lotus and guides them to the western Pure Land of Sukhāvatī.

Worship and religion

Among Japan's best-known temples are Sensō-ji and Kiyomizu-dera, and the Saigoku pilgrimage through 33 temples is the oldest in the country. The cult remains alive; Tibetan Buddhism venerates him as Chenrézik.

Legacy

The camera maker Canon takes its name from this divinity.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra and the Kāraṇḍavyūha Sūtra, Indian Buddhist texts.

Trivia

A home in Japan. According to tradition, Kannon's Japanese abode is Fudarakusan-ji, while in Tibet it is the Potala Palace.
Local names. In Vietnamese, Korean, Khmer or Thai she has local names, almost always derived from Guanyin.
