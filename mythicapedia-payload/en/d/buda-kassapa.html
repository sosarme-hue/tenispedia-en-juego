Buddha of the present kalpa, before Gautama

Kassapa Buddha

Kassapa is one of the ancient Buddhas of Theravada Buddhism, counted as the third of the five Buddhas of the present kalpa and the immediate predecessor of Gautama among the Seven Buddhas of Antiquity. His story is set in India, around Varanasi.

The myth

Tradition holds that Kassapa was born into a Brahmin family, the son of Brahmadatta and Dhanavatī. His body measured twenty cubits, and he lived two thousand years across three palaces: Hamsa, Yasa and Sirinanda. Married to Sunandā, he had a son, Vijitasena.

When he resolved to leave worldly life, his palace rose into the sky with its inhabitants and came down with a bodhi tree at its centre, where Kassapa and his household renounced the world. After seven days of austerities he accepted a milk-rice meal from Sunandā and a seat of grass from the grass-cutter Soma. He attained enlightenment beneath a banyan and gave his first sermon at Isipatana, present-day Sarnath. Among his conversions was that of the yaksha Nāradeva. According to the tradition, he died in Kashi at the age of sixteen thousand years, and a great golden stupa was raised over his relics.

Worship and religion

The sources describe the great stupa at Kashi as a place of devotion. To complete it, the devotee Sorata, a non-returner, is said to have travelled collecting funds, and was murdered by robbers in a forest later called Andhavana. Other accounts mention offerings of perfumes, flowers and butter at the stupa. Devotion to Kassapa remains part of Theravada Buddhism.

Ancient sources: His story appears in the Pali Canon's Buddhavamsa and in commentaries such as the Jataka tales and the Dhammapada commentary.

Trivia

Tree of awakening. Kassapa attained enlightenment under a banyan, which the sources name as his bodhi tree.
The Kashi stupa. Each brick of his stupa is said to have been worth one crore, about ten million rupees.
