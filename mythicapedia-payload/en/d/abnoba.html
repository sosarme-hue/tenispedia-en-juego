Celtic goddess of the Black Forest

Abnoba

Abnoba was a Celtic goddess linked to the Black Forest and the mountains of southwestern Germany. Her name also designated a mountain range, and the Romans identified her with Diana.

The myth

Ancient texts hardly preserve any stories about Abnoba, so she is known mainly through inscriptions and through what Roman authors wrote about the place that bears her name.

According to Tacitus, Abnoba was the name of a mountain from whose grassy slopes the source of the Danube flowed. Pliny the Elder placed that source opposite the town of Rauricum, in Gaul, and claimed the river rises in the Alps, which is not accurate. Modern scholars think Pliny confused the Rhine and its tributaries with the Danube. Ptolemy also placed the range in the wrong position, probably because of imperfect sources, and ended up associating it with the Taunus mountains.

Worship and religion

Her cult is attested by about nine inscriptions, including an altar at the Roman baths of Badenweiler and another at Mühlenbach. In them she appears identified with Diana, the Roman goddess of the hunt, through what the Romans called interpretatio romana, the habit of matching the gods of conquered peoples with their own. The result was the Gallo-Roman goddess Diana Abnoba, to whom votive stones and statues have been dedicated in several places in the Black Forest. The sources do not say whether her worship survived beyond the Roman period.

Legacy

Her name survives in the range stretching from the Rhine to the Neckar, made up of the Odenwald, the Spessart and the Baar mountains, though it takes different names depending on the region. Sources also record variants such as Arnoba or Arbona.

Ancient sources: Tacitus, Germania; Pliny the Elder, Natural History (4.79); Ptolemy, Geography (2.10).

Trivia

Two rivers, two Celtic names. The Danube begins with two small rivers draining the Black Forest, the Breg and the Brigach, both with Celtic names. The longer one, the Breg, is considered the most likely candidate for the source the ancient authors described.
A mapping error. Pliny placed the Danube's origin in the Alps, and Ptolemy put the Abnoba range north of what are now the Taunus mountains. Both errors are attributed to confused or imperfect sources.
