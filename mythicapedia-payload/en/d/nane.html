Armenian goddess of war and wisdom

Nane

Nane is a mother goddess of Armenian mythology, associated with war and wisdom. She was identified with the Hellenic Athena in the Hellenistic period, and her name has survived in Armenia as a personal name and as an affectionate term for grandmother.

The myth

According to Armenian tradition, Nane was a daughter of Aramazd and a sister of Mher, Anahit and Astghik. She was depicted as a beautiful young woman dressed as a warrior, with spear and shield in hand, much like Athena, with whom she was identified in the Hellenistic period.

The surviving sources say little about her. Many artifacts, books and stories were destroyed during the Christianization of the country, so much of her story is unknown to modern scholars.

Worship and religion

Her cult was closely linked to that of the goddess Anahit. She had a temple in the town of Thil, across the Lycus River, which was demolished during the Christianization of Armenia. Tradition holds that in ancient Armenia kings met with the oldest woman of their dynasty, who was seen as an embodiment of Nane.

Legacy

In Armenia and in other countries the name Nane is used as a personal name and also as an affectionate nickname for the grandmother of the household, with variants such as Nanna, Nani or Nannan.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources on Nane are scarce; the reference text records the destruction of her temple at Thil, as described by chroniclers of the Christianization of Armenia.

Trivia

A goddess with a grandmother's name. In Armenia, Nane lives on as an affectionate term for the grandmother of the household.
Identified with Athena. In the Hellenistic period she was identified with the Greek Athena, sharing her armed image with spear and shield.
