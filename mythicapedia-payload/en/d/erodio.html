son of Autonous in Greek myth

Erodius

Erodius is a son of Autonous in Greek myth, a figure who appears in a brief tragic tale and ends up transformed into a heron. His story stands out because it mixes a family disaster with wordplay between the names of birds and plants.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Antoninus Liberalis, Autonous owned many horses but neglected his land, which produced nothing but rushes and thistles. For that reason he named four of his children after plants, and Erodius after what the land had done.

One day, his brother Anthus led the family's mares away from the meadows. The mares went mad, attacked him, and devoured him while the family watched, helpless. Zeus and Apollo took pity on them and turned the whole family into birds whose names matched the names they had been given. Erodius became a heron, as did the family's manservant, though of a different kind. The text says he was smaller than the servant and avoided the anthos, the bird Anthus became.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis, a second-century author, is the only source that preserves the myth.

Trivia

A game of names. The family is given the names of birds and plants, and the myth depends on Greek wordplay linking the two.
A lost text. The original manuscript of the tale is lost and has doubtful passages, which makes it hard to analyse.
Possible theatrical origin. Several scholars suggest the myth comes from Anthos, a lost play by Agathon.
