queen of the Amazons in the Alexander Romance

Thalestris

Thalestris is, in the Alexander Romance, the queen of the Amazons who came to the Macedonian king accompanied by three hundred women warriors. Her story, of Greek origin, imagines an encounter between the Amazon world and the most famous conqueror of antiquity.

The myth

According to the tale, Thalestris presented herself before Alexander the Great at the head of 300 women with a clear purpose: to bear children as strong and intelligent as the king. Tradition says she stayed with him for thirteen days and nights, hoping the great warrior would give her a daughter.

The story, however, is not accepted by everyone. Plutarch, who wrote about Alexander's life centuries later, mentions it precisely to cast doubt on it. According to his account, Onesicritus, the second-rank naval commander, read this passage to Lysimachus of Thrace, who had taken part in the original expedition. Lysimachus smiled and asked where he had been at the time, a retort that makes clear the suspicion that the episode was an invention.

Legacy

The episode has become an example of the Amazon tales that blend heroism and legend around Alexander. Plutarch's anecdote is often cited as evidence of the skepticism that ancient writers themselves felt toward such stories.

Ancient sources: The Alexander Romance, a Greek work of legendary character, and Plutarch's Life of Alexander, which disputes it.

Trivia

Thirteen days and nights. According to the legend, Thalestris stayed with Alexander for thirteen days and nights, a period as precise as her wish to have a daughter.
Lysimachus's retort. Plutarch records that Lysimachus, hearing the passage, smiled and asked where he had been at the time, a jab at the story's truthfulness.
