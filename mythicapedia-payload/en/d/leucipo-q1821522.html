Leucippus

Leucippus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of King Oenomaus of Pisa, known for his love story with the nymph Daphne, in which a disguise allowed him to share the young woman's life and passion for hunting for a time, until the deception was revealed.

The myth

According to the tale, Leucippus fell passionately in love with the nymph Daphne, who cherished the freedom of the forests and rejected any talk of marriage. To get close to her, he let his hair grow, claimed he would offer it as a sacrifice to the god Alpheus, and dressed as a woman. In this guise he presented himself to her and asked to join her hunts. She agreed, and over time the two became inseparable friends.

Apollo, who also loved Daphne, grew jealous of their closeness. He intensified the sun's rays so that the group of huntresses would long to bathe in the river Ladon. When Leucippus was forced to undress, his deception was exposed, and Daphne's companions killed him with arrows or stabs.
