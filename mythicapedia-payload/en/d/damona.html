Celtic goddess of healing waters

Damona

Damona was a Celtic goddess worshipped in Roman Gaul as a healing deity of the thermal waters, mainly in what is now Burgundy. She appears as the consort of the spring-god Borvo and of other gods, but she also received worship on her own.

The myth

No myth about Damona survives. No ancient author mentions her, and she is known only from Latin votive inscriptions of the Roman period. What we can glean comes from her divine partners and her attributes, which suggest a goddess linked to health and fertility.

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on healing sanctuaries attached to thermal and mineral springs, where the sick bathed in hope of a cure. At Bourbon-Lancy and Bourbonne-les-Bains she was honoured alongside Borvo, and at Alesia alongside Apollo Moritasgus, in a precinct with sacred springs and baths. One modern interpretation links a dedication at Bourbon-Lancy to incubation, a ritual sleep in which a pilgrim sought a healing dream.

Ancient sources: Only Latin votive inscriptions of the Roman period; no ancient author mentions her.

Trivia

A head crowned with ears of corn. At Alesia a fragment of Damona's cult statue was found: a carved stone head crowned with ears of corn and a left hand coiled by a serpent. Both attributes link her to healing and fertility.
A name of bovine origin. Her name is usually linked to a Celtic stem *damo-, which in later languages designates bovids and cervids, although its exact meaning is debated among scholars.
