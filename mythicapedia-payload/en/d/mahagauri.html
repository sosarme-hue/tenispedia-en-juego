form of the goddess Mahadevi in Hinduism

Mahagauri

Mahagauri is a form of the Hindu goddess Mahadevi, venerated as the goddess of beauty and purity. She is the eighth of the Navadurga, the nine forms of Durga, and is worshipped on the eighth day of Navaratri, the great festival of the goddess.

The myth

According to tradition, Parvati once had a dark complexion. Shiva, to provoke her, kept calling her Kali, meaning black. Hurt, Parvati performed severe penance to obtain a fair complexion, and Brahma told her to bathe in the Ganges. As she entered the river, her dark skin fell away and the goddess Kaushiki emerged from it. Parvati became radiant once more, dressed in white, and so she received the name Mahagauri.

In the Devi Mahatmya, Kaushiki, born from that dark skin, goes to battle against the asuras Shumbha and Nishumbha, whom she finally defeats. In another version from the Shiva Purana, Parvati performed intense penance to win Shiva as her husband. The dust of the long years settled on her skin and darkened it; Shiva, pleased, promised to marry her, and the sacred waters of the Ganges washed away the dust and revealed her luminous complexion.

Worship and religion

She is shown riding a white bull, with four arms: in her upper hands she holds a trident and a damaru, her lower right hand makes the abhayamudra gesture, and her lower left hand holds a japamala or the varadamudra gesture. Her whiteness is compared to that of the conch, the moon and jasmine, and her white garments convey purity and calm.

Ancient sources: Devi Mahatmya, part of the Markandeya Purana, and the Shiva Purana.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Maha means great and Gauri means purity, which gives the goddess her epithet.
A comparison of whiteness. Her radiant complexion is compared to the conch shell, the moon and jasmine flowers.
