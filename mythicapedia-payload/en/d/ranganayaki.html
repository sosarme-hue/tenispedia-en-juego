Hindu goddess, consort of Ranganatha at Srirangam

Ranganayaki

Ranganayaki, also called Thayar, is the presiding goddess of the Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple in Srirangam. Consort of Ranganatha, a form of Vishnu, she is regarded as a manifestation of Lakshmi.

The myth

According to local tradition, when Malik Kafur of the Khalji dynasty invaded the temple in 1323 CE, the goddess's processional idol was buried under a bilva tree and a wall was built to protect it. After the plunder it could not be found, so a new one was made. Later a devotee dreamed of the goddess, who showed him where it lay; he dug it up and the idol was reinstalled.

Her processional idol never leaves her sanctum. Devotees carry Ranganatha to her instead, like a husband visiting his beloved, and for this she is called Paḍi Thānḍādha Pathni, "the wife who does not cross her threshold".

Worship and religion

Pilgrims are expected to visit her shrine right after seeing Ranganatha. Once a year, on Panguni Uttiram, their processional idols meet for a day in the sērti-sevai. Devotees chant Vedanta Desika's Sri Stuti and Adi Shankaracharya's Kanakadhara Stotram, and the Lakshmi Ashtothram is recited in rituals.

Trivia

Unusual hairstyle. At the Srirangam temple, Ranganayaki wears an unusual hairstyle: her hair is tied in a side-bun (kondai) positioned to her left.
No wedding festival. Because Ranganatha and Ranganayaki are regarded as a divine couple beyond mortal limits, this temple does not hold a Tirukalyāṇa-utsavam, or wedding festival.
