Persian hero, son of Rostam in the Shahnameh

Sohrab

Sohrab is a young warrior, son of the Persian hero Rostam and the princess Tahmina, and the protagonist of one of the most moving stories in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh. His tragedy, killed by his own father without either of them knowing the truth, makes him a symbol of courage and fated loss in Persian culture.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved by Ferdowsi in the Shahnameh, Rostam fathered a child with Tahmina, daughter of the king of Samangan, a neighbouring land, but the hero left before he ever met the boy. Sohrab grew into an exceptional warrior and, as an adult, reached the Iranian frontier determined to test himself against the greatest of the Persian champions. There he faced in single combat a knight he did not recognise: his own father, who was equally unaware of his opponent's identity.

In the duel, Rostam mortally wounded Sohrab. Only then, seeing the fatal injury, did the young man reveal that he was Rostam's son and show the bracelet his father had left with Tahmina as a sign. The story adds that King Kaykavus, fearing that the power of Rostam and his son might unite, delayed giving the magical remedy (Noush Daru) that could have saved him. Sohrab died, and Rostam was left in grief.

Legacy

The name remains popular in Persian-influenced cultures, from Turkey to India, and the legend of Rostam and Sohrab forms an important part of Persianate heritage. One of Pakistan's largest bicycle manufacturers, Sohrab Cycles, is named after him.

Ancient sources: The Shahnameh (Book of Kings), the Persian epic poem by Ferdowsi, where his story is told.

Trivia

Meaning of his name. His name is usually interpreted as "hot red water", related to the modern Persian sorkh-ab, though it can also mean "beautiful and shining face".
A bracelet as a sign. Rostam left Tahmina a bracelet as a reminder and a sign for their son, which Sohrab used to identify himself before he died.
