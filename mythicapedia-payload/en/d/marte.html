Roman god of war and agriculture

Mars

Mars is the Roman god of war, but he also watched over farming, a combination characteristic of early Rome. He was regarded as the father (pater) of the Roman people and, unlike the Greek Ares, he represented military power as a way to secure peace.

The myth

According to tradition, Mars was the son of Jupiter and Juno. Ovid, in his Fasti, tells a different story: Juno conceived him alone, after consulting the goddess Flora, who touched her belly with a magic flower. Juno then withdrew to Thrace, by the shore of the Sea of Marmara, to give birth. This may explain why the Matronalia, a festival of married women honouring Juno as goddess of childbirth, fell on the first day of March, the month named after him.

His consort was Nerio, whose name means something like valour and who personifies his vital force. In Rome's founding myth, Mars fathered Romulus and Remus by raping Rea Silvia in a forest. His most famous love affair was with Venus. Poets and philosophers preferred to picture them as lovers, although in Greek myth the adultery of Ares and Aphrodite became a joke when Hephaestus trapped them in an unbreakable chain. When Vulcan caught them, the other gods came to see them, and the shame made Mars afraid to repeat the affair. Queen Otrera is also called his wife, and according to some accounts the Amazons Hippolyta and Penthesilea were their daughters.

Worship and religion

His altar in the Campus Martius, the district of Rome named after him, was said to have been dedicated by King Numa. In Republican times the area was a centre of electoral activity. Augustus moved the focus of his cult inside the pomerium and built the temple of Mars Ultor in his new forum. His festivals fell in March and October, marking the start and end of the seasons for campaigns and harvests. Soldiers swore courage to him as Gradivus, the one who marches. His sacred animals were the wolf and the woodpecker.

Legacy

He gave his name to the fourth planet from the Sun, to Tuesday in the Romance languages (martes, mardi, martedì) and to the month of March. The Latin adjective martialis gave English the word martial, as in martial arts or martial law, and through the same root came names such as Marcus, Mark and Martin. The Renaissance philosopher Marsilio Ficino remarked that only Venus dominated Mars, and never the reverse.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Fasti recounts his birth without a father, Plautus mentions him in his comedies, and Hyginus lists his children.

Trivia

A birth with a flower. Flora tested a magic flower on a heifer, which became pregnant at once. She then touched Juno's belly with a flower, and Juno conceived Mars.
A birthday at the start of the year. In the earliest Roman calendar March was the first month, so the god would have been born with the new year.
A deception involving Minerva. It was said that Mars was in love with Minerva, and that Anna Perenna disguised herself as her to deceive him.
