king of Locris and father of Ajax the Lesser

Oileus

In Greek mythology, Oileus is the king of Locris, in Opuntian territory, and the father of the hero Ajax the Lesser. He also sailed with Jason as one of the Argonauts in search of the Golden Fleece.

The myth

According to tradition, Oileus was the son of Hodoedocus, about whom little is known, and of Agrianome, daughter of Perseon or Perseonte. He succeeded his father as king of Locris and, according to Hyginus, became the father of Ajax, who is called Ajax Oileus or Ajax the Lesser to distinguish him from Ajax the son of Telamon. He took part in the voyage of the Argonauts. In Apollonius of Rhodes, he is wounded in the shoulder by a feather of the Stymphalian Birds on the return from Colchis and is helped by his fellow Argonaut Eribotes.

The name of his wife is disputed. Homer names Eriopis as his lawful wife and Ajax's mother, while other authors give Alcimache as Ajax's mother, and others claim that Alcimache was simply another name for Eriopis. Tzetzes offers three options: Eriopis, Alcimache, or Astyoche, daughter of Itylus. Oileus was also the father of Medon, usually regarded as illegitimate, whose mother was said by some to be the nymph Rhene and by others Alcimache. Hyginus also makes Rhene the mother of Ajax.

Hesiod gives an explanation for the name of Oileus's father, whom he calls Ileus, meaning "the compliant one," because Apollo loved him after he found a nymph on the day Poseidon and Apollo raised the walls of Ilion. Seleucus links Ileus etymologically with Ilion.

Ancient sources: Homer, Hesiod, Apollonius of Rhodes, Hyginus, Seleucus and John Tzetzes mention him in their accounts of the Argonauts, the Trojan War and the lineage of Ajax.

Trivia

Wounded by a bird. According to Apollonius of Rhodes, during the return from Colchis a feather from the Stymphalian Birds wounded him in the shoulder.
Two Ajaxes. To tell his son apart from Ajax the son of Telamon, he is called Ajax Oileus or Ajax the Lesser, a name derived from his father.
