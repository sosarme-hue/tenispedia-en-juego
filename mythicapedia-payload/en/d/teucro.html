first mythical king of the Troad

Teucer

Teucer is the first mythical king of the Troad, the son of the river-god Scamander and the nymph Idaea. He is regarded as the forefather of the Trojans, whom Homer calls Teucrians, and his name gave rise to the name Teucria for the region.

The myth

According to tradition, Teucer came to the Troad from Crete, driven out by a great famine that forced part of the population to leave. Virgil, in the Aeneid, presents him as a Cretan, while Dionysius of Halicarnassus claims he came from Attica, from the region of the Xypetes. Before leaving, an oracle had told him to settle wherever earth-born enemies attacked his people at night. Mice that overran the camp showed him that this was the place, and he founded Hamaxitus, which he made his capital.

His reign was prosperous and, as the story goes, he succeeded in all his undertakings. He welcomed Dardanus into his court, after Dardanus had fled Samothrace following the death of his brother Iasion, and gave him his daughter Batea as a wife along with part of his kingdom. When Teucer died without a male heir, Dardanus received land on Mount Ida and founded Dardania. The kingdom was absorbed into his, and the whole region came to bear the name Dardania, although the people of Troy continued to call themselves Teucrians.

Worship and religion

He is credited with building the first temple to Apollo Sminthius, the god who, according to the story, destroyed the mice infesting the area during Teucer's reign. The sources do not describe a cult devoted to Teucer himself.

Legacy

His name was used for the Trojans in Homer's Iliad, and the region became known as Teucria. Some scholars propose a link between the Teucrians of the Greek texts and the Tjeker of Egyptian texts, a hypothesis that has not been proven.

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad), Virgil (Aeneid), Pseudo-Apollodorus (Library), Lycophron (Alexandra) and Diodorus Siculus, who call him the first king to rule the land of Troy.

Trivia

A temple for the mice. Teucer is remembered for the first temple to Apollo Sminthius, the "destroyer of mice", linked to the plague that struck his camp.
A name that was inherited. The Trojans continued to be called Teucrians long after Teucer had ceased to be the king who gave the region its name.
A possible Egyptian link. Some scholars connect the Teucrians of Greek texts with the Tjeker of Egyptian texts, though the link is not confirmed.
