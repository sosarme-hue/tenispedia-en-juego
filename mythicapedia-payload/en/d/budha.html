Hindu god of the planet Mercury

Budha

Budha is the Sanskrit name for the planet Mercury, personified in Hindu tradition as a god of intelligence and commerce. His name survives in the word for Wednesday in many Indian languages, and he is one of the nine planetary deities of the navagraha.

The myth

According to Puranic tradition, Budha is the son of Chandra, the Moon god, and of Tara, also called Rohini in some accounts. The story of the Tarakamaya War describes the events that led to his birth from Tara.

Budha married Ila, daughter of Vaivasvata Manu, and became the father of King Pururavas, the first ruler of the lunar dynasty. Because of this link, Pururavas is also called Budha Suta, son of Budha. Ancient texts portray him as a wise and eloquent male figure and a protector of merchants, and they carefully distinguish him from the Buddha, founder of Buddhism.

Worship and religion

Budha presides over Wednesday, known as budhavara or budha-dina, the day of Budha, a name still used in Hindi, Urdu, Telugu, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati and Kannada. In iconography he appears as a male figure with a greenish complexion, dressed in yellow, holding a scimitar, a club and a shield, and riding a winged lion, an eagle or a chariot pulled by horses. In Hindu astrology he is considered benevolent and linked to a nimble mind and memory.

Legacy

His name is the root of the word for Wednesday in several Indian languages, and in Thai it is Wan Phut. Medieval Sanskrit astronomers such as Aryabhata, Varahamihira and Brahmagupta recorded his planetary motions in their treatises. An emerald is called budha-ratna, the jewel of Mercury.

Ancient sources: The Pancha-vimsa Brahmana is one of the earliest texts to mention him; he also appears in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana and the Puranas.

Trivia

Not the Buddha. Budha should not be confused with the Buddha, the founder of Buddhism, who lived centuries later and belongs to a different tradition.
A day named after him. Wednesday takes its name from him in Hindi, Urdu, Telugu, Bengali and other Indian languages, and in Thai it is called Wan Phut.
