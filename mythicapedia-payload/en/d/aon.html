Greek hero, son of Poseidon

Aon

Aon is a figure of Greek mythology, a son of Poseidon, remembered mainly for giving his name to Aonia, an old name for the region of Boeotia. His importance is largely local, since the texts that mention him are few.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Aon was the son of Poseidon and Pitane. The ancient texts that record him are brief and offer no extended account of his deeds, so he is known mainly for his divine parentage and for the place that carries his name.

His story survives more as part of a genealogy than as a narrative of adventures, which is typical of Greek mythological figures whose role is mainly to link a lineage with a territory.

Worship and religion

Aon was particularly worshipped in Boeotia, the region that, according to tradition, took the name Aonia in his honour. The sources do not describe temples, festivals or specific rites, so his cult can only be outlined in general terms. There is no evidence that it survives today.

Legacy

His most visible legacy is the name Aonia, an ancient designation for Boeotia, which keeps his memory alive in the geography. Beyond that place name, the sources record no later references.
