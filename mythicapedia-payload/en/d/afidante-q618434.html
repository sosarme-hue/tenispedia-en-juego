legendary king of Athens

Apheidas

Apheidas is, in Greek myth, the fourteenth legendary king of Athens. His brief reign and his murder at the hands of his half-brother Timetes make him a figure of Athenian tradition rather than a hero of great deeds.

The myth

According to tradition, Apheidas was the son of Oxyntes and brother of Timetes. He appears in the list of legendary kings of Athens as the fourteenth to hold the throne, and his story is known mainly from a few brief episodes.

During his reign, the oracle of Dodona ruled that suppliants from Lacedaemon were to be respected. In this way the king is linked to the duty to protect those who sought refuge.

The tale ends with his death: he was said to have been killed by his younger half-brother Timetes, a bastard son, who then took the throne. According to this version, Apheidas's reign lasted only one year.
