Arcadian leader in the Trojan War

Teuthis

Teuthis is an Arcadian leader in Greek myth, from the city-state of Teuthis in the Peloponnese, who joined the expedition against Troy. He matters mainly for leaving the campaign early and for the punishment Athena inflicted on him.

The myth

According to the tradition, Teuthis gathered his Arcadian troops and sixty ships, alongside a general named Agenor, to bring back Helen. Becalmed at Aulis, where contrary winds held the fleet because Artemis was angry at Agamemnon, he quarrelled with the king of kings and resolved to sail home.

Athena appeared to him disguised as Melas and tried to make him stay. Not recognising her, Teuthis hurled his spear and wounded her in the thigh. Back in Arcadia, the goddess sent him a dream showing her wound, and he fell into a dreadful wasting sickness while his people suffered famine. According to Tzetzes, he died at home.

Worship and religion

The people consulted the oracle at Dodona, which told them to appease the goddess with a statue of her with her thigh wrapped in a purple bandage. Once the image was made, the famine ended.

Ancient sources: John Malalas and Tzetzes, Byzantine chroniclers who record the tradition; he does not appear in the Homeric poems.

Trivia

A second name. Some sources also call him Ornytus, and the city that gave him his name is the Arcadian Teuthis itself.
A bandaged statue. The image of Athena with her thigh wrapped in a purple bandage was the remedy the oracle at Dodona prescribed to end the famine.
