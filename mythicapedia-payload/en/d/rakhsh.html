Rostam's mythical horse in the Persian epic

Rakhsh

Rakhsh is the horse of the hero Rostam in the Shahnameh, the Persian national epic by Ferdowsi. His name means «luminous» and, in Persian tradition, he is the inseparable companion of one of the most celebrated heroes, a symbol of loyalty and strength.

The myth

According to the tale, Rostam finds Rakhsh among the herds of horses brought from Zabulistan and Kabul. His coat is described as «rose leaves that have been scattered upon a saffron ground», and while still a colt he shows the chest and shoulders of a lion and the strength of an elephant.

Rakhsh is a very intelligent animal, and his loyalty became legendary. Only Rostam may ride him: he recognizes no other master, and he is the only horse Rostam was ever able to ride, since the hero's great strength and weight would kill any other. The divine favor that protects Rostam also lengthens the horse's life, which lasts far longer than normal.

The deaths of both come through the treachery of Shaghad, Rostam's half-brother. So the story of the pair ends, bound in life by a loyalty that the tale stresses to the very end.

Legacy

Some hippologists consider that Rakhsh may belong to the famous Nisean breed, associated with the horses of Media. This idea is a modern interpretation and does not appear in the ancient texts, but it shows how Rostam's horse has also interested those who study horse breeding.

Ancient sources: Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, the Persian national epic, narrates him in detail within the story of Rostam.

Trivia

A name that means horse. In Aramaic, the word raḵšā, related to the name Rakhsh, simply means «horse».
A possible real breed. Some horse scholars link Rakhsh to the Nisean breed, a modern interpretation that does not come from the ancient texts.
