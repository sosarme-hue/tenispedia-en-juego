Pleiad, wife of Sisyphus

Merope

Merope is one of the Pleiades of Greek mythology, daughters of the Titan Atlas and the Oceanid Pleione. She is the only sister who married a mortal, Sisyphus, which is why she is known as the Lost Pleiad.

The myth

According to the tradition, the seven Pleiades were daughters of Atlas and Pleione. While their sisters united with gods, Merope married Sisyphus, and for this reason she shines more faintly in the sky. Accounts say she helped her husband in the trick by which he cheated death: by leaving him unburied, Sisyphus was able to ask to return to the world of the living to arrange his burial.

Accounts of her children differ. Some sources make her the mother of Glaucus, the adoptive father of Bellerophon, while other sons of Sisyphus, such as Thersander, Ornytion (or Porphyrion) and Almus, appear more often and are said to have founded the royal houses of Corinth and Lycia. Later sources add Laertes or Creon as possible sons.

Another tradition holds that the missing Pleiad is not Merope but Electra, who covered her face on seeing Troy destroyed. In another story, the hunter Orion pursued the sisters for years across Boeotia, until Zeus turned them into stars, and Orion still follows them across the sky.

Worship and religion

The Pleiades marked the beginning and end of summer with their appearance, so they were highly revered. The sources do not describe temples or rites dedicated specifically to Merope.

Legacy

Her story inspired the marble sculpture The Lost Pleiad (1874-1875) by Randolph Rogers, which shows the sister rising from a cloud in search of her lost siblings and is held at the Art Institute of Chicago. William-Adolphe Bouguereau also painted her in L'étoile perdue (1884). The eighteenth- and nineteenth-century plays and operas titled Merope deal with a completely different myth.

Ancient sources: Ovid recounts the legend of the seven sisters in his Latin poem Fasti.

Trivia

The missing Pleiad. One tradition holds that the star that cannot be seen is not Merope but Electra, who supposedly covered her face on seeing Troy destroyed.
A faint star. Merope is the faintest of the six Pleiades visible to the naked eye, and seventeenth-century astronomers did not follow the myth when naming them: the faintest named star is Asterope.
