Arcadian goddess of mystery cults, daughter of Demeter and Poseidon

Despoina

Despoina is a Greek goddess worshipped in Arcadia within mystery cults; her name, meaning "mistress", was kept secret and could be known only by initiates. She is considered of archaic origin and was later identified with Persephone, which makes her central to the study of ancient mystery religions. According to the myth, Poseidon desired Deméter, who tried to escape him by taking the form of a mare; he changed into a stallion and mated with her. From the union came Despoina and the fabulous horse Arion. Deméter's anger at this turn of events led her to take the epithet Erinys, "the furious". Despoina was honoured alongside her mother at the sanctuary of Lycosura, west of Megalopolis, where there were altars, a temple, a hall for the mysteries and a sacred grove. The cult statues included figures with animal heads, and women who attended had to avoid wearing black or purple. In the second century AD, Pausanias saw sculptures that were already over three hundred years old. The name Despoina relates to the Greek word for "lady" and to the Mycenaean potnia, a title of goddesses. In Greek Christianity the epithet Despina became a devotional title for the Virgin Mary, and a moon of Neptune bears her name.
