eponymous hero of the island of Naxos in Greek mythology

Naxos

Naxos is, in Greek mythology, the hero who gave his name to the island of Naxos in the Cyclades, according to one of the local versions of the myth. He is considered a son of Apollo and Acacallis, daughter of Minos, and his figure helps explain the origin of the island's name.

The myth

According to a tradition preserved by some ancient authors, Naxos was a son of Apollo and Acacallis, the daughter of Minos. His brothers could be Cydon, Amphithemis, Oaxes, Miletus, Phylacides and Phylander, all also called children of Apollo and Acacallis. Other traditions gave different accounts of the island's name: some made Naxos a son of Endymion, while others made him a son of Polemon, an upright and famous man whom the Carians, arriving from Latmia, made their king.

In this version, the text implies that the Naxos in question was the hero of the Cretan city of the same name, linking his lineage to Crete. The story survives in fragments and offers no detailed account of his deeds.

Trivia

An island with several namesakes. Ancient sources offer up to three different Naxos as possible eponyms of the island, each with a different genealogy.
Ariadne's island. In another part of the myth, Theseus abandons Ariadne on Naxos, and Dionysus carries her off to Mount Drios, where she disappears.
