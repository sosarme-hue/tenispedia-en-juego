regional Hindu deity of southern India

Karuppu Samy

Karuppu Samy is a regional deity of Hindu folk religion, widely revered by rural communities in Tamil Nadu and Kerala. He belongs to the Kaval Deivams, guardian deities associated with Ayyanar, and is seen as a fierce protector and upholder of justice.

The myth

According to the tradition passed down by his devotees, Karuppu Samy was once a historical figure who fought to defend his community and was later deified. His abode is the Earth, his weapons are the aruval and the sangili sword, his mount is a horse, and his consort is Karuppayi.

He appears in twenty-one forms, each representing a different role such as protection, justice, healing or punishment. These forms come from oral tradition and ritual rather than Vedic or Agamic texts, and they may vary from region to region.

Worship and religion

His temples stand outside settlements, and entire villages help maintain them. They have no gopurams but house large statues with wide eyes, holding bows, swords and sickles. Mostly non-Brahmin priests offer flowers and vibhuti, and during festivals oracles enter a trance called Swami Adudhal to counsel the community. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

In Indo-Caribbean communities of Trinidad and Tobago, Guyana, Suriname and Guadeloupe he is known as Sangani Baba. He is associated with the colour black, and devotees offer him camphor, butter, ghee and, at times, sacrifices of black chickens and goats.

Trivia

The colour black. In Indo-Caribbean communities, Sangani Baba is associated with the colour black, which symbolises his fierce and protective nature.
Temples without gopurams. Unlike many South Indian Hindu temples, his shrines have no gopuram, the monumental gateway tower.
