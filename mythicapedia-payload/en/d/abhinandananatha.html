fourth tirthankara of Jainism

Abhinandananatha

Abhinandananatha is the fourth tirthankara of the current age in Jainism, a teacher revered as a guide toward liberation. He is best known by his emblem, the monkey.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, he was born in Ayodhya, in the Ikshvaku clan, to King Samvara and Queen Siddhartha. After ruling his kingdom, he renounced the world to become an ascetic and attained omniscience while meditating beneath a priyangu tree.

After a long period of teaching the doctrine, he achieved liberation from the cycle of rebirth on Mount Shikharji, in present-day Jharkhand. As a siddha, Jain belief holds that he has destroyed all his karma.

Worship and religion

His footprints are venerated at Anand Koot, the hilltop shrine on Shikharji, where pilgrims from both main Jain traditions come to pray. The Abhinandanoday Teerth Kshetra in Uttar Pradesh and the Chaturmukha Basadi at Gerusoppa in Karnataka remain pilgrimage centres.

Legacy

In Jain art he is shown seated in meditation, with a golden complexion and a monkey carved on the pedestal of his images, a feature that sets him apart from other tirthankaras.

Ancient sources: The Svayambhustotra, a devotional hymn to the twenty-four tirthankaras composed by the monk Samantabhadra, devotes five verses to his qualities.

Trivia

A monkey as emblem. The monkey or ape is his emblem, carved or stamped on the pedestal of his images, and it is what allows him to be recognised among other tirthankaras.
A sacred mountain. For Jainism, Mount Shikharji is the place where twenty of the twenty-four tirthankaras attained liberation.
