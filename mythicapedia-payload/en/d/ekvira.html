Hindu goddess, kuladevi of the Koli people

Ekvira

Ekvira is a Hindu goddess worshipped in Maharashtra, India, regarded as a form of Parvati, Yamai or Renuka. She is the kuladevi (family guardian goddess) of the Koli community and is also central to the Aagri people.

The myth

The reference texts identify her with forms of the goddess Parvati, Yamai and Renuka, placing her within the wider tradition of Hindu goddesses. She is believed to have magical powers, and for this reason she is invoked in times of need by those who regard her as protector of their lineage.

Her main shrine stands beside the Karla Caves, once a Buddhist centre, on a hill near Lonavala. Three similar shrines were built there in a row, all facing west; two are preserved in full. Alongside the main goddess is an image of Jogeśvarī Devi.

Worship and religion

The Ekvira Aai Mandir draws devotees especially during Navaratri and Chaitra Navratra. Reaching it involves climbing around five hundred steps. Every year the Kolis honour the goddess and celebrate her festival there, and the Chandraseniya Kayastha Prabhu community also worships her. Her cult remains alive today.
