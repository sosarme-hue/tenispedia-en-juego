daughter of Dardanus and wife of Phineus in Greek myth

Idaea

Idaea (Greek Idaía) is the name of several female figures in Greek mythology, linked to the Mount Ida. The one covered here is a daughter of Dardanus, a Scythian king, and the wife of Phineus, remembered for falsely accusing her stepsons.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus, Idaea married Phineus, who already had sons by an earlier wife. She falsely accused the stepsons of making improper advances toward her. Phineus believed her, and the young men were imprisoned and tortured. The account gives no further resolution, so the episode stands as an unjust accusation with terrible consequences.

The name Idaea also belongs to other figures in the tradition, such as the mother of the Kuretes, the mother of Cres, and the mother of Teucer, which has led to confusion between them because of their shared link to Mount Ida.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus presents her as the daughter of Dardanus and wife of Phineus, responsible for the false accusation against her stepsons.

Trivia

One name, several women. Idaea names several female figures in Greek mythology, linked to the Mount Ida of Crete or the one in Phrygia.
Epithet of Cybele. Idaea is also one of the epithets of Cybele, the great mother goddess of Anatolia, associated with the Phrygian Mount Ida.
