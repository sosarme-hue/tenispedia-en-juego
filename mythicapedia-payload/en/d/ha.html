Egyptian god of the western desert

Ha

Ha is an Egyptian god, Lord of the western deserts and patron of the desert mountains west of the Nile. His figure links the west, the Duat and the protection of the oases and of the pharaoh.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Ha was the son of a little-known deity called Iaaw, and in origin he was associated with fertility. Over time he took the place of Ash, the god of the western desert. He was credited with creating the oases and with defending Egypt against enemies coming from the west, probably nomadic tribes from Libya. He was shown as a man wearing the sign of the desert hills, or the hieroglyph for the West, on his head, and sometimes carrying a harpoon to hunt hippopotamuses.

Ha was also tied to the Duat, the underworld. Texts carved in the pyramid of Unas present him as the protector of the dead pharaoh.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped as the god of the seventh nome of Lower Egypt and had a cult centre at Huy. He held an important place in the Heb Sed festival, alongside Igai, and also in funerary worship.

Legacy

The dinosaur Hagryphus, meaning «Ha's griffin», was named after him and discovered in Utah. His link to western deserts was carried over, by analogy, to the deserts of the southwestern United States.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, carved in the pyramid of Unas, mention him as protector of the dead pharaoh.

Trivia

A dinosaur named after him. The dinosaur Hagryphus was named in his honour, meaning «Ha's griffin».
A god of the west. Ha became the symbol of the west, while Sopdu represented the east and Dedun the south.
