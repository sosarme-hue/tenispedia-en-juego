Thor's goats in Norse mythology

Tanngnjóstr and Tanngrisnir

Tanngnjóstr and Tanngrisnir are the two goats that pull the chariot of the god Thor in Norse mythology. They are known for their ability to be eaten and brought back to life, a motif that scholars have compared with Scandinavian folk beliefs about herring bones.

The myth

According to the sources, Thor travelled in a chariot pulled by these goats. One night he stayed at the home of peasant farmers, slaughtered the animals, cooked them and shared the meal with the family. Thor laid the skins by the fire and asked the household to throw the bones onto them.

The farmers' son, Þjálfi, split a bone to get at the marrow. The next morning Thor blessed the skins with his hammer Mjölnir and the goats came back to life, but one was lame in a leg. Furious, Thor frightened the peasants, who offered him everything they owned. He accepted their children, Þjálfi and Röskva, as servants, and they have served him ever since.

Legacy

The names have inspired characters in Marvel Comics, a racecar in the Speed Racer film adaptation, Yu-Gi-Oh! cards and a weapon part in the 2018 video game God of War.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Hymiskviða, Þrymskviða) and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda mention Thor's goats.

Trivia

Names for goats. In the Prose Edda, the names of both goats also appear in a list of names for goats.
Bones and witchcraft. Scholars Reimund Kvideland and Henning Sehmsdorf link the motif of breaking bones with Scandinavian tales of herring that magically renew themselves.
