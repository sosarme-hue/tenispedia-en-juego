father of Sól and Máni in Norse mythology

Mundilfari

Mundilfari is a figure of Norse mythology, the father of Sól, the goddess of the Sun, and Máni, the god of the Moon. His name, whose meaning is debated, has been linked to the idea of time and cycles, and the sources present him mainly as the progenitor of these celestial figures.

The myth

The stories that mention him present him mainly as the father of the personified heavenly bodies rather than as the hero of tales of his own. Tradition places him at the head of the family line of Sól and Máni, and this genealogical role is almost all we know about him.

He is attested in the poem Vafþrúðnismál, in stanza 23 of the Poetic Edda, and in chapter 11 of Gylfaginning, in the Prose Edda. Some traditions also add Glaur among his companions or descendants, but the sources do not fully agree on this point.

His name has prompted several interpretations. Its first element, mundil-, may be related to mund, meaning 'period of time'. If so, Mundilfari could have been a kenning, a poetic periphrasis, for the Moon, an idea proposed by Rudolf Simek and discussed by John Lindow.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons bears his name, taken from this Norse figure, which shows how Norse mythology lives on in modern astronomical naming.

Ancient sources: He is attested in the poem Vafþrúðnismál of the Poetic Edda (stanza 23) and in the book Gylfaginning of the Prose Edda (chapter 11).

Trivia

A moon of Saturn. One of Saturn's moons is named after Mundilfari, an example of how Norse mythology still appears in modern astronomical naming.
A name with many spellings. The name appears in different forms in the sources, such as Mundilfari, Mundilföri or Mundilfœri, which complicates its interpretation.
