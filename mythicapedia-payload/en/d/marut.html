Vedic storm deities

Maruts

The Maruts are Vedic storm deities, sons of Rudra and companions of Indra in his battles. They belong to the oldest layer of Hindu tradition, and their figure embodies the violence and force of tempests.

The myth

According to the myth, the Maruts form a troop of fierce young warriors who roar like lions, have iron teeth and carry golden weapons, that is, lightning and thunderbolts. They dwell in the north and ride across the sky in golden chariots drawn by ruddy horses. Their number varies between sources, from 27 to 60, and the Rig Veda, in hymn 8.96.8, speaks of three times sixty. Their leader is Mātariśvan.

In the Vedic hymns they are sons of Rudra, though they are also described as sons of Pṛśni, of the heavens or of the ocean. They are accompanied by Rodasī, whom some versions name as their mother, others as the wife of Rudra, and others as their shared wife. The Maruts help Indra defeat Vṛtra and lend him their strength, but Indra later accuses them of abandoning him after encouraging him before the battle, and they quarrel over the sacrifice offered by the sage Agastya. Even so, their place at Indra's side remains fixed in the mythology.

Later tradition, found in the Rāmāyaṇa and the Purāṇas, gives another origin. In that account, the goddess Diti wished to stay pregnant for a hundred years to bear a son more powerful than Indra. Indra hurled a thunderbolt that shattered her womb and split the foetus into several deities, the Maruts, who were weaker than he was. In some versions their number is 7 or 49, that is, seven times seven.

Ancient sources: The Maruts appear mainly in the Rig Veda, and their origin from Diti is narrated in the Rāmāyaṇa and the Purāṇas.

Trivia

Three times sixty. The Rig Veda, in hymn 8.96.8, speaks of three times sixty Maruts, one of the highest figures the sources give for their number.
A divided foetus. In the later account, the child Diti carried for a hundred years is split into several deities after Indra's thunderbolt.
