king of the nagas in Hinduism

Shesha

Shesha is the divine serpent and king of the nagas in Hinduism, one of the primordial beings of creation. He is best known as the couch on which Vishnu rests upon the cosmic ocean, and his name, meaning "he who remains", reflects his endurance through the dissolution of the universe at the end of each kalpa.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Shesha holds the planets of the universe on his hoods and sings the praises of Vishnu with all his mouths. He is most often shown with about twenty heads, although he also appears with five, seven or, in classical texts, a thousand crowned heads.

According to the Mahabharata, his father was the sage Kashyapa and his mother Kadru, and he was the eldest of a thousand serpents, among them Vasuki and Takshaka. Distressed by the cruelty of his siblings, Shesha abandoned them and performed severe penance, subsisting on air. Pleased, Brahma granted him a boon: that his mind remain steadfast in dharma and devotion. Brahma then asked him to steady the Earth by descending beneath it, and Shesha supports her on his hood from the subterranean realm of Patala, where tradition says he still lives.

In the story of the churning of the ocean of milk, gods and demons pulled on his head and tail to spin Mount Mandara. Tradition also holds that he incarnated as Lakshmana, brother of Rama, and as Balarama, brother of Krishna.

Worship and religion

Shesha is venerated within Hinduism, and the city of Thiruvananthapuram in southern India takes its name from Ananta, one of his titles. In Thailand, within Tai folk beliefs, he is revered as a major naga, and shrines to him stand across the country, where he is honoured as a guardian deity.

Legacy

In Kerala, the palliyodam, a large snake boat used at Aranmula Parthasarathy Temple, is traditionally said to have been designed by Krishna to resemble Shesha. In Thailand he is represented in the Royal Barge Anantanakkharat, one of the kingdom's principal ceremonial vessels.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata (Adi Parva) names him as the son of Kashyapa and Kadru, and the Puranas describe him supporting the universe.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His name comes from the Sanskrit root śiṣ, meaning "what remains", because he stays unchanged when the universe is destroyed at the end of each kalpa.
A city named after him. The Indian city of Thiruvananthapuram takes its name from Ananta, one of Shesha's forms.
