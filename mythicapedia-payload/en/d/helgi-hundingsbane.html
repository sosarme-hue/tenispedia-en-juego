hero of the Norse sagas, avenger of his father

Helgi Hundingsbane

Helgi Hundingsbane is a hero of the Norse sagas, known for avenging his father Sigmund by killing King Hunding. His love story with the valkyrie Sigrún and his tragic death at the hands of his brother-in-law Dag make him one of the best-loved figures of Germanic and Scandinavian tradition.

The myth

According to the stories, Helgi is the son of Sigmund and Borghild. At only fifteen he avenged his father by killing Hunding, king of the Saxons, which gave him his nickname, meaning "Hunding's bane".

While he stood on his longship, the valkyrie Sigrún visited him; she knew of his deeds and the two fell in love. However, her father, King Högne, had promised her to Hothbrodd. Helgi gathered a force, and in the battle Högne, Hothbrodd and nearly all their family died, except the young Dag, who swore loyalty to him. Helgi and Sigrún married, but Dag, bound by the duty to avenge his father, received a spear from Odin and pierced Helgi with it. Sigrún cursed Dag to wander the woods living on carrion.

Helgi arrived in Valhalla, where he was given humble tasks. One night Sigrún saw him return to his burial mound; they spent the night together before he went back to the other world.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda, with the lays Helgakviða Hundingsbana I and II, and the Volsunga saga mention him.
