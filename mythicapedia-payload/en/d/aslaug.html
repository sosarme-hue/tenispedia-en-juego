Norse queen in mythology

Aslaug

Aslaug, also called Aslög or Kráka, is a figure of Norse mythology and a semi-legendary Viking queen, the third wife of King Ragnar Lodbrok. Her story appears in the Völsunga saga, Snorri Sturluson's Edda and the saga of Ragnar Lodbrok, and she is best known for her cleverness and independent spirit.

The myth

According to tradition, Aslaug was the daughter of Sigurd, the dragon slayer, and the shieldmaiden Brynhildr. After her parents died, her foster father Heimer hid her inside a harp large enough to conceal her and travelled with her as a poor harp player until they reached Spangereid in Norway. There, the farmers Åke and Grima plotted to murder Heimer for the supposed treasure, but when they broke open the harp they found a little girl. They raised her as their own under the name Kráka, meaning crow.

One day, while she was bathing, some of Ragnar Lodbrok's men discovered her. To test her wits, the king ordered her to arrive neither dressed nor undressed, neither fasting nor fed, neither alone nor in company. She came wearing a net, biting an onion and accompanied only by a dog, and Ragnar married her. Later, persuaded by the Swedish viceroy Eysteinn Beli, Ragnar planned to reject her for the Swedish princess Ingeborg. Aslaug rebuked his boldness and revealed her noble birth by foretelling a son with a snake in one eye, a nod to Sigurd Snake-in-the-Eye. Ragnar was captured by the English and thrown into a pit of venomous snakes; the magic mail coat she had made protected him until it was taken away, and then he died.

Legacy

William Morris's romantic poem The Fostering of Aslaug retells her relationship with Ragnar. Friedrich de la Motte Fouqué included her in Aslauga's Knight (1810), and the television series Vikings (2013-2016) features a character loosely based on her, played by Alyssa Sutherland. Scholars also link her tale to the folk story of the clever peasant girl (Aarne-Thompson 875).

Ancient sources: She appears in the Völsunga saga, Snorri Sturluson's Edda, the Hálfdanar saga Eysteinssonar and the 13th-century Tale of Ragnar Lodbrok.

Trivia

A boneless son. According to scholars, Ragnar insisted on bedding Aslaug right after the wedding, against her advice, and their first son, Ivar, was born weak and boneless.
An old folk tale. Her story is considered the prototype of the clever peasant girl folk tale, catalogued as Aarne-Thompson 875.
