sacred well of Norse mythology

Well of Urd

The Well of Urd is a sacred spring in Norse mythology, lying beneath one of the roots of the world tree Yggdrasil. It is the home of the norns and the source of wisdom, and its holy water keeps the great ash alive, which holds up the cosmos.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, the Well of Urd (Urðarbrunnr, the wellspring of Urðr) lies beneath the third of the roots of Yggdrasil, the great ash that holds up the cosmos. That root reaches up to heaven, among the Æsir, where the gods ride across the bridge Bifröst each day to hold court beside the spring. Nearby stands a beautiful hall from which three maidens come: Urðr, Verðandi and Skuld, who are called the norns. They are the ones who set down laws, choose lives and decide the fates of men.

The norns draw water from the well, together with the mud along its banks, and pour it over the tree so that its branches never rot or dry out. The water is so holy that anything that falls into it turns white, like the thin skin inside an eggshell. The dew that falls from the well onto the lands of men is honeydew, which feeds the bees, and two swans that feed on the well are said to be the ancestors of all swans.

The Völuspá places the well under Yggdrasil, where the tree is covered in white clay, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda counts it as one of three springs, alongside Hvergelmir in Niflheim and Mímisbrunnr near the frost giants. In stanza 112 of Hávamál, a speaker sits by the well of Urd, meditating and listening to the words of men, a passage scholars find obscure and much debated. In Skáldskaparmál, Urðr is described as rising from the wellspring.

Ancient sources: Völuspá and Hávamál from the Poetic Edda, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál).

Trivia

Swans of the well. According to the Prose Edda, two swans feed from the Well of Urd, and all other swans are said to descend from them.
Water that whitens. Anything that enters the well turns as white as the membrane inside an eggshell.
