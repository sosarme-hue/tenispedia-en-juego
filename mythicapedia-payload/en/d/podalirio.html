Greek hero, son of Asclepius and healer in the Trojan War

Podalirius

Podalirius is a Greek hero, son of Asclepius, the god of medicine, and Epione, who joined his brother Machaon in the Trojan War as a healer. He matters because he embodies the figure of the heroic physician, and because traditions about his later life place him in Caria and in Italy.

The myth

According to tradition, Podalirius and Machaon led thirty ships from Tricca, in Thessaly, to fight alongside the Greeks. Both had been suitors of Helen and, bound by the oath of Tyndareus, joined the war. In the camp he tended wounds and illnesses. One version credits him with curing Philoctetes, whose bow of Heracles was essential for taking the city, while another reserves surgery for Machaon. Homer shows him fighting on the Trojan plain, and Quintus of Smyrna counts him among the warriors hidden inside the wooden horse.

After the war the accounts diverge. According to the Pseudo-Apollodorus, he returned by land, helped bury the seer Calchas at Colophon and then consulted the Delphic oracle, which advised him to settle where the sky, if it fell, would not harm him. He thus reached a mountain-ringed valley on the Carian Chersonese. Pausanias, by contrast, says he lost his way at sea and settled at Syrnus, also in Caria. Another version has him shipwrecked and rescued by a shepherd named Bybassus, who at the court of King Damaethus healed the king's daughter Syrna after she fell from a roof. As a reward he received her hand and rule over the peninsula, and he founded the cities of Syrnus and Bybassus.

Worship and religion

Strabo places a heroön of Podalirius in Daunia, Italy, on a hill called Drium, along with another dedicated to Calchas. Beside the hero-shrine of Podalirius ran a stream believed to cure diseases in animals. Lycophron states that he was buried in Italy near the cenotaph of Calchas, though Tzetzes considers that information false.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, the Catalogue of Ships, Quintus of Smyrna, the Pseudo-Apollodorus, Pausanias, Strabo and Lycophron.

Trivia

A fall and a marriage. In one version, Podalirius healed Syrna, daughter of King Damaethus, after she fell from a roof. In gratitude, the king gave him her hand and rule over the peninsula.
A stream for animals. In Daunia, beside Podalirius's hero-shrine, a stream was believed to cure sick animals, according to Strabo.
