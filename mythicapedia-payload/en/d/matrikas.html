Matrikas

The Matrikas, whose name means "mothers" in Sanskrit, are a group of Hindu goddesses who are almost always shown together, usually in sets of seven or eight. They are regarded as the energies (shaktis) of gods such as Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, and they became especially important in Tantrism.

The myth

According to tradition, the Matrikas are the energies of the great gods: Brahmani emerged from Brahma, Vaishnavi from Vishnu, Maheshwari from Shiva, Indrani from Indra, Kaumari from Kartikeya, Varahi from Varaha, Narasimhi from Narasimha and Chamunda from Chandi. Sometimes Vinayaki, linked to Ganesha, is also included.

In the earliest accounts, such as the Mahabharata, they are fierce goddesses tied to conception, birth and childhood diseases, and they were propitiated to ward off the perils that killed so many children. Over time they took on a protective role, although they kept some of their wild and destructive traits. In the Devi Mahatmya they help Durga in her battle against demons. The lists vary: seven, eight, nine or sixteen, depending on the period and region.

Worship and religion

The Matrikas were a common feature of Hindu temples from the early medieval period, and worship of the Saptamatrikas is especially widespread in South India. In Nepal the eight are venerated, and in Bhaktapur a ninth is added to represent the centre. Some dynasties, such as the Guptas, made them court goddesses to strengthen the loyalty of their soldiers.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata and the Rig Veda mention groups of mother goddesses, and the Devi Mahatmya and the Puranas describe their iconography and functions.

Trivia

A variable number. The Matrikas may be seven, eight, nine or sixteen depending on the text and region.
Court goddesses. The Gupta kings raised the Matrikas from popular village goddesses to court goddesses.
