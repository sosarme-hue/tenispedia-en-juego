alleged Slavic goddess

Kupala

Kupala (or Kupalo) is an alleged deity of East Slavic mythology, first mentioned in the seventeenth century. Most modern scholars of Slavic mythology deny that such a god ever really existed.

The myth

Kupala appears in the sources only late: her first documented mention dates from the seventeenth century, when she was compared to the Greek goddess Ceres. Since no ancient Slavic accounts describe her, it is not possible to reconstruct a myth of her own, with deeds, family or ending.

Modern specialists in Slavic mythology consider that such a deity never existed. For them, the name and figure of Kupala are explained by that late first reference and by the comparison with Ceres, rather than by a religious tradition passed down among the Slavic peoples.

Legacy

Her current interest lies mainly in the academic debate over whether she ever existed as a deity.

Ancient sources: No ancient Slavic texts mention her; the first known reference dates from the seventeenth century.
