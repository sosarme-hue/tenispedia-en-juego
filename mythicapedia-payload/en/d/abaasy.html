demons of Sakha (Yakut) mythology

Abaasy

The abaasy are demons in the mythology of the Sakha, also known as the Yakuts, who inhabit the lower level of the universe. They are believed to be spirits of the long dead who haunt graves or deserted places and cause destruction, and the idea is so deeply rooted in Sakha thought that it has given rise to an everyday expression.

The myth

According to Yakut shamanism, the universe is divided into an upper and a lower layer, with the earth lying between them as a kind of indeterminate space. The abaasy occupy the lower level, known as the underworld or "kingdom of darkness." They are said to be the spirits of the long dead, who dwell near graves or in deserted places, or who wander about causing destruction.

They serve Arson-Duolai, ruler of the dead, who swallows people's souls and gives the living diseases. The abaasy are also said to cause sexual disturbances and madness, and they can be appeased with blood sacrifices.

They are described as one-eyed, one-armed and one-legged monsters riding two-headed, eight-legged, two-tailed dragons. In the Olonkho epic poems they are ugly, horrifying man-eaters. Their chief, Alyp Khara Aat Mogoidoon, is a giant with three heads, six arms and six legs, and a body made of iron.

Worship and religion

The abaasy were appeased with blood sacrifices, according to traditional Yakut shamanic belief.

Legacy

The concept of the abaasy is so deeply embedded in Sakha culture that the phrase "to see abaasy" is used every day to mean hating or disliking someone.

Trivia

A dragon with eight legs. The abaasy ride two-headed, eight-legged, two-tailed dragons, a very different image from European dragons.
Hatred in a phrase. In the Sakha language, the verb "to see abaasy" means to hate or dislike, showing how deeply the concept has entered everyday speech.
