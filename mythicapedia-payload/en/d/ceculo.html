Caeculus

Caeculus is a hero of Roman mythology, son of Vulcan and legendary founder of Praeneste, modern Palestrina. His story explains the origin of a Latin city and the gens Caecilia, which claimed him as its ancestor.

The myth

According to tradition, Caeculus was the son of Vulcan and a young sister of the divine twin brothers known as the Depidii (or Digidii). While she sat by the hearth, a spark fell on her and she became pregnant. After the birth, she exposed the child near the temple of Jupiter, where girls who had come to fetch water found him lying by the fire and took him to his uncles, who raised him.

As a young man, he gathered a band of youths his own age and founded Praeneste. In some accounts he doubted his divine ancestry and came through a fire unharmed; he also mastered fire, lighting and extinguishing it at will. The smoke damaged his eyes, leaving them small, which gave him his name, the little blind one. Another version has a child thrown into the ashes of a shepherds' hearth, raised by the herdsmen. The story ends with his recognition as Vulcan's son.

Legacy

Virgil mentions him in Book VII of the Aeneid as an ally of Turnus against Aeneas. His story recalls the ver sacrum rite and the tale of Romulus and Remus, and he was regarded as the eponymous ancestor of the gens Caecilia, and perhaps also of the lesser-known gens Caesia.

Ancient sources: Virgil, Aeneid, Book VII.

Trivia

Telling name. His name derives from caecus, "blind", and is explained by the smoke that damaged his eyes in the story.
Mastery of fire. According to the myth, he survived a fire and could kindle and extinguish flames at will.
