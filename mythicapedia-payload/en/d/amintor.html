king of Ormenium in Greek mythology

Amyntor

Amyntor is a hero of Greek mythology, king of Ormenium and father of Phoenix, later tutor of Achilles. His story connects with the family of Peleus and with the adventures of Heracles.

The myth

According to tradition, Amyntor was the son of Ormenus, although ancient authors differ on his genealogy: Strabo gives a line that made him the grandson of Cercaphus and great-grandson of Aeolus, and names his brother as Euaemon. His wife is called Hippodamia or Cleobule depending on the source, and their children were Phoenix, Crantor and Astydamia.

The best-known episode is the quarrel with Phoenix. In the Iliad, which Phoenix recounts to Achilles, Amyntor abandoned his wife for a concubine, and Phoenix, urged by his mother, slept with her. When Amyntor found out, he called upon the Erinyes to curse his son with childlessness. Enraged, Phoenix meant to kill him but was dissuaded, and he fled to Phthia, to Peleus. In a later version, preserved by Apollodorus, the concubine falsely accused Phoenix and Amyntor blinded him, until Chiron restored his sight.

Another episode tells of a war between Amyntor and Peleus, which ended with Crantor handed over as a pledge of peace. When Amyntor refused Heracles passage through his kingdom, the hero killed him and fathered Ctesippus with Astydamia. According to the Iliad, a thief also broke into his house at Eleon and stole a helmet that later passed to Odysseus during the Trojan War.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him as king and father of Phoenix, and Ovid's Metamorphoses recalls his son Crantor. Apollodorus, Strabo and the poets Callimachus and Lycophron also refer to him.

Trivia

A helmet with a long journey. According to the Iliad, the helmet stolen from Amyntor's house ended up with Odysseus during the Trojan War, passed on by Meriones.
A hostage for peace. After losing a war against Peleus, Amyntor handed over his son Crantor as a guarantee of peace.
