Welsh water horse

Ceffyl Dŵr

The ceffyl dŵr is a water horse from Welsh folklore, most often considered the Welsh counterpart of the Scottish kelpie. It lured unwary travellers into riding it, then threw them to the ground or dragged them to their deaths.

The myth

According to tradition, this spirit lives mostly in mountain pools and waterfalls, though it also haunts rivers and seashores. It takes the form of a grey or black horse with a white mane, sometimes glowing or winged. Whoever mounted it was carried off at breakneck speed until the animal rose into the sky or dissolved into mist, dropping the rider. It could be caught and put to work, but it always escaped in the end, dragging its captor to death.

Its temperament depended on the region. In North Wales it was seen as malevolent, with fiery eyes; in South Wales it was more often a mere nuisance to travellers, or even a help to the weary. It could also take other shapes, such as a goat that charged with great force, or a frog that leapt onto people's backs and choked them.

Trivia

A journey there and back. According to a story from the River Honddu, a water horse carried a man from a Roman camp near Brecon to the Towy. Three days later it brought him back, battered after being dragged through mire, brambles and briars.
A horse that could not be overtaken. In an early nineteenth-century account, a man tried to overtake a glowing horse ahead of him and could not catch it. When he reached the mill, the horse and its rider vanished into the darkness.
