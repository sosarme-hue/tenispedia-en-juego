Cronus

Cronus is the leader and youngest of the Titans in Greek mythology, son of Uranus and Gaia and father of the great Olympian gods, including Zeus. His story, marked by the castration of his father, a golden age of rule and his overthrow by Zeus, makes him a central figure in Greek accounts of the origins of the cosmos and, over time, a symbol of time that devours everything.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Uranus kept his children hidden in the womb of Gaia, which angered the Earth. Gaia forged a great stone sickle and asked her sons to punish their father. Only Cronus agreed: he ambushed Uranus and castrated him with the sickle. From the spilled blood came the Gigantes, the Erinyes and the Meliae, and from the foam formed by the discarded genitals emerged Aphrodite. Cronus then became king, and his rule is remembered as a golden age in which no laws were needed and immorality was absent.

His power, however, rested on fear of a prophecy that his own children would overthrow him as he had overthrown his father. To prevent this, he swallowed each child born to him and his sister Rhea. When Zeus was born, Rhea deceived him by giving him a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes, which he swallowed believing it was the infant. Zeus grew up hidden in Crete and later, with Gaia's help, forced his father to disgorge his siblings and the stone. After a great war, the Titanomachy, Zeus and his siblings defeated Cronus and the other Titans, who were confined in Tartarus.

Accounts of his final fate differ. The Iliad, the Theogony and Apollodorus say he was imprisoned in Tartarus, while some papyrus versions of Hesiod and Pindar place him ruling the Isle of the Blessed after being freed by Zeus.

Worship and religion

Cronus was likely a harvest god. In Athens, on the twelfth day of the month Hekatombaion, the Kronia festival was held in his honour to celebrate the harvest, and in many regions of Greece a month was named Cronion after him.

Legacy

His name became associated with time, Chronos, leading to the image of Father Time wielding a scythe. Over the centuries he was identified with the Roman god Saturn, from whom he inherited the sickle, and the planet Saturn takes its name from this identification.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the main account; the Iliad, Apollodorus, Plato, Diodorus Siculus and the Sibylline Oracles add further details of his story.

Trivia

Father Time. Because of the phonetic similarity with Chronos, meaning time, Cronus merged with the personification of time and, in the Renaissance, with the figure of Father Time armed with a scythe.
The swallowed stone. Cronus swallowed a stone wrapped in swaddling clothes believing it was Zeus, and later vomited it up. The stone was placed by Zeus at Pytho on Mount Parnassus.
