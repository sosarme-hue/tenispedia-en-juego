Greek goddess of violence and force

Bia

Bia is, in Greek mythology, the personification of violence and compelling force. Daughter of the Titan Pallas and the Oceanid Styx, she was a constant companion of Zeus alongside her siblings Kratos, Nike and Zelus, and she appears mainly in the story of Prometheus's punishment.

The myth

According to Hesiod's poems, Bia was born, together with Zelus, Nike and Kratos, to the Titan Pallas and the Oceanid Styx. The four siblings helped Zeus in the Titanomachy, the war against the Titans which, according to tradition, lasted ten years and ended with the victory of the Olympian gods. For this service they earned the god's favour, and he kept them always at his side on Olympus, entrusting them with enforcing his orders whenever strength was needed.

The best-known episode is the punishment of Prometheus, who stole fire for humankind. In Aeschylus's tragedy, Zeus orders Hephaestus to chain him on the Caucasus, and Kratos and Bia make sure it is carried out. Kratos speaks at length, pressing Hephaestus to stop hesitating, while Bia says nothing: the smith does his task by force and under Zeus's power. Plato retells the episode and calls Zeus's sentinels terrible, meaning once again Kratos and Bia.

In the sources Bia is almost always silent and in the background, yet her role as the enforcing arm of the god makes her essential to the myth of Prometheus.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes, on the way up the Acrocorinth, a sanctuary dedicated to Ananke (Necessity) and Bia, which people, he notes, did not usually enter. Plutarch also records an anecdote in which Themistocles arrives accompanied by the goddesses Persuasion and Force (Bia). There is no evidence of a widespread cult.

Ancient sources: Hesiod names her in the Theogony as the daughter of Pallas and Styx; Aeschylus features her in Prometheus Bound; Plato, Pausanias and Plutarch also mention her.

Trivia

A shared sanctuary. Pausanias places on the Acrocorinth a sanctuary where Bia appears alongside Ananke, Necessity.
A goddess who stays silent. In Aeschylus's tragedy, Bia says not a word, while her brother Kratos speaks at length.
