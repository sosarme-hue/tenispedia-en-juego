Aztec god of the west wind

Cihuatecayotl

Cihuatecayotl is, in Aztec mythology, the god of the west wind, one of the four personifications of the winds that blow from the cardinal directions. He matters because he shows how the peoples of ancient Mexico organized the world and its natural forces by direction.

The myth

According to the sources, the four winds did not act alone. All of them were under the command of Ehécatl, the wind god and a form of Quetzalcoatl. At the same time, each direction had its own ruler: Tezcatlipoca in the north, Xipetótec in the east, Quetzalcoatl in the west and Huitzilopochtli in the south, and the winds fell under their authority.

The texts consulted do not preserve a separate story about Cihuatecayotl, his deeds or his genealogy. The English reference calls him a brother of Mictlanpachecatl, Tlalocayotl and Huitztlampaehecatl and assigns them the north, east and south winds, but the Spanish version does not fix that correspondence, so it should be taken with caution.
