Vedic god of portion, one of the Adityas

Amsha

Amsha is a deity of Hinduism, one of the Adityas, a group of celestial gods who are children of Aditi and siblings of other Vedic gods. His name means 'portion' in Sanskrit, a meaning that fits his link with division and inheritance.

The myth

Amsha dwells in the heavens with his parents, Kashyapa and Aditi, as part of a group of divine brothers. The oldest legends speak of seven or eight Adityas, and the number was later raised to twelve. Historians have suggested that this figure may have been chosen so that each Aditya corresponded to a month, linking these gods to the cycle of the year and the seasons.

Among the brothers, Varuna stands out as their leader, the one to whom the name Aditya applies most fittingly. Amsha appears in the Rigveda, India's oldest text, where his name is associated with the idea of a share or lot.

Legacy

In modern India, many boys are given names derived from Amsha, such as Amsh or Ams, as a sign of respect for this god. The word is also used in everyday contexts and in the language of inheritance law.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda, the oldest Vedic text, and in the Taittiriya Brahmana.
