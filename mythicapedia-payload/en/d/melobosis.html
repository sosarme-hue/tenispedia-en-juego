Oceanid of Greek mythology

Melobosis

Melobosis is one of the Oceanids, the water nymphs daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, in Greek mythology. Her name means 'sheep-feeder', and she barely appears in the stories, although her presence helps complete the circle of companions around Persephone in the tradition.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Melobosis was one of the three thousand Oceanids, daughters of the Titan Oceanus and his sister and spouse Tethys. Like the rest of these nymphs, she belonged to the world of waters that surrounded the earth, a large lineage whose stories focus more on the group than on individual figures.

Her role in the myths is modest. Melobosis appears among the companions of Persephone, daughter of Demeter, at the moment when Hades abducted her. Together with her sisters she formed part of the group of young women who accompanied the goddess before the abduction. No deeds or individual episodes are attributed to her, and her story remains tied to that of the other Oceanids.
