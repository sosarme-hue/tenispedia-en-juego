water spirit of West Africa and the diaspora

Mami Wata

Mami Wata is a water spirit and goddess venerated across much of Africa and in the Atlantic diaspora, from the Caribbean to the Americas. Linked to wealth, fertility, healing and beauty, her image grew out of encounters with Europeans and was later shaped by Hindu imagery.

The myth

Accounts describe her as a fair-skinned woman with long hair and superhuman beauty, able to command oceans and rivers and to transform in order to seduce humans. In a Nigerian tradition, she appears to men as an attractive prostitute; afterwards she demands fidelity and secrecy. If the man agrees, she grants fortune and health; if not, ruin falls on his family, finances and work.

In Liberia, according to Ronald Wintrob's research, people believed that anyone in contact with her grew rich, though in some places wealth required sexual celibacy, and the ability to reach her was thought to be inherited. Some scholars link her to the Portuguese sereia and to the European mermaids West Africans encountered from the 15th century.

Worship and religion

Devotees honour her at shrines where mirrors are the main offering, since she is said to be fond of gazing at herself and so draws her attention to worshippers. Rituals aim to let the goddess manifest in the faithful, who may imagine themselves into her world to embody her powers. Her cult remains alive in several African regions and in diaspora communities.

Legacy

Her image as a mermaid and an exotic beauty survives in paintings, popular lithographs and prints, and has shaped African and Caribbean popular culture.
