scaly dragon of Chinese mythology

Jiaolong

The jiaolong is a serpent-like dragon from Chinese mythology, described as a scaled being that lives in rivers and waters. Its image is often confused with that of a crocodile, and its name has been translated in many different ways.

The myth

Ancient sources describe it as an aquatic creature with a serpentine body, a small head and a slender neck. According to the Shanhaijing, it lived in rivers such as the Kuang and the Lun, and the commentator Guo Pu claimed that large specimens could swallow a person whole. Tradition held that its eggs hatched on dry land and that, after a thousand years, the jiao turned into a dragon.

In Sima Qian's Shiji, a jiaolong hovers above the mother of the future emperor Liu Bang as she gives birth. Some scholars believe it was in fact a pair of dragons entwined, rather than a single creature.

Legacy

Its name has been rendered in English as kraken, flood dragon or scaly dragon. The sinologist Edward Schafer proposed kraken, since it suggests a powerful sea monster. In Vietnamese it is called giao long.

Ancient sources: Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas, c. 206–9 BCE), Shuowen Jiezi (121 CE) and Sima Qian's Shiji.

Trivia

Protective tattoos. Ancient texts say the Yue people tattooed their bodies to protect themselves from these aquatic monsters.
Crossed eyebrows. According to the Records of Strange Things (6th century), the name comes from eyebrows that cross over.
