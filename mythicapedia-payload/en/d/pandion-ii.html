Pandion II

Pandion II was, according to Greek tradition, a legendary king of Athens, son of Cecrops II and father of Aegeus, Pallas, Nisos and Lycus. He matters mainly as a link in the royal succession of Athens and as the ancestor of several branches of the royal family and of an Attic tribe.

The myth

According to most accounts, Pandion succeeded Cecrops II, son of Erechtheus. The sons of his uncle Metion stirred up a revolt that drove him out of Athens, and Pandion fled to Megara. There he married Pylia, also called Pelea, daughter of King Pylas. Later Pylas handed the throne of Megara to his son-in-law and went off to the Peloponnese, where he founded the city of Pylos after killing his uncle Bias.

Pandion's four sons were born in Megara: Aegeus, Pallas, Nisos and Lycus. Some said Aegeus was really the son of Sciron, but that Pandion had raised him as his own. When Pandion died, his sons returned to Athens, drove out the Metionids and divided power into four shares, though Aegeus kept the main authority. Strabo says Pandion assigned each son a territory: the coast to Aegeus, the garden facing Euboea to Lycus, the land next to Sciron's territory in Megara to Nisos, and the southern land to Pallas.

Scholars disagree about whether he really existed. Pausanias makes him the father of Procne and Philomela, who others assign to an earlier Pandion, and some think he was invented to fill a gap in the chronology of Athenian kings. Castor of Rhodes gives his reign as twenty-five years.

Legacy

One of the Attic tribes, the Pandionis, took its name from a Pandion, although authors do not always specify whether Pandion I or Pandion II is meant.

Ancient sources: The Parian Chronicle, Castor of Rhodes, Pausanias, the Bibliotheca of Apollodorus and Strabo.

Trivia

A disputed father. Pausanias presents him as the father of Procne and Philomela, but other authors give these daughters to an earlier Pandion, which has led scholars to suspect a single Pandion was split into two.
An adopted son. Some accounts state that Aegeus was the son of Sciron and that Pandion passed him off as his own.
