mythical founder of Ialysos on Rhodes

Ialysus

Ialysus is, in Greek mythology, a king of the island of Rhodes and the eponymous founder of the city of Ialysos. His story links the origins of Rhodes with Homeric tradition and with the island's artistic history.

The myth

According to tradition, Ialysus was the eldest son of Cercaphus and Cydippe. After his father's death, he succeeded to the throne together with his brothers Camirus and Lindus, and the three divided the kingdom. Each founded a city bearing his name, and Ialysus's share became the city of Ialysos.

Ancient sources recall that the fearsome Telchines lived in Ialysos, which earned them the epithet Ialysian. Homer mentions the city's inhabitants among those who went to the siege of Troy.

Legacy

Ialysus is the subject of a famous painting by Protogenes, which was greatly admired in antiquity; the painter Apelles held it in special reverence. According to Pliny, the work once saved the Rhodians from being put to death by Demetrius.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions the inhabitants of Ialysos in the Iliad, and Pliny the Elder records the anecdote of Protogenes' painting.

Trivia

A painting that saved a city. According to Pliny, the admiration for Protogenes' painting of Ialysus protected the Rhodians from an attack by Demetrius.
