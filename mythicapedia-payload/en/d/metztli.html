Mexica goddess of the Moon

Metztli

Metztli is the lunar deity of the Mexica tradition, whose name means 'moon'. She was linked to the night, water, fertility and farming, which gave her an important place in the calendar and in the lives of her people.

The myth

Sources describe Metztli as a deity who came to be identified with the Moon, a figure that Indigenous peoples often paired with the Sun in their view of the cosmos. According to these traditions, the goddess controlled the water of the sky, which was kept in the belly of a serpent that accompanied her. From it, storms or floods could be unleashed.

The same power that brought rain could also bring disaster. For this reason she was associated with death, linked to catastrophes caused by water, and she was shown wearing a skirt decorated with crossed bones. At the same time, she protected pregnant women and was credited with inventing weaving, so her image combined the destructive and the beneficial.

Worship and religion

The sources do not detail temples, festivals or specific rites devoted to Metztli, so her worship is poorly documented.

Legacy

It is said that her name may have given rise to the root that later formed the name of Mexico, a hypothesis not all researchers accept.

Trivia

A name with an echo. It has been proposed that her name may have given rise to the root that became Mexico, though this remains a hypothesis.
Moon and serpent. The serpent that accompanied her kept the water of the sky in its belly, linking the Moon with rain.
