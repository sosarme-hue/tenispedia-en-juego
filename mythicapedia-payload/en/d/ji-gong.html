Chan Buddhist monk turned Chinese folk deity

Ji Gong

Ji Gong (濟公), also known as Daoji, was a Chan Buddhist monk of the Southern Song dynasty whom Chinese tradition turned into a folk saint and deity. He is admired for his kindness and his rebellious defiance of injustice.

The myth

According to tradition, Li Xiuyuan was born in 1130 to a former military adviser and, after losing his parents, was ordained at Lingyin Temple in Hangzhou, where he received the name Daoji, «Helper on the Way».

His behaviour scandalised the monks: he ate meat, drank wine and wore tattered robes. Expelled from the monastery, he wandered the streets helping the sick and the oppressed. He was said to have gained supernatural powers and to be an incarnation of an arhat.

Worship and religion

After his death he was revered as a popular deity, and syncretic Taoism adopted him. Since at least 1869, mediums have invoked him through spirit writing, and his grave at Hupao Temple in Hangzhou still draws devotees. The Tung Cheng Yuen Association worships him today.

Legacy

He has appeared in countless films and television series since 1939, usually smiling, carrying a wine bottle and a fan.
