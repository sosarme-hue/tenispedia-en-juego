son of Aeneas and legendary founder of Alba Longa

Ascanius

Ascanius, also called Iulus or Julus, is the son of Aeneas and Creusa and a key figure in Roman mythology. He is regarded as an ancestor of the Roman people and of the gens Iulia, the family of Julius Caesar.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, after the fall of Troy Ascanius fled with his father Aeneas and his grandfather Anchises, while his mother Creusa died during the escape. After a long journey they reached Latium, where Aeneas, following a fierce war with the Rutulians and Latins, took power. Aeneas founded the city of Lavinium in honour of his wife Lavinia, and after his death, deified as Indiges, Ascanius took charge of the community.

The story goes that Ascanius, now fatherless, moved to the Alban Hills and founded Alba Longa, becoming its first king. According to Livy, his successor Silvius was his son; according to Dionysius of Halicarnassus, they were half brothers. Dionysius also gives his original name as Euryleon, while Virgil calls him Iulus, the name from which the Julian family claimed descent, and through which Romulus and Remus were said to descend.

Legacy

Virgil makes him a central figure of the Aeneid, where as a youth he shoots the arrow that kills Numanus. Apollo then addresses him with the phrase sic itur ad astra, which became proverbial. Augustus and the Julian family claimed descent from him, and the American motto annuit coeptis comes from a prayer to Jupiter in the same poem.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, who disagree about his parentage and his relationship with Silvius.

Trivia

A name with history. Virgil popularised the name Iulus instead of Ascanius, linking the Julian family of Rome with older mythology.
A national motto. The motto annuit coeptis, found on the seal of the United States, comes from Ascanius's prayer to Jupiter in the Aeneid.
