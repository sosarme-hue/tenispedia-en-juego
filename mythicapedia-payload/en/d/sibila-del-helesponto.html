Greek prophetess of the Apollonian oracle at Dardania

Hellespontine Sibyl

The Hellespontine Sibyl was a Greek prophetess who, according to tradition, presided over the Apollonian oracle at Dardania. Also called the Trojan Sibyl, she became especially famous in late Roman times and the early Middle Ages.

The myth

According to tradition, she was born in the village of Marpessos, near the small town of Gergis, during the lifetimes of Solon and Cyrus the Great. Heraclides of Pontus placed Marpessos within the boundaries of the Troad, the region around Troy. The word sibyl comes via Latin from the Greek sibylla, meaning prophetess or oracle.

Worship and religion

Her collection of oracles was kept in the temple of Apollo at Gergis and later passed to Erythrae, where it became renowned. As a priestess, she presided over the Apollonian oracle at Dardania, linking her to the cult of the god of prophecy.

Legacy

In late antiquity and the early Middle Ages, a claim circulated that she had predicted the crucifixion of Jesus Christ. The idea comes from the Sibylline Oracles, which should not be confused with the Sibylline Books.

Ancient sources: Heraclides of Pontus places her birthplace at Marpessos, within the Troad, and the Sibylline Oracles record the prophecy about the crucifixion.
