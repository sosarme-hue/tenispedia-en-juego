Trojan princess in Greek mythology

Cilla

Cilla is a Trojan princess in Greek mythology, daughter of Laomedon. Her story is tied to the birth of Paris and, with it, to the origins of the Trojan War.

The myth

According to tradition, Cilla was the daughter of Laomedon and a nymph named Estrimon, although other authors make her the daughter of Leucippe, daughter of Scamander, or of Plakia, daughter of Otreus. When Priam came to power in Troy, Cilla secretly married Timoetes, the king's brother, and had a son, Munippus.

Around that time, Hecuba, Priam's wife, dreamed that she gave birth to serpents instead of a child. The king consulted his son Aesacus, a seer, who prophesied that the newborn would bring about the city's downfall. When Hecuba gave birth to Paris on the same day Cilla's son was born, Aesacus persuaded Priam to kill the newborns along with their mothers. The king lacked the courage to kill his wife, so he killed Munippus and Cilla instead, and they were buried in the sanctuary of Troo.

Some authors, however, present Cilla as Priam's wife, with Munippus considered the son of them both.

Trivia

A dream of serpents. Hecuba dreamed she gave birth to serpents instead of a child, an omen that tradition links to the fate of Troy.
A city of Apollo. The name Cilla also designates a city dedicated to Apollo, according to geographical references.
