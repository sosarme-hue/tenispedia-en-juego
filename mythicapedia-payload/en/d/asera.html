Canaanite goddess of fertility

Asherah

Asherah was a leading goddess of the Northwest Semitic peoples, especially the Canaanites, linked to fertility, motherhood and sacred trees. The Bible calls her Astoret, while the Greeks knew her as Astarte.

The myth

In the Ugaritic texts, Asherah is the wife of El, the chief god, and bears titles such as “lady of the sea” and “progenitress of the gods”. For this reason she is considered the mother of Baal, although accounts differ from one place to another. Among the Babylonians she is identified with Ishtar, and some comparisons link her with Ninhursag. Her children are said to be Shapshu and Shalim.

In the Amarna letters she appears as Ashirtu and Ashratu, and her roles seem to have been varied, with parallels to Anat. The plural form of her name in some biblical passages may suggest that each town had its own version of the goddess.

Worship and religion

Her cult was widespread in Canaan and, according to the Bible, among some Israelites too. Worshippers honored her beside wooden stakes or tree trunks set up in temple courtyards, known as asherah poles. People burned incense, poured libations and baked cakes shaped like her, and the Bible calls her “Queen of Heaven”. After the reforms of Josiah her worship was removed from the official Yahwist cult, and it faded away along with the older religions of the region.

Legacy

Her name appears on the plinth of Judy Chicago’s The Dinner Party, and the asteroid 214 Aschera is named after her.

Ancient sources: The Ugaritic tablets (the Baal Cycle), the Amarna letters and the Hebrew Bible, which mentions her in Judges, Kings and Jeremiah.

Trivia

A distorted name. According to rabbinic tradition, biblical scribes took the vowels of the Hebrew word boset, “shame”, to turn Asherah into Astoret.
A tree as an image. Her most common symbol was a stake or tree trunk, and the word asherah came to name these sacred objects as well.
