stallion of Norse mythology

Svaðilfari

Svaðilfari is, in Norse mythology, a stallion that, according to the tradition, fathered the eight-legged horse Sleipnir together with Loki, who had taken the form of a mare. His story matters because it explains the origin of Odin's horse.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, Svaðilfari belonged to an unnamed builder, a giant who offered to raise a fortified wall for the gods after they had settled Midgard and built Valhalla. In return he asked for Freyja, the Sun and the Moon. The gods agreed, but with conditions: the work had to be finished in three seasons without help from any man. The giant asked to use his horse, and Loki, who spoke in his favour, secured permission. Svaðilfari doubled his master's strength and hauled huge rocks, to the surprise of the gods.

With three days left before the deadline, the wall was nearly finished. The gods blamed Loki, who had advised the bargain, and threatened him with a dreadful death unless he found a way to stop the builder from being paid. Loki swore he would find a solution. That night, as the giant led Svaðilfari out to fetch a stone, a mare appeared from the woods and neighed. The stallion became frantic, broke his harness and chased her into the forest, with the giant following. The two horses ran all night, and the building work came to a standstill.

When the Æsir realised the builder was a hrimthurs giant, they broke their oath and called on Thor, who crushed his skull with Mjölnir. Some time later, Loki gave birth to a grey foal with eight legs: Sleipnir, the finest horse among gods and men, later ridden by Odin. The Völuspá hin skamma, preserved within the Hyndluljóð, instead credits Svaðilfari as Sleipnir's father.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) and the Völuspá hin skamma, contained within the Hyndluljóð.

Trivia

A telling name. The Old Norse name can be translated as 'the one making an unlucky journey' or 'unlucky traveller', a reference to the horse's misfortune during the night of the chase.
A horse with two fathers. According to the Gylfaginning, Sleipnir is born of Loki, while the Völuspá hin skamma credits Svaðilfari as his father.
