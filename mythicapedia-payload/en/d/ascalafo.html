son of Ares, hero of Greek mythology

Ascalaphus

Ascalaphus is a hero of Greek mythology, a son of the war god Ares whose name is tied to the voyage of the Argonauts and to the Trojan War. He illustrates how Greek myths wove heroes into the lives of the gods and into great collective campaigns.

The myth

According to tradition, Ascalaphus was the son of Ares and Astyoche, daughter of King Actor of Orchomenus, although some accounts make him the son of Lycus and Pernis, as recorded by Hyginus in his Fabulae. He had a twin brother, Ialmenus, and the two were both counted among the Minyan rulers and heroes.

The twins sailed with the Argonauts. Ascalaphus was later among the suitors of Helen and, like the others, swore to come to the aid of whichever husband she chose should he ever be attacked, a pledge that came due when Paris carried Helen off.

Ascalaphus led thirty ships of the Orchomenian contingent to the Trojan War. There he was killed by Deiphobus, a death that angered Ares, who wanted to join the fighting more directly. Another tradition holds that he survived the war, traveled to Samarea and was buried there by his father.

Ancient sources: Hyginus preserves the alternative genealogy in his Fabulae; the accounts of the Argonauts and the Trojan War come down through the Greek epic tradition.
