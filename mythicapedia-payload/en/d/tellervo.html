Finnish goddess of the forest, daughter of Tapio

Tellervo

Tellervo is a forest deity of Finnish mythology, daughter of Tapio, the lord of the forest, and of Mielikki. She is known mainly from runic songs, which called on her for help in hunting and for protecting livestock.

The myth

According to tradition, Tellervo is the daughter of Tapio and Mielikki, and she is described as always dressed in gold and silver. Her name appears in several forms, such as Tillervo or Tellervö, and scholars have proposed different origins. Kaarle Krohn linked it to Pellervo, an agricultural god, arguing that the name changed to match Tapio's alliteration. M. A. Castrén and Martti Haavio, by contrast, thought it developed from Hillervo, the Mother of Otters, a name found in just one runic song and first recorded by Christfried Ganander in 1786.

Worship and religion

In runic songs she is invoked in Kainuu, North Karelia, the Karelian Isthmus and White Karelia. There, people asked her for help hunting bears, finding a cure for an illness that came from the forest, and protecting cattle. No records survive of temples or an organised cult.

Ancient sources: Finnish and Karelian runic songs, collected in modern times after centuries of oral transmission.
