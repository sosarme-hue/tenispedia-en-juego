Connacht warrior of Irish mythology

Nera

A warrior of Connacht in the Ulster Cycle, the hero of the Echtra Nerai, a medieval Irish tale about the dead and the otherworld.

The myth

One Samhain night, at the court of Ailill and Medb in Cruachan, the king offered a prize to anyone who would fasten a wicker band around the ankle of a hanged corpse. Only Nera dared. After two failed attempts, the corpse spoke and advised him to use a peg. It asked for water, and Nera carried it on his back to a house ringed with flames; at a second house, water surrounded it; at the third he got inside, and the corpse drank and spat on the householders, who died. Back at court, the hall was burning and everyone had been beheaded.

Nera saw an army enter the Cave of Cruachan and followed it. Inside the hill, a woman of the sidhe told him that the scene was a vision of what would happen next Samhain unless the warriors destroyed the hill and defeated the sidhe. When he returned, no time had passed. In some versions he spends a year there, marries, and fathers a child, and he convinces the kings with summer flowers: garlic, primrose and golden fern. Ailill rewards him with a sword. Afterwards the warriors defeat the sidhe, and Nera recovers three treasures from their world.

Ancient sources: Echtra Nerai (The Adventure of Nera), a Middle Irish tale from the 10th century.

Trivia

Gate to Hell. The Cave of Cruachan was also known as Ireland's Gate to Hell, the place where the sidhe army went in.
Night of the dead. Samhain was considered a night when the dead have power, which is why only Nera dared take the challenge.
