Gaulish goddess of war

Andarta

Andarta was a Gaulish goddess of war, known only from Roman-era sources and from inscriptions in southern France. Her name gave rise to the town of Die.

The myth

Accounts of her are scarce. Cassius Dio identifies her as a war goddess under the name Andrasta in the British Isles, where she was reportedly venerated among the Iceni of Queen Boudica.

Her name is debated. The traditional reading links it to the Gaulish word for 'bear', artos, while the linguist Blanca María Prósper Pérez proposes a meaning like 'well fixed' or 'staying firm', and finds the bear connection with the bear goddess Artio unconvincing.

Worship and religion

Her cult emerged at Die toward the end of the 1st century AD. Seven votive inscriptions dedicated to Dea Augusta Andarta survive, dated to about 150-200 AD. The cult of Cybele later arrived in the area, and the two may have merged or coexisted. No living cult remains today.

Legacy

The town of Die takes its name from her cult: the Latin word dea, 'goddess', came to designate the settlement, and in Occitan it is called Diá.

Ancient sources: Cassius Dio, who mentions her as Andrasta, and the seven votive inscriptions found at Die.

Trivia

A town named after her. The French town of Die preserves an echo of her cult, since its name comes from the Latin word for 'goddess'.
A name with a bear behind it. The traditional reading of her name links it to the Great Bear and the Gaulish word for bear, though this is now disputed.
