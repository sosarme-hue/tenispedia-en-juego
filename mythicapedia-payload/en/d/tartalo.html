man-eating giant of Basque folklore

Tartalo

Tartalo is a man-eating giant of Basque folklore, known for his single eye, his magic ring and his fatal fall from a cliff. His stories belong to the folklore of the Iberian Peninsula and were passed down mainly through oral tradition.

The myth

According to Basque tradition, Tartalo is a giant of enormous size and insatiable appetite who lived in mountains such as Erreniega, near Zizur Mayor, or Aitxuri, in Cegama. His favourite pastime was hurling stones from one mountain to another, and legend credits this game with the origin of some structures that still stand.

Unlike the jentilak, Tartalo is wicked and savage, feeding on sheep, children and sometimes adults. His magic ring gave away his prey: when he called out 'Non dago?' ('Where are you?'), the ring answered 'Hemen nago' ('Here I am').

In one tale, two brothers take shelter from the rain in his cave and are trapped. The younger steals the ring, blinds Tartalo with a roasting spit and escapes hidden among the sheep. The ring gives him away, and to be rid of it the boy cuts off his own finger at the edge of a cliff. Tartalo, following its cries, plunges over the edge.

Trivia

A ring with a Scottish echo. Webster noted that the ring motif has a parallel in Conall Cra Bhuidhe, a Scottish Gaelic tale published by John Francis Campbell.
An eye with classical parallels. The blinding of the giant recalls Polyphemus in Homer's Odyssey, and also the spear Lug hurls into the eye of Balor in Irish tradition.
