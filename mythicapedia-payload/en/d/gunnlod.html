giantess of Norse mythology, guardian of the mead of poetry

Gunnlod

Gunnlod (also Gunnlöð) is a jötunn of Norse mythology, daughter of the giant Suttung. She guarded the mead of poetry, and her story with Odin is one of the best-known accounts of how the god obtained wisdom.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál, Suttung entrusted his daughter with guarding the mead of poetry, kept in a chamber inside the mountain Hnitbjörg. Odin, taking the form of a snake, got inside. He seduced Gunnlod, spent three nights with her and, in return, was allowed three drinks of the mead. Then, rather than stay, he flew out of the cavern as an eagle.

The Hávamál, part of the Poetic Edda, differs in several details and pays much more attention to Gunnlod herself. In the poem, the giantess seats him on a golden seat and pours him the precious mead. Odin says that thanks to her he gained wisdom and brought to Asgard the sacred drink called Odhroerir. He adds that he would never have escaped alive from the grip of a fierce troll had Gunnlod not helped him, wrapping her arms around him.

The tradition thus preserves a story of deceit and debt: the poem acknowledges the giantess's role, while Snorri's version presents her mainly as the victim of the god's cunning.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons is named after her. The story also bears a striking resemblance to 'Jackie Blue', a song by the American band Ozark Mountain Daredevils. Her name has been translated as 'war-invitation' or 'battle-invitation', from an Old Norse word meaning battle.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál (Prose Edda) and the Hávamál of the Poetic Edda.
