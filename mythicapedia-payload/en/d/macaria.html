daughter of Heracles in Greek mythology

Macaria

Macaria is a heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of Heracles and Deianira, who offered herself as a sacrifice to save Athens and her siblings, the Heraclidae. Her story, known mainly from Euripides' tragedy, makes her a model of courage and self-giving.

The myth

According to the tradition, after Heracles' death, King Eurystheus pursued his children, the Heraclidae, who fled with Macaria and Iolaus. They were turned away at Trachis and made their way to Athens, where King Demophon, son of Theseus, gave them refuge.

Eurystheus demanded that the children be handed over and threatened war. An oracle then declared that Athens would win only if a maiden of noble birth were sacrificed to Persephone. Macaria volunteered, judging it nobler to die on the altar than to live as a coward who had doomed the city. She declined Iolaus' proposal to draw lots, and Demophon led her to the altar himself.

Other authors place the family in Trachis, ruled by Ceyx, and attribute the Athenian refuge to Theseus himself. Some later sources also present her as a daughter of Hades, reflecting a confusion between the heroine and a personification of death.

Worship and religion

After her death, the Athenians honoured her with lavish funeral rites. There is no evidence of a stable cult: the Suda treats her as a goddess without worship, close to the idea of a blessed death.

Legacy

A spring near Marathon has borne her name ever since. Her name, meaning 'blessed', gave rise to a Greek proverb, roughly equivalent to 'go to hell', used for those who risked their lives through reckless courage.

Ancient sources: Euripides' Children of Heracles offers the oldest account of her story; the Suda and the sophist Zenobius preserve the proverb linked to her name.

Trivia
