Aetolian leader in the Trojan War

Thoas

Thoas is an Aetolian leader in Greek mythology who, according to the Iliad, led the forty-ship Aetolian contingent in the Trojan War. He is a secondary hero, but respected for his skill with the javelin and his voice in the assembly.

The myth

According to tradition, Thoas was the son of Andraemon and Gorge, daughter of King Oeneus of Calydon, and belonged to the line of the Deucalids, descended from the eponym Aetolus. After the death of old Oeneus, his sons and his grandson Meleager, power over the Aetolians fell to him. Some accounts say that Diomedes restored the kingdom of Calydon to Andraemon, Oeneus's son-in-law.

In the Iliad he appears as leader of the forty-ship Aetolian contingent, skilled with the javelin, staunch in close combat and one of the few who surpass the others in the assembly. He killed two opponents during the siege and, in another episode, killed a Thracian without managing to strip his armour. Other sources name him among the suitors of Helen and among the warriors hidden inside the Wooden Horse. After the war, some say he returned to Aetolia, while others say he settled among the Brutti in Italy.

His son Haemon succeeded him on the throne and was the father of Oxylus. A daughter of his, whose name is not recorded, married Odysseus and bore Leontophonus, a name meaning Lion Slayer. Local tradition in Amphissa, in Western Locris, linked him with that city.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Iliad, and other ancient sources such as the Hesiodic Catalogue of Women, the Little Iliad, the Posthomerica, the Odyssey and Pausanias mention him in later episodes.

Trivia

Leontophonus. His name means Lion Slayer. According to some accounts, he is the son Odysseus had with a daughter of Thoas.
A statue of Athena. Pausanias reported that at Amphissa there was a bronze statue of Athena which, it was said, Thoas had brought from Troy as spoils of war.
