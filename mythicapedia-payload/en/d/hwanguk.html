alleged mythical Korean state

Hwanguk

Hwanguk is, according to the Hwandan Gogi, the first mythical state of Korea, said to predate Gojoseon. It matters mainly as a place in Korean pseudohistory, and because of the debate over whether it existed and how the idea arose from a confusion of characters.

The myth

According to the Hwandan Gogi, Hwanguk was the first mythical state of Korea and existed long before Gojoseon, the kingdom of Dangun. Mainstream Korean historians reject its existence for lack of credible evidence.

Its origin lies in a confusion of characters. In the Samguk Yusa, the mythical grandfather of Dangun appears as Hwanin (桓因), and in some editions the character 因 is written in a form resembling a simplified version of 國, meaning "nation". Il-yeon's footnotes identify Hwanin with Jeseok, the Buddhist name for Indra in Korea, but the resemblance encouraged readings such as "Hwanguk".

In 1918, the historian Choe Nam-seon argued that the term meant "heaven" and designated the abode of the Hwan people, though he later changed his view. In 1966, Moon Jeong Chang defended the idea in a book that blamed Japanese authorities for forging documents.

Ancient sources: The Hwandan Gogi, which places Hwanguk before Gojoseon, and the Samguk Yusa, which mentions Hwanin, the mythical grandfather of Dangun.

Trivia

A character mix-up. The idea of Hwanguk arose from a misreading of the character 因 in the Samguk Yusa, which looked like a simplified form of 國, meaning "nation".
A Japanese edition. In 1921, a Samguk Yusa edition published by Kyoto University corrected the character to 因, which provoked protests from Korean nationalists.
