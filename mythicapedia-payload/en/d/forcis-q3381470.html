Phrygian warrior of the Trojan War

Phorcys

A Phrygian warrior and leader of his people, an ally of King Priam in the Trojan War. He appears in the Iliad as one of the defenders of the city. His figure is one of the least known in the Trojan cycle, and he is known mainly from a few passages in the Iliad and from the Bibliotheca.

The myth

In the Iliad, Phorcys leads the Phrygians and, according to the poem, is a son of Phaenops. The Bibliotheca, however, makes him a son of Aretaon and brother of Ascanius, another Phrygian leader. Hector addresses the Trojan allies, Phorcys among them, in a speech in Book 17 of the Iliad. He is killed in battle by the Greek hero Ajax.

Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, explains why the Iliad calls him shieldless: he wore a two-piece corselet, which was thought to give enough protection in battle.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, which names him as a leader of the Phrygians; the Bibliotheca, which gives a different genealogy; and Pausanias' Description of Greece.

Trivia

A curious epithet. The Iliad calls him shieldless, a trait Pausanias attributes to a two-piece corselet that protected him in battle.
Two genealogies. The Iliad makes him a son of Phaenops, while the Bibliotheca presents him as a son of Aretaon and brother of Ascanius.
