artisan gods of Vedic Hinduism

Ribhus

The Ribhus are three artisan gods of Hindu tradition, skilled craftsmen who forged Indra's horses and performed other marvels. They appear in the Rig Veda, India's oldest scripture, and their name is linked to the idea of skill and labour.

The myth

According to tradition, the Ribhus are three brothers, Ribhu, Vaja and Vibhvan, though their parentage varies between texts. Some accounts make them sons of Indra and Saranyu; others, sons of Sudhanvan.

Their works include Indra's horses, the chariot of the Ashvins and Brihaspati's miraculous cow, and they also made their parents young again. In the myth, the gods, envious of their skill, asked them to make four cups from a single one. When they succeeded, they were admitted among the devas and received a share of the sacrifices. Each year they rest for twelve days in the house of the Sun.

Worship and religion

They are shown accompanying Indra, especially at the evening sacrifice, and they shared in the offerings humans made to the gods.

Legacy

Their root links them to European words for work, such as Latin labor and German Arbeit.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda, the Atharva Veda, the Vishnu Purana and the Aitareya Brahmana.

Trivia

A name with a European echo. The term ribhu is related to European words for work, such as Latin labor.
Astronomical interpretations. Some scholars associate them with the sun's rays or the seasons of the year, interpretations not all accept.
