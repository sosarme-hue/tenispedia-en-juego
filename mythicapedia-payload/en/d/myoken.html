Buddhist deity of the North Star

Myōken

Myōken is a deity of Japanese Buddhism, worshipped as the deification of the North Star. His cult, of Chinese origin and blended with Taoist beliefs about the Big Dipper, is mainly associated with the Shingon, Tendai and Nichiren schools.

The myth

According to tradition, Myōken introduces himself in the earliest text that mentions him as the bodhisattva of the North Star, named "Wondrous Vision". His name appears in apocryphal Chinese sutras rather than in early Buddhist literature.

A Japanese legend holds that Taira no Masakado was a devotee of Myōken, who aided his military campaigns. When Masakado became proud and arrogant, the deity withdrew his favour and instead supported his uncle Yoshifumi, ancestor of the Chiba clan.

Worship and religion

During the Heian period, pole star worship was so widespread that imperial decrees banned it as ritually impure. It nonetheless left its mark on imperial rituals such as enthronement and on the worship of the imperial clan deity at Ise Shrine. As the Chiba clan's guardian deity, Myōken received numerous temples across their former lands, and after Buddhism and Shinto were separated, some shrines became Amenominakanushi shrines. Veneration continues today in many Nichiren temples.

Legacy

His link to war comes from the worship of Alkaid, the easternmost star of the Big Dipper, known in Chinese as the "Destroyer of Armies". Some depictions of Myōken resemble those of Kisshōten, and his sword-wielding image recalls the Taoist god Xuanwu.

Ancient sources: The earliest text mentioning him is "The Divine Spells of the Great Dhāraṇīs Taught by the Seven Buddhas and Eight Bodhisattvas" (T. 1332), attributed to the Eastern Jin dynasty, together with the Sutra of the Big Dipper for Prolonging Life (T. 1307).

Trivia

A name in doubt. Although his name seems to be of Sanskrit origin, Myōken is absent from early Buddhist literature and first appears in apocryphal Chinese sutras.
A shifting appearance. He is sometimes shown as a youth and sometimes as a stern armoured warrior with a sword, standing or sitting on a cloud, a dragon or a tortoise.
