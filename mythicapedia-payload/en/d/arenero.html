folklore figure who brings sleep and dreams

Sandman

The Sandman is a mythical character from Germanic and Scandinavian folklore, though similar figures appear in British and other European traditions. Each night he sprinkles magical sleep-sand into children's eyes so they drop off and dream, and his figure explains the grit found in the eyes upon waking. That is why his image has spread through literature, music and television around the world.

The myth

According to tradition, the Sandman visits houses in the evening and sprinkles sand into children's eyes, sending them to sleep and bringing them dreams. The grit found on waking was the trace of his visit, and because sand was once used to clean homes, a less common explanation arose: dreams are washed out of memory as if rubbed away. His name simply means "man of sand".

Not all versions are kind. In E. T. A. Hoffmann's Der Sandmann (1816), the protagonist's nurse describes a man who throws sand into the eyes of children who refuse to sleep, pulls out their eyes and carries them to his iron nest on the Moon to feed his own children. In Hans Christian Andersen's Ole Lukøje (1841), by contrast, the figure is gentle: with two umbrellas, one patterned that gives beautiful dreams to good children and a plain one that gives dreamless sleep to naughty ones.

Legacy

His image has become part of popular culture. The German children's series Unser Sandmännchen has been broadcast since 1959, and Neil Gaiman created the comic The Sandman, which Netflix adapted in 2022. In music, "Mister Sandman", written by Pat Ballard in 1954 and popularised by The Chordettes, inspired other songs such as Metallica's "Enter Sandman". In the Netherlands and Belgium he lives on as Klaas Vaak, and in Norway and Sweden as Jon Blund.

Trivia

Danish name. "Lukøje" means "shut-eye", and Ole is a very common Danish first name.
Klaas Vaak at Efteling. In the Netherlands, Klaas Vaak stars in a musical and a TV series, and is one of the entertainers at the Efteling amusement park.
