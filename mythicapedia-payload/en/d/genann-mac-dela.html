legendary high king of Ireland of the Fir Bolg

Genann mac Dela

Genann mac Dela is, according to Irish tradition, a legendary High King of Ireland belonging to the Fir Bolg people. He ruled jointly with his brother Gann and appears in the stories of the island's mythical invasions.

The myth

According to the tales, he was a son of Dela, a descendant of Starn, and belonged to the Fir Bolg. When this people arrived in Ireland, the five sons of Dela divided the island among themselves. Genann landed with his brother Rudraige at Tracht Rudraige, in Dundrum Bay, and took the province of Connacht.

After Rudraige died, Genann and Gann ruled together as High Kings for four years. Both died of plague, along with about two thousand of their followers, and they were succeeded by Sengann. His wife was called Cnucha, and among his descendants is a son named Rinnal.

Ancient sources: Accounts of Genann appear in the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Annals of the Four Masters and Geoffrey Keating's Foras Feasa ar Érinn.

Trivia

Landing at Dundrum. According to the texts, Genann arrived in Ireland through Dundrum Bay, in what is now County Down in the north of the island.
Death by plague. According to tradition, Genann and his brother Gann died of plague, together with about two thousand of their followers.
