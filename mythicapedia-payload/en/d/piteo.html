king of Troezen, wise man and grandfather of Theseus

Pittheus

Pittheus was the king of Troezen, in Argolis, renowned for his wisdom and for being the maternal grandfather and tutor of the Athenian hero Theseus. A member of the Pelopid family, his importance lies mainly in his role in Theseus's birth and upbringing.

The myth

Pittheus was a son of Pelops and Hippodamia, although one source gives his mother's name as Dia, perhaps another name for the same woman. With his brother Troezen, he arrived from Pisatis in Elis and was welcomed by Aetius, son of Anthas, with whom they shared power. After Troezen died, Pittheus ruled alone, joined the villages of Hyperea and Anthea into a city he named Troezen after his brother, and is said to have dispensed justice from a marble throne.

His daughter Aethra was wooed by Aegeus, king of Athens, who had come to Troezen seeking advice on an oracle forbidding him to loosen the neck of his wineskin until he reached Athens. Pittheus understood the oracle, got his guest drunk and had him sleep with Aethra. That same night Aethra also lay with Poseidon, so Theseus could be seen as the son of either. Some accounts say Pittheus spread the story that the god was the father, to give his grandson a divine lineage.

Pittheus raised Theseus at his court. In a local tradition, when Heracles stayed in his house, the seven-year-old Theseus attacked the Nemean lion skin the hero had left on a couch with an axe. Later, when Theseus set out for Athens, Pittheus urged him to avoid the road full of bandits and travel by sea, but Theseus ignored him and cleared the way of brigands. Pittheus also appears in the myth of Hippolytus, Theseus's son, whom he raised and who would succeed him on the throne of Troezen.

Worship and religion

Pittheus was honoured in Troezen as its founding king. Behind the temple on the acropolis lay his tomb, and his judgment seat was still shown there in late times. He dedicated an altar to Themis, goddess of justice, and in a sanctuary of the Muses he taught the art of speech. At Larisa stood a temple of Apollo that Pausanias said he had built after returning from Delphi.

Legacy

Euripides calls Hippolytus the 'nursling of the pure and holy Pittheus', and Plutarch quotes a maxim from Hesiod's Works and Days that was ascribed to Pittheus, showing his fame for wisdom. Aristotle also cited that saying.

Ancient sources: Euripides, Plutarch, Pausanias and the scholia on Euripides mention him; Aristotle cites one of his maxims.

Trivia

A throne of justice. Pittheus was said to dispense justice seated on a marble throne, which was still shown at Troezen together with his tomb in late times.
The lion in the room. According to a Troezenian tradition, the young Theseus attacked with an axe the Nemean lion skin that Heracles had left in his grandfather's house.
