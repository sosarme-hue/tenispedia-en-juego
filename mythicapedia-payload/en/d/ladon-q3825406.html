Greek river god, son of Oceanus and Tethys

Ladon

Ladon is a river god of Greek mythology, son of Oceanus and Tethys, whose name is linked to a river in Arcadia, in the central Peloponnese. He matters mainly because his family tree shows how the stories tied rivers, nymphs and towns of one region together.

The myth

According to tradition, Ladon was one of the many river gods, sons of Oceanus and Tethys, and his river, which flowed through Arcadian territory, bore his name.

The accounts give him several naiad daughters. Metope married the god Asopus, and Telpusa gave her name to a town. Syrinx and Daphne also appear among his descendants, although in some versions Daphne is a daughter of Gaia. A single source names a consort, Stinphalis, perhaps linked to the town of Stymphalus, about whom nothing more is known. A late version makes him the father of Themis, whom the Romans identified with Carmenta.
