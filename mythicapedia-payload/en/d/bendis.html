Thracian goddess of hunting and the moon

Bendis

Bendis was a Thracian goddess of hunting and the moon whom the Greeks identified with Artemis, though she kept traits of a nocturnal deity of her own. Her cult reached Attica around 430 BC and eventually became an official festival of Athens.

The myth

In the tradition, Bendis hunted like Artemis but was linked more closely with the night and with Hecate. She was often shown accompanied by dancing satyrs and maenads, as on a red-figure cup kept in Verona. Thracian theologians addressed her with lunar epithets, among them 'mighty Bendis'.

In Athenian images she wears a short chiton like Artemis, yet also a Thracian cloak fastened with a brooch and tall boots, a sign of how the two traditions blended.

Worship and religion

Around 431 BC, following a decree of the Oracle of Dodona that obliged Athens to grant land, Thracian residents of the city obtained a shrine at Munichia, near the sanctuary of Artemis, and a temple also stood at Piraeus. By Plato's time the Bendideia, with night torch races on horseback, had become an official civic ceremony, with a joint procession of Athenians and Thracians. Outside Attica her cult barely spread.

Legacy

She barely appears beyond a few vases and reliefs, but modern followers have revived her worship.

Ancient sources: Plato's Republic describes the torch races of her festival; Strabo mentions Thracian and Phrygian rites in Athens, and Aeschylus mentions Kotys, a related deity.

Trivia

An Antarctic peak. A peak on the Trinity Peninsula in Antarctica is named after the goddess.
Torch races on horseback. Plato mentions in The Republic the night torch races on horseback held in her honour.
