Hindu goddess, form of Adi Parashakti

Akhilandeshwari

Akhilandeshwari is one of the main forms of Adi Parashakti, the supreme Goddess of Hindu Shaktism, and is regarded as an embodiment of Parvati. Her most famous abode is the Jambukeswarar Temple in Thiruvanaikaval, and she forms the Trishakti triad with Meenakshi and Kamakshi.

The myth

According to tradition, Parvati mocked a penance of Shiva, who sent her down to Earth to meditate. As Akhilandeshwari, she made a lingam from Kaveri river water beneath a Venn Naaval tree, known as the Appu Lingam. Shiva appeared and taught her Shiva Gnana: she received the teaching facing east, while he faced west.

Another tale involves an elephant and a spider, attendants of Shiva who quarrelled. The elephant bathed the lingam while the spider wove a web to shield it, until both died in a clash. Shiva granted them liberation.

Worship and religion

At the temple, the priest dresses as a woman during the noon puja to worship Shiva and the sacred cow Kamadhenu. Because Akhilandeshwari is a student and Shiva her teacher, no marriage between them is celebrated. Her worship continues today.

Legacy

The eighteenth-century composer Muthuswamy Dikshitar wrote three songs for her at this temple.

Trivia

Sri Chakra earrings. According to tradition, Adi Shankara installed earrings bearing the Sri Chakra to calm the goddess's fury, as she was once worshipped only from outside the temple.
Gifts from her brother. During the Tamil month of Margazhi, Ranganatha of Srirangam is believed to send garlands and jewellery to his sister Akhilandeshwari.
