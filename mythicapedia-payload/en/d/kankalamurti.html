terrifying form of Shiva, the god of skeletons

Kankalamurti

Kankalamurti is an iconographic form of Shiva, linked to his fearsome aspect Bhairava. He is widely worshipped in Shiva temples in South India but is almost unknown in the north.

The myth

According to the myth, Shiva, as the fearsome Bhairava, beheaded Brahma, and the creator's fifth head stuck to his palm like a beggar's bowl. To atone for the sin, he wandered as an ascetic and reached the abode of Vishnu, whose gatekeeper Vishvaksena refused him entry. Bhairava killed him and carried the corpse on his shoulder, and so he became Kankalamurti, the one with skeletons.

In Varanasi the sin was expiated: the skull fell from his hand and Vishvaksena was revived.

Worship and religion

He is depicted with four arms, holding a skeleton staff, a drum and a deer, accompanied by women and ghostly bhuta attendants. He appears in Shiva temples across South India, and at Chidambaram Temple he occupies one of the four niches in its gateway towers.

Ancient sources: The Kurma, Vamana and Matsya Puranas narrate the episode of Vishnu's gatekeeper, and the Shaiva Agamic texts and the Shilparatna describe his iconography.

Trivia

Four forms in one temple. The four gateway towers of Chidambaram Temple in Tamil Nadu each hold a niche with one of four forms of Shiva, among them Kankalamurti.
The women in his retinue. Sometimes seven women appear, the wives of the seven great sages, who serve or embrace him. At Lepakshi Temple there is often only one.
