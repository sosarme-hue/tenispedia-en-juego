naiad of Thessaly, mythical ancestor of the Greeks

Orseis

Orseis (also Orseide) is a naiad, a water-nymph linked to Thessaly in Greek myth. She is regarded as the wife of Hellen, the eponymous ancestor of the Greeks, and her story helps explain the origins of the Hellenic peoples.

The myth

According to the tradition, Orseis was a nymph of a spring in Thessaly. She married Hellen, son of Deucalion and Pyrrha and brother of Pandora, as the tradition tells it. Their sons were Dorus, Xuthus and Aeolus. The Library attributed to Apollodorus records this marriage, and the Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, says that the sons of Hellen and Orseis, together with those of Pandora and Zeus (Graecus, Magnes and Makedon), became the founders of the seven primordial tribes of Hellas.

Her name is uncertain. A scholium calls her Otreis or Otreide, and M. L. West suggested that the name may be corrupt for Othryis, a nymph of Mount Othrys. In the myths her role is to give her name to Phthiotis, Hellen's homeland. Vitruvius, by contrast, calls Hellen's wife and the mother of Doros Phthia, perhaps as a variant of the eponymous heroine. No source records who her parents were.

Ancient sources: The Library attributed to Apollodorus, the Catalogue of Women attributed to Hesiod, Vitruvius, and a scholium that records the variant Otreis.

Trivia

Several names. Orseis also appears as Otreis or Otreide in a scholium, and Vitruvius names her Phthia.
Only a few sources. She is barely mentioned in ancient sources, and no author says who her parents were.
