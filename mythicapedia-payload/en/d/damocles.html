Damocles

Damocles is a courtier at the court of Dionysius, tyrant of Syracuse, and the central figure of an ancient Greek moral anecdote that gave rise to the phrase "sword of Damocles", the constant threat hanging over whoever holds power.

The myth

According to the anecdote, Damocles flattered the tyrant and insisted that he was the most fortunate man alive because of his power and wealth. Dionysius offered to swap places with him for a day so he could experience that fortune firsthand. Damocles accepted and enjoyed a lavish banquet, served like a king and surrounded by beautiful attendants. Yet when he looked up, he saw a sharp sword hanging above his head, held by a single horsehair. Having lost his appetite and his desires, he begged the tyrant to let him return to his old life, since he no longer wanted such a dangerous fortune.

The story conveys the idea that power brings lasting anxiety. Cicero attributes it to Dionysius I, but the original version, recorded by Timaeus of Tauromenium, may have referred to his son Dionysius II, a ruler of dissolute habits whose reign ended in his overthrow.

Legacy

The phrase is now used for any danger that hangs visibly and imminently over someone. Shakespeare picks up the theme in Henry IV with "Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown". Kennedy likened the nuclear threat to that sword, and Khrushchev wanted the Tsar Bomba to hang "like the sword of Damocles" over the imperialists. Chaucer mentions it in The Canterbury Tales, and it appears in video games, the TV series NCIS and hip-hop, such as the Fugees' song "Zealots".

Ancient sources: Timaeus of Tauromenium (lost work), cited by Cicero in the Tusculan Disputations (V, 61-62); Horace alludes to the sword in his Odes (III, 1).

Trivia

A 1990 video game. Damocles is a 16-bit video game from 1990 in which the player tries to stop the comet Damocles from destroying a planet.
A virtual reality headset. The first virtual reality headset, developed by Ivan Sutherland in 1968, was called The Sword of Damocles because it hung from the ceiling of the lab where it was built.
