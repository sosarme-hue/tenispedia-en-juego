Greek hero, son of Telamon and lord of Salamis

Ajax the Great

Ajax the Great, also called Telamonian Ajax, is a hero of Greek mythology, son of King Telamon of Salamis and Periboea. He is remembered as the strongest Achaean warrior after Achilles, and his story, tied to the Trojan War and to his tragic end, is one of the best known in the Trojan cycle.

The myth

According to tradition, his birth was foretold by Heracles, a friend of Telamon. When an eagle sent by Zeus landed on the hero's shoulder, Heracles asked that the child be named Ajax, a name linked to the bird. Pindar records this scene, and Hesiod, in The Great Eoiae, tells it with variations. His sons included Eurysaces, born to the captive Tecmessa, and Philaeus, according to other traditions.

In Homer's Iliad he is a towering warrior of colossal strength, carrying an enormous shield of seven oxhides topped with a layer of bronze. He is never wounded in battle and is one of the few principal figures who receives no real divine help. He fought Hector twice: in a duel that ended in a draw, with the two men exchanging gifts, and in the defence of the Greek ships, where he struck Hector with a huge rock. With his half-brother Teucer he protected the body of Patroclus, and later helped recover the body of Achilles.

After Achilles' death, Ajax and Odysseus both claimed his armour. When the prize went to Odysseus, Ajax fell into a rage. In his delirium, which was said to have been caused by Athena, he attacked a flock of sheep, mistaking them for his leaders. When he came to his senses, ashamed, he took his own life with the sword Hector had given him. From his blood sprang a hyacinth flower with the letters AI, the first two of his name and also a cry of grief. Agamemnon prevented his cremation, and when Odysseus journeyed to the underworld, Ajax's shade refused to answer him.

Worship and religion

In Salamis, the Aiantes festivals were held in his honour. The sources consulted do not describe his rites or sanctuaries in more detail.

Legacy

He appears in the film Troy (2004), played by Tyler Mane, and in an episode of the television series Hercules: The Legendary Journeys. He is a character in the strategy game Age of Mythology and in Total War Saga: Troy. The Peruvian poet Jorge Eduardo Eielson wrote the poem Ajax in Hell, and Amsterdam's football club Ajax, which bears his name, shows his figure on its crest. The hero's shield also inspires the 'Rho Aias' barrier in Fate/Stay Night.

Ancient sources: Homer describes him in the Iliad, the Epic Cycle and the Little Iliad recount his madness and death, and Sophocles wrote the tragedy Ajax. Pindar and Hesiod record the story behind his name.

Trivia

The letters on the hyacinth. According to tradition, the flower that sprang from Ajax's blood bore the letters AI on its petals, the first two letters of his Greek name.
A name on an Etruscan tomb. On an Etruscan tomb dedicated to Racvi Satlnei in Bologna, from the 5th century BC, an inscription refers to the family of Telamonian Ajax.
