High King of Ireland of the Fir Bolg

Eochaid mac Eirc

Eochaid mac Eirc is a High King of Ireland in Irish mythology, a member of the Fir Bolg people. His reign and his death at the hands of the Morrigan make him a key figure in the struggle between the Fir Bolg and the Tuatha Dé Danann.

The myth

According to tradition, Eochaid was a son of Erc and a grandson of Rinnal, of the Fir Bolg. He became High King of Ireland after overthrowing Fodbgen, and he is credited with being the first king to set up a system of justice in the island. His reign was one of plenty: no rain fell, only dew, and the harvest came every year.

He was married to the goddess Tailtiu, in whose honour he named the capital, today Teltown in County Meath, where a festival was held every August. After ten years on the throne, the Fir Bolg were defeated by the Tuatha Dé Danann in the first Battle of Magh Tuiredh. During the fighting, Eochaid was tormented by thirst, because the druids of the Tuatha Dé used magic to hide every water source from him. As he searched for water, he was found and killed by the Morrigan on the strand at Beltra, in County Sligo. According to tradition, he was buried under Eochy's Cairn.

Trivia

A kingdom without rain. During his reign no rain fell, only dew, and the harvests came back every year.
A capital named after a goddess. Eochaid named his capital after Tailtiu. The site corresponds to today's Teltown in County Meath.
