Finnish god of waters and the sea

Ahti

Ahti is the water god of Finnish mythology, lord of the sea and the lakes. In the runic songs he gives fish to fishermen and helps shipwrecked sailors, which makes him one of the best-known water figures in Finnish folklore.

The myth

In the runic songs of Kainuu he is asked to give fish, and in the Kalevala he appears as Ahto, so he is not confused with Ahti Saarelainen. According to the poem, he lives at the bottom of the sea, in his sea-castle Ahtola, with his wife Vellamo. The Sampo ends up in his possession, and Ahto refuses to return it until Väinämöinen charms him with his magic harp.

Another tale tells how Ahto dived to recover a shepherd's lost knife. He first found a gold knife and then a silver one, which the shepherd refused, and finally the right knife, so he rewarded the shepherd with all three.

Worship and religion

He was invoked in fishing and hunting, and healing charms asked for Ahti's sacred water, which had to be 'bought' with gold and silver. The sources do not describe his own temples or festivals.

Legacy

A Karelian song about a fish that turns out to be a daughter of Vellamo was one of the sources Elias Lönnrot used to create the character of Aino in the Kalevala.

Ancient sources: He is first mentioned in writing in 1551 by Mikael Agricola, and later in Finnish runic songs and in Lönnrot's Kalevala.

Trivia

A much-debated name. Scholars have proposed Greek, North Germanic, Sami and even Christian origins for the name Ahti, without agreeing on one.
Two names for one god. To avoid confusion with Ahti Saarelainen, the Kalevala calls him Ahto.
