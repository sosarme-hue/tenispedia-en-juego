Triple Goddess

The Triple Goddess is a deity, or divine archetype, with three female aspects, central to many Neopagan traditions, especially Wicca. Her image as Maiden, Mother and Crone, linked to the phases of the moon, became popular through Robert Graves and other twentieth-century writers.

The myth

Ancient sources contain no single myth of a Triple Goddess, but they do have female triads: the Charites, the Horae, the Moirai and the Hindu Tridevi. According to Robert Graves, Hecate was the oldest triple moon goddess, and Diana (Artemis) was also seen as three aspects: huntress, moon and goddess of the underworld. Ovid and Lucan show witches invoking a three-formed goddess, and in the 3rd century Porphyry linked Hecate's three forms to the phases of the moon.

The Maiden, Mother and Crone image does not appear in ancient sources for Hecate or most of these triads. It was the synthesis of Jane Ellen Harrison in the early twentieth century, and Robert Graves in The White Goddess (1948), that made it popular.

Worship and religion

There was no general cult of a Triple Goddess in antiquity. At Stymphalos, Hera was worshipped as a girl, a grown woman and a widow. Modern Neopagans venerate her as a principal deity and, in Wicca, she is the feminine half of a duotheistic system alongside the Horned God. Marija Gimbutas proposed a prehistoric veneration of a 'great goddess' in Neolithic Europe, a thesis much debated by archaeologists.

Legacy

Her influence reaches feminism, literature, Jungian psychology and literary criticism. Graves made her a central figure in modern poetry, and his work remains a reference point in Neopaganism.

Ancient sources: Lucan (Pharsalia), Ovid (Metamorphoses) and Porphyry (On Images), plus the Orphic texts on the Moirai reported by the grammarian Epigenes.

Trivia

Triform Hecate. At first Hecate was shown only in single form. She became triform in the late 5th century BC, although all three forms were usually shown at the same age.
Hera at Stymphalos. In the city of Stymphalos, Hera was worshipped as a girl, a grown woman and a widow, one of the ancient examples of a goddess seen in three stages.
