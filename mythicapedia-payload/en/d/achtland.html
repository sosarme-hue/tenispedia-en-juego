mortal queen of Irish mythology

Achtland

Achtland is a queen of Irish mythology, a mortal woman who married one of the Tuatha Dé Danann and gained immortality by crossing into the land of the síde. Her story is one of the few in Celtic tradition centred on a woman's desire and love.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Achtland was a mortal woman who was notoriously hard to please: none of the men available to her could ever satisfy her. When one of the Tuatha Dé Danann, the people of the goddess Danu, approached her, her heart was won. She married him and, by crossing into the land of the síde to become his wife, she became immortal.

The stories dwell with particular fondness on a simple pleasure: brushing her husband's long, silken hair, which gave her great delight.

Trivia

A name with ancient roots. Her name appears to derive from the Proto-Celtic *Hek-to-landā, meaning roughly «cattle-adorned pasture».
A Romano-British form. It was probably recorded in Romano-British use as *Hectolanda, suggesting the name circulated beyond Ireland.
