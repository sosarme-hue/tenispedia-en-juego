Hippogriff

The hippogriff is a fantastic creature with the body of a horse and the head and wings of an eagle, invented by Ludovico Ariosto in his Renaissance epic Orlando furioso. Although earlier artistic images exist, its name and its role as a magical steed come from that work.

The myth

In Ariosto's poem, the hippogriff is born from a mare and a griffin, a union the poet presents as something considered impossible. It is extraordinarily fast and can fly around the world. Its first owner was the sorcerer Atlante, and later the knight Ruggiero rode it to free the beautiful Angelica.

Astolfo also borrows it to travel to the Moon, where he must search for Orlando's lost wits. As a symbol of unruly impulses, the creature links heroic deeds with reckless adventure.

Legacy

The hippogriff was often illustrated in the nineteenth century, especially by Gustave Doré. It appears in heraldry, though rarely, and in modern works such as role-playing games, video games and fantasy novels. Its best-known modern version is Buckbeak, a character in the Harry Potter saga.

Ancient sources: Virgil alludes to its origin in his Eclogues through the union of griffins and mares, without naming it. Pliny the Elder describes it without giving it a name.

Trivia

A late name. The English word hippogriff was adopted shortly before 1615, while in 1591 John Harington translated it as “Griffith horse”.
A fairground monster. In 1904, pranksters at Lake George in New York State displayed a fake wooden creature called “The Hippogriff”.
