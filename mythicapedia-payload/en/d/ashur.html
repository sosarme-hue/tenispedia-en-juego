national god of the Assyrians

Ashur

Ashur, also spelled Assur, was the national god of Assyria, and his name gave its name to the city and the empire. At first he was linked to plant life and later to war, royal power and the order of the world.

The myth

According to Assyrian tradition, Ashur was the god of totality and the creator of the sky, An, as well as lord of the underworld. Ancient texts describe him as a creator god, comparable to the Babylonian Marduk, although the myth placing him at the origin of the world survives mainly in versions adapted for the Assyrian court.

The sources do not agree on his family. Some pair him with Ishtar and name Ninurta as his son, while other texts stress that, unlike Sumerian and Babylonian gods, Ashur had no family and was associated with Mullisu, the traditional consort of Enlil. In later iconography he is usually shown as an archer within a winged disc, rather than as the tree he originally represented.

Worship and religion

The Assyrian king served him as his servant. Every year the king went to the temple of Ashur in the religious capital, the city of Assur, to celebrate the New Year festival, which renewed the order of heaven and earth. After each campaign the ruler offered him the richest part of the booty and reported his victories in writing; the text was read aloud to the people and then deposited in the temple archives. Prisoners could be given as sacrifices. In oaths and ordeals his weapon, a dagger or sword, served as a divine symbol. His cult remained confined to Assyria and was not moved into temples in conquered lands. Among Assyrian Christians today, Ashur is still used as another name for Jesus.

Legacy

His image appears on the Assyrian flag, alongside the god Shamash. Many Assyrian kings included his name in their own, such as Ashurbanipal and Ashur-uballit II. His figure influenced how the Book of Isaiah portrays Yahweh in relation to Assyria. In popular culture his name has been used for video game characters in Fallout 3, Vampire: The Masquerade and Sacrifice.

Ancient sources: Assyrian royal inscriptions, Old Assyrian treaties and the Assyrian version of the Epic of Creation, which his priests adapted to place him above Marduk.

Trivia

A flag with a god. The Assyrian flag shows Ashur alongside the god Shamash, who in a modern interpretation of the pair appears as the Son.
A name that changed. Under Sennacherib his name was often written as Anshar, and in Uruk he continued to be revered under that form after the fall of the Neo-Assyrian Empire.
