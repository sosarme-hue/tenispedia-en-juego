place in Norse mythology, the land of Gaul

Valland

In Norse legend, Valland is the name given to the part of Europe inhabited by Celtic and Romance-speaking peoples, and it is the setting for several tales in that tradition.

The myth

According to the Flateyjarbók, two kings of Valland, Auði and Kjárr, may be a late reflection of Julius Caesar and the Roman emperors. Kjárr and his daughter Ölrún also appear in the Völundarkviða, where she is a Valkyrie who marries the hero Egil.

In Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla, Valland is the Old Norse name for Gaul. There Rolf Ganger conquers the land and settles it with men from the north, giving rise to Normandy. In the Hrómundar saga Gripssonar, the hero Hrómund slays a sorcerer-king named Þráinn, a former ruler of Valland.

Legacy

The element Val- derives from Walhaz, a Proto-Germanic word that the Germanic peoples used for the inhabitants of the former Western Roman Empire.

Ancient sources: Flateyjarbók, Völundarkviða, Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla and Hrómundar saga Gripssonar.

Trivia

Origin of Normandy. Heimskringla tells how Rolf Ganger reached Valland, subdued it and settled it with men from the north, and that the name Normandy comes from them.
A Valkyrie from Valland. Ölrún, daughter of King Kjárr, appears in the Völundarkviða as a Valkyrie who marries the hero Egil.
