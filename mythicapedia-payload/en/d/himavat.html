personification of the Himalayas in Hinduism

Himavat

Himavat is the personification of the Himalayan mountains in Hinduism and the guardian deity of this sacred range, home to many gods and divine beings. His name, meaning "icy" or "snowy", appears in the Mahabharata and other Hindu scriptures.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Himavat fathered Ganga, the river goddess, as well as Ragini and Parvati, the second consort of Shiva. Accounts differ on his wife. According to the Ramayana, she is the Vedic goddess Mainavati, daughter of Mount Meru. The Vishnu Purana gives another version, in which she is the daughter of Svadhā and Kavi, a member of the class of Pitṛs. The Shiva Purana describes his wedding to Menā.

One of the most notable texts featuring him is the Devi Gita, which fills the last nine chapters of the seventh skandha of the Devi-Bhagavata Purana. In it, Parvati speaks with her father Himavat about the universal form of the Goddess, meditation on the Upanishads, ashtanga yoga, the paths of knowledge, action and devotion, and the temples and rituals dedicated to the Devi. His story is also mentioned in the Brahmanda Purana and the Kena Upanishad.

In another tale, Krishna performed a tapasya on the Himalayan peaks to appease Himavat, and from this penance his eldest son Pradyumna was born to Krishna's favourite wife, Rukmini.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, the Vishnu Purana, the Shiva Purana, the Devi-Bhagavata Purana, the Brahmanda Purana and the Kena Upanishad.

Trivia

Several names. Hindu scriptures also call him Himavant ("icy"), Himavān ("snowy"), Himaraja ("king of snow") and Parvateshwara ("god of mountains"), depending on the text and the aspect emphasized.
Krishna's penance. According to one account, Krishna performed a tapasya on the Himalayan peaks to pacify Himavat, and this effort led to the birth of Pradyumna.
