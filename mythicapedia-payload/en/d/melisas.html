Greek mythological nymphs with bee form

Melissae

The Melissae are nymphs of Greek mythology linked to bees and honey. It is believed that bees took their name from them, and in some versions the nymphs were transformed into these insects.

The myth

According to the reference entry, the Melissae were daughters of Melisseus and sisters of Amaltheia. In some accounts, Melissa in the singular is a woman who discovered and taught the use of honey.

The nymphs sometimes appear in bee form, and at other times they are said to have become bees. One of the nymphs of Mount Dicte, Panacris, fed the infant Zeus sweet honey. According to tradition, sacred bees acted as his nurses in a cave on Crete.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, bee-shaped nymphs guided the colonists who went to Ephesus.

Legacy

It is believed that bees took their name from these nymphs. The bee was the symbol of the Melissae in Greek tradition.

Trivia

Panacris. Panacris was one of the nymphs of Dicte, described as a bee that gave sweet honey to the infant Zeus.
Bee nymphs. Bees were the symbol of these nymphs, which is why they are sometimes called Melissae.
