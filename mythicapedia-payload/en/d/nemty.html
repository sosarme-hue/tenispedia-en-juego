Egyptian god of ferrymen

Nemty

Nemty, also read Anty, is an Egyptian god linked to ferrymen and travel, worshipped mainly in Upper Egypt. Closely tied to Horus and Seth, he appears in tales about Osiris’s inheritance and the solar barque.

The myth

In Egyptian tradition, Nemty was the ferryman who carried deities across the waters. The Pyramid Texts place him beside Sokar, watching over the sun-god’s boat, and show him ferrying Isis to the so-called “Island of the Middle.”

In The Contendings of Horus and Seth, Seth gathers the gods on an island to keep out Isis, whose magic he fears. Nemty takes an old woman across for a gold ring, not recognising her disguise as Isis. This error costs him his toes, so he can no longer perch as a falcon; hence the epithet “The Short-Legged.” In another tale, Horus wants to learn Nemty’s secret name and sends a venomous creature to sting him. In a story parallel to Isis’s, Nemty is blamed for beheading his mother Hathor, and Ra sentences him to be flayed alive. Anubis takes pity and gives the skin to Hathor, who sprinkles it with milk and revives him.

Worship and religion

According to the sources, his cult dates back to the 1st or 2nd dynasty. He was venerated in the 12th and 18th nomes of Upper Egypt, above all at Antaeopolis (Qau el-Kebir) alongside the goddess Matit, and also at Per-Nemty, the “House of Nemty.” In the 12th nome he was identified with Seth, accompanied by the lioness goddess Matyt. It is unclear whether his original role went beyond a title of Horus, and the sources do not say whether his cult survived.

Legacy

Two Sixth Dynasty pharaohs, Merenre Nemtyemsaf I and II, bore his name, meaning “Nemty is his protection.” Today “Nemty” is also the name of a ransomware program.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, the Ramesside papyrus Chester Beatty I, and The Contendings of Horus and Seth.

Trivia

A misread name. For a long time the god’s hieroglyphs were read as Anti or Anty, a reading that later studies have shown to be incorrect.
A falcon that could not perch. Losing his toes meant Nemty could no longer perch as a falcon, which kept him from residing on the boat.
