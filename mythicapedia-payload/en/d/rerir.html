legendary Norse chieftain, ancestor of the Völsungs

Rerir

Rerir is a legendary chieftain of Scandinavian protohistory, placed in the Vendel Era, and one of the ancestors of the Völsung dynasty in Norse mythology. He matters chiefly as the father of Völsung, the dynasty's patriarch.

The myth

According to the Völsunga saga, Rerir was the son of Sigi. After his father was murdered by the brothers of his wife, who wanted the throne, Rerir avenged him and succeeded him as ruler of Hunaland, where he became a powerful king.

The tradition tells that the couple could not have children until the goddess Frigg, wife of Odin, sent the giantess Hljod, in the form of a crow, carrying an apple of fertility. Soon after, the wife became pregnant, but Rerir fell ill and died. The pregnancy lasted six years; when she realised her death was near, she ordered a caesarean section, an operation that in those days killed the mother. Völsung was born already grown and kissed his mother before she died.

Ancient sources: The Völsunga saga, a medieval Norse text, is the main source that recounts his story.
