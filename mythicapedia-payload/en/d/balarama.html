Hindu god of agriculture, elder brother of Krishna

Balarama

Balarama is the elder brother of Krishna and one of the most revered figures in Vaishnavism and Krishnaism. He is linked to farming, to the plough he wields as a weapon, and to eternity and knowledge, and many devotees regard him as an incarnation of Vishnu or of the serpent Shesha.

The myth

According to tradition, Balarama was born to Vasudeva and Devaki. King Kamsa, Devaki's brother, had been warned that he would die at the hands of her eighth child, so he killed her children as they were born. When Balarama was conceived, the legend says his embryo was miraculously moved into the womb of Rohini, Vasudeva's wife, which gives him the name Sankarshana, 'one who was dragged away'. He grew up as a cowherd alongside Krishna in the household of Nanda, and was named Rama, Baladeva or Balabhadra, meaning 'strong Rama'.

In his youth he defeated demons sent by Kamsa and used his plough to redirect the course of the Yamuna towards Vrindavan. He married Revati, daughter of King Kakudmi, and they had several children. In the Mahabharata he teaches the mace to Duryodhana and Bhima and stays neutral during the great war, though he is enraged by Bhima's blow. According to the Bhagavata Purana, after the destruction of the Yadava clan and Krishna's death, he sat in meditation and died. Other traditions say a great white serpent emerged from his mouth, a sign of his identity with Shesha.

Worship and religion

Balarama is worshipped in Vaishnavism, Krishnaism and southern Indian devotion. The Pancharatra followers venerate him as the second vyuha of God, while the Gaudiya Vaishnavas believe he reappeared in the Kali Yuga as Nityananda. At Jagannath in Odisha he is part of the principal triad of deities. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

His image appears in art from Mathura, on Indo-Greek coins, and in later paintings and sculptures, as well as in Sanskrit epic literature. He is an important character in the 11th-century Javanese Kakawin Bharatayuddha, a poem based on the Mahabharata.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata and the Harivamsha, and his story is told in the Bhagavata Purana; he is also mentioned in Kautilya's Arthashastra.

Trivia

Name of the transfer. His name Sankarshana means 'completely dragged away' and refers to the transfer of his embryo from Devaki's womb to Rohini's.
The plough as a weapon. Balarama uses his plough to fight asuras and to redirect the Yamuna river towards Vrindavan.
