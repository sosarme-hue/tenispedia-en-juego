Aztec goddess of medicine, midwives and sweatbaths

Toci

Toci is a deity of the Mexica (Aztec) religion, honored as mother of the gods and heart of the Earth. She protected healers, midwives and those who used the sweatbath, and she is often identified with Tlazolteotl.

The myth

According to a Mexica tradition, during their long migration from the legendary homeland of Aztlan, the people served as mercenaries to the Culhua of Culhuacan. The Culhua ruler gave his daughter in marriage to a Mexica noble, but the Mexica's chief god Huitzilopochtli ordered that she be flayed and sacrificed instead. Once this was done, she transformed into Toci. The Mexica were expelled from Culhuacan for the deed and moved on to Lake Texcoco, where they later founded Tenochtitlan.

Toci is often shown with black markings around her mouth and nose and a headdress with cotton spools. Although regarded as an aged goddess, she is not always depicted with signs of old age. Some sources also link her to war and call her the "Woman of Discord".

Worship and religion

Her name means something like "our grandmother", and she was linked to the temazcal, or sweatbath, where she was called Temazcalteci, "Grandmother of sweatbaths". Bernardino de Sahagún describes her as venerated by physicians and surgeons, and during Ochpaniztli, the great sweeping festival of the Aztec calendar, harvest rites were held in her honor. Her main sanctuary was in what is now Santa Ana Chiautempan, Tlaxcala.

Legacy

The pictographic image of the goddess used today in Chiautempan was created in recent times by the artist Desiderio Hernández Xochitiotzin and has spread without citing its origin.

Ancient sources: Bernardino de Sahagún's Florentine Codex and Juan de Torquemada's Monarquía indiana record her rites and story.

Trivia

Sweatbath name. As goddess of the sweatbaths, she was called Temazcalteci, "Grandmother of sweatbaths".
Woman of Discord. Some sources give her the epithet "Woman of Discord", linked to her association with war.
