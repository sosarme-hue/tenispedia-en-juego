Oceanid, mother of the Pleiades

Pleione

Pleione is an Oceanid in Greek mythology, daughter of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, and mother of the Pleiades. Her story matters because it links the Titans to the tale of Orion's pursuit and to the origin of a group of stars in the constellation Taurus. Her name is connected to the Greek word pleíon, meaning 'more', a fitting name for a figure associated with the increase of flocks.

The myth

According to tradition, Pleione married the Titan Atlas and bore the Pleiades, the Hyades and a son named Hyas in Cyllene, in Arcadia. Some accounts add that she was also the mother of Calypso, although the genealogies vary from one ancient author to another, and not all sources agree on the names or number of daughters.

The best-known episode is the pursuit by Orion. While Pleione was travelling through Boeotia with her daughters, the hunter fell in love with her and tried to attack her. She escaped, but Orion searched for her for seven years without finding her. Pitying the girls, Zeus changed them into doves and later placed them in the sky as stars in Taurus, where, according to the story, they still seem to flee from Orion.

The Hyades also became stars in the same constellation, but the sources give two different reasons. One version says it was a reward for caring for the infant Dionysus and handing him over to Ino. Another holds that they wept so much after the death of their brother Hyas, killed by a boar, a lion or a serpent depending on the account, that their grief led to their transformation.

Some versions also present her as a protector of sailing, a fitting role for an Oceanid tied to the waters.

Legacy

Pleione gives her name to a star, Pleione, in the constellation Taurus. A small genus of mostly terrestrial orchids, Pleione, also bears her name, keeping the mother of the Pleiades alive in scientific nomenclature.
