central continent of the world in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain cosmology

Jambudvipa

Jambudvipa (jambudvīpa) is the continent where ordinary humans live according to Hindu, Buddhist and Jain cosmology. It takes its name from the great jambu tree that grows at its centre, and the term later came to denote the Indian subcontinent itself.

The myth

According to the Hindu Puranas, the world is arranged as seven concentric island-continents separated by seven oceans of different substances, each twice as wide as the one before. Jambudvipa is the innermost and smallest of them, surrounded by a sea of salt water.

The tradition places Mount Meru at its centre, king of mountains and support of the Earth. On its summit lies Brahmapuri, the city of the creator god Brahma, surrounded by the abodes of Indra and seven other gods. From Brahmapuri flows the river Akasha Ganga, which splits into four streams that irrigate the continent. According to the Markandeya Purana, the land has nine varshas, or zones, and eight principal mountains.

The name is explained by the jambu tree, whose fruits, according to the Vishnu Purana, are as large as elephants. When they rot and fall on the mountains, they form the Jambu River, whose waters its inhabitants drink.

In Buddhism, Jambudvipa is the southern continent around Sumeru, shaped like a triangle with a blunted point facing south, and it is the only realm where humans can hear the Buddha's teachings and attain enlightenment.

Legacy

The term moved from cosmography into politics. Ashoka used it in his third-century BCE edicts to refer to his own empire, and medieval kings such as the Rashtrakutas and Western Chalukyas later employed it in copper-plate charters to claim universal dominion.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata and in several Puranas, and in Buddhism in texts such as the Vinaya and the Pali commentaries.

Trivia

Giant fruits. According to the Vishnu Purana, the fruits of the jambu tree are as large as Asian elephants.
Lotus shape. The Markandeya and Brahmanda Puranas divide the continent into four regions shaped like lotus petals, with Mount Meru as the pericarp.
