upper realm of Inca cosmology

Hanan Pacha

Hanan Pacha is the upper world in Andean cosmology, one of three levels of the cosmos alongside Kay Pacha (this world) and Ukhu Pacha (the inner world). It matters because it held the archetypes of all existence and was the dwelling of deities such as Illapa.

The myth

According to colonial chronicles, Hanan Pacha was where the archetypes of existence were located, from animals and elements to the gods themselves, and a primordial force had created it. Thus a llama had a first form, the "mother of llamas", and the same applied to water.

The souls of the dead also had their destiny in this realm. The virtuous or those of privileged categories were transformed into stars, and children recognised by the community could reach the same fate. Warriors who fell in battle dwelt on sacred peaks and joined the domain of Illapa, god of lightning, thunder and war, who resided in the sky and on the highest mountains.

Ancient sources: Chroniclers such as Cristóbal de Molina, Blas Valera, Bernabé Cobo, Pedro Cieza de León and Guaman Poma describe these conceptions during the colonial period.

Trivia

Four heavens. Pedro Cieza de León recorded that some Indigenous people spoke of "four great heavens". Other sources mention stratifications of eight or even thirteen levels.
The Amaru serpent. Among curanderos of northern Peru, the great serpent Amaru dwells in the waters of Ukhu Pacha and rises to Hanan Pacha, returning to earth as lightning with rain.
