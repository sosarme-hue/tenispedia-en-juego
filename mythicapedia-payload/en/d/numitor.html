king of Alba Longa, grandfather of Romulus and Remus

Numitor

In Roman mythology, Numitor was king of Alba Longa and the maternal grandfather of Rome's founders, Romulus and Remus. His overthrow by his brother is the starting point of the myth of the city's foundation.

The myth

According to the tradition, Numitor was the son of Procas and a descendant of Aeneas, so the throne of Alba Longa, a city said to have been founded by Ascanius, should have passed to him. His younger brother Amulius ignored their father's wishes, overthrew him and, to secure power, killed Numitor's sons. He made his daughter Rhea Silvia a Vestal Virgin, a role that required chastity, and spared her life on that condition.

The god Mars nevertheless fathered the twins Romulus and Remus with her. Accounts differ on what followed: some say Rhea set them adrift in a basket on the Tiber to save them; others say Amulius sentenced her and ordered the boys drowned, but a servant left them in the basket. The twins were suckled by the she-wolf Lupercа and raised by the shepherd Faustulus. Once they learned their origin, they avenged themselves on Amulius and restored the throne to Numitor.
