Mesopotamian goddess of grain

Ashnan

Ashnan is a Mesopotamian goddess of grain, the personification of cereal and agriculture. She was already worshipped in the Uruk period and is among the oldest city goddesses attested.

The myth

According to Sumerian tradition, Ashnan, the kindly servant, was the daughter of Enlil and sister of Lahar, goddess of livestock. The Anunaki created her to provide food for humanity. She first lived in Duku, where Enlil, at Enki's command, made fields and farms for her while Lahar raised animals.

One day the two sisters got drunk and quarrelled, and Enki and Enlil had to settle the dispute. She sometimes appears paired with Šakkan.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in cities such as Nippur, Lagash and Ur, and a temple dedicated to her stood in Nippur in the Ur III period. Offerings were made to her, and she could be invoked alongside Nintur to stop bleeding after childbirth. Active worship ended by the Old Babylonian period.

Legacy

Her presence survives mainly in ancient texts, such as the poem Debate between Sheep and Grain, and in seals inscribed as servants of Ashnan. She still appears in the later god list An = Anum.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian poem Debate between Sheep and Grain, the Gudea Cylinders and the myth Ezina and her Seven Children mention her, as does the god list An = Anum.

Trivia

A digging mouse. In the myth Ezina and her Seven Children, the youngest daughter is called the «digging mouse». Some scholars think the story may explain the origin of mice, which eat grain.
Servant seals. Seals inscribed «servant of Ashnan» or «servant of Ezina» survive, showing personal devotion to the goddess.
