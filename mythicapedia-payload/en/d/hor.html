blind god of Norse mythology

Höðr

Höðr is the blind god of Norse mythology, son of Odin and brother of Baldr. His name is linked to war and battle, and his role in the death of Baldr makes him one of the most tragic figures of the Scandinavian pantheon, as told in the ancient sources.

The myth

According to the Eddas, Baldr had dreams foretelling his death, so his mother, the goddess Frigg, travelled the world making every thing swear never to harm him. Frigg overlooked the mistletoe, which she considered too harmless to ask for an oath, leaving the god's only weak point unprotected. The gods amused themselves by hurling weapons at Baldr, which failed to hurt him. Loki, learning the secret, made a dart from a mistletoe branch and guided Höðr, who was blind, to throw it as if it were a joke. The dart killed Baldr.

Höðr could not see what he was doing, so the myth presents him as a god who kills without meaning to. Odin then fathered Váli with Rindr to avenge Baldr; in the story, Váli grew to adulthood within a day and slew Höðr. Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda, stresses that Baldr cannot be restored to life despite his divinity, and that Höðr ends up in Hel.

The Völuspá places Höðr and Baldr in the world that emerges after Ragnarök, while Vafþrúðnismál does not name them among the surviving gods. Saxo Grammaticus offers another version in the Gesta Danorum, where Höðr is a human hero who competes with Balderus for the hand of Nanna. After several clashes, Hotherus slays Balderus in combat, marries Nanna and, after the deaths of his rivals, comes to rule Denmark and Sweden.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Prose Edda and the Poetic Edda, especially in the Völuspá and Baldrs draumar, and in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, which presents him as a human hero.

Trivia

A name of war. His name can be translated as warrior or battle, and it is related to the Old Norse word hǫð, meaning war or slaughter.
Höðr in warrior poetry. In skaldic poetry his name appears in kennings for warriors, such as Höðr of the byrnie, although in the Eddas he is a blind god unable to harm anyone without help.
