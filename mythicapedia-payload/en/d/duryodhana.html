eldest Kaurava prince in the Mahabharata

Duryodhana

Duryodhana is the eldest son of the blind king Dhritarashtra and Queen Gandhari, and the main antagonist of the Pandavas in the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He matters because he embodies the ambition, pride and resentment that lead to the Kurukshetra War.

The myth

According to the tradition, Duryodhana was born in an extraordinary way. Gandhari, pregnant for an unusually long time and envious of Kunti, mother of Yudhishthira, struck her belly and gave birth to a mass of flesh. The sage Vyasa divided it into one hundred parts, which were sealed in pots of ghee, and from them emerged the hundred Kaurava brothers, with Duryodhana the first. His birth was accompanied by ominous signs, and although his advisers urged that he be abandoned, his father refused.

Raised in Hastinapura, Duryodhana grew up rivalling his Pandava cousins, whom he resented, above all Bhima, with whom he competed with the mace. With the help of his uncle Shakuni he engineered the dice game in which the Pandavas lost their kingdom and were exiled, and he humiliated Draupadi during the match. Later, when the Pandavas returned from exile, he refused to give back what was theirs, which triggered the Kurukshetra War.

During the war he was supported by warriors such as Bhishma, Drona and his friend Karna, but he lost his brothers and allies one by one. After Karna's death he took refuge in a lake, where he accepted a final duel with Bhima. According to the tale, Bhima struck his thigh illegally, and Duryodhana died soon after, having first named his friend Ashvatthaman commander. Krishna reminded him that he had not respected dharma during his life either.

Legacy

His figure inspired derivative works such as Urubhanga and Venisamhara, in which his character is expanded and shown with pathos and tragedy. The epic itself also reveals a moral ambiguity toward him: his enemies sometimes call him Suyodhana, a name with the opposite meaning, highlighting that double reading.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, in its Adi Parva, presents him as the first of the hundred sons of Dhritarashtra and Gandhari.

Trivia

A name with two meanings. Duryodhana is often translated as hard to conquer, while his alternative name Suyodhana means easy to conquer. The epic plays on both meanings.
Gandhari's blindfold. Gandhari blindfolded herself when she married her blind husband, so she would see nothing but him. Later she tried to use her gaze to make her son invincible.
A noisy birth. According to the tale, at birth he cried with the voice of a donkey, and the donkeys in the royal stables answered him.
