regional goddess of Rajasthan, incarnation of Durga

Suswani Mata

Suswani Mata is a regional goddess of Rajasthan revered by both Hindus and Jains. She is regarded as an incarnation of Durga and serves as kuldevi to several Jain clans, such as the Surana and Dugar.

The myth

According to tradition, the goddess Ambe incarnated as Suswani, daughter of Sethji Satidas and Sugankanwarji of Nagaur. A Muslim nawab fell in love with her and asked to marry her, but her father refused, since he believed she was a divine incarnation. Enraged, the nawab imprisoned him. Suswani prayed to Arihant and in a dream received a promise that the nawab would never catch her if she ran seven paces ahead of him. When she grew tired, a lion appeared, and she fled on its back to Morkhana. There Shiva threw his chimta, which split a kera tree and the earth in two. Suswani entered the earth with her lion, and the nawab died fighting his companions over the pallu of her sari.

Worship and religion

Her main temple stands in Morkhana, in Bikaner district. An inscription dated around 1173-74 AD makes it one of the oldest in the region. It remains an important centre of devotion for Hindus and Jains, and the kera tree mentioned in the legend still stands beside the shrine.

Trivia

A tree still standing. The kera tree said to have split in two during the legend still stands in the temple grounds at Morkhana, lush and green.
A goddess of two faiths. Although she is a regional goddess, both Hindu and Jain communities worship her, which is unusual for a single figure.
