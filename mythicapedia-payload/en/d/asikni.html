Hindu goddess, consort of Daksha

Asikni

Asikni is a Hindu goddess and consort of Daksha in the Puranic pantheon. Texts present her as the mother of thousands of children, and her name has been given several meanings.

The myth

The Puranas do not agree on her origin. Some say she was born from Brahma's left thumb, while others make her a daughter of the Prajapati Panchajana or Virana. Brahma tasked Daksha with populating the cosmos, but the beings he created with his mind were not enough. After a successful penance, Vishnu granted Asikni to him as his wife and urged them to unite.

Their union produced five thousand sons, the Haryashvas, who were lured into worldly affairs by Narada's advice and never returned, and then another thousand, the Shabalashvas, who met the same fate. Angered, Daksha cursed Narada to wander forever and fathered sixty daughters with Asikni, who married sages and deities. According to the Shiva Purana, Shiva was reborn in her womb, and all the gods held her in high esteem. She later gave birth to Sati, whom both parents raised.

Ancient sources: The Puranas (Brahma, Bhagavata, Shiva, Vayu, Kurma and others), which give varying accounts of her origin, and the Rigveda, which uses her name for the Chenab River.

Trivia

A river with her name. The Rigveda uses the word asikni to describe the Chenab River, a name linked to the sense of dark.
Mother of Sati. According to some accounts, Asikni gave birth to Sati, who would later become the first wife of Shiva.
