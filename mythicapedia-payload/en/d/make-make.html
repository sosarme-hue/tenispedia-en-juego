Rapa Nui creator god linked to fertility and the birdman cult

Makemake

Makemake, also written Make-Make, is a deity of Rapa Nui (Easter Island) mythology, believed to have created humanity. Over time he became the chief god of the tangata manu, or birdman cult, and he gave his name to a dwarf planet in the outer solar system.

The myth

According to the myth, Makemake created the Earth but felt that something was missing. One day, looking at his reflection in a gourd of water, he saw a face with a beak, wings and feathers. When a bird landed on his shoulder, he joined his image with the bird's, and from that union his firstborn was born.

He then wished to create a being in his own image, one that could speak and think as he desired. He fertilised the waters of the sea, and fish appeared, but the result was not what he hoped for. He then fertilised a stone with red earth, and from it came humankind. Seeing man so lonely, he also created woman.

Long afterwards, Makemake appeared in a dream to Hau-Maka and showed him the way to an uninhabited island, where King Hotu Matu'a and his people would make their new home. Together with Haua, Makemake later led the birds to the islets off Rano Kau.

Worship and religion

Makemake was the chief god of the tangata manu cult, an annual ceremony in which the island's lineages competed for political power. According to the sources, this cult arose after internal conflicts that destroyed most of the moai. Four gods were linked to the ceremony, each with a supernatural servant, and participants swam to the islet of Motu Nui to find the first egg of the sooty tern, which had to be carried to Orongo. Makemake was also associated with fertility and food.

Legacy

His face, with large eyes and a phallic nose, is a frequent subject of the island's petroglyphs. In 2005 the International Astronomical Union named the trans-Neptunian object then known as 2005 FY9 Makemake, making it the fourth recognised dwarf planet and the first named after a Rapa Nui god.

Trivia

A name tied to Easter. The dwarf planet was discovered shortly after Easter 2005, and its code name was Easterbunny. The first European contact with the island also took place on Easter Sunday, in 1722.
A recovered carving. In 2000 the BBC broadcast the documentary The Lost Gods of Easter Island, in which David Attenborough bought a wooden carving of Makemake at auction for a fairly low price. Made from toromiro wood, now locally extinct, it had left the island with Captain Cook's crew.
