legendary Hindu king of the solar dynasty, symbol of truthfulness

Harishchandra

Harishchandra is a legendary king of the solar dynasty who appears in several Hindu tales. He is remembered as a model of truthfulness and of keeping one's word, even at the cost of losing everything.

The myth

In the Markandeya Purana, King Harishchandra ruled honestly and peacefully during the Treta Yuga, together with his queen Shaivya and their son Rohitashva. One day, while hunting, he heard cries for help that turned out to be an illusion created by Vighnaraja to disturb the meditation of the sage Vishvamitra. Once he realised this, the king apologised and promised to grant any wish the sage might have.

Vishvamitra demanded as a donation everything the king owned, except his family. Harishchandra gave up his kingdom and possessions, but the sage then asked for more. To pay, the king sold his wife and son, and finally sold himself into slavery to a keeper of a cremation ground in Kashi, where he had to collect fees for cremations. When his son died from a snakebite, Shaivya carried the body to the cremation ground with no money, and Harishchandra insisted on payment before allowing it. The gods then intervened, led by Yama, praised his virtue and invited him to heaven. He refused to go without his people, and Indra agreed to let them all ascend.

Legacy

He is very popular in India and is held up as a model of the ideal Hindu life. According to some traditions, his story inspired Mahatma Gandhi in his childhood.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Aitareya Brahmana, the Mahabharata, the Markandeya Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Celestial city. According to tradition, the gods fixed his capital, Saubha, in the atmosphere, and some Hindus believe it can sometimes be seen in the sky.
Golden name. His name means 'the one with golden splendour', from hari, 'golden', and chandra, 'splendour'.
