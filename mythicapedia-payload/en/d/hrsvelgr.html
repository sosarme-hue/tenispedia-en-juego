giant eagle of Norse mythology

Hræsvelgr

Hræsvelgr is a jötunn who takes the form of an eagle in Norse mythology. According to the Eddas, the wind arises from the beating of his wings, so his figure serves to explain the origin of the winds that move across the world of men.

The myth

In the Vafþrúðnismál, part of the Poetic Edda, the wise jötunn Vafþrúðnir answers a question from Odin about the origin of the wind. Hræsvelgr sits at the edge of the heavens, at its northernmost limit, in the form of an eagle. When he spreads his wings to fly, the wind that sweeps over the world of men is born from them.

Snorri Sturluson repeats the same account in Gylfaginning, in the Prose Edda. There the question is asked by Gangleri, the name taken by Gylfi in disguise, and it is answered by Hárr. Snorri specifies that Hræsvelgr sits at the north end of heaven and that the winds come from beneath his enormous wings when he spreads them for flight. Like other giants, he is linked to the outer limits of the known world.

His name has been translated as «corpse-swallower» or «shipwreck-current», two possible readings that point toward death and shipwrecks.

Ancient sources: Vafþrúðnismál (stanza 36) of the Poetic Edda and Gylfaginning by Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda.

Trivia

A disputed name. His name has been translated as «corpse-swallower» or «shipwreck-current», depending on the preferred reading.
Forms in other languages. The common Danish form is Ræsvelg, the Swedish form is Räsvelg, and English sources use variants such as Hraesvelgr.
