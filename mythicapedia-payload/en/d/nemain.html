Irish goddess of battle

Nemain

Nemain is a goddess in Irish mythology who embodies the frenzied chaos of war. She belongs to a group of warlike female figures that includes the Morrígan, Macha and Badb, and her name has been read as "venomous" or "terrible".

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Nemain is the wife of Neit, a god of war. The sources do not agree on this marriage: a poem in the Book of Leinster gives Neit the wives Badb and Nemain, while another passage names Fea instead, and the genealogy books make her a daughter of Elcmar of the Brugh, so some scholars think Fea and Nemain are really the same figure.

In the Táin Bó Cúailnge, the great Irish epic, Nemain throws armies into confusion, so that allied bands turn on one another and destroy each other. Her war cry is so powerful that it can kill a hundred men at once. In the episode of Breslech Maighe Muirthemne, Cúchulainn's fury calls up spirits of the air and the valleys, and Nemain causes the four provinces of Ireland to fall on their own spears and weapons.

The Dindsenchas calls her the "law-giving wife" of Neit and describes her as "of the wounds of war". In the account that links her to the Tuatha Dé Danann, she died with her husband when the eye of Balor looked upon them.

Legacy

Her name has inspired etymologies linking it to Indo-European roots meaning "to take" or "to deal out", such as the Greek Nemesis, suggesting a possible connection with the idea of retributive justice.

Ancient sources: The Táin Bó Cúailnge, the Book of Leinster, the Lebor na hUidhre, the Dindsenchas, and Irish genealogy books and glossaries such as Cormac's.

Trivia

Deadly cry. According to the sources, Nemain's war cry could kill a hundred men at once.
Two names, one figure. Some scholars believe Fea and Nemain, both named as wives of Neit, are in fact the same figure.
