legendary hero of the Hrólfr Kraki cycle

Bödvar Bjarki

Bödvar Bjarki, whose name means 'little warlike bear', is a hero of Norse tradition, one of the twelve berserkers of Hrólfr Kraki and Denmark's most famous champion. He matters because his story joins the warrior with the man who turns into a bear.

The myth

According to tradition, Bjarki was the son of Björn, who had been turned into a bear by a spell from his stepmother Hvit, and of Bera. After Björn's death, Bera bore triplets: Elgfróði, with an elk's body from the waist down; Thorir, with a hound's feet; and Bjarki, the only normal one. Bjarki later killed Hvit in revenge.

At Hrólfr Kraki's court in Lejre, he killed a monster that had terrorized the Danes and turned the thrall Hott into the warrior Hjalti. He became Denmark's finest champion. In the last battle, while Bjarki sat entranced, his bear-spirit fought for the king; Hjalti woke him, the bear vanished, and Bjarki died with Hrólfr.

Legacy

Some scholars believe Bjarki and Beowulf were originally the same figure, though others dispute this. Tom Shippey has noted that Beorn, the shapeshifter in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit, has a very close analogue in Bödvar Bjarki.

Ancient sources: Hrólfs saga kraka, the Latin epitome of the Skjöldunga saga, Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum, and the poem Bjarkamál.

Trivia

A name with a bear. Bjarki means 'little warrior' and is tied to the bear, which also appears in his father's name, Björn.
From thrall to warrior. The king renamed the thrall Hott as Hjalti, meaning 'hilt', after a feat that Bjarki helped him accomplish.
