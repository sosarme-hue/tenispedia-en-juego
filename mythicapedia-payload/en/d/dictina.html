Cretan goddess of the hunt

Dictynna

Dictynna is a Cretan goddess associated with hunting, whose cult spread across Crete and to a few places beyond the island. She was sometimes identified with Artemis and sometimes with Britomartis, and her name is linked to the net (diktyon).

The myth

According to one legend, Minos pursued her relentlessly, and while fleeing she ended up caught in the nets of some fishermen. In this version, the legend explains where her name came from.

Diodorus Siculus gives a different explanation: her name was said to come from the fact that she was credited with inventing hunting nets. In this way, the goddess is tied both to hunting and to the world of nets and fishing.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped mainly on Crete, in places such as Cydonia, Lisos and Falasarna, and also in Sparta and Amyzon, outside the island. Some identified her with Artemis and others with Britomartis.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus mentions her when explaining that she was credited with inventing hunting nets. The Mycenaean Linear B tablets contain names that some scholars have linked to her.

Trivia

A name in a net. A legend about fishermen explains her name, which is linked to the Greek word for net.
Traces on the tablets. The names qe-ra-si-ja and pi-pi-tu-na, found on Mycenaean Linear B tablets, have been linked to her.
