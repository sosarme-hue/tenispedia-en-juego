Arcadian hero of Greek mythology

Parrhasius

Parrhasius is a hero of Greek mythology linked to Arcadia, credited with founding the Arcadian city of Parrhasia, which bears his name.

The myth

According to tradition, Parrhasius was one of the fifty sons of the impious king Lycaon. His mother is named differently in various accounts: the naiad Cyllene, Nonacris, or an unknown woman. Some authors made Zeus his father instead.

He is also named as the father of Arcas, the son who gave his name to the region of Arcadia. In this way Parrhasius links Lycaon's line to the land that later took its name from his descendant.

Worship and religion

No worship of Parrhasius himself is recorded. Apollo, however, bore Parrhasius as a surname, and on Mount Lycaeus in Arcadia an annual festival honoured the god as Epicurius, the helper.

Legacy

His name survives mainly in place names: the city and district of Parrhasia. Ancient Greek poets also used the adjective parrhasios as a synonym for "Arcadian".

Ancient sources: Ancient Greek poets used the adjective parrhasios to mean "Arcadian"; the sources do not name a specific work that narrates his story.

Trivia

A second Parrhasius. Another Parrhasius, twin brother of Lycastus and son of Ares and Phylonome, was cast into the river Erymanthus and survived after a wolf suckled him. The shepherd Gyliphus raised him.
