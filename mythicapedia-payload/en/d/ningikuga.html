Mesopotamian goddess

Ningikuga

Ningikuga is a little-documented Mesopotamian goddess, whose name can be rendered as "lady of the pure reed". She matters because god lists place her in Enki's circle and connect her with the family of Ningal and Inanna.

The myth

The surviving texts do not narrate any deeds of Ningikuga; instead they place her within a web of divine kinship. According to one tradition, she was a wife of Enki. In two sources, an Old Babylonian balbale and a love song, she appears as the mother of Ningal and, by extension, as the grandmother of Inanna.

Scholars do not agree on whether Enki's wife and Ningal's mother were the same goddess. Thorkild Jacobsen assumed they were, while Antoine Cavigneaux and Manfred Krebernik keep the two uses of the name separate. A hymn to Inanna also uses the name Ningikuga for a manifestation of that goddess, describing her as "the mistress of all, the pure one, who purifies the earth".

Ancient sources: God lists such as An = Anum (tablets II and III), an Old Babylonian balbale and love song, and a hymn to Inanna.

Trivia

A name with a landscape. Her name is interpreted as "lady of the pure reed". Jacobsen deduced from this that she and her daughter were associated with reeds and marshes.
Mother and shared name. Line 28 of tablet III of An = Anum explains Ningikuga as a name of Ningal, showing how closely the two goddesses were linked.
