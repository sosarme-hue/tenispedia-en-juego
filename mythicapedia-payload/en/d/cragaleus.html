Greek hero, son of Dryops and judge of the gods

Cragaleus

Cragaleus is a just and wise figure of Greek myth, a son of Dryops who lived in the land of Dryopis beside a spring. His reputation for judgement made him the arbiter of a dispute between three gods over who should become the patron of Ambracia, in Epirus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Apollo, Artemis and Heracles came to him to settle which of them should protect Ambracia. Apollo argued that the region had been conquered by his son Melaneus and that he had brought order to the city. Artemis reminded everyone that she had saved the Ambraciotes by sending a lioness against the tyrant Phalecus. Heracles claimed that he had destroyed the non-Greek peoples of Epirus who tried to steal the cattle of Geryon, and that the Corinthians who founded the city were his descendants.

Cragaleus found Heracles' argument the most convincing and declared him the winner. Apollo, enraged, turned him into stone.

Worship and religion

The Ambraciotes sacrificed to Apollo the Saviour, but believed their city belonged to Heracles and the Heracleidae. They also honoured Cragaleus with sacrifices. The sources do not say whether this cult lasted.

Trivia

A spring with a story. According to the tradition, the spring of his region appeared where Heracles struck the earth with his club, near Thermopylae.
Punishment in stone. After his verdict, Apollo turned Cragaleus into stone, a punishment the tradition links to the sacrifices made in his honour.
