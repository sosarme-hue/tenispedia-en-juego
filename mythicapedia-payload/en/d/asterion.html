Greek river-god of Argos

Asterion

Asterion is a river-god in Greek mythology, a river of Argos and a tributary of the Inachus. His story is tied to the dispute over the Argolid between Hera and Poseidon.

The myth

According to tradition, Asterion was a son of Oceanus and Tethys, like most rivers. Together with his brothers Inachus and Cephisus, he ruled that the Argolid belonged to Hera rather than Poseidon, ending the dispute between the two gods.

His daughters Euboea, Prosymna and Acraea had been Hera's nurses, which explains his bond with the goddess. Poseidon took revenge: unable to cause floods, he dried up Asterion and his brothers, so their rivers flowed only after rain.

Worship and religion

Pausanias notes that a plant also called asterion grew on the riverbanks, offered to Hera, with whose leaves garlands were woven for the goddess. Nonnus of Panopolis mentions a rite in which young men dedicated locks of their hair to the river.

Ancient sources: The Dionysiaca of Nonnus of Panopolis, the herbal of Dioscorides and the Description of Greece by Pausanias.

Trivia

A plant with his name. In the herbal of Dioscorides, asterion refers to Silene linifolia, a plant that, according to Pausanias, grew near the Heraion of the Argolid.
A name meaning starry. The Greek name Asterion is related to the word for 'starry', as indicated in the reference sources.
