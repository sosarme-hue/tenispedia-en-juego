Yoruba orisha of the river and of cemeteries

Yewá

Yewá is a Yoruba orisha linked to the river that bears her name in south-western Nigeria and, in Cuban Santería, to cemeteries and death. Her cult remains alive in Afro-Caribbean and Afro-Brazilian religions.

The myth

According to tradition, Yewá is the daughter of Ogun and Obbá and the sister of Babalú Ayé, though she always kept her virginity.

Some accounts claim that Sango had relations with her, showing how varied the stories surrounding this deity are.

Worship and religion

In Cuba she is especially honoured in Santiago de Cuba, where she is given as a guardian orisha, and her children, regarded as diviners, live with great austerity. In her presence composure is required, and quarrels and displays of affection are avoided. Offerings include fish, gofio and peanut balls. In Santería she is syncretised with Our Lady of Sorrows (Desamparados), patron of Valencia, and in Brazilian candomblé with Our Lady of the Snows.

Legacy

The Yewa River in Nigeria takes its name from this deity, and in 1995 the Ẹgbado people adopted that same name as their own.

Trivia

A river named after a goddess. The Yewa River runs parallel to the Nigerian coast until it reaches the Lagos lagoon, and it gives its name to the area and its people.
Guardian of the dead. In Cuban Santería she is considered the mistress of the cemetery, and it is said that she carries the eggun to Oyá.
