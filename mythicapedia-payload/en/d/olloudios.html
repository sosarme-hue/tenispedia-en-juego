Celtic god identified with Mars

Olloudios

Olloudios is a Celtic epithet of Mars, known from Latin inscriptions in southern Gaul and Roman Britain. He was invoked as a protector of wellbeing, belonging to a group of Celtic deities with that protective role.

The myth

Ancient texts say almost nothing about Olloudios. He is known mainly from dedications carved in stone and bronze, so no genealogy or mythic episodes can be reconstructed with confidence.

His identification with Mars, the Roman god of war, shows how Celtic peoples folded their local deities into the Roman pantheon while keeping a distinct name that signalled a particular role.

Worship and religion

Vigilia Metia, a Roman woman, dedicated a bronze tablet to him at Antibes in fulfilment of a vow. Another relief, found at Bisley in England, shows a male figure framed by a small temple, with an altar at his side. Some scholars have proposed that his sanctuary stood on Mont-Saint-Cassien, though there is no evidence for this.

Ancient sources: Only Latin inscriptions: the dedication from Antibes (Marti Olloudio) and the Bisley relief inscribed Marti Olludio, now almost illegible.

Trivia

A disputed name. From 1726 onward, some scholars proposed reading OLLOVBO instead of OLLOVDIO on the Antibes inscription. Several accepted this reading and took it as a place-based surname.
A relief turned into a summerhouse wall. In the nineteenth century the Bisley reliefs were set into the wall of a summerhouse on an estate, their edges shaved and painted black. In 1929 they went to the Gloucester museum.
