heroine of Greek mythology, wife of Alcmaeon

Callirhoe

Callirhoe is a heroine of Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Achelous and wife of Alcmaeon. Her story, in which a craving for a necklace sets off a chain of killings and revenge, survives in several ancient versions.

The myth

According to the tradition, Achelous gave his daughter in marriage to Alcmaeon on condition that he win back the necklace and robe of Harmonia, heirlooms that had passed through the hands of Eriphyle. Alcmaeon, haunted by the Furies after killing his mother, had dedicated the necklace at Delphi, but Callirhoe, eager to own it, pressed him to recover it. When his father-in-law Phegeus discovered the deception, Phegeus's sons killed Alcmaeon.

Remorseful, Callirhoe begged Zeus to let her young sons grow up at once so they could avenge their father. The god agreed, and the young men killed Phegeus and his wife and then took the jewels to Delphi. According to Apollodorus, the brothers later settled Epirus and Acarnania, a region the tradition links with Acarnan, one of Callirhoe's sons.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Pausanias and Ovid's Metamorphoses all recount her story, with variations among them about Alcmaeon's fate and the role of the sons.

Trivia

A necklace with a past. The necklace and robe at the heart of the plot pass from Harmonia to Eriphyle depending on the version, and the quest for them sets the whole story in motion.
Youth granted. Ovid tells how Zeus allowed Callirhoe's sons to reach manhood at once, with Hebe, goddess of youth, granting that gift.
