queen of Pylos, wife of Neleus

Chloris

Chloris is a figure of Greek mythology, queen of Pylos and mother of Nestor, the Homeric hero. Daughter of King Amphion of Orchomenus, her story survives mainly in later sources that blend several versions of her family line.

The myth

According to tradition, Chloris was the youngest daughter of King Amphion of Orchomenus, son of Iasus. Her name, meaning something like "greenish," distinguishes her from another Chloris, one of the Niobids, whom some later authors confuse with her.

Chloris married Neleus and became queen of Pylos. According to the stories, she bore twelve sons, among them Nestor, Chromius and Periclymenus, as well as a daughter, Pero. Many authors held that Periclymenus was the son of Poseidon, who was also Neleus's father, and the god gave him the power to change shape at will. A minority account claims Chloris had only three sons with Neleus, the rest born to other women, though not all accept this version.

In the Odyssey, Odysseus meets Chloris during his descent to Hades. Pausanias also describes a painting by Polygnotus in which she appears among other notable women of the underworld, kneeling beside her friend Thyia.

Ancient sources: Homer names Nestor, Chromius and Periclymenus in the Odyssey, and Pausanias describes Polygnotus's painting that shows Chloris.
