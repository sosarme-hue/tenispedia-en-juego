Chinese goddess of women, children and pregnancy

Chen Jinggu

Chen Jinggu, also known as Linshui furen or the Lady of Linshui, is a protective goddess of women, children and pregnancy in Chinese folk religion, and the patron deity of Fuzhou. She combines the figure of a former Taoist priestess with that of a deity worshipped in Fujian, Taiwan and across East Asia.

The myth

According to tradition, Chen Jinggu was born in Fuzhou with supernatural powers. As a young woman she studied on Mount Lu under Xu Xun, mastering Taoism but not the traditional female roles of motherhood. After marrying and becoming pregnant, she faced a drought in northern Fujian. To bring rain and defeat a demonic white snake, she sacrificed her unborn child and died soon after. The people honored her as a goddess.

Other versions link her to Guanyin: the goddess, struck with silver ingots, let her blood flow into the river, and from it Chen Jinggu was born.

Worship and religion

More than 130 temples in Taiwan are dedicated to her, and Fujian has many ancestral temples. She is revered as both a Taoist and a Confucian deity.

Legacy

Her novel Linshui pingyao, written in the 17th century or possibly earlier, brought her story to wider audiences, with French and English translations in 1988 and 2008. Some scholars have suggested a link between the monkey spirits worshipped in her region and the Monkey King of Journey to the West.

Ancient sources: The novel Linshui pingyao and Song-dynasty legends about her.

Trivia

Three sworn sisters. Chen Jinggu, Lin Jiuniang and Li Sanniang were sworn sisters. Together they are called the "Three Ladies".
A birth without a child. According to the novel, Chen Jinggu sacrificed her fetus to save her people. Some versions say she died of hemorrhage after the self-abortion.
