king of the frost giants in Norse mythology

Þrymr

In Norse mythology, Þrymr is a jötunn, the king of the frost giants and the thief of Thor's hammer. He is best known from the Eddic poem Þrymskviða, which gives him his name and tells how Thor won back his weapon.

The myth

According to the Eddic poem Þrymskviða, Þrymr stole Mjǫlnir and buried it eight leagues underground, demanding the goddess Freyja as his bride in exchange for its return. Loki flew to Jǫtunheimr in Freyja's feather cloak and found the giant sitting on a mound, twisting gold leashes for his dogs.

Thor dressed as a bride, with Loki posing as her handmaiden. At the feast he devoured an astonishing amount of food, and Loki explained that Freyja was so excited she had not eaten for days. When the giant tried to kiss the bride and lifted her veil, Thor's fiery glare sent him reeling across the hall. Þrymr then had the hammer brought in to bless the union, and Thor seized it, threw off his disguise and slew the giant and all his kin.

Legacy

A moon of Saturn was named after him. In the tabletop game Dungeons & Dragons he is the god of frost giants, and he is the main villain of the novel Hellboy: The Bones of Giants by Christopher Golden, illustrated by Mike Mignola. He also appears in the anime Sword Art Online as the king of the ice giants.

Ancient sources: Þrymskviða, from the Poetic Edda; Hversu Noregr byggðist, and the þulur appended to the Prose Edda.

Trivia

Several Þrymrs. The Hversu Noregr byggðist mentions three figures named Þrymr, including a king and a jötunn of Verma, showing that the name was common in Norse genealogies.
