queen of the Amazons in Greek mythology

Hippolyta

Hippolyta is the queen of the Amazons, daughter of Ares and Otrera, and a central figure in the myths of Heracles and Theseus. Her name means roughly "she who unleashes the horses", and ancient accounts portray her so differently that some seem to refer to several women.

The myth

According to legend, Hippolyta owned a magical belt, the zoster, given to her by her father Ares. Heracles was sent to fetch it as his ninth labour, for Admete, daughter of King Eurystheus. Most versions say Hippolyta was so impressed by the hero that she handed it over willingly, perhaps while visiting his ship. In Pseudo-Apollodorus, however, Hera disguised herself as an Amazon and spread a rumour that Heracles was abducting the queen; the Amazons attacked the ship, and the hero killed Hippolyta and took the belt.

In another tradition, Heracles abducts her sister Melanippe and demands the belt as ransom. Some accounts add that he killed Hippolyta and gave her battle-axe to Omphale; the Lydian kings later kept it as a royal emblem, and the Lydians called this weapon the labrys.

In the story of Theseus, who had joined Heracles, Theseus abducts another sister, Antiope, and the Amazons attack Athens to free her, without success. Hippolyta flees to Megara and dies there of grief. Some versions say Theseus marries Hippolyta or Antiope and has a son, Hippolytus. Later Theseus abandons his wife to marry Phaedra; Hippolyta gathers the Amazons to attack the wedding, but dies in the battle, killed by Theseus' men or by her sister Penthesilea, depending on the version.

Legacy

Hippolyta appears in Vivaldi's opera Hercules on the Thermodon, based on a version of Heracles' ninth labour. In William Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream she is the intended bride of Duke Theseus. The asteroid (10295) Hippolyta is named after her. In DC Comics' Wonder Woman, created by William Moulton Marston, she is Diana's mother, and in the novel La Rebelión de los robots she appears as an antagonist.

Ancient sources: Homer, Herodotus, Apollonius Rhodius, Diodorus Siculus, Seneca, Plutarch and Pseudo-Apollodorus mention her in accounts of the belt and the war with Athens.

Trivia

An axe with a Lydian name. According to some sources, the Lydians called the battle-axe attributed to Hippolyta a labrys, and the statue of Zeus Labrandeus in Caria holds it.
Many Hippolytas. Ancient authors disagree so much that some attribute the events to Antiope, Melanippe or Glauce instead of Hippolyta.
