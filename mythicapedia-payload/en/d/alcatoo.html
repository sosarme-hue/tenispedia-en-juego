king of Megara, son of Pelops

Alcathous

Alcathous is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Pelops, who became king of Megara. He matters mainly for his legend of the Lion of Cithaeron, the rebuilding of the walls of Megara and the origin of several local cults.

The myth

According to tradition, Alcathous was the son of Pelops and Hippodamia, and brother of Atreus and Thyestes. King Megareus had promised his daughter Euaechme and the succession to the throne to whoever killed the Lion of Cithaeron, a beast that had already killed his son Euippus and his eldest son Timalcus. Alcathous overcame the animal, married Euaechme and, after Megareus died, inherited the kingdom.

As king he rebuilt the walls of Megara, which the Cretans had destroyed. The Megarians said Apollo helped him with the work and left his lyre on a stone that still sounds like an instrument when struck. Alcathous had several children. Ischepolis died during the Calydonian hunt in Aetolia, and when Callipolis ran up to the acropolis to tell his father, Alcathous was making a sacrifice to Apollo. Thinking the young man was committing sacrilege by throwing wood from the altar, he struck him dead in anger. Polyidus later came to purify him of the murder. According to Pausanias, Alcathous was also grandfather of Ajax and Iolaus through his daughters Periboea and Automedusa.

Worship and religion

Alcathous gave his name to the acropolis of Megara. The city celebrated games called the Alcathoea, a name attested in only one source, the scholia to Pindar's odes. The hero's sanctuary served as an archive in the historical period, and as late as the 2nd century CE visitors were shown the altar of the Prodomeis, where Alcathous had sacrificed before building the wall, and the temple of Apollo and Artemis he founded. There is no evidence that this cult survives today.

Legacy

Some scholars see Alcathous as a counterpart to Heracles, since both defeated lions in their youth. His daughter's marriage to Telamon may have justified Megarian control over Salamis, of which Telamon was considered king. When the island passed to Athens, Periboea came to be regarded as an Athenian. The Alcathoea games are his main mark on the city's religious life.

Ancient sources: Pausanias describes his temple, Callipolis's tomb and the walls of Megara in his Description of Greece.

Trivia

A stone that sounds. The Megarians claimed a stone near the acropolis kept Apollo's lyre, and that striking it with a small pebble made it sound like an instrument.
An interrupted sacrifice. According to the story, Alcathous killed his son Callipolis believing he was profaning the sacrifice, before learning that his brother Ischepolis had died in Aetolia.
