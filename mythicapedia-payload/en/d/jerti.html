Egyptian god of the underworld

Kherty

Kherty is an ancient Egyptian god of the Duat, the underworld, usually depicted as a ram. He is attested from the Second Dynasty, and his figure, both protective and threatening, appears in the Pyramid Texts.

The myth

According to Egyptian funerary tradition, Kherty acted as a guide who ferried the dead king, as a boatman, to the afterlife and protected him from demons sent by Seth. He was also feared as death in person, a god who attacked the dying person's physical heart and made it stop beating.

In the Old Kingdom he was said to rule the afterlife alongside Osiris: Kherty held the entrance and corridors leading to the Hall of Truth, while Osiris ruled the hall and the Field of Reeds. Prayers were therefore addressed to Kherty to win his favour, and Ra was asked to take the king away from him. He does not appear in the Coffin Texts of the Middle Kingdom, where Aker replaces him as ferryman. In the Book of the Dead he is a guardian who guides the celestial barque of Ra.

Worship and religion

His cult dates to the early Second Dynasty, and the title 'god's servant of Kherty' appears under King Peribsen. His main centre was at Letopolis, today Ausim, and a second centre was later founded at Nesat, whose location is unknown.

Ancient sources: Egyptian Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts and Book of the Dead.
