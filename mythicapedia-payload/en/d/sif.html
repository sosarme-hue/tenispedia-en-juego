Norse goddess, wife of Thor

Sif

Sif is a goddess of Norse mythology and one of the Æsir, the wife of the thunder god Thor. She is associated with fertility, cultivated earth, marriage and kinship, and her golden hair is her most distinctive feature.

The myth

According to the stories, Sif was Thor's wife and the mother of Ullr and Þrúðr, and Snorri Sturluson also names Lóriði as her son. Her best-known trait was her long golden hair. In the Prose Edda, Loki cut it off while she slept, as a prank or out of envy depending on the version. When Thor discovered this, he seized Loki and forced him to promise a remedy: the Sons of Ivaldi, a group of dwarfs, would make Sif a headpiece of gold that would grow like natural hair.

Loki kept his word, but the dwarfs also forged other treasures for the gods: Odin's spear Gungnir, the ship Skíðblaðnir, the boar Gullinbursti for Freyr, the ring Draupnir and Thor's hammer Mjöllnir. In the Poetic Edda, Sif appears in Lokasenna, where Loki accuses her of having a lover, and in Hymiskviða and Þrymskviða Thor is called the husband of Sif. In the Prose Edda, the giant Hrungnir, drunk, boasts that he wants to take Sif home with him, but Thor kills him in a duel.

Legacy

Her name comes from an old Norse word meaning relation by marriage, which is why scholars link her to family and wedlock. Some researchers have proposed that her golden hair represents fields of ripe wheat, and that her figure is tied to fertility and to the earth as a mother. In Värmland, Sweden, as recorded by Jacob Grimm in the nineteenth century, Thor's wife was affectionately called godmor, good mother. In modern cinema she appears in the Marvel Thor films as a black-haired Asgardian warrior, played by Jaimie Alexander.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda, Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and skaldic poetry.
