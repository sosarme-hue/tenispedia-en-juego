nymph who nursed Dionysus in Greek myth

Nysa

Nysa is a nymph of Greek mythology, one of the nurses who raised Dionysus on Mount Nysa. According to Diodorus Siculus she was a daughter of Aristaeus, and her figure is tied to the childhood of the god of wine.

The myth

In the myth, Zeus wanted to shield Dionysus, his son by Semele, from Hera's wrath. He entrusted the child to Hermes, who handed him over to the Nysiads, the nymphs who lived on Mount Nysa. Nysa was one of them, alongside her sisters Ciseis, Erato, Erophyle, Bromia and Polymnia.

Silenus also lived in that region; he protected Dionysus, became his tutor and later his loyal companion. In some accounts the Nysiads are confused with the Hyades, or the term is used collectively for the Pleiades and the Hyades as the god's guardians. At the end of the story, Zeus turned the nymphs into stars as a reward, forming a constellation.

Legacy

In the Hellenistic period Nysa came to be personified as Dionysus's nurse, and she was said to be buried at Scythopolis (Beit Shean) in Palestine, a city that claimed Dionysus as its founder. Several cities named Nysa are thought to have been named in her honour, and the asteroid (44) Nysa also bears her name.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions her in his Hymn XXVI to Dionysus, and Diodorus Siculus, in his Bibliotheca historica, makes her a daughter of Aristaeus.

Trivia

A nurse with two names. Some sources also give her the name Macris as an equivalent.
An asteroid named after her. The asteroid (44) Nysa carries this name, in memory of the nymph.
