Chinese Taoist deity of healing

Wong Tai Sin

Wong Tai Sin, also known as Huang Daxian, is a Taoist deity from China, widely worshipped in Jinhua and Hong Kong for his healing powers. His divine figure is based on Huang Chuping, a shepherd who became a Taoist hermit and, according to tradition, attained immortality.

The myth

According to the text Self-Description of Chisongzi, preserved at the temple in Hong Kong, Wong was born Cho Ping Wong in Lanxi, in Zhejiang province. Western sources place his life somewhat later, between 284 and 364.

Tradition says he suffered poverty and hunger and was already working as a shepherd at the age of eight. At fifteen he began practising Taoism after meeting an immortal in his hometown. Decades later, according to legend, he could turn stones into sheep. He eventually became known as the Red Pine Immortal.

Worship and religion

His main shrine, the Wong Tai Sin Temple in Hong Kong, is one of the city's most visited tourist sites and draws many devotees. The temple grew out of a shrine that a devotee named Leung set up in 1921 after a healing attributed to the deity. His birthday is celebrated on the 23rd day of the eighth lunar month.

Legacy

A district and an MTR station in Hong Kong bear his name. The charity Sik Sik Yuen, which grew out of the original shrine, runs free clinics and schools. The figure also starred in TVB series and films in the eighties and nineties.

Ancient sources: The ancient text Self-Description of Chisongzi (Master Red Pine) recounts his life.

Trivia

Stones turned into sheep. Legend credits Wong with the ability to transform stones into sheep, a feat that symbolises his power over nature.
A shrine that became a temple. The Hong Kong sanctuary began as an altar at the back of a herbal medicine shop, after a healing that people credited to the deity.
