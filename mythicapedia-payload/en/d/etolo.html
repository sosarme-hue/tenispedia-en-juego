Aetolus

Aetolus is, in Greek mythology, a son of Endymion and the eponymous ancestor of the Aetolians, who took their name from him. His story explains how a people of central Greece received both their identity and their land.

The myth

According to tradition, Aetolus was a son of Endymion, king of Elis. His mother is given different names depending on the author: a Naiad nymph or Iphianassa in one source, and Asterodia, Chromia or Hyperippe in others. His brothers were Paeon, Epeius and Eurycyda.

Endymion settled the succession with a race at Olympia. Epeius won and took Elis, while Aetolus stayed in the Peloponnese and, on his brother's death, became king. Some accounts say that Salmoneus, king of the Epeans and Pisatans, drove him out, and that Aetolus took refuge in lands later called Aetolia.

During funeral games for Azan, chariot races were held in Greece for the first time. In them Aetolus accidentally ran over and killed Apis, son of Phoroneus or of Jason, and Apis's sons forced him into exile. He reached the mouth of the Achelous, on the northern side of the Gulf of Corinth, where he killed the Curetes Dorus, Polypoetes and Laodocus and gave the region his name. He drove the Curetes into Acarnania, and according to the myth the Aetolians, with the Epeans who had followed him, founded the oldest cities there. He is said to have married Pronoe, with whom he had two eponymous sons, Pleuron and Calydon.

Other traditions make him a son of Amphictyon and father of Physcius, and in those he rules Locris. The Argonaut Palaemon is also named as his son in some accounts.

Legacy

His name is tied to the region of Aetolia and its people, the Aetolians, who regarded him as their founding ancestor. Pleuron and Calydon gave their names to Aetolian cities.

Ancient sources: Pausanias records several genealogies of Aetolus and his account of the expulsion and the conquest of Aetolia.

Trivia

The first chariot races. According to the myth, the chariot races at Azan's funeral games were the first held in Greece, and it was there that Apis died accidentally.
A name passed from man to people. Aetolia takes its name from the hero, and the Aetolians regarded him as their founding ancestor.
