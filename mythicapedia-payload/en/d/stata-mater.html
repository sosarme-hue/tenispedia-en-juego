Roman goddess protecting against fire

Stata Mater

Stata Mater, whose name means "Mother who stops or stabilizes", was a Roman compital goddess who protected the city against fire. Her cult was especially prominent in the Forum and in the city's neighborhoods.

The myth

Ancient sources say more about her cult than about any mythical story. Her image stood in the Forum, and from there her worship spread through the city's neighborhoods (vici), according to Festus.

Tradition linked the origin of her statue to an Aurelius Cotta, who had supervised new paving in the Forum at the end of the 80s BC. The goddess's role was to protect the stonework from fire damage.

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on the compital shrines of the neighborhoods, where the heads of neighborhood associations (vicomagistri) left numerous inscriptions. Only the Lares Augusti outnumber her surviving dedications. Her popularity reflects the constant danger of fire in metropolitan Rome. She was sometimes given the title Augusta, perhaps linked to the reorganization of the city's regions under Augustus, which created neighborhood boards tasked with fire control. Her cult is an example of localized imperial cult.

Ancient sources: Festus mentions her cult and the neighborhoods where it spread, and inscriptions from compital shrines preserve her dedications.

Trivia

Place in the indigitamenta. The scholar W. H. Roscher includes her among the indigitamenta, the list of deities Roman priests kept so that the correct divinity would be invoked in rituals.
A neighborhood named after her. Rome had a Vicus Statae Matris, or Stata Mater's Neighborhood, located on the Caelian Hill.
