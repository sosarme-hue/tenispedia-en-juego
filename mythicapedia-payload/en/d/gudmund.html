semi-legendary king of the Norse warriors' paradise

Guðmundr

Guðmundr (Gudmund) is a semi-legendary king of Norse mythology who ruled Glæsisvellir, a warriors' paradise in Jotunheim. He matters because after his death the Norwegians came to worship him as a god.

The myth

According to the Hervarar saga, the men of Glæsisvellir lived several times as long as ordinary men, so the realm seemed out of reach. His son Höfund married Hervor, and their sons were Angantýr and Heiðrekr.

In the legend of Helgi Hundingsbane, Guðmundr faces Helgi and Sinfjötli, trading taunts with Sinfjötli from the shore before the fight. According to the tale, he falls beneath Helgi's blade. Saxo Grammaticus, in the Gesta Danorum, calls him a giant and the brother of Geirröðr.

Worship and religion

After his death he was worshipped as a god, and sacrifices were offered to him.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar saga (the saga of Hervör and King Heiðrekr), the legend of Helgi Hundingsbane in the Poetic Edda, and Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A horse-like nickname. He is sometimes given the epithet faxi, meaning "the one with a mane", which is also a word for horse. Some scholars link it to the army of the dead said to roam Norway at Yule.
A name shared with his father. Father and son were both called Guðmundr. To tell them apart, the son was given the added name Úlfhéðinn.
