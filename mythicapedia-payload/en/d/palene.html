Macedonian or Thracian princess, daughter of Sithon

Pallene

Pallene is a Macedonian or Thracian princess, daughter of King Sithon, whose name was given to an ancient peninsula and city. Her story links her to a deadly contest for suitors and, in a later version, to the god Dionysus.

The myth

According to the most common version, King Sithon promised his daughter only to whoever defeated him in combat, but he killed every suitor. Pallene loved Clitus and, to save him, had Dryas' chariot wheels come loose, so Clitus won. Sithon tried to kill his daughter, but Aphrodite snatched her to safety.

In Nonnus' Dionysiaca, Dionysus arrives disguised as a mortal suitor to punish the king. He defeats him in a wrestling match, Sithon dies at the god's hand, and Pallene marries Dionysus, though he soon leaves to resume his adventures.

Worship and religion

Coins from Potidaea show a nymph identified with Pallene, and Aphytis worshipped the nymphs and Dionysus.

Legacy

Her name was given to the Pallene peninsula, now Kassandra, and to an ancient city. Some also link her to the Gallipoli peninsula.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis in the Dionysiaca; Parthenius and Conon preserve tales from Hegesippus of Mecyberna and Theagenes, Macedonian authors.

Trivia

An earlier model. The tale resembles that of Pelops, who beats King Oenomaus in a chariot race to win Hippodamia, after bribing a servant to sabotage the king's chariot.
The peninsula of the Sithones. The middle peninsula, Sithonia, took its name from the Sithones, a Thracian people driven out by the Argead Macedonians.
