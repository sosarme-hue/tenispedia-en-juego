possible Slavic water deity or demon

Pereplut

Pereplut is a Slavic deity, or perhaps a demon, whose function is unclear. We know of him almost only from lists of gods and demons in medieval Christian texts, which leaves many questions open about his real role.

The myth

Sources tell us very little about Pereplut. He appears in the list of gods and demons in a twelfth-century Ruthenian interpolation of the Word of St. Gregory, whose manuscript dates from the fifteenth century, and also in the Word of St. John. The passage is damaged, and his name may have been distorted when the text was copied.

Alexei Sobolewski corrected one word in the text and understood Pereplut as a demon who brought prosperity when people crossed river thresholds, which would link him to a water deity. His name would derive from pere-, "through", and pluti, "to flow". Stanisław Urbańczyk reconstructed it as Pereput, and Boris Rybakov considered him a variant of the archaic deity Simargl.

Worship and religion

Christian texts say that pagan Slavs worshipped him, but the passage is incomplete. The verbs for spinning and drinking in it may be traces of magical rituals with dance and libation. There is no surviving evidence of temples or of any modern cult.

Ancient sources: The Word of St. Gregory (twelfth-century Ruthenian interpolation, preserved in a fifteenth-century manuscript) and the Word of St. John, both from Christian tradition.
