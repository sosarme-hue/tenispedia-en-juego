Roman nymph of the ebb and flow of the sea

Venilia

Venilia is a nymph of Roman mythology who personifies the ebb and flow of the waves and is also associated with favorable winds. Ancient writers linked her name to venire ("to come") and ventus ("wind").

The myth

According to Virgil, Venilia was the mother of Turnus, whose father was Daunus, and her sister Amata was the wife of Latinus. Ovid, by contrast, gives her a child by Janus, Canens, whose songs enchanted nature and who became the wife of Picus.

Servius identifies her with Salacia, the wife of Neptune. The philologist Georges Dumézil saw her as Neptune's paredra and proposed that the two goddesses expressed two modes of the god's power: Salacia the leaping, rebellious water, and Venilia the calm, gentle water. For Walter F. Otto she was a clan deity of the Venilii family.

Legacy

One of the mountains on the planet Venus, Venilia Mons, bears her name.

Ancient sources: Varro, Plautus, Virgil, Ovid and Servius mention her in their works.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Varro linked the name Venilia to the verb venire ("to come") and to the word ventus ("wind"), and Plautus quotes a line that plays on that idea.
A mountain on Venus. One of the surface features of Venus is named after the Roman nymph.
