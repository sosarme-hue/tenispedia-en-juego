Hindu goddess of misfortune

Alakshmi

Alakshmi is the Hindu goddess of misfortune, bad luck and poverty, and the elder sister of Lakshmi, goddess of fortune. Her name means "not Lakshmi", in other words her opposite.

The myth

According to one account, Lakshmi and Alakshmi emerged from the ocean of milk when gods and demons churned it. Alakshmi came from the poison Kalakuta dripping from the mouth of the serpent Vasuki, and she is the elder of the two. Another tradition says Lakshmi was born from the face of Prajapati and Alakshmi from his back.

While Lakshmi married Vishnu and lived in the paradise of Vaikuntha, Alakshmi had neither husband nor home. Resentful, she claimed a place of her own, and Lakshmi, or Vishnu himself, decreed that her husband would be Mrityu, god of death, and that her home would be any place marked by filth, laziness, envy or greed. Wherever she enters, she brings jealousy and malice: brothers quarrel and family lines fall to ruin.

In other texts she is identified with Nirriti, goddess of destruction, and with a form of the great Mahadevi. She is described as repellent, with a shriveled body, bull's teeth and antelope's feet, riding a donkey and sometimes accompanied by an owl.

Worship and religion

Devotees of Lakshmi avoid owls, which symbolize arrogance and foretell misfortune. In northern India, a lime and seven green chilies are hung at the doorstep to ward her off, or to offer her a share. During Deepavali, the festival of Lakshmi, she is ceremonially banished from the home with rituals and the Alakshmi Nashana Mantra.

Legacy

Her figure serves as an image of the effort needed before success: the hardship that forces people to work so that prosperity can arrive.

Ancient sources: She is mentioned in the Mahabharata and the Kalki Purana; the Padma Purana includes her in the account of the churning of the ocean of milk.

Trivia

Name. Alakshmi combines the negative prefix a- with Lakshmi, meaning "not Lakshmi" or "anti-Lakshmi".
Lime and chilies. In northern Indian homes, a lime with seven green chilies is hung at the door. Some believe the smell is unbearable to her; others think it offers her food while her sister enters.
