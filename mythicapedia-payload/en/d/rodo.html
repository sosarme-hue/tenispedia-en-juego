Greek goddess of the island of Rhodes, wife of Helios

Rhodos

Rhodos is the sea goddess who gave her name to the island of Rhodes and was its protector in Greek myth. She was the wife of Helios, and their seven sons, the Heliadae, ruled the island after the Telchines.

The myth

According to myth, Rhodos was the daughter of Poseidon, though her mother's name varies by author: Halia, Amphitrite, Aphrodite or Pholife. Pindar makes her a daughter of Aphrodite with no father named, while Apollodorus calls her the daughter of Poseidon and Amphitrite and sister of Triton. Epimenides places her among the descendants of Oceanus, and a scholion makes her a daughter of the river Asopus.

The tradition tells that when the gods divided the earth, Helios was absent and received nothing. He asked Zeus for a new island that had not yet risen from the sea, and Rhodes emerged. With Helios, Rhodos bore seven sons, the Heliadae, and a daughter, Electryone. In another version, Helios drained the waters that had flooded the island and named it after his wife.

Some of the brothers killed Tenages and had to flee: Macareus to Lesbos, Candalus to Cos, Triopas to Caria, and Actis to Egypt, where he founded Heliopolis. Those who stayed began building the Colossus in honour of their father.

Worship and religion

Rhodos was the tutelary goddess of the island in the Hellenistic period, and she was worshipped on Rhodes alongside Halia or Leucothea. Rhodian coins sometimes showed her head beside that of Helios.

Legacy

The rose, Greek rhodon, became the island's symbol, and her name is etymologically linked to the flower.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Apollodorus, Diodorus Siculus, Epimenides, and scholia on Pindar and the Odyssey.

Trivia

An island rising from the sea. According to Pindar, Rhodes rose from the sea after Helios claimed an island still submerged while the gods divided the earth.
A rose on the coins. The rose became the symbol of Rhodes, and some Rhodian coins showed the goddess's head.
