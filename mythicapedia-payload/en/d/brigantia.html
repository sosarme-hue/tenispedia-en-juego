Brigantia

Brigantia is a protective goddess of the Brigantes, the Celtic tribal confederation of northern Britain, and in Irish and Galician legend she also appears as a mythical city raised by King Breogán. She matters because her name, meaning roughly 'the high one', links her to the Irish goddess Brigid.

The myth

Little is known about her myths, since no classical author mentions her. Most of what we know comes from Latin votive inscriptions of the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD, where she appears as dea Brigantia, and from a relief at Birrens in Scotland that shows her with the helmet and spear of Minerva, the wings and globe of Victoria, and a mural crown marking her as guardian of Brigantian land.

In Irish and Galician tradition, by contrast, Brigantia is a city. According to the medieval Leabhar Gabhála Érenn, King Breogán built it with a tall tower from which the coasts of Ireland could be seen. After his son Ith was killed on the island, Breogán's descendants, led by Míl, conquered Ireland with only a thousand men. Some scholars identify this legendary city with the Roman Brigantium, whose site is disputed between Betanzos and A Coruña in Spain.

Worship and religion

Inscriptions show that she had worshippers across the territory of the Brigantes, from Yorkshire to Dumfries and Galloway, mostly near Hadrian's Wall. Some dedications call her Victoria Brigantia, tying the tribal protector to military victory. At Greetland, a dedicant who describes himself as master of the sacred rites was probably her priest. There is no sign that her cult survived the Roman period.

Legacy

Her name is related to that of the Brigantes and to that of the Irish goddess Brigid, though scholars still debate how closely the two are connected. Brigantia also lends her name to rivers: the Brent in England, the Braint on Anglesey and the Brighid in Ireland. In an early Welsh poem, the Braint overflows at the death of a king.

Ancient sources: Latin votive inscriptions of the 2nd and 3rd centuries AD and the Birrens relief; the Irish Leabhar Gabhála Érenn tells of Breogán's city.

Trivia

A mix of goddesses. On the Birrens relief, the goddess wears Minerva's gorgon and Victoria's wings, yet her mural crown ties her to Brigantian territory.
A name meaning height. The name comes from a Celtic root meaning roughly 'elevated', the same element found in many Celtic place-names ending in -briga.
