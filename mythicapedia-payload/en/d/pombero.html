Guarani spirit of the night and the countryside

Pombero

The Pombero is a spirit of the rural forests and fields in Guarani culture, widely believed in Paraguay, northeast Argentina, southern Brazil and southeastern Bolivia. He is seen as a guardian of nature and birds, and how he treats people depends on whether he receives offerings or is offended.

The myth

According to tradition, the Pombero is a short, unsightly being with very short arms and hairy hands and feet. He never shows his face, usually wears a large straw hat and, in some versions, black clothing. His hairy feet let him approach unheard, and he can take the form of a bird, a floating log or inert objects.

The stories present him as a mischievous figure of ambiguous character. He grows angry when a hunter kills more game than he will eat or when trees are felled needlessly, and then he leads the offender astray deep into the forest. Those who leave him honey, cane liquor or tobacco may find him helpful with crops and livestock. After asking him for a favour, one must leave the same offering every night for thirty nights; if it is forgotten, his anger brings havoc to the home. He is also blamed for household mischief, such as doors slamming shut by themselves, and is said to drink the beer at gatherings.

Legacy

He lends his name to an album by the Argentine band Kchiporros and another by Haitón del Guarataro. He appears in the 1976 film Embrujada and in the episode "El Luisón y el Pombero" of Destination Truth. The video game Pombero - The Lord of Night was released in 2020 and Pomberito in 2024.

Trivia

Other names. He is known as Pomberito, Pyrague ("hairy feet") or Karai Pyhare ("lord of the night"). In the Esteros del Iberá he is called Kuarahy Jára, "master of the sun".
Protector of birds. He is said to imitate the calls of nocturnal birds and to protect birds from children who hunt them with slingshots.
