Greek goddess of daybreak

Auge

Auge is, in Greek mythology, the first of the Horae, goddesses who governed the passing of time and the seasons. She presides over the first light of day, the dawn, and is therefore linked to the beginning of the day.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Auge is one of the Horae, a group of minor goddesses tied to the hours of the day and to human activities. Although she was not originally counted among them, she came to be regarded as the first of the group, in charge of the hour of first light.

Accounts describe her as the sister of eleven other Horae, among them Anatole, sunrise, and Hesperis, evening, as well as Dysis, sunset, and Arctus, the night sky. Their father is not settled: some traditions name Helios, the sun, and others Chronos, time.
