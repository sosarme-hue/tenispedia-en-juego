Chinese legendary creature, half dragon and half dog

Denglong

The denglong is a legendary creature of Chinese tradition, often described as half dragon and half dog. It is associated with guardianship and was regarded as a protector of its master, especially the emperor.

The myth

According to some legends, the denglong is one of the sons of the Dragon King, and its main role is watchfulness. Buddhist tales make it one of the sons of the Naga King and the mount of the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, which explains its alternative name, Chaotianhou.

The accounts in Accounts of Marvels place it in the East China Sea. According to that text, it is fierce, can hover in mid-air, eats the brains of dragons and, in combat, spews flames for dozens of feet to defeat them. One episode from the 25th year of the Kangxi reign tells how a denglong died after fighting dragons and jiaolong; when it fell into a valley, its scales burst into flames.

Worship and religion

The denglong was venerated as a protective being that drove away evil, defended against hostile wishes, dispelled misfortune and guarded wealth. Its presence on the huabiao, ornamental pillars, symbolised the link between heaven and people.

Legacy

Denglong figures stand atop the huabiao of Tiananmen Gate in Beijing. The one inside the gate, named Wangdigui, faces north and reminds the emperor to return and govern. The one outside, Wangdichu, faces south and urges him to go out and learn about the people's concerns.
