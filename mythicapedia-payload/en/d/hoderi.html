Japanese god of the sea and fishing

Hoderi

Hoderi is a figure of Japanese mythology linked to the sea and fishing, the eldest son of Ninigi and older brother of Hoori. His quarrel with Hoori over a magic fishhook explains the origin of the Hayato, who according to the myth still guard the palace today.

The myth

According to the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Hoderi was born with his brothers to Konohanasakuya-hime, the wife of Ninigi, after she proved her fidelity by giving birth amid a fire. Hoderi, born when the flames burned most fiercely, received a magic fishhook from his father and became a skilled fisherman, while his brother Hoori became a hunter with a magic bow.

One day Hoderi asked to swap gifts with Hoori. Hoori agreed to please his elder brother, but lost the hook at sea. Furious, Hoderi demanded its return and refused any compensation. Hoori broke his own sword and forged its pieces into five hundred fishhooks, yet Hoderi would not accept them.

Hoori searched for the lost hook and reached the palace of the sea deity Watatsumi, where he met the god's daughter Toyotama and married her. Watatsumi summoned the fish, found the hook in the throat of a sea bream, and gave it to Hoori along with two jewels that could raise and lower the tides. When Hoori returned, Hoderi attacked him, but Hoori raised a tide that nearly drowned him. Hoderi begged for mercy, and Hoori lowered the water with the second jewel. Grateful, Hoderi vowed that he and his descendants would serve Hoori and his children forever. According to the myth, his descendants are the Hayato.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki record him as the eldest son of Ninigi and the ancestor of the Hayato people of Aka, in Satsuma and Ōsumi.

Trivia

Five hundred hooks. To make up for the loss, Hoori broke his sword and forged its fragments into five hundred fishhooks, which Hoderi rejected.
A name of fire. His name is usually interpreted as «fire shine», in reference to the flames in which he was born.
