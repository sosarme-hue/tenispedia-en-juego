queen of Thrace turned into a mountain range

Rhodope

Rhodope is, in Greek mythology, the queen of Thrace and wife of Haemus. For their arrogance, she and her husband were turned into mountains, and the Rhodope range takes its name from her.

The myth

Rhodope and her husband Haemus, king of Thrace, enjoyed a good marriage, but over time they grew arrogant and began calling themselves Zeus and Hera, the highest gods. As punishment, the gods turned them into icy peaks: he became the Haemus, today the Balkan Mountains, and she became the Rhodopes.

A parodic text attributed to Plutarch, now known not to be his, presents them as siblings whose incestuous union, together with their hubris, provoked divine anger. Another tradition mentions a Rhodope, daughter of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, who played with Persephone before her abduction, though it is unclear whether she is the same figure. Athena, in her weaving contest with the Lydian maiden Arachne, wove Rhodope's fate into her tapestry as a warning to anyone who dared challenge the gods. These tales belong to the stone myths, in which petrification punishes lust or contrasts cold, inanimate stone with human love.

Legacy

The Rhodope Mountains, now shared by Bulgaria and Greece, still bear her name. The river Hebrus, named after her son, is today the Maritsa, which forms a border between West and East Thrace.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Demeter mentions a Rhodope who played with Persephone, and a scholiast makes her the daughter of the river-god Strymon.

Trivia

A mountain range named after her. The Rhodope Mountains are now shared between Bulgaria and Greece, and their name comes from the legend of the queen turned into a mountain.
A river with a new name. The Hebrus, named after Rhodope's son, is now more commonly known as the Maritsa.
