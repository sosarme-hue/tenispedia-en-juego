maiden of Greek myth turned into a tortoise

Chelone

Chelone is a maiden, or sometimes a river nymph, in Greek myth who was turned into a tortoise for mocking the wedding of Zeus and Hera. She matters mainly because her story explains why the tortoise always carries its house on its back.

The myth

According to Servius' gloss, Zeus asked Hermes to invite all the gods, men and animals to his wedding with Hera. Everyone came except Chelone, who refused to attend and mocked the union. Hermes, noticing her absence, returned to earth, threw the house of the young woman, which stood on a riverbank, into the water, and transformed her into the animal that bears her name, condemned to carry her home forever.

Aesop tells a related version: the tortoise skips Zeus' banquet and, when asked why, replies that one's own home is the best. The god punishes her by making her walk about with her house on her back. Servius does not say who her parents were or whether she was a nymph, though some have suspected it.

Ancient sources: Servius, in a gloss, and Aesop's fable of Zeus and the tortoise. Pausanias mentions in passing Mount Chelydorea, whose name means flayed tortoise.

Trivia

Flayed tortoise. Pausanias says Mount Chelydorea, near Cyllene, means flayed tortoise. There Hermes is said to have made a lyre from a tortoise's shell.
Tortoise or turtle. The Greek word khelonê referred to both the land tortoise and the sea turtle, so the character's name covers both animals.
