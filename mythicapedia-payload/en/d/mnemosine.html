Greek goddess of memory, mother of the Muses

Mnemosyne

Mnemosyne is the Greek goddess of memory, the personification of the power to recall the past. In Hesiod's Theogony she is one of the Titans, daughter of Uranus and Gaia, and the mother of the nine Muses by Zeus, which places her at the heart of Greek thinking about remembrance.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Zeus lay with Mnemosyne, the fair-haired lady of the hills of Eleuther, for nine consecutive nights, keeping away from the other gods. From their union were born nine maidens of one mind, devoted only to song. Ovid tells that Zeus disguised himself as a shepherd to approach her. Other authors give a different parentage: Hyginus makes her the daughter of Zeus and Clymene, and the Romans also called her Moneta.

Her role as a Titan is unusual. The Titans were hardly worshipped and seemed to belong to a distant past, whereas Mnemosyne appears at the opening of many oral epic poems, invoked to help the bard remember exactly what he was about to recite, in the Iliad and the Odyssey among others. Plato, in the Euthydemus, recalls the custom of invoking the Muses and Mnemosyne.

In the afterlife, tradition speaks of a river of Mnemosyne opposed to Lethe. Greek funerary inscriptions from the 4th century BC describe souls drinking from Lethe so they would forget their past lives, while initiated Orphics drank from the river Mnemosyne to keep the memory of the mysteries. Mnemo, a shortened form of her name, appears in the Orphic gold tablets.

Worship and religion

Her cult was modest but widespread. She was venerated at Athens, Tegea, Mount Helicon and Lebadeia. At Lebadeia in Boeotia she had a role in the oracle of Trophonius: those who consulted the god drank in turn from the springs of Lethe and Mnemosyne. At Ascra in Boeotia she was one of the three earliest Muses worshipped, together with Meletê and Aoide, and at Athens her statues stood beside those of the Muses, Zeus and Apollo. Later she entered the cult of Asclepius: the supplicant made an offering to Mnemosyne before sleeping in the sanctuary, hoping to remember the dreams he had there.

Legacy

Her name survives in language: it comes from mnēmē, 'memory', and gives us 'mnemonic'. Aristophanes parodied her in Lysistrata, where a drunken Spartan ambassador invokes her while pretending to be an old-time bard. The river of remembrance has been compared with Virgil's Eridanus and Dante's Eunoe.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony), Homer (Iliad and Odyssey), Plato (Euthydemus and Republic), Aristophanes (Lysistrata), Pausanias, Ovid and Hyginus all mention her.

Trivia

A river of memory. In the underworld there was a river of Mnemosyne, opposed to Lethe. While ordinary souls drank from Lethe to forget, initiated Orphics chose to drink from the river of remembrance.
Two springs at Lebadeia. Those who consulted the oracle of Trophonius at Lebadeia drank in turn from two springs, one called Lethe and the other Mnemosyne, representing forgetting and memory.
