Hippocampus

The hippocampus is a mythical sea horse with the forequarters of a horse and the hindquarters of a fish or sea monster. Its name comes from Greek, and it was mainly linked to Poseidon, who used it to pull his chariot across the sea.

The myth

The oldest texts do not present the hippocampus as an independent creature but as the horses of Poseidon. In the Iliad, Homer shows the god of the sea, horses and earthquakes driving a chariot over the waves, pulled by bronze-hooved horses. Apollonius of Rhodes tells how Poseidon's horse rose from the sea and galloped across the Libyan sands, while Valerius Flaccus describes hippocampi with two hooves.

Over time, poets and artists pictured these horses as a blend of horse and fish, about five metres long and strong enough to carry many sea creatures at once. In Hellenistic and Roman art, Poseidon often rides a sea chariot drawn by hippocampi.

Legacy

The hippocampus appears in heraldry from the Renaissance onward, especially on the arms of places and families with maritime ties. In heraldry, the word hippocampus now refers to the real seahorse, while the mythical creature is called a sea-horse. A winged hippocampus served as the symbol of Air France, and the Trevi Fountain in Rome shows it alongside Neptune. A small moon of Neptune has borne its name since 2019.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad describes Poseidon's sea-horses, and Apollonius of Rhodes and Valerius Flaccus also mention them.
