supreme deity of Chinese mythology

Di Jun

Di Jun, also known as Emperor Jun, is one of the ancient supreme deities of China. He is known mainly from five chapters of the Shanhaijing, the Classic of Mountains and Seas, so the details of his myths survive only in fragments.

The myth

According to tradition, Di Jun had two wives or consorts, Xihe and Changxi, and he figures in several stories of Chinese mythology. One of the best-known tales is that of the archer Houyi, to whom Di Jun gave a bow and arrows. It is one of the most famous episodes in which he appears.

Di Jun is also linked to the agricultural arts, either directly or as the progenitor of other farming innovators, especially his son Houji, ancestor of the Zhou. Some scholars think Di Jun and Di Ku were variations of a single original source.

Ancient sources: The Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas), in five chapters, is the main ancient reference for Di Jun.
