snake-like yōkai of Japanese folklore

Tsuchinoko

The tsuchinoko is a yōkai from Japanese folklore, a snake-like creature that belongs to the country's tradition of strange beings. Its name means "child of hammer," and it is one of the best-known creatures in Japan's fantastic bestiary.

The myth

According to the tradition, the tsuchinoko measures between 30 and 80 centimetres and has a body much wider at the middle than at the head or tail, giving it a bottle-like shape. It has venomous fangs like a viper's and, according to some accounts, can leap up to a metre, followed by a second jump while still airborne. Its sound is unpleasant.

Popular stories credit some of them with the ability to speak, a taste for lies and a fondness for alcohol. A widely repeated image shows the creature swallowing its own tail so that it can roll like a hoop. Tradition describes it as a peaceful, solitary animal.

Legacy

The tsuchinoko has a lasting place in Japanese popular culture. It appears in Doraemon, and Pokémon's Dunsparce was inspired by its descriptions. In Metal Gear Solid 3 it is one of the hardest species to find, and several Yu-Gi-Oh! cards are based on it. Some travel agencies organise tsuchinoko hunts in rural areas to attract visitors, offering large prizes to anyone who catches one. In Forbidden Siren, a bounty poster offers one million yen for finding a live one.

Trivia

The 1988 expedition. In 1988, a member of the village council of Shimokitayama, in Nara, organised an expedition to search for it, offering one million yen for a live one. It lasted until 1990 and found no proof that the creature existed.
A million-yen reward. The town of Yoshii, in Okayama Prefecture, once offered 20 million yen for the capture of one.
