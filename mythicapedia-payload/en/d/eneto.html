Greek mythical prince, son of Deioneus and Diomede

Aenetus

Aenetus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of King Deioneus and Diomede, and a prince of Phocis according to tradition. He is a very little-known character, although his parents and brothers play a prominent part in the stories.

The myth

According to tradition, Aenetus belonged to a royal line: his father, Deioneus, was a son of Aeolus, and his mother, Diomede, was a daughter of Xuthus. He was the brother of Cephalus, Actor, Phylacus and Asterodia, all of whom appear far more often in the stories than he does. No particular episode of his deeds or fate has been preserved.

Ancient texts say very little more about him. Modern scholars such as West consider his name to be a late formation, so he was probably an invention of Apollodorus rather than a figure from older tradition.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, to whom his invention is probably owed, is the ancient source that mentions him as a son of Deioneus and Diomede.
