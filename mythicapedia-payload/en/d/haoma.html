sacred plant and divinity of Zoroastrianism

Haoma

Haoma is, in Zoroastrianism and later Persian culture, a sacred plant, the ritual drink made from it, and the divinity who embodies it. Its origins lie in Indo-Iranian religion, and it is the Avestan counterpart of the Vedic soma.

The myth

According to Zoroastrian tradition, Haoma appeared before Zoroaster in the form of a beautiful man and asked him to gather and press the plant for the purification of the waters. The Yasna and the Hōm Yašt describe him as righteous, wise and a giver of revelation. He is said to have been the first priest, installed by Ahura Mazda with the sacred girdle, and to serve the Amesha Spentas in that role.

The legend of Zoroaster's conception also centres on the plant. His father, Pouroshaspa, mixed haoma with milk, and he and his wife Dugdhova drank the mixture; Zoroaster was thus conceived, imbued with the spirit of the plant. In Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, Hom appears as a mountain hermit of great strength who binds Afrasiab with the sacred girdle.

Worship and religion

Haoma was central to Zoroastrian ritual. The parahaoma was prepared for the Ab-Zohr, a rite of purification of the waters, by pounding twigs with a little water. Today Parsi Zoroastrians use a variety of ephedra imported from the Hari River valley in Afghanistan, since the plant does not grow in India. Some texts say Zoroaster condemned the worship of Haoma, and the divinity lost importance in the angelic hierarchy.

Legacy

The identity of haoma and the Vedic soma has long been debated. It has been proposed to be an ephedra, although some hypotheses linked it to a psychotropic substance. It remains a central element of Zoroastrian ritual and a reference point for the comparative study of both religions.

Ancient sources: The Avestan texts, especially Yasna 9-11 and the Hōm Yašt, along with the Denkart and Ferdowsi's Shahnameh.

Trivia

Modern Persian name. In Middle Persian the plant was called hōm, a name that remains in modern Persian and other living Iranian languages.
Zoroaster's conception. According to the Denkart, the substance of Zoroaster's body fell with the rain, passed into plants and into milk drunk by two heifers, and so he was conceived.
