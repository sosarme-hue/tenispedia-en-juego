legendary hero of Norse mythology

Örvar-Oddr

Örvar-Oddr, "Arrow-Oddr", is a legendary Norse hero who stars in an Icelandic saga written in the late 13th century. His story gathers voyages, battles and old legends, and it became very popular. According to the tale, as an infant he was foretold by a seeress that he would be killed by his horse Faxi at the age of three hundred. To avoid this, Oddr slew the animal, buried it and left home in Hålogaland. His father gave him magic arrows that earned him his nickname. His great friendship was with the Swedish champion Hjalmar, with whom he swore blood brotherhood. After the battle of Samsø, Oddr carried Hjalmar's corpse to Uppsala. He later sailed the Mediterranean, was baptised in Sicily and reached the Holy Land. He also posed as "bark man" and married the daughter of the king of Hunaland, Silkisif, becoming king himself. His death is the most famous episode: despite mocking the prophecy, he tripped over Faxi's skull, from which a snake emerged and bit him.

The myth

Ancient sources: The Ǫrvar-Odds saga, an Icelandic fornaldarsaga from the late 13th century, and the Hervarar saga and the Gesta Danorum, which also mention him.

Trivia

The death of Oleg. The motif of mocking the prophecy and dying from a horse's skull has parallels in the Primary Chronicle, which describes the death of Oleg of Novgorod.
A Russian ballad. Alexander Pushkin published in 1826 one of the best-known ballads in the Russian language about Oleg's death by a horse's skull.
