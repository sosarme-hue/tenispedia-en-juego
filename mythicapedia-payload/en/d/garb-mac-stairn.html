giant from Celtic tradition in Ireland and Scotland

Garb mac Stairn

Garb mac Stairn is a giant from Celtic tradition in Ireland and Scotland, an antagonist of Cú Chulainn in the Ulster Cycle. He appears in late prose tales and poems, perhaps from the 17th century, and is sometimes said to have Scandinavian roots.

The myth

In the late accounts, Garb arrives in Ireland demanding tribute, but in a Highland poem he is defeated and beheaded by Cú Chulainn. In a South Uist folk version he appears as the antagonist within an adaptation of the Táin Bó Cúailnge.

In a Highland folktale, Garb is a giant who lives nearby and owns a prized light-coloured bull. Cú Chulainn disguises himself as a herdsman, slips into the giant's home and crawls into the bed of his mistress, who mistakes him for a baby. The hero's cunning resolves the episode.

Legacy

James Macpherson linked Garbh mac Starn with Suaran, son of Starno, king of Lochlin, in his works attributed to Ossian.

Ancient sources: Late prose narratives and poems (duan) of the Ulster Cycle, perhaps from the 17th century.

Trivia

A baby in the bed. In the Highland tale, Garb's mistress mistakes Cú Chulainn, disguised as a herdsman, for a small baby when he crawls into her bed.
A giant in Ossian. In Macpherson's works attributed to Ossian, Suaran, son of Starno, is presented as a figure many associate with Garbh mac Starn.
