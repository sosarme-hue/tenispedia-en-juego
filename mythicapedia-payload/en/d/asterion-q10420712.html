giant of Greek mythology

Asterius

Asterius is a giant in Greek myth who fought against the gods and who, according to tradition, was killed by Athena. His name means 'starry' or 'bright'.

The myth

Accounts disagree about his origin. Pausanias describes him as a son of Anax, king of Anactoria, whose bones measured ten cubits and who was buried on a small island near Lade. Another tradition says Miletus, son of Apollo, defeated him and took his land. In the epic Meropis, an invulnerable warrior from Kos fights Heracles until Athena flays him. Apollodorus tells a similar story about a giant named Pallas, whose skin the goddess used as her aegis.

Worship and religion

The Athenians marked his death at the Panathenaea, the festival of Athena, and credited its founding to the city's earliest inhabitants. The gods' victory over the giants was woven into the festival's robe.

Legacy

He appears on the Siphnian Treasury at Delphi, a sixth-century BC marble frieze where he is labelled 'Astarias' lying dead on the ground.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Apollodorus, the Meropis and Euripides' Ion, which calls him Gorgon.

Trivia

A treasury at Delphi. The frieze of the Siphnian Treasury shows the Gigantomachy and is one of the best surviving examples of Archaic Greek art.
A shining name. His name comes from the Greek word for 'star', the same root shared by Astraeus and Astraea.
