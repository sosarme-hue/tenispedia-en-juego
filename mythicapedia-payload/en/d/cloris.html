Greek nymph of spring and flowers

Chloris

Chloris is a Greek nymph associated with spring, flowers and the growth of plant life. Tradition identifies her with the Roman goddess Flora, who shares those same roles.

The myth

According to myth, Chloris was an Oceanid, one of the many daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. Zephyrus, the god of the west wind, abducted her and married her, then transformed her into a goddess of flowers and gave her dominion over them. Her wind-god brother Boreas was said to have competed for her love as well.

Together they had two children: spring and Karpos, the god of fruit. Chloris was said to stay eternally young, and she was believed to have turned Adonis, Attis, Crocus, Hyacinthus and Narcissus into flowers.

In one of her best-known stories, Chloris helped Hera escape from Olympus and gave her shelter in one of her temples. Later, when Chloris asked Hera to pick the most beautiful flower in her garden, the small blossom Hera took in her hands turned into Ares, the god of war. In Ovid's account, Hera conceived Ares this way as revenge for the birth of Athena.

Worship and religion

Festivals were held in her honour in the streets, where young women sang and danced. She was very popular for a time. The sources do not say whether her cult survives today.

Legacy

In Rome her figure merged with that of Flora, who inherited her role as goddess of spring and vegetation. Flora was given a place in the city for her part in Ares' birth.

Ancient sources: Ovid, in his Fasti (Book V), presents her as the nymph who helped Hera conceive Ares.

Trivia

A flower that becomes a god. In one of the stories, a simple flower that Hera held in her hand turned into Ares, the god of war.
Flowers that were once men. According to tradition, Chloris turned several young men into flowers, among them Narcissus and Hyacinthus.
