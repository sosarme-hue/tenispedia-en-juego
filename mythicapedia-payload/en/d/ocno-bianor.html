legendary hero who founded Madrid

Ocno Bianor

Ocno Bianor is a legendary hero whom Madrid's local tradition credits with founding the city. His story links to Homer's Iliad, but it was created in the 17th century, during the rivalry with Valladolid over the capital.

The myth

According to the legend, the Trojan leader Bianor I was killed by Agamemnon, but his son Bianor II escaped and founded a kingdom in what is now Albania. Bianor III, born to the peasant woman Manto, was expelled and settled in Mantua.

Guided by a dream of Apollo, he took the name Ocno, meaning one who sees the future in dreams, and reached a grassy plain inhabited by the Carpetani, where he began building a city. To consecrate the temple, Apollo asked him to offer his own life, as the goddess Metragirta had once done. Ocno let himself be buried in a pit, and the goddess made him vanish amid a storm. The city was named Metragirta, which became Magerit and then Madrid.

Legacy

The tale spread through Jerónimo de Quintana's 1621 book and was used in the dispute with Valladolid over the capital.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions a Trojan leader named Bianor, from whom the legend takes its name.

Trivia

First written reference. The first written mention of Bianor is attributed to Francesc Tarafa, a canon of Barcelona Cathedral.
A name that is a prophecy. Ocno means one who sees the future through dreams, a name that reflects Apollo's role in the legend.
