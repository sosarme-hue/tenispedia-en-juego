Zoroastrian god of divine judgement

Rashnu

Rashnu is a yazata and the god of judgement in Zoroastrianism and ancient Iranian religion. His name relates to rectitude, and his role as judge of souls makes him a central figure of divine justice in Persian belief.

The myth

According to the texts, Rashnu is the son of Ohrmazd and Armaiti, and brother of Soroush, Mithra, Ahrishwang and Daena. In Middle Persian writings he appears as Rashn, with epithets meaning "true, just" and "straightest". The Bundahishn counts him as the thirteenth Iranian deity created. The twelfth Yasht of the Avesta is composed in his praise.

His foremost function is to sit in judgement over souls after death. He also takes part in the varah, a ritual ordeal in which an accused person swore to the truth of their claim and submitted to a test, and he protects Ohrmazd's creations. At the end of the world he will help Pēšōtan alongside Mithra and others. Finally, he encourages human beings to repent of their sins.

Legacy

Biruni mentions his place in the calendars of the Iranians, Sogdians and people of Khwarazm, and Persian poets refer to Rashn Day or Rash Day in their verse.

Ancient sources: His name and functions appear in the Avesta, especially the Rashn Yasht, and in Middle Persian texts such as the Bundahishn and the Dēnkard.

Trivia

Name and justice. His name derives from a root meaning "to move in a straight line" or "to direct", and its superlative, "the straightest", is his standing epithet.
Rashn Day. Persian poets mention a Rashn Day or Rash Day, showing that his memory lingered in the calendar.
