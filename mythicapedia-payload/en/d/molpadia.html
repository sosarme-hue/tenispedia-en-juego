Amazon of Greek mythology

Molpadia

Molpadia is an Amazon from Greek mythology who fought in the Attic War, sparked by Theseus's abduction of the queen Antiope. She is remembered mainly for her death and for the role attributed to her as a guide of the Amazons to Hades.

The myth

According to the tradition, the Amazons attacked Attica to win back their queen Antiope, whom Theseus had carried off. The assault was led by Queen Orithyia, and Molpadia fought in her ranks. During the battle, Antiope, who was fighting on the Greek side, was fatally struck by an arrow from Molpadia. Other versions say the queen's death was an accident.

Theseus avenged his wife's death by killing Molpadia. She was buried beside Antiope, and the Amazons mourned her. In time she came to be revered as a deity who led the Amazons to Hades.

Worship and religion

Her tomb, beside that of Antiope, was still shown in the time of Pausanias.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions her tomb, which was still pointed out in his day.

Trivia

Two women with the same name. Molpadia is also the name of a second woman, daughter of Staphylus and Chrysothemis and sister of Parthenos and Rhoeo, sometimes called Hemithea. The scholar Otto Gruppe suggested the name may have come from Boeotia.
A name with an echo of death. Some scholars believe her name was inspired by a deity who escorted the dead, which would fit her role as a guide to Hades.
