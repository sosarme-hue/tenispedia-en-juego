dwarf demon of ignorance in Hinduism

Apasmara

Apasmara is a dwarf-like being in Hindu mythology who embodies spiritual ignorance and the meaningless chatter that keeps beings trapped in maya. He appears in Shiva's iconography as Nataraja, the Lord of the Dance, who tramples him under his right foot.

The myth

According to tradition, Apasmara is a small figure, often shown clutching a cobra, which symbolizes ahamkara, the I-maker, the faculty by which souls identify with the physical body rather than their higher self. Sources link him to the ignorance of selfhood, a universal form of forgetting that lets beings function in samsara across countless rebirths, each time forgetting past lives.

The story holds that Apasmara must be subdued, not killed, since destroying him would upset the balance between spiritual knowledge and ignorance, and wisdom would be gained without effort or dedication. To subdue him, Shiva takes the form of Nataraja and performs the cosmic dance of tāṇḍava. During it he crushes the demon under his right foot, and because Apasmara is one of the few demons destined for immortality, he is believed to remain pinned there forever.

Legacy

The English writer Aldous Huxley reworked the symbol in his utopian novel Island, where he describes Muyalaka as a horrible little subhuman creature trampled beneath Nataraja's foot. Huxley notes that the pointing finger aims not at the trampling foot but at the raised left foot, the sign of release and moksha. In Ayurveda, the name refers to a group of neurological disorders, one of which may correspond to epilepsy.

Trivia

A cobra in hand. In iconography he is often shown clutching a cobra, which in the Shaivite tradition symbolizes ahamkara.
Four types in Ayurveda. According to Caraka, there are four types of apasmara: vataja, pitaja, kapahaja and sannipataja, classified by the body's doshas.
