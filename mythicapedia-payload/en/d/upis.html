Hyperborean maiden, companion of Artemis

Upis

Upis is a maiden from Hyperborea, daughter of Boreas, the north wind god, who served as a companion of Artemis on Delos. She is closely tied to the goddess, to the point that some regarded her as an aspect of Artemis herself.

The myth

According to the tradition, Upis and her sisters, whose names vary by author (Arge, Hecaerge and Loxo), were the first to reach Delos with Apollo and Artemis, ahead of Hyperoche and Laodice, who carried the offering promised to Eileithyia for the birth of the twins. For this, the Delians gave them great honours. Upis often accompanied Artemis on her hunts and was present when Actaeon stumbled upon the goddess bathing and was turned into a deer. Later, the giant Orion joined Artemis as a hunting companion and assaulted Upis, so Artemis shot him dead with her arrows.

In the Aeneid, Artemis orders Upis to avenge Camilla, the Amazon-like warrior killed by the Etruscan Arruns. Upis mourns her and slays Arruns with an arrow. Some traditions identify her with a mythical being who raised the young Artemis, and Cicero mentions a male Upis who fathered the third Artemis, or Diana.

Worship and religion

Her tomb on Delos was worshipped, and after sacrifices the ashes of the burnt thighbones were placed in it. Young girls offered her a lock of their hair, and boys offered the hair growing on their cheeks. The Delian cult, which included deities linked to Artemis and childbirth such as Eileithyia, may go back to the Archaic period. In Ephesus, Upis was an epithet of Artemis as goddess of childbirth.

Legacy

A scene of Orion's assault on Upis may have been depicted on a funerary frieze in Taranto, dating to around 300 BC.

Ancient sources: She appears in Virgil's Aeneid and in mentions by Cicero, as well as in Greek and Roman sources on Delos and Artemis.

Trivia
