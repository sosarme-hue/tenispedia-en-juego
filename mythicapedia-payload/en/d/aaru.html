heavenly paradise in Egyptian mythology

Aaru

Aaru, also known as the Field of Reeds (sekhet-aaru), is the heavenly paradise of Egyptian mythology, ruled over by Osiris. Souls that passed the judgement of the afterlife could live there for eternity, which shows how much importance tradition placed on moral conduct.

The myth

According to tradition, Aaru was first placed in the north, among the imperishable stars, close to or within the Field of Offerings (sejet hetep). Later it was located in the northeast and also in the northwest. It was pictured as a series of islands covered in reeds, similar to the Nile Delta, with ideal hunting and fishing grounds.

To reach it, the deceased's heart was weighed against a feather, the symbol of Maat, cosmic order. If the heart balanced the scales, the soul could begin a long and dangerous journey. If it was heavy with wrongdoing, it fell to the jaws of Ammit, and the dead person was doomed to wander the Duat. Even a righteous soul had to pass through gates guarded by gods and demons, whose number varies between 15 and 21 depending on the source, and then cross the water by boat.

Worship and religion

To reach Aaru, the dead depended on their own virtues and knowledge, and on the spells of the Book of the Dead. Their relatives had to preserve the body, which served as a tomb and home for the Ka, and offer it food, drink, prayers and sacrifices. Chapters of that book were also written on tomb walls or on papyrus scrolls tucked among the mummy's bandages.

Legacy

Aaru has entered modern popular culture. It appears in the episode 'Asylum' of the Disney+ series Moon Knight (2022), where Taweret weighs the hearts of Marc Spector and Steven Grant to decide whether they may enter the Field of Reeds. It is also a heavenly setting in the video game Assassin's Creed Origins.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts place it near the Field of Offerings, and the chapters of the Book of the Dead describe the journey and trials that await the deceased.

Trivia

A feather on the scales. The deceased's heart was weighed against an ostrich feather, which represented Maat, order and justice.
Gates with different counts. Sources disagree on how many gates the soul had to pass to reach Aaru: some give 15 and others 21, all guarded by gods and demons.
