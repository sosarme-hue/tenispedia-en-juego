Roman goddess of the dead and of silence

Dea Tacita

Dea Tacita, "the silent goddess", is a Roman goddess of the dead, identified with the naiad Larunda and linked to silence and funerary rites. She matters because she brings together the cult of the dead, the ritual value of silence and an oracular tradition in a single deity.

The myth

According to Ovid's Fasti, Jupiter ripped out Dea Tacita's tongue as punishment. She had warned the nymph Juturna to flee from him, since the god planned to rape her. Her name is said to derive from this silence.

Tradition identifies her with the naiad Larunda, and in other accounts she appears as Dea Muta or Muta Tacita. Plutarch, who described her as a Muse, claimed that Numa Pompilius credited her with his oracular insight and taught the Romans to worship her.

Worship and religion

Dea Tacita was honoured during the Feralia, held on 21 February, when a rite was performed to seal hostile mouths and tongues. At the Larentalia, on 23 December, she was worshipped under the name Larenta. The Mutae Tacitae were also invoked to destroy a hated person, as an inscription from Cambodunum in Raetia shows.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Fasti describe her rite and the loss of her tongue, and Plutarch mentions her in connection with Numa Pompilius.

Trivia

A goddess without a tongue. According to Ovid, Jupiter ripped out her tongue, and that is why her name refers to silence.
A curse against an enemy. An inscription from Cambodunum asks that a man named Quartus be struck mute and flee like a mouse.
