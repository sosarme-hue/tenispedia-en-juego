norn of the present in Norse mythology

Verðandi

Verðandi is one of the three principal Norns of Norse mythology, alongside Urðr and Skuld. Her name, rooted in the verb 'to become', suggests what is happening or still in the making, which is why she is linked to the present.

The myth

According to the Eddas, the Norns shape the fates of people, and Verðandi appears in the Völuspá alongside her sisters Urðr and Skuld. The Eddas also mention lesser Norns tied to particular individuals.

Tradition holds that she decides the length of the thread of life: how long and how each being will live, and when and how it will die. Not even the gods were beyond her will.

Her name shares a root with Urðr's, and it is usually read as 'that which is becoming'. Some scholars point out, however, that the Eddas do not divide time among the Norns: all of them are tied to destiny, and past, present and future are woven together. Since her name carries neither the idea of destiny nor of necessity, she is often considered the most beautiful and the least powerful of the three.

Legacy

Her name lives on in language: in modern Swedish, the phrase 'i vardande' means 'in progress' or 'being made', and the related verb 'vända' means 'to turn' or 'reappear'. Her etymological relatives include German werden and Old English weorðan, both meaning 'to become'.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá, in the Poetic Edda, names her alongside Urðr and Skuld; the Eddas in general describe the Norns as determining fate.

Trivia
