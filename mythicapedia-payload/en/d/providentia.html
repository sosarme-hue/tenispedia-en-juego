Roman goddess of foresight and provision

Providentia

Providentia is the Roman personification of foresight and provision, one of the deified virtues that formed part of the imperial cult. Less a figure with myths of her own, she is a moral abstraction that appears mainly in art, state worship and coinage.

The myth

Very few stories are told about Providentia. Roman tradition gives her no genealogy, deeds or episodes of her own, so her presence is mostly conceptual and visual.

Worship and religion

After Augustus died, Tiberius set up an altar to Providentia Augusta, in recognition of his father's provision for the Roman state. In 28 AD, amid propaganda after Sejanus was executed, sacrifices were offered to her alongside Salus, Libertas and the Genius, and she received a permanent priest. In 59 AD the Arval Brethren also honoured her. She has no living cult today.

Legacy

Her image appears on coins of several emperors, and the 2002 100 euro Sculpture gold coin shows on its reverse the Providentia Fountain in Vienna, the work of Georg Rafael Donner. The Latin word providentia gave rise to the Christian concept of divine providence.

Ancient sources: Cicero mentions her as one of the three components of prudence, along with memory and understanding.

Trivia

A 100 euro coin. The Sculpture gold coin, issued in 2002, is the most recent coin with Providentia as its main motif.
Two faces for Janus. At the centre of the coin is a medallion of the god Janus, who had two faces, alongside the figure of Providentia.
