ancient Egyptian god of the underworld

Aken

Aken, also spelled Aqen, was a little-mentioned ancient Egyptian deity of the underworld and the dead. He first appears in the Book of the Dead, where he accompanies the sun god Ra along his course.

The myth

According to these texts, Aken was the protector of Ra's celestial bark, the boat that carried the sun god. He is described as bringing the shen-ring to his majesty, a phrase that links him to the protection of the divine ruler. Beside him stands Amentet, the one of the West, his consort and also a goddess of death, and both belong to the realm of the dead.

In the funerary texts, the deceased king needed the services of the ferryman to cross the river that led to the underworld. Before setting out on that dangerous journey, the king asked the ferryman Mahaf to wake Aken so that he would accompany them and protect them along the way. Aken thus appeared as an essential guardian in the passage of the dead.

Another image describes him as the 'mouth of time', from which gods and demons pulled the 'rope of time'. This scene appears in the tomb of King Seti I, showing that his figure survived in the funerary decoration of Egypt's New Kingdom. Aken therefore appears in several funerary contexts, from the book to the royal tomb.

Ancient sources: The Book of the Dead, where he first appears, and the tomb of King Seti I, where the rope of time is depicted.

Trivia

The mouth of time. Aken is described as the 'mouth of time', an unusual image that links him directly to the passing of hours and cycles.
A rope for time. According to the tomb of King Seti I, gods and demons pulled a 'rope of time' that came out of that mouth.
