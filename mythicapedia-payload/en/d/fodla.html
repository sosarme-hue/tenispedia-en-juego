Irish goddess of the land

Fódla

Fódla is a goddess of Irish mythology, one of the three tutelary goddesses of Ireland alongside her sisters Banba and Ériu. Her name is believed to come from the word for sod or land, which ties her to the soil of the island itself.

The myth

According to tradition, Fódla was a daughter of Delbáeth and Ernmas, of the Tuatha Dé Danann, and the wife of Mac Cecht. When the Milesians arrived from Spain, each of the three sisters asked that her name be given to the island. Ériu seems to have won the argument, but the poets hold that all three received their wish, which is why Fódla and Banba are sometimes used as literary names for Ireland.

In the Tochomlad mac Miledh a hEspain i nErind, Fótla is said to reign as queen in any year when Mac Cecht rules as king. As the Milesians travelled through Ireland, she met them on Naini Mountain, surrounded by her swift fairy hosts.

Legacy

Her name serves as a poetic name for Ireland, much like 'Albion' for Great Britain. An early Pictish kingdom was also called Fotla. The name Fódla grew in popularity as a given name in Ireland in the 2010s and 2020s.

Ancient sources: The Book of Leinster, the Tochomlad mac Miledh a hEspain i nErind, the Duan Albanach and the Pictish Chronicle mention Fódla or Fótla.

Trivia

Naini Mountain. A footnote identifies the Naini Mountain of Ebliu with the Slieve Felim Mountains in County Limerick.
An argument over a name. The Book of Leinster offers a fanciful etymology linking her name to the death of a daughter of Míl Espáine.
