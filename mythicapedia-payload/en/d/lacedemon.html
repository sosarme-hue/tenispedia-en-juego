eponymous hero of Lacedaemonia in Greek mythology

Lacedaemon

Lacedaemon is the eponymous hero of Greek mythology who gave his name to Lacedaemonia, the region whose main city was Sparta. According to tradition he was a son of Zeus and the Pleiad Taygete, and his story explains how the land and its people came to bear his name.

The myth

According to the myth recorded by Apollodorus, Lacedaemon was the son of Zeus and the Pleiad Taygete. He married Sparta, daughter of King Eurotas, who had no sons, and received the kingdom from his father-in-law. As king, he replaced the name of the land and its inhabitants with his own, and founded a city that he named after his wife: Sparta. His descendants included Amyclas, who succeeded him on the throne, and Eurydice, who married King Acrisius of Argos.

Pausanias adds that Lacedaemon founded the sanctuary of the Graces between Sparta and Amyclae, and gave the goddesses the names Cleta and Phaenna. Local traditions differ about his family: some say Taygete was his wife rather than his mother, and that their children were Himerus and Cleodice; others name Asine and Himerus as his children. In the latter story, Himerus violated his sister and, overcome with remorse, threw himself into the river Maratho, which was then called Himerus and later Eurotas.

Worship and religion

Pausanias credits Lacedaemon with founding the sanctuary of the Graces, located between Sparta and Amyclae, where the goddesses were called Cleta and Phaenna. The sources do not describe rites, festivals or any cult devoted to Lacedaemon himself, so his religious role is tied to this sanctuary.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus and Pausanias mention Lacedaemon, and his story survives mainly through the ancient Greek written tradition.

Trivia

A river with three names. The river Maratho was renamed Himerus after a young man threw himself into its waters, and later took the name Eurotas.
A city named after a queen. Sparta was named after Lacedaemon's wife, the daughter of King Eurotas.
