king of Hell in demonology

Beleth

Beleth is a king of Hell in European demonology, the thirteenth spirit of the Goetia and commander of eighty-five legions of demons. He matters because he illustrates how early modern grimoires described the spirits a conjurer could summon, command and bind to his service. According to the grimoires, Beleth rides a pale horse, and a variety of music is heard before him. His appearance is fierce, perhaps a test of whether the conjurer is brave. The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum claims that Ham, Noah's son, was the first to summon him after the flood and that, with his help, he wrote a book on mathematics. In this tradition, Beleth can bind men and women in obsessive love until the conjurer's will is fulfilled.

The myth

According to the grimoires, Beleth commands eighty-five legions of demons and rides a pale horse, with a variety of music sounding before him. His look is fierce, perhaps a test of whether the conjurer is brave.

The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum claims that Ham, Noah's son, was the first to invoke him after the flood and that, with his help, he wrote a book on mathematics. In this tradition, Beleth can bind men and women in obsessive love until the conjurer's will is fulfilled.

Worship and religion

He was never a venerated deity but a figure of ceremonial magic. The texts describe his summoning: the brave conjurer holds a hazel wand, draws a triangle outside the circle and recites incantations, wearing a silver ring on the middle finger of the left hand held against the face to honour his royal rank.

Legacy

He appears in early modern European demonology, in works such as the Dictionnaire Infernal and within the Goetia tradition, where he is listed among the spirits a magician could summon alongside other demons.

Ancient sources: The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum and the Dictionnaire Infernal, early modern grimoires drawing on the Solomonic Goetia; they are not ancient texts in the strict sense.

Trivia

A ring held to the face. According to the Dictionnaire Infernal, the summoner should wear a silver ring on the middle finger of the left hand, held against the face as a sign of respect for his rank as king.
Noah's son as a conjurer. The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum credits Ham, Noah's son, with the first invocation of Beleth after the flood, and says he wrote a book on mathematics with his help.
