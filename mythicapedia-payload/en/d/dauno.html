king of the Rutulians in Roman mythology

Daunus

Daunus is, in Roman mythology, the king of the Rutulians, a people of Latium, and the father of Turnus, the great rival of Aeneas. His figure comes almost entirely from Virgil's Aeneid.

The myth

According to Virgil's Aeneid, Daunus was the son of the god Pilumnus and the husband of the goddess Venilia. He ruled the Rutulians of Latium and was the brother of Amata, wife of King Latinus, which is why his niece Lavinia ended up married to Aeneas.

Daunus was the father of Turnus, the Rutulian hero who fought Aeneas, and of the nymph Yuturna. According to tradition, Vulcan forged for Turnus a sword tempered in the waters of the Styx, yet even with it he was defeated in battle against Aeneas. Virgil also mentions Diomedes, the Greek hero who became Daunus's son-in-law.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, the only source that describes him in any detail.

Trivia

A sword from the Styx. According to the tradition recorded by Virgil, the sword Vulcan forged for Turnus was tempered in the waters of the Styx, the river of the underworld.
A Greek son-in-law. Virgil makes Diomedes, the Greek hero, a son-in-law of Daunus, a link that connects the Rutulians with Greek tradition.
