jötunn, brother of Loki in Norse mythology

Helblindi

Helblindi is a jötunn in Norse mythology, brother of Loki and Býleistr according to the Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson. He is a barely documented figure, and his interest lies mostly in the questions he leaves open about Loki's family.

The myth

According to the sources, Helblindi belongs to the family circle around Loki. The texts present him as a son of Fárbauti and Laufey, and as a brother of Loki and Býleistr. His name is usually rendered as 'Helblind', though no specific connection between him and Hel, the goddess of the underworld, is known.

Most of what we know comes from the Prose Edda, written in the thirteenth century by Snorri Sturluson, where he is several times named as Loki's brother and, implicitly, as the son of Laufey and Fárbauti. He is also mentioned in two poems: the Haustlöng, by Þjóðólfr ór Hvini, and the Húsdrápa, by Úlfr Uggason. In both cases his name appears in passing, without any story of his own.

No tales of deeds or episodes featuring him survive. His role in the mythic complex around Loki's family remains unclear. Scholars consider it likely that he was a son of Fárbauti, though that parentage is not directly attested in the original sources.

Helblindi is also one of the many names of Odin, as found in the Grímnismál and the Gylfaginning. This overlap leaves open whether the name designates a separate figure or is an epithet of the god, a question the texts do not settle.

Ancient sources: He appears in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in Þjóðólfr ór Hvini's Haustlöng and in Úlfr Uggason's Húsdrápa.

Trivia

A name shared with Odin. The name Helblindi appears as one of the many epithets of Odin in the Grímnismál and the Gylfaginning, which complicates whether he was a distinct figure.
