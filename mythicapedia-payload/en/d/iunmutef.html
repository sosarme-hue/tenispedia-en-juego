Egyptian god of filial devotion and funerary ritual

Iunmutef

Iunmutef, also known as Horus-Iunmutef, was an ancient Egyptian deity and an aspect of Horus as the loving son who cares for his deceased father, Osiris. His name means "Pillar of His Mother", and he matters because he linked family, kingship and funerary ritual.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Iunmutef embodies filial duty and family responsibility. As the heir, he could revive the dead father and open the way to eternity. The Pyramid Texts and Coffin Texts mention him as the protector and caretaker of the deceased, who accompanies the dead person into the realm of Osiris.

He is always shown in human form, wearing the child's sidelock and a leopard skin of the priesthood, which sometimes led people to confuse him with the sem priest. Iunmutef was understood as the divine counterpart of that earthly priest.

Worship and religion

His most visible role was in funerary rites, especially the Opening of the Mouth ceremony, which restored the senses of the dead for the afterlife. In the tomb of Seti I he appears performing purifications and rites of revival. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts (Spell 587) and the Coffin Texts (Spells 280, 294 and 945), along with Greek documents that name him Huronmefis.

Trivia

An estate with his name. In the Saqqara tomb of the official Raemka, a list of royal estates includes one called "the Work of Iunmutef", linked to the 9th Upper Egyptian nome.
A Greek name. Greek documents record him as Huronmefis, an adaptation of Horus-Iunmutef.
