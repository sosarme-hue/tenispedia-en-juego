Trojan prince, son of Priam and Hecuba

Deiphobus

Deiphobus is a Trojan prince, son of Priam and Hecuba, and the bravest of the Trojan warriors after his brother Hector. He belongs to the Greek tradition of the Trojan War, and his end, tied to Helen, gave rise to some of the cycle's best-known episodes.

The myth

In the Iliad, Deiphobus fights at the wall alongside Helenus and Asius, blocks spears with his shield and wounds the Achaean Meriones. When Achilles chases Hector, the goddess Athena takes on Deiphobus's shape and urges him to stop. When Hector turns to ask his brother for another spear, Deiphobus has vanished, and the hero realises the gods have abandoned him. After Hector's death, Deiphobus helps prepare the treasure used to ransom his body.

After Paris died, Deiphobus won Helen as a bride for his deeds in the war, ahead of Helenus, although Euripides says she felt bitterly enslaved by the marriage. During the fall of Troy, the Odyssey shows him walking with Helen around the wooden horse and calling out in the voices of the Greek wives hidden inside. When Odysseus and Menelaus attack his house, Helen stabs him in the back with a dagger, and Menelaus mutilates his corpse.

In Virgil's Aeneid, he appears in the underworld with his ears and nose cut off and tells Aeneas that Helen had hidden the weapons to let Menelaus and Odysseus in. Aeneas later raises a cenotaph to him on the promontory of Reteum.

Legacy

Shakespeare includes him as a minor character in Troilus and Cressida. Roger Lancelyn Green's novel The Luck of Troy portrays him as a particularly unpleasant figure, and in the Xena: Warrior Princess episode 'Beware Greeks Bearing Gifts' he is the main villain. A Trojan asteroid bears his name: 1867 Deiphobus.

Ancient sources: Iliad (books XII, XIII, XXII and XXIV), Odyssey (books IV and VIII), Aeneid (book VI) and Hyginus's Fabulae.

Trivia

An asteroid with his name. The Trojan asteroid 1867 Deiphobus carries his name, like other Trojan-group asteroids that are named after heroes of the Trojan War.
A physical portrait. The chronicler Malalas describes him as tall, keen-eyed, somewhat snub-nosed, dark-skinned and with a good beard.
