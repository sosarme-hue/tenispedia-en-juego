Japanese creator deity, the first to appear in heaven

Ame-no-Minakanushi

Ame-no-Minakanushi is a deity of Japanese mythology, recorded as the first, or one of the first, kami to appear when heaven and earth came into being. He matters because he belongs to the triad of the "three creators", and his role has been debated for centuries.

The myth

According to the Kojiki (712), after heaven and earth emerged from primeval chaos, Ame-no-Minakanushi was the first god to appear in the heavenly realm of Takamagahara. Like the first seven gods, he was "solitary": he came into being without a partner and, according to the account, hid his presence as soon as he existed.

He is counted as the first of the "three deities of creation" (zōka sanshin), alongside Takami-musubi and Kami-musubi, and as one of the five "distinguished heavenly gods". The Nihon Shoki (720) barely mentions him: its main narrative names Kuni-no-Tokotachi as the first kami, and Ame-no-Minakanushi appears only in passing in one variant. Imperial records of 815, the Shinsen Shōjiroku, place two clans among his descendants.

Worship and religion

No undisputed ancient shrine dedicated to him is known, and the Engishiki (early 10th century) mentions none. Some scholars consider him an abstract deity, created in court texts under Chinese influence; others argue that the scarcity of evidence does not rule out a lost cult. No living cult is known.

Legacy

In the medieval period he was little discussed. Yoshida Kanetomo identified him with Kuninotokotachi as the primordial god of the universe, and Ise Shinto linked him to the deity of the Outer Shrine of Ise. In the Edo period, Motoori Norinaga denied that the order of appearance conferred rank, Hirata Atsutane described him as a supreme god residing in the pole star, and Tsurumine Shigenobu associated him with gravity.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Nihon Shoki (720), Japan's foundational chronicles, are the ancient texts that mention him.

Trivia

A solitary god. The first seven gods in the Kojiki came into being one by one, without partners, and are described as hiding their presence once they existed.
The pole star. Hirata Atsutane placed him in the pole star, at the very center of heaven.
