mythical king of Megara, ancestor of the Leleges

Lelex

Lelex was a mythical king of Megara, regarded as the ancestor of the Leleges, a people of ancient Greece. His figure links the founding of the city to a genealogy that traces back to Poseidon.

The myth

According to tradition, Lelex was a son of Poseidon and Libya, daughter of Epaphus. He arrived in Greece from Egypt in the twelfth generation after Car, son of Phoroneus, who reigned in the land of Caria, and there he became king of Megara.

He fathered Bias and Cleson, and possibly Pterelaus too. After him, his son Cleson succeeded to the throne, and Cleson's son Pylas was followed by Sciron, the figure whom the stories place as the malefactor of Theseus, though some versions present him rather as a Megarian warlord.

Worship and religion

His tomb was shown below Nisaea, the acropolis of Megara, suggesting that his memory was tied to the city. The sources do not describe an organised cult in his honour.
