Chinese god of fire and agriculture

Chidi (Red Emperor)

Chidi, or the Red Emperor, is a deity of Chinese mythology linked to fire, agriculture and medicinal plants. The title has been held by several mythical gods and rulers, such as Yandi, Shennong and Zhurong, and he is regarded as a manifestation of the supreme god associated with the essence of fire.

The myth

According to the stories, Chiyou was originally a vassal of the Red Emperor, who had appointed him to rule the land of Shaohao. As Chiyou grew more powerful, he fought the Red Emperor at Zhuolu. The Red Emperor was defeated and asked the Yellow Emperor for help, and the Yellow Emperor defeated Chiyou and restored peace to the world.

The tradition tells the two rivals apart by their appearance: Chiyou has the horns of a fighting bull, while the more peaceful Shennong Yandi has the horns of a plowing buffalo.

Legacy

During the Han period he became an ancestor of the dynasty: legend says that Liu Bang, its founder, was a son of the Red Emperor. In astrology his astral body is Mars, and Taoist deities who share his title, such as Hung Shing, appear in later religion.

Ancient sources: The Yi Zhou Shu, Sima Qian's Shiji and Wang Fu's The Theory of Subtlety mention him in connection with Yandi, Shennong and the Yellow Emperor.

Trivia

An ancestor of the Han dynasty. Legend made Liu Bang, founder of the Han dynasty, a son of the Red Emperor, giving his rule a divine origin.
Bull horns and buffalo horns. The stories distinguish Chiyou, with the horns of a fighting bull, from Shennong Yandi, with the horns of a plowing buffalo, reflecting violent and peaceful natures.
