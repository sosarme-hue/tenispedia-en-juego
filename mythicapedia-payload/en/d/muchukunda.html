solar king of Hinduism who slept in a cave

Muchukunda

Muchukunda is a king of the Solar dynasty (Suryavamsha) in Hindu tradition, son of King Mandhata and brother of Ambarisha. He is known for protecting the gods in a war and for the long sleep he enjoyed in a cave.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, the devas were defeated by the asuras and sought refuge with Muchukunda, a great warrior and a pious man. The king sheltered them until they regrouped under Kartikeya, son of Shiva, and won the victory. Grateful, Indra and the devas revealed to him that an entire age had passed: the Treta Yuga had given way to the Dvapara Yuga, and his whole family had died. They offered him any boon except liberation (moksha), which lay beyond their power. Grieving and exhausted from the battles, Muchukunda asked for undisturbed sleep, on the condition that whoever woke him would be reduced to ashes. The boon was granted, and he went to sleep in a cave.

According to the Mahabharata, the yavana Kalayavana, unbeatable thanks to a boon and allied with Jarasandha, marched against Mathura, Krishna's kingdom. Krishna withdrew from the battle, and Kalayavana chased him into a dark cave, where Muchukunda was sleeping. Mistaking him for Krishna, Kalayavana attacked him; when Muchukunda opened his eyes, his gaze reduced the attacker to ashes. After recognising Krishna, the king received advice to practise tapas to cleanse his sins and attain moksha. Leaving the cave, he noticed that all creatures had shrunk over the ages. He then travelled to Mount Gandamadana and later to the Badrika ashrama to perform penance.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana recounts his pact with the gods, and the Mahabharata his encounter with Kalayavana.

Trivia

A sleep of ages. On waking, Muchukunda found that all living beings had shrunk while he slept, a sign that long ages had passed.
A deadly gaze. According to the Mahabharata, simply opening his eyes was enough to reduce Kalayavana to ashes.
