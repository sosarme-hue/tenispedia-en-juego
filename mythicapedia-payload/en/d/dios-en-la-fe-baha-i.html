the Bahá'í conception of God

God in the Bahá'í Faith

In the Bahá'í Faith, God is the single, uncreated and eternal Being from whom all existence proceeds. Since God's essence is considered unknowable, believers come to know God through divine attributes and through the messengers they call Manifestations of God, seeing the world's religions as chapters of one progressive revelation.

The myth

Rather than a cycle of stories, the Bahá'í conception of God is a doctrine about God's nature. It teaches that God is the creator of all that exists and that his essence lies completely beyond human reach. According to this teaching, the many ideas of God that people have held throughout history reflect the human mind rather than God's true reality.

God makes himself known through the Manifestations of God, prophets and messengers who have founded religions from prehistoric times to the present. Like divine mirrors, they reflect attributes such as omnipotence, infinite love, mercy and glory, without being incarnations of God's essence. Shoghi Effendi, who led the faith in the first half of the 20th century, described God as personal, rational and omniscient, yet without human or physical form, and rejected pantheistic and anthropomorphic beliefs.

Worship and religion

Believers seek a close relationship with God through prayer, meditation, study of the scriptures and service to humanity. The greatest of God's names is Bahá, meaning All-Glorious, which gives rise to the greeting Alláh-u-Abhá, always expressed in Arabic whatever language is spoken.

Trivia

A supreme name. Bahá'ís consider the highest name of God to be All-Glorious, or Bahá in Arabic. From this root come Bahá'u'lláh, meaning The Glory of God, and Bahá'í, meaning Follower of the All-Glorious.
Pronouns by convention. Although Bahá'í scripture uses masculine pronouns for God, this is a convention and does not indicate that God has a gender.
