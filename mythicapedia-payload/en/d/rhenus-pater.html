god of the Rhine in Celtic and Germanic tradition

Rhenus Pater

Rhenus Pater, «Father Rhine», is the divine personification of the Rhine river, attested in ancient inscriptions. He matters because he shows how great rivers were imagined as powerful beings with their own will.

The myth

Ancient authors describe him with features that set him apart from other river gods. Martial calls him «father of nymphs and rivers» and associates him with Neptunus. Because of his horned depiction he is also called Rhenus bicornis, and Ovid speaks of a Rhenus cornibus fractis, «Rhenus with broken horns», an image understood as an allegory of subjugated barbarian tribes.

Worship and religion

There are records of human sacrifice among Celts and Germans offered to river gods, and for the Rhine specifically there is evidence of a custom of submerging newborn infants, either as a test of their vitality or as an oracle to determine whether they had been conceived within marriage. There is no data on a surviving cult today.

Legacy

The allegory reappeared as a motif in the German Baroque and again in 19th-century German Romanticism, within the movement known as Rheinromantik.

Ancient sources: Martial (10.7) and Ovid refer to the god through his epithets of horns and rivers.

Trivia

Broken horns. Ovid calls him Rhenus cornibus fractis, an image interpreted as a symbol of defeated peoples.
