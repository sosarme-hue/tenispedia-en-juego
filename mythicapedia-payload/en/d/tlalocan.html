paradise of the Mexica rain god

Tlalocan

Tlalocan was the paradise of Tlaloc, the Mexica god of rain, lightning and earthquakes. It belongs to Aztec cosmology and was the destination of those who drowned, were struck by lightning or died of illnesses linked to water.

The myth

According to accounts recorded after the conquest, Tlalocan was a land of endless spring, with fruit trees, maize, beans and chia, located in the east. Its waters sustained life on earth. Tlaloc ruled it alongside his consort Chalchiuhtlicue. It also received lepers and people with physical deformities, who were considered under the god's care.

Sources place it at times as the first of the heavens and at others as a level of the underworld, showing that Aztec cosmology was not uniform.

Worship and religion

No documented temples dedicated to Tlalocan are known. Among modern Nahua communities of the Sierra Norte de Puebla, some shamans still describe this place during healing sessions, saying they journey there in dreams through underground waterways.

Legacy

The film Black Panther: Wakanda Forever (2022) features Talokan, Namor's underwater city, whose name recalls the Mexica paradise.

Ancient sources: The Florentine Codex, compiled by Bernardino de Sahagún in the sixteenth century, is the main source describing Tlalocan as a realm of eternal spring.

Trivia

A mural at Teotihuacan. Centuries later, a mural discovered at Teotihuacan was found to seem to depict Tlaloc's paradise, offering a visual image of what the chronicles described.
Entering through a whirlpool. Among Nahua shamans of the Sierra Norte de Puebla, entry into Tlalocan happens in dreams, often through a whirlpool in underground water.
