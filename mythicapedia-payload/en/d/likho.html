Slavic embodiment of evil fate and misfortune

Likho

Likho is the embodiment of evil fate and misfortune in Slavic mythology. It belongs mainly to fairy tales rather than to the major canon of Slavic belief, and it is often pictured as an old, skinny woman dressed in black with a single eye.

The myth

According to folk tales, Likho can appear before anyone and try to deceive them. The stories have different morals: in one, the person eventually outwits him; in another, a person tricks Likho and flees, then grabs a valuable object out of greed, their hand sticks to it, and they must cut it off to escape.

In another version, Likho cheats someone and rides on their neck. The victim who tries to drown him jumps into a river along with him, but Likho floats free and goes on hunting new victims. In other tales, the misfortune can be passed on to someone else through a gift.

Legacy

The name comes from a noun meaning bad luck in Russian and Ukrainian, and in Polish it has meant the odd number. It appears in proverbs such as "Don't wake likho while it is quiet," and in Ukrainian folklore it is sometimes described as a bad spirit that can cling to one's neck.
