Canaanite god of the dawn

Shahar

Shahar is the Canaanite god of the dawn, attested in inscriptions found at Ugarit (today Ras Shamra, in Syria). His name means "dawn", and his figure helps show how these peoples understood the passage of the day.

The myth

According to Ugaritic tradition, Shahar was a son of El, the supreme god, and had a twin brother, Shalim. Together they marked the edges of the day: Shahar opened it with the dawn and Shalim closed it with the dusk, so they embodied the temporal structure of each day.

In some texts they appear together as "the morning and evening star", a divine pair understood as two sides of the same alternation. William F. Albright read their roles in this way, although that is a modern interpretation rather than an explicit statement in the texts.

Legacy

The name survives in modern Hebrew, where Shahar is also used as a unisex personal name. In Arabic, saḥar refers to the period before daybreak, and from the same root comes suḥūr, the meal Muslims eat before dawn during Ramadan.

Ancient sources: Inscriptions and texts from Ugarit, and the Hebrew Bible, where the word appears about 43 times, mostly as a noun meaning dawn.

Trivia
