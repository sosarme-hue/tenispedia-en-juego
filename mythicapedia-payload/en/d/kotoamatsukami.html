Kotoamatsukami

Kotoamatsukami is the collective name for the first gods of Japanese mythology, who came into existence when the universe was created. They are part of the Shinto account of creation and hold a central place in traditions about the origins of the world.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, three gods appeared first in Takamagahara, the heavenly realm: Ame-no-minaka-nushi, Takami-musubi and Kami-musubi. They were born without procreation and, according to tradition, without gender. Shortly after, Umashi-ashikabi-hikoji and Ame-no-tokotachi emerged. Later came the Kamiyonanayo, including Izanagi and Izanami, the parents of the other Japanese gods. Afterwards, the first gods went into hiding as hitorigami.

The Nihon Shoki tells a different story: its first three deities are Kunitokotachi, Kuninosatsuchi and Toyokumonu, said to have arisen through the male principle alone. Takamimusubi later reappears alongside Amaterasu, and his daughter became the mother of Ninigi.

Worship and religion

Takamimusubi was worshipped by the Jingi-kan and regarded as the god of matchmaking. Emperor Jimmu honoured him by acting as a medium priest who took on his identity during the rites before his enthronement. Some clans, such as the Saeki, claimed descent from him.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki.

Trivia

The sun crow. Takamimusubi sent the Yatagarasu, a three-legged crow associated with the sun, to help Emperor Jimmu.
A divine pole star. The theologian Hirata Atsutane identified Ame-no-Minakanushi with the spirit of the North Star, master of the seven stars of the Big Dipper.
