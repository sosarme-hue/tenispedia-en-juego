legendary hero of Norse legend

Svipdagr

Svipdagr ("sudden day") is a Norse hero who stars in the Eddic poems Grógaldr and Fjölsvinnsmál, known together as Svipdagsmál. Other sagas and chronicles give him different roles, so scholars debate whether they all refer to the same character.

The myth

According to the Eddic poems, his wicked stepmother sets him an impossible task: to reach Menglöð, his "destined bride," who lives in a castle on Mount Lyfjaberg. Before setting out, he summons the shade of his dead mother, Gróa, who chants nine spells to protect him on the journey.

At the castle, a watchman named Fjölsviðr, another name for Odin, questions him in a game of eighteen riddles. Svipdagr hides his name and calls himself Vindkald, but in the end it turns out that only he can open the gates. Once he reveals who he is, the gates open and Menglöð welcomes him. In the saga of Hrólfr Kraki he appears as a young warrior who loses an eye among berserkers.

Legacy

Jacob Grimm identified Menglöð with Freyja, an idea that much later scholarship has followed. Viktor Rydberg linked Svipdagr with Óðr, Freyja's husband.

Ancient sources: The Eddic poems Grógaldr and Fjölsvinnsmál, the Prose Edda, Heimskringla, Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, and the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle.

Trivia

A meaningful name. His Old Norse name means "sudden day." In the poem, he gives the watchman the false name Vindkald, "wind-cold."
A lost eye. In the saga of Hrólfr Kraki he loses an eye in battle, a trait that links him to Odin, the one-eyed god of Norse mythology.
