Japanese Buddhist goddess of music, fortune and water

Benzaiten

Benzaiten is a Japanese Buddhist goddess, patron of music, the arts, learning and fortune, and the only woman among the Seven Lucky Gods. Her origins lie in the Hindu goddess Saraswati, whom Mahayana Buddhism adopted and who in Japan merged with local deities and Shinto.

The myth

Most accounts of Benzaiten come from the Golden Light Sutra, where the goddess appears before the Buddha's assembly and vows to protect those who recite, copy or believe in the text. There she teaches mantras that heal illness and ward off misfortune, and she is described with eight arms holding weapons, which present her as a protector of the state as well.

A later work, the Enoshima Engi of 1047, makes her the daughter of the dragon king of Lake Anavatapta, which Buddhist cosmology places at the centre of the world. In Japan she was identified with Ichikishima-hime, one of the daughters of the sun goddess Amaterasu, and with the kami Ugajin, a white snake with a human head. Her link to water associated her with snakes and dragons, and in the medieval period she was merged with the goddess Kisshōten, who was credited with material fortune.

Worship and religion

Her temples and shrines, called benten-dō, are usually built near rivers, ponds or springs. Among the best known are the shrines on Enoshima, Chikubu Island in Lake Biwa and Itsukushima Island. Her worship remains alive in Japan and also in Taiwan, where she is venerated at the Xian Dong Yan temple in Keelung.

Legacy

She is one of the most depicted figures in Japanese art, usually shown playing a biwa, which links her to music just as Sarasvati is linked to the veena in India. The image of a woman beside a white snake is part of popular imagery, and Ryōhō-ji, known as the Moe Temple, portrays her in an anime style.

Ancient sources: The Golden Light Sutra, translated into Classical Chinese, is the main Buddhist source, with a chapter devoted to her. In India, the figure of Sarasvati appears in the Rigveda.

Trivia

A warrior goddess. In the Golden Light Sutra she is described with a bow, arrow, sword and spear, and is said to fight on battlefields and always win.
A white snake in dreams. Many Japanese people believe that seeing a white snake in a dream is a sign of good luck, a link to the goddess.
