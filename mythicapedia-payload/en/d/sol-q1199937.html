Roman god of the Sun

Sol

Sol is the Roman god of the Sun, one of the oldest deities in the Roman pantheon and the counterpart of the Greek Helios. His cult, linked to Sabine tradition and the origins of the city, lasted until the late Empire.

The myth

According to tradition, the worship of Sol was introduced to Rome by the Sabine king Titus Tatius after his alliance with Romulus. Many of his stories come from Helios, although the Romans added motifs of their own. In Virgil's Aeneid, he becomes the grandfather of King Latinus, son of Circe, who appears crowned with rays and driving a chariot.

In Cicero's On the Nature of the Gods, the existence of five Suns is recorded according to different traditions. Hyginus, in his Fabulae, presents him as the son of the Titans Hyperion and Thea, brother of Luna and Aurora, and father of Circe, Aeetes and Phaethon. His episodes include the punishment of Odysseus's crew for eating his sacred cattle and the report to Vulcan of Venus's affair with Mars.

Worship and religion

Sol had old shrines on the Quirinal and in the Circus Maximus, to which a sanctuary in Trastevere and a temple built by Aurelian were added. Varro included him among the Dii Selecti, the most important gods of the pantheon. Sacrifices were offered to him on the Quirinal on 9 August, and a festival was held on 11 December. Under Aurelian a college of pontiffs was created for his cult, which survived until classical Roman religion was abolished under Theodosius I. It does not survive today.

Legacy

His name lives on in the Romance languages (sol, soleil, sole, soare) and the Scandinavian ones. Emperors used him to express their power, and the radiate crown on some third-century coins was associated with him. His epithets Invictus and Aeternus appear in dedications and on coins.

Ancient sources: Varro, Cicero in On the Nature of the Gods, Virgil in the Aeneid and Hyginus in his Fabulae.

Trivia

Five Suns. Cicero records up to five different figures called Sol, each with its own genealogy, showing how much this god could vary depending on the author.
A disputed date. Some scholars link 25 December to a festival of Sol Invictus, although the sources do not confirm this and the origin of that date is still debated.
