Vedic god of the solar impulse

Savitr

Savitr is a deity of the Vedic pantheon, one of the Adityas, the offspring of the mother goddess Aditi. He is regarded as the vivifying power of the Sun, distinct from the solar disc itself, and he is invoked in the Gayatri mantra in Hindu tradition.

The myth

According to the hymns of the Rig Veda, Savitr is the one who sets the day in motion and who brings rest to all beings. Before sunrise the Sun is called Savitr, and from sunrise to sunset it is called Sūrya. The hymns describe him with golden arms, golden eyes and a pleasant tongue, and show him driving a golden chariot drawn by radiant steeds.

In these texts Savitr upholds the world and maintains ṛta, the eternal order. He protects all beings, drives away nightmares and sorcerers, and is asked to guide the soul of the departed to the abode of the righteous. He also grants immortality to the gods and long life to humans. The hymns present him as the impeller of sacrifice: according to the Yajur Veda, the ritual is performed at the instigation of the god Savitr.

Worship and religion

Savitr was widely venerated in the Vedic period, when hymns were addressed to him for morning and evening offerings. Over time he ceased to be an independent deity of the pantheon, although he is still worshipped in modern Hinduism under the name Sāvitrī.

Legacy

His name appears in the Gayatri mantra, one of the best-known prayers in Hinduism, recited at dawn and dusk.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda, which devotes eleven full hymns to him and mentions him about 170 times, and the Yajur Veda, which names him in its rituals. The scholar Yaska also discusses him in his Nirukta.

Trivia

The Sun before dawn. In Vedic tradition, the Sun is called Savitr before rising and Sūrya from sunrise until sunset.
A single solar epithet. The term sūrya-raśmi appears only once in the Rig Veda, and it is applied to Savitr.
