city of Kubera in Hindu mythology

Alaka

Alaka, also called Alakapuri or Alkavati, is the mythical city of Kubera, king of the yakshas and god of wealth in Hinduism. It was said to rival Amaravati, the capital of Indra, in splendour.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Kubera lost the kingdom of Lanka to his half-brother Ravana and then founded his new capital near Mount Kailasha. There Alaka came to be regarded as the most splendid city in the world, guarded by yakshas armed with javelins and swords.

The Bhagavata Purana tells how Dhruva attacked the city to avenge his brother's death at the hands of a yaksha, slaying thousands until his grandfather Manu persuaded him to stop. The Brahmanda Purana describes Parashurama passing the city on his journey to Kailasha.

The texts portray mansions and palaces set with jewels, groves, parks and vast lakes. The Alakananda, a branch of the Ganges, encircles it; elephants drinking from its waters turn golden because apsaras mix saffron into them when they bathe. Music by gandharvas and apsaras fills the city.

Legacy

Alaka appears as the setting of the Sanskrit lyric poem Meghadūta by Kalidasa. The name of the Alakananda river, a Ganges tributary in the Himalayas, derives from this tradition.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, which presents it as the capital of the Yaksha kingdom; the Bhagavata Purana and Brahmanda Purana, which mention it in the stories of Dhruva and Parashurama; and Kalidasa's Meghadūta.

Trivia

A golden elephant. According to the texts, elephants that drink from the river of Alaka turn a golden-green hue, due to the saffron the apsaras add when they bathe.
A rival of Amaravati. Alaka was said to rival Amaravati, the capital of the god Indra, in splendour.
