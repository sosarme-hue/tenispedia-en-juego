twin founders of Rome in Roman mythology

Romulus and Remus

Remus is one of the twin sons of Mars and Rhea Silvia whose story recounts the founding of Rome. According to tradition, he was killed by his brother Romulus after a quarrel over the site of the new city, which Romulus went on to found as its first king.

The myth

In Roman mythology, Rhea Silvia, daughter of King Numitor of Alba Longa and a Vestal Virgin, was visited by the god Mars and gave birth to twins. Her uncle Amulius, who had seized the throne, feared they would become rivals and ordered them abandoned on the Tiber, but the current carried them to a place where a she-wolf and a woodpecker, sacred animals of Mars, nursed them until the shepherd Faustulus found them.

When grown, the young men learned their origin, killed Amulius and restored their grandfather to the throne. They then wished to found a city but disagreed about the hill: Romulus preferred the Palatine and Remus the Aventine. To decide, they watched for birds; Remus saw six and Romulus twelve, and Romulus claimed victory. The dispute turned into a fight in which Remus died. Romulus founded Rome on the Palatine, traditionally dated to 21 April 753 BC, and ruled as its first king.

The myth also includes the rape of the Sabine women, which secured wives for the new city, and Romulus's victory over King Agron of the Ceninenses.

Legacy

The she-wolf suckling the twins became a symbol of Rome and the ancient Romans, appearing on coins and emblems from antiquity onward.

Ancient sources: Livy, Plutarch, Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Cassius Dio recount the myth, and Ovid includes it in his Fasti.

Trivia

The vultures. In the accounts of Plutarch and Livy, the dispute is settled by birds, and the number of vultures decides who gains command.
A ghost in the Fasti. Ovid tells that the ghost of Remus appeared in a dream to Faustulus and his wife Acca to complain about his death at the hands of Celer, one of Romulus's men.
