Norse god of silence and vengeance

Víðarr

Víðarr, also spelled Vidar, is a god among the Æsir in Norse mythology, son of Odin and the giantess Gríðr, associated with silence, vengeance and justice. He is best known for his role at Ragnarök, when he avenges his father by killing the wolf Fenrir.

The myth

According to the sources, Víðarr is the son of Odin and the giantess Gríðr. He is described as a quiet god, nearly as strong as Thor, whom the gods rely on in times of great difficulty. His name is often interpreted as "wide ruler", though its meaning remains uncertain.

His destiny is foretold mainly in the Poetic Edda and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda. At Ragnarök, Odin will be devoured by the wolf Fenrir, and Víðarr will avenge him. The Völuspá says he kills the wolf by thrusting his sword into its heart. The Vafþrúðnismál and the Prose Edda instead describe him standing on the lower jaw and tearing the upper jaw apart with his hands. The ancient texts thus disagree on how the death occurs.

His most distinctive feature is his shoe. According to the Prose Edda, it is a thick shoe made of the leather scraps people cut from their own shoes at the toes and heels. Those who wish to help the gods should throw such scraps away, since the god keeps them for his great battle. Some scholars suggest his mother Gríðr designed the shoe to protect him from fire.

After Ragnarök, Víðarr survives, along with his brother Váli, the flooding sea and the blaze unleashed by Surtr. He then dwells in the field Iðavöllr, where the city of Asgard once stood, and the renewed world welcomes him.

Worship and religion

Ancient sources say little about a cult of Víðarr. His name appears in place names and myths, but there is no clear evidence of temples or rituals devoted to him. Modern Norse neopagans (Ásatrú) hold a festival in his honour on 16 October.

Legacy

Víðarr appears in contemporary poetry and in popular fantasy fiction, where his silent character and his shoe are often reinterpreted. On the Gosforth Cross, a mid-11th-century monument in Cumbria, England, a scene of a man fighting a monstrous beast has been read as Víðarr against Fenrir. Georges Dumézil linked him to a spatial god of Indo-European origin.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Völuspá, Vafþrúðnismál, Grímnismál and Lokasenna of the Poetic Edda, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, where he is called "the silent son of Odin".

Trivia

A famous shoe. According to the Prose Edda, his shoe is made from the leather scraps people discard from their own shoes.
The god who does not speak. His best-known epithet is "the silent", and scholars have proposed that his silence may relate to ritual abstentions taken by those seeking vengeance.
