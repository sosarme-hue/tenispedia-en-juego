epithet of the deities who protected children in ancient Greece

Kourotrophos

Kourotrophos (“child nurturer”) was the epithet given in ancient Greece to deities believed to protect children and young people. Rather than a single god, it named a role shared by several divinities.

The myth

Numerous gods and goddesses bore the epithet, among them Athena, Apollo, Hermes, Hecate, Aphrodite, Artemis and Gaia. They were usually shown holding an infant in their arms, although the epithet has no origin story of its own: it describes one facet of each of these figures.

In Athens, Kourotrophos was also a local deity, not among the major Olympians, who protected children and young people.

Worship and religion

Athens built a sanctuary in her honour, the Kourotropheion, and she appeared in sacrifice groups linked to fertility and child care.

Legacy

Cypriot figurines from roughly 2000 to 1800 BCE, showing women holding infants, have been interpreted as representations of a great goddess, fertility charms, childbirth aids or companions to the dead. Their meaning is still debated.

In art

Trivia

A sanctuary in Athens. The Kourotropheion was built in Athens in her name, honouring her local cult.
Bird faces. Some later Cypriot figurines have bird faces with prominent beaks and fuller, more voluptuous bodies.
