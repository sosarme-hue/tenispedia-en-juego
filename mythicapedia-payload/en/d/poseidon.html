Greek god of the sea, earthquakes and horses

Poseidon

Poseidon is one of the twelve Olympian gods of Greek mythology, lord of the sea, storms, earthquakes and horses. Brother of Zeus, he links the maritime world with some of the oldest layers of Greek religion, since he was already worshipped in Mycenaean Bronze Age Greece.

The myth

According to the tradition, Poseidon was a son of Cronus and Rhea, and one of three brothers who divided the world by lot after overthrowing their father: Zeus received the sky, Hades the underworld, and Poseidon the sea, while the earth and Mount Olympus were shared by all three.

In the Iliad, Poseidon supports the Greeks in the Trojan War, although in Book XX he rescues Aeneas when Achilles lays him low. In the Odyssey his enmity with Odysseus drives the story: the hero had blinded the god's son, the Cyclops Polyphemus, and Poseidon punished him with storms, the loss of his ship and his companions, and a delay of ten years in returning to Ithaca. His wrath could devastate cities through a sea monster.

Other tales tell of his rivalry with Athena for the patronage of Athens. In the story, Poseidon offered a spring of salt water while Athena gave the olive tree, and the Athenians chose her; as punishment, the god sent a great flood onto the Attic plain. Plato, in the Timaeus and Critias, places the legendary island of Atlantis under his domain, where he fathered a royal line. He is usually shown as a bearded, muscular man with a trident, riding a chariot over the sea, accompanied by tritons, nereids and hippocamps.

Worship and religion

His cult spread across Greece and southern Italy, especially strong in the Peloponnese and the Ionian coastal cities. Bulls, boars and rams were offered to him, and at the Isthmus of Corinth the Isthmian Games included horse and chariot races in his honour. The best-preserved temple is at Cape Sounion. According to Herodotus, the cult reached the Greeks through the Pelasgians, and Alexander the Great is said to have sacrificed a four-horse chariot in his name before the battle of Issus.

Legacy

The trident is his most recognisable attribute in art and literature. In Rome he was identified with Neptune, whose name was given to the planet Neptune, and Renaissance humanists imagined him as the god of the Mediterranean. In film he appears in Jason and the Argonauts (1963) and in the Percy Jackson series, where he is the father of the title hero, and he is a final boss in the video game God of War 3.

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad and Odyssey), Hesiod (Theogony), the Homeric Hymns, Pindar, Herodotus and Pausanias.

Trivia

A horse and a goddess. Pausanias tells that Poseidon united with Demeter in the form of a horse, and from this union were born Arion and Despoina.
Horses and springs. In Greek, the words for horse and spring are related, which fits the myth of a god who made springs burst forth with a strike of his trident.
