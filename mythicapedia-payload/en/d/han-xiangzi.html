Taoist immortal, one of the Eight Immortals and patron of music

Han Xiangzi

Han Xiangzi is one of the Eight Immortals of the Taoist pantheon and is regarded as the patron of flutists and musicians. According to tradition he was the nephew of the Tang statesman and poet Han Yu, although it is unknown whether he existed historically.

The myth

According to tradition, Han Xiangzi was a carefree young man who wandered with Lü Dongbin, another of the Eight Immortals. One day Lü asked him to climb a peach tree to fetch a fruit; as he came down he fell, died, and, freed from his body, became an immortal.

The stories say he tried to persuade his uncle to give up his official posts. To show him the power of the Tao, he made wine flow endlessly from a gourd and caused blue flowers to appear bearing verses about a snowbound pass, which Han Yu did not understand until years later. Exiled and caught in a storm at Lan Pass, Han Yu was helped across by his nephew. Later novelistic versions make Han Xiangzi an adopted son of Han Yu, married off to keep him from the Tao, and show him saving his relatives so they too could become immortal. Another popular tale tells of his love for the Dragon Girl, who gave him a golden bamboo that he turned into a flute he carried ever after.

Legacy

He is a prominent figure in Taoist art and Chinese popular iconography, where he appears with his flute, a symbol of harmony. His story has inspired television series and operas and has appeared in the series Jackie Chan Adventures.

Trivia

A fatal peach tree. His fall from a peach tree, while reaching for fruit, is what opened the way to his immortality.
Flowers that change colour. In an old account, he could change the colour of peony flowers, astonishing his uncle.
