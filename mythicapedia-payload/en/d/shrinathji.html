child form of Krishna worshipped at Nathdwara

Shrinathji

Shrinathji is a form of Krishna manifested as a seven-year-old child, and the central deity of the Pushtimarg Vaishnava sect founded by the saint Vallabhacharya. His worship remains very much alive today, centred on the temple of Nathdwara in Rajasthan.

The myth

According to tradition, the image of Shrinathji self-manifested in stone and emerged from Govardhan Hill near Mathura, where it was first worshipped under the name Devadaman, "the conqueror of gods", recalling Krishna's defeat of Indra when he lifted the hill.

To save it from the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb, who wanted to destroy it, the image was moved from Mathura in 1672, stayed for some months in Agra, and then travelled south on a chariot. The wheels are said to have sunk into the mud at Sihad in Mewar, a sign that this was the deity's chosen place, and the image was installed there under the protection of Maharana Raj Singh.

Worship and religion

The main temple is at Nathdwara, where the image receives eight services a day: it is dressed, offered food and treated as a living child. The eight public viewings, called darshan, draw many devotees, and major festivals such as Janmashtami, Holi and Diwali bring large crowds.
