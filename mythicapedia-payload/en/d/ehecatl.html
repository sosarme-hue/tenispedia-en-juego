Mexica god of wind and air

Ehecatl

Ehecatl is the Mexica (Aztec) god of wind and air, one of the creator gods and culture heroes of central Mexico. He is often seen as an aspect of the Feathered Serpent, Quetzalcoatl, and is then called Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl.

The myth

According to Aztec myth, his breath sets the Sun in motion and announces the rain, then pushes it aside so the sky clears. After the fifth sun was created, it stood fixed in the sky, as the moon did, until Ehecatl blew on them and set them moving. His breath also brings life to what is lifeless and can be felt in the breath of every living being.

The stories tell that he fell in love with a human girl named Mayah. So that she could return his passion, he gave humankind the ability to love, and also the ability to fly so she could reach him. Their love is symbolized by a beautiful tree that grows where he first came down to earth.

He was usually shown wearing a red, beak-shaped mouth mask, with which he cleared the path for Tlaloc, the rain god, and the tlaloque, lesser gods.

Worship and religion

His temples were circular, which reduced air resistance and helped the wind circulate. He was associated with all four cardinal directions, since the wind blows in every direction. The Otomi people knew him as Edahi. In June 2017, archaeologists from Mexico's INAH, led by Eduardo Matos, announced the discovery of a temple of Ehecatl and a ballcourt in Mexico City's historic center. The temple faced the Templo Mayor, while the court, aligned from east to west, pointed toward the shrine of Huitzilopochtli, the god of war. This arrangement reflects both the rivalry and the cooperation between the Mexica gods.

Legacy

His image still inspires modern art, as in El Vigilante, a sculpture by Jorge Marín in Ecatepec, State of Mexico.

Trivia

Death whistle. The Aztec death whistle was found at his temple. Researchers still debate how it was used in rituals.
Two masks. He was sometimes depicted with two masks through which the wind blew. He has a conch shell on his chest, which imitates the sound of the wind.
