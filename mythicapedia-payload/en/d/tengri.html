sky god of the Turkic and Mongolic peoples

Tengri

Tengri, also spelled Tengger, is the all-encompassing god of the sky in the traditional religions of the Turkic and Mongolic peoples. He was the chief deity of the ruling class across the Central Asian steppe between the 6th and 9th centuries, and his name gave rise to Tengrism, a faith that blends shamanism, animism, totemism and ancestor veneration.

The myth

According to Turkic tradition, Tengri is the supreme god who created all things and controls the celestial sphere. The oldest surviving record of his worship is found in the Orkhon inscriptions of the 8th century, which tell of the mythical origins of the Turks and state that Tengri creates death, so that all humans are born to die and only Tengri can grant life again.

In one Turkic story, Tengri is a pure white goose flying endlessly over a boundless sea, which stands for time. Beneath the waters, the White Mother, Ak Ana, urges him to create. To escape his loneliness, Tengri makes Er Kishi, a being less pure than himself who turns demonic and tries to lead people into darkness. Tengri then takes the name Tengri Ülgen and withdraws into heaven, from where he guides humanity through sacred animals.

In the traditional family of gods, Tengri is the father of the sun (Koyash), the moon (Ay Tanrı) and Umay, and is sometimes linked with Ülgen and Erlik. The core beings of Tengrism are the Sky Father and the Earth Mother (Yer Tanrı). The Göktürk khans claimed to be sons of Tengri, charged with representing him on earth.

Worship and religion

Tengri was worshipped across the steppe with offerings and shamanic ceremonies, and rulers governed in his name. His importance declined in the 8th century, when the Uyghur khagans made Manichaeism the state religion. Today Tengrism survives among communities in Central Asia, Mongolia and Siberia, and has been revived by contemporary religious movements.

Legacy

The name lives on in modern Turkish, where Tanrı means "God" and can refer to the God of the Abrahamic faiths. Several places bear his name, such as Khan Tengri peak in the Tian Shan and the Tengger Desert in Inner Mongolia. He has often been compared with sky gods such as Jupiter, Zeus and Perun.

Ancient sources: The earliest sources are Chinese annals, which record the name as Cheng-li in describing Xiongnu beliefs, and the 8th-century Orkhon inscriptions.

Trivia

A mountain named after him. A pyramidal peak in the Tian Shan range, between China, Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan, is called Khan Tengri.
An Antarctic namesake. The Tangra Mountains on Livingston Island in the South Shetland Islands of Antarctica are also named after this deity.
