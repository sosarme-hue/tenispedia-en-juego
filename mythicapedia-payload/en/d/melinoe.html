Greek goddess of nightmares and madness

Melinoë

Melinoë is a chthonic goddess of Greek religion, known mainly from an Orphic Hymn that portrays her as a bringer of nightmares and madness. Her traits resemble those of Hecate and the Erinyes, and her name may be an epithet of Hecate.

The myth

In the Orphic Hymn that invokes her, Melinoë is the daughter of Persephone and Zeus, who deceived Persephone by taking the form of Hades. The episode parallels another Orphic story, the birth of Dionysus, in which Zeus, disguised as a serpent, mates with Persephone.

She is born at the mouth of the Cocytus, one of the rivers of the underworld, where Hermes stands in his role as psychopomp. Her body was said to be half light and half darkness, reflecting both parents. She appears in frightening forms, sometimes plainly visible, sometimes in shadow or shining in the dark, and drives mortals to madness through phantoms and hostile contact at night. The hymn asks her to send the disturbance of the soul to the edges of the earth and to show a kind face to her initiates.

Legacy

Melinoë appears on a bronze tablet discovered at Pergamon, dated to the first half of the 3rd century AD, used in a private ritual usually called magic. The triangular tablet shows three crowned goddesses named Dione, Phoebe and Nyche, and its inscription includes a triple invocation naming Melinoë alongside Persephone and Leucophryne. Today she is the protagonist of the video game Hades II by Supergiant Games, where she is princess of the underworld.

Ancient sources: Her only known classical source is Orphic Hymn 71, which addresses her as a young infernal woman.

Trivia

Name and colour. Melinoë may derive from a Greek word meaning having the colour of quince, a yellowish green that the Greeks associated with the pallor of illness or death.
Clad in saffron. The hymn describes her as clad in saffron, an epithet also used for Eos, the personification of dawn.
