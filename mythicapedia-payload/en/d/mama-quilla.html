Inca moon goddess

Mama Quilla

Mama Quilla, also known as Killari, is the moon goddess of Inca religion and the mythical ancestor of the empire's rulers. She matters because she was the wife of Inti, the sun god, and because she governed the calendar, fertility and the protection of women.

The myth

According to Inca tradition, Mama Quilla was the daughter of Viracocha, the creator god, and Mama Cocha, goddess of the waters, although in another account she is the daughter of Pachamama, the earth goddess. She married her younger brother Inti, and their children were Manco Cápac, the first Inca, and Mama Ocllo, the first coya. As ancestor of the dynasty, she made the rulers divine by descent, which is why marriage between siblings was practised to keep the divine blood pure.

Stories also explain the dark patches on the Moon. A fox fell in love with her beauty and rose into the sky, but when he reached her she squeezed him against her body, leaving the marks on her surface. Another myth says that during eclipses a wild animal, perhaps a puma or a serpent, was attacking her, so people shouted, banged and threw weapons to drive it away.

A legend tells that Illapa, god of thunder, kept water drawn from the Milky Way in a urpu and gave it to Mama Quilla to guard. When the vessel filled, Illapa fired a projectile that made the thunder, the sparks were lightning, and the water spilling out became rain.

Worship and religion

Mama Quilla had her own temple in Cusco, the imperial capital, served by priestesses dedicated to her. She was imagined as a beautiful woman who wept silver tears, and her images included a silver disc covering an entire wall. In the tenth month of the calendar, Coya Raymi (September), a festival in her honour was held, a feast for women.

Legacy

The words for the Moon in Quechua, Aymara and Mapuche share similarities that some linguists attribute to a possible borrowing from Puquina, and the lunar and solar couple also appears in Mapuche cosmology, where the Moon (Quilla or Cuyen) and the Sun (Inti or Antu) are spouses.

Ancient sources: The chronicles of Father Bernabé Cobo, written in the mid-sixteenth century, record Inca lunar worship.

Trivia

Silver tears. Mama Quilla was said to weep tears of silver, a metal associated with the Moon.
A shared word. Quechua, Aymara and Mapuche have similar words for the Moon, although the languages are not genetically related.
