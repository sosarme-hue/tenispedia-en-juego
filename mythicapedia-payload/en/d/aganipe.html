nymph of the spring of the Muses on Mount Helicon

Aganippe

In Greek myth, Aganippe is both a spring and the naiad who was associated with it, one of the Crinaeae, at the foot of Mount Helicon in Boeotia. Its waters were linked to poetic inspiration and to the Muses.

The myth

According to the tradition, Aganippe was a daughter of the river-god Permessus, whom Pausanias calls Termessus. Ancient sources place her spring near Thespiae, at the base of Helicon, and connect the nymph with the water itself.

The stories say that drinking from her spring granted poetic inspiration. For this reason the Muses, sometimes called Aganippides, were associated with the fountain. Ovid mentions her alongside Hippocrene, the other spring of Helicon, which was also said to have been created by the hooves of the winged horse Pegasus.

Legacy

The name of the spring became a modern symbol of poetry. In Madrid's Retiro Park, Leandro Fernández de Moratín and his friends would meet by a fountain they called the "Fuente Aganipe" because of its link to the Muses.

Ancient sources: Ovid links her with Hippocrene, and Pausanias names the father river Termessus.

Trivia

Spring of Pegasus. The winged horse Pegasus was said to have made the spring of Aganippe burst forth with his hooves, as he did with Hippocrene on the same mountain.
A name in Madrid. A fountain in Madrid's Retiro Park was called the "Fuente Aganipe" after the gatherings of Moratín and his friends.
