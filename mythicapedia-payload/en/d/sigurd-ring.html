legendary king of Sweden and Denmark

Sigurd Ring

Sigurd Ring, also called Sigurd Hring (Old Norse for 'ring'), is a legendary king of Sweden and Denmark in Norse tradition. He is best known as the father of Ragnar Lodbrok, a central figure in the Viking sagas.

The myth

According to most sagas, Sigurd was the son of Randver, king of Denmark, and the nephew of Harald Wartooth, who had inherited much of the lands of his maternal grandfather, Ivar Vidfamne. After Randver's death, Sigurd received Denmark, probably under his uncle's authority. Saxo Grammaticus, however, makes him the son of the Swedish king Ingjald and his maternal nephew through Harald, a sign of how much genealogies differ between authors.

The central episode is the battle of Brávellir, in Östergötland, where Sigurd faced his uncle. Harald died with many of his men, and Sigurd took the Danish crown. Some accounts add that Odin himself intervened in the fight.

In old age, tradition places Sigurd at Skiringssal, where great blóts were held. According to the Skjöldunga saga, he fell in love with Alfsol, daughter of King Alf of Vendel. Her brothers refused to let him marry her; after Sigurd killed them, he discovered she had been poisoned so that she could never be his. He laid her body on a ship beside her brothers and sent it out to sea in flames.

His wife was Alfhild, daughter of King Alf of Alvheim, and with her he had Ragnar Lodbrok, who succeeded him in Denmark. Ragnar placed Eysteinn Beli as viceroy in Sweden, and Eysteinn was later killed by Ragnar's sons.

Ancient sources: Bósa saga ok Herrauðs, Hervarar saga, Sögubrot af nokkrum fornkonungum, Skjöldunga saga, Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, Heimskringla and Hversu Noregr byggdist, among others.

Trivia

A nickname meaning ring. His epithet Hring means 'ring' in Old Norse, which is where the name by which he is known comes from.
Two Sigurd Rings. In book 9, Saxo Grammaticus presents another Sigurd Ring, son of a Norwegian chief, who fought a civil war against his cousin. He is not the victor of Brávellir.
