son of Oedipus and Theban prince in Greek mythology

Polynices

Polynices is, in Greek mythology, the son of Oedipus and brother of Eteocles, Antigone and Ismene. His fratricidal struggle for the throne of Thebes makes him a central figure of the Theban Cycle and of Sophocles' tragedy Antigone.

The myth

According to the tradition, after Oedipus discovered that he had killed his father and married his mother, Jocasta, he left Thebes and left his sons Polynices and Eteocles to rule together. A curse from their father, which in the Thebaid follows a slight at the dinner table, soon undid their agreement. The brothers agreed to rule in alternate years, but when Polynices' turn came, Eteocles refused to give up the throne and Polynices was driven out.

In Argos, Polynices married Argia, daughter of King Adrastus, who promised to help him regain Thebes. Adrastus gathered seven champions to attack the city. The expedition failed, and during the siege the two brothers fought in single combat and killed each other. Creon, who took the throne, forbade the burial of Polynices for having fought against his homeland, and Antigone defied him, with a tragic fate for herself. According to the tradition, his son Thersander, born to Argia, later ruled Thebes after the Epigoni captured the city.

Legacy

His story is central to Sophocles' three Theban plays, Oedipus Rex, Oedipus at Colonus and Antigone, and the episode of his burial drives the plot of the last one. He also appears in the Thebaid, Aeschylus' Seven Against Thebes and Statius' epic. Philosophers such as Aristotle, Plato and Epictetus discussed his story.

Ancient sources: The Thebaid, Aeschylus' Seven Against Thebes and Sophocles' Oedipus at Colonus and Antigone.

Trivia

A name with character. His name means quarrelsome, a trait that fits his role as his brother's rival.
A lion skin. He arrived in Argos wearing a lion skin, and the oracle Adrastus had received about a lion and a boar led him to marry his daughters to him and to Tydeus.
