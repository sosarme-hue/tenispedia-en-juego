primordial Buddha in Mahayana Buddhism

Adi-Buddha

The Adi-Buddha, or Primordial Buddha, is a Mahayana Buddhist concept for the most fundamental and supreme Buddha, understood to have been awakened from the very beginning. The idea appears mainly in tantric literature and expresses the ultimate nature of the mind and innate enlightenment.

The myth

According to these traditions, the Adi-Buddha did not reach awakening at a particular moment but has always been awake. Buddhism holds that the cosmos had no beginning, so this buddha is seen as prior to all others. The word ādi can be read two ways: 'first', as the first to attain Buddhahood, and 'primordial', as an innate wisdom present in every sentient being.

Indian tantric works give it several forms. The Kalachakra Tantra links it with the ultimate buddha-nature and awakened gnosis, while the Guhyasamāja Tantra presents Vajradhara, the 'vajra holder', as its central figure. One Indian current identified it with Mañjuśrī, whom the commentator Vilāsavajra describes as non-dual wisdom itself.

In Tibetan Buddhism it takes three main forms: Samantabhadra in the Nyingma and Bön traditions, Vajradhara in the Kagyu, Gelug and Sakya schools, and Kalachakra in the Jonang. Nyingma art often shows Samantabhadra as a naked blue buddha, a colour that stands for boundless space and a reality beyond concepts.

Worship and religion

The main temple on Swayambhu hill, near Kathmandu in Nepal, is dedicated to the Adi-Buddha. In Nyingma Dzogchen, Samantabhadra is understood more as a metaphor for our original condition than as a creator, so practice focuses on recognizing that innate nature.

Ancient sources: Tantric texts such as the Kalachakra Tantra and the Guhyasamāja Tantra, and the Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra, which discusses related terms such as Mūlatathāgata.

Trivia

A naked blue buddha. In Nyingma art, Samantabhadra appears naked and blue. The lack of robes symbolizes reality beyond any conceptual clothing.
