king of the spirits in Balinese mythology

Barong

Barong is the king of the spirits in Balinese mythology, a lion-like creature who embodies good. His figure may predate the arrival of Hinduism on the island, and today he is the most popular symbol of Balinese culture.

The myth

According to tradition, Barong is the protective spirit that animates Banas Pati Raja, the fourth spiritual "brother" who accompanies each person throughout life, much like a guardian angel. He is often shown with two monkeys and described with a red head, thick white fur and gilded jewellery decorated with mirrors.

His great enemy is Rangda, the demon queen. In the story of Calon Arang, a widow witch causes havoc in ancient Java during the reign of Airlangga after failing to find a husband for her daughter. Another version links Rangda to Queen Mahendradatta, Airlangga's mother, who sought revenge after being widowed. In the final battle, Airlangga asked Barong for help: Rangda made his soldiers stab themselves with their own keris, but Barong protected them with a spell and defeated her, though she escaped.

Worship and religion

In Bali he is venerated mainly through the Barong dance, performed during the Galungan and Kuningan holidays, when several barongs are paraded through the villages. During the dance, a priest sprinkles holy water over the creature. This tradition remains alive as part of Balinese religious life.

Legacy

The dance is one of the most popular shows for tourists visiting Ubud. There are variants shaped like a lion, tiger, pig, elephant or dog, such as the barong asu of Pacung.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Barong may derive from the local word bahruang, related to beruang, which means 'bear' in Indonesian.
A canine resemblance. Barong's shape is said to resemble that of a Pekingese dog.
