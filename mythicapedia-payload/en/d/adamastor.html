Adamastor

Adamastor is a monstrous giant who haunts the Cape of Good Hope, created by Luís de Camões in Os Lusíadas (1572). He personifies the perils of the sea that Portuguese navigators had to overcome during the Age of Discovery.

The myth

According to Camões, Adamastor was one of the Giants of Greek mythology who fell in love with Tethys, daughter of the sea goddess Doris. Punished by her, he was banished to the cape, where he appeared within storm clouds to threaten anyone who tried to round it and reach the Indian Ocean, his domain.

When Vasco da Gama's expedition arrived, the giant answered the question "Who are you?" by telling his story. Deeply moved, he vanished, scattering the clouds and calming the sea, leaving the route to India open. From then on, the Cape of Storms became known as the Cape of Good Hope.

Legacy

A popular Lisbon viewpoint takes its name from the large stone statue of him that stands there. Fernando Pessoa dedicated verses to him in Mensagem (1934), and José Saramago mentions him in several novels. He appears in Meyerbeer's opera L'Africaine, and in works by Victor Hugo and Alexandre Dumas. A sauropod dinosaur found in Angola bears his name: Angolatitan adamastor.

Ancient sources: The main source is Camões's Os Lusíadas (1572), which gives the character his Greek backstory; no specific ancient texts about him are cited.

Trivia

A Lisbon viewpoint. The Miradouro de Santa Catarina in Lisbon is known as Adamastor because of its large statue, and offers views of the Tagus, the 25 de Abril Bridge and the Cristo-Rei.
A dinosaur with his name. The sauropod Angolatitan adamastor, found in Angola, was named by the paleontologist Octávio Mateus.
A Greek origin for the name. Adamastor comes from the Greek Adamastos, meaning "untameable", a word the Portuguese managed to tame in their own language.
