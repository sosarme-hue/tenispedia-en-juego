Norse god of will and brother of Odin

Vili

Vili is one of the Æsir in Norse mythology, a son of Borr and brother of Odin and Vé. He is credited with giving humans emotions and intelligence, though his role is minor next to Odin's, and he matters mainly as part of the trio of brothers who shaped the world and the first people.

The myth

According to the myths, Vili, Vé and Odin were the three brothers who slew the giant Ymir, and they made the world from his body. They then created the first humans, Ask and Embla, from two trees on the seashore. Odin gave them soul and life; Vili gave them wit and the sense of touch; and Vé gave them appearance, speech, hearing and sight. In the Völuspá the brothers appear as Hœnir and Lóðurr, and since Snorri knew that poem, he considered it likely that Hœnir was another name for Vili.

The best-known story about Vili comes from Loki's taunts in the Lokasenna. While Odin was away for a long time, his brothers ruled in his place, and Loki accuses Vili and Vé of having been involved with Odin's wife, Frigg. When Odin returned, he took his wife back. The scholar Grimm saw in this episode a reflection of the basic unity of the three brothers, so that Frigg could be considered the wife of any of them. Saxo Grammaticus tells a similar tale with a different substitute ruler, showing how the stories varied between sources. Vili and Vé have little presence in the surviving texts, while Odin holds the leading place in the pantheon.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and Snorri's prose Edda (Gylfaginning) mention him, and the Lokasenna in the Poetic Edda recounts the episode with Frigg.

Trivia

A name meaning will. Vili means "will" in Old Norse, a word related to Gothic wilja and Old English willa.
A Proto-Germanic triad. In Proto-Norse the names of the three brothers alliterated (Wōdinaz, Wiljô, Wīhaz), which suggests a triad linked to inspiration, will and the sacred.
