king of the nagas in Buddhist mythology

Mucalinda

Mucalinda (also Mucilinda) is the king of the nagas, serpent-like beings in Buddhist and Hindu tradition, who according to the story sheltered the Buddha from a great storm just after his enlightenment. It is one of the most frequently depicted episodes in Buddhist art.

The myth

According to tradition, some weeks after Sakyamuni Gautama began meditating beneath the bodhi tree, the sky darkened for seven days and a torrential rain fell. The mighty King of Serpents then rose from the earth, coiled his body around the Buddha seven times, and spread his hood above him to shelter him.

When the storm had passed, the serpent king took human form, bowed before the Buddha and returned joyfully to his palace. In the Mucalinda Sutta, the protection lasts seven days; in the Lalitavistara, Mucalinda comes together with nagas from the four cardinal directions, and on leaving he circles the Buddha three times. The versions differ in details such as the number of weeks or the serpent king's final form.

Legacy

The scene of the Buddha meditating under Mucalinda's protection is very common in Lao Buddhist art, and a gigantic modern rendition of it stands in Bunleua Sulilat's Sala Keoku sculpture park. Tibetan and Mongolian depictions also feature him often. The earliest surviving artwork, from around the 2nd century BC, comes from a stupa in Pauni, Maharashtra, and shows the naga with five heads. Some scholars suggest the image may have been influenced by Jain art of Parshvanatha, who is shown beneath a cobra-like hood.

Ancient sources: The Mucalinda Sutta in Pali and the Lalitavistara Sutra, Buddhist texts that narrate the episode.

Trivia

A tree with his name. In Sanskrit, muchilinda referred to the Pterospermum acerifolium, a tree known in other countries as karnikara or baiur. Its broad leaves are used as disposable plates, which gives it the popular name 'food plate tree'.
A Hindu parallel. The myth has a parallel with the Hindu god Vishnu resting on the serpent Shesha, showing the links between Buddhist and Hindu traditions.
