Argonaut son of Helios

Thersanon

In Greek mythology, Thersanon is one of the Argonauts, the crew of the Argo that sailed in search of the Golden Fleece. A son of the sun god Helios, he is a minor figure whose mother is named differently depending on the ancient author.

The myth

According to the tradition, Thersanon was born on the island of Andros, the son of the sun god Helios and a woman called Leucothoe. He joined Jason and the other Argonauts in their quest for the Golden Fleece, guarded by King Aeëtes of Colchis, who was also a son of Helios and therefore Thersanon's paternal half-brother.

The mother's story is the complicated part. In Ovid, Leucothoe was a mortal princess of Babylon loved by Helios. When her father learned of the affair through Clytie, he buried her alive, and Helios turned her body into a frankincense tree. Hyginus, however, gives her a son, which suggests he knew a version in which she survived long enough to give birth, or that he means another woman, perhaps the sea goddess Leucothea, whom he also calls 'Leucothoe' elsewhere in the same work.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, in his Fabulae, and Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, which tells the story of Leucothoe.

Trivia

A frankincense tree. In Ovid's version, Leucothoe's body does not simply vanish; Helios transforms it into a tree that produces frankincense.
