frost giant of Norse mythology

Ivaldi

Ivaldi is one of the frost giants of Scandinavian mythology, best known as the father of two lines of offspring: giants, elves and craftsmen. He matters because his descendants link the giants with the world of the gods.

The myth

According to the stories, Ivaldi had two sets of children, which suggests he had different mothers. From the first set came Idun, the youngest, who belonged to the elf race and was a sister or half-sister of the so-called sons of Ivaldi, famous craftsmen who worked for the gods and were half giant and half elf or dwarf. Among his descendants is also the giant Þjazi.

The giant's name has variants: Ölvaldi and Allvaldi. The Skáldskaparmál gives Ölvaldi three sons: Þjazi, Idi and Gang. In the poem Hárbarðsljóð, an Allvaldi is named as the father of the giant whose eyes Thor turned into stars, so it is probably another form of the same name.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Skáldskaparmál and in the poem Hárbarðsljóð from the Norse Eddas.

Trivia

Alternative names. Ivaldi also appears as Ölvaldi or Allvaldi, two parallel forms of the name that may refer to the same giant.
Eyes turned into stars. In Hárbarðsljóð, Thor turns the eyes of a giant whose father is named Allvaldi into stars.
