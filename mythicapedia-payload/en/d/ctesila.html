maiden of Ioulis on the island of Ceos

Ctesylla

Ctesylla is a maiden of Ioulis on the island of Ceos, daughter of Alcidamas, and the heroine of a Greek love story built around a binding oath. Her cult in Ioulis links her to Aphrodite, while elsewhere on the island she was linked to Artemis.

The myth

According to the tradition, during the Pythian festival the Athenian Hermochares saw Ctesylla dancing before the altar of Apollo and fell in love with her. He threw her an apple carved with an oath in Artemis' name, and when she read it aloud she was bound to marry him.

Her father Alcidamas had sworn by Apollo to consent, but once the festival ended he forgot his word and planned to marry her to someone else. Hermochares found Ctesylla at Artemis' temple, where she was making sacrifices, and the two fled to Athens and wed. As punishment for her father's broken oath, Ctesylla died in childbirth. At her funeral a dove flew from her deathbed and her body vanished. An oracle told Hermochares to found a sanctuary in her honour.

Worship and religion

At Ioulis she received sacrifices as Aphrodite Ctesylla, while elsewhere on Ceos Ctesylla was an epithet of Artemis.

Trivia

A famous parallel. Her story parallels that of Cydippe and Acontius, another couple bound by an oath written down for them.
A dove at the funeral. According to the story, a dove flew from her deathbed and her body disappeared.
