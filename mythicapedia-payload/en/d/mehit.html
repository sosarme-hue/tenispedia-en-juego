ancient Egyptian lioness goddess

Mehit

Mehit, also spelled Mehyt or Mekhit, was an ancient Egyptian lioness goddess whose name means "the complete one." Consort of the hunter Anhur, she appears in art and worship from the Early Dynastic period and is linked to the full moon and to the Eye of Ra.

The myth

In the Early Dynastic period she was shown as a reclining lioness with three bent poles projecting from her back. According to tradition, Anhur, a hunter god worshipped at Thinis, tracked her down in Nubia and brought her to Egypt as his wife. His name is often read as "bringer-back of the distant one."

Several texts allude to this episode, and late sources link it to the myth of the Distant Goddess, in which the Eye of Ra runs away from her father Ra, who sends one of the gods to retrieve her. In that version, Anhur is identified with Shu and Mehit with Hathor-Tefnut, Shu's mythological sister and wife. Because Shu and Tefnut sometimes represented the sun and moon, Mehit could also stand for the full moon, and her return to her proper place would represent the restoration of the Eye of Horus, a symbol of the moon and of the divine order of the cosmos.

Egyptologist Geraldine Pinch suggests that the Distant Goddess may originally have been a personification of the wild deserts of Nubia, whose myth was absorbed into a larger complex surrounding the Eye of Ra. Toby Wilkinson, for his part, proposes that in early dynastic times Mehit may have been a protector goddess tied to holy places, without being assigned to a single locality.

Worship and religion

Her main places of worship were Hierakonpolis and Thinis, and she was also venerated at Edfu. The sources do not describe her rites or festivals in detail.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Mehit means "the complete one," and her name also appears in forms such as Mehyt or Mekhit.
Nubian origin. Some sources describe her as a lioness goddess of Nubian origin, and her myth places her in the deserts of Nubia before she is brought to Egypt.
