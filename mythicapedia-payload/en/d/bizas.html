legendary founder of Byzantium, son of Poseidon

Byzas

Byzas is the legendary founder of Byzantium, the city later known as New Rome and Constantinople, and today as Istanbul. In Greek tradition he was a son of Poseidon and the nymph Ceroessa, and his story explains the mythical origin of one of the most important cities of antiquity.

The myth

According to the most widely told account, King Nisos of Megara sent his son Byzas to search for the land opposite the city of the blind, as the oracle at Delphi had instructed. On reaching the point where the Bosporus meets the Sea of Marmara, Byzas noticed that the colonists of Chalcedon, on the Asian shore, had chosen the worst site, so he founded Byzantium on the European shore.

Other traditions make him a son of Poseidon and the nymph Ceroessa, after whom the Golden Horn was said to be named, Khrysokeras. When the Thracian Haemus attacked the city, Byzas pursued him into Thrace. In his absence, Odrises, king of Scythia, besieged Byzantium, but the city was saved by his wife Phidalea, who with other women threw snakes into the enemy camp.

Worship and religion

The people of Byzantium regarded him as their oikistes, or founder, and according to ancient sources they raised a statue of him in a prominent place in the city, alongside one of Phidalea.

Legacy

The city he founded later became New Rome and Constantinople, and today it is called Istanbul. His story remains tied to the legendary origins of a site that lies between two seas and two continents.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, Strabo and Tacitus refer to the city and the saying about the blind, and Hesychius of Miletus preserves the various founding myths in the Patria of Constantinople.

Trivia

The Golden Horn. According to some versions, the inlet that borders the city was named Khrysokeras in honour of Ceroessa, the mother of Byzas.
A joke about blindness. Herodotus recounts that the Persian general Megabazus called the people of Chalcedon blind for choosing the worst of the available sites.
