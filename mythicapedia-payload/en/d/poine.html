Greek spirit of punishment and vengeance

Poine

Poine, also called Poena, was in Greek mythology the spirit of punishment and vengeance for homicide. Some sources link her with Praxidice, the personification of justice, while others present her as an attendant of Nemesis, goddess of divine retribution.

The myth

According to tradition, the Poenai, in the plural, were avenging spirits who punished killings. They were often depicted in tragedy and were akin to the Erinyes. Hyginus lists a figure called Ultio, Vengeance, among the personifications born of Aether and Earth.

Valerius Flaccus tells how Aeson and Alcimede called upon Poena and the Erinyes to punish the usurper Pelias. Statius places her in the court of Erebus, presided over by Hades. Pausanias relates that Apollo sent Poine against the Argives because of the death of his son Linus. In that account she snatched children from their mothers until Coroebus killed her.

Legacy

The Greek word meant recompense or punishment. From it comes the Latin poena, meaning pain, punishment or penalty, which gave rise to English words such as subpoena and pain.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Hyginus, Valerius Flaccus, Statius and the Suda mention her, among other ancient authors.

Trivia

The case of Linus. Pausanias says Apollo sent Poine to punish the Argives for the death of Linus. The legend presents her as a threat to children until Coroebus killed her.
A word with history. From Greek, through Latin poena, come English words such as subpoena and pain, used today without much thought to their origin in the idea of punishment.
