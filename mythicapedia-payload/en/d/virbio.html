Roman god of the sacred grove of Nemi

Virbius

Virbius is an ancient Roman god worshipped in the sacred grove of Nemi, near Aricia, and linked to the cult of Diana. He matters because of a late legend that identifies him with the hero Hippolytus, whom the goddess brought back to life. He is recognised as one of the companions in her retinue.

The myth

According to tradition, horses were not allowed into his grove. From this a late belief arose: Virbius would be Hippolytus, son of Theseus, killed by his own horses. Diana was said to have restored him to life with the help of Asclepius and taken him to Italy, where he joined her retinue.

The name is explained by Latin vir ("man") and bis ("twice"), alluding to his double life.

Worship and religion

His sanctuary stood in the sacred grove of Nemi, in the territory of Aricia, and his cult was joined to that of Diana, whom he accompanied as a secondary figure in the goddess's circle.

Ancient sources: Virgil, in the Aeneid, and Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, record the story of the resurrected Hippolytus and his link to Diana.

Trivia

A name that sounds like a paradox. Virbius means "twice a man", a label that sums up the hero's fate: killed and brought back to life.
