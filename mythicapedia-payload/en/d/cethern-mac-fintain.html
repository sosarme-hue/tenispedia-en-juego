Ulster warrior in Irish mythology

Cethern mac Fintain

Cethern mac Fintain is an Ulster warrior who aids Cúchulainn in the Táin Bó Cuailnge, part of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. His refusal to yield even to fatal wounds makes him one of the most memorable fighters in the story.

The myth

According to the myth, when Cúchulainn lay wounded after his duel with Ferdiad, Cethern charged into the battle on his chariot, stark naked and armed only with a silver spike. He killed many men, but was badly wounded.

Cúchulainn sent for doctors from the Connacht camp. The first one said his wounds were fatal, so Cethern punched his brains out. He did the same to the other forty-nine, except one who was only stunned.

Then Fingín, Conchobar's doctor, arrived and gave him a choice: be cured over a year, or be made fit to fight with only three days left to live. Cethern chose the second. Fingín reinforced his body with chariot parts, and he returned to battle, dying after a day and a night of fighting.

Ancient sources: It belongs to the Táin Bó Cuailnge, the epic tale of the Ulster Cycle.

Trivia

An unusual treatment. According to the story, Fingín prepared a bath of bone marrow for Cethern and replaced some of his ribs with chariot parts.
Teacher of a great hero. Cethern was said to have been a mentor of Fionn Mac Cumhaill, one of the great heroes of Irish tradition.
