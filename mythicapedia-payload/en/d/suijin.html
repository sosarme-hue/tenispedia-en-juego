Shinto water god of Japan

Suijin

Suijin is the Shinto god of water in Japan, a benevolent deity of freshwater who manifests both in heaven and on earth. He matters because he protects fishing, drinking water, crops and fertility, and his worship remains very much alive in Japan.

The myth

Suijin has no long, detailed myth of his own, as some other Japanese deities do; he is mainly described as a divinity who dwells in water. The name can refer to his heavenly and earthly manifestations, but also to a wide range of beings in lakes, ponds, springs and wells, such as serpents, dragons, eels, fish, turtles and the flesh-eating kappa.

He is often confused with Ryūjin, the dragon god of the sea, and sometimes with Fudō Myōō, because of his link to waterfalls. In popular lore he appears as the guardian of fishermen and the protector of those who seek clean water.

Worship and religion

Suijin is widely worshipped at Suitengū shrines, where offerings are made for pure water, good harvests, successful fishing, fertility and a safe childbirth. Stone votive markers placed near springs, canals, wells and rice paddies make his presence highly visible. Festivals are held in summer and winter, and the shrine in Kurume is the main Suitengū shrine in Japan.

Legacy

His influence runs through Japanese culture, and the Tsukiji Suijin Shrine beside the Tokyo fish market shows his close ties to fishing.

Trivia

Votive stones. Many of his cults take the form of small stone markers placed beside dikes, canals, rice paddies and springs, sometimes even in household wells.
Kamakura igloos. At the Kamakura festival in Akita Prefecture, igloos are built with a small alcove dedicated to Suijin to ensure good crops.
