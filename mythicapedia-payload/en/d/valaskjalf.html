Odin's hall in Norse mythology

Valaskjálf

Valaskjálf is one of Odin's halls in Norse mythology, a great dwelling roofed with pure silver. From it, the god can watch over the entire universe thanks to his high seat, Hliðskjálf.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Valaskjálf is one of Odin's halls, built by the gods and roofed with pure silver. Its name is often rendered as "the Shelf of the Slain," although its exact meaning is debated.

Inside stands Hliðskjálf, the high seat from which Odin looks out over all lands and the entire universe. Tradition describes it as the throne of the "Allfather," from which the god keeps watch over the nine worlds.

The hall appears in the Grímnismál, a poem of the Poetic Edda, and in the Gylfaginning section of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Grímnismál, part of the Poetic Edda, and in the Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.
