king of the devas in Buddhism

Śakra

Śakra is the ruler of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven and the king of the devas in Buddhism, where he is regarded as a protector of the teaching. His figure descends from the Vedic god Indra, although in Buddhist texts he takes on a character of his own.

The myth

According to Buddhist stories, Śakra reigns over Trāyastriṃśa, a heaven on the summit of Mount Meru, the polar centre of the physical world around which the Sun and Moon revolve. It is the highest of the heavens in direct contact with the human world. Although he lives for a very long time, he is not immortal: when he dies, another deity takes his place and becomes the new Śakra.

He is married to Sujā, daughter of the chief of the asuras, and a state of war usually exists between the devas and the asuras, which he manages to resolve with minimal violence. The stories often show him consulting the Buddha on questions of morality. In the Theravada tradition he is identified with Vajrapāṇi and, according to the commentator Buddhaghoṣa, is regarded as a bodhisattva.

Worship and religion

Together with Brahmā, he is venerated as a protector of Buddhism. In China he is numbered among the Twenty-Four Guardian Devas, and in some places he is identified with the Taoist Jade Emperor, whose birthday falls on the ninth day of the first lunar month. In Korean folk belief he is equated with Haneullim, and in Mongolia he is syncretised with Qormusta Tengri. In Southeast Asia he is depicted with blue or black skin, riding the three-headed elephant Airavata.

Legacy

His name appears in the ceremonial name of Bangkok. In popular culture he has inspired Indra Ōtsutsuki in the Naruto series, and he has been linked to Kami and Mr. Popo in Dragon Ball. He also appears as a character in the mobile game Monster Strike.

Ancient sources: He appears as an epithet of Indra in the Rigveda (hymn 5.34), and he is also mentioned in the Jātaka tales, the Saṃyutta Nikāya, the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra.

Trivia

Name of Bangkok. The ceremonial name of Bangkok alludes to Sakra. It begins with the Thai word krung, meaning 'capital'.
A three-headed elephant. In Theravada iconography, Sakra rides Airavata, a three-headed elephant.
Mr. Popo. The name of the Dragon Ball character Mr. Popo sounds similar to the Chinese pronunciation of Sahassākkha, an epithet of Sakra.
