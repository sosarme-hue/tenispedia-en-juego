Greek heroine, daughter of Perseus

Gorgophone

Gorgophone is a Greek heroine, daughter of Perseus and Andromeda, whose name means 'Gorgon slayer'. She is remembered as the wife of two kings and, in some accounts, as the first woman to marry twice.

The myth

According to the tradition, Gorgophone's name honours her father's killing of Medusa. Apollodorus records two versions of her marriage. In the Messenian one she wed Perieres, son of Aeolus, and bore him Aphareus, Leucippus and Tyndareus; in the Laconian one, Perieres is the son of Cynortas, and the children are Tyndareus, Icarius, Aphareus and Leucippus. Apollodorus also lists her brothers: Alcaeus, Electryon, Sthenelus, Heleus, Mestor and Perses. Other sources deny that she was Perseus's daughter at all, naming Autochthe instead.

Pausanias tells that, after Perieres died, she married Oebalus, breaking the custom that obliged widows to stay unmarried. With Oebalus she had Tyndareus, Hippocoon and Icarius, while Aphareus and Leucippus were Perieres's sons. He adds that her tomb stood at Argos, beside that of the Gorgon.

Modern historians often read the story as a memory of changing customs, when widows were barred from remarrying and some were even sacrificed on funeral pyres, as in the myths of Evadne and Polyxena. This is an interpretation, not something the ancient texts state.

Legacy

Her descendants are central to Homeric epic and to Greece's legendary prehistory. Tyndareus was the father of Helen of Troy, Clytemnestra, Castor and Pollux, and Icarius was the father of Penelope, wife of Odysseus.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus' Library, Pausanias and the Catalogue of Women.

Trivia

A telling name. Her name means 'Gorgon slayer', a tribute to her father Perseus's killing of Medusa.
Another Gorgophone. One of the Danaids also bore the name Gorgophone. According to the story, she killed her husband Proteus on their wedding night.
