vampire-like creature from Filipino folklore

Manananggal

The manananggal is a creature from Philippine folklore, especially the Visayan regions, able to separate its upper torso from its lower body and fly at night in search of victims. Its fangs and bat-like wings give it a vampire-like look, and it belongs to the wider family of aswang beings.

The myth

In the tradition, the manananggal is usually pictured as a woman who, after dark, detaches her upper torso from her lower body and flies off on large bat-like wings, with her entrails dangling. Its name comes from the Tagalog word tanggal, meaning to remove or separate, so it can be read as the one who separates itself.

The most widespread tale says it prefers to hunt pregnant women while they sleep, using a long, proboscis-like tongue to draw out a fetus or blood. It also pursues newlyweds, lovers, newborn babies and, above all, grooms abandoned after the wedding. Its weak point is the lower half, which stays motionless on the ground: sprinkling salt, rubbing on crushed garlic, ash or fire keeps the torso from rejoining it, and the creature dies at sunrise. It is also said to shun garlic, salt, vinegar and light.

Legacy

The manananggal has appeared in Filipino cinema since 1927, when a silent film, considered the first Filipino horror film, was made. Since then it has starred in many films and series, such as Lipad, Darna, Lipad! and Shake, Rattle & Roll, and it appears in comics, animation, documentaries and a Marvel anime episode. It also features in the graphic novel Banzai Girl and in the documentary The Aswang Phenomenon.

Trivia

First Filipino horror film. The silent film Manananggal (1927), directed by José Nepomuceno, is considered the first Filipino horror film. Actress Mary Walter plays the creature with its upper torso detached.
Garlic, salt and vinegar. Besides garlic and salt, the creature was said to avoid vinegar, spices and stingray tails, which can be used as a whip.
