Arcadian prince, founder of Pallantion and teacher of Athena

Pallas

Pallas was an Arcadian prince, son of King Lycaon, to whom tradition attributes the founding of the town of Pallantion and the role of teacher of Athena. His figure links Arcadian myth with the origins of Troy and with the Roman legend of the Palatine hill.

The myth

According to the stories, Pallas was one of the fifty sons of the impious King Lycaon. Sources differ about his mother: she may have been the naiad Cyllene, Nonacris, or an unknown woman. He is considered the teacher of Athena, who according to local myths was born in Aliphera.

The tradition tells that Pallas and his brothers were especially wicked and careless. Zeus visited them disguised as a peasant to test them, and the brothers served the god a meal mixed with a child's entrails. Enraged, the king of the gods overturned the table and struck them down with a lightning bolt.

Pallas had a daughter, Chryse, who married Dardanus and brought the Palladium to Troy.

Legacy

In Roman times, authors used Pallas' name to explain the origin of the Palatine Hill. In Pallantium, in Pausanias' time, stone statues of Pallas and his grandson Evander were still standing.

Ancient sources: Pallas appears in accounts by Pausanias and in Roman literary traditions.
