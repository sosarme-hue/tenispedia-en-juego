mythical beings of Cantabria on St John's Eve

Caballucos del Diablu

The Caballucos del Diablu are seven damselfly-winged horses that fly over Cantabria on the night of St John's Eve. Folklore holds them to be the souls of sinners condemned to wander the region, and they are feared locally.

The myth

According to Cantabrian folk tradition, the caballucos are seven winged beings with long, transparent wings, each one a different colour: red, white, blue, black, yellow, green and orange. They always fly together, and the red one, the largest and sturdiest, resembling a Percheron, leads the raid. Some tellings say the Devil himself rides it, while lesser demons ride the others.

Each horse is the soul of a sinner: a moneylender, a miller who stole grain, a hermit who manipulated people, a corrupt judge, an innkeeper, a landowner who dishonoured young women, and a son who abused his parents. Doomed to roam Cantabria, they trample and burn the crops, and their hooves leave marks on stone. Their breath is as cold as the winter wind and their eyes glow like embers.

During the night they destroy the water flower and the four-leaf clover, which would bring love, happiness or four gifts. At dawn they retreat to caves, leaving a slime that hardens into gold bars; whoever takes it becomes rich, but their soul goes to hell after death.
