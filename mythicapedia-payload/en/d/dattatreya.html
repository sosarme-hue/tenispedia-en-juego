Hindu deity, combined avatar of the Trimurti

Dattatreya

Dattatreya is a Hindu deity and also a sage and master of yoga, regarded as a combined avatar of Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, the Trimurti. He is widely venerated in western India, especially in Maharashtra, Karnataka and Gujarat, and the Avadhuta Gita is attributed to him.

The myth

According to tradition, Dattatreya was born to the sage Atri and his wife Anasuya, to whom many hymns of the Rig Veda are credited. In the best-known story, the sage Narada praised Anasuya's devotion to her husband, and the wives of the gods, jealous, asked their husbands to test her. Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva visited Atri's home disguised as beggars and asked for food that Anasuya would serve only if naked. She treated them as children, turned them into infants and nursed them. Grateful, the gods granted her a wish: to be born as her sons. Thus were born Chandra, Dattatreya and Durvasa, though other traditions present Dattatreya as the union of the three gods in one being.

Accounts differ about his origin. The Mahabharata calls him a descendant of Atri's line rather than his direct son. After leaving home, he wandered naked and attained enlightenment near Ganagapur. According to the Tripura-rahasya, Parashurama found him meditating on Mount Gandhamadana. He taught that one can learn from everything, and he is credited with twenty-four teachers drawn from nature, such as earth, the sea, the bee and the spider.

Worship and religion

Dattatreya is venerated by Hindus of many currents, especially the Shaiva, Vaishnava and Shakta traditions of the Deccan. His festival, Datta Jayanti, is celebrated in the month of Margashirsha, in November or December. He is most often shown with three heads and six arms, accompanied by four dogs, representing the Vedas, and a cow, a symbol of the earth. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

His figure inspired monastic movements and the Nath tradition, which regarded him as Adi-Guru, the first teacher of its lineage. The devotional poet Tukaram praised his simple life and generosity. His twenty-four teachers have served as a model for artists and yogis who believe learning can come from any source.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, Magha's Shishupala vadha, the Brahma Purana, the Puranas and the Bhagavata Purana mention him with varying origins.

Trivia

The twenty-four lessons. According to the Puranas, Dattatreya listed twenty-four teachers, among them the bee, the spider and the moth, from whom he drew lessons on detachment and the mind.
Footprints in the rock. On the slopes of a lonely hill near Mount Girnar there are hollows shaped like feet, which local Hindus believe are the footprints of the god Datta.
