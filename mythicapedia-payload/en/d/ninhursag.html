Sumerian goddess of earth and motherhood

Ninhursag

Ninhursag is a Sumerian goddess of the earth, the mountains and motherhood, one of the great deities of Sumer. Her name means lady of the sacred mountain, and she bore many titles, among them Ninmah, Nintu and Belet-ili.

The myth

In Sumerian tradition, Ninhursag is the female personification of the earth, separated from the sky by Enlil. She is credited with creating hills and mountains and appears as the mother of many gods. Sources disagree about her family: some make her Enlil's sister, others his wife, and she is also described as a daughter of An and Nammu or of Kishar.

In the myth of Enki and Ninhursag, the god blesses the paradise land of Dilmun, but after he sleeps with his daughters, the goddess punishes him with eight illnesses. She then creates eight deities to heal them. In the myth of Enki and Ninmah, the two compete to create humans, and she shapes them from clay together with Nammu.

Worship and religion

Her worship is attested mainly in towns and villages, with shrines at Eridu and Kish. At Lagash, King Entemena built a temple first dedicated to her and later to Ninmah. She was venerated as a protector of mothers and mother of the world, and in the Neo-Sumerian period she became more closely linked to childbirth. Sumerian kings regarded themselves as nourished by her milk. Her cult does not survive today.

Legacy

In Babylon she was known as Belet-ili, and her prestige declined as that of Ishtar rose, though as Damkina, mother of Marduk, she kept a place in the pantheon. Some modern writers have linked her with Cybele or Astarte.

Ancient sources: Tablets of the Enki and Ninhursag myth (Ur III and Old Babylonian periods), the Atrahasis, the Enuma Elish, the Anzu epic and the Nippur god lists.

Trivia

A renamed goddess. According to the myth of Ninurta's exploits, her son changed her name from Ninmah to Ninhursag to commemorate her creation of the hills and mountains.
Titles that became goddesses. Epithets such as Dingirmah and Ninmah started as titles of Ninhursag and developed into separate goddesses at the end of the Early Dynastic period.
