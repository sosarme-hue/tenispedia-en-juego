Mesopotamian god of farming and patron of Dilbat

Urash

Urash was an agricultural god of ancient Mesopotamia and the protective deity of the city of Dilbat. He was often linked to Ninurta, and his figure shows how local gods fitted into the pantheon of the great cities.

The myth

Stories about Urash are rare and very fragmentary, mostly from late periods. According to the tradition, he was the husband of the goddess Ninegal and the father of Lagamal and Nanaya.

In a myth preserved on a single damaged tablet from Ur, Marduk creates plant life for Urash. In another late story, after Marduk rises to power, Urash receives a city as his domain.

Worship and religion

His main temple was E-ibbi-Anum, in Dilbat, and he was also worshiped in Babylon, Kish, Sippar, Larsa, Assur and Nippur. Kassite kings and Nebuchadnezzar II left inscriptions about that temple, and he was still worshiped in early Achaemenid times. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: He appears in the prologue to the Code of Hammurabi and in god lists such as An = Anum and those of Weidner and Nippur, as well as in the late commentary KAR 142.

Trivia

A temple named after Anu. The name of his main temple, E-ibbi-Anum, means «Anu has called it into being». Temple names that credit another god with creating the temple are unusual.
A wife under another name. In the god list An = Anum, his wife appears as Nin-uru, «lady of the city», instead of Ninegal.
