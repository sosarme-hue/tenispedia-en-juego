The Serpent of Pedro Bernardo

The Serpent of Pedro Bernardo is a popular legend from the village of Pedro Bernardo, in the Tiétar valley of Ávila, about a huge serpent that guarded a treasure. It belongs to the many Castilian tales of serpents and snakes that watch over caves and springs.

The myth

According to tradition, a huge serpent came down to drink from the Tiétar river and fed on the livestock grazing near the cave where it kept its treasure. It was so large that it could swallow a goat in a single bite.

In one version, it was killed when it was given something to swallow that set its insides on fire, and it died while trying to reach the river's water. The parish priest Juan González-Robles recorded the legend, already known at the time, in the parish book Becerro de la parroquia, and identified the slayer as Juan García Granado.

Legacy

More than thirty years ago the local play La Sierpe premiered, in which grandparents tell their granddaughter a story about a serpent, cattle, a misunderstanding and a very beautiful sister, with a happy ending. The legend has also been told to schoolchildren using the Japanese kamishibai technique, and it gives its name to a cycling race. In the village, the Risco de la Sierpe appears in the tale of how Pedro Bernardo came to be.

Trivia

A baptism from 1641. A researcher found the baptism record of Juan García Granado, the villager identified as the serpent's slayer, dated 1641.
A place named after the serpent. Pedro Bernardo has a site called Risco de la Sierpe, which the villagers connect with the legend.
