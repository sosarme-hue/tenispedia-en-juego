Arcadian nymph, nurse of Zeus

Myrtoessa

Myrtoessa was an Arcadian nymph, of the Crenaean people according to the tradition, and one of the nurses said to have raised the god Zeus. Her name appears alongside those of four other nymphs in Arcadian accounts.

The myth

According to the tradition, Myrtoessa was one of the nymphs who looked after Zeus in his infancy, together with Neda, Anthracia, Hagno and Anchirhoe. Her story is set in Arcadia, the mountainous region of the Peloponnese where she was linked to the Crenaeans.

The reference material lists her as a daughter of the river Alpheus, but the sources do not develop further episodes of her life or describe her end, so her story rests mainly on this role as a nurse.

Worship and religion

She was depicted carrying water-pots, which were thought to hold the water that flowed from her. She appears on a table in a sanctuary of Demeter and Persephone at Megalopolis, alongside Anchirhoe. There is no evidence of a cult that survives today.

Legacy

A non-marine mollusc, Myrtoessa hyas, a mud snail of the family Hydrobiidae, is named after the nymph.

Trivia

A snail named after her. A freshwater mollusc, Myrtoessa hyas, belongs to the mud snail family and was named in honour of the nymph.
Water-pots at Megalopolis. At a sanctuary of Demeter and Persephone in Megalopolis she is shown alongside Anchirhoe, carrying water-pots.
