Nana Buluku

Nana Buluku is a supreme deity of the Fon and Dahomey peoples of West Africa, also honored by the Yoruba and in Afro-Brazilian religions. She is regarded as the primordial mother from whom the universe and the other deities arise, and her figure links creation with death and rebirth.

The myth

In Fon tradition, Nana Buluku is the supreme mother creator. She gave birth to the twins Mawu and Lisa, the moon and the sun, who completed the work of creation. After that birth she withdrew and left the world in her children's hands, though some believers picture her as both male and female.

In Yoruba tradition she is placed at the meeting point of the stagnant waters and the firm earth, the mud where she appears. Because her worship predates many other orishas, accounts vary by people: for the Iyesá she is a form of Obatalá, while for others she is an independent deity.

Nana receives the souls of the dead, cradles them like soft earth and prepares them for a new birth, though Obatalá decides the essence that will animate the new body.

Worship and religion

Her worship spread from the kingdom of Dahomey into Yoruba lands and later to Brazil and Cuba. Offerings of water are poured onto the earth, and her rites avoid metal cutting tools, since they are associated with times before iron. The baobab is her sacred tree. In Candomblé she is affectionately called Avó, grandmother, and in the Arará rite in Cuba it is said she does not feed on animal blood. Her worship remains alive in Brazil, Cuba and West Africa.
