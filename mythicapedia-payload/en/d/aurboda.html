giantess of Norse mythology

Aurboða

Aurboða is a giantess (jötunn) in Norse mythology, married to the giant Gymir and mother of the giantess Gerðr. She matters mainly for the family link she provides to the world of giants, although the ancient texts devote little space to her.

The myth

The sources present her mainly as the mother of Gerðr. Both the Gylfaginning, in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and the poem Hyndluljóð portray her in this role, and Gerðr is an important figure in the myths about the gods.

The Poetic Edda, a compilation of earlier traditions, also mentions her, though briefly. In the poem Fjölsvinnsmál, part of the Svipdagsmál cycle, she is one of the nine maidens sitting at the knees of Menglöð during her captivity. There she is shown as a girl rather than a giantess, a detail that has led scholars to debate its meaning.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Poetic Edda, in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (in the Gylfaginning), in the poem Hyndluljóð and in Fjölsvinnsmál, within the Svipdagsmál.

Trivia

A disputed name. The meaning of her name is unclear. Many scholars link it to Old Norse words for 'gravel' or 'mud' and translate it as 'gravel-offerer', though another theory proposes 'gold-bidder'.
A giantess shown as a maiden. In Fjölsvinnsmál she appears as one of Menglöð's nine maidens, portrayed as a girl rather than a giantess, which contrasts with her usual role in other texts.
