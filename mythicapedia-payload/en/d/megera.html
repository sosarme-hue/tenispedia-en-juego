Megaera

Megaera is one of the Erinyes, the avenging goddesses of Greek mythology, known in Rome as the Furies. She embodies strife, hatred and the resentment that drive people and cities toward violence and ruin.

The myth

According to Hesiod, the Erinyes were born from the blood that fell when Cronus castrated his father Uranus, with Gaia as their mother. Later sources instead make them daughters of Acheron and Nyx. Megaera belongs to a trio with her sisters Alecto and Tisiphone, though some authors also speak of the Meliae, born from the same blood.

Her name has several explanations. Tzetzes ties it to being full of jealousy and spite toward wrongdoers, while other readings link it to strife and hatred, or to the jealous one. In the tradition she drives heroes and mortals into discord, murder and war, among them Heracles, Agamemnon and Oedipus. Claudian describes her rising from her seat of sorrow to bring frenzy, blood and destruction. Virgil places her in Tartarus, unlike her sisters, whom he puts in the heavens.

Legacy

In the nineteenth century her name came to denote a jealous or spiteful woman in several European languages, including French, Portuguese, Italian and Russian. The asteroid 464 Megaira is named in her honour.

Ancient sources: Hesiod traces her origin to the blood of Uranus, while Virgil and Claudian describe her in Tartarus and in her actions among mortals.
