sea spirit of Japanese folklore

Umibōzu

The umibōzu is a sea spirit from Japanese folklore, usually described as a giant, bald, round-headed figure that sinks ships when the sea suddenly turns rough. Its name combines the characters for “sea” and “Buddhist monk”, a nod to its appearance.

The myth

According to tradition, the umibōzu appears to sailors when a calm sea suddenly surges. It rises with its shaven head and upper body, and can capsize or destroy a vessel. Many tales say it asks the crew for a ladle to scoop water; if they hand over one with a bottom, it will fill the boat and sink it. The only safe course is to give it a bottomless ladle and sail away while it is confused. Some say smoke drives it off.

It is often thought to be the ghost of a drowned priest, an idea found in Chiba, where a story tells of a priest from Chōshi who became an umibōzu after drowning at sea. In Tohoku, anyone who failed to offer the first catch of the season to the sea god risked having the creature wreck their boat and carry off its owner.

Worship and religion

There was no organised cult of the umibōzu; it was a danger to be avoided through offerings and precautions. In Tohoku, presenting the first catch to the sea god served to appease it. Fishermen in the Gotō Islands had their own rules, such as the belief that it never climbed aboard from the stern, only from the prow.

Legacy

The umibōzu is well known in modern Japanese popular culture. It appears in Shigeru Mizuki’s manga Gegege no Kitarō and as a final boss in the video game Nioh. In Naruto, Amachi summons one, and in One Piece a giant shadow in the fog is identified with it. Ghost in the Shell: Stand Alone Complex uses its name for a special forces unit, and Pathfinder Bestiary 3 recasts it as the “Sea Monk”.

Ancient sources: Edo period literature records local tales of the umibōzu, such as the story of the Chōshi priest, but the sources do not cite specific ancient texts devoted to it.

Trivia

A ladle with a hole. In many versions, the key to the tale is a simple wooden ladle. Punching out its bottom is enough to save the ship.
A monster said to bring long life. In Uwajima, tradition held that anyone who saw an umibōzu would live a long life, though elsewhere it was a deadly threat.
