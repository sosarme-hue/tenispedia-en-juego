Mahāyāna bodhisattva who removes obstacles

Sarvanivāraṇaviṣkambhin

Sarvanivāraṇaviṣkambhin is a bodhisattva revered in Mahāyāna Buddhism and one of the Eight Great Bodhisattvas. His name means "he who blocks all obstacles", and he is invoked to clear away the hindrances that stand in the way of a successful meditation.

The myth

Texts place him among the eight great mahāsattvas, alongside Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara, Mahāsthāmaprāpta, Ākāśagarbha, Kṣitigarbha and Maitreya. In the Lotus Sutra he pays homage to Avalokiteśvara, and he is the main interlocutor in the Ratnamegha and Tathāgata-guṇa-jñānācintya-viṣayāvatāra-nirdeśa sutras.

In Tantric Buddhism (Vajrayāna) he is usually placed in the lineage of the Buddha Amoghasiddhi, one of the five Dhyani Buddhas, and more rarely in that of Akshobhya. Some have considered him a form of Vajrapāṇi, though this is probably a confusion with the mahāsattva Mahāsthāmāprāpta, of whom Vajrapāṇi is one of the incarnations.

Worship and religion

He is known mostly to meditators, but he does not receive the individual devotion given to the other seven great bodhisattvas. In iconography he often holds a flower in one hand, sometimes alongside a wish-fulfilling gem (cintāmaṇi). His mantra is recited to remove obstacles and disturbances.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in the Lotus Sutra and the Mahavairocana Tantra, the Vairocanābhisaṃbodhi Sūtra gives his mantra, and the Dharanisamgraha collects his spell.

Trivia

Irregular spelling. His name is most often written in an irregular form, nīvaraṇa.
Five hindrances. In Theravada texts, nivāraṇa refers to the five mental hindrances: desire, hostility, sloth and torpor, restlessness and worry, and doubt.
