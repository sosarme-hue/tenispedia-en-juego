horse of the god Heimdallr in Norse mythology

Gulltoppr

Gulltoppr is, in Norse mythology, the horse that belongs to the god Heimdallr. His name means "golden mane" in Old Norse, and his main interest lies in what he reveals about how the world of the gods was organized in medieval sources.

The myth

Older texts preserve Gulltoppr mainly in lists. He appears among the horses named in Grímnismál, a poem of the Poetic Edda, and in the Nafnaþulur section of the Prose Edda, which gathers names of horses and other beings.

In the Prose Edda, specifically in Gylfaginning, Snorri Sturluson presents him as the horse of Heimdallr. Rudolf Simek theorizes that Snorri assigned a horse to Heimdall in an attempt to systematize the mythology, so this detail may reflect Snorri's own way of organizing the material rather than a shared, older tradition.

Ancient sources: Poetic Edda (Grímnismál) and Prose Edda (Nafnaþulur and Gylfaginning).
