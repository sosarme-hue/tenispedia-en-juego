chthonic demon of Etruscan mythology

Tuchulcha

Tuchulcha is a chthonic demon of Etruscan mythology who dwells in the underworld known as Aita. Its monstrous look, with snakes for hair and bird-like wings, makes it one of the most recognisable infernal figures in Etruscan art.

The myth

Según la tradición iconográfica is not used here; instead, the depiction tells us that Tuchulcha has a human form, but with a vulture-like beak, donkey-like pointed ears, snakes for hair and large wings. Its dominant colour is yellowish. Its only known depiction is a wall painting in the Tomb of the Orcus II, in Tarquinia.

In that painting, the creature appears during the visit of Theseus (Etruscan Ces or These) to the Etruscan underworld. While Theseus and his friend Peirithous play a board game, the monster attends the scene. Its gender is debated: many scholars consider it male because of its animal facial hair, but Nancy de Grummond argues it is probably female, judging by its dress, pale pinkish skin and apparent breasts.

Legacy

Tuchulcha inspired the serial killer's murders in the 1972 film The Dead Are Alive, directed by Armando Crispino, in which a fresco of this figure serves as the model for the crimes.

Trivia

A disputed figure. Although many regard it as male, some specialists argue that Tuchulcha is female or genderless, based on its dress and skin.
A game in the underworld. In its only known depiction, the monster watches Theseus and Peirithous playing a board game in the underworld.
