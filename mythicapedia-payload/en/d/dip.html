demonic hound of Catalan myth

Dip

The dip is an evil, black hellhound from Catalan myth, an emissary of the Devil who sucks people's blood. The creature remains very much alive in Pratdip (Tarragona), where it appears on the village coat of arms.

The myth

According to legend, dips came out only at night and sucked the blood of cattle and of drunken night owls returning from the village taverns. They were described as lame in one leg, and evil eyes could be seen in the shadows.

Scholars suppose the tale was meant to frighten drinkers and keep them from wandering at night, although no documented witnesses confirm that it was told in this way.

Legacy

Joan Perucho drew on the figure for his novel The Natural Stories (1960), set during the Carlist wars, in which the vampire Onofre de Dip can transform himself into many animals. At the entrance to Pratdip stands a monument to the dip.

Trivia

Origin of the name. Pratdip means meadow of the dips, and tradition links the village name to these dogs. They are said to have disappeared in the nineteenth century.
A novel vampire. The figure became popular mainly through the vampire fiction of Joan Perucho, who turned the dip into a vampire able to take the shape of animals.
