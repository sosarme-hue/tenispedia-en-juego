Egyptian crocodile and falcon god of Athribis

Khenti-kheti

Khenti-kheti is an Egyptian solar god, first shown as a crocodile and patron of the city of Athribis in the western Nile Delta. Later he was merged with Horus, becoming Horus Khenti-kheti.

The myth

In the tradition, Khenti-kheti embodied the young sun driving away the creatures of darkness. His name means «the one who retreats furthest». Some legends say he could take the form of a giant black bull called Kemwer. His consort was Juit.

In the New Kingdom he was linked to Horus and shown as a creator falcon. He was then also called Horas or Hor-Khenti-Khati, «Horus in the womb». In local tradition he was eventually replaced by Osiris, known as «Osiris who resides in Athribis».

Worship and religion

He was worshipped mainly at Athribis, where he was lord of the city. He is also called «Lord of Athribis» in inscriptions at the temple of Amun-Ra at Karnak, and an inscription at the base of one of Ramesses II's obelisks, now in Cairo, mentions him.

Trivia

A giant black bull. According to one legend, Khenti-kheti could take the form of a giant black bull called Kemwer.
An obelisk in Cairo. An inscription at the base of one of the two obelisks raised by Ramesses II and finished by his son Merneptah names Horus Khenti-kheti. The obelisk now stands in Cairo.
