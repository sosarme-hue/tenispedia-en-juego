Liparus

Liparus is the king who gave his name to the island and city of Lipari, in the Aeolian islands. In Greek tradition he was a son of King Auson, who reached the island after being driven out of Italy by his brothers.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus, Liparus was the son of Auson. His brothers rose against him, so he fled Italy with warships and soldiers toward an uninhabited island, which was named after him. There he founded the city of Lipari and brought the nearby islands under cultivation.

In his old age, Aiolos, son of Hippotes, arrived on the island and married Liparus' daughter Cyane. Aiolos shared rule with his men and the local people and became king of the island. Liparus, longing for Italy, received his help in taking the Sorrento area, where he reigned with great renown until his death. After splendid funeral rites, the inhabitants honoured him as a hero. The myth adds that Odysseus visited this Aiolos during his wanderings.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, after his death the people of Sorrento paid him honours as a hero.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus, in his Library of History, is the main source that records his story.
