Sardus

Sardus is an eponymous hero of classical mythology, from whom the Lydian city of Sardes and the island of Sardinia are said to take their names. He matters because ancient accounts use him to explain the mythical origin of the island's name.

The myth

Classical authors offer differing versions. In one, Sardus was a woman, the wife of Tyrrhenus, who gave her name first to Sardes and then to Sardinia; in another, she was the daughter of Sthenelus and eponym only of Sardes. The most widespread version makes Sardus a man and the son of Makeris, identified with Melqart, whom Egyptians and Libyans called Heracles.

Sardus led the Libyans who sailed to the island of Ichnusa and renamed it after him. According to Sallust, he was the son of Heracles. The newcomers did not drive out the native inhabitants; they were welcomed, more out of necessity than goodwill. Neither group built cities, and both lived scattered in huts and caves.

Legacy

His name lives on in that of the island of Sardinia, which the myths link to him.

Ancient sources: Sallust, Solinus and Pausanias, who in the second century CE confirms Sallust's account and names Sardus's father.

Trivia

Melqart behind the name. Makeris, Sardus's father, is identified with Melqart, the deity that Egyptians and Libyans associated with Heracles.
Another name. He is also called Sid Addir and Sardus Pater, meaning 'Sardinian Father'.
