Greek hero who slew Medusa

Perseus

Perseus is a Greek demigod, son of Zeus and the mortal Danaë, famous for beheading the Gorgon Medusa and for rescuing Andromeda from a sea monster. Tradition also credits him with founding Mycenae, and he was later linked to the constellation that bears his name.

The myth

According to the myth, King Acrisius of Argos, fearing an oracle that foretold he would die at the hands of his grandson, locked his daughter Danaë away so she could bear no children. Zeus visited her as a shower of gold, and Perseus was born. Acrisius, unconvinced of the divine origin, cast mother and child into the sea in a wooden chest. Poseidon calmed the waters, and they reached the island of Seriphos, where the fisherman Dictys took them in and raised the boy.

As a young man, Perseus protected his mother from King Polydectes, who wanted to be rid of him. The king asked for the head of Medusa, one of the three Gorgons, whose gaze turned people to stone. With help from Athena and Hermes, Perseus obtained the eye of the Graeae, who revealed where the nymphs lived; they gave him winged sandals, a pouch and the helm of Hades. Athena lent him her shield so he could see Medusa's reflection without looking at her directly, and he beheaded her. From her neck sprang Pegasus and Chrysaor. On his journey Perseus turned Atlas to stone when he refused him hospitality.

In Ethiopia he found Andromeda chained to a rock as an offering to a sea monster sent by Poseidon to punish her mother, Queen Cassiopeia. Perseus freed her and married her, though he had to face Phineus, her former betrothed, whom he turned to stone with Medusa's head. Back in Seriphos, he punished Polydectes by showing him the head, and left the kingdom to Dictys.

The prophecy came true years later: at athletic games in Larisa, Perseus threw a discus that struck Acrisius and killed him. Perseus then exchanged the kingdom of Argos for that of Tiryns. In one version, he founded Mycenae after drinking from a spring that welled up when he pulled up a mushroom, mýkēs in Greek, the origin of the city's name.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the constellation Perseus. In tradition he is regarded as the ancestor of the Perseid dynasty, which ruled at Tiryns through his son Perses.

Ancient sources: Pseudo-Apollodorus, Pausanias and Ovid in his Metamorphoses record different versions of his story.

Trivia

A name with several explanations. Some etymologists link his name to the Greek verb pérthein, 'to sack', which would make him 'the sacker of cities'.
An ancestor of the Persians. Herodotus tells how the Persians took their name from Perses, a son of Perseus and Andromeda.
