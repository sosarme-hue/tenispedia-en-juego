Etruscan god of the underworld and prophecy

Lur

Lur is an Etruscan deity linked to the underworld, prophecy, and both oracular and martial functions. He is known mainly from inscriptions and a few images, so his exact role is still debated.

The myth

Very little is known about Lur. His name appears in forms such as Lurs, Luridus or Lurmitla, and he is linked to Laran, which some scholars see as the source of his name. Inscriptions associate him with darkness and the underworld, and one describes him as "Lurs, of Laran."

He appears among underworld deities on the Lead Plaque of Magliano and in the Liber Linteus, the longest surviving Etruscan text, where he is named in a ritual alongside Tinia. On a bronze mirror from Vulci, Tinia points to him as a prophetic figure, while a head emerging from the clouds seems to deliver a message to him.

Worship and religion

The many candelabra inscribed with Lur's name suggest they were votive offerings. Traces of his cult have been found at sanctuaries such as Cetamura and San Giovenale, and in small altars and shrines at Gravisca, Vulci and Perugia.

Ancient sources: The Liber Linteus, the longest surviving Etruscan text, names him in a ritual alongside Tinia, and the Lead Plaque of Magliano lists his name among underworld deities.

Trivia

An inscribed vase. A fifth-century BCE vase found near a sanctuary at San Giovenale bears the inscription "I am Lurs, of Laran."
A mirror from Vulci. A bronze mirror from Vulci shows Lur with Tinia and an unidentified young man, a scene that suggests his prophetic character.
