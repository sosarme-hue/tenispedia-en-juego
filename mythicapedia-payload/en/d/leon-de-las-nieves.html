celestial animal of Tibetan culture

Snow Lion

The Snow Lion is a celestial animal of Tibetan culture and the emblem of Tibet. It represents the snowy mountain ranges of the country and, in Buddhism, joy, courage and the teachings of the Buddha.

The myth

In Tibetan tradition the Snow Lion lives on the highest peaks, as the king of beasts, and leaps from summit to summit without ever touching the ground. Its roar is so powerful that a single one could make seven dragons fall from the sky.

According to the tradition, the culture heroes Gesar and Milarepa were raised on the milk of a snow lioness, which is believed to have healing properties. Legend says this milk comes from her paws and passes into hollow balls she plays with. Milarepa used it as a symbol of the Dharma: a liquid so precious should not be poured into an ordinary vessel.

Worship and religion

Tibetan peoples mention the lion in folk songs and proverbs, and associate it with hermits and yogis who live high in the mountains. In Buddhist art it holds up the Buddha's throne, one on each side, and sometimes eight lions appear representing the eight main bodhisattvas. The snow lion dance, Senggeh Garcham, may be secular or ritual, and is sometimes performed by Bon monks.

Legacy

It was the national emblem of Tibet from 1909 to 1959, appearing on coins, stamps, banknotes and the national flag. The flag with two lions, introduced in 1912, is still used by the Tibetan Government in Exile. Its resemblance has also given its name to the Tibetan Lion Dog, a label applied to breeds such as the Lhasa Apso.

Trivia

Wheels of joy. The hollow balls the lioness plays with can be shown in Tibetan art as three-coloured wheels of joy (dga' 'khyil).
One roar, seven dragons. According to the tradition, a single roar from the Snow Lion would be enough to make seven dragons fall from the sky.
