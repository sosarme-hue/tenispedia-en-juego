epithet of the goddess Themis in ancient Greece

Ichnaea

Ichnaea is an epithet applied to the goddess Themis and, in some sources, to Nemesis, both tied to justice in ancient Greece. The name, Ichnaía, means "she who tracks", alluding to the pursuit of those who break the law.

The myth

Ancient texts mention her mainly at the birth of Apollo. The Homeric Hymn to Delian Apollo tells how, after nine days of Leto's labour pains on Delos, the great goddesses of the old order gathered on the island, among them Dione, Rhea, Themis, Ichnaea and Amphitrite, while Hera stayed behind in the palace of Zeus.

A Hellenistic source presents her as an independent figure and a daughter of Helios. Lycophron, in his Alexandra, calls her the Sun's daughter when describing the justice that belongs to her lineage.

Worship and religion

Strabo says that the Ichnaean Themis was worshipped in the town of Ichnae, in Thessaly, within the district of Thessaliotis. The town's name may have been derived from the goddess's epithet.

Legacy

Her name survives mainly in the place name of the ancient town of Ichnae.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Delian Apollo, Strabo's Geographica and Lycophron's Alexandra.

Trivia

Witness to a birth. In the Homeric hymn, the goddesses come to Delos to bear witness that Apollo's birth is lawful.
A name that gave rise to a city. According to Strabo, the name of the town of Ichnae comes from the epithet of the goddess worshipped there.
