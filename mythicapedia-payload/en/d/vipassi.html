ancient Buddha, one of the twenty-eight Buddhas

Vipassī

Vipassī is the twenty-second of the twenty-eight Buddhas of the past described in the Buddhavaṃsa, within the Theravada Buddhist tradition. He matters because he forms part of the line of Buddhas that, in this tradition, preceded Gautama Buddha.

The myth

According to the Buddhavaṃsa and Buddhist legend, Vipassī lived ninety-one kalpas, many billions of years, before the present, when human life lasted 84,000 years. He was born in Bandhumatī, in Khema Park, to the warrior-chief Bandhumā and Bandhumatī. He married Sutanu, with whom he had a son, Samavattakkhandha, and he lived as a householder for 8,000 years in the palaces of Nanda, Sunanda and Sirimā.

When he renounced worldly life, he rode out of the palace in a chariot. After eight months of asceticism he attained enlightenment under a pāṭalī tree. Just before awakening he accepted a bowl of milk rice from the daughter of Sudassana-setthi, and grass for his seat from a guard named Sujâta. He died in Sumitta Park at an age the sources give as either 80,000 or 100,000 years. His first sermon, in the Khamamigadâya, reached 6,800,000 disciples, and his foremost disciples included Khanda and Tissa, and Candâ and Candamittâ.

Worship and religion

His relics were kept in a stupa seven yojanas high. Mendaki, then called Avaroja, built the Gandhakuti, a scented pavilion, for him. The texts say he held the uposatha once every seven years and that his sangha kept the discipline perfectly. As one of the Buddhas of the past, he is honoured within Theravada Buddhism, although the sources do not describe modern rituals devoted specifically to him.

Ancient sources: The Buddhavaṃsa, fourteenth book of the Khuddaka Nikāya, within the Sutta Piṭaka of the Pāli Canon.

Trivia

The meaning of his name. Vipassī comes from vi (well) and passī (saw), meaning roughly one who has seen clearly. The word belongs to the same family as vipassanā, or contemplation.
A human lifespan of 84,000 years. According to the Buddhavaṃsa, human life in Vipassī's time lasted 84,000 years, far longer than in our own era.
A vast first sermon. His first sermon, in the Khamamigadâya, is said to have gathered 6,800,000 disciples.
