mischievous one-legged trickster of Brazilian folklore

Saci

Saci is a character from Tupi and Guarani folklore, best known in Brazil, where his legend took its present shape. He is a one-legged boy who smokes a pipe and wears a magic red cap that lets him vanish and reappear at will.

The myth

According to popular tales, Saci is an annoying prankster who causes small disasters at home and on the farm: he hides toys, dulls needles, spills the salt or tangles the threads. He can turn into the Matitaperê bird, whose melancholy call seems to come from nowhere. He is said to dance inside dust devils. Whoever traps him can win wishes in exchange for his freedom, by shutting him in a dark glass bottle sealed with a cross-marked cork, or by stealing his cap, the source of his power.

As for his origins, some link him to the Tupi-Guarani Yaçi-Yaterê, a red-haired child tied to the moon. African slaves then reshaped the figure: he became black, his hair turned into a cap, and he took up a pipe. Christian traits, such as fleeing from a cross, were added later.

Legacy

The writer Monteiro Lobato popularised him in his 1921 children's book, where Pedrinho captures him. He is also the traditional mascot of Porto Alegre's Sport Club Internacional, thanks to his red cap.

Trivia

A lunar name. According to the sources, Yaçi means moon in old Tupi, which explains the nocturnal character of the original being.
A storybook character. Much of the Saci's present image comes from what Monteiro Lobato collected in 1917-1918 and from his 1921 children's book.
