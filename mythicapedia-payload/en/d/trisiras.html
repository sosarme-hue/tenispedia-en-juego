three-headed son of Tvashtri in Hindu myth

Trishiras

Trishiras, also called Vishvarupa, is the three-headed son of the craftsman god Tvashtri in Hindu myth. His story centres on his clash with Indra, king of the devas, and the vengeance that followed.

The myth

According to the Taittiriya Samhita and the Brihaddevata, Vishvarupa is the son of Tvashtri and an asura wife. In the Mahabharata and the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Tvashtri has him with Virochana, daughter of Prahlada, in order to dethrone Indra. His three heads drink soma, drink liquor and eat, watch the surroundings, and recite the Vedas. As his power grows, Indra sends apsaras to seduce him; he scorns them, but Indra ends up killing him with his thunderbolt.

Fearing he might revive, Indra has a carpenter cut off his heads, from which three different birds emerge. Furious, Tvashtri creates the demon Vritra to take revenge. In other versions, Vishvarupa is a priest of the devas, and Indra beheads him out of suspicion that he sides with the asuras, thereby committing brahmanahatya, the sin of killing a Brahmin.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda, the Taittiriya Samhita, the Mahabharata, the Bhagavata Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana mention the story or Vishvarupa.

Trivia

Names of the heads. According to the Mahabharata, each head had its own name: Somapītha, Surāpītha and Annāda, according to what it drank or ate.
Birds born from his heads. In the epic and Puranic version, a different kind of bird emerged from each severed head: kapiñjala, kalapiṅga and tittiri.
