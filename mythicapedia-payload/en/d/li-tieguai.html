Chinese Taoist god of medicine and one of the Eight Immortals

Li Tieguai

Li Tieguai, the "Iron-Crutch Li", is one of the Eight Immortals of the Taoist pantheon and, according to tradition, the oldest of the group. He is linked to medicine and to the protection of the poor and the sick, which makes him one of the most loved figures in Chinese folklore.

The myth

According to the myth, Li practised Taoist magical arts and became a disciple of Laozi. Laozi tested him with a wooden woman and with money, and Li refused both. As a reward he received a pill that kept him free from hunger and illness, and another that let him fly at great speed. When his spirit travelled to heaven to meet other immortals, his disciple Li Qing, who had to tend to his sick mother, cremated the body after waiting six and a half days.

On his return, Li's spirit found only the corpse of a beggar who had starved to death, a lame man with a repellent appearance, and entered it. Laozi gave him a gourd of medicine that never emptied and turned his staff into an iron crutch. With the liquid from the gourd he brought the disciple's mother back to life. From then on he wandered the world curing the sick and defending the oppressed, until he ascended to the land of the immortals. Sources differ on his era: some place him in the Zhou dynasty, others in the Yuan.

Worship and religion

Li is associated with medicine. His iron crutch still hangs outside some traditional apothecaries, and the gourd is a sign highly valued by professional exorcists. Some non-devout people seek prescriptions through Taoist priests. He is not among the most popular immortals, partly because of his reputation for a bad temper and eccentricity.

Legacy

In the television series Jackie Chan Adventures he appears as the Immortal who sealed away Bai Tza, the Water Demon. His iron crutch has also become a symbol of traditional Chinese medicine.

Trivia

A soul in the form of vapour. A cloud of vapour rises from his gourd, and inside it is his hun, the soul of the sage. It may be drawn as a shapeless form or as a miniature version of his own body.
A beggar's disguise. In his iconography he descends to earth disguised as a beggar to fight for the oppressed and heal others, which explains his almost clownish image.
