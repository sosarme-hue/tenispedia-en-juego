Slavic god of Rügen with seven faces

Rugiaevit

Rugiaevit, also written Rugievit or Ruyevit, is a god of the Slavic Rani people, worshipped on the island of Rügen in the Baltic Sea. He is mentioned in only two written sources, the Gesta Danorum and the Knýtlinga saga, so many details of his cult and appearance remain uncertain. He is remembered above all for his many faces and his weapons, making him one of the most distinctive figures among the Baltic Slavs.

The myth

The texts preserve no story of his origin or deeds, so what we know comes almost entirely from descriptions of his statue. According to the tradition recorded in these two sources, he had seven faces, seven swords at his belt and an eighth in his hand. Beneath his lips lay a nest of swallows, a strange detail the authors do not explain.

The statue, with its many faces and weapons, was the most visible form of his presence. He was associated above all with war, but also with sexuality, a double character that paints a deity both martial and sensual.

Worship and religion

His temple stood in the stronghold of Charenza, probably today's Garz, alongside those of Porevit and Porenut. From the rulers' point of view, his cult was 'private', and it competed with the 'public' and theocratic cult of Svetovit at Arkona. That contrast suggests the island's religion had different centres of loyalty depending on who led it.

Ancient sources: The Gesta Danorum by Saxo Grammaticus and the Knýtlinga saga, which describe his statue and his temple.

Trivia

Seven faces and eight swords. His statue had seven heads and seven swords at the belt, plus an eighth sword in its hand.
A nest at the lips. Beneath his lips was a nest of swallows, a detail the sources describe without explaining it.
