mythical king of Argos, son of Melampus

Antiphates

Antiphates is a mythical king of Argos from the line of the Melampodids, descendants of Melampus. He is a minor figure in Greek myth, yet he links Melampus to the hero Amphiaraus.

The myth

According to the Odyssey, Melampus settled in Argos, married, and had two sturdy sons, Antiphates and Mantius. Antiphates fathered Oicles, who in turn begot Amphiaraus. Homer does not name Antiphates' wife.

Diodorus Siculus gives a more detailed version: Melampus married Iphianira, daughter of Megapenthes, and had Antiphates and Bias, as well as the daughters Manto and Pronoe. Antiphates, married to Zeuxippe, daughter of Hipponous, had Oicles and Amphalces. According to Diodorus, Melampus, Bias and their descendants shared the kingdom of Argos, and after Melampus' death Oicles took the throne. Pausanias, however, replaces Antiphates with Mantius as Oicles' father.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Odyssey, in Pherecydes, in Diodorus Siculus' Bibliotheca historica and in Pausanias' Description of Greece.

Trivia

A telling name. His Greek name means "spokesman" or "mouthpiece", a hint at the role the tradition gives him.
Conflicting genealogies. Homer and Diodorus make him the father of Oicles, while Pausanias replaces him with his brother Mantius, showing the variations among ancient authors.
