hero of Baltic myth

Sovij

Sovij is a figure in Baltic myth who, according to tradition, introduced the cremation of the dead and guided their souls to the underworld. His story survives in a Byzantine chronicle known through a Russian translation.

The myth

Sovij hunted a boar and gave nine of its spleens to his children to cook, but they ate them instead. Enraged, he went down to hell; after passing through eight gates, he needed one of his sons to help him through the ninth.

That son promised to bury him. He laid him first in the ground and the next day in a tree, but Sovij was dissatisfied with both until he was burned, likening his rest to that of a toddler in a cradle. According to the account, he also led souls to worship the gods Andajus, Perkūnas, Žvorūnė and Teliavelis.

Worship and religion

The myth served to justify cremation and fire sacrifices offered to the Baltic gods. The sources do not describe temples or festivals, and there is no evidence that this cult survives today.

Ancient sources: The Chronography of John Malalas, in its Russian translation, which records it as the first recorded Baltic myth.

Trivia

A disputed name. The origin of the name Sovij is debated. Some consider it a borrowing from Arabic or the name of a Lithuanian tribe, but most etymologists link it to an Indo-European root meaning sun.
A boar with several readings. Some researchers see the hunted boar as a symbol of the sun or of the end of an older system of worship, leaving the myth open to several interpretations.
