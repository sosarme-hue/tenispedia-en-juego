legendary king of Athens

Menestheus

In Greek mythology, Menestheus was a legendary king of Athens and a member of the Erechtheids, the line descended from one of the city's early kings. His story links the Trojan War with the absence of Theseus and explains how the Athenian throne changed hands for a time.

The myth

Son of Peteus, grandson of Orneus and great-grandson of Erechtheus, Menestheus had as his mother either Polyxene or Mnesimache, according to different authors. While Theseus was in the Underworld, the twins Castor and Pollux, brothers of Helen, seized Athens and set Menestheus on the throne after attacking Aphidna, where Helen was held. Menestheus had been one of her suitors. When Theseus returned, he exiled Menestheus from the city, although Pausanias notes that Menestheus had tried to win over the people.

In the Iliad, Menestheus leads the Athenians at Troy with forty ships sailing from Phaleron, and he is praised for arranging chariots and warriors in battle order, a skill only Nestor matched. Herodotus calls him the best man to lead troops, though other accounts describe him as not valiant. He was among the warriors hidden inside the Trojan Horse. After the sack of Troy, he sailed to Melos and became king there.

Another tradition holds that while he was away at war, his throne was usurped. He wandered aimlessly until he reached the mouth of the river Crisus, today the Guadalete, where he founded a colony he named the Port of Menestheus, probably on the site of modern El Puerto de Santa María. According to Eusebius, he reigned for twenty-three years, and at his death the throne returned to Theseus' family.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes a bronze statue at the sanctuary of Artemis Brauronia on the Athenian Acropolis, showing a wooden-style horse with Menestheus, Teucer and Theseus' sons emerging from it. The sources record no cult of his own.

Legacy

The Jovian asteroid 4068 Menestheus is named after him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, Herodotus, Pausanias and Eusebius of Caesarea, who gives him a twenty-three-year reign.

Trivia

A reluctant warrior. In the Iliad, Agamemnon finds him in the rear ranks, apparently avoiding combat, and in another episode Menestheus trembles before Sarpedon and calls on Ajax and Teucer for help.
An asteroid named for him. The asteroid 4068 Menestheus, a Jovian asteroid, keeps his name alive in modern astronomy.
