minor Roman goddess of joy

Laetitia

Laetitia is a minor goddess of ancient Rome, the personification of happiness and celebration. Her name comes from laetus, "happy", and she appears mostly on imperial coins.

The myth

Laetitia appears more in imagery than in stories, and no myth of her own survives describing her origin or deeds. Her name carries meanings such as "joy", "pleasure", "delight" or "sweetness" of speech, and even the "fertility" of plants and soil.

She was most often shown as a woman holding a wreath in one hand, with the other resting on an anchor or a ship's rudder, which has led scholars to link her with seafaring. On one coin of the emperor Claudius Gothicus she holds a cornucopia, an attribute usually linked to Fortuna.

Under the emperors Septimius Severus, Caracalla and Geta, coins show her beside a ship unloading tigers and lions, inscribed LAETITIA AVG, "joy of the emperor".

Trivia

A coin with a public message. A denarius of the empress Julia Maesa shows Laetitia holding a globe, inscribed LAETITIA PVBL, "joy of the public". It may suggest that the population rejoiced in the expansion of the Empire.
A goddess in philosophy. The philosopher Boethius, in the 6th century, connected laetitia with other abstract forces of good, such as sufficientia, reverentia, potentia and claritudo.
