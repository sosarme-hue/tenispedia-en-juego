epithet of Athena in Pella (Macedonia)

Athena Alkidemos

Alkidemos ("defender of the people") was an epithet under which Athena was honoured as the protector of the city of Pella, in ancient Macedonia. The name sums up the goddess's role as guardian of the civic community.

The myth

The ancient sources do not preserve a narrative of their own for Athena Alkidemos. The title appears as one aspect of the goddess rather than as a figure with a story of her own. The name combines demos (people) with a root meaning defence, presenting her as a protector of the citizens. A related Macedonian epithet, Alcis, expressed a similar idea of protection.

Worship and religion

Her worship was located in Pella, the capital of Macedonia. The only ancient source that documents it is Livy, who mentions it in connection with the goddess worshipped there. Hellenistic tetradrachms often showed her with a thunderbolt and an aegis, symbols of her power and protection.

Ancient sources: Livy, in Book 42 of his History of Rome, is the only ancient source that mentions her.

Trivia

A thunderbolt on the coin. Hellenistic tetradrachms often showed the goddess with a thunderbolt and an aegis, a combination that identifies her in coin iconography.
A sister epithet. Alcis, another Macedonian epithet of Athena, carried a similar sense of protection.
