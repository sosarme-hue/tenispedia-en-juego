Greek god of laughter

Gelos

Gelos was, in Greek mythology, the divine personification of laughter. He appears in only a few sources, yet his place in the retinue of Dionysus and in Spartan worship shows that the Greeks gave him a recognised role among the abstract forces surrounding the gods.

The myth

According to Philostratus the Elder, Gelos was believed to join the retinue of Dionysus, alongside Comus, the spirit of revelry. In this account he is a companion of the wine god's festivities.

The tradition offers no genealogy or deeds of his own. He is mainly a figure of celebration and everyday merriment.

Worship and religion

Plutarch relates that Lycurgus of Sparta dedicated a small statue of Gelos to the gods and that Sparta had a sanctuary dedicated to him. The same author mentions similar sanctuaries for Thanatos (death) and Phobos (fear), suggesting that Sparta honoured several personifications of emotions and experiences. A festival in honour of Risus, the Latin name for Gelos, in Thessaly was described by Apuleius, though it is unknown whether it was a real event or the writer's invention.

Legacy

Gelos left little later trace. His name survives mainly in the Latin form Risus, which gave rise to modern words related to laughter in European languages.

Ancient sources: Philostratus the Elder places him in the retinue of Dionysus, Plutarch mentions his statue and Spartan sanctuary, and Apuleius describes the Thessalian festival in honour of Risus.

Trivia

Risus in Latin. His Latin name, Risus, was the translation of Gelos. The sources use it for the same personification of laughter.
Sanctuaries of emotions. Plutarch places Gelos' sanctuary in Sparta alongside those of Thanatos and Phobos, showing that the Spartans also honoured abstract emotions and experiences.
