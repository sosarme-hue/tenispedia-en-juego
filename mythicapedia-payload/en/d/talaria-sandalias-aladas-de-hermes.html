Talaria (Winged Sandals of Hermes)

The talaria are the winged sandals of Hermes, the Greek messenger god, known to the Romans as Mercury. According to tradition, Hephaestus made them of imperishable gold, and they carried the god as swiftly as a bird.

The myth

According to the myth, Hephaestus made the sandals, and they let Hermes fly and leave no footprints, a useful gift when he stole Apollo's cattle, as the Homeric Hymn to Hermes recounts. Homer mentions them as golden and immortal, but not as winged; that feature appears later, in the Shield of Heracles.

The hero Perseus also wore them to slay Medusa. According to Aeschylus, Hermes handed them over directly, while in a better-attested version Perseus had to take them from the Graeae.

Legacy

The Latin term talaria was used for these sandals by authors such as Ovid and Virgil. In medieval Ireland, the Irish versions of the Aeneid and the Destruction of Troy describe Mercury wearing a feather mantle that clearly derives from these sandals.

Ancient sources: Homer, the Homeric Hymn to Hermes, the Shield of Heracles, Aeschylus and Ovid mention or describe these sandals, though not all present them as winged.

Trivia

No footprints. In the Homeric Hymn to Hermes, the sandals let him steal Apollo's cattle without leaving a trace.
Perhaps not wings. Some early Greek vases show a curved piece on Hermes' boots that may be a pull strap rather than a wing.
