idolatrous god linked to the star of Saturn

Remphan

Remphan (also Rephan) is the name Stephen uses in the Book of Acts for the star of an idolatrous god. It is generally identified with the planet Saturn and with the Mesopotamian deity Kiyyun.

The myth

Its earliest reference is in Amos, who condemns the northern Israelites for carrying in procession their Moloch, their Chiun and the star of their god. The Septuagint renders Chiun as Raiphan, the source of the form Stephen uses centuries later. According to Stephen, the idolatry of his ancestors explained their exile beyond Babylon.

The texts say little about the god, and scholars debate whether Remphan and Kiyyun are proper or common nouns, since each word appears only once in the Bible.

Worship and religion

The sources mention only figures and images made for worshipping it; they do not describe temples or festivals.

Legacy

Christian commentators long debated it. In 1625 Thomas Godwyn separated the star of Remphan from Kiyyun, and in the 18th century Christian Gottlieb Wolff proposed an Egyptian origin linked to the king Remphis of Diodorus Siculus.

Ancient sources: The Book of Acts (7:43) in the New Testament and the Book of Amos (5:26), in the Greek Septuagint translation.

Trivia

Words that appear once. Both Kiyyun and Remphan are hapax legomena, meaning they occur only once in the surviving sources.
A possible pharaoh. Wolff linked the name to Remphis, an Egyptian king cited by Diodorus Siculus who may have been Ramesses III.
