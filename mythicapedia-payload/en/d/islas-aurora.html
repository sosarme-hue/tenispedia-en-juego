Aurora Islands

The Aurora Islands were a group of three phantom islands in the South Atlantic, between the Falklands and South Georgia, that appeared on charts and in sightings for over a century without ever being confirmed.

The myth

The Spanish merchant ship Aurora first reported them in 1762 on the route from Lima to Cadiz, and its officers claimed to see them again in 1774. In 1794 the corvette Atrevida, part of Alejandro Malaspina's expedition, was sent to check and also reported sighting them, without being able to confirm them. The last reported sightings date from 1856.

Their origin may lie with Amerigo Vespucci, whose 1504 letter describes a cold, harbourless land at about 52 degrees south. That description does not fit the Falklands, which has led people to compare the case with other phantom islands, such as Saint Brendan's Island.

Legacy

Edgar Allan Poe mentions them in his novel The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym, in which the crew searches for them in vain. In 2001, Barbara Hodgson made them the subject of the novel Hippolyte's Island.

Ancient sources: Amerigo Vespucci's 1504 letter (Lettera), which describes a southern land that may correspond to these islands.

Trivia

Shag Islands. In British historiography, James Sheffield rediscovered them and named them the Shag Islands.
An Argentine atlas. The Atlas Geográfico Argentino, published in 1888, listed them under the name Aurora Islands.
