warrior people and kingdom in the Mahabharata

Kalinga

Kalinga is a kingdom and warrior clan described in the Mahabharata, settled in the historical Kalinga region, roughly today's Odisha and northern Andhra Pradesh. It matters mainly for its role in the great war of Kurukshetra and for the tradition that its people founded several eastern kingdoms.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, the Kalingas were a warrior people who sided with Duryodhana in the Kurukshetra War, owing to marriage ties and alliances with the Kurus. Among the generals of the Kaurava army was Srutayudha, king of Kalinga, alongside allies such as Shakuni, Shalya and Jayadratha.

The epic adds that the Kalingas were the founders of five eastern kingdoms, Anga, Vanga, Odra, Pundra and Suhma, which shared a common ancestry. It also names two Kalinga capitals, Dantapura and Rajapura, and suggests that several Kalinga kings ruled different territories. A Kalinga princess, Karambha, married the Puru king Akrodhana, and their son was Devatithi.

Ancient sources: They appear in the Mahabharata, the great Indian epic poem.

Trivia

Two capitals. The Mahabharata names two capitals of Kalinga, Dantapura and Rajapura.
Five sister kingdoms. According to the epic, Kalinga gave rise to five eastern kingdoms that shared a common ancestry.
