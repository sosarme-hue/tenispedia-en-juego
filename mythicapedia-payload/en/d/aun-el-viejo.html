legendary Swedish king of the House of Yngling

Aun the Old

Aun the Old is a legendary Swedish king of the House of Yngling, remembered in Norse tradition for his wisdom and for sacrificing his sons to prolong his own life. His story survives mainly in medieval Scandinavian literature, which places him among the ancestors of the kings of Uppsala.

The myth

According to tradition, Aun was the son of King Jorund and ruled from Uppsala. He was a wise king who made offerings to the gods, but he had little taste for war. He was defeated by the Danish prince Halfdan and fled to the Geats, where he stayed until his rival's death.

Back in Uppsala at sixty, Aun sacrificed a son to Odin to gain sixty more years. Years later Ale the Strong drove him out again, and on his return Odin granted him more years in exchange for a son every ten years, and for naming a Swedish province after the number of sacrifices. Aged and bedridden, Aun tried to sacrifice his tenth son, but the Swedes stopped him and he died. His last son, Egil, identified with Ongentheow, succeeded him.

Legacy

The Scandinavian expression "Aun's sickness" for dying in bed of old age comes from this story.

Ancient sources: Found in Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla, with the Latin summary of the Historia Norwegiae and the Ynglingatal.

Trivia

A name with variants. He appears under forms such as Ane, On, One or Aukun, depending on the language and the source.
Ongentheow in Beowulf. His son Egil is identified with Ongentheow, a character in the Old English poem Beowulf.
