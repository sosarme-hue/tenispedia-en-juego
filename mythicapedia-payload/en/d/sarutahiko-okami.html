Shinto god of guidance, crossroads and paths

Sarutahiko Ōkami

Sarutahiko Ōkami is a deity of Japanese Shinto, leader of the earthly kami (kunitsukami) and associated with guidance, crossroads and light. He is best known for guiding the heavenly descendants down to earth and for his later link to the martial arts.

The myth

According to the Nihon Shoki, Sarutahiko meets Ninigi-no-Mikoto, grandson of Amaterasu, when he descends from Takama-ga-hara. He is described as a towering man with a great beard, a ruddy face, a long nose and eyes that shine red like mirrors, so fearsome that the other heavenly deities hesitated to confront him. Only Ame-no-Uzume dares approach him, and she persuades him to let Ninigi pass. He later marries her, and their children founded the Saru-me clan, whose women served at court as dancers and ritual officiants, a tradition linked to the origins of kagura and noh.

According to the Kojiki, Sarutahiko died in Ise: a giant clam trapped his hand in the Isuzu river at Azaka, and he drowned. His spirit received three names according to the stage of his death: Sokodokumitama as he sank, Tsubutatsumitama as bubbles rose, and Awasakumitama when the bubbles burst at the surface.

Worship and religion

His worship centres on shrines such as the Tsubaki Grand Shrine in Mie Prefecture, Sarutahiko Jinja in Ise and Ōasahiko Shrine in Tokushima, alongside some 2,000 shrines dedicated to him. In Ise he was revered as the god who illuminates heaven and earth, even before Amaterasu. His cult remains alive within present-day Shinto practice.

Legacy

Ueshiba Morihei regarded him as a kind of god of cosmic life, the god of aiki, and understood aikidō as a form of misogi, purification. Because of this association he is regarded as the patron of martial arts such as aikidō.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, Nihon Shoki, Norito and Jinnō Shōtōki, which names him as ancestor of Otanomikoto.

Trivia

A god with monkey traits. The anthropologist Emiko Ohnuki-Tierney notes that his name contains saru, meaning monkey, and that his features include red buttocks, a prominent trait of the Japanese macaque. The story of a hand caught in a shell also echoes Japanese folktales about monkeys.
One of seven great kami. Sarutahiko is one of only seven kami honoured with the title Ōkami, or Great Kami. Together with Inari, he is one of the few Ōkami among the earthly kunitsukami, while the others are heavenly deities.
