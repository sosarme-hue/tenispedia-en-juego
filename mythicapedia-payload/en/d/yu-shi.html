Chinese god of rain

Yu Shi

Yu Shi is a Chinese spirit or god of rain, sometimes confused with Chisong, the Red Pine. In Chinese folk religion he appears alongside Feng Bo, the god of wind, and Lei Gong, the god of thunder.

The myth

According to one version, Chisongzi, during the reign of Shennong, ended a severe drought by sprinkling water from an earthen bowl. He was rewarded with the title Lord of Rain and a dwelling on the mythical Kunlun Mountain. Another form depicts him as a silkworm chrysalis with a black-faced concubine who holds a snake in each hand.

In the older historical myths, Yu Shi and Feng Bo aided Chiyou in his battle against the Yellow Emperor at Zhuolu, but were defeated when the drought goddess Ba intervened. His consort is Yu Shiqie.

Worship and religion

Han Chinese, Maonan people and others in southwest China still worship him as an important rain god. Ritual prayer ceremonies are held to beg for rain, so his cult remains alive today.

Legacy

He appears in ancient poetry and in references within Chinese popular culture, usually alongside other gods of weather.

Ancient sources: The Chu ci poems "Tian Wen" and "Yuan You" mention him.

Trivia

A chrysalis with snakes. In one of his forms, Chi Songzi appears as a silkworm chrysalis, with a red snake and a green snake coming out of his ears.
