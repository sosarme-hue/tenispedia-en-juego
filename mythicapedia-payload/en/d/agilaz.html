legendary archer of Germanic mythology

Agilaz

Agilaz, also known as Egil, is a legendary archer hero of Norse and Germanic tradition, central to the Völundarkviða and the Thidrek saga. He matters because his figure links Scandinavian tales with early Germanic records from England and Alamannia.

The myth

According to the Völundarkviða, Egil is a son of the Frisian chieftain Finn, younger brother of Slagfiðr and elder brother of Völund. The three brothers married valkyries they met in swan form: Slagfiðr wed Hlaðguðr svanhvít and Völund wed Hervör alvitr, daughters of King Hlödver, while Egil married Ölrún, daughter of the Roman emperor Kjárr of Valland.

In the Thidrek saga, Egil is a masterly archer forced by King Nidung to shoot an apple off his son's head. He had two arrows and needed only the first; when asked about the second, he said it was meant for the king had he missed. Unlike William Tell, the king thanked him for his frankness. Later, to free his crippled brother Völund, Egil gathered birds' feathers so Völund could make wings. When Nidung ordered him to shoot the fugitive, he aimed at a blood-filled bladder tied to Völund, deceiving the king and letting him escape.

Legacy

The name goes back to Proto-Germanic *Agilaz, and the figure appears in Anglo-Saxon and Alamannic inscriptions on the Pforzen buckle and the Franks Casket, where he is shown with his wife Alruna shooting at enemy troops. The apple-shooting tale is compared with legends such as William Tell and Palnatoke.

Ancient sources: The Völundarkviða and the Thidrek saga, alongside epigraphic and casket evidence from Anglo-Saxon and Alamannic sources.

Trivia

Two arrows. In the Thidrek saga, Egil keeps a second arrow in case he misses, meant for the king.
A king who forgives. Unlike William Tell, Nidung thanks the archer for his candour instead of punishing him.
