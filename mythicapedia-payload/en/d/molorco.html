labourer of Cleonae, host of Heracles

Molorchus

Molorchus was a humble labourer from Cleonae who, according to Greek tradition, hosted Heracles on his way to Nemea to kill the lion. His story is a classic example of the hospitality the hero found even among ordinary people.

The myth

According to the story, Molorchus was about to sacrifice an animal when Heracles arrived. The hero asked him to wait thirty days: if he came back safe from the hunt, Molorchus should offer the sacrifice to Zeus the Saviour; if he died, he should honour him as a hero.

On the last day, Molorchus was about to sacrifice the animal as if Heracles were dead, when the hero suddenly returned. Together they offered the sacrifice to Zeus the Saviour, and Heracles later took the lion to Mycenae. Lucius Ampelius adds that Molorchus was a friend of the hero and gave him the club he used to kill the lion.

Legacy

Martial mentions him in his Epigrams as a model of simple, honourable hospitality, set against luxurious estates. The Nemean forest was poetically called the grove of Molorchus, and an ancient town in the area, Molorchia, also took its name from him.

Ancient sources: Lucius Ampelius, Martial's Epigrams and Vibius Sequester, who places a grove bearing his name at Nemea.

Trivia

A town named after him. Molorchia was an ancient city of Nemea which, according to the sources, took its name from the labourer.
The grove of Molorchus. Poets called the Nemean forest the grove of Molorchus, in memory of his hospitality.
