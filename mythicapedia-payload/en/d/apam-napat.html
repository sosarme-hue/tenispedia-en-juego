Iranian god of the waters, protector of royal glory

Apam Napat

Apam Napat is an Iranian deity linked to the waters, whose name means "Son of the Waters". Once an exalted figure among the ancient Ahuras, credited with creating humankind, he retains a place in Zoroastrian liturgy although his prominence declined over time.

The myth

According to the Avestan texts, Apam Napat distributes the waters of the cosmic sea Vourukasha across the inhabited world, protects seafarers, and guards the Farr-e Izadi, the royal glory through which Iranian kingship is legitimized. Alongside Mithra, he maintains order in human society and defends the lands against disturbance.

In the Zamyad Yasht he is invoked as the "High Lord", and the hymn describes him as the creator and shaper of mankind. In the Middle Persian Bundahishn he emerges from the waters to awaken the mythical bird Chamrosh against forces threatening Iran. Over time his creative role was overshadowed by Ohrmazd.

Worship and religion

In Zoroastrianism his name appears in liturgical invocations, and he is still honoured daily in Zoroastrian worship. His cult lost prominence to Anahid, the goddess of waters, who came to overshadow him in that sphere.

Legacy

Some scholars have proposed that the word naphtha, which passed from Iranian into Greek and then English, may derive from his name, though the evidence is modest. His links with fire, Agni, and the tale of sacred fires burning on the Caspian Sea have inspired speculation, but no firm connection has been established.

Ancient sources: The Avesta, especially the Yasna and the Zamyad Yasht (Yasht 19), and the Middle Persian Bundahishn; in the Vedas his name appears as an epithet of Agni.
