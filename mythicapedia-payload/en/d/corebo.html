Greek hero who founded Tripodiskos

Coroebus

Coroebus is a Greek hero who, according to tradition, founded the city of Tripodiskos in Megara. His story is tied to the cult of Apollo and to his purification at Delphi.

The myth

According to tradition, Psamathe, daughter of Crotopus, king of Argos, bore a son to Apollo named Linus. Fearing her father's anger, she abandoned him, but the temple dogs found the child and devoured him. Enraged, Apollo sent Poine, a monster that snatched children from their mothers in Argos. Coroebus killed the beast and freed the Argives.

Yet the god remained angry over his son's death and sent a plague upon the land. Coroebus had to go to Delphi to be purified, and the Pythia told him to drink from a sacred tripod and leave: wherever the vessel fell from his hands, he was to build a temple to Apollo and settle there. The tripod fell on the slopes of Mount Geranea, where the hero founded Tripodiskos. In the agora of Megara stood his tomb, with a sculpture of him slaying Poine; Pausanias considered it one of the oldest works of Greek sculpture.

Ancient sources: Callimachus told the story in the first book of his Causes, and Pausanias describes the statue on his tomb in Megara.
