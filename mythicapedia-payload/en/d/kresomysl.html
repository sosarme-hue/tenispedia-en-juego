mythical prince of Bohemia

Křesomysl

Křesomysl is, according to Czech tradition, the fifth of the seven mythical princes of Bohemia who came before the Přemyslid dynasty. His name survives thanks to the chronicle of Cosmas of Prague.

The myth

According to tradition, Křesomysl was the son of Vnislav and the father of Neklan. He ruled between the legendary Přemysl the Ploughman, founder of the Přemyslid dynasty, and Bořivoj, the first historical prince. His name is usually read as meaning «lighting the mind», from křesat (to strike a light) and mysl (mind or spirit).

Some scholars think Cosmas may have invented these names while trying to decipher an old Latin transcription of a Slavonic message, possibly addressed to the Franks around 849. Another theory links the names to the days of the week: Křesomysl would be the fifth, Thursday, the day of thunder.

Legacy

The name entered 19th-century historiography through works such as František Palacký's The History of the Czech Nation in Bohemia and Moravia. The frescoes in the Rotunda in Znojmo have been linked to these princes, although that interpretation is not universally accepted.

Ancient sources: The chronicle of Cosmas of Prague, which is the first source to record the names of Bohemia's mythical princes.

Trivia

A lost message. Some researchers propose that, when combined, the princes' names form a Slavonic phrase with a peaceful tone, perhaps addressed to the Franks.
The days of the week. According to Záviš Kalandra, the seven names could be coded references to the Slavic days of the week, with Křesomysl standing for Thursday.
