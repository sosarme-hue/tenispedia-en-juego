Gandhari

Gandhari is a princess of Gandhara and queen of Hastinapura, wife of the blind king Dhritarashtra and mother of the hundred Kauravas. Her devotion and moral strength make her a model of virtuous wifehood in the Mahabharata, and a voice of wisdom that cannot stop the war.

The myth

In the Mahabharata, Gandhari is the daughter of King Subala and sister of Shakuni. From a young age she worshipped Shiva, who granted her the boon of a hundred sons. Bhishma arranged her marriage to Dhritarashtra, a prince born blind. On learning this, Gandhari blindfolded herself for life to share her husband's darkness, a gesture the tradition reads as devotion and some modern scholars as protest.

After a pregnancy of two years, she gave birth to a lump of flesh, which the sage Vyasa divided into 101 portions. From these came one hundred sons, the Kauravas, and a daughter, Dushala. Gandhari urged her son Duryodhana to make peace with the Pandavas, but she could not check his ambition, and all her sons died in the Kurukshetra War. Grief-stricken, she cursed Krishna, whom she held responsible for the destruction. The tradition says the curse came true years later with the fall of the Yadava dynasty. She later retired to the forest with her husband and died in a forest fire, attaining moksha.

Legacy

In Hebbya, near Nanjangud (Mysore, India), a temple dedicated to Gandhari stands, whose foundation stone was laid in 2008. Rabindranath Tagore wrote a Bengali poem centred on her, Gandharir Abedon, and several Indian television series have portrayed her in their adaptations of the Mahabharata.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, especially its Adi Parva, where she is first introduced.

Trivia

A temple in Mysore. In the village of Hebbya, near Nanjangud, a temple dedicated to Gandhari was built; its foundation stone was laid on 19 June 2008.
A poem by Tagore. Rabindranath Tagore wrote a Bengali poem about Gandhari titled Gandharir Abedon, in which she, her husband and her son Duryodhana are the central figures.
