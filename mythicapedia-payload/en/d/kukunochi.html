tree god of Japanese mythology

Kukunochi

Kukunochi is a kami of Japanese mythology, the lord of trees and of the trunk that holds them up. Ancient chronicles count him among the children of the creator couple Izanagi and Izanami, and his worship still survives in house-building rites.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Kukunochi emerged during the creation of the deities, before Ōyamatsumi, the mountain deity, and Kaya-no-hime, the deity of the grasslands. The Nihonshoki places his appearance after the rivers, mountains and seas came into being, so the accounts do not agree on the order of his birth.

His name is explained as a combination of the possessive particle no and chi, a suffix added to the names of deities, giving a sense of "tree trunk god" or "god of trees". Some versions make him a son of Izanagi and Izanami, while others have him descend from Shinatsuhiko, and in certain traditions he is the brother of Ōyamatsumi, Shimatsuhiko and Watatsumi.

Worship and religion

His main place of worship is the Chito Shrine in Nishinomiya, Hyōgo Prefecture. He also receives offerings at the Tarumaeyama Shrine in Tomakoma, Hokkaidō, alongside Ōyamatsumi and Kaya-no-hime. Today he is invoked at roof-raising ceremonies and at the blessing of new houses.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihonshoki both mention him among the deities born during the creation.
