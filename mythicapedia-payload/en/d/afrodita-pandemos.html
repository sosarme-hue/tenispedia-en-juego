epithet of Aphrodite, goddess of love, "common to all the people"

Aphrodite Pandemos

Pandemos, "common to all the people", is an epithet of the Greek goddess Aphrodite, carrying two meanings: the shared pleasures of the senses and the goddess who unites the inhabitants into a single civic body. Her worship was especially important in Athens.

The myth

According to Plutarch, the epithet Epitragia ("from a she-goat") comes from an episode in the life of Theseus. Before sailing to Crete, the hero sacrificed a she-goat to Aphrodite, following Apollo's advice, hoping she would guide his voyage. During the sacrifice the animal suddenly turned into a male goat.

In Plato's Symposium, Pausanias presents Aphrodite Pandemos as the goddess of sensual pleasures, set against the heavenly Aphrodite Urania. Scopas depicted the goddess riding a ram at Elis.

Worship and religion

Pausanias says Theseus instituted her cult when he united the scattered townships of Attica into one body of citizens, and at Athens she was worshipped alongside Peitho (persuasion). According to Harpocration and Suidas, Solon erected her sanctuary, perhaps in the agora. White goats were offered to her, and Athenaeus mentions a festival in her honour. Her worship also took place at Megalopolis and Thebes.

Ancient sources: Plato (Symposium), Plutarch, Pausanias, Harpocration, Suidas and Athenaeus mention her in their works.
