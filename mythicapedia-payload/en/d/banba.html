Irish goddess, matron of Ireland

Banba

Banba is a matron goddess of Irish mythology, daughter of Delbáeth and Ernmas and a member of the Tuatha Dé Danann. Together with her sisters Ériu and Fódla she forms a triad of goddesses who personify the land of Ireland.

The myth

According to the stories, Banba was married to Mac Cuill, a grandson of the Dagda. When the Milesians arrived in Ireland, she offered them wealth and happiness if the island were named after her, but they passed her by. Some versions say they eventually banished her by singing spells.

Seathrún Céitinn records another tradition that makes her the first person to set foot in Ireland before the flood, a variant of the legend of Cessair. In the Tochomlad mac Miledh a hEspain i nErind, the Milesians meet her among her troop of magical faery hosts on Senna Mountain, identified with Slieve Mish in County Kerry.

Legacy

The name Banba is used as a poetic term for Ireland, and Malin Head in Ulster is locally known as Banba's Crown. A ship of the Irish Naval Service, the LÉ Banba, was named after her.

Ancient sources: The Tochomlad mac Miledh a hEspain i nErind mentions her, and the seventeenth-century historian Seathrún Céitinn records traditions about her.

Trivia

Link to pigs. The name Banba resembles banb, the Irish word for piglet, and pigs were closely associated with prosperity in Ireland. For this reason they are sometimes considered a symbol of the goddess.
Banba's Crown. Malin Head, at the northern tip of Ireland, is locally known as Banba's Crown, showing how strongly the goddess is tied to the land.
