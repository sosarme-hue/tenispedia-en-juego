Vanir goddess, twin sister and wife of Njörðr

Sister-wife of Njörðr

The sister-wife of Njörðr is an unnamed goddess of Norse mythology, the twin and first wife of the god Njörðr and the mother of Freyr and Freyja. She belongs to the Vanir and barely appears in the texts, so she remains a very shadowy figure.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson in the Ynglinga saga, Njörðr married his sister following Vanir custom, and Freyr and Freyja were born from that union. However, in the Gylfaginning Snorri places their birth after Njörðr came to live among the Æsir, following his unhappy marriage to Skaði, and in the poem Skírnismál Freyr is presented as the son of Njörðr and Skaði. The Lokasenna hints at a Vanir incest between Njörðr and his sister, during which Freyja was supposedly caught.

In his Germania, Tacitus describes rituals surrounding a goddess named Nerthus, whose name is etymologically identical to Njörðr's, although she is female. For this reason scholars have proposed an original hermaphroditic deity or a married twin pair, parallel to Freyr and Freyja.

Ancient sources: The Lokasenna and Snorri Sturluson's 13th-century Ynglinga saga mention her, and Tacitus's Germania describes Nerthus as a possible precursor.

Trivia

A possible name. Some scholars propose that the goddess was called Njörun, a name that appears in some skaldic kennings and in the Nafnaþulur appended to the Prose Edda.
An old hypothesis. Jacob Grimm suggested that Freyja was the daughter of Nerthus, the goddess, and Freyr the son of Njörðr, a reading that reversed the usual order of the pair.
