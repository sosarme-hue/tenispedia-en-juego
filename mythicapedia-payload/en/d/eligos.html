great duke of hell in demonology

Eligos

Eligos is a demon from European demonology, described as a great duke of hell who commands sixty legions. He appears in early modern catalogues of demons, and his figure later passed into the handbooks of ceremonial magic.

The myth

In demonological treatises, Eligos, also called Abigor or Eligor, appears as a great duke of hell in command of sixty legions of demons. According to these descriptions, he uncovers hidden things and knows the future, and he has particular knowledge of wars and soldiers, which is why he is often linked to military affairs.

Among his powers is also the ability to win the favour of lords, knights and other influential people. The texts do not give him an origin or a family; they present him mainly as one more figure in an infernal hierarchy, each with specific powers.

His image varies from source to source. He is shown as a knight carrying a lance, a banner and a sceptre, and at times the sceptre takes the form of a serpent, as some authors, among them Aleister Crowley, have noted. Other depictions show him as a ghostly phantom, sometimes riding a winged horse.

Legacy

His name and image have passed into modern occult literature. Aleister Crowley adopted the idea of his serpent-shaped sceptre within the tradition of ceremonial magic, which continues to cite these catalogues of demons.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, a catalogue of demons that circulated in sixteenth-century Europe and served as a basis for later treatises.

Trivia

A serpent-shaped sceptre. According to some authors, such as Aleister Crowley, the sceptre carried by Eligos can take the form of a serpent.
A phantom on a winged horse. Some depictions show him as a ghostly phantom, sometimes riding a winged horse.
