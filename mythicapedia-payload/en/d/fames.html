Roman goddess of hunger

Fames

Fames is, in Roman mythology, the personification of hunger, able to arouse an insatiable appetite. She was regarded as a deity associated with the evils that dwell at the entrance to the Underworld.

The myth

According to Ovid, in his Metamorphoses, Fames lives in Scythia, a desolate land where she scrabbles tirelessly for the scant vegetation that grows there. She appears above all in the punishment of Erysichthon: when Ceres commands it, Fames takes hold of the man and plunges him into a hunger that never ends, no matter how much he eats.

Tradition also places her among the evils surrounding the entrance to the realm of the dead, alongside other personifications of misfortune. The ancient commentator Servius calls her the greatest of the Furies, a sign of how much power was attributed to her. The Romans linked her with Limos, the Greek figure of famine, so the two traditions correspond.

Ancient sources: Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, describes her in the story of Erysichthon; the commentator Servius places her among the Furies.

Trivia

A goddess of distant lands. Ovid places her in Scythia, a cold and desolate region where almost nothing grows to eat.
The greatest of the Furies. Servius claims that Fames was the greatest of the Furies, ranking above the other avenging figures.
