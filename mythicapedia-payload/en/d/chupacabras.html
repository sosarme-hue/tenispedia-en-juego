legendary creature of the Americas, linked to livestock attacks

Chupacabra

The chupacabra is a cryptid from the folklore of several parts of the Americas, said to prey on livestock, especially goats, by draining their blood. Its name means 'goat-sucker', and its modern fame began in Puerto Rico in 1995, although most later sightings have never been verified.

The myth

According to the accounts, the chupacabra is a creature that attacks farm animals at night and leaves them drained of blood, with two or three puncture wounds. Its appearance varies greatly by region. In Puerto Rico and much of Hispanic America it is pictured as a reptile-like beast with grey-green scaly skin, a row of spines from the neck to the tail, large red eyes, and a size close to that of a small bear. In the southwestern United States, by contrast, it is usually imagined as a hairless, dog-like animal with fangs and a pronounced spine.

The modern story begins in March 1995, when eight sheep were found dead in Puerto Rico with three chest wounds each. Months later, a resident of Canóvanas, Madelyne Tolentino, said she had seen the creature. Soon after, animal deaths were reported in other American countries, and sightings spread through Mexico, South America and the southern United States.

Researchers have explained many of these cases naturally. In northern Mexico and the southern United States, animals identified as chupacabras turned out to be coyotes, dogs or other canids with severe mange, a skin disease that can leave them hairless and strange-looking. One study linked Tolentino's description to the creature Sil from the science fiction film Species, although this conclusion is debated. Moreover, the analysis of hundreds of alleged victims found no signs of blood loss, and predators may kill without eating their prey.

Legacy

The name has become popular in Hispanic culture as an expression for any mysterious attack on livestock. The myth has inspired series, films and video games, and appears in popular culture as a contemporary urban legend. Some researchers connect it with the vampire of Moca, a 1975 case in Puerto Rico in which animal deaths were blamed on a mysterious entity.

Trivia

Origin of the name. The term is attributed to Puerto Rican comedian Silverio Pérez, who coined it in 1995 during a radio program in San Juan.
A mange case. In Texas, an animal captured in 2004 and named the Elmendorf Beast turned out to be a coyote with mange.
