group of five heroines of Hinduism

Panchakanya

The Panchakanya ("five maidens") are a group of five iconic women of the Hindu epics: Ahalya, Draupadi, Kunti, Tara and Mandodari. Reciting their names is believed to dispel sin, and they are regarded as ideal wives in Hinduism.

The myth

According to tradition, the list comes from a Sanskrit hymn recited at dawn. Ahalya, Tara and Mandodari belong to the Ramayana, while Draupadi and Kunti belong to the Mahabharata. In some versions of the hymn, Sita takes the place of Kunti.

Ahalya, wife of the sage Gautama, was seduced by Indra, who came disguised as her husband. According to the stories, Gautama cursed them both. In the popular version, Ahalya was turned into stone until Rama's foot touched her, and Indra was marked with a thousand vulvas that later became a thousand eyes.

Tara, queen of Kishkindha and wife of the vanara king Vali, advised her husband not to accept a duel with his brother Sugriva, but he ignored her and died at Rama's hands. Tara then married Sugriva and became his counselor, even calming Lakshmana. Mandodari, wife of Ravana, urged him to return Sita to Rama, without success, and after Ravana's death she married his brother Vibhishana.

Worship and religion

In Hinduism, married women especially recite the names of these five women in a morning prayer called pratah-smaraniyah, said in the early hours of the day. It remains a living practice among devout Hindus.

Ancient sources: These figures appear in the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, and the hymn listing them is found in a variant attributed to the Brahma Purana.

Trivia

A mark turned into eyes. According to the stories, Indra was marked with a thousand vulvas that later became a thousand eyes.
A hymn to remember. The hymn listing the five women is recited so that their names dispel sin, and it is said in the early hours of the morning.
