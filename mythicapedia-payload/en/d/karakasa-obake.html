umbrella yōkai of Japanese folklore

Karakasa-obake

The karakasa-obake (傘お化け) is a yōkai of Japanese folklore that takes the form of an umbrella, hopping about on a single leg and sporting one eye. It is the best-known yōkai based on a household object, although it does not appear in eyewitness accounts from folk tradition.

The myth

According to popular belief, old umbrellas that reach a hundred years of age come to life as yōkai, which is why it is often considered a tsukumogami. Not all sources classify it this way, however, and no classical texts confirm the idea.

Its appearance varies in early depictions. In the Hyakki Yagyō Emaki scroll from the Muromachi period, umbrella yōkai are shown folded and with human faces. The umbrella with one eye and one leg became established in the Edo period, and the Obake karuta picture cards from the Edo to Taishō periods often show versions with one leg.

It is described as a mischievous rather than malicious creature. Its usual trick is to sneak up on people and lick them with its enormous tongue. The fright can cause panic, but it does no real harm. Related tales exist too, such as a rain umbrella said to appear in the valleys of Ehime, leaving onlookers unable to move, or the yūreigasa of Tottori, which on windy days was said to blow people up into the sky.

Legacy

After the war it became a familiar figure in monster drawings, in the haunted houses of amusement parks, and in anime, manga and films about yōkai. It appears in Super Mario Land 2, in The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask, in the 2005 film The Great Yokai War, and in the Nioh game series. It also appears in the manga Dandadan and in the twelfth Touhou Project game, where a karakasa named Kogasa Tatara features.

Ancient sources: The Hyakki Yagyō Emaki scroll (Muromachi period) and the Obake karuta cards (Edo and Taishō periods), although no classical texts verify it.

Trivia

Enemy in Kirby. In the Kirby games Dreamland 3 and UHFR9D there is an enemy called Jumpershoot that is based on the karakasa.
Hat in Zelda. In Majora's Mask the Skull Kid's hat is the head of a karakasa.
