king of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology

Bres

Bres is a king of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology, the son of the Fomorian prince Elatha and the goddess Ériu. His unpopular reign and his death at the hands of Lugh make him a key figure in the stories of the battles of Magh Tuireadh.

The myth

According to tradition, Bres was born to Elatha, prince of the Fomorians, and Ériu of the Tuatha Dé Danann, so he was half Fomorian. When King Nuada lost a hand in the First Battle of Magh Tuireadh, and a maimed man could not rule, Bres was chosen as king to seal a peace between the two peoples. He married Brigid, daughter of the Dagda, and they had a son, Ruadan, who died trying to kill Goibniu.

His rule was harsh. He made the Tuatha Dé pay tribute to the Fomorians and forced them to work as slaves: Ogma had to carry firewood and the Dagda had to dig trenches. He also neglected the duties of hospitality, as his guests found their knives never greased and their breath did not smell of ale. The poet Cairbre composed a scathing poem against him, the first satire in Ireland, and from then on everything went wrong for him.

After seven years, Nuada recovered his hand, first replaced by a silver one and later by one of flesh and blood thanks to Miach, and Bres was exiled. His father Elatha refused to help him regain by unjust means what he had not been able to keep by justice. Bres then turned to Balor and led the Fomorians in the Second Battle of Magh Tuireadh, where he was defeated. Pleading for his life before Lugh, he was spared in exchange for teaching the Tuatha Dé the art of agriculture. According to an account from the dindsenchas, Lugh later killed him: he made three hundred wooden cows filled with a bitter, poisonous liquid and offered it to Bres, who, bound by the law of hospitality, drank it without refusing.

Ancient sources: The Cath Maige Tuired, the Lebor Gabála Érenn and the dindsenchas, medieval Irish texts that preserve earlier oral tradition.

Trivia

Ireland's first satire. The poem Cairbre composed against Bres is considered the first satire in Ireland. According to the story, everything went wrong for the king from then on.
Wooden cows. In a dindsenchas account, Lugh made three hundred wooden cows to bring about Bres's death. The poison was presented as milk drawn into pails.
Rapid growth. Some texts say Bres grew so fast that by the age of seven he was the size of a fourteen-year-old boy.
