Tantric Hindu goddess of music and learning

Matangi

Matangi is a Tantric Hindu goddess, one of the ten Mahavidyas and the Tantric form of Sarasvati, goddess of music and learning. She governs speech, music, knowledge and the arts, and she is also worshipped to gain supernatural powers.

The myth

According to Tantric texts, Matangi is the ninth of the Mahavidyas, although some lists leave her out or identify her with other figures. One of her best-known stories tells how Sati, insulted because Daksha did not invite her or her husband Shiva to his yagna, transforms into the ten Mahavidyas, and Matangi takes the northwest to surround Shiva.

Another account, from the Shaktisamgama-tantra, describes the birth of Uchchhishta-Matangini. After Vishnu and Lakshmi give a banquet for Shiva and Parvati, a maiden arises from the food that falls to the ground. She is a manifestation of Saraswati and asks for the leftovers. The gods grant them to her as prasad, and Shiva declares that whoever invokes her will have their desires fulfilled and gain power over enemies.

Other versions tell of the ascetic Matanga who, after long penances, gains with her help the power to subdue all beings, and some texts present her as the daughter of the sage Matanga.

Worship and religion

Her worship is practised mainly within Tantrism, using mantras and meditations that describe her forms: Raja-Matangi, green, with a veena and a parrot, and Uchchhishta-Matangini, seated on a corpse and offered leftover food. One tale is linked to the temple of Kauri-bai, an aspect of Matangi, in the low-caste area of Varanasi. Her ritual aims include knowledge, artistic skill and control over enemies.

Legacy

Her image with the parrot and the veena links her to music and speech. The green colour is associated with Budha, the deity of the planet Mercury, who presides over intelligence.

Ancient sources: Tantric and Purana texts such as the Tantrasara, the Purashcharyarnava, the Shaktisamgama-tantra, the Devi Bhagavata Purana and Kalidasa's Shyamaladandakam.

Trivia

A green goddess. Meditation descriptions paint her as emerald green or, in some texts, blue. Green is linked to deep knowledge.
Sacred leftovers. In the Shaktisamgama-tantra, the gods' leftovers become prasad. Because of its negative connotation, the word uchchhishta is never explicitly used for prasad.
