Roman goddess of lightning

Fulgora

Fulgora is a Roman goddess associated with lightning, known only from a brief mention by Varro that survives through Augustine's City of God. She matters because she is one of the few Roman deities of whom barely any trace remains.

The myth

The surviving texts offer almost no stories about Fulgora. According to Varro, in his Antiquities of Human and Divine Things, as quoted by Augustine of Hippo, she was an example of a widow goddess, alongside Populonia and Rumina. Tradition thus presented her as a divinity without a husband, but nothing is said about her origins, her family or her deeds.

Modern scholarship offers other views. Robert Dyson considers her presumably a goddess of lightning or a protector against it. In 1910 Georg Wissowa thought it evident that she was the female equivalent of an epithet of Jupiter, though he noted that using it for Juno would clash with her status as a widow.

Ancient sources: Varro, Antiquities of Human and Divine Things, known through Augustine's quotation in The City of God (5th century AD).

Trivia

A widow goddess. According to Varro's quotation, Fulgora was listed among divinities described as widows, alongside Populonia and Rumina.
Almost no trace. Apart from this passage in Augustine, no other known reference to Fulgora survives.
