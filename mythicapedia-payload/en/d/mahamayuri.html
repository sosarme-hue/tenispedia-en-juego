bodhisattva and Wisdom Queen of Buddhism

Mahamayuri

Mahamayuri, the 'great peacock', is a bodhisattva and Wisdom Queen in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism, widely venerated in China and Japan. She protects against poison and disease, and is thought to originate from an Indian goddess of the same name.

The myth

According to legend, the Phoenix, leader of flying beings, gave birth to the peacock Mahamayuri in primordial times. The peacock preyed on humans and once swallowed the Buddha in a previous life, but he escaped by cutting open her stomach. When he meant to kill her, the gods intervened; in exchange for giving up her human-eating, the Buddha made her his godmother.

In the Mahamayuri Vidyarajni Sutra, a monk bitten by a snake is saved by her mantra.

Worship and religion

Her cult is important in Chinese and Japanese Vajrayana, where practitioners seek protection, healing, and control over rain or its cessation. She is often shown riding a peacock, holding a lotus and citrus fruit.

Legacy

She inspired the peacock spirit Kong Xuan in the Ming novel Fengshen Yanyi, and appears in Journey to the West.

Ancient sources: The Mahamayuri Vidyarajni Sutra and its dharani, translated into Chinese by Kumārajīva between 402 and 412 CE.

Trivia

Only mention of the Rig Veda. Mahamayuri's dharani, translated into Chinese by Kumārajīva, is the only place in the entire Chinese Buddhist canon that mentions the Rig Veda.
One of five protectors. She is the chief of the five rakṣā of the Nepali pañcarakṣā, occupying the northern position in the five-parasol arrangement.
