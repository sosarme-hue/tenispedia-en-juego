Hindu goddess, consort of Brihaspati and mother of Budha

Tara

Tara, also known as Taraka or Chandramasi, is a Hindu goddess and the wife of Brihaspati, priest of the gods. Her story, told in the Puranas, centres on the Tarakamaya episode, an affair that sparks a celestial war.

The myth

According to the Devi Bhagavata and Padma Puranas, Tara was the wife of Brihaspati, guru of the devas. Because he spent most of his time dealing with the affairs of the gods, she felt neglected. One day Soma, also called Chandra, the moon god, visited Brihaspati, saw Tara and was captivated by her; she fell for him too, and the two ran away together.

Brihaspati, jealous and furious, demanded his wife back, but Chandra replied that she was happy with him. The dispute led to a war between devas and asuras until Brahma stepped in and forced Chandra to return her, pregnant. Before the divine tribunal Tara stayed silent, until the unborn child asked who his father was; she admitted it was Chandra. Brahma ruled that the boy, Budha, was legally Brihaspati's son, and Brihaspati cursed him to be born without gender. Budha preferred his biological father, and his son Pururavas founded the lunar dynasty. The Padma Purana skips the legal battle: Chandra blesses the child and places him in the solar system.

Ancient sources: The Devi Bhagavata and Padma Puranas narrate the Tarakamaya episode.

Trivia

Goddess of Thursday. Tara is linked to Brihaspati's day, Thursday, and in Cambodia she is regarded as the protector of that day.
Kirinei Devi. In Cambodia she is known as Kirinei Devi; she is said to descend from heaven to care for the people for one year, until the next New Year.
