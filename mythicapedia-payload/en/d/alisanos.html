local Celtic god of eastern Gaul

Alisanos

Alisanos was a local Celtic god worshipped near Dijon, in what is now the Côte-d'Or department of France. Known as Alisanus in Latin, he is named in dedicatory inscriptions, making him one of the few Gaulish gods attested directly.

The myth

No story about Alisanos survives, so very little is known about his myths. His name is linked to the Gaulish word alisia, which may have meant 'rock' or 'whitebeam' (a kind of rowan tree), leading scholars to see him either as a god of rocks or of whitebeams.

The most widely accepted reading connects him with Celtic animism, the belief that natural features of the landscape had their own divine presence. Another hypothesis sees him as the eponymous god of Alesia, but other place names built from the same root, with no link to that town, weaken this idea.

Worship and religion

Offerings were dedicated to him on bronze votive bowls (paterae) found at Couchey and Visignot in the Côte-d'Or, inscribed in Gaulish and in Latin. The sources mention no known festivals or temples.

Ancient sources: Dedicatory inscriptions in Gaulish and Latin engraved on bronze votive bowls; no ancient literary texts mention him.

Trivia

A name with several readings. The name could mean 'rock' or 'whitebeam', and the same root appears in Alesia, a town further north in Burgundy.
