fierce warrior, wrathful form of the god Shiva

Virabhadra

Virabhadra is a fierce and terrifying form of the Hindu god Shiva, born from Shiva's wrath after the death of Sati at Daksha's sacrifice. His name means "hero" combined with "good" in Sanskrit, and he is one of the best-known warrior figures in Hindu mythology.

The myth

According to the stories, Sati, daughter of Daksha and wife of Shiva, immolated herself in fire after her father humiliated her at a great sacrifice to which Shiva had not been invited. Grief-stricken and furious, Shiva tore a lock of his matted hair and threw it to the ground, and from it arose Virabhadra and Bhadrakali.

Virabhadra appeared as a towering, dark-skinned being with three blazing eyes and a garland of skulls, leading an army of ganas. He blinded Bhaga, broke the teeth of Pushan, subdued Indra and routed the gods attending the sacrifice. In some versions Vishnu confronted him but could not stop him, and Virabhadra eventually beheaded Daksha. Shiva intervened, restored Daksha's life with the head of an animal, and ended the bloodshed.

Worship and religion

Virabhadra is venerated chiefly by Shaivas and especially by the Lingayats, who regard him as their mythical ancestor. Many temples are dedicated to him in southern India, such as the Lepakshi temple in Andhra Pradesh, built between 1530 and 1540 by two governors of the Vijayanagara Empire.

Legacy

His figure appears in the art and temple iconography of southern India and in Shaiva devotional literature.

Ancient sources: The Brahmanas of the Yajurveda and Atharvaveda recount the Daksha sacrifice episode, and the Puranas develop the figure of Virabhadra.
