mountain creature of French and Alpine folklore

Dahu

The dahu is a creature of modern folklore from France, Switzerland and northern Italy, resembling a mountain goat with legs of unequal length on either side of its body. It began as a mountain joke and is now remembered mainly for the fake hunt that was once played on gullible tourists.

The myth

According to the tradition, the dahu's defining trait is that the legs on one side of its body are shorter than those on the other, which lets it walk steep slopes, but only in one direction around the mountain. There are two variants: the laevogyrous dahu, with shorter left legs, circles the mountain counterclockwise, while the dextrogyre dahu, with shorter right legs, circles it clockwise. The legend says the two rarely mate and that, if they did, two other kinds would result, though nobody has ever seen them.

Stories add details about its love life: the males supposedly drag their testicles across the ground, leaving a scent trail that helps females find them, and they use the same trail to track rivals or future targets of their harassment.

In the prank version, catching a dahu takes two people: one holding a sack at the foot of the slope and another who imitates the animal's call behind it. When the dahu turns to look, it loses its balance and tumbles down the hill into the sack.

Legacy

In the twentieth century the dahu became part of popular culture in Lorraine, the Alps, the Jura and French-speaking Switzerland. The so-called dahu hunt, in which mountain guides tricked wealthy tourists into waiting all night on a freezing slope, later became a summer camp prank and, in some places, a recreational outdoor activity. The Alps Museum at Bard Fort in the Aosta Valley gave part of its permanent exhibition to the creature. Marcel Jacquat, former director of the Natural Science Museum of La Chaux-de-Fonds, wrote a monograph on it and staged an exhibition devoted to it.

Trivia

A homophone princess. In a Breton legend, Princess Dahut of Ys is sometimes linked to the animal because their names sound alike, although she is a different figure.
A joke sanctuary. On 1 April 1967, the prefect of Haute-Savoie declared the mountain outskirts of Reignier a sanctuary for the dahu, where hunting and photography are forbidden.
