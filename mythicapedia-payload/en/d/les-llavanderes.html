water spirits of Asturian folklore

Les Llavanderes

The Llavanderes, or Washerwomen, are water beings of Asturian folklore, alongside the Xanas and the Nuberu. They are ambivalent figures: feared by those who see them, yet capable of helping villages and people lost in the woods.

The myth

According to Asturian tradition, they appear as old women with wrinkled faces and long white hair, living in caves along the rivers. They take their name from their habit of washing clothes on the banks, beating the garments with wooden paddles while letting out harsh, unpleasant voices.

Folk belief warns that anyone who spots them may be seized and drowned in the river, since they dislike being disturbed. Yet they have a kinder side: when fire threatens a nearby village, they swing their paddles with startling force so the river floods the area and puts it out. Likewise, when a storm approaches, they come to the aid of children and elderly people lost in the forest, then vanish into the foam of the whirlpools.

Trivia

Unexpected help. Despite their dangerous reputation, they are said to put out fires in villages near the river by raising its water with their paddles.
Protectors of the lost. When a storm is coming, tradition says they rescue lost children and elderly people in the forest before disappearing into the whirlpools.
