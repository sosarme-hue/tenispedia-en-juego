Aztec goddess of waters, rivers and childbirth

Chalchiuhtlicue

Chalchiuhtlicue, "She of the Jade Skirt", is the Aztec goddess of lakes, rivers, seas and storms, wife or sister of Tlaloc and patroness of childbirth. She mattered because she governed the waters on which farming and daily life in central Mexico depended.

The myth

According to Aztec tradition, Chalchiuhtlicue presided over the Fourth Sun, the age of Water. During that time the sky filled with water and, after fifty-two years of rain, a great flood destroyed that world and its people were turned into fish. Some versions say the goddess built a bridge between heaven and earth that only those in her favour were allowed to cross.

The sources disagree about her family. For some she is the wife of Tlaloc and mother of the moon god Tecciztecatl; for others she is Tlaloc's sister or the wife of Xiuhtecuhtli. In a Tlaxcalan tradition she appears as Matlalcueitl, "She of the Green Skirts", the old name of the mountain now called La Malinche.

Worship and religion

She presided over the day 5 Serpent and the trecena of 1 Reed. Five of the twenty great festivals of the calendar were dedicated to her and to Tlaloc; priests dived into a lake, imitating the movements and croaking of frogs, to bring rain. She also watched over births: midwives invoked her when bathing newborns and gave the baby a second bath and a name four days later.

Legacy

Her name survives in the Nahuatl name Chalchiuhtlicueyecatl, the name once given to the Gulf of Mexico. Many of her sculptures were carved from green stone, and a monolithic statue of about twenty tons, attributed to her, was found near the Pyramid of the Moon at Teotihuacan and is now displayed in the National Museum of Anthropology in Mexico City.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Borgia, Borbonicus and Ríos codices and in the Florentine Codex; Bernardino de Sahagún describes her in his work on the Mexica.

Trivia

A gulf with her name. The Gulf of Mexico was once called Chalchiuhtlicueyecatl in Nahuatl, in honour of the goddess.
A bridge over the flood. According to some accounts, the goddess built a bridge between heaven and earth that saved those in her favour while others became fish.
