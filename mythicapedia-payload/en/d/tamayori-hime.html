Japanese goddess, mother of Emperor Jimmu

Tamayori-hime

Tamayori-hime is a goddess of Japanese mythology, daughter of the sea-dragon god Watatsumi and younger sister of Toyotama-hime. She is considered the mother of Emperor Jimmu, the first emperor of Japan according to tradition, and her figure has been linked to shamanic priestesses who received the spirits of the gods.

The myth

According to the myth, Toyotama-hime gave birth to her son Ugayafukiaezu on the shore and then returned to the sea, leaving the child in the care of her sister. Tamayori-hime raised him and, once he grew up, married him, her nephew. The Kojiki presents Tamayori-hime arriving later to look after the child, while the Nihon Shoki has her accompany her sister from the start.

Their union produced four children, among them Itsuse, who was killed by an arrow during the eastern expedition, Inai, who according to some traditions became a god of the plough, and the youngest, who would become Emperor Jimmu.

Worship and religion

In Kyushu and southern Japan there are numerous shrines dedicated to her, such as Miyaura Shrine in Nichinan City, said to be her residence. In tradition she is revered as ancestor of the Kaijin tribe, linked to the dragon god Ryujin.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki record her story and that of her children, with variations between the two texts.
