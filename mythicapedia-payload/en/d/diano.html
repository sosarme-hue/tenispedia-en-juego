mocking spirit of Galician, Asturian and Leonese folklore

Diaño

The diaño is a mocking spirit or sprite from the folklore of Galicia, Asturias and León, related to the Castilian martinico but with its own traits. It is best known for the tricks it plays on travellers out at night.

The myth

The diaño can take the shape of a horse, a cow, a ram or any other domestic animal, and even that of a human baby. It is active at night, leading astray anyone searching for lost livestock, pestering the miller who grinds by moonlight and mocking young people who return late from a festival.

Its pranks are varied. A white donkey offers itself as a mount to a traveller and, once he climbs on, keeps growing without end. A horse gallops him off on a hellish ride until he is brought back to where he started, or throws him headfirst into the river. A little goat, half frozen, is taken home to dry by the fire and then mocks the very person who rescued it. There are also black dogs that chase the wayfarer, toads that outrun a horse and its rider, and naked babies playing in the snow, along with strange noises and mysterious lights.

Trivia

The white donkey. It is one of its best-known pranks: it offers itself as a mount and, once ridden, keeps growing without limit.
The mocking kid goat. A goat chilled by the cold, taken home and dried by the fire, ends up mocking the person who helped it.
