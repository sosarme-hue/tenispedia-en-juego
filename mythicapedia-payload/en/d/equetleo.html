Athenian hero of the Battle of Marathon

Echetlus

Echetlus is a hero of the Battle of Marathon who, according to Athenian tradition, fought the Persians with a plough handle. His story shows how the Greeks believed heroes could step into war.

The myth

According to the account, a man who looked like a farmer appeared among the Athenians during the battle and struck down many Persians with a plough handle, in Greek echetle. Once the fighting ended, he vanished, and the Athenians could not find him anywhere.

When they consulted the oracle at Delphi, they were ordered to honour the hero Echetlaeus, whose name is tied to that tool. His appearance is read as an epiphany, a hero visiting the present, and it fits a period when hero cult still sat alongside the new gods of the Classical age, as Pfister explains.

Worship and religion

After the consultation at Delphi, the Athenians gave him honours as a hero. The sources do not describe specific temples or rites.

Legacy

Panaenus painted him in the Stoa Poikile in Athens, among the heroes of Marathon, alongside Callimachus and Miltiades. The painting shows how important he was to the Athenians.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records his story and the oracle's answer; the text also mentions Herodotus giving a different version of the warrior and his weapon.

Trivia

A name about ploughing. His name means something like 'he of the plough handle'. He is compared with Bouzyges, a hero regarded as the founder of agriculture, who was shown with oxen and a plough.
The Painted Porch. The Stoa Poikile, or Painted Porch, was one of the most famous sites in Athens for its war paintings and the loot on display.
