Greek hero, son of Helios and Rhodos

Macareus

Macareus, also known as Macar, is a hero of Greek mythology, a son of the god Helios and the nymph Rhodos, and one of the Heliadae. His story, tied first to the island of Rhodes and later to Lesbos, shows the rivalries and the sharing of gifts among the children of the Sun.

The myth

According to tradition, Macareus grew up on Rhodes with his many brothers, the Heliadae, and his mother Rhodos. When the Telchines fled the island to escape destruction at the hands of Zeus, it was this family that took over its rule. His father had taught him astrology and metalworking, and he excelled in both arts.

However, his brother Tenages came to surpass him in these skills. Jealous, Macareus plotted with other brothers to murder him. The crime was discovered, and the guilty brothers had to leave Rhodes, with Macareus going into exile on the island of Lesbos. Among his descendants are named Mitylene, Methymna, Antissa and Eresus, the names of cities on Lesbos.

He should not be confused with Macareus, son of Crinacus, who some sources present as a legendary king of Lesbos. The two figures have often been merged, which is why they sometimes appear as one and the same person.

Trivia

Children with city names. Among the children attributed to Macareus are Mitylene, Methymna, Antissa and Eresus, all of them names of cities on the island of Lesbos.
Brothers who stayed behind. According to the account, two of the Heliadae, Ochimus and Cercaphus, kept out of the plot and remained on Rhodes.
