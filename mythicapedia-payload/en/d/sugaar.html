Basque god of storms and thunder

Sugaar

Sugaar is a male deity of pre-Christian Basque mythology, linked to storms and thunder. He is the consort of the goddess Mari and the father of Atarrabi, the kindly son, and Mikelats, the wicked one.

The myth

According to tradition, Sugaar can take human form, but he is usually shown as a serpent or dragon, and sometimes as pure fire or lightning. He dwells in caves and mountains, such as Amunda and Atarreta in Ataun, or the Baltzola cave in Dima. Every Friday he is said to visit Mari at Anboto: some versions say he does her hair, others that together they create thunderstorms.

His character varies by region. In Betelu he is an evil spirit, while in Ataun he serves as a bogeyman. One legend from Dima tells of two brothers who found him in the Baltzola cave, where the younger cut off his tail with a stone. Years later a man led the elder back to the cave and gave him a box of gold and a red belt for his brother. When the younger refused the belt and tied it to a chestnut tree, the tree burst into flames, and they understood the stranger had been Sugaar. In Biscayan tradition, a Scottish princess sheltering in Mundaca bore Jaun Zuria to him, the mythical first lord of Biscay.

Trivia

Name of a serpent. His name is usually derived from Basque words meaning serpent and male, that is, male serpent. The idea that it comes from fire and flame is considered folk etymology.
Weekly visits. According to tradition, every Friday he goes to Anboto to visit Mari, a meeting some versions describe as intimate.
