South Indian Hindu goddess of rain

Mariamman

Mariamman, often shortened to Amman, is a Hindu goddess of weather and rain, venerated mainly in the rural areas of South India. She is seen as a form of Mahadevi and an aspect of Kali or Durga, and many villages regard her as their guardian deity.

The myth

There is no single, consistent legend about her origin, only many oral stories from different regional traditions. One of the most widespread concerns Renuka, wife of the sage Jamadagni and mother of Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu. After losing her powers when she was distracted by a glimpse of something, her husband ordered their son to kill her. Parashurama beheaded her, and when he tried to restore her, he accidentally swapped her head with that of a low-caste woman. Renuka then became Mariamman, the changed mother.

According to the Vanniyars, an agrarian community, Draupadi, the shared wife of the Pandavas, was an incarnation of the goddess Shakti. During her husbands' exile she visited villages in a fierce form, and the Vanniyars offered her prayers and barley, which turned her into Mariamman, protector against rain and disease.

Worship and religion

Her worship centres on bringing rain and curing illnesses such as smallpox, cholera and chickenpox. In the hot months, devotees walk long distances carrying pots of water mixed with turmeric and neem leaves. The favoured offering is pongal, a dish of rice and green gram cooked in earthen pots, and festivals include folk dances, night processions with oil lamps, fire walking and mouth or nose piercing. Her cult remains alive: the Samayapuram Mariamman Temple holds a ten-day festival every April.

Legacy

The sources record no prominent presence in modern art or literature. Her name combines mari, meaning rain in Tamil, and amman, meaning mother, and appears in variants such as Marikambe in Karnataka and Mari Aai in Maharashtra.

Ancient sources: Tamil Sangam literature describes the rites performed by Kuravar priestesses at the shrine of Palamutircholai.

Trivia

Pongal. The favourite offering to her is pongal, a dish of rice and green gram cooked in earthen pots.
Symbolic sacrifice. In some villages chickens and goats are offered to the goddess, but the animals are not killed; they are sold after the offering.
