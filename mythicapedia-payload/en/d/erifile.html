wife of Amphiaraus and mother of Alcmaeon in Greek mythology

Eriphyle

Eriphyle is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of King Talaus of Argos and wife of the seer Amphiaraus. She is remembered above all for betraying her husband for a necklace, an act that helped set off the war of the Seven against Thebes.

The myth

According to the tradition, Polynices, son of Oedipus, wanted to regain the Theban throne and won over Eriphyle to persuade her husband Amphiaraus to join the expedition. Amphiaraus, a seer, knew the campaign was doomed, but he had sworn to accept whatever she decided in any dispute, an oath made with his brother-in-law Adrastus. Polynices offered her the Necklace of Harmonia, and she ultimately pushed him into war.

Before dying, Amphiaraus told his sons Alcmaeon and Amphilochus to avenge him by killing their mother. Alcmaeon obeyed, and the Erinyes pursued him across Greece. An oracle told him he would be freed once he reached land that had not existed when he committed the crime; he reached a new island formed by the river Achelous, which purified him.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions her in the Odyssey as a woman who sold her husband for gold, and Virgil places her in the Underworld, still bearing her son's wounds, in the Aeneid. Statius takes her up in the Thebaid.

Trivia

The Necklace of Harmonia. The necklace, a wedding gift to Harmonia, passed through many hands and was also called the necklace of Eriphyle because it was the bribe she received.
A necklace on Cyprus. In the time of Pausanias a relic said to be this necklace was shown at Amathus on Cyprus, though he doubted it was the one Homer mentions.
