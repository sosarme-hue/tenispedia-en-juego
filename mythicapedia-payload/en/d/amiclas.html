king of Sparta and eponymous founder of Amyclae

Amyclas

In Greek myth, Amyclas is a king of Sparta and the eponymous founder of Amyclae, a town in Laconia that took its name from him. He matters because he links the Spartan royal line to several genealogies and to the tragic death of his son Hyacinth.

The myth

According to the myth, Amyclas was the son of King Lacedaemon and Queen Sparta, and the brother of Eurydice, whom Acrisius married according to the Library attributed to Apollodorus. With Diomede, daughter of Lapithes, he fathered Cynortas and Hyacinth. Apollo loved Hyacinth, but killed him by accident while throwing the discus.

When Amyclas died, the throne passed to Argalus, his eldest son, and after Argalus died, to Cynortas. In some versions Cynortas succeeded his father directly. Harpalus, another son, had a distant descendant named Patreus, who gave his name to the city of Patras.

Accounts differ on Amyclas's daughters. Some name Leaneira, wife of Arcas, whom other versions call Laodamia; she is said to have borne Triphylus to her husband. Hegesandra married Argius, son of Pelops. Polyboea might also be his daughter, though she appears only as Hyacinth's sister, and one version makes Daphne Amyclas's daughter.

Ancient sources: The Library attributed to Apollodorus records his genealogy, his succession to the throne and the death of Hyacinth.
