legendary king of Argos, seventh of his line

Triopas

Triopas is, in Greek mythology, the seventh king of Argos and a member of the house of Phoroneus. Some authors regard him as a human aspect of the Argive Zeus, who was sometimes shown with a third eye.

The myth

According to the genealogy preserved by Hyginus, Triopas was the son of Piranthus and Callirhoe, and brother of Argus. Other versions make him the son of Phorbas and Euboea, and in that case his children would be Pelasgus, Iasus, Agenor and a daughter, Messene, who gave her name to Messenia.

The sources disagree about who succeeded him: some name Iasus, others Agenor, and Pausanias adds Crotopus, Agenor's son. Eusebius gives him a reign of forty-six years and places him in the same era as Prometheus, Epimetheus, Atlas and Io. A scholiast credits Pelasgus with founding a sanctuary of Demeter Pelasgis, near the temple of Hera Antheia.

Ancient sources: Hyginus in his Fabulae, Eusebius of Caesarea and Pausanias, together with ancient scholia on classical texts, record his genealogies and reign.

Trivia

A name with three eyes. The popular etymology of his name links it to the Greek for "he who has three eyes", although its ending suggests a origin older than Greek.
