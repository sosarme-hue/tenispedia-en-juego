legendary predecessor of the Kingdom of Aragon

Kingdom of Sobrarbe

The Kingdom of Sobrarbe is a legendary realm that Aragonese tradition presented as the forerunner of the Kingdom of Aragon. Born from the legend of the Reconquista, it explained the origins of the kingdom's liberties and gave its name to the modern comarca of Sobrarbe.

The myth

According to the late medieval legend, after the Muslim arrival, local Christians gathered in the Espelunga de Galión cave in 724, on the site of today's monastery of San Juan de la Peña. There they chose García Ximéniz as their leader and took Aínsa, which became the capital.

When the Muslims counterattacked with four times as many troops, a vivid red cross appeared in the sky above an oak on a golden field. The Christians took it as a divine sign, won the battle, and García Ximéniz founded a hermitage dedicated to John the Baptist, the origin of the monastery. The kingdom took the name Sobrarbe from the phrase sobre arbre, meaning 'upon the tree'.

Legacy

The red cross on an oak tree on a gold field was added to the Aragonese coat of arms. The legend was embraced by Aragonese tradition in the fifteenth century and given a historicising account by Lucio Marineo Sículo (1509). The Fueros de Sobrarbe fed the foralism movement, a legal current defending Aragonese liberties against the king, which cited the legend in lawsuits in the 1580s.

Ancient sources: Gualberto Fabricio de Vagad, in his Crónica de Aragón (1499), and Lucio Marineo Sículo, in De Aragoniae Regibus (1509), record and order the legend.

Trivia

Origin of the name. The name Sobrarbe is explained by the Latin phrase super arborem, which the legend reads as 'above the tree', referring to the cross that appeared over the oak.
A twentieth king. The chronicler Gualberto Fabricio de Vagad numbered the kings of Aragon as a continuation of those of Sobrarbe, so Alfonso III of Aragon appears as the twentieth king of Sobrarbe.
