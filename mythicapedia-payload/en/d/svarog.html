Slavic god of fire and smithing

Svarog

Svarog is a Slavic god associated with fire, smithing and the sun. Russian chronicles equate him with Hephaestus and Helios, which shows how he was understood within early Slavic religion.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the Primary Chronicle, Svarog is the father of Dazhbog and Svarozhich. The chronicle presents him as the Slavic counterpart of Hephaestus, the Greek god of the forge, which links him to fire and metalwork. The texts recount no particular deeds for him and no ending.

His name also points to fire: the most widely accepted etymology connects it to a root meaning heat and light. Svarozhich, by contrast, appears in the Hypatian Codex identified with Helios, a role other texts give to Dazhbog, and in one Russian sermon he is reduced to a ghost of fire. Some scholars think the two are the same god, with the diminutive meaning a young one or a son; others reject this.

Worship and religion

The sources place him among the gods worshipped by the Slavs, but they do not describe his temples, festivals or rites. Among the South Slavs his role partly passed to Dazhbog, and in Rus' the cult of the Varangian Perun seems to have displaced that of Svarog. There is no evidence of a living cult.

Legacy

After Christianization his name survived in place names and vocabulary. Bulgaria has towns called Svarog and the Czech Republic has Mount Svaroh. In the Novgorod dialect the word svarog, meaning 'fire' or 'blacksmith', was preserved, and the Romanian sfarog, 'charred', probably came from a South Slavic language.

Ancient sources: The Primary Chronicle and the Hypatian Codex mention him, along with Thietmar of Merseburg, Bruno of Querfurt and the sermon Slovo někoego christoljubca.

Trivia

Doubtful place names. Brückner linked several Polish place names to Svarog, but the idea is doubtful. The modern form Swarożyn is considered a false transcription.
A name that sounds like a quarrel. Brückner proposed that svar means 'dispute' and translated the name as 'brawler', an idea criticized today.
