Roman goddess who guided children and brides home

Domiduca

Domiduca is a goddess of Roman mythology who protected children on their way back to their parents' home. Her male counterpart was Domitius, Domidius or Domiducus, and both were also gods of marriage.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Domiduca watched over little ones as they returned to the family home. Her name comes from domus, 'house', and eo, ire, itum, 'to go', so it conveys the idea of leading someone back home.

Domiduca and Domiducus also had a role in weddings: they accompanied the bride's procession, and the couple reached their new home together on the wedding night. The names also appear as epithets of Jupiter and Juno. Once the bride had been led home, the god Domitius was invoked to settle her into her new house.

Worship and religion

Her role was felt mostly at the wedding, where the two gods escorted the bridal procession to the new home. The sources do not describe temples or festivals of their own.

Trivia

Epithets of the great gods. The names Domiduca and Domiducus appear as epithets of Jupiter and Juno, showing that their domestic role was also associated with the chief deities.
A name meaning 'leading home'. Her name combines the Latin roots for 'house' and 'to go', fitting her role of guiding someone back home.
