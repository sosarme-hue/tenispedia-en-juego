demon of Iranian and Armenian mythology

Azhdahak

Azhdahak is the Armenian form of the name of Azhi Dahaka, a demon of the Avesta, the sacred book of Zoroastrianism, and a key figure in Iranian mythology. His story concerns the end of time and also passed into Armenian tradition, where he was linked to a historical king.

The myth

According to Iranian tradition, Azhdahak remains imprisoned in Mount Damavand, but at the end of time he will break free. He will then be finally defeated by Fereydun, the hero who ends his threat.

In his History of Armenia, the writer Movses Khorenatsi identified him with Astyages, king of the Medes. The name Astyages, a Greek transliteration of an old Iranian form, resembled Azhdahak, and the name of the Medes, pronounced in Armenian, sounded like an Iranian word meaning snake. The Armenian form Azhdahak was borrowed from Parthian.

Ancient sources: The Avesta, where the demon Azhi Dahaka appears, and Movses Khorenatsi's History of Armenia, which identifies him with Astyages.

Trivia

A king turned into a serpent. Movses Khorenatsi linked him with Astyages, king of the Medes, in an identification that mixes history with legend.
