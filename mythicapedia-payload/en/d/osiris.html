Egyptian god of fertility, resurrection and the afterlife

Osiris

Osiris is one of the most important gods of the Egyptian pantheon, lord of the dead and the underworld, and a symbol of vegetation and the Nile floods. His story of death and resurrection was central to Egyptian religion and explained the hope of life after death.

The myth

According to the Osiris myth, Osiris was a good king who ruled Egypt with his wife Isis. His brother Set, jealous of his power, tricked him into lying in a specially made chest, sealed it and threw it into the Nile.

Isis, helped by her sister Nephthys, searched Egypt for the scattered pieces of his body. She gathered them, wrapped him and, through magic, briefly restored him to life, which allowed her to conceive Horus. Osiris did not return to the world of the living; instead he became sovereign of the afterlife, where he judges the dead according to Maat. Horus avenged his father by defeating Set.

Ancient texts differ on details: in some versions the body was dismembered and a part, the genitals, was never recovered. This symbolism of death and renewal linked him to the cycles of nature, such as the sprouting of crops and the annual flooding of the Nile.

Worship and religion

Abydos became the main centre of his cult during the Middle Kingdom, where his head was said to be kept as a sacred relic that drew pilgrims during New Year festivals. From the first millennium BCE his worship spread across Egypt. In Memphis, Greek settlers adopted the Osiris-Apis form, and the Ptolemies later promoted Serapis in Alexandria. After the Roman conquest, his cult reached Rome and its empire, where it lasted until Christianity spread. The temple of Isis on Philae, the last Egyptian sanctuary, was closed around 530 CE by order of Justinian.

Legacy

Osiris shaped Greco-Roman ideas about death and rebirth through the figure of Serapis. His name is tied to the theme of resurrection and he remains one of the most studied gods of Egypt in modern Egyptology and popular culture.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, the Shabaka Stone, The Contendings of Horus and Seth and, later, Plutarch and Diodorus Siculus.

Trivia

The head of Abydos. Abydos kept a sacred relic identified as the god's head, one of the reasons for the city's prestige.
A colour of rebirth. He was shown with green skin, the colour of rebirth, or black, alluding to the fertility of the Nile valley.
