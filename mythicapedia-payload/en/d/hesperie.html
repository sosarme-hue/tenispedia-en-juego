Greek nymph, daughter of the river god Cebren

Hesperia

Hesperia is, in Greek mythology, a nymph and the daughter of the river god Cebren. Her tragic story with the Trojan hero Aesacus makes her one of the lesser-known figures in the world of Greek nymphs and rivers.

The myth

Aesacus, a Trojan hero, fell in love with Hesperia, but she fled to escape his pursuit. During her flight, a serpent bit her and she died.

In despair over her loss, Aesacus tried to take his own life by throwing himself into the sea. Thetis, however, softened his fall, so he did not die. According to the tradition, he was transformed into a diving grebe, and so his story remained forever tied to the sea and to the lost nymph.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions her in the Metamorphoses, Book XI, where he recounts the fate of Aesacus.
