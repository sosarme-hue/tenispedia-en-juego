blue monk ghost of Japanese folklore

Aobōzu

The aobōzu is a yōkai, a Japanese spirit, typically depicted as a blue-skinned Buddhist monk. It appears in many regional legends and in traditional Japanese art, especially in Toriyama Sekien's bestiary.

The myth

Accounts of the aobōzu vary widely from place to place. In many versions it is a large humanoid figure resembling a Buddhist priest, though the legends disagree about its actions and the nature of its hauntings.

In Nagano, a person who stops breathing and spins seven times at the base of a certain pine tree may hear the spirit ask that the rocks not be trampled and the pine not be snapped. In Shizuoka, children who come home late at sunset and run through a wheat field may be abducted by one rising from the grain. In Okayama it appears in vacant houses with a blue body or clothing, and in Yamagata it stares up from the toilet of an elementary school.

Legacy

The aobōzu appears in Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yakō, where it is probably shown as a one-eyed priest beside a thatched hut. Some scholars suggest the blue colour may have hinted at an insufficiently trained priest, since the Japanese word for blue can also mean immaturity.
