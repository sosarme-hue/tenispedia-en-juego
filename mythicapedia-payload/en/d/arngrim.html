Norse berserker warrior

Arngrim

Arngrim is a Viking warrior of Norse mythology, known as a berserker, who appears in the Saga of Hervor and in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum. He matters mainly because he links the cursed sword Tyrfing to a large family of warrior sons.

The myth

According to Hervarar saga, Arngrim raided Garðaríki and met King Svafrlami, who owned the sword Tyrfing. When the blade split Arngrim's shield, he cut off the king's hand, seized the sword and killed him with it. He then forced the king's daughter Eyfura to marry him.

In Saxo Grammaticus, by contrast, Arngrim asks for the hand of Eyfura, daughter of the Danish king Frodi, who refuses. To earn his respect he must defeat two kings: he subdues the Sami, who pay a tribute of reindeer, and kills Egther of Bjarmaland in single combat.

The couple lived on Bolmsö and had twelve berserker sons, whose names vary by source.

Legacy

His name survives in the medieval Telemark ballad The Restless Men, whose refrain speaks of the sons of Arngrim of the north. Several Faroese ballads also recall him.

Ancient sources: Hervarar saga, Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum and the Lay of Hyndla in the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A sword that splits shields. In Hervarar saga, the sword Tyrfing cuts through Arngrim's shield and sinks into the ground.
Reindeer tribute. According to Saxo, the Sami were forced to pay a cartload of reindeer every third year after their defeat.
