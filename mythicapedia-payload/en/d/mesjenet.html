Egyptian goddess of childbirth and destiny

Meskhenet

Meskhenet is an ancient Egyptian goddess linked to childbirth, infancy and fate. According to tradition, she created the ka, or life force, of each child in the womb and breathed it into them at birth, which made her a central figure for Egyptian mothers.

The myth

According to Egyptian accounts, Meskhenet was the wife of Herishef and took part in the making of the world. She laid out the foundations of the universe and the primeval mound on which the world was built, as well as the foundations of sacred buildings. Her most intimate work took place in the womb, where she shaped the child's ka, the part of the person that was linked to their destiny.

Tradition also connects her with the afterlife. In the Hall of the Two Truths, during the Judgment of Osiris, she accompanied the deceased and recounted their life before the gods.

In art she appears as a woman with two long, curved plants, as a brick with a human head, or as a cow with the uraeus. Her symbol was a heifer's uterus. The birth brick is among her most distinctive images, recalling the custom of giving birth in a squatting position on two bricks.

Worship and religion

She was venerated from the earliest times, above all in Memphis and Herakleopolis Magna, where she received worship alongside other local deities.

Ancient sources: The Westcar Papyrus mentions her in its last tale, which tells of the birth of the first three kings of the Fifth Dynasty.

Trivia

Three kings in one birth. In the Westcar Papyrus, Meskhenet appears after each of the three sons said to be born at once, and prophesies that they will become kings of Egypt: Userkaf, Sahure and Neferirkare Kakai.
Birth bricks. Egyptian women gave birth while squatting on two bricks, which is why the goddess was sometimes shown as a brick with a human head.
