rat vahana of the Hindu god Ganesha

Mushaka

Mushaka is the mouse or rat that serves as the vahana, or mount, of the Hindu god Ganesha, who is often shown riding on it or standing beside its feet. It matters because it turns an animal usually seen as a pest into the companion of one of Hinduism's most beloved deities.

The myth

According to one tradition, Mushaka was originally a gandharva, a heavenly musician named Krauncha. He accidentally stepped on the foot of the sage Vamadeva, who cursed him to become a giant rat. As a rat he damaged fields and hermitages, until he entered the ashram of the sage Parashara, who called on Ganesha for help. Ganesha caught the rat with his noose, the pasha. Defeated, the rat offered to serve him, and Ganesha accepted it as his vehicle. Worried that it could not carry his weight, the rat was relieved when Ganesha made himself lighter.

Other accounts identify him with Mushikasura, a demon defeated by Ganesha who then became his mount. In a South Indian tradition, the elephant-faced demon Gajamukhasura broke one of Ganesha's tusks in battle; the god hurled the tusk at him and turned him into a rat. In another story, a rat drawn by the smell of a modaka, a sweet linked to Ganesha, nibbled at the offering, became immortal and then served the god.

Worship and religion

At some Ganesha temples, devotees whisper their wishes into the ear of a statue of Mushaka, believing that the rat will carry them to the god. A similar practice is reported at the Huai Khwang Ganesha Shrine in Bangkok.

Ancient sources: The mouse as a mount first appears in the Matsya Purana, and later in the Brahmananda Purana and Ganesha Purana. The Ganapati Atharvashirsa mentions it in a meditation verse, and the Ganesha Sahasranama records its names, 'mouse-mount' and 'rat-banner'.

Trivia

From pest to mount. According to the sources, the rat was seen as a pest that damaged crops, and its subjection symbolizes Ganesha's mastery over obstacles.
Whispers to the rat. Devotees entrust their wishes to the ear of Mushaka's statue so that it will carry them to Ganesha.
