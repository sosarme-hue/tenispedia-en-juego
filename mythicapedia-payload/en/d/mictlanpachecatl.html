Aztec god of the north wind

Mictlanpachecatl

Mictlanpachecatl is the Mexica god of the north wind, one of four personifications of the winds that blow from the cardinal directions in Aztec mythology.

The myth

In Mexica tradition, Mictlanpachecatl belongs to a group of four wind gods, each linked to a direction of the world. His brothers are Cihuatecayotl, Tlalocayotl and Huitztlampaehecatl, who personify the winds of the west, east and south.

All of them served Ehecatl, the wind god and an aspect of Quetzalcoatl, yet they also answered to the lords of each quarter: Tezcatlipoca in the north, Xipetotec in the east, Quetzalcoatl in the west and Huitzilopochtli in the south. Sources do not fully agree on which wind belongs to which direction, and they say little about specific episodes in his own story.
