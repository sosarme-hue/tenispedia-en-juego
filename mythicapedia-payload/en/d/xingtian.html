Chinese deity of the indomitable spirit

Xingtian

Xingtian is a deity in Chinese mythology who, after losing a struggle for supreme power, was beheaded yet kept fighting. His figure has become a symbol of defiance that refuses to surrender in the face of adversity.

The myth

According to tradition, Xingtian was an official of the Yan Emperor, who contested the position of Supreme Divinity with the Huang Emperor. Yan lost, but Xingtian carried on the struggle after his lord's defeat. Defeated in turn, he was beheaded by Huang, who buried his head on Changyang Mountain.

Yet Xingtian did not give up. Headless, with a shield in one hand and a battle axe in the other, he used his nipples as eyes and his navel as a mouth, and kept challenging his enemy through a martial dance. The Shanhaijing, or Classic of Mountains and Seas, chapter 7, tells how the supreme god defeated him and buried his head. Other accounts present him as a minister who composed music to help farmers plough and harvest.

Legacy

The poet Tao Qian wrote verses about him in his Thirteen Poems upon Reading the Guideways through Mountains and Seas, and the scholar Guo Pu praised his defiant spirit in an encomium. In popular culture he appears as a playable character in the video game Smite, as a spirit ally in Shaman King: Red Crimson, and as a Chinese titan in Age of Mythology: Retold.

Ancient sources: The Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas), chapter 7, and the Huainanzi, which calls him the corpse of Xingcan.

Trivia

Eyes on the chest. Headless, Xingtian used his nipples as eyes and his navel as a mouth, an image that stands out among descriptions in early Chinese literature.
Compared with other rebels. Guo Pu linked him to the corpse of Geng of the Xia, who also regenerated and kept resisting, while Tao Qian connected him with Jingwei.
