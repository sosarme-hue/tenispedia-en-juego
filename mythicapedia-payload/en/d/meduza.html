sea creature of Russian folklore

Meduza

Meduza (also Meluza or Meluzina) is a mythical creature of Russian folklore, depicted as a sea monster with the head of a maiden and the body of a beast, and also regarded as a deity of deception. She appears in a Russian lubok of the 17th or 18th century.

The myth

According to tradition, Meduza is a sea monster with the head of a beautiful dark-haired maiden, who wears a crown. Her body and belly are those of a striped beast, and her tail is a dragon's, ending in a serpent's mouth. She also has legs resembling an elephant's, each ending in a serpent's mouth.

Popular belief held that these serpent mouths contained a deadly dragon poison. She was said to live in the sea near the Ethiopian abyss, or in the Western Ocean, depending on the version. She was described as half woman and half serpent, or as half woman and half fish, reflecting the variety of accounts of her form.

Trivia

A lubok. Her image was captured in a Russian lubok, a popular print from the 17th or 18th century.
Poisonous mouths. According to popular belief, the serpent mouths on her body held a deadly dragon poison.
