Greek hero of the Trojan War, companion of Achilles

Patroclus

Patroclus is one of the Greek heroes of the Trojan War and the dearest friend of Achilles. His death at the hands of Hector, told in the Iliad, is the event that brings Achilles back into battle.

The myth

According to tradition, Patroclus was the son of Menoetius, one of the Argonauts, and was born in Opus. The sources disagree about his mother, who might be Sthenele, Periopis, Polymele, Philomela or Damocratea, depending on the author. Apollodorus tells that, in Opus, he killed a boy during a dispute over a game of dice. He had to flee with his father and was taken in by Peleus, king of Phthia, where he grew up alongside Achilles and became his inseparable companion.

In the Iliad, when the Trojans threaten to burn the Greek ships, Patroclo persuades Achilles to let him fight at the head of the Myrmidons. Achilles lends him his armour so the Trojans will mistake him for the hero. Patroclus drives the enemy back to the walls of Troy, disobeying the order to return, and kills many warriors, among them Sarpedon, son of Zeus. Apollo strips him of his wits, Euphorbus wounds him, and Hector runs him through with a spear. Menelaus and Ajax protect his body, and Achilles, stirred by his friend's death, returns to the fighting and defeats Hector.

After the funeral, the ghost of Patroclus appears to Achilles and asks him to burn his body as soon as possible so he can cross into Hades. Achilles holds funeral games, with chariot races and wrestling contests, and orders the pyre built. Tradition adds that the ashes of both heroes were buried together in a golden urn by the Hellespont.

Legacy

His name, meaning glory of his father, is the source of the Latinised form Patroclus. A variant with the same elements in reverse order, Kleopatros, gave rise to the feminine name Cleopatra. In English, the usual stress on the second syllable of the name comes from Alexander Pope, who chose it in his translation of Homer for metrical reasons. From antiquity, some authors read his bond with Achilles as a love affair, and modern scholars still debate the question.

Ancient sources: He appears mainly in Homer's Iliad, and also in the Odyssey, the Cypria, Pindar's Olympian Odes and the work of Apollodorus.

Trivia

A game of dice. According to Apollodorus, the death of a boy during a game of dice forced Patroclus to flee from Opus.
Prizes at the funeral games. At the games Achilles organised, Diomedes won the chariot race and received a slave woman and a tripod, and Odysseus won the foot race.
