Hindu god of the Moon

Chandra

Chandra is the Hindu god of the Moon, associated with the night, plants and vegetation. He is one of the Navagraha, the nine planets of Hinduism, and in post-Vedic literature he is identified with Soma, the name of an ancient Vedic ritual drink and of the deity who represented it.

The myth

According to most epic texts and the Puranas, Chandra was the son of the sage Atri and his wife Anasuya, and the brother of Dattatreya and Durvasa. Other traditions give him a different origin: one text makes him a son of Dharma, and another names Prabhakar as his father. The Devi Bhagavata Purana even presents him as an avatar of Brahma.

One of the best-known tales tells of his love for Tara, the wife of Brihaspati, preceptor of the Devas. Tara came to Chandra's abode during her wanderings, and the two fell in love. Despite Brihaspati's efforts to win her back, she stayed with Chandra. Indra threatened war to force her return, but the Devas were divided, and the Asuras, led by Shukra, sided with the Moon. The conflict caused great cosmic turmoil until Brahma stepped in, rebuked Chandra and Shukra, and Chandra reluctantly returned Tara. She was already pregnant, so Brahma asked her directly who the father was. She named Chandra, and the child, Budha, was handed over to him. Budha later became a deity linked to intellect and wisdom.

Chandra is married to the twenty-seven daughters of the sage Daksha, who personify the nakshatras, or lunar mansions, and whose sequence reflects the lunar cycle. His special affection for Rohini provoked jealousy among the others, who complained to Daksha. Despite warnings, Chandra kept favouring her, so Daksha cursed him with kshayaroga, a consumption that also harmed medicinal plants. The Devas pleaded, and Daksha softened the curse: Chandra would be free of the disease for half of each month if he bathed in the Sarasvati Tirtha. In this way the myth explained the waxing and waning of the Moon.

In the story of the Churning of the Ocean, the demon Rahu disguised himself as a Brahmin and drank the nectar of immortality. Surya and Chandra exposed him to Vishnu, who severed his head with the Sudarshana Chakra. Because he had already drunk the nectar, his head and body became immortal as Rahu and Ketu. According to tradition, they periodically swallow the Sun and the Moon, which explains eclipses.

Legacy

Aldous Huxley took the name of the Vedic ritual drink soma for the drug that the state in his novel Brave New World uses to control its population. In Hindu astrology Chandra is one of the Navagraha, and his son Budha represents the planet Mercury.

Ancient sources: The Rig-veda uses Soma for both a drink and a deity, while the Ramayana, the Mahabharata and the Puranas present Chandra as a lunar god.

Trivia

The drug in Brave New World. Aldous Huxley took the name of the Vedic ritual drink soma for the dystopian drug the state uses to control its population.
Eclipses. According to the myth, Rahu and Ketu, born from the immortal head of the demon, periodically swallow the Sun and the Moon.
Phases explained by a curse. Chandra's periodic bathing in the Sarasvati Tirtha symbolises, in the story, the waxing and waning of the Moon.
