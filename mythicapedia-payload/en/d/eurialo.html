Greek hero, Achaean commander in the Trojan War

Euryalus

Euryalus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Mecisteus and Diomede, known for his role in the Trojan War told in the Iliad and for having been one of the Argonauts. He matters because he appears in two major heroic cycles of the Greek tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, Euryalus was one of the Argonauts who accompanied Jason in the quest for the Golden Fleece. Later, in the Trojan War, the Iliad mentions him three times. In the Catalogue of Ships he appears alongside Diomedes and Sthenelus as leader of the Achaean contingent from Argolis.

In Book VI he fights successfully, defeating Dresus, Opheltius, Aesepus and Pedasus. His third appearance comes at the funeral games for Patroclus: encouraged by Diomedes, he faces Epeius in boxing, but is defeated.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, which names him in the Catalogue of Ships, in Book VI and in the funeral games for Patroclus.
