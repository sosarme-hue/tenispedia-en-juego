Norse god, father of Frigg

Fjörgynn

Fjörgynn is, in Norse mythology, the father of Frigg, wife of Odin. He has few stories of his own and is linked to the earth, probably as the masculine form of Fjörgyn, another name for the goddess Jörð.

The myth

In Norse sources, Fjörgynn appears mainly as the father of Frigg. Snorri Sturluson presents him this way in the Prose Edda, within accounts of the family of the gods, but no lengthy stories centred on him have survived.

The feminine name Fjörgyn appears in the Poetic Edda, in Völuspá and Hárbarðsljóð, where she is named as the mother of Thor. Scholars suspect that both forms belonged to a divine pair about which little information has survived.

Another reading holds that Fjörgyn is simply another name for Jörð, the earth, and that the masculine form derives from her. Some researchers also connect the name with an earlier Indo-European thunder or rain god, though this is an academic hypothesis rather than something the texts transmit.

Ancient sources: Appears in the Poetic Edda (Völuspá, Hárbarðsljóð) and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál).

Trivia

A name linked to mountains. In Proto-Germanic, Fjörgyn is related to a word meaning mountain. Its Old High German cognate Firgunnea gave its name to the Ore Mountains, on the border between Germany and today's Czech Republic.
