Japanese spirit of the storytelling gatherings

Aoandon

The Aoandon is a spirit from Japanese folklore linked to the ghost-story gatherings of the Edo period. Its name means "blue paper lantern".

The myth

According to tradition, in the game known as Hyakumonogatari Kaidankai, a hundred people gathered with a lit candle set inside a blue-paper andon lamp and took turns telling ghost stories. After each tale they extinguished their lamp, and when the last one went out, the Aoandon appeared.

Accounts differ on its look. Some describe a blue-skinned man with two horns and sharp teeth, while others portray a woman with similar features. It is said to emerge only at night and to make the stories told come true.

Legacy

Toriyama Sekien drew it in his Konjaku Hyakki Shūi. It appears as a shikigami who loves storytelling in the mobile game Onmyōji, lends its name to the bioluminescent marine worm Polycirrus aoandon, and is a mini-boss in the side mission "The Yokai" in Assassin's Creed Shadows (2025).

Ancient sources: Toriyama Sekien's 18th-century compendium Konjaku Hyakki Shūi, which records the Aoandon among the yokai.

Trivia

Named after the light. The name comes from the blue-paper lamps used at these gatherings, chosen to create an eerie atmosphere.
Two versions of its form. Sources disagree on its appearance: some describe a man and others a woman, though both share blue skin and horns.
