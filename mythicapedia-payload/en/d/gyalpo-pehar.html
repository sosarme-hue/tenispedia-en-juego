guardian spirit of the Buddha's teachings

Gyalpo Pehar

Gyalpo Pehar, also spelled Pekar, is a protective spirit in Tibetan Buddhism, regarded as the guardian of the Buddha's teachings. He matters for his role as chief protector of Tibet's first monastery and for his link to the Nechung Oracle.

The myth

According to Tibetan Buddhist tradition, Gyalpo Pehar belongs to a class of spirits called gyalpo. When Padmasambhava arrived in Tibet in the eighth century, he subdued all the gyalpo spirits and placed them under Pehar's control, and Pehar promised not to harm any sentient being. Pehar was made chief guardian of Samye, the first monastery in Tibet, and leads a band of five gyalpo spirits.

According to legend, he protected Samye for some 700 years before moving to Nechung, near Drepung, during the time of the Fifth Dalai Lama. There he became the protector of the monastery in the seventeenth century. The spirit Tsiu Marpo later succeeded him at Samye.

Worship and religion

A temple within the Samye complex is dedicated to him and is known as Pekar lha khang. After the fall of the Western Xia, the arrival of Tangut refugees in Tibet led to his adoption into Tibetan Buddhism, and today he serves as the state oracle through the Nechung Oracle.

Trivia

A temple at Samye. A chapel within the Samye complex bears his name, Pekar lha khang, or Pekar Temple.
From protector to oracle. His figure entered Tibetan Buddhism after the arrival of Tangut refugees and eventually became the state oracle at Nechung.
