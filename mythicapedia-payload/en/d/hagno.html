Arcadian nymph, nurse of Zeus

Hagno

Hagno is an Arcadian nymph who, in Greek tradition, was one of the nurses that raised the god Zeus as a child. Her name means 'pure, chaste, holy', and her image appears in several places in the region.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Pausanias, Hagno was an Arcadian nymph who, together with other nymphs, looked after Zeus when he was a child. Her name, meaning 'pure, chaste, holy', reflects the purity of these nurses.

Pausanias lists several groups of nurses. On one occasion he names them as Tisoa, Neda and Hagno; on another, in the agora of Megalopolis, she appears alongside Neda, Anthracia, Anchirhoe and Myrtoessa, holding a water-pot in one hand and a bowl in the other. On the altar of Athena Alea at Tegea, Hagno is among the nymphs surrounding Rhea and the nymph Enoe, who hold the infant Zeus.

The names of other places recall these nymphs: Tisoa gave her name to a city in Parrhasia, Neda to a river, and Hagno to a spring on Mount Lycaeum whose flow stays the same in winter and summer.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his «Description of Greece», mentions her when describing Arcadia and Megalopolis.

Trivia

A steady spring. According to Pausanias, Hagno's spring on Mount Lycaeum has the same volume of water in winter as in summer, like the river Ister.
A nymph on an altar. Hagno is depicted on the altar of Athena Alea at Tegea, alongside Rhea, who holds the infant Zeus.
