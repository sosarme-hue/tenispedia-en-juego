dwarf of Norse mythology

Durin

Durin (also Durinn) is one of the first dwarves in Norse mythology, created alongside Mótsognir. J. R. R. Tolkien borrowed his name for several dwarf kings in his legendarium.

The myth

According to the Völuspá, Durin and Mótsognir came into being when Odin and his brothers Vili and Vé created the world from the body of the giant Ymir. At first they took the form of maggots feeding on the giant's dead flesh; the gods then gave them intelligence and human appearance, and they became the first of their kind.

The dwarves dwelt in Nidavellir, one of the nine worlds attached to the world tree Yggdrasil. To human eyes they were small and ugly, and they feared the light, which could turn them to stone. In the Hervarar saga, Durinn forged the magic sword Tyrfing with the help of the dwarf Dvalin.

Legacy

Tolkien took the name for Durin the Deathless, the eldest of the Seven Fathers of the dwarves in his works, and reused it for several dwarf kings.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) mention him among the dwarves created by the gods; the Hervarar saga recounts the forging of Tyrfing.

Trivia

A legendary sword. In the Hervarar saga, Durinn and Dvalin are credited with forging Tyrfing, a magic sword of great fame in the tradition.
Variant names. In some versions of the saga the character appears as Dulinn, showing that his name varied between texts.
