Mayan underworld

Xibalba

Xibalba is the underworld of Maya mythology, ruled by gods of death and disease. Its fullest account appears in the K'iche' Popol Vuh, where it is the setting for the trials faced by the Hero Twins.

The myth

According to the Popol Vuh, Xibalba was a court beneath the surface of the earth, ruled by lords such as Hun-Came («One Death») and Vucub-Came («Seven Death»), who dispensed sickness, hunger, fear and death. Reaching it meant descending steep stairs, crossing rivers of scorpions, blood and pus, and choosing among four roads. Only the black one led to the council hall.

There, visitors were greeted by mannequins seated beside the lords, and whoever greeted them was invited to sit on a bench that was actually a hot cooking surface. Six deadly houses followed, from the Dark House to the Hot House. The twins Hun-Hunahpu and Vucub-Hunahpu lost a ballgame in Xibalba and died. Their sons, Hunahpu and Ixbalanque, defeated the lords through cleverness and magic.

Worship and religion

According to the story, the lords of Xibalba once received human offerings from the surface and later accepted lesser ones. Dennis Tedlock has suggested the tale may be a K'iche' criticism of earlier forms of worship. In Yucatan, Diego de Landa described a dance linked to the underworld.

Legacy

Its name is associated with caves in Alta Verapaz, Guatemala, where local tradition places an entrance to the underworld. Some researchers propose that the Nahuatl word Mictlan may have been borrowed from Maya mythology.

Ancient sources: The K'iche' Popol Vuh, which narrates the twins' descent and the trials of Xibalba, and Diego de Landa's account of Yucatan.

Trivia

Four roads. Past the rivers, travellers reached a crossroads with four roads of different colours. Only the black one led to Xibalba.
Deceptive mannequins. Mannequins seated beside the lords were used to humiliate newcomers, who greeted them before being invited to sit on a hot bench.
