Pelopid hero of Greek mythology

Dias

Dias (also Diante) is a figure of Greek myth, one of the Pelopids included in Hesiod's genealogy of the Atreids. His importance is mainly genealogical: he was the father of Cleola, who appears as the mother or grandmother of Agamemnon.

The myth

According to a tradition preserved in a few scholia, Dias was a son of Pelops. No source names his mother, though Hippodamia is presumed to be the most likely candidate. He is described as a brother of Atreus and the father of a daughter, Cleola.

Cleola held a key place in the lineage. In the Catalogue of Women she is the wife of Atreus and mother of Plisthenes. Tzetzes, who gives the fullest account, says that according to Hesiod, Aeschylus and others, Agamemnon, Menelaus and Anaxibia were children of Cleola, daughter of Dias, and of Plisthenes, son of Atreus.

Stephanus of Byzantium gives another tradition: Dias was a son of a certain Abas and brother of Alcon and Aretusa, of whom little else is known. He is also credited with founding a city called Athens in Euboea, which he named after his homeland.

Ancient sources: Scholia, the Catalogue of Women and Tzetzes, who record the genealogy attributed to Hesiod and Aeschylus, plus Stephanus of Byzantium.

Trivia

An Athens in Euboea. Tradition credits Dias with founding a city called Athens on the island of Euboea, distinct from the great Attic Athens.
A figure with few mentions. Dias appears in only two scholia, leaving very little trace in the ancient texts.
