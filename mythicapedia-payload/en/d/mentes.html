king of the Taphians in Greek mythology

Mentes

Mentes is a king of the Taphians in Greek mythology and the son of Anchialus. He appears in the Odyssey, where the goddess Athena takes his form to advise Telemachus.

The myth

In the Odyssey, Athena presents herself to Telemachus disguised as Mentes, an old friend of the family of Odysseus. She arrives with her own crew, claiming to be sailing to Temese in search of bronze. Speaking in that assumed identity, she advises him to call an assembly to remove Penelope's suitors, then to visit Nestor at Pylos and Menelaus in Sparta to learn what became of his father. On his return, he should kill the suitors, either by stealth or in public.

Mentes hardly features in the earliest Greek myths. Over time, however, his name came to stand for a guardian and mentor, though later it took on a derogatory tone and came to mean a sinister messenger.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the figure of a protector and counsellor and, later, to an ominous messenger. He should not be confused with Mentor, Odysseus's elderly friend, nor with the Mentes who rules the Cicones in the Iliad.

Ancient sources: The Odyssey (Book I), where he appears in the identity Athena adopts.
