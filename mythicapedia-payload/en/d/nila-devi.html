Hindu goddess of joy, consort of Vishnu

Nila Devi

Nila Devi, also known as Nappinnai, is a Hindu goddess of bliss and one of Vishnu's consorts. She is mainly revered in South India, especially within Tamil culture.

The myth

According to the Sri Vaishnava tradition, Nila Devi incarnated as Nagnajiti, a wife of Krishna, and is identified with Nappinnai, a favourite gopi of Krishna. As Nappinnai, she was the daughter of Kumbagan, brother of Yashoda. Krishna won her hand in marriage after subduing her father's seven ferocious bulls.

In southern tradition she is seen as a counterpart of Radha, Krishna's beloved gopi in North Indian traditions. The Vaikhanasa Agama texts describe Vishnu's will energy appearing in three forms, Sridevi, Bhudevi and Nila Devi, which represent the three gunas.

Worship and religion

In the Tiruppavai, Andal wakes Nappinnai before waking Krishna, and in this tradition complete surrender to God is made through the consort. Her figure is associated with the Thirunarayur Nambi Temple.

Legacy

She appears in Vishnu's iconography as Vaikunthanatha, in a depiction held by the British Museum, where she supports the god's foot.

Ancient sources: The Divya Prabandham of the Alvars, the Cilappatikaram, Andal's Tiruppavai, the Sita Upanishad and the Vaikhanasa Agama texts.

Trivia

Her father's bulls. According to tradition, Krishna won Nappinnai by subduing seven ferocious bulls belonging to her father.
Andal and Nappinnai. In the Tiruppavai, Andal wakes Nappinnai before Krishna as an act of devotion.
