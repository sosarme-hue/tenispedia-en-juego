celestial bow of Arjuna in Hindu tradition

Gandiva

Gandiva is the divine bow of Arjuna, one of the Pandavas at the heart of the Mahabharata. According to the epic, Brahma forged it to protect dharma, and after passing through several divine hands it reached the mortal warrior who used it in the great battle of Kurukshetra.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Brahma made the bow to protect dharma. It then passed to Shiva, who held it for a thousand years, and returned to Brahma for 503 years. Indra wielded it for 580 years, Chandra for 500, and finally Varuna kept it for 100 years before giving it to Arjuna.

The story goes that Agni, the god of fire, wanted to devour the forest of Khandavaprastha to regain his splendour, and he enlisted Krishna and Arjuna to help. Arjuna, regarded as the finest archer of his age, asked for a weapon worthy of his strength, so Agni asked Varuna, who gave him the Gandiva along with two quivers of inexhaustible arrows. The bow struck fear into Kurukshetra, where it defeated great warriors and even gods. It was said to instil confidence in its wielder, to carry the strength of a hundred thousand bows, and to have 108 celestial strings; it was indestructible and revered by the celestials and the Gandharvas.

At the end of the Dvapara Yuga, Krishna left the Earth and Dwaraka was swallowed by the ocean. Arjuna, unable to string the bow or recall the mantras to summon his celestial weapons, understood that his work on earth was done. As the Pandavas set out on their final journey to the Himalayas, Agni asked him to return the bow to Varuna, since it belonged to the gods, and Arjuna cast it into the sea.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit Hindu epic, recounts its origin, its passage through divine hands and its final return to the sea.
