Thracian prince, son of Strymon

Brangas

Brangas is a Thracian prince in Greek mythology, the son of King Strymon and brother of Rhesus and Olynthus. His story is very brief, survives in a single ancient author, and centres on mourning his brother and founding a town.

The myth

According to the account, Rhesus, who fought for the Trojans in the Trojan War, was killed by Diomedes. Later, Olynthus died during a hunt, when a lion attacked him. Brangas buried him on the very spot where he had fallen and, there, founded a town that he named after his brother: Olynthus. The story focuses on grief and foundation rather than on heroic deeds.

This is the only version in which Brangas appears. Conon records it without naming his sources, so we do not know where it came from or whether other tales about this figure existed.

Ancient sources: He appears only in the work of Conon, the Greek mythographer, who does not cite his sources.

Trivia

A city named after a brother. According to the account, the town Brangas founded was named Olynthus, in honour of his brother killed by the lion.
