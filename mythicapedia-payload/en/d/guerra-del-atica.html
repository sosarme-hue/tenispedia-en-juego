war between Athenians and Amazons in Greek mythology

Attic War

In Greek mythology, the Attic War was the conflict between the Amazons, a people of warrior women led by queen Penthesilea, and the Athenians, led by Theseus or Heracles. It is one of the best-known episodes of Amazon presence in Greece.

The myth

According to the tales, the Amazons attacked Attica to free either Antiope or her sister Hippolyta, who had been captured after being abducted by a Greek hero. Plutarch says they came close to conquering much of the city, reaching the Pnyx and the Museum.

Theseus attacked the Amazon camp in the Athenian month of Boedromion. The war ended four months later with a peace treaty at the Horeomosium, near the temple of Theseus. In some versions, Molpadia killed the wounded Antiope so that the Athenians could not capture her.

Worship and religion

After the peace, the Athenians agreed to offer sacrifices to the Amazons before each festival of Theseus.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, in his Life of Theseus, records the versions of Hellanicus and Cleidemus of this conflict.

Trivia

The Amazoneum. According to Kleidemus, the left wing of the Amazon army stood in a place in Athens later known as the Amazoneum.
Amazon tombs. Plutarch visited Amazon burial places at Megara, where he mentions a lozenge-shaped crypt.
