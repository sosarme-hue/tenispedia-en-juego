Trojan warrior and seer of the Trojan War

Polydamas

Polydamas is a Trojan warrior and seer, son of Panthous and friend of Hector, who appears in Homer's Iliad. He matters because he is the prudent adviser whom the Trojans almost always ignore.

The myth

According to the Iliad, Polydamas was born on the same night as Hector. He was the son of Panthous, a priest of Apollo, and Phrontis, and brother of Euphorbus and Hyperenor. As a seer he often urges caution in battle, while Hector prefers a direct attack.

In Book XII he advises against storming the Achaean wall after an eagle drops a snake among the Trojans, an omen Hector ignores. In Book XIII, however, Hector heeds his advice to regroup. Polydamas kills Prothoenor, Mecisteus and Otus, and in Book XVIII he counsels retreat after the death of Patroclus. Hector refuses, and soon Achilles, back in the fight, kills Hector himself.

In Quintus of Smyrna's Posthomerica, Polydamas argues that Helen should be returned to the Greeks to end the war, and Paris calls him a coward. He is wounded by Ajax the Lesser and kills Cleon and Eurymachus. According to Dictys of Crete, a warrior named Polydamas dies at the hands of Ajax the Greater, though later Byzantine writers treat him as a different character. Another tradition has him survive the fall of Troy and travel to Italy with Antenor, Theano and Helicaon, helping to found Padua.

Legacy

In the Roman epic Punica, the Roman warrior Pedianus, a descendant of Antenor, goes into battle wearing the armor of Polydamas.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, and Quintus of Smyrna's Posthomerica, which mentions him in the later part of the war.

Trivia

Born the same night. According to tradition, Polydamas was born on the same night as Hector, with whom he shared a close bond in the story.
Armor passed on. In the Roman epic Punica, a Roman warrior goes into battle wearing the armor of Polydamas.
