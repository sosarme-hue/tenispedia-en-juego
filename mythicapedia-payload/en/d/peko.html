Finnish and Estonian god of barley

Peko

Peko is a god of the fields, associated above all with barley and beer brewing, revered by Finns, Estonians and especially the Seto people. He remains a living figure and national symbol in south-eastern Estonia.

The myth

According to Finnish tradition, Peko is the protective spirit of barley, known as 'Pekko of the Field'. The runic songs mention him among the grains and link him with beer, which makes people festive and sing. In a rare Karelian song, Pekka makes a swidden, Onni sows barley and brews the first beer. According to Siikala, Onni is the same figure as Osmo, the mythic sower, which would suggest Peko is older than any reference to Saint Peter.

Among the Setos, Peko is a hero and king: he frees the country, gives bear skins to the villagers, presides over weddings and ploughs with a wooden plough. According to the tale, he sleeps eternally in a cave and, when someone calls his name, he brings rain to the fields. If his descendants remember his advice and work hard, he grants them abundant crops.

Worship and religion

In the past he was honoured with spring rites: before Pentecost, young Seto men held a ritual fight that ended with the first drop of blood, and the bleeding person became host of the next year's feast. Black candles were lit before wooden idols of Peko, kept in granaries, and people chanted for him to come and drink beer. Today the Setos hold a festival around 4 August with their anthem, a music competition and the election of their 'vice-king'.

Legacy

Seto folksinger Anne Vabarna composed the epic 'Songs of Peko', which portrays him as a hero of her people. The Finnish folk metal band Korpiklaani dedicated the song 'Pellonpekko' to him, on the album Spirit of the Forest.

Ancient sources: Bishop Mikael Agricola mentions him in 1551 as the one who granted the growth of barley, and he appears in Finnish and Karelian runic songs.

Trivia

Butterfly. According to Seto tradition, Peko's spirit can fly over the fields as a butterfly.
A king chosen each year. Every August the Setos elect their representative of Peko, known as the 'vice-king'.
