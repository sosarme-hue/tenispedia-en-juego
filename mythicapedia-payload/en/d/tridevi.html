trinity of supreme goddesses in Hinduism

Tridevi

The Tridevi are a group of three goddesses who form a trinity of supreme divinity in Hinduism. They are usually personified as Saraswati, Lakshmi and Parvati, and in Shaktism they are seen as manifestations of one primordial goddess, Mahadevi.

The myth

In the traditionally male-centred branches of Hinduism, the three goddesses appear as consorts and auxiliary deities of the Trimurti, the triad of gods formed by Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva. Saraswati, goddess of learning, arts and music, is the consort of Brahma the creator. Lakshmi, goddess of fortune, wealth, fertility and light, is the consort of Vishnu the preserver. Parvati, goddess of power, war, beauty and love, is the consort of Shiva the destroyer or transformer.

In Shaktism the roles are reversed: the three goddesses hold the leading roles of creation, preservation and destruction, while the male gods serve as auxiliary agents. Mahasaraswati creates, Mahalakshmi preserves and Mahakali destroys. The Devi Mahatmya links each to a quality (guna) of Mahadevi: sattva, purity and knowledge; rajas, dynamic energy; and tamas, darkness. According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, Mahalakshmi emerged from the left side of Mahadevi and later divided herself into Lakshmi, Radha and other forms to become consort of Vishnu's avatars. Mahakali is described as a powerful cosmic aspect who personifies transformation and time.

Legacy

Through Buddhism and syncretism with Japanese Shinto deities, the Tridevi entered Japanese mythology as Benzaitennyo (Saraswati), Kisshoutennyo (Lakshmi) and Daikokutennyo (Mahakali or Parvati).

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya and the Devi Bhagavata Purana present these goddesses as manifestations of Mahadevi.
