Aztec goddess, mother of Quetzalcoatl

Chimalman

Chimalman, also written Chimalma, is a goddess in Aztec mythology and the mother of Quetzalcoatl according to Aztec tradition. Her name means “shield-hand”, and her story explains the birth of one of the most important deities in the pantheon.

The myth

According to the myth, Mixcoatl, a powerful Toltec chief leading his warriors on campaigns, met Chimalman, a warrior woman from outside Toltec culture. She stood naked before him, setting her shield, bow and arrows on the ground, and dodged his arrows again and again. She then fled into a cave. Unable to find her, Mixcoatl mistreated the women of Cuernavaca until they tracked her down and brought her to him.

When she repeated the gesture, neither could defeat the other, and they came together and conceived Quetzalcoatl. Another version, preserved in the Codex Chimalpopoca, says she swallowed a piece of jade and became pregnant without a man. Some traditions say she died giving birth to her son Topiltzin, who later discovered that he was Quetzalcoatl.

Ancient sources: The Manuscript of 1558 and the Codex Chimalpopoca preserve versions of her story within the Mexica tradition.

Trivia

Her name. “Shield-hand” refers to the way she caught arrows with her bare hand.
An unusual conception. In one version, Chimalman conceives Quetzalcoatl after swallowing a green jade stone, without a man.
