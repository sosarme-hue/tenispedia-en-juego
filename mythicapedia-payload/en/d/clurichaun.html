mischievous Irish fairy of drink and cellars

Clurichaun

The clurichaun is a solitary, hard-drinking and mischievous fairy from Irish folklore, known for haunting wine cellars, breweries and pubs. Often seen as a meaner, nocturnal cousin of the leprechaun, it is sometimes confused with him.

The myth

Storytellers describe the clurichaun as a little man with a face like a withered apple and a nose reddened by heavy drinking. In the tale "The Haunted Cellar", recorded in 1825, a clurichaun named Naggeneen empties a lord's wine cellar and terrifies the servants. In another story, a young man accepts a drink from one of these fairies and, when he refuses to pay, finds himself bound to serve it for seven years and a day. Its magic lets it slip through keyholes and ride sheep or dogs, and it carries a purse with a shilling that never runs out.

Legacy

Neil Gaiman made him a recurring character, under the name Cluracan, in his comic series The Sandman. Spider Robinson turned a clurichaun into the guardian of a bar in his Callahan stories, and in Dungeon Crawler Carl clurichauns appear as enemies who brew alcohol.

Trivia

Fairy pipes. The small clay pipes often turned up while digging or ploughing are said to belong to clurichauns, who also enjoyed smoking.
