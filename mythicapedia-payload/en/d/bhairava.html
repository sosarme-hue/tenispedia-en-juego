fearsome Hindu deity, terrifying form of Shiva

Bhairava

Bhairava is a fierce deity worshipped in Shaivite Hinduism and in Vajrayana Buddhism. In Hinduism he is a terrifying manifestation of Shiva, and in the Kashmir Shaivism tradition he represents the Supreme Reality. His name means roughly "terribly fearsome form", and he is one of the most popular deities across the Indian subcontinent.

The myth

According to the Shiva Purana, Bhairava's origin lies in a dispute between Brahma and Vishnu over which of them was superior. Shiva appeared as a pillar of light to settle the matter. Brahma falsely claimed he had found the top of the pillar, and to punish his lie and arrogance, Shiva produced Bhairava from between his brows. Bhairava beheaded Brahma's fifth head, which made him guilty of brahmahatya, the murder of a Brahmin, one of the five greatest sins. Brahma's skull remained stuck to his hand. To atone, Bhairava wandered the three worlds. He visited Vaikuntha, where Vishnu and Lakshmi honoured him, and when he reached Kashi, the city of the liberated, the sin left him and the skull fell from his hand.

Another version tells that Brahma, noticing he and Shiva both had five heads, came to believe he equalled Shiva. When his arrogance began to disrupt his role in the universe, Shiva plucked a lock of hair, which became Bhairava, who cut off one of Brahma's heads. Holding the skull, the creator lost his ego and found enlightenment. Tradition holds that Shiva, in the form of Bhairava, guards each of the Shakta pithas, shrines of the goddess Shakti, except the Kamakhya Temple.

Worship and religion

Temples or shrines to Bhairava stand within or near most Jyotirlinga temples. Among the best known are the Kal Bhairava temple in Ujjain and the Patal Bhairava and Vikrant Bhairava shrines in the same city. Bhairava Ashtami, the day Kala Bhairava is said to have appeared on earth, is observed in the month of Margashirsha with special prayers and rituals, and each month the Kalashtami is marked with fasting and prayers for protection and spiritual growth. His worship remains alive in India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, and in Tibetan and Mongolian Buddhism.
