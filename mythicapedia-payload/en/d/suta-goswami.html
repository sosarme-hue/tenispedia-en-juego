narrator of the Puranas in Hindu tradition

Sauti

Sauti, also called Ugrashravas, is a sage of Hindu tradition and the narrator of several Puranas, including the Mahabharata. His voice frames the great epic, as he recounts it to the sages gathered in the Naimisha Forest.

The myth

According to tradition, Sauti was the son of Lomaharshana (also called Romaharshana), a disciple of the sage Vyasa, author of the Mahabharata. His name means 'son of a suta', a charioteer and royal bard, though he was not one himself.

The Bhagavata Purana recounts that during the Kurukshetra war, Balarama went on pilgrimage and reached the Naimisha Forest, where he saw Romaharshana narrating the Puranas. Because the narrator did not greet him with joined palms, Balarama killed him with a blade of kusa grass. The sages asked that he be revived, and Balarama allowed his son to take up the role of narrator, endowed with long life and great vigour.

Legacy

The Naimisha Forest, where his narrations are set, is a place of pilgrimage, and the seat from which he narrated the Puranas is known as Suta Gaddi.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, Harivamsa, Shiva Purana, Bhagavata Purana, Brahmavaivarta Purana and Padma Purana present him as a narrator.

Trivia

A name tied to a trade. Sauti means 'son of a suta', though he was not one himself. The suta were charioteers and royal bards charged with remembering the deeds of the king.
A story within a story. The Mahabharata is structured as a dialogue in which Sauti recounts it to Saunaka, and within that account other tales are told by different sages.
