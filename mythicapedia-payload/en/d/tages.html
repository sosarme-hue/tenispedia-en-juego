Etruscan god of divination by entrails

Tages

Tages (or Tagete) is a deity of Etruscan tradition associated with the haruspices, the priests who read the future in the entrails of sacrificed animals. The Romans absorbed him into their own tradition, and his figure helped legitimise this method of divination.

The myth

In the account preserved by Cicero, a ploughman working the land in the territory of Tarquinii turned up a deeper furrow than usual, and from the earth sprang a being with a child's face and a sage's wisdom. Astonished, the farmer raised a loud cry, and so many people gathered that in time all the Etruscans came together. Tages spoke before the crowd, who wrote down his words. That knowledge became the foundation of the haruspices' art, later expanded with new findings.

Ovid gives another version, in which a Tyrrhenian ploughman sees a clod of earth turn into a man who begins to foretell what will happen. In some accounts Tages is said to be a grandson of Jupiter. His revelations were kept in sacred books that set out the rules of divination.

Worship and religion

His role was mainly doctrinal: his revelations provided the basis for haruspicy, which the Romans practised through the haruspices. The sources mention no temples, festivals or rites dedicated specifically to him.

Legacy

His presence in art is scarce and debated. A third-century BC bronze mirror from Tuscania shows a youthful haruspex in a conical cap examining a liver, labelled pavatarchies and read by scholars as 'the child Tarchies'. Some fourth-century BC engraved gemstones also seem to depict his myth. Experts disagree about how many other images truly show Tages.

Ancient sources: Cicero and Ovid, both Latin authors, mention him. John the Lydian, in the sixth century AD, is the last writer to claim to have read texts of the Etrusca Disciplina, which recorded his revelations.

Trivia

A disputed name. Because the Etruscan alphabet had no letter G, the name Tages may be a Latinised form. Massimo Pallottino proposed reading the mirror inscription as pava Tarchies, 'the child Tarchies'.
A real figure or a parable?. John the Lydian suggested that Tages might be nothing more than a parable. Nearly all we know of him comes from classical authors who treat him as a legendary figure.
