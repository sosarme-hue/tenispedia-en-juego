Roman god of manure

Sterquilinus

Sterquilinus is a Roman god of manure, linked to the fertilizing of the fields. His name comes from the Latin stercus, meaning fertilizer or dung, and his figure reflects the rural character of early Roman religion.

The myth

According to tradition, Sterquilinus taught farmers to use manure to enrich the soil, a practical task that mattered greatly in an agrarian society. Some modern sources identify him with Picumnus or present him as an aspect of Saturn, under whom he supervised the manuring of the fields.

His name also appears as Stercutus or Sterculius. Some sources say the name was altered to avoid confusion.

Legacy

His name gave rise to scientific terms, such as the plant family Sterculiaceae and the seabird family Stercorariidae. In popular culture he appears in the pilot episode Peace, Love and Understanding of Beavis and Butt-Head (1992), where Butt-Head identifies him correctly.

Trivia

God of the privy. An editor of An Encyclopaedia of Plants, published in 1836, claimed that Sterculius was the god of the privy, from the Latin word for excrement.
Birds with a bad reputation. Skuas give their name to the family Stercorariidae. The food other birds disgorged when chased by skuas was once thought to be excrement.
