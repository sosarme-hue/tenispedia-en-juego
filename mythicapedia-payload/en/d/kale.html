one of the Charites of Greek mythology

Kale

Kale, also called Kalleis or Calleis, is one of the Charites, the Graces of Greek myth, who are described as daughters of Zeus. She appears mainly in a story about beauty and in her role toward Teiresias.

The myth

According to some authors, Kale was the wife of Hephaestus, although most sources give that role to Aphrodite. Her name has also led some readers of Homer to argue that the poet mentions two Charites, Pasithea and Kale. That separation seems forced, since the line can be read as Pasi thea kale, meaning roughly «the goddess who is beautiful to all men».

According to Sostratus, as cited by Eustathius, Aphrodite and the three Charites, Pasithea, Kale and Euphrosyne, quarrelled over who was the most beautiful. Teiresias was the judge and awarded the prize to Kale. Aphrodite punished him by turning him into an old woman, but Kale, grateful, gave him a beautiful head of hair and took him to Crete.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions Pasithea and Kale, and the beauty contest episode is transmitted through Sostratus, preserved by Eustathius in his commentaries on Homer.

Trivia

A disputed name. Some readers believe Homer names two Charites, Pasithea and Kale, while others think the words form a single phrase.
A punishment with a gift. Teiresias loses his youth because of his verdict, but Kale grants him a beautiful head of hair before taking him to Crete.
