Greek nymph, daughter of the river god Maeander

Cyanee

Cyanee is a nymph in Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Maeander. Her story is tied to the founding of Miletus and to the tragedy of her twin children, Caunus and Byblis.

The myth

According to tradition, Cyanee was one of the daughters of Maeander, the river whose name gave us the word meander in western Anatolia. The hero Miletus had left Crete to found a city, and in Asia Minor the two met and joined in marriage. Their union produced twins, a boy named Caunus and a girl named Byblis, whose fate turned tragic when they fell in love with each other.

Another version of the story tells of a young man who loved Cyanee, but she refused to listen to him. Faced with her scorn, she took her own life in his presence, and he felt no pity for her at all. In this variant, Cyanee was turned into a rock.
