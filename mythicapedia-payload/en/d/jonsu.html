Egyptian god of the Moon

Khonsu

Khonsu is the ancient Egyptian god of the Moon, son of Amun and Mut within the Theban Triad. His name means «traveller», a nod to the Moon's nightly journey across the sky, and he was also seen as a protector of night travellers and a healer.

The myth

Egyptian texts link Khonsu to the Moon, known as Iah, as it crosses the night sky. Together with Thoth he marked the passage of time, and he played a decisive role in the creation of new life. He bore titles such as «Embracer», «Pathfinder» and «Defender», since he watched over those who walked at night and was invoked against wild animals and for healing.

It was said that when he made the crescent moon shine, women conceived, cattle became fertile and every nostril and throat filled with fresh air. In art he appears as a bearded man who turns into a mummified child with the sidelock of youth, or as a falcon-headed figure crowned with the moon disk. He was closely linked to Horus, Shu and other divine children, and in late times he was shown as a healer who drives away evil spirits.

He appears in the Pyramid Texts and the Coffin Texts, where he has a fierce aspect. He rose to prominence in the New Kingdom, when he was called «the Greatest of the Great Gods», and at Karnak a creation myth describes him as the great serpent who fertilizes the Cosmic Egg. He merged with the god Iah and, in some regions, took on traits of Osiris, Ptah, Hathor and Thoth.

Worship and religion

His main temple stands in the Amun-Ra precinct at Karnak, and much of its construction centred on him during the Ramesside period; it remains in relatively good condition. He was also worshipped at Memphis, Hibis and Edfu, and at Kom Ombo as a son of Sobek and Hathor. His healing reputation reached beyond Egypt: a stela tells how a princess of Bekhten was instantly cured when an image of him arrived, and King Ptolemy IV called himself «Beloved of Khonsu».

Legacy

Modern popular culture has revived him several times. Rick Riordan includes him in The Kane Chronicles, where he bargains with the heroes for hours in exchange for secret names during a game of Senet. In Marvel Comics, Khonshu grants supernatural powers to Moon Knight, whose strength follows the phases of the moon, and the god also appears in the Marvel Cinematic Universe series Moon Knight. In Night at the Museum, the Tablet of Ahkmenrah is powered by him and needs moonlight to recharge. In the web serial Worm, Jonsu is the name of one of the Endbringers.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts and the Coffin Texts mention him, the former portraying him in a fierce aspect.

Trivia

Three associated animals. He was linked to the baboon, regarded by the Egyptians as a lunar animal, and in other depictions he appears as a bull or double bull, a symbol of fertility.
A god who helps travellers. In The Kane Chronicles, Khonsu bargains with the heroes over a game of Senet, trading hours for their secret names, or Rens.
