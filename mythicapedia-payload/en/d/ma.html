Anatolian mother and warrior goddess

Ma

Ma was a local goddess of Comana in Cappadocia, Anatolia, worshipped both as a mother and as a warrior. Her name means "Mother", and her figure later merged with Enyo and Bellona, which makes her important for understanding the religion of Asia Minor.

The myth

Tradition presents Ma as a goddess linked to fertility and life, associated with the passage into adulthood of both sexes. According to ancient accounts, her biennial festivals included sacred prostitution. She was also seen as a moon goddess, connected with the Anatolian moon god Mēn.

Ancient writers identified her with other deities. She was compared to Cybele and Bellona, and the Greeks linked her with Enyo and Athena Nikephoros. Plutarch also likened her to Semele and Athena. According to Strabo, her cult was founded by Orestes, and the name of Comana came from his hair offered in mourning, though scholars regard this explanation as artificial, since the name derives from Luwian and means "holy".

Worship and religion

Her cult centred on the great temple at Comana, which Strabo describes in detail, where solemn rites were performed. At Ameria, near Cabira, in the Kingdom of Pontus, a temple estate dedicated to Mēn and Selene was built, seemingly to counter Ma's influence. Ma-Enyo, a fusion of the Anatolian and Greek goddesses, was also worshipped in Macedonia alongside other foreign deities.

Legacy

Ma's cult shaped the Roman form of Bellona, whose priestesses wounded themselves in trance, as Tibullus describes. Sulla recounted a dream in which a Cappadocian goddess handed him a thunderbolt to defeat his enemies.

Ancient sources: Strabo describes her temple at Comana, and Plutarch compares her with Semele and Athena; Stephanus of Byzantium presents her as a companion of Rhea.

Trivia

Ma and Dionysus. According to Stephanus of Byzantium, Ma raised the infant Dionysus and, to hide his identity from Hera, said he was the son of Ares. This gave rise to the epithet "Masaris".
A name with a bull. The city of Mastaura was said to take its name from this cult, interpreted as "Ma's bull", since a bull was sacrificed in Lydia in honour of Rhea, also called Ma.
