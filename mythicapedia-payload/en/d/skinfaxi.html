horse of Dagr, day, in Norse mythology

Skinfaxi

Skinfaxi, meaning 'shining mane', is the horse of Dagr, the personification of day, in Norse mythology. Together with Hrímfaxi, the horse of Nótt, night, he forms a pair of steeds that mark the rhythm of the sky.

The myth

According to the tradition, every day Skinfaxi pulls Dagr's chariot across the sky. His mane shines so brightly that it lights up both the heavens and the earth below. His very name describes him: in Old Norse it means 'shining mane'.

His companion, Hrímfaxi, whose name means 'frost mane', has a different task. In the story, the froth that falls from his bit reaches the ground as dew, so the morning droplets have a mythical origin here. In this way, both horses link the cycle of day and night to the everyday world of light and dew.

Trivia

Names with meaning. Skinfaxi means 'shining mane' and Hrímfaxi means 'frost mane'. The names describe the appearance of each horse.
The origin of dew. According to the myth, morning dew comes from the froth that falls from Hrímfaxi's bit.
