Buddha of Long Life, aspect of Amitābha

Amitāyus

Amitāyus, meaning «Measureless Life», is the aspect of Amitābha associated with infinite life and longevity. He is a central figure in Mahayana Buddhism, especially in East Asia and Tibet.

The myth

According to the Pure Land sutras, long ago the bodhisattva monk Dharmākara, who in some versions was a former king, contemplated the buddhafields of the cosmos for five kalpas and made forty-eight vows. After long practice he became Amitābha and created Sukhāvatī, a pure land in the west where beings can be reborn and attain enlightenment.

His measureless life is linked to his infinite compassion. For this reason he is associated with long life, and in art he is often shown radiating light, a symbol of his wisdom.

Worship and religion

In East Asia, Amitābha is venerated above all through nianfo or nembutsu, the recitation of his name, seen as a path open to everyone. In Tibetan Buddhism, Amitāyus is distinguished from Amitābha and is linked to phowa, the transference of consciousness at death. This devotion remains alive today.

Legacy

In East Asia, Amitābha often appears in triads with the bodhisattvas Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta. Himalayan art depicts him differently from Amitāyus, and the eternal flame is associated with his symbol of light.

Ancient sources: The Indian Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtras, such as the Sutra of Measureless Life, which narrate Dharmākara's vows.

Trivia

Forty-eight vows. The most common sutra attributes forty-eight vows to Dharmākara, which form the foundation of Pure Land doctrine.
The closed lotus. According to the sutra, those who doubt Amitābha are reborn in lotus flowers that stay closed for five hundred years.
