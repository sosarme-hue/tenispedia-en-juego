Þrúðgelmir

Þrúðgelmir is a jötunn of Norse mythology, the son of the primordial giant Aurgelmir, whom Snorri identifies with Ymir, and the father of Bergelmir. He appears almost only in one poem of the Poetic Edda, which has led scholars to doubt how central he was to the tradition.

The myth

According to the stories, Þrúðgelmir was the son of Aurgelmir, the primordial giant that Snorri Sturluson identifies with Ymir. He had an elder brother and an elder sister, and he may have been born from Ymir's legs. When his father was killed, Þrúðgelmir drowned in the blood that flowed from the body, and only Bergelmir and his wife survived.

The poem Vafþrúðnismál, in the Poetic Edda, mentions him in a dialogue between Odin, disguised as Gagnrad, and the giant Vafþrúðnir. When Odin asks who is the eldest of the Æsir or the giants, Vafþrúðnir answers that, uncountable winters before the earth was made, Bergelmir was born, with Þrúðgelmir as his father and Aurgelmir as his grandfather.

Scholars disagree about his identity. For Rudolf Simek, he is the same as the six-headed son begotten by Aurgelmir's feet, mentioned in stanza 33 of the same poem. John Lindow, however, noted that he appears in only one source apart from the þulur, and suggested the poet may have invented him. The identification cannot be confirmed, since that stanza says Aurgelmir had more than one male offspring.

Ancient sources: Vafþrúðnismál in the Poetic Edda, where he is Bergelmir's father; the þulur also mention him.

Trivia

The meaning of his name. His Old Norse name is usually translated as 'Strength Yeller'. It is also written as Thrudgelmir.
A six-headed brother?. A six-headed son begotten by Aurgelmir's feet might be Þrúðgelmir himself, according to one reading, though this is not certain.
