main goddess of Basque mythology

Mari

Mari is the main goddess of pre-Christian Basque mythology, lady of the earth, the weather and the mountains. Her most famous dwelling is the cave on the eastern face of Anboto, and according to tradition the rains, droughts and harvests depend on her.

The myth

According to tradition, Mari lives in caves on the Basque peaks, and each mountain bears her name. She usually appears as a woman with long blonde hair, which she combs in the sun with a golden comb, though she can also take the form of a fiery woman, a blazing rainbow or a horse dragging clouds. She is the wife of Sugaar and the mother of Mikelats, the wicked son, and Atarrabi, the kind one, who are always at odds.

Her power extends to the weather: she announces storms, sets the climate according to the peak she inhabits, and punishes lying, theft and pride through the rule of the no. Legends about her origin tell of a daughter carried off by the devil, and in another version of a girl cursed by her mother to wander the mountains.

Worship and religion

Believers visited her in her caves and observed a strict protocol: addressing her informally, leaving the cave the same way they entered, and never sitting down while speaking with her.

Legacy

She is linked to Gaia and to goddesses of fertility, and some scholars compare her to Zeus or Jupiter for ruling the weather. After Christianization she was identified with the Virgin Mary, and the eguzkilore flower, placed at house entrances as protection, preserves her trace.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources are scarce; the first written mention of the Lady of Anboto comes from the chronicler Esteban de Garibay, historian of Charles V.

Trivia

A mountain shaped like a lady. From certain viewpoints, the outline of the peaks looks like a reclining lady, with the nose, mouth and feet formed by different summits.
Protection with flowers. The eguzkilore, a thistle-like flower, was placed at house entrances to protect the inhabitants from evil spirits.
