dual creator deity of Dahomey vodun

Mawu-Lisa

Mawu-Lisa is a dual creator deity of West African Vodun, especially of the former Dahomey Kingdom, today Benin. Mawu, linked to the Sun, is a goddess, and Lisa, linked to the Moon, is a god; together they form a deity regarded as agender.

The myth

According to tradition, Mawu alone created human beings from clay, while her husband Lisa was charged with teaching them how to build civilization. The couple are parents of Oba Koso, known as Shango and called Hebioso among the Fon.

Once the Earth and its creatures existed, Mawu worried it was too heavy and asked the primeval serpent Aido Hwedo to coil beneath it and thrust it up into the sky. In another episode, the monkey Awe, also made by the goddess, boasted that he could make animals from clay and climbed to the heavens to prove he could give life too. He failed, and Lisa gave him a bowl of porridge containing the seed of death, reminding him that only Mawu gives life and that she can also take it away.

Worship and religion

Mawu-Lisa became the state deity of Dahomey. The pair was not originally a Fon phenomenon; it is generally traced to Yoruba religion, where Lisa corresponds to Obatala. In Ife, the Yoruba cradle, both deities are honoured in twinned temples known as Orisa-Yemowo. In eighteenth-century Dahomey they were placed below the divinized ancestors of the royal house.

Legacy

Under Christian influence, the name Mawu came to serve as the equivalent of the one God, and among other Gbe-speaking peoples the goddess has been elevated to an omnipotent deity. The name is often rendered as something like The Unsurpassable One.

Trivia

Meaningful name. Mawu is translated as The Unsurpassable One, formed from a negative particle and a verb meaning to surpass or exceed.
Twinned temples. In Ife, both deities are venerated together in conjoined temples known as Orisa-Yemowo.
