Greek hero, son of Zeus and founder of Melitaea

Meliteus

Meliteus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Zeus and the nymph Othreis, traditionally credited with founding the city of Melitaea. His story blends divine protection with a tale of tyranny and revenge.

The myth

According to the tradition, his mother abandoned him in a forest, fearing Hera's jealous wrath over Zeus's affair. Zeus made sure the infant survived by sending a swarm of bees to nourish him, until the shepherd Phagrus, his maternal half-brother and, in the story, a son of Apollo, found him and raised him.

As an adult, Meliteus became a strong and capable leader who ruled many people in the region and founded Melitaea. As a ruler, he had maidens seized and taken for himself, including Aspalis, who took her own life before the soldiers reached her. Her brother Astigites disguised himself in her clothes, hid a sword beneath them, and killed the tyrant. The townspeople threw the body into the river and proclaimed Astigites king.

Legacy

A species of bee bears his name, and his figure is linked to the Latin word for 'honey'.

Trivia

Bees named after him. A species of bee carries the name of Meliteus. The link comes from the episode in which a swarm fed him as an infant.
