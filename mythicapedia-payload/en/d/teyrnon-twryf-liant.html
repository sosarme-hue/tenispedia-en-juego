Welsh lord of Gwent and foster father of Pryderi

Teyrnon Twryf Liant

In Welsh tradition, Teyrnon Twryf Liant is lord of Gwent and foster father of the divine boy Pryderi. He features most prominently in Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed, the first branch of the Mabinogi, and briefly in Culhwch and Olwen as a knight of King Arthur.

The myth

According to the tale, the newborn son of Pwyll and Rhiannon vanishes on his first night, while six of the queen’s ladies-in-waiting are guarding him. To escape the king’s anger, they smear dog’s blood on the sleeping Rhiannon and accuse her of devouring her child.

Teyrnon owns a mare that gives birth to a foal every year, yet every foal disappears. One night he keeps watch over the stable, cuts off the arm of a clawed creature, and finds the child outside. He and his wife raise the boy as their own and call him Gwri Wallt Euryn, “Gwri of the Golden Hair.” As the boy grows ever more like Pwyll, Teyrnon realises who he really is. Gwri is reunited with his parents and renamed Pryderi. Teyrnon refuses the treasures offered to him, and Pwyll promises protection for him and his lands.

Ancient sources: The medieval Welsh tales of the Mabinogion, specifically Pwyll Pendefig Dyfed and Culhwch and Olwen.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Teyrnon derives from the Common Brittonic tigernonos, meaning “great lord”, which reflects his high rank as a nobleman.
The golden-haired child. Teyrnon names the boy Gwri Wallt Euryn because all his hair was as yellow as gold.
