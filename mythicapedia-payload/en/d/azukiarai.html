creature of Japanese folklore who washes azuki beans by rivers

Azukiarai

The azukiarai is a ghostly creature or phenomenon from Japanese folklore that lingers by rivers and other bodies of water. It is known by the noise of someone washing or grinding azuki beans and by a teasing song that mixes menace with mischief.

The myth

Tradition says the azukiarai can be heard at riverbanks, where it seems to be washing azuki beans. As it works, it sings a line that roughly asks whether it should wash its beans or catch a person to eat. The sounds shoki shoki imitate something being scrubbed.

Accounts describe it as a timid being that enjoys frightening people more than harming them. Anyone who comes too close often ends up in the water. Depending on the story, it takes the form of a baby, a tiny old man or a very small, very old woman. In other tellings it appears as a short, grotesque man with a large bald head, crooked teeth, a thin moustache and large bulging yellow eyes, dressed in rags and bent over a pail. Sometimes the mischief is blamed on a tanuki or a weasel instead.
