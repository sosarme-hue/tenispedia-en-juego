urban legend creature from Maryland

Goatman

Goatman, or the Goat Man, is a creature from American urban legend, a half-human, half-goat being blamed for dog deaths in the woods of Prince George's County, Maryland. It is a modern tale, emerging in the 1970s, that shows how local folklore can take shape around a specific place.

The myth

According to the legend, Goatman lives in a makeshift shelter in the wooded northeast of the county, near Bowie, and now and then ventures out to kill dogs or smash cars with an axe. Versions differ on his appearance: some describe a hairy humanoid with a human face, others a man with the lower body of a goat, similar to the fauns of Roman mythology.

The tradition offers several origins. One variant says he was a scientist at the Beltsville Agricultural Research Center who was transformed after an experiment on goats; another says he was an old hermit who walked alone at night along Fletchertown Road. Some accounts place dog deaths in the 1930s or 1950s, though the state of the remains suggests trains may have caused them.

Legacy

Graffiti reading "Goatman was here" and sighting calls to the police, many of them pranks, have kept the legend alive among local students. According to folklorist Barry Pearson, the story is passed on mainly by bored teenagers who repeat it and link it to the local lovers' lane.
