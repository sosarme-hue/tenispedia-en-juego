Greek goddess of applied justice

Praxidice

Praxidice is a figure of Greek mythology linked to justice carried out among mortals. She appears as an epithet of Persephone and is sometimes identified with Dike, goddess of justice.

The myth

According to the Orphic Hymn to Persephone, Praxidice was an epithet of the goddess, a subterranean queen and mother of the Eumenides. As praxis ('application') of dike ('justice'), her name suggests justice put into practice, which is why she is sometimes identified with Dike.

Stephanus of Byzantium records a different tradition: a daughter of Ogygus named Praxidike married Tremiles and had four sons, among them Tlos, who gave his name to a Lycian city. In the plural, the Praxidicae were groups of figures who presided over the exacting of justice, such as Arete and Homonoia, or the daughters of the Boeotian king Ogyges.

Worship and religion

At Haliartos in Boeotia, Pausanias saw an open-air sanctuary of the Praxidicae, where people swore oaths that were taken very seriously. Their images showed only the head, and only animal heads were sacrificed to them.

Ancient sources: The Orphic Hymn to Persephone, Stephanus of Byzantium and Pausanias.

Trivia

Serious oaths. At Haliartos, locals swore by the Praxidicae, and the oath was not taken lightly.
A city with a mythic name. The Lycian city of Tlos took its name from one of Praxidice's sons, according to Stephanus of Byzantium.
