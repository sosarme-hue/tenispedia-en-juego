Castilian house sprite

Martinico

The martinico is a sprite from Castilian popular tradition, especially in La Mancha, known for its mischief around the household and its gloomy, hooded appearance.

The myth

According to tradition, it is a gloomy creature, often dressed as a friar and hidden beneath a hood, although some tales describe it with a childlike, friendly face. It slips into homes to move objects around and play tricks on the household. It punishes misers by turning their gold into charcoal and pesters maidens with noises in the pantry.

Some legends say that a sinister demon banished it to the woods and that, once its sentence was served, it went down to the town to get closer to people. It prefers children, whom it either protects or plays with.

Legacy

Calderón portrays it in La dama duende and Antonio de Zamora satirises it in Duendes son alcahuetes. Goya drew it as a dwarf with a huge head dressed as a Franciscan, and the priest Feijoo fought the superstition in his essays.

Ancient sources: El conde Lucanor by Don Juan Manuel, where the demon serving a man is called Martín, and Calderón de la Barca's comedy La dama duende.

Trivia

A demon named Martín. In tale XLV of El conde Lucanor, the demon serving a man is called Martín, a name used for the devil in the Middle Ages.
Little friars in Extremadura. In Extremadura these sprites are known as frailecillos, which reinforces their image as hooded friars.
