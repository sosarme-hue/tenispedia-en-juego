Bergelmir

Bergelmir is a jötunn, a giant of Norse mythology, and one of the few beings to survive the flood of blood that followed the death of Ymir. His story, in which he escapes with his wife aboard a wooden vessel, explains how a new race of frost giants came into being.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Bergelmir was the son of Þrúðgelmir and the grandson of Ymir, the first giant, born of frost. When the sons of Borr, Odin, Vili and Vé, killed Ymir, his blood flooded the world and drowned nearly all the frost giants.

Bergelmir and his wife survived by taking refuge in a lúðr, a word that may mean chest, cradle or boat. In Snorri's account the container floats like a vessel, while in the older poetry Bergelmir is laid inside it, as an infant would be. After the waters subsided, the couple restocked the race of frost giants.

In the poem Vafþrúðnismál, Odin tests the wise giant Vafthrúdnir by asking who is the oldest among the æsir and the jötnar, and later asks him about the monstrous birth of Aurgelmir's offspring. In the Prose Edda, Snorri makes Bergelmir a Noah-like figure, a detail scholars have noted and debated, since the poetic and prose versions do not fully agree.

Ancient sources: He appears in the poem Vafþrúðnismál of the Poetic Edda and in Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A disputed name. His name has been translated as bear-yeller or mountain-yeller. The linguist Jan de Vries proposed reading it as one who roars like a bear.
A possible Norse Noah. Robert D. Fulk notes that in the Prose Edda the lúðr may be an ark, because of Bergelmir's resemblance to Noah. Fulk argues that the word lúðr originally referred to a flour bin.
