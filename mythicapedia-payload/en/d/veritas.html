Roman goddess of truth

Veritas

Veritas is the goddess of truth in Roman mythology, daughter of Saturn and mother of Virtus. Her name is the Latin word for "truth," and she embodies truthfulness, one of the main virtues a good Roman was expected to have.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Veritas is the daughter of Saturn, the Titan of Time, although some accounts make her a daughter of Jupiter or a creation of Prometheus. Her parentage varies by source, but she is almost always described as the mother of Virtus, or virtue.

The stories portray her as an elusive goddess who hides at the bottom of a holy well, so that truth can only be found with effort and patience. Her elusiveness is the defining trait of her myth.

Legacy

The Latin word appears in the mottos of many colleges and organizations, including Harvard, Yale and the Dominican Order, and the phrase "In vino veritas" ("in wine, truth") comes from the same word. In late nineteenth-century France, during the Dreyfus affair and the debate over the separation of Church and State, her image was used as a political symbol. Artists such as Jean-Léon Gérôme depicted her emerging from the well.

Trivia

From Greece to Rome. The Greek equivalent is Aletheia, a name meaning something like "unconcealment."
A motto with a famous home. Veritas is the motto of Harvard University, the oldest institution of higher learning in the United States.
