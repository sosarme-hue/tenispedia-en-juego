hero of Greek mythology, king of Dardania

Assaracus

Assaracus is a hero of Greek mythology, king of Dardania and ancestor of the Trojan lines. He matters because he links the founding of Troy with the family line that leads to Aeneas.

The myth

According to the tradition, Assaracus was the second son of Tros, king of Dardania, and his wife Callirhoe, although other versions give Acallaris, daughter of Eumedes, as his mother. He was the brother of Ilus and Ganymede. When Ilus chose to rule the city he had just founded, Ilium, later known as Troy, Assaracus inherited the throne of Dardania.

He married Hieromneme, daughter of Simoeis, although some authors say his wife was Clytodora, daughter of Laomedon. Either union produced Capys, his son and heir. In a less common version, Aesyetes and Cleomestra are named as his parents, and in that account his brothers were Alcathous and Antenor. The Roman mythographer Hyginus, however, insists that Ganymede was not Assaracus' brother but his son.

When he died, the kingship passed to Capys. Assaracus was buried in the heart of Troy, near the temple of Athena and the palace that Priam would later build. Capys would become the father of Anchises and therefore the grandfather of Aeneas, which places Assaracus among the ancestors of a line the tradition connects with the Trojans.

Ancient sources: The Roman mythographer Hyginus records the genealogies of Assaracus and his relation to Ganymede.

Trivia

A brother who was a son. The Roman mythographer Hyginus claims that Ganymede was not Assaracus' brother but his son, which changes their relationship entirely.
Buried in Troy. Assaracus was buried in the heart of Troy, beside the temple of Athena and close to the palace later built by Priam.
