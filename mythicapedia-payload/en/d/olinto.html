Thracian hero, son of Heracles or Strymon

Olynthus

Olynthus is a hero of Greek mythology linked to Thrace. According to Athenaeus, the ancient city of Olynthus and the river of the same name, near Apollonia, were believed to have taken their name from him.

The myth

According to Athenaeus, Olynthus was a son of Heracles and Bolbe. Other authors make him a son of King Strymon; according to Conon and Stephanus of Byzantium, he was the brother of Brangas and Rhesus.

The tradition tells that Olynthus was killed by a lion while hunting. His brother Brangas buried him where he had fallen and later founded a town there, which he named Olynthus in his memory.

Legacy

The name of Olynthus passed into the geography of the ancient world: the city of Olynthus and the nearby river are said to bear his name, according to the tradition recorded by Athenaeus.

Ancient sources: Athenaeus, Conon and Stephanus of Byzantium mention him in their accounts of the city and river that bear his name.

Trivia

A name with two fathers. The sources disagree: Athenaeus makes him a son of Heracles, while Conon and Stephanus of Byzantium present him as a son of King Strymon.
