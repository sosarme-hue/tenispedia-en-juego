king of Elis, Epeian commander in the Trojan War

Polyxenus

Polyxenus is a king of Elis in Greek myth, son of Agasthenes, who fought at Troy at the head of the Epeians. His story links the Trojan War with the family line of the kings of Elis.

The myth

According to tradition, Polyxenus was king of Elis, son of Agasthenes and Peloris or Pelorides. During the Trojan War he led the Epeian contingent, forty ships strong, and is counted among Helen's suitors. He returned safely from Ilion.

He had a son whom he named Amphimachus, in honour of his friend Amphimachus, son of Cteatus, who had died in the war. Amphimachus was the father of Dius, and during Dius's reign the Dorians, together with the sons of Aristomachus, gathered to return to the Peloponnese.

In another episode, Amphitryon recovered from the Taphians the cattle that had been stolen from Electryon, son of Perseus, and handed over to Polyxenus, restoring Licymnius, son of Electryon, to his family.

Trivia

A name from friendship. Polyxenus named his son Amphimachus after Amphimachus, son of Cteatus, a friend who had died at Ilion.
Recovered cattle. The Taphians stole cattle from Electryon but ended up handing them over to Polyxenus, who passed them to Amphitryon.
