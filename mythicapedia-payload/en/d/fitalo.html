hero and king of Attica in Greek mythology

Phytalus

Phytalus is, in Greek mythology, a hero and king of Attica who offered hospitality to Demeter when she was searching for her daughter Persephone. In return, the goddess gave him a fig tree.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phytalus welcomed Demeter into his home while the goddess wandered the world searching for Persephone. To thank him for his kindness, Demeter gave him a fig tree.

The story adds that the Phytalidae, a clan who claimed descent from Phytalus, cleansed Theseus of the murders he had committed on his way from Troezen to Athens. As a reward for their hospitality, the Phytalidae were put in charge of the hero cult of Theseus.

Worship and religion

Phytalus was revered at Eleusis, the centre of the mysteries of Demeter. His tomb was shown in the deme of Lakidai, near the river Cephisus, and his descendants kept charge of the cult of Theseus for generations.

Trivia

A fig tree as a gift. Demeter thanked Phytalus for his hospitality with a fig tree, a gift that links him to this plant in the story.
Theseus' purifiers. The Phytalidae, descendants of Phytalus, cleansed Theseus of his crimes and later were put in charge of the hero's cult.
