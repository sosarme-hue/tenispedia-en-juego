king of Athens, son of Theseus and Phaedra

Demophon

In Greek legend, Demophon was a king of Athens and a veteran of the Trojan War who hid inside the Wooden Horse. His love story with Phyllis, a Thracian king's daughter, is among the best-known tales of a hero's homecoming.

The myth

The son of Theseus and Phaedra, Demophon was raised in exile after his father lost the Athenian throne to Menestheus. Together with his brother Acamas, he grew up on Euboea under the care of Elephenor. As an adult he joined the Achaeans at Troy and entered the city inside the horse. After the fall of Troy, he rescued his grandmother Aethra, who had been held as Helen's handmaid, and brought her home.

On the way back, according to one version, he stopped in Thrace and married Phyllis, the king's daughter. He then sailed for Athens, promising to return soon. Phyllis gave him a casket and asked him not to open it while he still hoped to come back. As time passed without word, she took her own life. Demophon opened the casket, fled on horseback in terror, and died after falling on his own sword. Other traditions say he returned to Thrace to find Phyllis transformed into an almond tree, which blossomed when he embraced it.

As king, according to Euripides' tragedy Heracleidae, he sheltered the children of Heracles pursued by Eurystheus. To defeat the enemy army, an oracle demanded the sacrifice of a noble maiden to Persephone, and Macaria, a daughter of Heracles, volunteered. Demophon also wrested the Palladium from Diomedes and founded the court of the Ephetae, before which he himself was the first to stand trial for an accidental killing. He was succeeded by his son Oxyntes.

Ancient sources: Pindar names him as a son of Theseus, and Euripides stars him in his tragedy Heracleidae. Homer does not mention him.
