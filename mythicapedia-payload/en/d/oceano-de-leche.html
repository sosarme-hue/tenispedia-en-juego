Hindu mythical ocean churned by gods and demons

Ocean of Milk

The Kshira Sagara, or Ocean of Milk, is in Hindu cosmology the fifth of the seven oceans counting from the centre of the world, and the setting of the Samudra Manthana, the churning that produced the nectar of immortality. The story is told in the Puranas and in epics such as the Mahabharata.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, the Kshira Sagara is where the god Vishnu reclines on the serpent Shesha, accompanied by his consort Lakshmi. Once, devas and asuras agreed to churn this primordial ocean together to obtain amrita, the elixir of immortality. They used the serpent Vasuki as the rope and Mount Mandara as the churning pole, which rested on the back of the tortoise Kurma, an avatar of Vishnu.

During the churning the poison halahala rose, its fumes threatening to engulf the universe. The gods turned to Shiva, who swallowed it; Parvati stopped the poison from spreading beyond his throat, and his neck turned blue, earning him the epithet Nilakantha, the blue-throated one. Treasures then emerged from the ocean: the cow Kamadhenu, the tree Parijata, the apsaras, the crescent moon and Dhanvantari holding a cup of amrita. After him came Lakshmi, the horse Uchchaishravas, the gem Kaustubha, the elephant Airavata and the wish-granting tree Kalpavriksha.

When the amrita appeared, devas and asuras quarrelled over it. Vishnu, in the form of the enchantress Mohini, persuaded the asuras to let him distribute it, and gave it only to the gods. An asura named Svarbhanu disguised himself as a deva to drink it, but the sun and moon exposed him. Vishnu beheaded him; the head, now immortal, became Rahu, and the body became Ketu.

Ancient sources: The episode appears in Valmiki's Ramayana (canto 45) and the Mahabharata; the Vishnu Purana describes Lakshmi's origin from this ocean, and the Tamil Tiruvaymoli and the Devi Bhagavata Purana also mention it.

Trivia

Names in other languages. In Bengali it is called Khir Śagôr, in Tamil Tiruppāṟkaṭal and in Telugu Pāla Samudram.
