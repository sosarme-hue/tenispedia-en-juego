Lake Champlain monster

Champ

Champ, or Champy, is a reputed lake monster said to live in Lake Champlain, shared by New York, Vermont and Quebec. Despite the lack of scientific evidence, more than 300 sightings have been reported, and it is one of the best-known creatures in North American folklore. According to tradition, the Iroquois and the Abenaki told of a similar creature long before European contact, and the Abenaki called it Tatoskok. Later, a sighting in 1609 was attributed to Samuel de Champlain, founder of Quebec and namesake of the lake. That claim comes from a 1970 magazine article, and there is no evidence that Champlain ever recorded it. The first sighting to reach the press came in 1883, when Sheriff Nathan H. Mooney described a large water serpent with white spots inside its mouth. In 1977, Sandra Mansi photographed an object rising from the water, though Joe Nickell argues that the bay is too shallow to hide a giant animal and that the object could be a log. A 2005 video by fishermen showed something some interpret as a head and neck. Skeptics suggest mistaken identities or hoaxes, while believers propose surviving plesiosaurs or basilosaurs.

The myth

Legacy

Champ is the mascot of the Vermont Lake Monsters baseball team and draws tourists to Burlington and Plattsburgh. In Port Henry, New York, a giant model of the monster stands in the village, and Champ Day is held on the first Saturday of August. Several local businesses use Champ as a logo. Drone footage from the 2024 film Lucy and the Lake Monster revived interest. Scottish tourists at the World Cup nicknamed him Nessie's cousin.

Trivia

Barnum's rewards. The showman P. T. Barnum offered rewards for the monster in 1873 and 1887, hoping to exhibit it.
A day for Champ. Port Henry holds Champ Day every first Saturday of August, with a giant model of the monster in the village.
