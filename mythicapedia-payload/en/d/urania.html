Muse of astronomy in Greek mythology

Urania

Urania is, in Greek mythology, the muse of astronomy and, in some accounts, of astrology. Her name means "heavenly", and she is linked to the sky, the stars and knowledge of the cosmos.

The myth

In the most common genealogy, Urania was a daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, and therefore a sister of the other muses. Other accounts make her a daughter of Uranus and Gaia, which would make her a great-granddaughter of Uranus. Diodorus explains her name by saying that those instructed by her are lifted up to heaven, since imagination and thought raise the soul to lofty heights.

The stories give her offspring by different fathers. According to some authors, she was the mother of the musician Linus, whose father was said to be Apollo, Hermes or Amphimarus, son of Poseidon. Hymenaeus, the wedding song, is also named as her son in some versions. Other traditions say that Apollo fathered Linus, Ialemus and Hymenaeus with different muses, and that in some cases the mother of Hymenaeus was an unnamed muse.

Later she was linked with three lesser muses, daughters of Apollo, named Nete, Mese and Hypate. They were assigned the strings of the lyre and one of the three levels of the cosmos, from the world below the moon to the fixed stars.

She is usually shown dressed in blue, the colour of the vault of heaven, with a crown of stars and a cloak full of them. She holds a celestial globe and measures positions with a compass, while mathematical instruments lie at her feet.

Legacy

The planet Uranus is mostly named after the sky god, but is also indirectly named after Urania. Her name is used by observatories in Berlin, Vienna, Zurich and Antwerp, and by Tycho Brahe's historic Uraniborg. Many amateur astronomy societies and some esoteric groups have adopted it. The asteroid (30) Urania is named after her too. Her classic image owes much to Simon Vouet's painting "The Muses Urania and Calliope" (1634). In the Renaissance she became the muse of Christian poets, and John Milton invokes her at the opening of Book 7 of "Paradise Lost".
