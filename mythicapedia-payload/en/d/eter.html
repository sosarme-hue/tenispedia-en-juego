Aether

Aether is, in Greek mythology, one of the primordial deities and the personification of the bright daytime sky, the luminous upper air where the gods dwell. He matters because later Greek philosophers turned him into a natural element, the aether, a concept that lasted for centuries.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Aether was the son of Erebus, Darkness, and Nyx, Night, and the brother and consort of Hemera, Day. Other ancient authors offer different accounts. Acusilaus has Aether, Eros and Metis born from the union of Erebus and Nicte, while Theocritus makes him the father of Eros with Nyx. Aristophanes credits him with fathering the cloud nymphs on his own, and Euripides calls him the consort of Gaia, the Earth.

In the Roman tradition, Hyginus makes him a son of Chaos and Caligo, and with Dies he begets Terra, Caelus and Mare, the earth, the sky and the sea. Cicero says he was the father of the first Jupiter. In the Orphic writings he takes on a cosmogonic role: Time begets him together with Chaos, and within Aether a silver egg is placed, from which light and Phanes, the firstborn, emerge.

Hesiod sets this abstract Aether apart from Uranus, the sky proper, who is more personal. In the Homeric poems, by contrast, Aether is the region above the air where the gods live and where Zeus rules, the source of clouds and the place from which the winds blow. As a god he has little part to play: he appears mainly within cosmogonic stories or as the parent of other beings.

Legacy

In Greek philosophy, thinkers turned the mythical Aether into a natural element, the aether, a substance purer and lighter than air and located above it. Ovid describes this region as the highest and most subtle, free of earthly heaviness, and Boccaccio takes him up allegorically as a son of Nox and Erebus, sometimes reading him as the element of fire.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, the Homeric poems, Acusilaus, Aristophanes, Euripides, Hyginus, Cicero and the Orphic writings mention him with differing genealogies and roles.

Trivia

The root of his name. His name comes from a root that in archaic epic is linked to burning and shining, the same root found in Ethiopia, meaning «the one with the burnt face».
A Jupiter born of Aether. Cicero states that Aether was the father of the first Jupiter, and that this Jupiter in turn fathered the Sun.
