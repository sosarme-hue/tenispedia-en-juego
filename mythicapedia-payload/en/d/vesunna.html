Celtic goddess of water and protector of Périgueux

Vesunna

Vesunna is a Celtic goddess of water and fertility, worshipped in Roman Gaul as the protector of the city that bore her name, now the southern district of Périgueux. Her cult was later linked to the Roman guardian goddess Tutela.

The myth

Little survives of Vesunna's myths, so most of what we know comes from inscriptions and the remains of her temple. She is considered a water deity and possibly a goddess of fertility, connected with the river and the renewal of fertility.

Her name is often confused with Venus, though the two are distinct. Local tradition regarded her as the patron of the city and its people, whom she protected.

Worship and religion

The Petrocorii worshipped her in a Gallo-Roman temple in their city, where a statue of the goddess stood and processions moved through the peristyle. Her cult was most prominent at local festivals, especially the anniversary of the city's founding, with sacrifices of sheep, cattle and even horses. There is no evidence that her worship survives today.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the ancient city's name and to the Vesunna Museum, opened in 2003 over the ruins of her temple to a design by Jean Nouvel. In 2015 a rabaska canoe was named Vesunna I for river tours on the Isle.

Ancient sources: She is known mainly from Gallo-Roman inscriptions, such as the one calling her Tutela Augusta Vesunna.

Trivia

Modern vote. In 2013 a regional newspaper asked its readers for their favourite woman of the Périgord, and Vesunna won 85 % of the votes.
A variant in Baden-Baden. An inscription from Baden-Baden invokes the goddess Visuna, probably a written variant of Vesunna.
