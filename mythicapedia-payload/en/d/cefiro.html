Greek god of the west wind

Zephyrus

Zephyrus, also spelled Zephyr, is the god of the west wind and one of the four Anemoi of Greek mythology. Associated with spring, flowers and fertility, he is the gentlest and most favourable of the winds, and his name survives in the word zephyr for a mild breeze.

The myth

According to tradition, Zephyrus was the son of Astraeus and Eos, the goddess of the dawn, although Aeschylus makes him born of the earth, Gaia. Homer shows him as a stormy wind: in the Odyssey, Aeolus frees him to blow Odysseus's ship gently back toward Ithaca, until the crew foolishly open the bag of winds. In the stories, he lived with his brother Boreas in a palace in Thrace.

His love affairs are numerous. With Iris, the goddess of the rainbow, he is said by Nonnus to have fathered Pothos, the god of desire. He carried off the nymph Chloris, to whom he gave dominion over flowers, and from this union came Carpus, the fruit. With the Harpy Podarge he fathered Balius and Xanthus, the horses of Achilles.

His best-known story is that of Hyacinthus, a Spartan prince he loved. Hyacinthus preferred Apollo, and Zephyrus, jealous, diverted with a gust the discus they were throwing together, and it struck the youth on the head. From his blood sprang the hyacinth flower.

Worship and religion

Several authors attest at least two temples in his honour, one on Rhodes and another in Attica. His cult was minor compared with that of the Twelve Olympians, and in Classical Athens and the surrounding region he was often worshipped alongside the other wind gods. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Legacy

His name survives in the word zephyr for a gentle breeze. The Greek colony of Epizephyrian Locris in southern Italy takes its name from a word linked to the west, the direction he personified. In Latin he was known as Favonius, a name very common in ancient Rome.

Ancient sources: Hesiod makes him the son of Astraeus and Eos, Homer mentions him in the Iliad and the Odyssey, and Aeschylus makes him earth-born in his Agamemnon.

Trivia

A colony with his name. The Greek colony of Epizephyrian Locris in southern Italy bears a name linked to the west, the same root as the god's name.
Swans and lament. According to Philostratus the Elder, Zephyrus joined the song of the swans in mourning Phaethon's death, linking his breeze with the singing of these birds.
