Egyptian deity, an aspect of Horus linked to Saturn

Horkapet

Horkapet, meaning “Horus the Bull of the Sky”, is a deity of ancient Egypt and an aspect of the great god Horus. In Egyptian astronomy he personified the planet Saturn, which makes him a clear example of how religion and sky-watching were intertwined.

The myth

The account appears in the Book of Nut, a collection of Egyptian astronomical texts, in the chapter devoted to the planets. Lines 153 to 159 describe a cosmic battle between Seth and Horus that took place at dusk.

According to the text, Seth’s celestial followers, and Seth himself, were said to have met in the western sky. The clash was brought to an end through the intervention of the god Thoth, who quelled it. Horus was traditionally placed in the west, and Saturn was called the “Star of the West”, so it seems very likely that, for the Egyptians, the planet and Horus in his aspect of Horkapet coincided.

Ancient sources: The Book of Nut, a collection of Egyptian astronomical texts, in its chapter on the planets.

Trivia

The Star of the West. Saturn was called the “Star of the West”, a name that fitted Horus’s association with the western sky.
Horus in many aspects. Horkapet is only one of the aspects of the god Horus, which the Egyptians distinguished according to their roles and places.
