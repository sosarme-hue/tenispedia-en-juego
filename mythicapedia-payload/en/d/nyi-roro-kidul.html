Queen of the Southern Sea in Javanese and Sundanese myth

Nyi Roro Kidul

Nyi Roro Kidul is a supernatural being in Indonesian folklore, queen of the Southern Sea in Sundanese and Javanese tradition. She is regarded as an ocean goddess who protects and punishes those who live along Java's southern coast, and her figure remains very present in the religious and everyday life of the island.

The myth

In a Sundanese legend, the heroine was a princess named Dewi Kadita, the only daughter of a king of Sunda. Her stepmother, Dewi Mutiara, wanted the throne for her own son and persuaded a shaman to curse the girl, whose body was covered in scabies, boils and a foul smell. Convinced the illness was a curse, the king exiled her from the palace.

Kadita wandered for almost seven days and seven nights until she reached the Southern Sea, where a supernatural voice told her to jump into the water. The waves cleared her skin without leaving a trace and made her even more beautiful. From then on she has ruled the ocean at the head of an army of phantoms, under the name Nyi Roro Kidul, and she is said to live forever. Palabuhanratu Bay is especially linked to this story.

In Javanese tradition she sometimes appears as a subordinate of Kanjeng Ratu Kidul, a higher-ranking figure, although many accounts identify the two as one. She is shown as a very beautiful woman or as a mermaid with a snake's or fish's lower body, and she is believed to be able to take the soul of anyone she chooses.

Worship and religion

She has been venerated on Java's south coast for centuries. Swallow-nest gatherers, who descend sheer cliffs to caves such as Karang Bolong, regard her as their patron and honour her with offerings and wayang performances and ritual dances to gamelan music at each harvest. In Palabuhanratu a festival in her honour is held every 6 April, with the sedekah laut ceremony, in which fishermen cast rice, batik, cosmetics and animals into the sea. There is also a belief that wearing green on that coast brings misfortune, since green is her favourite colour.

Legacy

She appears in Indonesian literature, folklore and many popular depictions of Javanese culture.
