Tiamat

Tiamat is the primordial goddess of the salt waters in Mesopotamian myth, and in the Enûma Eliš she also appears as a monster of chaos. She matters because her story of creation and defeat at the hands of Marduk is one of the oldest known cosmogonies.

The myth

According to the Enûma Eliš, Tiamat is the mother of the gods. Joined with Apsu, the god of the subterranean fresh waters, she formed a cosmic ocean from which the first divine generations were born, among them Lahmu and Lahamu, parents of Anshar and Kishar, who in turn begot Anu and Ki.

When the noise of the younger gods disturbed Apsu, he wanted to destroy them, but he was captured and killed by Enki. Enraged, Tiamat took Kingu as her consort, gave him the Tablet of Destinies, which stood for legitimate rule, and created eleven monstrous hybrids, including serpents, dragons, scorpion-men and fish-men, to avenge her husband. The terrified gods chose Marduk, Enki's son, as their champion, on condition that he be given supreme power.

Marduk first defeated Kingu and then Tiamat: he made her open her mouth with a fierce wind and shot an arrow into her belly. He then split her in two. From her upper half he made the sky and from her lower half the solid earth. From Kingu's blood humans were created, destined to serve the lesser gods. In some versions, Tiamat's tears became the sources of the Tigris and Euphrates, and her tail became the Milky Way.

Legacy

Tiamat shows how an ancient figure can be reshaped over time. Her parallels with Vritra, Typhon, Tehom or Nun have led scholars to compare her story with the Chaoskampf motif, the struggle of a culture hero against an aquatic monster. In modern fantasy, the tabletop game Dungeons & Dragons turned her into a five-headed chromatic dragon in the 1975 Greyhawk supplement. From there she passed to the 1983 television series, to Final Fantasy (1987), where she guards the Crystal of Wind, to Fate/Grand Order (2015), and to Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire (2024), where she is a serpentine Arctic monster defeated by Godzilla.

Ancient sources: The Babylonian epic Enûma Eliš is the main source. The Hellenistic writer Berossus also mentions her in his universal history.

Trivia

Two seas. The Arabic name of Bahrain means 'two seas'. The region, where fresh groundwater mixes with salt water, is linked to Dilmun, the original site of creation for the Sumerians.
Poison instead of blood. According to the Enûma Eliš, Tiamat filled the bodies of the monsters she created to fight the gods with poison instead of blood.
