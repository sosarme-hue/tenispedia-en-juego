river god of Greek mythology, formerly a mortal shepherd

Selemnus

Selemnus is the river god who personifies the Selemnos river in Achaea, in the northern Peloponnese. His tragic love story with the nymph Argyra, preserved by Pausanias, makes him an unusual figure among Greek river deities.

The myth

According to a local tradition from Patras, Selemnus was once a handsome young shepherd who tended his flock by the Argyra spring, near the town of the same name. The sea-nymph of that spring, Argyra, fell in love with him and often visited to sleep at his side.

As the years passed and Selemnus grew older and lost his good looks, Argyra visited less and less until she stopped altogether and returned to her watery home. Heartbroken, the shepherd wasted away and died of grief. Aphrodite took pity on him and turned him into a river that bore his name. Even as a river he still pined for Argyra, so the goddess erased all his memories of her and of his love. For this reason the people of Achaea bathed in his waters to rid themselves of erotic passions. Pausanias, who rarely comments on the legends he records, remarks that if true, the river would be worth more to humankind than any wealth.

Legacy

The story seems modelled on the myth of the river god Alpheus and the nymph Arethusa, to which Pausanias explicitly compares it. It also works as a doublet of the tale of Tithonus and Eos, in which an immortal goddess abandons her mortal lover as he ages, though Selemnus' fate is somewhat less grim. Propertius, for his part, wrote that Eos did not forsake the aged Tithonus and still held him in her arms.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece (2nd century AD), is the only surviving source that narrates the legend.

Trivia

A river that makes you forget. According to the legend, the people of Achaea bathed in the waters of the Selemnus to rid themselves of erotic passions.
