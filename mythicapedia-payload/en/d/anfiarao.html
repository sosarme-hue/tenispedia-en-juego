seer and hero of Greek mythology

Amphiaraus

Amphiaraus was a seer and hero of Greek mythology, king of Argos and one of the leaders of the Seven against Thebes. His story, shaped by a prophecy of his own death, inspired poets and artists and gave rise to a healing oracle.

The myth

According to tradition, Amphiaraus was the son of Oicles, although some authors made him the son of Apollo. He was married to Eriphyle, sister of Adrastus, with whom he ruled Argos. He foresaw that the expedition against Thebes would end in disaster and refused to join, but Eriphyle accepted the necklace of Harmonia offered by Polynices and persuaded him.

Before leaving he asked his sons Alcmaeon and Amphilochus to avenge his death. In battle he blamed Tydeus for starting the war, and when Tydeus devoured the brains of Melanippus, whose severed head Amphiaraus had brought him, Athena was revolted. As he fled from Periclymenus, Zeus opened the earth to swallow Amphiaraus and his chariot, and later made him immortal. Alcmaeon fulfilled his father's wish by killing his mother, and for this he was pursued by the Erinyes.

Worship and religion

At Oropus, on the northeast coast of Attica, he was revered as a healing hero and had an oracle. Those who wished to consult it made sacrifices and slept inside the sanctuary, awaiting a dream that would reveal a cure or the future. This practice is known as incubatio.

Legacy

Dante places him among the seers in Hell, in the Circle of Fraud. Schubert set to music a poem by Theodor Körner about him, and Lydgate includes him in his Siege of Thebes. According to an Etruscan tradition inherited by the Romans, his son Catillus founded the town of Tibur, now Tivoli. In philosophy, the Python, the first book to describe Pyrrhonism, begins in his temple.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned by Hyginus, Pausanias, Plutarch, Asius and Statius's Thebaid, among other ancient texts.

Trivia

A spear turned into laurel. In a story recorded by Statius, an eagle snatched Amphiaraus's spear and dropped it to the ground, where it took root and became a laurel tree.
A sanctuary of dreams. At Oropus, worshippers slept in the sanctuary hoping to receive a cure in their dreams, and games called the Amphiaria were held there.
