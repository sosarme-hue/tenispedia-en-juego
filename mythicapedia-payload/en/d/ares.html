Greek god of war

Ares

Ares is the Greek god of war and courage, son of Zeus and Hera and one of the Twelve Olympians. He embodies the physical valour needed in battle, but also brutality and bloodlust, in contrast to his sister Athena, whose martial role includes strategy and generalship.

The myth

In Hesiod, Ares is the son of Zeus and Hera, and with Aphrodite he fathers Phobos, Deimos and Harmonia. In Homer he is a far less dignified figure, savage and violent, disliked by the other gods and with little individual personality. In the Iliad his entourage embodies the horrors of war: Deimos ("Terror"), Phobos ("Flight"), the furious Eris ("Strife"), Kydoimos ("Tumult") and Enyo, the "sacker of cities", who serves as his charioteer.

In the Trojan War Ares first fights for one side and then for the other, a reflection, in the myth, of his chaotic nature. Zeus scolds him as the most hateful of the gods for his love of strife and fighting. Later stories also remember his affair with Aphrodite, which Hephaestus discovered and exposed to the ridicule of the gods by trapping the lovers in a net.

Late hymns give him more admirable traits: protector of cities, guide of just men, father of Victory (Nike) and helper of Justice (Dike). Ovid offers another account of his birth, in which Juno conceived Mars without union after touching a certain flower.

Worship and religion

For the Greeks, Ares was a dangerous force that had to be kept under control, so his few sanctuaries were usually built outside the city walls. Rituals aimed not to win his favour but to calm his violence or turn his fury against the enemy before a battle. His name already appears on Mycenaean tablets as a-re.

Still, in the Archaic and Classical periods he was not among the most widely worshipped gods, partly because he was considered of foreign, probably Thracian, origin. At Sparta there was a chained statue of Ares, showing that the spirit of war and victory would never leave the city. In Scythia he was worshipped in the form of a sword, and the Areopagus in Athens, the "hill of Ares", hosted trials from archaic times. Alexander the Great, a Macedonian, performed rites to the god on the eve of his battles. The sources do not describe a cult that survives today.

Legacy

His name lives on in the planet Mars, which astrology associates with him, and in the zodiac sign Aries, linked to the golden-fleeced ram. In the Iliad, the word ares works as a common noun meaning "battle". It also gave rise to the epithet areios, added to other gods such as Zeus Areios or Athena Areia when they took on a warlike role.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Homer's Iliad, Ovid and the Homeric and Orphic Hymns, among other ancient texts.

Trivia

A god in the form of a sword. In Scythia Ares was worshipped in the form of a sword, to which horses and cattle were sacrificed.
Birds of Ares. According to the Argonautica, the birds of Ares were a flock whose feathers could be hurled like darts at enemies.
