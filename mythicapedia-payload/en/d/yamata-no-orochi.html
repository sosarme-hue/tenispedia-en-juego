eight-headed serpent of Japanese mythology

Yamata no Orochi

Yamata no Orochi is a monstrous eight-headed, eight-tailed serpent or dragon from Japanese mythology, slain by the storm god Susanoo. Its story, recorded in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, explains the origin of the Kusanagi sword, one of Japan's Imperial Regalia.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, after being banished from heaven, the god Susanoo descended to Torikami in Izumo and followed the River Hi upstream until he met an elderly couple weeping beside their daughter. Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi explained that a serpent from Koshi had devoured their eight daughters, one each year, and now it was coming for the last, Kushinada-hime.

Susanoo agreed to fight the monster in exchange for the girl's hand. He turned Kushinada-hime into a comb worn in his hair, and had strong sake brewed and set in eight vats at eight gates. The serpent drank until it fell asleep, and Susanoo cut it to pieces. When his blade chipped on one of the tails, he found another sword inside it and later presented it to Amaterasu. The Nihon Shoki tells the story with several variations, such as the sword's name and where the monster was slain.

Worship and religion

In Shinto, Yamata no Orochi is venerated as a mountain deity. Local tradition holds that the creature survived its encounter with Susanoo and fled to Mount Ibuki, where it was worshipped.

Legacy

The tale has inspired many modern works, including video games, anime and comics, where the serpent appears as an enemy or a reference. It gave its name to a saga in The King of Fighters, and the Pokémon creature Hydreigon is partly based on it.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Nihon Shoki (720) are the oldest sources that record the myth.
