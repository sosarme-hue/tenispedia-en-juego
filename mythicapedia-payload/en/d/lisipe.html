queen of the Amazons in Greek mythology

Lysippe

Lysippe is a queen of the Amazons in Greek mythology, credited with founding their capital, Themiscyra, and with establishing many of the customs of this warrior people. Her story matters for how it combines military leadership, family tragedy and religion.

The myth

According to tradition, Lysippe was the daughter of the god Ares and the naiad Harmonia. She lived with her people by the river Amazonius when she had a son, Tanais, who loved war and scorned love. Aphrodite, offended by his disdain, filled him with an incestuous passion for his mother; ashamed, Tanais threw himself into the river.

Tormented by her son's spirit, Lysippe left the river, which would thereafter be called Tanais, and led the Amazons to the Black Sea coast, to the mouth of the Thermodon, where she founded Themiscyra. Together with Marpesia, Lampedo and Hippo, she extended the kingdom at the expense of Phrygia and Thrace. From the spoils she built temples to Ares and Artemis. She also instituted the separation of men and women: men were assigned domestic tasks, while girls were given government, hunting and war.
