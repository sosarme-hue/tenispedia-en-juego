Svanhildr

Svanhildr, known in English as Sunilda, is a heroine of Germanic and Norse legend, the daughter of Sigurd and Gudrun and noted for her beauty. Her death at the hands of her husband Ermanaric gave rise to one of the best-known tales of vengeance in northern Europe.

The myth

According to tradition, Svanhildr was the daughter of Sigurd and Gudrun, and her father married her to Ermanaric, king of the Goths. There she was accused of infidelity with Randver, the king's son, and Ermanaric had her trampled to death by horses.

Her mother sent her half-brothers Hamdir and Sörli to avenge her. The accounts differ in details: in the Poetic Edda and the Völsunga saga the revenge involves mutilating the king, while other versions have him die from spear wounds.

Ancient sources: Her story appears in the Poetic Edda (Hamðismál and Guðrúnarhvöt), the Prose Edda, the Völsunga saga, Bragi Boddason's Ragnarsdrápa and the Gesta Danorum.
