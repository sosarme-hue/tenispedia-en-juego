Norse god of desire, passion and inspiration

Óðr

Óðr is a Norse god, husband of Freyja and father of Hnoss and Gersemi, and one of the Vanir. He is associated with desire, passion and inspiration, but the surviving accounts of him are few and his figure remains largely a puzzle.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Óðr is the husband of Freyja, the goddess of the Vanir. The couple had two daughters, Hnoss and Gersemi, so lovely that their names gave rise to the words hnossir and gersamr, used for anything beautiful and precious. Óðr would leave on long journeys, and Freyja wept for him tears of red gold. Searching for him among strange peoples, she took many names, including Mardöll, Hörn, Gefn and Sýr.

The Völuspá in the Poetic Edda mentions him only in passing, in the phrase "Óðr's girl", a poetic way of naming Freyja. Heimskringla presents the couple in an euhemerized account, as real people of an ancient line. Some scholars suggest Óðr is another name for Odin, given the similarity of their names and their long journeys, although Snorri keeps them apart. Other theories link him to Adonis or to the tale of Cupid and Psyche, but all remain speculation.

Legacy

The name of his daughter Hnoss gave rise to the Old Norse word hnossir, meaning treasures or precious things. Freyja's tears of red gold and her search through foreign lands are the features of his story most often cited in the sources.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and Hyndluljóð in the Poetic Edda, Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and Ynglinga saga in Heimskringla.

Trivia

Tears of gold. According to the Prose Edda, Freyja wept red gold tears for Óðr, a detail that gives color to his story.
A name with two faces. Some ancient authors record that Óðr was also one of Odin's names, although Snorri himself treats them as separate gods.
