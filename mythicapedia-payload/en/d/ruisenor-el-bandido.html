Nightingale the Robber

Nightingale the Robber (Russian: Solovei Rakhmatich) is an epic bandit from the byliny, the traditional folk poems of the East Slavs. His story is linked to Ilya Muromets, one of the great heroes of Russian folklore.

The myth

According to the tellings, Nightingale lived in a forest, in a nest in a tree. He had partly human and partly bird-like features: he could fly, had a family, and drank with his hands. His weapon was a whistle so powerful that it tangled the grass, shed the blossoms from the flowers and bent the dark woods to the ground, and anyone who heard it died.

The legend holds that Ilya Muromets survived the whistle, although Nightingale flattened half the surrounding forest. The hero shot him down with arrows through the eye and temple and dragged him before Prince Vladimir of Kiev. Claiming he was too wounded to whistle, Nightingale asked for wine to heal himself; when he finally whistled, Vladimir's palaces were destroyed and many people died. Ilya then took him into an open field and cut off his head.

Legacy

The story is known from the seventeenth century, when Pavel Melnikov found a handwritten version and published it in the newspaper he edited in Nizhny Novgorod in 1845 and 1847. The bylina has been recorded 132 times, making it one of the most popular Russian epics. In 2007 Vladimir Toropchin released the animated film Ilya Muromets and Nightingale the Robber, and in 1956 Aleksandr Ptushko directed Ilya Muromets, in which the monster appears as the 'Wind Demon' in the English version.

Ancient sources: The Russian byliny, epic folk poems passed down orally and later written down; the oldest known version is a seventeenth-century manuscript.
