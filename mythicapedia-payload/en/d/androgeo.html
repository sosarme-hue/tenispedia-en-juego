son of Minos, athlete of Greek myth

Androgeus

Androgeus is a Cretan prince in Greek mythology, son of Minos and Pasiphaë, known for his outstanding athletic skill. His death in Athens led to war between Crete and Attica and to the tribute of young Athenians sent to the Minotaur.

The myth

According to the tradition, Androgeus was an athlete who excelled in every event. He travelled to Athens to compete in the Panathenaic Games and won every contest. King Aegeus, resentful of his success, sent him against the Marathonian Bull, and the young man was killed by the beast.

Minos, enraged, invaded Attica, won the war and imposed a tribute on Athens: seven youths and seven maidens to be sent to the Minotaur in the labyrinth of Crete.

Other traditions told of his death differently. Some said he died on his way to Thebes, where he was to compete in games honouring Laius; others that he fell in a war between Cretans and Athenians. Diodorus Siculus says Aegeus killed him fearing he would back the sons of Pallas. Pausanias presents the bull death as an accident, though Minos reportedly did not believe it. The Athenians later honoured him as a hero, with an altar at Phaleron.

Worship and religion

The Athenians established a hero cult for Androgeus, with an altar dedicated to him at Phaleron. According to the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus, Minos received news of his son's death while sacrificing to the Charites on Paros; he halted the music and garlands, and the festivals there kept that custom afterwards.

Legacy

Androgeus is best known as the background to Athens' tribute and the Minotaur story, and as one of Minos's sons whose fate is tied to the legend of Theseus.

Ancient sources: Accounts of his death appear in Diodorus Siculus, Pausanias, Plutarch, Servius and the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus; Propertius mentions a version in which Asclepius restores him to life.

Trivia

Altar at Phaleron. The Athenians dedicated an altar to him at Phaleron, showing that he received heroic cult.
Festivals without music. According to the Bibliotheca, the festivals of the Charites on Paros had no music or flowers after Androgeus's death.
