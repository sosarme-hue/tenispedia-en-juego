legendary gift-giver of Christmas Eve

Santa Claus

Santa Claus, also known as Father Christmas, Saint Nicholas or Papá Noel depending on the region, is a legendary figure of Western Christian folklore who brings gifts to children on Christmas Eve. His image blends the memory of a 4th-century bishop, Nicholas of Myra, with English, Dutch and Nordic folk traditions, which is why he has become the great emblem of the modern Christmas. According to the tradition, Santa Claus makes the toys with the help of elves in a workshop and delivers them by air in a sleigh pulled by flying reindeer. Good children receive toys or sweets, while naughty ones, according to the legend, find coal or nothing at all, depending on the list he keeps. The story of Nicholas of Myra says that this bishop, born in Patara in Lycia, in Asia Minor, inherited a great fortune and gave it to the poor. One of his best-known tales tells how he helped a ruined father who could not pay dowries for his three daughters: Nicholas secretly tossed bags of gold coins through the window, so the family would not feel humiliated. The figure changed over the centuries. In the Middle Ages, gifts were given on the evening of 5 December, the eve of the feast of Saint Nicholas. The Protestant Reformation moved the gift-giving to Christmas, and Martin Luther proposed the Christkind as the giver, although Saint Nicholas remained the people's favourite. In England appeared Father Christmas, a jolly, large man in green or scarlet robes trimmed with fur, a symbol of Christmas good cheer. In Norse folklore, the god Odin, who rode the winter sky on the midwinter night, may have influenced features such as the white beard or the night journeys. Finally, in the United States in the 19th century, the figure took on its definitive shape.

Trivia

From Dutch to English. The name Santa Claus derives phonetically from Sinterklaas, the Dutch Saint Nicholas brought to New Amsterdam by Dutch settlers.
A helper with a broom. In Switzerland, the sinister companion Schmutzli carries a twig broom to spank naughty children.
