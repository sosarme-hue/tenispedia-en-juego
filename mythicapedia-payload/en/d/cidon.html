Cretan hero of Greek mythology

Cydon

Cydon is a hero of Greek mythology, the eponym of the Cretan city of Cydonia, whose story mixes foundation, secret love and sacrifice. The account is preserved by Pausanias in his Description of Greece.

The myth

According to the version told by the Tegeans themselves, Cydon was a son of Tegeates and Mera, daughter of Atlas. After his brothers died, he, Archedius and Gortys voluntarily emigrated to Crete, and the cities of Cydonia and Gortyna were named after them. The Cretans, however, held that he was the son of Acacallis, daughter of Minos, and of Hermes or Apollo.

On Crete, his daughter Eulimene was at the center of the story. She had been promised to Apterus, but the young Licastus visited her in secret. When rebels attacked Cydon, the oracle told him to sacrifice a maiden to the heroes of the land, and the lot fell on his own daughter. Licastus then confessed their relationship, but the assembly decided she should die. After the sacrifice, the priest opened her body and discovered she was pregnant. Apterus, outraged by the insult, ambushed and killed Licastus, then fled to the court of Janthus in Termera.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records both the Tegean version and the Cretan version of Cydon's origin.
