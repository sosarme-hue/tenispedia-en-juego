legendary island of the Atlantic, also known as the Isle of Seven Cities

Antillia

Antillia is a legendary island that medieval maps placed somewhere in the Atlantic, far west of Portugal and Spain. Its legend, rooted in the Iberian Peninsula, gave the Antilles their name and fuelled centuries of searching for hidden lands across the ocean.

The myth

According to the legend, around the year 714, during the Muslim conquest of Hispania, seven Christian Visigothic bishops, led by the Bishop of Porto, boarded ships with their parishioners and sailed west to escape the conquerors. After reaching an island, the story goes, they burned their ships to cut every link with their former homeland, now under Muslim rule, and founded seven cities.

The versions vary. In 1539 Ferdinand Columbus adjusted the date and added the episode of the burned ships. Another variant, recorded by Manuel de Faria e Sousa in 1628, tells of Sacaru, a Visigothic governor of Mérida who set out for the Canary Islands and may have ended up on an island populated by Portuguese speakers. In these accounts the island could be seen on the horizon but vanished as sailors drew near.

Legacy

The name Antillia passed to the Caribbean archipelago of the Antilles after 1492. The island appeared on medieval charts, from a 1424 chart to Martin Behaim's globe (1492) and Johannes Ruysch's world map (1507). The legend also inspired the tale of the Seven Cities of Gold, which conquistadors sought on the American mainland. Portuguese kings even mentioned it in official documents: in 1475 Afonso V granted Fernão Teles the Seven Cities he might find, and in 1486 John II authorised Fernão Dulmo to search for it.

Ancient sources: It does not appear in ancient texts; its best-known versions come from sixteenth- and seventeenth-century chroniclers such as Pedro de Medina, Ferdinand Columbus, António Galvão and Faria e Sousa.

Trivia

A name that survives. The Antilles archipelago took its name from this legendary island after 1492, although the island itself was never found.
Silver in the sands. As early as the 1490s, rumours claimed that silver could be found in the island's sands.
A map from 1507. Johannes Ruysch's world map, published in 1507, places Antillia southeast of Greenland with a lengthy legend.
