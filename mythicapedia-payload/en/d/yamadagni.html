Hindu sage, father of Parashurama

Jamadagni

Jamadagni is a rishi, or sage, in Hindu mythology, counted among the seven sages (saptarishi) and the father of the warrior Parashurama, whom tradition regards as an avatar of Vishnu. His story, shaped by obedience, anger and revenge, is one of the best-known tales in the Hindu tradition.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, Jamadagni was the son of the sage Richika and a descendant of Bhrigu. He married Renuka, and the two lived with their sons in a hermitage by a river. One day Renuka returned from the water later than usual, and the sage, imagining she had been distracted by celestial beings, ordered his sons to kill her. All refused except the youngest, Rama, who obeyed and beheaded her with his axe. Pleased by this devotion, his father granted him a wish, and Rama asked for his mother's life to be restored, earning the name Parashurama, 'Rama of the axe'. Other versions, such as the Brahmanda Purana, say Parashurama killed his mother with a single arrow and that the father exiled his four older brothers.

Later, the Kshatriya king Kartavirya Arjuna stole Surabhi, or Kamadhenu, the wish-granting cow, from Jamadagni. Parashurama slew the king in turn, but the king's sons avenged him by killing the sage. Parashurama then waged a long campaign against the warrior caste, which tradition says he wiped out over twenty-four generations. According to the account, Shiva brought the sage back to life and made him one of the seven sages.

Legacy

His name is linked to that of Parashurama, one of the most revered figures in Hinduism, and to the tradition of the seven sages. Buddhist Vinaya texts also mention him with respect among the ancient rishis to whom the Vedas were revealed.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana, the Brahmanda Purana, the Mahabharata and the Buddhist Vinaya Pitaka.

Trivia

Sandals and an umbrella. In the Mahabharata, Jamadagni becomes annoyed with the sun god Surya for the heat he causes. Surya appears to him as a Brahmin and gives him sandals and an umbrella to shield himself.
Dharma's test. In the Mahabharata, the god Dharma takes the form of Anger and slips into the milk of the cow Kamadhenu. Jamadagni drinks it without losing his composure, and Dharma blesses him with a righteous life.
