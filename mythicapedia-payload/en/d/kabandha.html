headless demon of Hindu mythology

Kabandha

Kabandha is a rakshasa (demon) in Hindu mythology, whose name means "headless torso". According to tradition, he was once a gandharva (celestial musician) named Vishvavasu, transformed into a monster, and his story appears in the epics Ramayana and Mahabharata.

The myth

According to the legend, Vishvavasu was a gandharva, son of Sri, who received immortality from Brahma. Growing arrogant, he attacked Indra, who struck him with the thunderbolt Vajra and drove his head and thighs into his body. Vishvavasu begged for a way to find food, and Indra gave him two long arms and a mouth on his belly, decreeing that he would regain his original form when Rama cut off his arms.

In the Ramayana, the sage Sthulashira cursed him to keep this appearance forever, though after Kabandha's pleading the curse was softened: he would be freed once Rama and Lakshmana severed his arms. Kabandha waited in the Krauncha forest for their arrival. When the brothers, searching for Sita, who had been kidnapped by Ravana, came upon him, he seized them, and Lakshmana suggested cutting off his hands, which they did. Before dying, Kabandha regained his gandharva form, asked for funeral rites, and advised Rama to seek an alliance with Sugriva, the monkey-king hiding on Mount Rishyamukha.

Legacy

His story was retold in many later works: Kalidasa included it in the Raghuvamsa, Bhatti in the Bhattikavya, Bhavabhuti in the Mahaviracharita, and Tulsidas in the Ramacharitamanas. In everyday usage, the word kabandha also describes a big-bellied barrel or a trunk shaped like one that still seems full of life.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana (Bala and Aranya kanda), the Mahabharata (Aranya Parva and its appendix, the Harivamsa), and the Adhyatma Ramayana.

Trivia

A name that describes his body. Kabandha means "headless torso", because after losing his head Vishvavasu kept only two arms and a mouth on his belly.
A different version. In the Mahaviracharita, Kabandha plays a different role: he captures a woman named Shramana, and it is Lakshmana who kills him before bringing her to Rama.
