Viking king of Södermanland

Granmar

Granmar was a Viking chieftain and king of Södermanland in Sweden, according to Norse tradition. His story, recorded by Snorri Sturluson, is tied to the wars against King Ingjald and to the legend of Helgi Hundingsbane.

The myth

According to Heimskringla, Granmar ruled Södermanland and was married to Hilda, daughter of the Geatish king Högne. Together with his son-in-law Hjörvard of the Ylfings and Högne, he formed an alliance to defend their lands against Ingjald, a Swedish ruler notorious for his cruelty. Snorri tells how Ingjald invited several kings to a feast in order to burn them alive; Granmar escaped because he did not attend.

A truce later followed, but Ingjald broke it. One night he surrounded a farm on Selaön where Granmar and Hjörvard were holding a feast and burned it to the ground, an act known as hús-brenna. Heimskringla says Granmar had two sons, while the legend of Helgi Hundingsbane names three: Hothbrodd, Gudmund and Starkad, all slain by Helgi.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla, the Volsunga saga, and the Poetic Edda poems Helgakviða Hundingsbana I and II.

Trivia

Hús-brenna. This is the name given to burning a house with its occupants inside, like the attack Ingjald ordered against Granmar and Hjörvard.
A historic waterway. The Myrkva fjord, where Hjörvard arrived according to the saga, is today the inlet Mörköfjärden, still used to pass between Lake Mälaren and the Baltic Sea.
