hero of Greek mythology, son of Porthaon

Agrius

Agrius is a hero of Greek mythology, son of the Calydonian king Porthaon and brother of Oeneus. His story, tied to a dispute over the kingdom of Calydon in Aetolia, shows how his sons seized the throne and how the feud was settled.

The myth

According to the tradition, Agrius was the son of Porthaon and Euryte and brother of Oeneus, prince of Calydon. He fathered six sons, including Thersites, and together they seized Oeneus's kingdom and handed it to their father.

Accounts of the ending vary. The Bibliotheca places these events before the Greek expedition against Troy, when Diomedes, Oeneus's grandson, killed most of Agrius's sons and restored the throne to his grandfather. Hyginus says Diomedes returned after the fall of Troy and drove Agrius out, and Agrius then took his own life. Other sources say Diomedes himself killed Agrius and all his sons.

Legacy

The city of Agrinio, the largest in Aetolia, takes its name from him.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in the Bibliotheca, and Hyginus, along with other ancient sources that describe Agrius's death at the hands of Diomedes.

Trivia

A city bearing his name. Agrinio, the largest city in Aetolia, takes its name from Agrius, keeping his memory alive in modern Greek geography.
Two different endings. Apollodorus and Hyginus disagree on how the story ends: in one version Diomedes kills Agrius's sons during the Trojan War, while in the other Agrius takes his own life after being expelled.
