dragon of Basque mythology

Herensuge

The herensuge is a dragon from Basque mythology that flew leaving fire in its wake and preyed on horses and people. It is one of the best-known creatures in the folklore of the Basque Country and Navarre.

The myth

According to Basque tradition, the herensuge flew trailing fire and a terrifying sound. It fed on horses and donkeys, and sometimes seized people, drowning them, devouring them or driving them mad. It could lure animals with its breath. Accounts differ: some give it seven heads and others only one, and it was said to have several lairs.

How it died is told in several ways, each with a different hero. In one, a prince aided by the giant Tartalo defeats the seven-headed monster and marries the princess destined for it. In another, the Navarrese knight Teodosio de Goñi, chained to the dragon during his penance at Aralar, calls on Saint Michael, who ends up beheading it.

Trivia

Inherited heads. According to one account, the seven heads were offspring of the dragon itself, which fell from its mother's head once they were fully grown.
Many names. It is known by more than ten names, such as Erensuge, Lerensuge or Igensuge, reflecting the variety of oral versions.
