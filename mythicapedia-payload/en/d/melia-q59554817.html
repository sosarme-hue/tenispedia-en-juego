Oceanid nymph of Greek mythology

Melia

Melia is an Oceanid, a water nymph of Greek mythology, mother of Phoroneus and Aegialeus by the river-god Inachus. She matters because Argive tradition makes her son Phoroneus the first man, or first inhabitant, of Argos.

The myth

According to the tradition, Melia was one of the three thousand water nymphs, daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys. She became the consort of her brother Inachus, the river-god of Argos, and bore him Phoroneus, a culture hero, and Aegialeus, also called Phegeus in some accounts.

Other accounts say Inachus fathered Phoroneus with the Oceanid Argia instead. Melia was also named mother, by Inachus, of Mycene, wife of Arestor and eponym of Mycenae, and perhaps of Io, ancestress by Zeus of the royal houses of Argos, Thebes and Crete. Some sources even make her the consort of Apollo, a god highly honoured at Thebes.
