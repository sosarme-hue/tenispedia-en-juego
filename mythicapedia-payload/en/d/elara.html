mother of the giant Tityos in Greek mythology

Elara

Elara is, in Greek mythology, a princess described as the daughter of King Orchomenus or of Minyas, loved by Zeus and mother of the giant Tityos. She matters mainly for the story of Tityos's underground birth and for the cave named after her, an episode that shows how the tradition joined the divine and earthly origins of a giant.

The myth

According to the myth, Zeus fell in love with Elara, but fearing the jealous rage of his wife Hera, he hid her deep beneath the earth. There she gave birth to Tityos, a giant born of the earth who, in some versions, was a son of Gaia, the earth goddess, and was raised under the ground. The sources do not agree on Elara's parentage: some authors called her the daughter of King Orchomenus, others of Minyas, and in one account she appears under the name Larisa.

Hesiod called Tityos by the metronymic Eilarides, meaning son of Elara, and later writers combined his two descents, from Elara and from Gaia, into a single story. Another version adds that Elara died in childbirth because the baby was enormous. When the time came, Tityos came up to the surface through a cave on Euboea, which was named the Elarion in honour of his mother.

Legacy

One of the moons of Jupiter is named after Elara, a modern use of the mythological name that has no connection to the ancient story. Her figure appears mainly in astronomy and in scholarly references to the myths of the giant Tityos.

Ancient sources: Hesiod refers to her indirectly by calling Tityos Eilarides, son of Elara.

Trivia

The Elarion cave. The cave through which Tityos was believed to have reached the surface lay on Euboea and was called the Elarion, in honour of his mother.
A death in childbirth. Some versions say Elara died giving birth to Tityos, because the baby was so enormous.
