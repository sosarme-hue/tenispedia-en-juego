form of the goddess Parvati in Hinduism

Katyayani

Katyayani is a form of the Hindu goddess Parvati or Durga, venerated as the slayer of the demon Mahisha. She is the sixth of the Navadurga and is honoured on the sixth day of the Navaratri festival, within Shaktism.

The myth

According to the Vamana Purana, the gods, enraged by the tyranny of Mahisha, released flames from their eyes and faces that formed a mountain of light, from which Katyayani emerged with three eyes, black hair and eighteen arms. Shiva, Vishnu and the other gods gave her their weapons, and so armed she set out for the Mysore hills. Mahisha wished to marry her, but she insisted she must be won in battle. In the fight, she leapt onto the bull Mahisha had become, struck his head with her feet and beheaded him, and from then on she was called Mahishasuramardini.

The tradition also tells that she was born from the anger of the gods, according to the Skanda Purana, or that the sage Katyayana was the first to worship her, according to the Kalika Purana. In the regional legend of Karvir, she fought the demon Raktabija and drank his blood so that not a drop fell to the ground. In the Bhagavata Purana, the young gopis of Vraja observed the katyayani vrata during the month of Margashirsha to win Krishna as their husband.

Worship and religion

Her festival is held on the sixth day of Navaratri, and her exploits are commemorated during Durga Puja across much of India. In the south, young girls keep penances during Pongal and shape an idol from wet sand. Temples dedicated to her exist in many regions, and in Tantra she is linked to the ajna chakra, the third eye, where devotees concentrate to invoke her blessing.

Ancient sources: She is first mentioned in the Taittiriya Aranyaka of the Yajurveda, and the Devi Bhagavata Purana and Devi Mahatmya describe her exploits.
