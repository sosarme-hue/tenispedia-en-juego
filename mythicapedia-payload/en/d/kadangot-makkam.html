regional Hindu goddess of North Malabar

Kadangot Makkam

Kadangot Makkam is a regional goddess worshipped in North Malabar, in Kerala (India), who is believed to have been a heroic woman that attained divine status after a violent death. She is especially honoured through theyyam, the ritual performance in which a performer embodies the deity.

The myth

According to tradition, Makkam was the only daughter of a noble family of Kunhimangalam, near Payyannur, and the only sister among twelve warrior brothers of the Kolathiri king. She had twin sons, Chathu and Chiru, from her marriage to a maternal cousin.

While the brothers were away at war, their sisters-in-law, who disliked her, spread a slander that she was involved with an oil seller. When the oil arrived at a time when Makkam was ritually impure, she asked for it to be left inside the house, and the sisters-in-law used the moment to accuse her before the brothers, who resolved to kill her.

Fleeing towards Kottayam, mother and children reached Chala exhausted, where an old woman gave them milk; Makkam left a ring in the vessel and walked on. Beside a well near Kayalode, the brothers murdered her and her sons, along with a Mavilan man who had witnessed the scene. Only Kuti Rama, the youngest brother, refused to take part. Tradition says Makkam became a fierce deity and set fire to the family home, sparing only the room of her protective goddess, Veera Chamundi. She then appeared in a dream to the old woman of Chala, asking for a shrine, and people began to worship her with her children and the Mavilan.

Worship and religion

Makkam, her children and the Mavilan are worshipped in the form of theyyam, a ritual performance in which the officiant embodies the deity. The figure is also known as Makkappothi. Her theyyam is performed during the annual festival of her shrines, and families wishing for children may sponsor a performance.

Ancient sources: The story of Makkam was written in kilippattu style and published in 1918 as Kadangot Makkam (kilippattu) by K. M. Kunjilakshmi Kuteilamba, Kuttamath Kanyoor Kunhikrishna Kurup and K. M. K. Nair.
