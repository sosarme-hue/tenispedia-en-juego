Koan woman turned into a bird in Greek myth

Byssa

Byssa is a woman from the Greek island of Kos, daughter of Eumelus and sister of Agron and Meropis. Her story, preserved by Antoninus Liberalis, tells how she was turned into a bird for refusing to honour Artemis, Athena and Hermes.

The myth

Byssa lived on Kos with her father Eumelus, her brother Agron and her sister Meropis. Although they worshipped the island's local goddess, they refused to honour Artemis, Athena and Hermes. When neighbours invited them to feasts or sacrifices for those gods, they declined, since they disliked grey eyes, owls, a goddess who roamed at night, and thieves.

One night Artemis, Athena and Hermes visited them disguised as two country maidens and a shepherd. Hermes persuaded Agron and Eumelus to sacrifice to him, but Byssa and Meropis still refused to honour the goddesses. All four were turned into birds. Byssa became a byssa bird, sacred to the goddess Leucothea.

Some scholars have suggested it was a kind of horned owl, given its likeness to the words buza, buxa and buas, which refer to owls. Francis Celoria argued instead that a bird sacred to Leucothea would more likely be a seabird, such as a shearwater or a gull.

Ancient sources: Her story survives in the works of Antoninus Liberalis.

Trivia

Owl or seabird?. Some propose a horned owl because its name resembles words for owl, while Celoria thought of a seabird.
