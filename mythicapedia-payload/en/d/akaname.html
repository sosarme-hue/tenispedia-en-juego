Japanese yōkai that licks filth from bathrooms

Akaname

The akaname is a Japanese yōkai that, according to tradition, lives in old, neglected bathrooms, where it licks up the grime that builds up in bathtubs, floors and even toilets with its long tongue. Its name means 'scum-licker', and it is one of the most charming figures in popular Japanese folklore.

The myth

The name comes from aka, the dead skin and grime that is scrubbed off the body in the bath, combined with the idea of licking. The earliest known version of the name with the same meaning appears in a 1686 kaidan book, but the classic image comes from Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō (1776), where the creature stands in a corner of a bathhouse with no written description. The Nittō honzō zusan does give one: a childlike being with a pebbly head, round eyes and a very long tongue.

According to the stories, the akaname is harmless and even kindly, since it cleans up what people neglect. Nobody knows exactly how it gets in and out, but the tradition suggests its presence was a warning to keep the bath clean. A more sinister variant, the akaneburi, takes the form of a beautiful woman and licks the flesh of anyone who lets her scrub their back until only the bones remain. The Nittō honzō zusan tells of a man at the hot springs of Banshū who died this way.

Legacy

The akaname has entered modern popular culture. It appears in the song 'Filthlicker (Akaname)' by the British heavy metal band Sa-da-ko, from their 2013 album Awakenings. In The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask, a creature lives in a hotel toilet in Clock Town and may have been inspired by the legend. Kenkou Cross's Monster Girl Encyclopedia includes it, and it also features in an episode of Yo-kai Watch. In 2020, Lush released a bath bomb named after it and modeled on it.

Ancient sources: It appears in an 1686 kaidan book and in Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō (1776), and its detailed description comes from the Nittō honzō zusan.

Trivia

A play on words. The aka of filth sounds the same as aka, meaning 'red' in Japanese. For this reason, many modern artists tend to draw the akaname in red.
An episode of Yo-kai Watch. In the localized version, the protagonist Nate is possessed by an akaname named Tublappa, which forces him to lick food and people's hair. He is freed when his friend Katie gives him a salty-sweet candy.
