Hindu folk hero, grandson of Bhima

Barbarika

Barbarika is a hero of Hindu tradition, son of Ghatotkacha and the princess Maurvi, and grandson of the Pandava Bhima. He belongs mainly to folk tradition and regional legend, since the Mahabharata does not mention him, which makes him a good example of how the epic was adapted to local beliefs.

The myth

According to popular accounts, Barbarika learned the art of warfare from his mother, who was also known as Kaamktankta. His lineage links him to two prominent figures of the epic: his grandfather Bhima, second of the Pandava brothers, and his father Ghatotkacha, born of Bhima and the rakshasi Hidimba. The goddess Durga gave him three infallible arrows, each able to finish its task with a single shot.

In Nepal, tradition identifies Barbarika with the Kirati king Yalambar, to whom an encounter with Indra, lord of heaven, is attributed; Indra had come to the valley in human form. The people of the Kathmandu Valley also venerate him as Akash Bhairav, so in that region his figure connects the memory of the king with that of a protective deity.

Worship and religion

In Rajasthan he is worshipped as Khatu Shyam at the Khatu Shyam Temple, one of the best-known shrines in the region. In Gujarat he is venerated as Baliyadev. These local forms show how a single hero can become an object of devotion in different places, with its own names and rites.

Trivia

Three infallible arrows. Tradition credits the goddess Durga with giving Barbarika three arrows that never miss.
A hero with two names in Nepal. In the Kathmandu Valley he is venerated as Akash Bhairav, while Nepali tradition identifies him with King Yalambar.
