elderly woman of Greek myth who sheltered Theseus

Hecale

Hecale is an elderly woman in Greek mythology who gave Theseus shelter from a storm on his way to Marathon, where he was going to capture the Marathonian Bull. Her story matters for the hospitality it embodies and for the local memory it preserved of such a modest figure.

The myth

According to the tradition, Theseus was heading to Marathon to capture the bull that was troubling the people of the Tetrapolis when a storm forced him to seek refuge in the hut of Hecale, an old woman who lived alone. She welcomed him warmly and spoke to him as an old woman would to a young man, using affectionate pet names. Before he set out again, she vowed to sacrifice to Zeus if he returned safely.

Theseus did overcome the bull, but when he came back to her hut, Hecale had died. In gratitude he ordered that she be honoured: an Attic deme was named after her and belonged to the tribe of Leontis. The story survives in a fragmentary poem by Callimachus called Hecale and in Plutarch's Life of Theseus, which also cites Philochorus.

Worship and religion

Residents of the surrounding demes gathered in Athens for the Hecalesia, a festival honouring Zeus Hecalesius and Hecale herself, whom they affectionately called Hecaline. The sources do not document whether this cult survived into later times.

Legacy

A residential district on the northern edge of Athens, Ekáli, on the western slopes of Mount Penteli, still carries a name that comes from hers.

Ancient sources: Callimachus, in his fragmentary poem Hecale, and Plutarch, in his Life of Theseus.

Trivia

An affectionate pet name. Her neighbours called her Hecaline, a diminutive that, according to Plutarch, came from the way she treated Theseus with the affection of a grandmother.
A deme named after her. An Attic deme bore the name Hecale and belonged to the tribe of Leontis, as a reward for her hospitality.
