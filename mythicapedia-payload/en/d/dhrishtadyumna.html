Dhrishtadyumna

Dhrishtadyumna is a hero of the Mahabharata, the great Hindu epic, a prince of Panchala and son of King Drupada. He matters chiefly because, according to tradition, he fulfilled the prophecy of his birth by slaying Drona during the Kurukshetra War.

The myth

Dhrishtadyumna is the son of King Drupada of Panchala and the brother of Draupadi, the shared wife of the five Pandavas. According to the myth, he was born from a sacrificial fire (yajna) that Drupada commissioned to obtain a son capable of defeating Drona, a former friend who had humiliated him and whom the Pandavas later helped to capture him. The officiating sages poured the offering before the queen had finished purifying herself, and from the flames emerged a youth with a fiery complexion, crowned and armed. Then came Draupadi, described in the text as ayonija, born without a womb.

As a young man, Drona accepted him as a student despite knowing the prophecy, and trained him in the military arts and celestial weapons until he became a maharathi. Dhrishtadyumna hosted his sister's svayamvara and, when the young Brahmin who won her turned out to be Arjuna, he secretly followed them to confirm it.

In the Kurukshetra War he was appointed senapati, commander-in-chief of the Pandava army, and kept the post until the end. The Bhishma Parva shows him at the front of the fighting, facing Drona, Bhishma and other great warriors. On the fifteenth day, when Drona, believing his son Ashvatthama dead, had laid down his weapons and was meditating, Dhrishtadyumna beheaded him, thus fulfilling the prophecy. According to the account, on the last night of the war Ashvatthama attacked the Pandava camp, wounded and killed him, and his body vanished.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, especially its Adi, Bhishma and Drona Parvas.

Trivia

Born from fire. According to the account, Dhrishtadyumna and Draupadi emerged from the sacrificial fire rather than a womb, which is why they are called ayonija.
A favour returned. Bhima had to be rescued by Dhrishtadyumna during a battle, and later Dhrishtadyumna repaid the debt by saving Bhima in turn.
Battle formations. He is credited with organising battle formations shaped like a crane, a half-moon, a hawk and a crocodile, used on different days.
