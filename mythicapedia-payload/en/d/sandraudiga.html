Germanic goddess of Zundert

Sandraudiga

Sandraudiga is a goddess of Germanic tradition known from a Latin inscription found in North Brabant, in the Netherlands. The meaning of her name is still debated, and it links her to a local cult from the Roman period.

The myth

The surviving sources on Sandraudiga are almost entirely epigraphic, so no detailed myth survives about her origins, family or deeds. Her figure is known mainly from the votive stone.

Scholars have proposed several readings of her name: some connect it with Germanic words for "true" and "rich", reading her as a goddess of abundance and fertility; others take it as "she who dyes the sand red", alluding to the iron-rich soil of the area; and others see her as a protector of the settlement that gave its name to Zundert.

Worship and religion

A limestone altar, found in 1812 near Tiggelt and Rijsbergen and dated between the 2nd and 3rd centuries, bears the Latin inscription "DEAE SANDRAUDIGAE CULTORES TEMPLI", recording that the temple's cultores donated the stone. Excavations in the 1950s uncovered remains of a possible temple at the site, dated to the 2nd century.

Legacy

Today the goddess lives on in local popular culture: a float bearing her image took part in the Zundert flower parade, winning both a jury prize and a public prize.

Ancient sources: She is known only from the Latin inscription on the Tiggelt altar, which is her main testimony; no ancient literary texts mention her.

Trivia

A stone with cornucopias. The altar shows cornucopias on both sides, a detail that led some scholars to see the goddess as a deity of abundance.
A ligature in the name. In the inscription, the final A and E of the goddess's name are carved together as a single letter, Æ.
