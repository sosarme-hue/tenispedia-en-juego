five-headed form of Ganesha

Heramba

Heramba is an iconographic form of the Hindu god Ganesha, shown with five elephant heads. He is especially popular in Nepal and plays an important part in the Tantric worship of Ganesha.

The myth

According to the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, the name is explained this way: the syllable he denotes helplessness or weakness, while ramba means protecting the weak from harm, so Heramba is the protector of the weak and of good people.

Tradition presents him as a son of Shiva and Parvati. He is described with ten arms and a golden yellow or sometimes white complexion. He rides a lion, which he inherited from his mother and which stands for royalty and fierce nature. Sometimes he is also shown with Dinka, the rat that is Ganesha's usual mount.

Worship and religion

Heramba protects the weak and is believed to grant courage and to bring defeat or destruction to enemies. The Tantric Hairamba or Heramba sect worships him alongside Devi or Shakti, regarded as his mother and the consort of Shiva. He was also linked to the six abhichara rites, spells used for malevolent purposes.

Ancient sources: The Mudgala Purana, the Skanda Purana, the Padma Purana and the Brahma Vaivarta Purana list his name among the epithets of Ganesha.

Trivia

Five heads. Each head is linked to one of the five aspects of Shiva: Ishana, Tatpurusha, Aghora, Vamadeva and Sadyojata.
Varied attributes. His attributes include a noose, his broken tusk, a rosary, a battle-axe and the sweet modak.
