Leucrota

The leucrota is a legendary beast, a blend of hyena, lion and stag, famous for mimicking human voices to lure its prey. It comes from accounts of India or Ethiopia that reached the West through Greek and Roman writers.

The myth

According to ancient authors, the leucrota lives in India or Ethiopia and is a deadly enemy of men and dogs. Ctesias mentions it in his Indika, and Pliny the Elder describes it as a swift animal the size of an ass, with a badger's head and a mouth stretching back to its ears, its teeth replaced by a single bony ridge.

Its most feared trait is its ability to imitate human voices: it calls travellers by name from the thickets and attacks them from behind as they approach. It was also said to dig up corpses. Only fire, and specifically Greek fire, could kill it.

Legacy

The scientific name of the spotted hyena, Crocuta crocuta, comes from the ancient crocotta. Jorge Luis Borges included it in his Book of Imaginary Beings, it appears in Susanna Clarke's novel Jonathan Strange & Mr Norrell, and in Rick Riordan's series The Demigod Diaries.

Ancient sources: Ctesias, Strabo, Pliny the Elder, Aelian and Porphyry mention it.

Trivia

A hyena's name. The scientific name of the spotted hyena derives from the mythical creature.
An invented word. The poet Cyril Tourneur used the coined word leucrocutanized in his allegorical poem The Transformed Metamorphosis.
