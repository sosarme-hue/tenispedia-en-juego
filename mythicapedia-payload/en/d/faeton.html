Greek hero, son of Eos

Phaethon

Phaethon is a figure of Greek mythology, son of the goddess Eos (Dawn) and, depending on the account, of Cephalus of Athens or Tithonus. Born in Syria according to the reference text, he matters mainly for the lineage attributed to him, which leads to King Cinyras of Cyprus, and for the alternative name Adymnus found in some sources.

The myth

Tradition holds that Aphrodite carried him off while he was still a child to serve as night-watchman at her most sacred shrines. The account thus places him within the sphere of the goddess of love, and it offers no independent adventures from his life.

His descendants are better documented: Phaethon fathered Astynous, who in turn fathered Sandocus, and Sandocus was the father of the famous King Cinyras of Cyprus.

Trivia

A lineage down to Cinyras. Phaethon is the first link in a chain of three generations that ends with Cinyras, king of Cyprus.
An alternative name. He also appears as Adymnus, a variant of his name in other sources.
