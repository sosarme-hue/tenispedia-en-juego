Egyptian child deity of Akhmim

Kolanthes

Kolanthes is a child deity from the late period of ancient Egyptian religion, worshipped in the circle of the gods of Akhmim, in the ninth Upper Egyptian nome. His name is spelled in many different ways, which suggests he may not have been of Egyptian origin.

The myth

Within the divine family of Akhmim, Kolanthes is the son in a father-mother-child triad: the god Min acts as his father, and his mother is either the lioness goddess Repit (Triphis in Greek) at Athribis or the goddess Aperetiset at Akhmim.

He was identified with Horus, who was called the child of Osiris and Isis, and he bore the titles "The Child" and "He who came from the Eye of Horus".

Worship and religion

His cult sites lay north and south of Akhmim. He is attested in the quarries of Gebel el-Haridi and Ptolemais Hermiou, where Demotic rock inscriptions mention him, and in the temple of Athribis. The sources do not indicate that his worship survives today.

Ancient sources: Demotic rock inscriptions, Greek inscriptions, a stele from the time of the Roman emperor Hadrian, and the inscriptions of the temple of Athribis; the sources do not cite literary texts that mention him.

Trivia

Several Greek spellings. In Greek inscriptions the name appears as Κολάνθης, Κολάνθας or Κολάνθος, and the hieroglyphs in the temple of Athribis vary from scene to scene.
A name that does not sound Egyptian. Because his name does not appear to be Egyptian, it is thought that this deity may not have originated in Egypt.
