Latvian god of light and horses

Ūsiņš

Ūsiņš is the Latvian god of light, spring and fertility, guardian of horses and bees. He is one of the few Latvian deities whose early sources are considered relatively reliable, and his festival marks the start of summer.

The myth

According to tradition, Ūsiņš is a son of Dievs, the sky god, and is considered the god of light: his horses were said to have carried the sun across the sky. Folk songs (dainas) describe him as the protector of horses, which he himself looks after with great devotion.

Worship and religion

His festival, Ūsiņš Day, coincided with Saint George's day and fell on 9 May in the Gregorian calendar. Farmers entrusted their horses to his protection, and a black cock with nine crests and red legs was sacrificed, its blood poured onto the oats in the manger to bless the stable. The bird was then cooked and eaten during the pieguļa, alongside boiled eggs prepared as a special dish, the pantāga.

Legacy

In popular tradition his name merged with that of Saint George (Jurģi), and his sign decorated gloves called atslēgaiņi, believed to bring good luck on the road.

Trivia

Indian and Greek relatives. Scholars compare Ūsiņš with the Indian Ashvini and the Greek Dioscuri, since all of them transport the sun.
Gloves for luck. Gloves bearing the sign of Ūsiņš are called atslēgaiņi, and people believed they brought good fortune on journeys.
