river god of Thessaly, an Oceanid of Greek myth

Enipeus

Enipeus is, in Greek mythology, the personification of the river of the same name that flows through Thessaly. He is counted among the Oceanids, the children of Oceanus and Tethys, and his story is tied above all to the tale of Tyro and Poseidon.

The myth

According to the tradition, Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus and Alcidice, was married to Cretheus when she fell in love with Enipeus. The river, however, did not return her affection. Poseidon, who also desired Tyro, took the form of Enipeus to seduce her, and from their union were born the twin boys Pelias and Neleus.

The stories place Enipeus among the Oceanids, the children of Oceanus and Tethys, and present him as the personification of a real watercourse. Strabo also mentions another river called Enipeus in the Peloponnese, which later took the name Barniquio. Its sources lay near the ancient city of Salmone, and it flowed into the Alpheus.

The myth does not develop Enipeus any further. His role is limited to being the god of the river and the means by which Poseidon deceives Tyro, so the narrative focuses on the consequences of that union and on the offspring that led to Pelias and Neleus.

Legacy

The Battle of Pharsalus, fought between the forces of Julius Caesar and those of Pompey during the second civil war of the Roman Republic, took place beside the river Enipeus. The river is known today as the Enipeas.

Trivia
