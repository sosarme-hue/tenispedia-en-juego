seeress of Norse mythology

Heiðr

Heiðr is a Norse female name borne by several figures in Norse myth and legendary history. The best known is a völva, a seeress and witch who appears in the Völuspá, though a Danish shield-maiden also carried the name.

The myth

In the Völuspá, a stanza describes a seeress called Heiðr who comes to the home of the Æsir and bewitches those who give in to her magic. Most scholars think she is another name for Gullveig, mentioned in the stanza before and often identified with Freyja, although some argue that the völva reciting the poem is speaking of herself.

In later sagas she appears as a seeress who foretells the death of Örvar, and in the Danish chronicles as a warrior. According to the Chronicon Lethrense and Saxo Grammaticus, she served Harald Wartooth at the Battle of Brávellir. After the defeat, the Danes made her their queen, though another king eventually displaced her. The Chronicon credits her with founding Hedeby.

Ancient sources: Völuspá, Landnámabók, Örvar-Odds saga, Hrólfs saga kraka, Sögubrot af nokkrum fornkonungum, Gesta Danorum and Chronicon Lethrense.

Trivia

A warrior with a man's soul. Saxo Grammaticus describes Heiðr (Hetha) as having a woman's body but a man's soul. In his account, she commanded the right flank of Harald Wartooth's army.
A city named after her. The Chronicon Lethrense says Hetha founded the city of Hedeby, which was named after her.
