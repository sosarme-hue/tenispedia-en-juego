legendary queen and goddess of ancient Mesopotamia

Kubaba

Kubaba is a figure of ancient Mesopotamia who appears as the only queen in the Sumerian King List, with a hundred-year reign, and also as a goddess worshipped at shrines across the region, especially in Karkemish. She matters because she links a legendary ruler with a divine cult that spread into Anatolia and Lydia.

The myth

According to the Sumerian King List, Kubaba was an innkeeper who ruled Kish for a hundred years, the only sovereign on that list. She was succeeded by her son Puzur-Suen, and then by her grandson Ur-Zababa, who according to tradition opposed Sargon of Akkad.

In the later Weidner Chronicle, Marduk grants her kingship after she brings an offering of fish to his temple Esagil. The story serves as a warning that kings who neglected Marduk lost their power.

Scholars regard Kubaba as a legendary rather than a historical figure: the earliest sources about her were written centuries after the time she supposedly lived, and her name does not appear in inscriptions of real rulers. Some omens present her as a warrior, and another links her to the birth of a being with both male and female traits that foretold the ruin of the kingdom.

Worship and religion

Her cult spread across Mesopotamia. In the Hurrian area she was identified with Kebat or Hepat, a title of the Hurrian mother goddess Hannahannah, and she was the protective deity of Karkemish on the upper Euphrates during the late Hurrian and early Hittite periods. Some reliefs show her seated, wearing a cylindrical headdress, holding a round mirror in one hand and a poppy capsule or pomegranate in the other. Over time her name was linked to the goddess of the Neo-Hittite kingdoms and, from there, to Cybele in Phrygia. In Sardis, the Greeks knew her as Kybebe.

Legacy

Ionian Greeks rendered her Lydian name as Kybêbê. Hipponax called her "Kybêbê, daughter of Zeus", and Semonides of Amorgos mentioned a woman who followed a kybêbos, as Jan Bremmer notes.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian King List, the Weidner Chronicle and liver omen texts, along with Luwian and Hittite texts where her cult appears.

Trivia

A name with several readings. Her Sumerian name can be translated as "radiant Baba" or "silver of Baba", although the exact reading of the last sign is still debated.
A queen without a king. One version of the king list from Ur states that there was no king while Kubaba reigned.
