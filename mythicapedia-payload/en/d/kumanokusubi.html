Japanese god of Shinto mythology, son of Susanoo and Amaterasu

Kumanokusubi

Kumanokusubi is a kami of Japanese mythology linked to the Kumano region. He matters chiefly for his role in the birth of the kami from the famous contest between Amaterasu and Susanoo.

The myth

According to tradition, Kumanokusubi was born from a contest in which Amaterasu and Susanoo produced kami. In many versions, Susanoo chewed Amaterasu's beads and five male deities emerged from his breath.

Kumanokusubi was the fifth of them, after Amenooshihomimi, Ame-no-hohi, Amatsuhikone and Ikutsuhikone. His name is usually read as "strange spirit" or "strange fire", and he is known by several other names.

Worship and religion

Some scholars identify this kami with the saijin (tutelary deity) of Kumano Jinja in Shimane Prefecture. At Kumano-taisha the main deity is Kushimikino-no-mikoto, though one theory holds that the original object of worship was Kumanokusubi.
