king of Liguria turned into a swan

Cycnus

Cycnus is, in Greek mythology, a king of Liguria and the son of Sthenelus, and a close friend and lover of Phaethon. After Phaethon's death, tradition turns him into a swan and later into a constellation, and his figure became linked to song and melancholy.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cycnus left his kingdom to mourn Phaethon on the banks of the river Eridanos. Day and night he wandered along the water, singing sorrowful songs, while the murmur of the river and the trembling of the poplars, the Heliades, Phaethon's sisters transformed into trees, seemed to answer his grief. He grew old without finding comfort, and the gods, moved by pity, changed his grey hair into feathers and turned him into a swan.

As a bird, he kept the memory of Zeus's thunderbolt that struck his friend: he would not fly over open ground and shunned the sun's heat. Other versions say he came to hate the heavenly brightness and spent his days among lagoons and rivers.

Legacy

His name passed to the constellation Cygnus, the Swan. According to a tradition recorded in modern sources, he also gave his name to the Cidneo hill in the Italian city of Brescia. As a musician blessed by Apollo, he is linked to the idea of the swan song, the phrase for an artist's final work.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Virgil, Pausanias, Servius and Hyginus mention him, either as Phaethon's friend or as a singer or musician transformed into a swan.

Trivia

A constellation named after him. The constellation Cygnus, visible in the northern hemisphere sky, recalls this figure transformed into a bird.
A musician favoured by Apollo. Pausanias and Servius describe him as a great musician, and both attribute his gift for singing to Apollo.
