Briareus

Briareus is one of the three Hundred-Handers, giants with a hundred arms and fifty heads in Greek mythology, children of Uranus and Gaia. He is the most prominent of the three and the only one given a second name, Aegaeon, which links him to the sea.

The myth

According to the Hesiodic tradition, Briareus and his brothers Cottus and Gyges were imprisoned by their father Uranus. Later Cronus, with Gaia's help, overthrew Uranus, but he too kept the Hundred-Handers captive in Tartarus. When Zeus fought the Titans in the Titanomachy, he freed them, since Gaia had foretold that victory would depend on them. Once the Titans were defeated, the Hundred-Handers were left as guards of their prison.

As a reward, Poseidon gave him Cymopolea as his wife, and Briareus settled with her in a palace by the river Ocean, without offspring according to Hesiod. Homer tells that when Hera, Athena and Poseidon chained Zeus, Thetis called on Briareus, who came and set him free; the gods called him Briareus and mortals Aegaeon. Ovid, by contrast, places him on the side of the Titans.

Legacy

Cervantes mentions him in chapter VIII of the first part of Don Quixote, and Dante uses him as a famous example of pride in the Purgatorio.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Homer's Iliad and Ovid's Fasti, among others.

Trivia

The Pillars of Briareus. According to Aristotle, the Strait of Gibraltar was once called the Pillars of Briareus, before they became known as the Pillars of Heracles.
Arbiter at Corinth. According to Pausanias, the Corinthians said Briareus settled a dispute between Poseidon and Helios over the land of the isthmus, giving the Acrocorinth to the sun god.
