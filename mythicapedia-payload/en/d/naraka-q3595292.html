Hindu and Buddhist hell

Naraka

Naraka is the Sanskrit name for the underworld in Hinduism and Buddhism, a place of punishment after death to which souls go according to their deeds. It is also the abode of Yama, god of death, and it matters because it explains how karmic justice is carried out beyond this life.

The myth

According to tradition, after death Yama's messengers, the yamadutas, bring every being before Yama's court, where he weighs their virtues and vices. The virtuous are sent to the heaven of Svarga, while sinners are taken to one of the hells.

Texts disagree on how many hells there are: the Agni Purana mentions four, some texts speak of seven and the Manu Smriti of twenty-one, while the Bhagavata Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana describe twenty-eight. Each is reserved for a kind of sin, such as stealing wealth or another man's wife, and the punishments include being bound, starved or devoured by beasts.

Unlike the Christian hell, the punishment is not eternal. When it ends, the soul may be reborn as an animal or as a higher being according to its karma, until it eventually reaches liberation.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda barely describes it, while the Atharvaveda, the Shatapatha Brahmana and the Manu Smriti treat it in more detail, and the Bhagavata Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana list its twenty-eight hells.

Trivia

Hell, but not quite. Scholars note that the term is usually translated as hell, though it is closer to purgatory, since the punishment does not last forever.
A name in Malaysia. In Malay the word neraka, a corruption of Naraka, means hell, even though most of the population is Muslim.
