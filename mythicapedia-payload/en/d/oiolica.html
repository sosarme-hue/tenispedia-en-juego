sea nymph of Greek mythology

Oiolica

Oiolica, also called Eolice or Eolica, is a sea nymph of Greek mythology and a daughter of the hundred-handed giant Briareus. She is linked to the waves raised by storms and to the golden belt that Heracles had to obtain as one of his twelve labours.

The myth

According to the story, Oiolica was a sea nymph, the daughter of Briareus, one of the Hecatoncheires, giants with a hundred arms. She was regarded as a deity of the waves that rose during storms. Some versions also make her a daughter of Cymopoleia, goddess of the waves.

Her link to the best-known legend lies in the golden belt that Heracles had to win. Oiolica is named as its first owner, but the sources disagree about the later owners, naming Hippolyte, Eolica and a woman called Deilica.

Trivia

A name with several readings. Her name could mean "lone she-wolf", perhaps referring to the jaws of the waves that sank ships or broke against the shore.
An uncertain mother. Briareus is her father in the sources, but her mother varies: Cymopoleia, goddess of the waves, is suggested with some reservation.
