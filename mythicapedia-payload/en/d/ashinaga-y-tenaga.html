pair of yōkai in Japanese folklore

Ashinaga and Tenaga

Ashinaga and Tenaga are a pair of yōkai from Japanese folklore who usually appear together: one has extraordinarily long legs and the other extraordinarily long arms. They are said to live in Kyūshū and were first described in the Japanese encyclopedia Wakan Sansai Zue.

The myth

According to the tradition, the two are fishermen from two imaginary lands, the Long-legged Country and the Long-armed Country, and they fish together by the shore. Tenaga, the long-armed one, climbs onto Ashinaga's back, and Ashinaga wades out with his legs above the waves while Tenaga plucks fish from his back.

The Wakan Sansai Zue adds that tenaga is also called chōhi and that his arms can reach three jō, a little over nine metres, while Ashinaga's legs stretch to two jō, just over six metres.

An essay called Kasshiyawa, by Matsura Seizan, recounts a man fishing on a clear moonlit night who spotted a figure with nine-shaku legs, about 2.7 metres tall, strolling along the beach. Soon a heavy rain began, and his servant explained they had seen an ashinaga, a yōkai whose appearance always brought bad weather.

Ancient sources: The Japanese encyclopedia Wakan Sansai Zue, which first describes them, and the essay Kasshiyawa by Matsura Seizan.

Trivia

Another name. According to the Wakan Sansai Zue, tenaga is also known as chōhi, a name that roughly means long arm.
Extraordinary measurements. The arms of tenaga could reach about nine metres, and the legs of ashinaga about six, according to the same encyclopedia.
