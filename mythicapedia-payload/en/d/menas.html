epithet of Selene, goddess of the months

Mene

Mene is an epithet of Selene, the Greek lunar goddess, when she is invoked as the presiding goddess of the months. Its root is shared with the Greek word that means both the Moon and the lunar month.

The myth

The sources do not preserve independent stories about Mene as a separate figure. Her role is best understood as another way of naming Selene in her function as guardian of the lunar cycle and the months.

An older reference presents her differently: fifty goddesses, daughters of Selene and the mortal king Endymion, each presiding over one of the fifty lunar months. This genealogy appears in only one source and should be read as an isolated version with no narratives developing it.

Legacy

The name is related to Men, the Phrygian moon-god, and to words in other Indo-European languages meaning Moon, such as English moon and Old Norse Máni. From the Greek mēnē comes the idea of the lunar month, which still links the Moon with the calendar.

Trivia

The Moon and the month in one word. The Greek mēnē means both the Moon and the lunar month, reflecting how the lunar cycle marked the passage of time.
A light that may have been a euphemism. Selene's name derives from selas, "light". Some scholars suggest it may have originated as a euphemism before becoming the goddess's proper name.
