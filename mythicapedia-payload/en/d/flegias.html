king of the Lapiths in Greek mythology

Phlegyas

Phlegyas is a king of the Lapiths, or of the Phlegyans, in Greek mythology, known for his impiety and as the grandfather of Asclepius. His punishment in the underworld made him a well-known figure in later literature.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phlegyas was a son of Ares, although sources disagree about his mother: Dotis in some accounts and Chryse according to Pausanias. He ruled Orchomenus after Eteocles died without children, renamed the region Phlegyantis and founded a city bearing his name. His Phlegyans raided their neighbours until they marched on Delphi; a plague destroyed them and their king.

His daughter Coronis was seduced by Apollo during Phlegyas's journey to the Peloponnese, and bore Asclepius near Epidaurus. Later she fell in love with Ischys. A crow told Apollo, who sent Artemis to kill her, though the god rescued the infant and gave him to the centaur Chiron. Enraged, Phlegyas set fire to Apollo's temple at Delphi, and the god killed him with arrows.

Legacy

Virgil places him in Tartarus, warning others not to scorn the gods, and Statius shows him starving before an endless feast. Dante makes him the ferryman of the Styx marsh in Canto VIII of the Inferno. In modern times he appears as a giant rock monster in the video game Dante's Inferno.

Ancient sources: Pausanias (Description of Greece), Virgil (Aeneid, Book VI) and Statius (Thebaid).

Trivia

A city named after him. According to Pausanias, Phlegyas founded a city that bore his name, gathering there some of the finest soldiers in Greece.
An infernal ferryman. Dante places him in Canto VIII of the Inferno, where he steers the boat across the Styx marsh.
