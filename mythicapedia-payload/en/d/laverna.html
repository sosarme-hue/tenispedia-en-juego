Roman goddess of thieves and impostors

Laverna

Laverna was a Roman goddess who protected thieves, cheats and those who worked under cover of darkness. Her cult, probably of Etruscan origin, was also tied to the underworld and to profit.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Laverna watched over those who planned their schemes in the shadows. Thieves asked her for help to carry out their plans successfully without losing their reputation for piety and honesty.

In origin, Etruscan sources placed her among the spirits of the underworld. As Rome replaced Etruscan culture, she came to be seen as protector of thieves, of the lower classes and of refugees. She was honoured with libations poured with the left hand.

Worship and religion

She had an altar on the Aventine Hill, near the gate named after her, the Porta Lavernalis, on the northern summit of the southernmost of Rome's seven hills. She also had a sacred grove on the Via Salaria, with a cave dedicated to her worship. Libations poured with the left hand were part of her offerings.

Legacy

Her name is given to the main villain of the CGI animated Barbie: Fairytopia film series, an evil fairy. Edgar Allan Poe mentions her in The Murders in the Rue Morgue to describe an inspector too cunning to be profound. On Tuscany's Mount La Verna stood an ancient sanctuary of the goddess, later given to St Francis of Assisi in 1213.

Ancient sources: Plautus, Lucilius, Horace, Arnobius, Varro and Pseudo-Acron mention her as protector of thieves and cheats.

Trivia

Name of a mountain. Mount La Verna, in Tuscany, held an ancient sanctuary of the goddess. In 1213 Count Orlando of Chiusi gave it to St Francis, who chose it as the site for his religious order.
An Etruscan cup. A cup found in an Etruscan tomb bears an inscription with the goddess's name, showing she was already worshipped by the Etruscans.
