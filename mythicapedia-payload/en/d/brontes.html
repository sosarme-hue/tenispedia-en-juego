Cyclops of Greek mythology, son of Uranus and Gaia

Brontes

Brontes is one of the three Cyclopes, sons of Uranus and Gaia, who alongside Steropes and Arges form a famous trio in Greek mythology. His name means 'thunderer', fitting his role as one of the makers of Zeus's thunderbolt.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, the Cyclopes are giants with a single eye in the middle of their foreheads, brothers of the Titans and known for their fierce hearts. Uranus imprisoned them, but Zeus set them free, and in gratitude they forged the thunderbolt that helped him overthrow Cronus and the Titans. Hesiod presents them as skilled craftsmen whose strength became proverbial.

Callimachus later places them in a forge on the island of Lipari, working under Hephaestus. There Brontes received Leto and her little daughter Artemis, who was only three. The girl grabbed the thick hair on his chest and tore it out, and the tale says that half his chest has been bare ever since. The scholia on the Iliad preserve a version in which Brontes fathered Athena, whom Metis bore before Zeus swallowed her.

Worship and religion

According to Pausanias, a sanctuary called the altar of the Cyclopes stood on the Isthmus of Corinth, in a place sacred to Poseidon, where sacrifices were offered to them. No other cult is attested for the Cyclopes, and no source mentions a separate worship of Brontes.

Legacy

His name, along with those of his brothers, became a byword for strength and power, and was used to refer to especially well-made weapons. The Spartan poet Tyrtaeus used the Cyclopes to convey extraordinary size and strength.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony (c. 700 BC) and Catalogue of Women; Callimachus' Hymn to Artemis; Virgil's Aeneid and Ovid's works; and the Iliad scholia for the Athena tradition.

Trivia

A name that sounds like a storm. Brontes means 'thunderer', while Arges and Steropes refer to brightness and lightning. The names reflect their role as forgers of the thunderbolt.
A bare chest. Callimachus tells how Artemis, at just three years old, tore out the hair on Brontes' chest, leaving half of it bare.
An unusual father of Athena. Only the scholia on the Iliad name Brontes as the father of Athena, a version that appears in no other ancient text.
