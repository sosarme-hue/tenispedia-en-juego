Roman goddess of first steps and departure

Abeona

Abeona was the Roman goddess who presided over the act of going away from home and protected children as they took their first steps without their mothers’ support. Her name comes from Latin abeo, “to go away,” and she was paired with Adeona, her counterpart for the return.

The myth

The surviving texts say little about Abeona, so her role is understood mostly from her function. Tradition places her at the moment when a child lets go of their mother’s arms and takes the first steps alone, a delicate instant that fell under her protection.

She also watched over those who left the family home for the first time, whatever the reason for the journey. Her actions are not told as a story of deeds but as constant guardianship during the transitions of everyday life.

Abeona is paired with Adeona, the goddess who looked after the return home. Together they covered the full cycle of leaving and coming back, so that the child or traveller was protected both on the way out and on the way back.

Worship and religion

According to the sources, Abeona belonged to the di indigetes, the “native gods” of Roman religion: abstract deities and concepts that predated the later blending of beliefs from different peoples. No temples, festivals or rites dedicated to her are documented, so her worship is known only in general terms, and there is no evidence that it survives today.

Legacy

Her name has reached astronomy: in 1997 Abeona Mons was approved as the name of a hill on Venus. A ship delivered to Jeff Bezos in 2023 also bears her name.

Trivia

A hill on Venus. In 1997 the name Abeona Mons was approved for a hill on the planet Venus, showing how mythological names remain in use in astronomy.
