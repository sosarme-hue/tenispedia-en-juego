four-headed form of Vishnu in Hinduism

Vaikuntha Chaturmurti

Vaikuntha Chaturmurti is a form of Vishnu shown with four heads, human, lion, boar and wrathful, expressing his status as the Supreme Being. The icon arose within Hinduism and was especially popular in Kashmir and Nepal.

The myth

The idea of a four-headed Vishnu first appears in the Mahabharata, though the complete iconography was set down in a 5th-century Pancharatra text. One interpretation holds that the animal heads represent Vishnu's avatars Narasimha, the lion-headed man, and Varaha, the boar. Another links the four heads to the Chaturvyuha, the four manifestations of the god: Vasudeva, Samkarshana, Pradyumna and Aniruddha.

The form goes by many names, including Vaikuntha, "Lord of Vaikuntha", and Chaturanana, "four-faced". The word vaikuntha was applied to Indra in the Vedas and passed to Vishnu by the time of the Mahabharata.

Worship and religion

Between the 8th and 12th centuries a cult centred on the deity developed in Kashmir, with royal patronage. In the 10th century, the Lakshmana Temple at Khajuraho suggests his worship in the Chandela kingdom of central India.

Ancient sources: The concept appears in the Mahabharata, and the full iconography in the 5th-century Pancharatra text Jayakhya-Samhita, as well as in the Vishnudharmottara Purana.

Trivia

A fierce head. The rear face, called kapila, may have a moustache, bulging eyes and fangs, and is read either as wrathful or as the sage Kapila.
