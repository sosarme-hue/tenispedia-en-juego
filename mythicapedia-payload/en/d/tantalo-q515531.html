Tantalus

Tantalus is a prince of Argolis, son of Thyestes, whose story is bound up with the house of Atreus and with Agamemnon. His tragic fate shows the brutal family rivalries at the heart of Greek myth.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Tantalus was born to Thyestes and Aerope, wife of his brother Atreus. After his wife and brother betrayed him, Atreus killed Tantalus and his brother Pleisthenes and served their bodies to their father as food.

Pausanias, the travel writer of the 2nd century AD, describes him as a son of either Thyestes or Broteas, whom other authors make Tantalus's father, so his parentage is unclear.

According to Tzetzes and Apollodorus, Tantalus grew to adulthood and became the first husband of Clytemnestra, a Spartan princess. After his death, Agamemnon married her against her will and killed Tantalus together with their newborn child.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Pausanias, Tzetzes and Apollodorus, who record differing versions of his family and his death.
