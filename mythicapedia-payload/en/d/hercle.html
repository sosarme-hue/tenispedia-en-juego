Etruscan god, counterpart of Heracles and Hercules

Hercle

Hercle is the Etruscan god linked to Heracles and Hercules, son of Tinia and Uni. For the Etruscans he was a much-venerated legendary figure, protector of shepherds, springs and warriors.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Hercle was the son of Tinia and Uni, and husband of Menrva, the Etruscan equivalent of Minerva. Unlike the Greek hero, in Etruria he was regarded as a god rather than merely a hero.

Bronze mirrors show episodes unknown from Greek or Roman myths. In one, Uni offers him her breast, a gesture that admits him into the family of the gods; in others he appears with Menrva and a child named Epiur, whom they may have been fostering.

Worship and religion

Hercle had a sanctuary at Caere (modern Cerveteri), and his name appears in many inscriptions on plates, vessels and votive objects. In Etruria he was more of an oracular god than in Greece, and he protected farmworkers and herdsmen.

Legacy

His figure survives in Etruscan bronze mirror imagery and in his identification with Heracles and Hercules, whose myths spread across the Mediterranean world.

Ancient sources: Etruscan sources are mainly inscriptions and bronze mirrors, since Etruscan literature has not survived.

Trivia

An Etruscan Hercules. Etruscan mirrors show Hercle in adventures not found in Greek or Roman myths.
