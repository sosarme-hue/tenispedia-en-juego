Celtic god of tribal unity

Teutates

Teutates, also called Tutatis, was a Gaulish and Britannic deity linked to tribal unity and the protection of peoples, and in some interpretations to war. His name, from proto-Celtic, is usually understood as meaning "tribe".

The myth

According to the Gaulish-Celtic tradition, Teutates was the ancestor of humankind, its lawgiver, guardian and arbiter, and the defender of its peoples. Ancient sources say little about his deeds, so he is known mainly as a protector of the community.

The Roman poet Lucan, in his Pharsalia, names him alongside Esus and Taranis as a recipient of human sacrifice, in a passage whose details are often doubted. Medieval commentaries on Lucan describe victims of Teutates being plunged headfirst into a vessel, a ritual some scholars compare with the Gundestrup cauldron, though the link remains speculative.

Worship and religion

He was revered as a spirit of tribal unity and harmony, and each tribe had its own Teutates. Stone altars were raised to him, especially in Gaul and Britain. In inscriptions he is almost always paired with Mars. In the Iberian Peninsula his cult was tied to mountains under the name Teleno, and place names such as Mount Teleno or Tullonium survive in the Basque Country.

Legacy

His name became familiar through the exclamation "By Toutatis!" used by the Gauls in the Asterix comics by René Goscinny and Albert Uderzo. He also appears in the Imperivm video game series, in a song by the Spanish band Extremoduro, and in the track "Crom En Su Montaña" by Hora Zulú.

Ancient sources: Lucan mentions him in the Pharsalia, and medieval scholia on that poem describe his sacrifices.

Trivia

A comic catchphrase. The exclamation "By Toutatis!" is one of the most recognisable phrases in the Asterix comics.
Roman rings. More than sixty Roman rings bearing inscribed letters have been found, mostly in Lincolnshire, and these may abbreviate "Totatis", a late form of the name.
