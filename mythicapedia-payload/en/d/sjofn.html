Norse goddess of love and relationships

Sjöfn

Sjöfn is one of the ásynjur of Norse mythology, whom Snorri Sturluson credits with turning people's minds toward love. She appears only rarely, and scholars debate whether she was an independent goddess or simply another name for a better-known deity.

The myth

In the Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson includes her in chapter 35 of Gylfaginning, in a list of sixteen ásynjur briefly described by High. She is seventh on the list, and her task is to turn people's minds to love, both women's and men's. According to Snorri, her name gave rise to the Old Norse word sjafni, meaning «love».

Her name appears again in a list of goddess names in Skáldskaparmál and in three skaldic kennings, where it serves as a general term for «woman». Thus the phrase «Sjöfn of gold» means «woman». Neither the Poetic Edda nor other sources develop her story, so no episodes or family ties of her own are known.

Scholars read these scraps cautiously. John Lindow notes that some propose she may be another name for Frigg. Rudolf Simek suggests that Snorri's etymology may derive from sefi, «sense» or «relation», and that the sources allow us to see her only as a goddess of marriage, love or relationships, one of the matron-like guardian goddesses of Snorri's account.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the Old Norse word sjafni, meaning «love», which Snorri recorded in the word lists included in his works.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (13th century), in Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál, along with skaldic poetry and its kennings.

Trivia

A word named after her. Snorri claims that the Old Norse word sjafni derives from her name. It means «love» and appears in the þulur word lists.
Barely attested. Her name does not appear in the Poetic Edda. Outside Snorri, it appears only in kennings that use it to mean a woman.
