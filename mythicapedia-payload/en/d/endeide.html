wife of Aeacus and mother of Peleus and Telamon

Endeis

Endeis is, in Greek mythology, the wife of King Aeacus of Aegina and the mother of Peleus and Telamon, who became the fathers of Achilles and Ajax. Her name is thought to be a dialect form of Engaios, "in the earth".

The myth

The sources disagree about her parentage. One tradition makes her a daughter of Sciron, the Megarian warlord, and Chariclo; others call her a daughter of the centaur Chiron, or of Pandion of Athens. Aeacus chose her as his wife and she bore him two sons, Peleus and Telamon. Pherecydes, however, says that Telamon was not Peleus's brother but his friend, and makes him a son of Acteus and Glauce.

The best-known episode concerns Phocus, Aeacus's son by the Nereid Psamathe and thus a half-brother to the other two. In one version Endeis hated him and wished him dead, and the two brothers were jealous of his talent in athletics. They lured him into a pentathlon, where Peleus threw a stone in place of a discus and aimed it at Phocus, killing him. Aeacus then exiled both sons from Aegina.

Robert Graves linked Endeis with the cuttlefish, "the entangler". He suggested that the name Peleus, meaning "muddy", may have become a title for a sacred king anointed with cuttlefish ink, since he is called a son of Endeis.

Ancient sources: Pherecydes discusses Telamon and Peleus, offering the version in which Telamon was Peleus's friend rather than his brother.

Trivia

A brother who was not one. Pherecydes holds that Telamon was Peleus's friend rather than his brother, a version that contradicts the more common account.
An entangling mother. Her name is linked with the cuttlefish, an animal whose name evokes the idea of entangling.
