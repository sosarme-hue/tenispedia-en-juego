Japanese creation god linked to the imperial family

Takami-musubi-no-kami

Takami-musubi-no-kami is a deity in Japanese mythology, one of the first beings to emerge from primordial chaos and associated with creation and the imperial family.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, after heaven and earth came into being, he appeared as the second deity, after Ame-no-minaka-nushi, and is described as a hitorigami, a god who arises without a spouse. He shares rule of Takamagahara with Amaterasu. In one tale he sends the bird Nakime to spy on Amewakahiko; Amewakahiko shoots it with an arrow that pierces the bird and flies up to heaven. Takamimusubi hurls the arrow back down, and it kills Amewakahiko as he lies in bed.

Accounts of his family vary. According to the Nihon Shoki he is the father of Sukunabikona and grandfather of Ninigi-no-Mikoto, ancestor of the imperial line; other versions make him the father of Omoikane or Futodama.

Worship and religion

He was regarded as a deity of the imperial family, and elements of his worship appeared in court ceremonies, where prayers were offered for a long and prosperous reign. Izumo-taisha, one of the oldest Shinto shrines, is dedicated to him.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki describes him as a hitorigami, and the Nihon Shoki links him to the imperial family through Ninigi-no-Mikoto.

Trivia

An arrow that returns. In the Nakime tale, Amewakahiko's arrow pierces the bird and reaches heaven, where Takamimusubi throws it back down to earth.
A god who arises alone. Hitorigami roughly means 'single god', a deity who appears without a partner according to the Kojiki.
