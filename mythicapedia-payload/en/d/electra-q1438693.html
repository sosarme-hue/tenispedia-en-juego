Oceanid of Greek mythology

Electra

Electra is an Oceanid of Greek mythology, daughter of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys and wife of Thaumas, with whom she became the mother of Iris and the Harpies. Her name means "amber", and her figure helps explain the origin of several beings linked to the wind, the rainbow and the waters.

The myth

According to Hesiod and Apollodorus, Electra married Thaumas, a son of Pontus and Gaia, and bore him Iris, the messenger of the gods, along with the Harpies. The Harpies' names vary from author to author: Hesiod and Apollodorus name Aello and Ocypete, Virgil mentions Celaeno, and Hyginus lists Celaeno, Ocypete and Podarce as daughters of Thaumas and Electra.

Hyginus also offers another version in which the Harpies are daughters of Thaumas and a woman called Ozomene, a name found nowhere else and identified with Electra because she is the only mother the tradition gives them. The late poet Nonnus also credits her with a son, the river god Hydaspes, who flows through India and witnessed Dionysus' campaign against King Deriades.

Among the companions of Persephone, Electra was gathering flowers with her when the earth opened and Hades seized the young goddess. Pausanias, for his part, mentions streams in Messenia that bear Electra's name.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Apollodorus, Hyginus, Virgil, Pausanias and the poet Nonnus.

Trivia

Ozomene. Hyginus is the only author who calls the mother of Iris and the Harpies Ozomene, a name meaning "of strong branches" that appears in no other source.
A river in India. Nonnus credits her with a son, the river god Hydaspes, whose name is given to a river that flows through India.
