wrathful protector goddess of Tibetan Buddhism

Palden Lhamo

Palden Lhamo ("Glorious Goddess") is a wrathful deity of Vajrayana Buddhism, regarded as the principal protector of Tibet and the special dharmapala of the Dalai Lamas. She is the only woman among the traditional Eight Guardians of the Law, and she is especially revered in Tibet and Mongolia.

The myth

According to tradition, Palden Lhamo was originally Remati, wife of the evil king of Lanka, both of them rakshasas in one version. Remati vowed to end the dynasty if she could not convert the king to Buddhism. After failing again and again, and seeing that her son had been raised to kill Buddhists, she slaughtered him while the king was out hunting. She ate his flesh, drank his blood from his skull as a cup, and flayed his skin to make a saddle. She fled north on a mule. The king caught up and shot the mule's rump, but she turned the wound into an eye and swore to wipe out the lineage of Lanka's kings.

She travelled through India, Tibet, China and Mongolia, and finally settled on Mount Oikhan in eastern Siberia. When she died she was reborn in hell, from which she escaped by stealing a bag of diseases and a sword. Finding no peace in the charnel grounds, she prayed to the Buddha for a reason to live, and the Buddha Vajradhara asked her to protect the dharma. Since then she has used her weapons only against enemies of Buddhism. Yama, lord of death, also appointed her a guardian of the teachings. Her retinue includes the dakinis Simhamukha and Makaravaktra, the four Goddesses of the Seasons, the five Sisters of Long Life and the twelve Tenma goddesses.

Worship and religion

Tradition holds that she appeared in a vision to the 1st Dalai Lama, promising to protect the reincarnation lineage of the Dalai Lamas. Since the 2nd Dalai Lama, regents and monks have gone to the sacred lake Lhamo La-tso to meditate and seek guidance on choosing the next reincarnation. There she takes a peaceful form, Gyelmo Makzor Ma. The mountain south of Chokorgyel Monastery is her "blue" residence, and a sky burial site lies on it.

Legacy

She is regarded as the tutelary deity of Tibet and its government. Her role as protector of the Dalai Lamas' lineage has made her a central figure in Tibetan religious and political life.

Trivia

An eye on the mule. According to the myth, the king's arrow struck the mule's rump, and the goddess turned the wound into an eye that watches over the twenty-four regions.
Linked to Kali. The lake Lhamo La-tso is also called "Palden Lhamo Kalidevi", indicating that the goddess is seen as an emanation of Kali.
