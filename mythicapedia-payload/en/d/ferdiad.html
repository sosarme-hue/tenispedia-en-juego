Connacht warrior in the Ulster Cycle

Ferdiad

Ferdiad is a warrior of Connacht in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. His duel with his best friend Cú Chulainn in the Táin Bó Cúailnge makes him one of the most memorable figures of Celtic epic.

The myth

Ferdiad, son of Damán and of Dáire of the Fir Domnann, was a companion in arms of Cú Chulainn, with whom he had trained under the warrior Scáthach. They were equal in nearly every feat, with two exceptions: only Cú Chulainn could use the barbed spear Gáe Bulg, and only Ferdiad had a horny skin no weapon could pierce.

When Ailill and Medb invaded Ulster to steal the bull Donn Cúailnge, Cú Chulainn halted them by demanding single combat. After he defeated several champions, Medb persuaded Ferdiad to face him, through seduction, drink and pressure. They fought at the ford for three days with different weapons. On the third day Ferdiad was gaining the upper hand, until Cú Chulainn used the Gáe Bulg, which Laeg floated down the river to him, and killed him. Cú Chulainn mourned his friend.

Legacy

The ford where he died is called Áth Fhirdiad, and the town of Ardee in County Louth is named after him. A bronze statue in Ardee depicts his combat and death.

Trivia

A forbidden spear. The Gáe Bolga was a barbed spear that Scáthach taught only Cú Chulainn to use, which made it decisive in the duel's outcome.
A name on a town. Ardee is thought to take its name from Ferdiad, although the origin of his name itself is uncertain.
