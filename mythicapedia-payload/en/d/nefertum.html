Egyptian god of the lotus and the birth of the Sun

Nefertem

Nefertem is a god of Egyptian mythology associated with the blue lotus and the birth of the Sun. He is regarded as a primordial deity who combines the delicate and the fragrant with the light that rises from the primal waters.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Nefertem was born from a lotus that emerged from the waters of Nun, the primeval ocean. In the cosmogonies of Heracleopolis and Hermopolis Magna, the child who came out of the flower symbolised the birth of the Sun. At Hermopolis he was seen as the energy that gives life to everything that grows, but also as the fire that destroys. His name is linked to the young Atum, and it is often read as 'Atum the beautiful'. In Hermopolitan theology he was identified with Atum or with Ra the Younger, always in connection with the sun rising from the lotus.

In the theology of Memphis, from the New Kingdom onwards, Nefertem appears as the son of Ptah and Sekhmet, forming a triad with them, although at times Imhotep occupied his place. At Buto, by contrast, he is considered the son of Wadjet and Ra. Despite his primordial character, he was never a demiurge. His task was to watch over and guard the eastern borders of Egypt, and he was regarded as the protector of the second hour of the day.

Worship and religion

He was mainly associated with Memphis, although he also received worship at Hermopolis Magna. Egyptians carried small statuettes of him as good-luck charms. At Memphis and Bubastis he was identified with Horhekenu, a form of Horus. His worship belongs to ancient Egyptian religion, which is no longer practised.

Legacy

His image has survived mainly through art. The so-called Head of Nefertem, a wooden bust found in the tomb of Tutankhamun, shows the young king represented as the god, with his face emerging from a lotus.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts call him 'Lord of Perfumes', and the Book of the Dead describes him as 'the lotus flower that is in the nose of Ra'.
