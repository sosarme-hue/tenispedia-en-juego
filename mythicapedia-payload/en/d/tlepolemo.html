Rhodian hero, son of Heracles

Tlepolemus

Tlepolemus was a hero of Greek mythology, son of Heracles, who led the Rhodian forces in the Trojan War. His story, shaped by a killing and an exile, explains how Rhodes came to be divided into three city-states.

The myth

According to the tradition, Heracles took the city of Ephyra, whose king was Phylas, and united with his daughter Astyoche, who bore him Tlepolemus. Raised in the palace, the young man killed Licymnius, Heracles' aged maternal uncle. Pindar says the killing was deliberate and driven by anger, while the Bibliotheca presents it as an accident: Licymnius stepped between Tlepolemus and a servant he was beating. To escape the vengeance of Heracles' other sons and grandsons, he gathered a host, built ships and sailed away. He consulted Apollo's oracle, which ordered him to sail to a land surrounded by the sea, and so he reached Rhodes. There he settled with his Argive wife Polyxo and divided the island into three groups that founded the cities of Lindus, Ialysus and Cameirus.

In the Trojan War, Tlepolemus commanded nine Rhodian ships. On the first day of fighting recounted in the Iliad he met Sarpedon, taunted him for lacking courage and doubted he could really be Zeus's son. After wounding Sarpedon, he was slain by him. The authors do not agree on his mother's name: Homer calls her Astyoche, Hesiod Astydameia and Pherecydes Astigeneia. After the war, according to some accounts, his men reached Crete and later settled on the Iberian islands.

Ancient sources: Homer in the Iliad, Hesiod, Pindar, Hyginus and Pausanias mention him, with variants about his mother, his killing and his death.

Trivia

A killing with two versions. Pindar describes Licymnius' death as deliberate and driven by anger. The Bibliotheca presents it as an accident that happened when the uncle stepped into a fight with a servant.
The death of Helen. According to Pausanias, Polyxo killed Helen to avenge her husband's death. Polyaenus instead says the Rhodians killed a servant dressed in Helen's clothes while Menelaus and Helen escaped.
