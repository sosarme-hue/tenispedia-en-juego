palace of the goddess Frigg in Norse mythology

Fensalir

Fensalir is the hall where Frigg, wife of Odin and the highest of the Norse goddesses, dwells. It appears in the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda as the most splendid of the gods' residences.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Frigg lives in Fensalir, a hall the sources describe as magnificent. In the Völuspá, she weeps there over the death of her son Baldr, a foreshadowing of the vengeance that will follow.

In the Prose Edda, Loki goes to Fensalir disguised as a woman to learn how Baldr might be harmed. Frigg tells him that everything had sworn not to hurt Baldr, except mistletoe, which she thought too young to be made to swear. Loki then leaves at once and, using a mistletoe dart, brings about Baldr's death.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá of the Poetic Edda and the Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.
