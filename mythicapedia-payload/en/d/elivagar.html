primordial rivers of Norse mythology

Élivágar

The Élivágar, or "Ice Waves", are primordial rivers in Norse mythology that flowed through Ginnungagap, the yawning void that existed before the world. Their poisonous waters gave rise to Ymir, the first giant, and their role as borders between worlds makes them a key element of the Scandinavian cosmos.

The myth

According to the Scandinavian tradition, before earth and sky existed there was only a vast emptiness called Ginnungagap. To the north lay Niflheim, a world of cold and mist, and at its heart the spring Hvergelmir welled up, the source of the Élivágar. Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda, describes how these rivers carried a thick venom that hardened into ice as it moved away from its source, much like slag running from a fire. When the ice stopped moving, the venomous drizzle froze into rime, which piled up layer upon layer across the void.

When the rime met the heat of Muspelheim, the fire realm to the south, it melted, dripped and took human form, and so Ymir, the first giant and progenitor of the frost giants, was born. The Vafthrúdnismál in the Poetic Edda says Ymir was formed from the poison dripping from the rivers, and the Prose Edda expands greatly on the idea. According to the text, so many serpents live in Hvergelmir with Níðhöggr that no tongue could count them, and they are probably the source of the venom.

The sources link eleven rivers to the Élivágar, among them the Svöl, Gunnthrá, Slíðr and Gjöll, which flows near the gate of Hel and is crossed by the bridge Gjallarbrú. These streams served as borders between realms, between gods and giants or between the mythic world and the mortal one. In the Helgakviða Hundingsbana II, the valkyrie Sigrún curses her brother Dagr for killing her husband Helgi, although he had sworn loyalty to Helgi upon the "bright waters" of the Leiptr, one of these rivers, showing that their waters were also used for sacred oaths.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning), the Vafthrúdnismál and the Helgakviða Hundingsbana II of the Poetic Edda are the sources that describe the Élivágar and their role in the origin of the world.

Trivia

A sacred oath. In the Helgakviða Hundingsbana II, the river Leiptr is where loyalty was sworn, and breaking that oath brings the curse of the valkyrie Sigrún.
A bridge over the underworld. The Gjöll is the river that flows closest to the gate of Hel, and it is crossed by the bridge Gjallarbrú.
