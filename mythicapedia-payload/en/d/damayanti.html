princess of Vidarbha, heroine of Hindu mythology

Damayanti

Damayanti is a princess of Vidarbha and the heroine of the love story between Nala, king of Nishadha, and herself, told in the Nalopakhyana episode of the Vana Parva of the Mahabharata. Her beauty, intelligence and steadfast loyalty make her one of the most admired heroines of Indian literature.

The myth

According to the tale, Damayanti is the daughter of King Bhima of Vidarbha. She hears of Nala's virtues from a golden swan that Nala had captured and that, in exchange for its life, promised to praise him to the princess. The two fall in love before they ever meet. At her swayamvara, the ceremony in which a princess chooses her husband, several gods who have heard of her beauty also appear and ask Nala to act as their messenger. Damayanti stays resolute and, once the gods reveal their true forms, picks out the real Nala from five identical figures. They marry and live happily.

Happiness breaks when Kali, enraged at being rejected by Damayanti, possesses Nala and drives him into a dice game with his brother Pushkara, which costs him his kingdom. Nala abandons the sleeping Damayanti in the forest. She survives a serpent and a hunter, whom she curses, and reaches a hermitage. After many trials she finds refuge at the court of Chedi, where a brahmin sent by her father tracks her down. To lure Nala out, she calls a second swayamvara, which only he could reach in a single day. The plan works: freed from Kali, Nala is reunited with his wife and regains his kingdom.

Legacy

Her story is retold in the Naishadhiya Charita, a Sanskrit poem by Sriharsha and one of the five great epic poems (mahakavyas) of that literature, and it appears in many Hindu texts written in various Indian languages.

Ancient sources: Mahabharata, Vana Parva (the Nalopakhyana episode).

Trivia

A swan messenger. The golden swan that Nala captures is the one who awakens Damayanti's love, describing the king's virtues before the two ever meet.
Five Nalas. At the swayamvara, Damayanti sees five men identical to Nala and must recognize the real one; she succeeds only when the gods reveal their true forms.
