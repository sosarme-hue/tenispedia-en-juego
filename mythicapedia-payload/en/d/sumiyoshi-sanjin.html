three kami gods of the sea and sailing

Sumiyoshi sanjin

A triad of kami gods of the sea and sailing in Japanese mythology. Sailors invoke them for protection, and they are regarded as deities of good fortune.

The myth

According to the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, the Sumiyoshi sanjin were born when Izanagi purified himself through a misogi after returning from Yomi, the land of the dead. They are grouped with the Watatsumi, sea deities whom the texts associate with three zones of the ocean: the surface, the middle depths and the deep sea.

A modern theory suggests their names allude to the three main stars of Orion, a constellation ancient navigators used as a guide.

Worship and religion

The Sumiyoshi-taisha shrine has four buildings dedicated to four kami: the three brothers and Empress Jingū, who is also enshrined there. Several ancient shrines exist, such as those in Fukuoka, Iki and Kobe, though it is unknown which is the oldest. Their worship continues today among sailors and fishermen.

Legacy

In The Tale of Genji, the father of the Akashi Lady prays to Sumiyoshi for years to protect his daughter. His prayers are answered when she bears a daughter to Genji, who later becomes Empress of Japan.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki recount their birth during Izanagi's purification.

Trivia

Orion. A modern hypothesis links the three deities to the three stars of Orion's Belt.
Sumiyoshi-taisha shrine. It dedicates four buildings to the three kami and Empress Jingū.
