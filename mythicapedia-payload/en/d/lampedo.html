Amazon queen in Greek mythology

Lampedo

Lampedo is an Amazon queen who, according to Greco-Roman tradition, ruled alongside her sister Marpesia. Her name, whose meaning is uncertain, may be linked to the torchlit New Moon processions held in honour of Artemis.

The myth

According to Roman historiography, Lampedo and Marpesia ruled the Amazons together and called themselves daughters of Ares, hoping to strike fear into their enemies and show that they were formidable warriors. Lampedo is named as a daughter of Ares with Marpesia as her sister.

The tradition also mentions Otrera, also called Hippo, meaning horse, who was Ares' consort and ruled alongside the two sisters. The account stays within the wider stories of the Amazon nation, without a fixed ending in the cited sources.

Legacy

Giovanni Boccaccio devoted a chapter of his Famous Women to Lampedo and Marpesia. Her name is also linked to the speculation about torchlit New Moon processions in honour of Artemis.

Ancient sources: Greek and Roman historians preserve the Amazon account that features Lampedo and her sister, including Ephorus, Diodorus Siculus, Xenophon and Justin.
