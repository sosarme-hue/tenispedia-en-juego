Roman goddess, counterpart of Cybele

Magna Mater

Magna Mater, or 'Great Mother', is the Roman goddess descended from Cybele, the great Anatolian mother goddess of Phrygia. Her cult was established in Rome in the late third century BC and became one of the city's most important.

The myth

According to tradition, the Hittite goddess Atargatis had as her consort Attis, a bright son of the sun, and together they ensured fertility, harvests and fruit. Once Romanized as Cybele, she yoked her lions to a chariot and rode seated in it.

A black stone represented her in Phrygia and was brought to Rome around 205 BC, when a Sibylline oracle recommended her during the war against Carthage. Roman mythographers made her a Trojan goddess, ancestor of the Roman people through the prince Aeneas. Later, the Julias of the house of Septimius Severus, priestesses of Emesa, spread the cult of another sacred stone from that city.

Worship and religion

Greek communities celebrated the Metroa, festivals with mysteries and a banquet, as an incomplete decree from Minoa on Amorgos describes. Priestesses led initiations, received a share of the offerings and organized collections. Each initiate paid a drachma, which was lent at interest to fund ritual banquets. In Piraeus, a lectisternium honoured the goddess and Attis with statues laid on couches. The sources do not say whether the cult survives today.

Legacy

Romanized forms of her cult spread throughout the Roman empire. Greek and Roman writers debated the meaning and morality of her rites and priesthoods, a debate that remains open in modern scholarship.

Ancient sources: Pindar, Homeric Hymn 14, Euripides' Bacchae, the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus, Pausanias and Strabo all mention her.

In art

Trivia

A stone instead of a statue. In Phrygia, the goddess was worshipped as an unshaped stone of black meteoric iron, which was later taken to Rome.
Lions at her side. Lions accompany the goddess from Phrygian art of the 8th century BC, and in Greek images she is seated with a lion beside her.
