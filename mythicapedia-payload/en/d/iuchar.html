Celtic hero of Irish mythology, son of Tuireann

Iuchar

Iuchar is one of the Sons of Tuireann, heroes of Gaelic Irish mythology. His story, best known from the tale Oidheadh Chlainne Tuireann, shows the consequences of a killing and the revenge that follows.

The myth

According to the tale, Iuchar, his brother Iucharba and Brian, also called Uar in some manuscripts, killed Cian, the enemy of their father and the father of Lugh, one of the great Tuatha Dé Danann. To hunt Cian, who had shapeshifted into a pig, the brothers took the form of dogs. They killed him after he had changed back into a man, and they tried to hide their crime.

Lugh discovered what they had done and set them impossible tasks: to travel across the known world and fetch magical weapons for the Second Battle of Magh Tuireadh. The brothers obtained them, but returned to Ireland badly wounded and begged Lugh to heal them, and he refused. In some versions, Lugh gave them eight tasks, seven of which they completed, and when they failed the last one he killed them.

Legacy

Because of the long voyage in search of magical objects, the tale has been compared to an Irish Argonautica, a Celtic version of the voyage of the Argonauts.

Ancient sources: The medieval Irish tale Oidheadh Chlainne Tuireann (The Tragedy of the Sons of Tuireann), which preserves Gaelic tradition.

Trivia

A name with variants. In many manuscripts the brother appears as Uar or Uair, but modern editors have corrected it to Brian, the form found in the older texts.
