Etruscan goddess of the underworld gate

Culsu

Culsu is an Etruscan deity with two faces who guards the gateway to the underworld. Linked etymologically to the male Culsans and comparable to the Roman Janus, she shows how the Etruscans thought about the thresholds between life and death.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Culsu appears as a winged, two-faced fury standing over the entrance to the underworld. Her main attributes were a torch and a pair of shears, probably symbolizing the definitive break with the world of the living as the soul crosses the portal she guards.

Her name is known from two inscriptions. Its etymological link with Culsans, a male deity equivalent to the Roman Janus who also has two faces, suggests that both figures belonged to the same circle of beliefs about thresholds.

Worship and religion

One of the two inscriptions attests a specific cult dedicated to the goddess, although the surviving sources do not describe its rites.

Trivia

Etymological pair. Culsu shares an etymological root with Culsans, her male counterpart. Culsans is the Etruscan equivalent of the Roman god Janus, who also has two faces.
