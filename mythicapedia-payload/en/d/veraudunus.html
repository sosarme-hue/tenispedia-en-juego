Celtic god known from Luxembourg inscriptions

Veraudunus

Veraudunus was a Gallo-Roman Celtic god known only from two votive inscriptions found in Luxembourg. He matters because he may have been an epithet of Lenus Mars, the important god of the Treveri.

The myth

No ancient story about Veraudunus has survived. The only sources are inscriptions, so we know nothing of his origin, family or deeds, and his myth remains unknown.

Worship and religion

Veraudunus received votive offerings, almost always alongside Inciona. On a stone altar found at Mensdorf on the Widdebierg, both are invoked for the imperial family, in fulfilment of a vow made by Alpinia Lucana, mother of Marcus Plautius Restitutus. The second inscription is a small bronze plaque from Kaul, also dedicated to both gods. If the reading Leno Marti is correct, that plaque would link him with Lenus Mars. Nothing suggests his cult survived.

Legacy

The name of the Widdebierg, the hill where the altar was found, has been said to derive from Veraudunus.

Ancient sources: No ancient literary texts mention him; only two Roman-period votive inscriptions from Luxembourg do.

Trivia

A name that passes to a hill. It has been proposed that the name of the Widdebierg derives from Veraudunus, making the god a possible echo in the local landscape.
An altar for the imperial family. The Mensdorf altar invokes Veraudunus and Inciona in honour of the imperial family, showing a blend of local devotion and Roman loyalty.
