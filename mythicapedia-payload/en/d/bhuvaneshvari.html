Hindu goddess of the world and queen of the universe

Bhuvaneshvari

Bhuvaneshvari is a goddess in Hinduism, venerated in Shaktism as the fourth of the ten Mahavidya and as one of the highest forms of Mahadevi. Her name means "goddess of the world" or "queen of the universe", and she matters because she rules over the three worlds.

The myth

According to a myth recorded by David Kinsley from a contemporary Hindi-language source, it was the god Surya who first created the three worlds. The rishis offered him soma, and he received power from Tripura Sundari, the main Shakti of that time.

Once Surya was empowered to create, the goddess took an appropriate form, pervaded the triple world and directed it. That form came to be known as Bhuvaneshwari, goddess of the world. The story thus stresses that Bhuvaneshwari is not a separate deity from Tripura Sundari, but one of her manifestations.

The name comes from bhuvana ishvari, and the worlds she governs are the traditional three regions: bhooh, the Earth; bhuvah, the atmosphere; and svah, the heavens. This is why she is described as sovereign of the whole universe, from the ground beneath our feet to the celestial realms.

In the Shakta tradition she appears as an aspect of Mahadevi, the great goddess, and her place among the Mahavidya places her among the most powerful manifestations of divine feminine energy.

Ancient sources: The Devi Bhagavata Purana identifies her as a form of Adi Parashakti, the primordial feminine energy.

Trivia

Place among the Mahavidya. She is the fourth of the ten Mahavidya goddesses, a group revered within Shaktism.
Compound name. Her name joins bhuvana (world) and ishvari (lady), and refers to the three worlds: earth, atmosphere and heavens.
