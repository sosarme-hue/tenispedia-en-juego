Lampetia

Lampetia is a nymph in Greek mythology, daughter of the sun god Helios and sister of Phaethusa. Together with her sister she tended their father's sacred herds on the island of Thrinacia, identified with Sicily. Her name means 'bright', and she appears mainly in the story of Odysseus and in accounts of the death of Phaethon.

The myth

According to Homer's Odyssey, Lampetia and Phaethusa looked after the beautiful cattle and sheep of Helios on Thrinacia, animals that never aged or bred. When Odysseus and his men reached the island, a storm kept them from sailing and their food ran out. While Odysseus was away praying, his companions killed some of the sacred cattle. Lampetia ran to tell their father, who demanded punishment for the offenders. When the storm finally cleared and the men set sail, Zeus struck their ship with a bolt of lightning, and all of them drowned except Odysseus, who clung to a mast.

Ovid, by contrast, places Lampetia among the Heliades, the daughters of Helios and Clymene and sisters of Phaethon. After their brother's death, the Heliades mourned so long that they were turned into poplar or alder trees, and their tears hardened into amber. Other traditions make her the wife of Asclepius, the god of medicine, and give her several children; according to the writer Hermippus, these included Machaon, Podaleirius, Iaso, Panacea and Aegle. Accounts differ from author to author, and in the Argonautica Lampetia is still alive, tending her father's herds.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey (Book XII), Ovid's Metamorphoses and Apollonius of Rhodes' Argonautica mention her, and Hermippus gives her offspring by Asclepius.

Trivia

A meaningful name. The name Lampetia means 'bright', in keeping with her status as a daughter of the sun god.
Children of Asclepius. According to Hermippus, Lampetia had five children with Asclepius, among them Machaon and Podaleirius, who appear in tradition as physicians.
