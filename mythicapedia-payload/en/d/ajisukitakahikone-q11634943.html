Japanese god, son of Ōkuninushi and tutelary deity of Kamo

Ajisukitakahikone

Ajisukitakahikone is a kami in Japanese mythology, a son of Ōkuninushi and the tutelary deity of Kamo. He is best known for the funeral episode with Ame-no-Wakahiko, whose strong resemblance to him led to a striking mix-up.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Ajisukitakahikone is one of two children Ōkuninushi had with Tagori-hime, one of the three Munakata goddesses. As a baby he could not sleep, and his mother carried him up and down a ladder to soothe him, which is said to create the sound of rising thunder. His crying was so loud that he was placed in a boat and sailed around the islands of Japan until he calmed down. As an adult he became the father of Takitsuhiko, a rain god.

His best-known episode involves Ame-no-Wakahiko. The heavenly gods demanded that Ōkuninushi cede the land to the descendants of the sun goddess, and Ame-no-Wakahiko, sent on that errand, tried to take the land for himself. He was killed by an arrow, and at his funeral Ajisukitakahikone, his friend, arrived. The family mistook him for the dead man come back to life. Insulted, since being compared to a corpse was a grave offense, he hacked the funeral hut to pieces with his sword and kicked it away. The debris landed in Mino and became Mount Moyama, the mourning mountain. His sister Shitateru-hime then sang a song to reveal his true identity to the mourners.

Worship and religion

He is the tutelary deity of Kamo, the place with which his worship and protection were associated.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Kojiki, the Nihon Shoki, the Izumo Fudoki and the Harima Fudoki, with variant forms of his name in each text.

Trivia

Origin of thunder. In the story, the rising sound of thunder comes from his mother carrying him up and down a ladder to get him to sleep as a baby.
A mountain of mourning. Mount Moyama, in Mino Province, has a name meaning mourning mountain, tied to the debris of the funeral.
