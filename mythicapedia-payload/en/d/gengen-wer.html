Egyptian goose-shaped god of creation

Gengen-Wer

Gengen-Wer, also written Negeg-Wer, is an Egyptian god of creation who took the form of a goose, possibly the Egyptian goose. His name can be translated as "great honker" or "great cackler".

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Gengen-Wer guarded a celestial egg that held a powerful creative energy. For this reason he is placed at the very origin of creation, as the guardian of what allowed the world to begin.

His role in the stories is modest. He appears only briefly in a spell from Chapter 59 of the Book of the Dead, where he plays a minor part. The sources that mention him are few, so many details of his story remain unknown.

The image of the egg combines two ideas common in Egyptian mythology: the egg as a source of life and the goose as a bird linked to creation. This reading should be taken as a modern interpretation, since the texts do not develop it.

Ancient sources: He appears in a spell from Chapter 59 of the Book of the Dead.

Trivia

A humorous name. His name translates as "great honker" or "great cackler", a title that stresses his bird-like appearance.
Guardian of the celestial egg. He is credited with guarding a celestial egg holding a powerful creative energy, a trait that sums up his role in creation.
