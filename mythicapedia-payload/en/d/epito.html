Arcadian king in Greek mythology

Aepytus

Aepytus was a mythical king of Arcadia in Greek myth, ruling Phaesana and Azania. His death while hunting and the tomb still shown in Pausanias's time kept him remembered in Arcadian tradition.

The myth

The son of Elatus, Aepytus received a small territory called Phaesana, on the river Alpheius, when his father and uncle Azan divided the Arcadian kingdom. When King Cleitor died without children, he also inherited Azania, and together with his brother Stynphalus he came to rule almost all of Arcadia. Part of the country was later named Aepytis after him.

According to the story, he raised Evadne, daughter of Poseidon, as his own daughter. He died while hunting on Mount Sepia, bitten by a venomous snake, and the place took its name from the event. His tomb, near Mount Cyllene, was famous in antiquity. Some sources credit him with sons Tlesenor and Peirithous, but the throne passed to his cousin Aleus.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions his tomb, and Pausanias describes it centuries later.

Trivia

A region named after him. Part of Arcadia was called Epitis, probably in his honor.
A much-visited tomb. His grave, mentioned by Homer, was still shown in Pausanias's time, and Pausanias was eager to see it.
