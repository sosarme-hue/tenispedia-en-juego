Hindu goddess, daughter of Shiva and Parvati

Ashokasundari

Ashokasundari is a Hindu goddess, daughter of Shiva and Parvati, said to have been born from the wish-granting tree Kalpavriksha. Her story is told in the Padma Purana, which recounts her abduction by a demon, her rescue and her marriage to King Nahusha.

The myth

According to the Padma Purana, Parvati, lonely after her son Kartikeya left Mount Kailash, asked Shiva to take her to the most beautiful garden in creation, Nandanvana. There, beside the Kalpavriksha, she wished for a daughter, and the tree granted it. Parvati prophesied that the girl would marry Nahusha of the Lunar Dynasty, a man destined to equal Indra.

While wandering with her attendants, the rakshasa Hunda fell in love with her, but she rejected him. He then disguised himself as a grieving widow and lured her to his palace. Once she discovered his treachery, the goddess cursed him to die at Nahusha's hand and fled back to Kailash. In revenge, Hunda kidnapped the infant Nahusha, but a maid rescued the child and placed him under the care of the sage Vashistha.

When Nahusha grew up, he learned that he was destined to defeat Hunda. The demon seized Ashokasundari again, falsely telling her that he had killed Nahusha. A Kinnara couple comforted her and foretold that she would bear a mighty son, Yayati, and a hundred beautiful daughters. Nahusha defeated Hunda in a fierce battle, rescued the goddess and married her. Later, while Indra was absent, he was briefly made regent of heaven.

Worship and religion

According to the sources consulted, she is venerated mainly in southern India under the name Bala Tripurasundari. No further details about temples or rites are given.

Legacy

The goddess appeared in the television series Devon Ke Dev... Mahadev, centred on the lives of Shiva and Parvati, which drew many viewers' attention to her. She later appeared in other series, including Vighnaharta Ganesh.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Padma Purana, practically the only major Hindu scripture that tells her story.
