daughter of Aeolus in Greek myth

Pisidice

Pisidice is a figure of Greek myth, daughter of Aeolus and Enarete. She matters because, through her marriage to Myrmidon, she links to the Aeolid line that leads to Peleus and Menoetius.

The myth

Pisidice was the daughter of Aeolus and Enarete. According to the Library of Apollodorus, she had seven brothers, Creteus, Sisyphus, Athamas, Salmoneus, Deioneus, Magnes and Perieres, and four sisters: Canace, Alcyone, Calyce and Perimede. Other sources add more brothers, such as Macareus, Arne or Tanagra, in different contexts.

Tradition holds that Myrmidon, a man of violent strength, took her as his wife. With him she bore Antiphus and Actor. Myrmidon also had other children whose mother is not specified: Erisichthon, Diopletes, Eupolemeia and Hiscilla.

From this branch of her descendants, mythographers arrive at Peleus, king of Phthia, and at Menoetius, father of Patroclus, the finest of the Myrmidons.

Ancient sources: The Library of Apollodorus, attributed to Apollodorus, records her genealogy and her siblings.

Trivia

A lineage that reaches Troy. Through the Aeolid branch of her descendants, mythographers lead to Menoetius, father of Patroclus, the finest of the Myrmidons.
Many siblings. The Library of Apollodorus gives her seven brothers and four sisters, though other sources add more names.
