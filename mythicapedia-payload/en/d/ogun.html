Yoruba orisha of iron, war and metalworking

Ogun

Ogun is a deity of the Yoruba religion, the god of iron, war and metalworking, and the patron of blacksmiths, hunters, soldiers and drivers. His worship spread to Cuba, Brazil and Haiti, where he merged with the devotion to Catholic saints.

The myth

According to Yoruba tradition, Ogun is one of the Irunmole, the primordial orishas. He is said to have cleared a path with his metal machete so that the other deities could come down to Earth, which is why he is honoured as Osin Imole, the first of the primordial orishas to come to Earth.

In some accounts, during his earthly life he was the first king of Ire and fought for his people. When some of his subjects showed him disrespect, he killed them and, in the end, took his own life with his sword. His followers believe he did not die but sank into the earth at a place called Ire-Ekiti, from where he promises to help anyone who calls on him. In an Ile-Ife tradition, he seized the throne after the fall of Obatala, but was expelled by Obalufon.

Worship and religion

Iron is his primary symbol, and his altars and festivals display knives, tools, weapons and other metal objects. Offerings include meats such as dogs, roosters, snakes, snails and yams, along with palm wine and red palm oil. Hunters and blacksmiths avoid eating snakes, and his followers swear to tell the truth in court by kissing a piece of iron in his name. His worship remains alive among the Yoruba, in Benin and across the African diaspora.

Legacy

His figure merged with several Catholic saints: with Saint Peter because of the metal keys, with Saint George for the sword in Rio de Janeiro, and with Saint James the Great in Haitian vodou. In Brazil he is also linked to Saint Anthony of Padua. Drivers carry an amulet of his to ward off traffic accidents.
