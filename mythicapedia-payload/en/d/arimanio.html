obscure deity of darkness in Greek and Latin texts

Arimanius

Arimanius is the name of an obscure deity found in a few Greek literary texts and five Latin inscriptions. Greeks and Romans linked him with Ahriman, the principle of evil in Persian Zoroastrianism, though in the Mithraic context his role remains far less clear.

The myth

According to Plutarch, Zoroaster named Areimanios as one of two rival artificers of good and evil, opposed to Oromazes, the god of light. While Oromazes creates six benevolent gods, Areimanios creates rivals equal in number, and the two remain locked in conflict. The account foresees a future time when Areimanios will be annihilated and humanity will live in one harmonious order.

Plutarch also describes a ritual in which Areimanios was invoked with a plant called omomi, pounded and mixed with the blood of a wolf and cast into a place where the sun never shines. He identifies this deity with Hades, or with a gloomy Pluto.

Worship and religion

In the Mithraic context, the inscription Deo Areimanio appears on a few altars to Mithras, but without any image that would link the name to a particular figure. No trace of the omomi cult described by Plutarch has been found in any mithraeum.

Ancient sources: Plutarch mentions Areimanios in several passages on Zoroastrianism and Persian religious practice.
