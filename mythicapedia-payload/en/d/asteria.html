Titaness, goddess in Greek mythology

Asteria

Asteria is a Titaness of Greek mythology, daughter of Coeus and Phoebe and sister of Leto. Her story is tied to the origin of the island of Delos, where Apollo and Artemis were born, and to the goddess Hecate, her only child according to Hesiod.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Asteria married Perses, her cousin, who took her to his house, where she bore their only child, Hecate. Other authors make her the mother of Hecate by Zeus, in a version attributed to Musaeus, and some even speak of a fourth Heracles born to them.

The best-known episode is her pursuit by Zeus. To escape him, Asteria turned into a quail and flung herself into the Aegean Sea. There she became a floating island, called Ortygia or Asteria, later identified with Delos. Pindar was the first to link her to the island's origin, and Callimachus took up the story in his Hymn to Delos, where she chooses the sea over Zeus.

When Leto, pregnant by Zeus, fled the wrath of Hera, Delos was the only land willing to shelter her, defying the goddess's order. There she gave birth to Apollo and Artemis. Afterwards the island was fixed to the seabed and took its name because it became visible. Nonnus, in the fifth century, tells how Poseidon chased her instead, and how Apollo rooted the island in the waves.

Asteria also fought beside her mother Phoebe against the Giants in the Gigantomachy.

Legacy

She appears in the Gigantomachy frieze of the Pergamon Altar. Callimachus wrote a hymn to Delos, and Lucian parodied the episode in his satirical dialogues, showing Poseidon struggling to fix the island. In the tale of the weaver Arachne, one of the tapestries she weaves shows Asteria fleeing Zeus in the form of an eagle.

Ancient sources: She appears in Hesiod's Theogony, in fragments of Pindar, in Callimachus's Hymn to Delos, in Apollodorus, in Hyginus, and in the work of Nonnus.

Trivia

A star's name. Her name comes from the Greek word astēr, meaning star, and shares its root with Astraea, her cousin Astraeus, and his daughter.
A floating island. According to the accounts, before Apollo's birth the island lay hidden beneath the waves and drifted, until it was fixed to the seabed.
