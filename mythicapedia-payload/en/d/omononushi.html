god of Mount Miwa in Japanese mythology

Ōmononushi

Ōmononushi is a kami in Japanese mythology linked to Mount Miwa in Nara Prefecture. He appears closely bound to Ōkuninushi in the imperial myths of the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, where he is sometimes presented as another name for that deity or as his spirit (mitama).

The myth

According to tradition, Ōmononushi emerges in the story of Ōkuninushi, who was developing the land of Ashihara-no-Nakatsukuni. When the dwarf Sukunabikona leaves him, a god appears promising help in exchange for worship, and Ōkuninushi enshrines him on Mount Miwa. In the Nihon Shoki, this god identifies himself as Ōkuninushi's spirit.

In another version, Ōmononushi and his son Kotoshironushi swear loyalty to the heavenly gods; as a reward he receives Mihotsuhime as his wife and returns to earth to protect the descendants of Amaterasu. In the Kojiki, a plague strikes the land during the reign of Sujin until the god asks to be worshipped through Ōtataneko, ancestor of the priestly clans of Kamo and Miwa.

The tradition also tells of his affair with the mortal Ikutamayorihime, who bore his child without knowing his identity until she traced a hemp thread to the mountain, and of Yamato-Totohimomosohime, who saw him as a small snake and died after a gesture of remorse.

Worship and religion

His chief place of worship is Ōmiwa Shrine at the foot of Mount Miwa, where the mountain itself serves as the object of worship. He is also enshrined at Ōsugi Shrine in Ibaraki, and the deity of Kotohira Shrine in Kagawa is today identified with him.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki (720 CE) mention him, as does the Fudoki of Harima in a passage on Misaka Shrine.
