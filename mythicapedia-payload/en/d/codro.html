last legendary king of Athens

Codrus

Codrus was, according to tradition, the last king of Athens, a semi-mythical ruler who sacrificed himself to save his city from the Dorian invasion. His gesture made him an ancient model of patriotism and self-sacrifice in Greek culture.

The myth

Codrus was the son of Melanthus, whose line was traced back to the sea god Poseidon. His reign coincided with the Dorian invasion of the Peloponnese, around 1068 BC.

The Dorians, led by Aletes, had consulted the Delphic Oracle, which declared that their invasion would succeed as long as the Athenian king came to no harm. Learning of this, Codrus resolved to die to save Athens. Disguised as a peasant, he left the city and provoked a group of Dorian soldiers near their camp. He was killed in the quarrel, and when the invaders learned that the king had fallen, they feared the prophecy had come true and withdrew.

His son Medon succeeded him, but it was claimed that no one thought himself worthy to follow Codrus, so the title of king was abolished and replaced by that of archon. According to the earliest account, preserved by Lycurgus of Athens, it was the house of Codrus itself that brought about this change.

Legacy

The name of Codrus gave rise to the Spanish verb 'codrear', used for claiming to sacrifice oneself for another. His story was used in antiquity as a civic example of self-sacrifice.

Ancient sources: Lycurgus of Athens, in his speech Against Leocrates, gives the earliest version of the story; the Constitution of the Athenians, attributed to Aristotle or one of his students, records the abolition of kingship.

Trivia

A sacrifice in disguise. According to the story, Codrus approached the enemy dressed as a peasant so he would not be recognized as king.
The end of kingship. Tradition links his death to Athens' shift from monarchy to rule by archons.
