son of Apollo and Evadne, prophet of Olympia

Iamus

Iamus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo and Evadne, and ancestor of the Iamidae, a family of seers who worked at Olympia. He matters because his story explains the origin of one of the most famous prophetic families of the Olympian sanctuary.

The myth

In Pindar's sixth Olympian Ode, Evadne conceived by Apollo, but, ashamed of her pregnancy, left the newborn in the wild after giving birth. Two snakes fed him honey, and he was later found among violets, which gave him his name, from ion, "violet".

As a young man, Iamus went down into the waters of the Alpheus and called on Poseidon, his grandfather, and Apollo, his father, to reveal his destiny. Only Apollo answered: he appeared to him, led him to Olympia and taught him prophecy, including the understanding of birdsong.

Worship and religion

The Iamidae read the future mainly from the flames of sacrificial fires. According to Pausanias, their tomb was in Sparta, and they are also linked with Messene and Mantinea.

Ancient sources: Pindar, in his sixth Olympian Ode, recounts his birth and upbringing.

Trivia

A violet name. His name is linked to the Greek word for violet, the flower among which he was found according to the story.
Fed by snakes. According to Pindar, two snakes nourished him with honey, a detail the poet describes with a metaphor: the "harmless venom of bees".
