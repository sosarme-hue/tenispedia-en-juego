lord of Calydon and Calydonian hunter in Greek myth

Dryas

Dryas is a lord of Calydon and one of the famous Calydonian hunters of Greek myth. He is remembered for a tragic episode in which his brother Tereus kills him by mistake, believing he was plotting against Tereus's son.

The myth

According to Hyginus, in his Fabulae, Dryas was a son of Ares and the brother of Tereus. The same text also calls him a son of Iapetus, a name meaning roughly "the piercer" that is probably an epithet of Ares.

Tereus had heard a prophecy that his son would be killed by a relative. Convinced that Dryas was the one who would fulfil it, he murdered him. The man was innocent: the boy was in fact killed by Procne.

Ancient sources: Hyginus's Fabulae, a Roman-era collection, preserves this version of the story of Dryas and Tereus.

Trivia

Two fathers. Hyginus gives Dryas both Ares and Iapetus as his father, the latter probably an epithet of Ares himself.
A fatal mistake. Tereus kills his innocent brother because of a misread prophecy: the child was actually killed by someone else.
