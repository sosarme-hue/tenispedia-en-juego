creator deity of Japanese mythology

Kami-musubi-no-kami

Kami-musubi-no-kami is one of the creator deities of Japanese mythology, the third of the first three kami (Kotoamatsukami). The name refers to the generative force that gives rise to life, and the deity is linked to the origin of plants.

The myth

According to tradition, this kami was present in Takamagahara from the beginning, alongside Ame-no-minaka-nushi and Takami-musubi, and after creation withdrew into the background. Some accounts describe it as genderless, while others treat it as a goddess, since its daughters Kisagaihime and Umugihime were healed with mother's milk.

In the myth of Ōkuninushi, who was killed by the eighty gods, his mother asks for help and Kamimusubi brings him back to life. It also transforms the grains that grew from the body of Ōgetsu-hime, the food goddess killed by Susanoo, into the five staple grains. In many versions it is the mother of Sukunabikona, who later helped Ōkuninushi shape the land.

Worship and religion

It has a strong connection to the Izumo tradition, where it is regarded as an ancestor of the earth kami. In the Heian period Kogo Shūi presented it as ancestor of the Ki clan, and in the early modern era Motoori Norinaga argued that it should be venerated above Ame-no-minaka-nushi.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Kojiki and the Izumo-no-kuni Fudoki, while the Nihon Shoki mentions it only as a pair with Takamimusubi.

Trivia

A genderless deity. At the start of the texts it is described as a kami without gender, although other passages treat it as a goddess.
The five grains. According to one account, the five staple grains arose from the body of Ōgetsu-hime after she was killed by Susanoo.
