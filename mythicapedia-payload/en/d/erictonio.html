legendary king of Athens, son of Hephaestus

Erichthonius

Erichthonius was, in Greek tradition, one of the earliest kings of Athens, often described as born from the earth. His story is closely linked to the origins of the cult of Athena in the city.

The myth

According to the Bibliotheca, Athena visited the smith-god Hephaestus to ask for weapons, but he became infatuated with her and pursued her. When he caught her and tried to assault her, the virgin goddess resisted; his semen fell on her thigh, she wiped it away with wool and threw it to the ground, and from that seed Erichthonius was born.

Athena wanted to raise the child in secret and entrusted him, inside a box, to the three daughters of Cecrops, Aglaurus, Herse and Pandrosus, with a strict order not to open it. Herse and Aglaurus disobeyed, saw the infant with a serpent's body and, driven mad, threw themselves off the Acropolis. Accounts differ on details such as the number of sisters or the role of the serpents.

As an adult, Erichthonius drove out Amphictyon and took the throne. He married the naiad Praxithea and had a son, Pandion I. Tradition credits him with founding the Panathenaic Festival, introducing the worship of Athena and inventing the horse-drawn chariot, and says that Zeus placed him among the stars after his death as the constellation Auriga.

Worship and religion

Erichthonius's cult was tied to that of Athena on the Acropolis, where the Erechtheum, the most sacred building on the site, was dedicated to him. He was regarded as a divinized hero, and his association with the snake, a symbol linked to his figure, was remembered in Athens.

Legacy

The snake that represents him is said to be hidden behind the shield of Phidias's Athena Parthenos in the Parthenon. His name also survives in modern mythography as an example of the kings born from the earth.

Ancient sources: The Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus, Pausanias, Hyginus, Strabo and the Parian Chronicle mention him, with differing versions of his origin.

Trivia

A name with several etymologies. Ancient writers linked his name to eris (strife) and chthon (earth), or to erion (wool), though the modern view treats these as folk etymologies.
A constellation. According to tradition, Zeus placed him in the sky as the constellation Auriga.
