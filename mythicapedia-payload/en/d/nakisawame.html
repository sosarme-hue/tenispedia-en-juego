Japanese kami of weeping and spring water

Nakisawame

Nakisawame is a female kami in Japanese mythology, associated with spring water. Her name means roughly "weeping, wailing woman," and her origin is tied to one of the most dramatic scenes in the Shinto creation myth.

The myth

In the myth of the birth of the gods, Izanami died after giving birth to the fire deity Kagutsuchi. Her husband, Izanagi, clung to her lifeless body and wept bitterly. From his tears, Nakisawame emerged.

Shinto tradition places her at the foot of Mount Kagu, where she dwells, and regards her as a spirit of spring water. The Kojiki also names her among the deities of this story.

Worship and religion

She is enshrined at a shrine in Kinomoto, in Kashihara (Nara), where she is honoured as a water deity. Her worship remains tied to this Shinto site.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki names her among the deities that arose from the creation myth.

Trivia

A name that describes weeping. Her name can be translated as "weeping, wailing woman," a direct expression of her origin in Izanagi's tears.
Grief gives birth to a water spirit. A gesture of grief gives rise to a deity linked to spring water, an element associated with life and renewal.
