giant and Norse god of the wind

Kari

Kari is a giant of Norse mythology, son of Fornjót and a personification of the wind. He matters because he appears in only a few sources, where his lineage and the names of his descendants vary from one text to another.

The myth

According to the Norse tradition preserved in medieval texts, Kari was one of the sons of Fornjót, a giant who ruled ancient kingdoms. The thulur, lists of poetic synonyms, use his name as a term for the wind.

He appears in very few stories. In Hversu Noregr byggdist, the account of how Norway was settled, his descendants end up ruling Finland and Kvenland. The Orkneyinga saga presents him as an heir to his father's realms. Kari had a son whose name changes between sources: Frosti ("frost") in the Orkneyinga saga and Jökul ("icicle" or "glacier") in Hversu Noregr byggdist. This son was the father of Snær the Old, "Snow the Old".

Ancient sources: The thulur, Hversu Noregr byggdist and the Orkneyinga saga.

Trivia

Two names for one son. Depending on the source, Kari's son is called Frosti or Jökul. Both names refer to ice and snow, which fits the wintry character of his lineage.
A wind with a personal name. In the thulur, the lists of poetic synonyms, Kari's name is used to refer to the wind, which is unusual for a giant.
