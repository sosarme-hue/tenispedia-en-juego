goddess of love and marriage from Ebla and Mesopotamia

Išḫara

Išḫara is a goddess first worshipped in Ebla in the third millennium BCE, associated with love, marriage and oaths. Her cult later spread through Mesopotamia, Assyria and the Hittite world.

The myth

According to tradition, she protected Ebla's royal family and, in the Hurrian Song of Release, tries to save the city from destruction. She appears as a goddess of marriage in the Epic of Gilgamesh and Atrahasis. She was usually regarded as unmarried and childless, and linked with mythical reptiles such as the bašmu and, later, with the scorpion.

Worship and religion

She had her own temple in Ebla and received offerings in that of the city god Kura. Her worship is documented into the first millennium BCE, with no living continuity.

Legacy

In Kassite and Middle Babylonian art she was shown only as a scorpion, never in human form.

Ancient sources: Epic of Gilgamesh, Atrahasis, the Hurrian Song of Release and the Song of Kumarbi, Mesopotamian and Hurrian texts.

Trivia

A name with many spellings. Her name appears in different cuneiform spellings, from Ebla to Ugarit.
A scorpion as logogram. In the god list An = Anum, a logogram meaning scorpion is linked to her.
