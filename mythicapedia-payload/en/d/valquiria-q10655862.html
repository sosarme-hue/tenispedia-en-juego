Battle maidens who choose the slain in Norse mythology

Valkyrie

Valkyries are female figures in Norse mythology who decide who will die in battle. The name, from Old Norse valkyrja, means "chooser of the fallen", and they are among the most recognizable figures of the Germanic imagination.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, the valkyries are a large group of women who ride over battlefields and choose half of the dead warriors. The other half go to Fólkvangr, the field ruled by the goddess Freyja. The chosen are brought to Valhalla, the hall of the slain ruled by the god Odin, where the einherjar prepare for Ragnarök and the valkyries serve them mead.

Valkyries also appear as lovers of heroes and other mortals. Sometimes they are described as daughters of royalty, sometimes accompanied by ravens or linked to swans.

The Old Norse poems Völuspá, Grímnismál and Darraðarljóð, and the Nafnaþulur section of the Prose Edda, list their names, many of them evoking battle and the spear, a weapon tied to Odin. Scholars such as Hilda Ellis Davidson and Rudolf Simek suggest the names were not individual but descriptive of war-goddesses, possibly coined by skalds.

Ancient sources: They appear in the Old Norse poems Völuspá, Grímnismál and Darraðarljóð, in the Prose Edda, and in the Helgakviða Hundingsbana poems, where Sigrún is named.

Trivia

Spear names. Many valkyrie names allude to the spear, a weapon closely associated with Odin.
Possible Germanic echoes. The name Herja may be connected to Hariasa, a Germanic goddess attested on a stone from 187 CE.
