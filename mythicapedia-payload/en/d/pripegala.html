god of the Polabian Slavs

Pripegala

Pripegala was a god of the Polabian Slavs, known from a single mention in a 1108 letter by Adelgot, bishop of Magdeburg. His name is distorted in the text, and scholars have proposed several readings.

The myth

The letter places him among the pagan crimes of the Slavs and compares his worship with that of Priapus, the Greek god of fertility. The most accepted reading comes from priby-, "to increase, to arrive", and golva, "head", roughly meaning "the one to whom heads arrive". Michał Łuczyński believes Pribyglav was an epithet of Svarozhits, a god some interpret as a sun god.

Worship and religion

His possible cult would be linked to the sanctuary of Svarozhits-Radogost at Rethra, the main centre of the Veleti, where according to the sources human heads were accepted as sacrifices, such as that of Bishop John of Mecklenburg in 1066.

Legacy

Some connect his name with the Serbian village of Privina Glava, whose oldest record, from 1566-1567, is Pribiglava, though the modern form may be a distortion.

Ancient sources: The 1108 letter by Bishop Adelgot of Magdeburg, the only known mention of the god.
