monster of Lake Manitoba, Canada

Manipogo

The Manipogo is a supposed monster of Lake Manitoba in Canada, part of the region's modern folklore. Its name blends the lake's name with that of Ogopogo, the famous monster of Okanagan Lake in British Columbia.

The myth

Indigenous peoples in the area have spoken for centuries of serpent-like creatures in Lake Manitoba, but the modern monster first appears in 1909, when fur trader Valentine McKay claims to see a huge creature in Cedar Lake. Witnesses describe a brownish-black body, one or more humps, and a cry like that of a prehistoric dinosaur.

In 1960 it receives the name Manipogo, a blend of Manitoba and Ogopogo. Skeptics propose more ordinary explanations: floating logs, river otters, or swimming moose with their heads above the water.

Legacy

It gave its name to Manipogo Provincial Park, appeared in an episode of the documentary series Northern Mysteries, and is mentioned in John Kirk's 1998 book. Every March, the community of St. Laurent holds the Manipogo Festival.

Trivia

A single photograph. The only photo ever linked to the creature was taken in August 1962 by Richard 'Dick' Vincent while fishing near Meadow Portage.
A claimed carcass. In 1997, a man from the Sandy Bay First Nation said he had killed the creature, and the Mounties were said to have taken it away, but the RCMP denied any knowledge of the incident and the accounts contradicted each other.
