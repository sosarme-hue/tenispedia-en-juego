minor deity of the Eleusinian Mysteries

Iacchus

Iacchus (Greek Íakchos) was a minor deity of Greek religion, linked above all to Athens and Eleusis through his role in the Eleusinian Mysteries. He was often identified with Dionysus, perhaps because his name resembled that of Bacchus.

The myth

Ancient texts give him little story of his own, and his parentage varies by author. Some make him a son of Demeter, others a son of Zeus and Demeter, others the surviving son of Dionysus and the nymph Aura, and others identify him with the chthonic Zagreus, son of Zeus and Persephone. He carried the torch in the procession from Athens to Eleusis and was sometimes regarded as the herald of the divine child born in the underworld, or even that child himself. He was called the star that brings light to the nocturnal rites, which led some to link him with Sirius and Sothis.

In Aristophanes' The Frogs, a chorus of initiates calls on him as a lively dancer in a meadow. A paean found at Delphi calls him Iacchus at Eleusis, and Sophocles names the god of the mysteries both Bacchus and Iacchus in Antigone.

Worship and religion

His statue stood in a temple of Demeter near the Dipylon Gate in Athens, reportedly by the sculptor Praxiteles. Probably on 19 Boedromion, it was carried in procession to Eleusis, about 22 kilometres away, while participants cried out the ritual exclamation "Iacche!" At the bridge over the Cephissus they were jeered by local people, recalling Demeter's hardships in searching for Kore. On arrival, Iacchus was welcomed with joy, and initiates danced in honour of both goddesses. An official called the Iakchagogos accompanied the statue. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Legacy

Lucretius uses the image of Ceres nursing Iacchus as an example of love. His name also gave rise to the ritual cry and hymn that accompanied the procession.

Ancient sources: Aristophanes' The Frogs, Sophocles' Antigone and Euripides' Bacchae, a paean found at Delphi, and the accounts of Pausanias, Strabo and Plutarch.

Trivia

A ghostly procession. During the Greco-Persian Wars, a ghostly procession was supposedly heard advancing from Eleusis crying "Iacchus". It was read as a sign of the Greek victory at Salamis.
A name that is a cry. Some scholars think the god emerged as the personification of the ritual cry "Iacche", linked to Greek words meaning to cry out.
