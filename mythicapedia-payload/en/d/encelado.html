Enceladus

Enceladus is one of the Giants of Greek mythology, born according to Hesiod from the blood that fell on Gaia when Cronus castrated Uranus. He is best known for his role in the Gigantomachy, the war between the Giants and the Olympian gods, and for the explanation that tradition gave for the earthquakes and eruptions of Mount Etna.

The myth

According to tradition, Enceladus fought Zeus and the Olympian gods for control of the cosmos. During the Gigantomachy he fought Athena, and in some versions the goddess defeated him by charging at him in her chariot; in others, she hurled the island of Sicily on top of him. Other accounts place him buried beneath the island of Inarime, modern Ischia, and in some traditions he was struck down by a thunderbolt from Zeus.

In Euripides' satyr play Cyclops, Silenus boasts of having killed him with his spear. To explain volcanic activity, it was imagined that Enceladus lay beneath Etna: its flames and smoke were his breath, and when he shifted his weary side, the island of Sicily trembled. Nonnus of Panopolis, in his Dionysiaca, also has him fighting Dionysus, who scorched him with fire before Zeus struck him down.

Legacy

Shakespeare mentions him in Titus Andronicus, and Keats includes him among the Titans in his Hyperion. Longfellow wrote a poem about him in 1859, and Dumas likens Porthos to him when he is buried under a rockfall in The Man in the Iron Mask. At Versailles, the Bosquet de l'Encélade fountain portrayed his defeat as part of the iconography of Apollo's triumphs. A moon of Saturn also bears his name; its jets of ice and vapour make it one of the places in the Solar System most likely to harbour microscopic life, and the Finnish eSports organisation ENCE takes its name from him.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned by Hesiod, who counts him among the Giants born from the blood of Uranus, and by Euripides, Callimachus, Apollodorus, Virgil, Claudian, Pausanias and Nonnus of Panopolis.
