Castilian-Leonese legend of the origin of Lake Sanabria

Legend of Lake Sanabria

A folk tale from the Sanabria region of Zamora that explains the origin of the lake of the same name: a village swallowed by the waters as punishment for refusing alms to a pilgrim. Its link to the legend of Lucerna ties it to the Camino de Santiago and to Unamuno's literature.

The myth

According to the tradition, a pilgrim arrived at the village of Valverde de Lucerna asking for alms, and no one helped him except some bakers, who let him into their oven to warm up and eat. The dough they had put in grew so much that it spilled out of the oven, and the pilgrim, revealed as Jesus Christ, warned them that the village would be flooded for its lack of charity and told them to flee to the hills.

He then struck his staff into the ground and water gushed out, drowning the village; only the oven remained standing, today an island in the lake. When the villagers tried to recover the bells of the sunken church with two young goats, one failed to pull because it had been separated from its mother, and the bell still rings under the water on Saint John's night for those with a good heart.

Legacy

Miguel de Unamuno renamed the village Valverde de Lucerna in his novel «San Manuel Bueno, mártir» (1930).

Ancient sources: The Pseudo-Turpin, the fourth book of the Codex Calixtinus compiled in the early twelfth century, describes the destruction of the city of Lucerna Ventosa, the distant origin of the tale.

Trivia

Bell under the water. Tradition says the bell of the sunken church rings from the depths on Saint John's night, but only those with a good heart can hear it.
A Swiss Lucerne. The name Lucerna may have been taken from the Swiss city of the same name, which in the Middle Ages was linked to the resting place of Pontius Pilate's body.
