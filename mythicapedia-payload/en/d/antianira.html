queen of the Amazons

Antianira

Antianira is, in Greek mythology, a queen of the Amazons who succeeded Penthesilea after her death at Troy. She is remembered above all for a proverb attributed to her in ancient poetry.

The myth

According to tradition, Antianira took the Amazon throne when Penthesilea was killed by Achilles during the Trojan War. Her fame, however, rests less on deeds than on a saying attributed to her: a lame man makes the better lover. The joke alludes to a legendary custom of this warrior people, who maimed male infants at birth by depriving them of an arm or a leg.

Apollonius of Rhodes also mentions another Antianira, daughter of Menetes, who bore Hermes two sons, Equion and Eurytus. Both joined the Argonauts on their quest for the Golden Fleece. She is a namesake rather than the same figure.

Ancient sources: Mimnermus (the proverb attributed to her) and Apollonius of Rhodes (Argonautica, for the other Antianira).

Trivia

A proverb attributed to her. Antianira is remembered for a saying attributed to her, a lame man makes the better lover, preserved in fragments of the poet Mimnermus.
Two Antianiras. Apollonius of Rhodes tells of another Antianira, daughter of Menetes, who was the mother of Equion and Eurytus, who sailed with the Argonauts.
