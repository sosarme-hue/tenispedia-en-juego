Buddhist god of wealth, linked to Kubera

Jambhala

Jambhala is the Buddhist deity of fortune and wealth, a member of the Jewel Family, sometimes equated with the Hindu god Kubera. He is venerated in Tibetan Buddhism and is considered an emanation of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion.

The myth

According to Tibetan tradition, Jambhala appears in five forms, each with its own colour, practice and mantra, all aimed at relieving poverty. The best-known, White Jambhala, is linked to a story about the master Atisha: he found a starving man, could not feed him even with his own flesh, and then Chenrezig appeared and took on the form of the god of wealth to end destitution.

Yellow Jambhala, the most popular, is associated with Vaisravana, one of the Four Heavenly Kings. In another legend, Red Jambhala guarded the heavenly treasury of Mahesvara's son, but gave wealth indiscriminately to good and evil alike; the guardian Mahakala beheaded him, and after he repented, gave him an elephant's head and took him as a retainer.

Worship and religion

Practitioners recite his mantras and carry out specific practices to obtain material and spiritual prosperity. In Tibetan Buddhism, White Jambhala is also invoked to find hidden treasures, the teachings concealed by ancient masters.
