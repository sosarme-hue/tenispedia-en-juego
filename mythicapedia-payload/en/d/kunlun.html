mythical mountain in Chinese mythology

Kunlun

Kunlun is a mythical mountain or mountain range in Chinese mythology, a key symbol of the axis mundi and the divine. It was regarded as the dwelling of gods and goddesses, of marvelous plants and fabulous creatures, and as a place linked to immortality.

The myth

According to the stories, Kunlun is a place that is hard to reach, surrounded by waters and towering cliffs. Its base is said to reach deep into the earth, while its visible part rises into the sky, so some versions present it as a pillar holding up the heavens and separating earth from the celestial realm.

Tradition describes it in paradisiacal detail: gem-like rocks, jasper and jade cliffs, jewel-studded plants, magical fungi and birds of extraordinary colours. The Supreme Deity (Taidi) is said to dwell there, with his throne on the top tier, called the Palace of Heaven. Whoever climbed to its summit would become an immortal spirit. Later, the goddess Xiwangmu was moved to Kunlun from a jade mountain further north, and in her palace immortals feasted on exotic delicacies.
