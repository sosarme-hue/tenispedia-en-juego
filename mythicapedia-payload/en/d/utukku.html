class of demons from ancient Mesopotamia

Utukku

The utukku (in Sumerian, udug) were a class of spirits and demons from Mesopotamian mythology, attested in Sumerian, Akkadian, Assyrian and Babylonian texts. They are one of the most ambiguous figures in the region's demonology, since their name was often used as a general term for any demon.

The myth

According to the texts, the word udug originally designated a protective spirit that accompanied a person, not a malevolent being by nature. Inscriptions from the time of Gudea of Lagash (c. 2144–2124 BCE) speak of a udug that goes before the ruler, and of other udugs driven out of the city, suggesting they could be benevolent or hostile depending on the case.

Over time the word acquired negative connotations. Incantations described the evil utukku as beings that seize people by the shoulder or kill them on the plain, with a hostile appearance, great height and a step that poisons. The great exorcism corpus known as Utukkū Lemnūtu gives these demons a threatening appearance and a deafening voice. They appear alongside other demons such as shedu, alu and ekimmu, and are sometimes confused with them; in some accounts they are counted among the seven demons born of An and Antu.

Worship and religion

Exorcism texts known as Udug Hul contained incantations to drive out the evil udug, to which physical and mental illnesses were attributed. The exorcist cured the patient by reciting these formulas.

Ancient sources: The Akkadian incantations of the Utukkū Lemnūtu corpus and the Sumerian and Akkadian Udug Hul texts are the main sources on these demons.
