divine horse of Greek mythology

Arion

Arion is a legendary horse of Greek mythology, known for his dark mane and prodigious speed, and believed to be of divine descent. He is remembered above all for rescuing his master Adrastus during the expedition of the Seven against Thebes.

The myth

According to the myth, Arion was the son of Poseidon and Demeter, though ancient authors disagreed about his parentage. The best-known version tells how Demeter, searching for her daughter, fled Poseidon by turning into a mare and hid among the horses of King Oncius at Telpusa. Poseidon discovered the trick, took the form of a stallion and mated with her, and Arion was born from that union.

Other accounts make him the offspring of Gaia, the Earth, and the poet Antimachus names him thus. The writer Quintus of Smyrna preferred another lineage, that of Zephyrus, the west wind, and an unnamed harpy.

Tradition then passes him to several heroes. Heracles rode him in his campaign against the Eleans and in his fight with Cycnus, and later gave him to Adrastus, king of Argos. During the expedition against Thebes, Adrastus was the only leader to survive, because Arion carried him swiftly from the battlefield.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions him in the Iliad as the horse of Adrastus, 'of the race of the gods'. The lost epic Thebaid recounts his flight with Adrastus, and Antimachus of Colophon celebrated him in his verses.
