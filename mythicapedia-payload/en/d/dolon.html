Trojan warrior in the Trojan War

Dolon

Dolon is a Trojan warrior in Greek mythology, known for his speed and for the risky night mission he undertook against the Greek camp during the Trojan War. His story is told mainly in Book X of Homer's Iliad, one of the tensest episodes of the poem.

The myth

According to the tradition, Dolon was the son of Eumedes and had five sisters. Despite his unappealing looks, he was famed as a swift runner. When the Trojans needed to know whether the Greeks were still guarding their ships, he volunteered to spy on them at night. As his reward he asked for the horses and bronze chariot of Achilles, and he made Hector swear an oath that the promise would be kept.

Disguised in a wolf skin and a marten-pelt cap, he crept along on all fours to fool the sentries. On the way, however, he ran into Diomedes and Odysseus, who were also prowling at night, though on the Greek side. After being tortured, Dolon revealed everything he knew, including which Trojan allies were camped in which tent. Odysseus, however, broke his word: Diomedes beheaded him, and his remains were hung from the stern of Odysseus's ship as an offering to Athena.

Legacy

Dolon also appears in Euripides' tragedy Rhesus, and Virgil recalls him in the Aeneid through his son Eumedes, who survived Troy, sailed with Aeneas and died fighting Turnus. The asteroid (7815) Dolon was named in memory of this mythological warrior.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad (Book X), Euripides' tragedy Rhesus and Virgil's Aeneid.

Trivia
