buffalo asura of Hindu mythology

Mahishasura

Mahishasura is an asura in Hindu mythology, a powerful being with the form of a buffalo who waged war against the gods. His defeat at the hands of the goddess Durga is one of the most celebrated stories in Hinduism, especially within the Shakta tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition, Mahishasura was the son of the asura Rambha and a buffalo-demoness, which is why he could shift into a buffalo at will. Although he was pious and meditated on Brahma, the god granted him a boon: he could not be defeated by any man or god. Protected by this gift, he drove the devas out of heaven and defeated Indra.

Unable to overcome him, the gods combined their energies and created Durga, a beautiful young woman who fought him for nine days. On the tenth, during the waning moon, she killed him. For this victory she received the epithet Mahishasuramardini, the slayer of Mahishasura. In some battles Durga took the form of Kali to defeat Raktabija, whose spilled blood created new copies of himself; Kali drank all of it before it touched the ground.

Worship and religion

Durga's victory is celebrated during the festivals of Navaratri and Dussehra, which symbolise the triumph of good over evil, and in Bengal and Odisha through the Durga Puja. On the Chamundi Hills above Mysuru, a huge statue of Mahishasura faces the city at the temple of Chamundeshwari. Among the Santal and other Asur peoples, by contrast, he is revered as a benevolent ancestor, and his death is mourned during Durga Puja.

Legacy

The scene of Durga defeating the buffalo demon was carved in caves and temples across India, such as those at Mahabalipuram and Ellora, and appears in the pandals of Durga Puja. The story has inspired films, plays and dance dramas. The name of the city of Mysore is also linked to the figure of Mahishasuramardini.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya, part of the Markandeya Purana, is the standard telling of the story, though it also appears in the Lakshmi Tantra, the Narada Purana and the Garuda Purana.

Trivia

The origin of Mysore. Local tradition holds that the city of Mysore takes its name from Mahishasuramardini. According to the legend, the goddess killed him on top of the Chamundi Hills.
A view from the Santals. The Santal and Asur peoples regard Mahishasura as an ancestor and Durga as the villain. They call their former king Hudur Durga and say that his story was reinterpreted by the Aryans.
