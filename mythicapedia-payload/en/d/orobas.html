Great Prince of Hell in goetic demonology

Orobas

Orobas is a demon of Western demonology listed in the Ars Goetia, the first book of the Lemegeton or Lesser Key of Solomon. He is described as a Great Prince of Hell commanding twenty legions, and he stands out as one of the more favourably portrayed spirits in that catalogue.

The myth

According to the grimoire tradition, Orobas first appears in the form of a horse and, at the exorcist's command, takes human form. He is credited with revealing things past, present and to come, answering questions about divinity and the creation of the world, granting dignities and winning the favour of both friends and foes.

The texts present him as truthful and faithful to the conjurer, unwilling to deceive and unwilling to allow other spirits to tempt the magician.

Legacy

His best-known image comes from the illustration in Jacques Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire Infernal (1863). He has appeared in the figure game Monster in My Pocket, in Dragon Quest V, and in the Persona series. He is also linked to the horse-headed figure at the end of Eduardo Sánchez's Lovely Molly (2011).

Ancient sources: The Ars Goetia in the Lemegeton, and Johann Weyer's Pseudomonarchia daemonum, where he also appears as a great prince who takes the form of a horse.

Trivia

An oracle, not a demon. In the figure game Monster in My Pocket, Orobas (number 78) is described more as an oracle than as a demon.
An uncertain name. His name may come from the Latin orobias, a kind of incense, although this etymology is not firmly established.
Dragon knight. In Divinity: Ego Draconis he appears as a dragon knight, a deity venerated in the fjords for his power and wisdom.
