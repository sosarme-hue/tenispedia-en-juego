supreme god of the Guanches of Tenerife

Achamán

Achamán was the supreme being of the Guanche mythology of Tenerife, the people who lived on the island before the Castilian conquest. He was regarded as a creator linked to the sky, and his name is interpreted as 'the Celestial' or 'the Sparkling One'.

The myth

According to the accounts recorded after the conquest, Achamán created a first group of men and women from water and earth, and gave them livestock to sustain themselves. Later he created more people, but gave them no livestock and told them they should serve the first ones.

For the Guanches, this origin myth explained the different social classes within their people. A modern literary legend, not found in the traditional sources, tells how the evil spirit Guayota imprisoned the sun god Magec inside Teide; Achamán defeated him, freed the sun and sealed the crater with Guayota inside.

Worship and religion

The Guanches invoked Achamán in their oaths. Chronicles also record other names for the same celestial being according to his attributes, such as Achuhurahan, Achuhucanac or Achguayaxerax, and on other islands forms such as Abora on La Palma or Acoran on Gran Canaria appear.

Legacy

The name of Achamán survives mainly in the chronicles and in studies of Guanche culture. The legend of his battle with Guayota is a modern invention that has become popular in Tenerife.

Ancient sources: The chronicles written after the conquest of Tenerife are the main sources that record his name, its variants and the creation myth.
