Greek hero, father of Briseis

Briseus

Briseus is a figure of Greek mythology, the father of Briseis, the captive whose dispute between Achilles and Agamemnon sets the Iliad in motion. He barely appears in the poem, but other traditions gave him a fuller role.

The myth

In the Iliad, Agamemnon takes Briseis from Achilles, yet the poem says little about her father, despite her central place in the plot. Later writers filled the gap. The commentator Eustathius of Thessalonica makes him a brother of the priest Chryses, both sons of an otherwise unknown Ardys, and places him in Pedasus, a town in the Troad, while Chryses lived in Chryse. Another version has Briseus serving as a priest in Lyrnessus.

Dictys Cretensis tells how Briseus hanged himself after losing his daughter. The sources do not agree on where he lived or on his status, so his profile remains vague.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, Eustathius of Thessalonica's commentary on Homer, and Dictys Cretensis.

Trivia

A priest brother. Eustathius of Thessalonica makes him the brother of Chryses, a priest in Chryse.
A tragic end. According to Dictys Cretensis, Briseus took his own life after losing his daughter.
