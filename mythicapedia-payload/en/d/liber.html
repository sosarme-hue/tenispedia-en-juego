Roman god of wine, viticulture and freedom

Liber

Liber, also known as Liber Pater ("the Free Father"), was a god of viticulture, wine, male fertility and freedom in ancient Roman religion. As a patron of the plebeians, his festival Liberalia became associated with free speech and the rights that came with adulthood.

The myth

According to Hyginus, in his Fabulae, Liber was a son of Jupiter and Proserpina, although other versions make him a son of Ceres. The best-known story makes him a son of Semele, whom Juno tricked into asking Jupiter to appear before her in all his splendour. Struck down by lightning, she died, and Jupiter took the child from her womb and gave him to Nysus, which is why he was called Dionysus, "the one of two mothers". His episodes include the punishment of Pentheus, who denied his divinity and was torn apart by his own relatives, and the transformation of Tyrrhenian pirates into dolphins when they tried to kidnap him.

Liber also descended to the underworld to seek his mother and, after Ariadne was abandoned on the island of Dia, he took her as his wife.

Worship and religion

Liber was worshipped alongside Ceres and Libera in a temple on the Aventine, dedicated in 493 BC after a vow by the dictator Aulus Postumius, with scenic games held in his honour. His festival, the Liberalia, was held on 17 March, though its games were later moved to the Cerealia. His Dionysiac rites were strictly regulated in 186 BC, when the Senate curbed the Bacchanalia without banning the cult outright.

Legacy

His name, meaning "free", gave rise to the idea that Liber personified liberty and its rights. His early scenic games helped set the satirical and subversive character of Roman theatre. His name is also linked to libation, the ritual offering of drink.

Ancient sources: Livy recounts the founding of his temple and the Bacchanalia affair, Hyginus preserves his legends in the Fabulae, and Cicero, Pliny and Augustine of Hippo all mention him.

Trivia

Tyrrhenian dolphins. The legend explains that the pirates transformed into dolphins gave their name to the Tyrrhenians and to the Tyrrhenian Sea.
Inventor of the triumph. Pliny credits him with inventing the diadem, the emblem of royalty, and the triumphal procession.
Games relocated. His Liberalia games of 17 March were officially transferred to the April Cerealia festival.
