divine twin brother pair of Hinduism

Nara and Narayana

Nara and Narayana are a pair of sage brothers, regarded as partial incarnations of the god Vishnu on earth. They belong to the Hindu tradition, and their most important temple stands at Badrinath in the Himalayas.

The myth

According to tradition, the twins were sons of Dharma and Murti, or of Ahimsa, non-violence. They lived at Badrika, performing austerities and meditation for the welfare of the world. The sages defeated the demon Sahasra Kavacha, whose name means 'thousand armours'.

The Bhagavata Purana tells how the apsara Urvashi was born. While the brothers meditated at Badrinath, the gods, alarmed by the power of their penances, sent Kamadeva, spring and several nymphs to disturb them. Narayana took a flower and placed it on his thigh, and from it rose a woman whose beauty surpassed that of the nymphs. She was sent to Indra, and her name comes from uru, the Sanskrit word for thigh.

In other accounts, Shiva's trident pierced Narayana's chest while he was doing penance, and Nara answered by turning a blade of grass into an axe, which Shiva had to break. Later, in a duel with the asura king Prahlada, the brothers proved invincible, and Prahlada had to beg for forgiveness.

Worship and religion

Their main temple is at Badrinath, where in the sanctum Nara and Narayana stand beside Badri-Narayana. Devotees believe the two brothers still dwell there, and the site is an important centre of devotion.

Legacy

In the Mahabharata, Arjuna and Krishna are identified with Nara and Narayana, and the epic opens by honouring these two devas. The Devi Bhagavata Purana also regards Arjuna and Krishna as their reincarnations.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Bhagavata Purana, the Vamana Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Mountains named after them. Two mountains in the Himalayas near Badrinath are named Nara and Narayana.
Fair and dark. In iconography, Nara is painted with fair skin and Narayana with dark skin.
