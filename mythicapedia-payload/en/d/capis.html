Dardanian king in Greek and Roman myth

Capys

Capys is a king of Dardania in Greek and Roman mythology, the father of Anchises and grandfather of Aeneas. Later traditions also link his name to the founding of Capua, in Campania.

The myth

According to the myth, Capys was the son of Assaracus and Hieromneme, although some accounts name Clytodora as his mother. He succeeded his father as king of the Dardanians and married Themiste, daughter of Ilus, founder of Troy. The sources do not agree on his children: some give him Ilus and Anchises, others Anchises and Acoetes. Anchises, a strikingly handsome young man, became the beloved of the goddess Aphrodite and the father of Aeneas. Acoetes, in turn, was said to be the father of the priest Laocoon.

In some versions Capys is the brother of Ganymede, and his mother is sometimes also called his wife, a sign of how much the ancient genealogies varied.

Later legends speak of another Capys, a companion of Aeneas on his journey to Italy, to whom the founding of Capua is credited. Other traditions give the city to a son of Aeneas, who named it after his great-grandfather, or to a Samnite with the same name. Capys is also linked to the founding of Cafias, in Arcadia.

Legacy

The name of Capua, the city in Campania, is explained in some traditions by this hero. A modern hypothesis connects the name with an Etruscan word for the hawk, though its origin remains uncertain.

Trivia

A possible Etruscan origin. It has been proposed that the name Capua derives from an Etruscan word for the hawk. The idea is a linguistic hypothesis, not a proven fact.
A link to Laocoon. According to some versions, his son Acoetes was the father of the Trojan priest Laocoon, known for opposing the Trojan Horse.
