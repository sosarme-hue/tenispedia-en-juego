Titan of the second generation in Greek mythology

Menoetius

Menoetius is a second-generation Titan in Greek mythology, son of Iapetus and brother of Atlas, Prometheus and Epimetheus. He barely appears in the stories, but his fate illustrates the punishment of pride in the war between the Titans and the Olympian gods.

The myth

According to tradition, Menoetius was the son of Iapetus and one of the Oceanids, whom some sources call Clymene and others Asia. Hesiod, in the Theogony, first describes him as "very renowned" and then recounts his downfall during the Titanomachy: Zeus, the wide-seeing, struck him with a blazing thunderbolt for his insolence and reckless audacity, sank him into Erebus, and later imprisoned him in Tartarus.

His name is usually read as "doomed might", from the Ancient Greek words for might and doom, which fits the picture of a Titan driven by violence and rash action. Hesiod gives him neither a consort nor children, and nothing more is told of his life. His role is so minor that Hyginus leaves him out of his list of the Iapetids, and the same author also calls the mother of these brothers a Nereid, departing from the usual version.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the main source describing his fall in the Titanomachy; Hyginus mentions him only in passing when listing the sons of Iapetus.

Trivia

A shared name. Greek mythology has other characters called Menoetius or Menoetes, such as a keeper of Hades' cattle who wrestles with Heracles during his twelfth labor.
