female ghost of Bengali folklore

Shakchunni

The shakchunni is a female ghost from Bengali folklore, associated with Bangladesh and the Indian state of West Bengal. She is the spirit of a married Hindu woman who died before her husband, leaving her domestic longings unfulfilled.

The myth

According to tradition, the shakchunni returns from beyond because she longs for the married life she left unfinished. She is said to possess married women, especially those from well-off households, in order to relive those experiences. Her name comes from the Sanskrit shankhachurni, and she keeps the signs of a married woman: conch-shell bangles and vermilion on her forehead.

In folk tales she usually appears as a thin, emaciated figure with greenish skin, like leafy vegetables, dressed in a red-and-white sari. She is fond of fish, a food considered suitable for married women in Bengal, and depending on the version she prefers it raw, fried, burnt or even rotten. She is said to dwell in certain trees, such as the sheora and mango. In many stories an exorcist (ojha) eventually defeats her or drives out her spirit.

Legacy

She appears in traditional Bengali tales, such as those gathered in the collection Thakurmar Jhuli.

Trivia

A culinary weakness. She is described as craving fish, which in Bengal was considered a especially suitable food for married women.
Inhabited trees. She is believed to live in trees such as the sheora (Streblus asper) and mango trees.
