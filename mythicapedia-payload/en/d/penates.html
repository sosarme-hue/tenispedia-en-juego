Roman household gods of the storeroom and hearth

Penates

The Penates were the household gods of ancient Rome, guardians of the family and, in their public form, of the Roman state. Originally protectors of the storeroom, they came to represent the continuing life of the whole household.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, their name came from penus, the innermost part of the house where food, wine, oil and other supplies were kept. By guarding these stores, they came to stand for the family's survival over time. Cicero linked them to what dwells inside, while Varro, as reported by Macrobius, saw them as the powers through which we breathe in our innermost self, through which we have a body and a rational mind.

The public Penates of the Roman people had a temple on the Velia, near the Palatine, which according to Dionysius of Halicarnassus housed statues of two youths in the archaic style. Their public cult began at Lavinium, where they were closely tied to Vesta. One tradition identified them with the sacred objects Aeneas rescued from Troy and carried to Italy, perhaps alongside rival copies, which eventually ended up in the Temple of Vesta in the Forum. Unlike the Lares, who were bound to one place, the Penates could travel.

Worship and religion

At each family meal a portion was thrown into the hearth fire for the Penates, and part of the daily food was offered to the figure that represented them. Magistrates swore oaths before the public Penates. Each year on 1 March, Roman magistrates first sacrificed to Capitoline Jupiter in Rome, then travelled to Lavinium to make offerings to Jupiter Indiges and Vesta and to pay a ceremonial visit to the “Trojan” Penates.

Ancient sources: Mentioned by Propertius, Cicero, Macrobius and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, among other ancient authors.

Trivia

A morsel for the gods. At every meal a portion was tossed into the hearth fire for the Penates, a way of sharing the food with the household gods.
Gods that could travel. Unlike the Lares, tied to one place, the public Penates could be moved. Aeneas was said to have carried them from Troy to Italy.
