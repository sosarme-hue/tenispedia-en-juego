legendary Chinese official, alleged rocket pioneer

Wan Hu

Wan Hu is a legendary figure attributed to a Chinese official who supposedly tried to launch himself into space using rockets. With little documentary support and a doubtful origin, his story is one of the most frequently cited anecdotes about early attempts at spaceflight.

The myth

In the best-known account, Wan Hu, an imperial official, wanted to reach the heavens. He built a chair tied between two large kites and fixed forty-seven rockets to it. When his servants lit the fuses, the rockets exploded in a huge blast, and when the smoke cleared, Wan Hu and his chair had vanished.

There are variants. A version printed in the United States in the early twentieth century calls the protagonist Wang Tu and has him punished with a severe beating ordered by the emperor. Other Chinese versions describe him as an unlucky pioneer who died burned in the explosion.

Legacy

The lunar crater Wan-Hoo, on the far side of the Moon, has borne his name since 1970. The story appears in the children's film Kung Fu Panda (2008), where the hero launches himself into the sky in a firework-powered chair, and the MythBusters programme tested it in 2004 and concluded it could not work.

Ancient sources: No ancient Chinese texts are known to mention him directly; the story spread in the twentieth century through American publications.

Trivia

A crater in his name. In 1970 the International Astronomical Union named a crater on the far side of the Moon Wan-Hoo after him.
A chair in Tokyo. A painting of his story hangs in the queue area of the Soaring: Fantastic Flight attraction at Tokyo DisneySea, alongside other legendary attempts at flight.
