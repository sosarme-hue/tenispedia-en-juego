Sumerian goddess of healing

Ninti

Ninti is a Mesopotamian goddess of healing, worshipped mainly in Lagash. She appears in the myth of Enki and Ninhursag as one of the eight deities created to ease Enki's pain.

The myth

In the myth of Enki and Ninhursag, Enki falls ill in eight parts of his body after eating eight plants that sprouted from the seed Ninhursag had removed from the body of Uttu, Enki's daughter. To heal him, Ninhursag creates eight deities, and Ninti is responsible for his rib pain.

At the end of the tale, Ninti is given the office of "lady of the month". Her name is read in two ways through wordplay: as "lady rib" (nin-ti) and as "lady of the month" (nin-iti). Scholars see this as a scribal game rather than a religious feature of the goddess.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in Lagash, where a temple dedicated to her is known. She is attested in texts from Fara and the Early Dynastic period, and later in offering lists and god lists of the Ur III and Old Babylonian periods.

Ancient sources: The myth of Enki and Ninhursag and god lists such as An = Anum, which equates her with Enki's spouse Damkina.

Trivia

Mother of Ninkasi. A hymn to Ninkasi states that her parents were Ninti and Enki, although she was raised by Ninhursag.
Not Nintinugga. Although both are healing goddesses, scholars warn that Ninti should not be confused with Nintinugga, a healing goddess from Nippur.
