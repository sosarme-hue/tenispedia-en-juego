Germanic wise woman, mother of the Lombard founders

Gambara

Gambara is a wise woman or priestess of Germanic tradition, mother of the leaders Ibor and Agio. Her story explains the origin of the Lombard people, and she is one of the few women who appear as the origin of a genealogy in post-Roman chronicles.

The myth

According to the legend, the Winnili, ancestors of the Lombards, lived beside the Vandals, whose leaders Assi and Ambri demanded tribute from Ibor and Agio. Gambara advised her sons to refuse.

Before the battle, the Vandals called on Odin, but Gambara asked for help from his wife, the goddess Frea. Frea suggested that the Winnili women spread their hair over their faces to look bearded. When Odin saw them, embarrassed, he asked who the longobardae, the long-beards, were. By naming them he became their godfather and granted them victory.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe an organised cult in her honour. Scholars debate whether Gambara was a priestess or simply a wise woman. Some link her to the worship of Freyja within the Vanir, and point to a possible Lombard sanctuary at Žuráň, in today's Czech Republic.

Ancient sources: The 7th-century Origo Gentis Langobardorum, Paul the Deacon's Historia Langobardorum in the late 8th century, the early 9th-century Historia Langobardorum codicis Gothani, and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum in the 12th century.

Trivia

Long beards. In the legend, the name of the Lombards comes from the long beards the women pretended to wear to trick Odin.
A harsh proposal. In the Gesta Danorum, Gambaruc opposes an assembly that wants to kill infants and the elderly to avert famine.
