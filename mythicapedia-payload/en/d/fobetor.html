Greek god of dreams that take the form of beasts

Phobetor

Phobetor is one of the thousand sons of Somnus ("Sleep"), the somnia who personified dreams in Greco-Roman tradition. His Greek name, Φοβήτωρ, means "the one who frightens", and he matters because he shows how dreams were imagined according to the shape they took before the sleeper.

The myth

According to the myth, Phobetor was one of the somnia, the thousand sons of Somnus, known to the Greeks as Hypnos. Humans call him Phobetor, but the gods name him Icelus or Icelos, "the one of the likeness". In Ovid's Metamorphoses he appears as a dream able to turn into a beast, a bird or a long serpent, and he ranks among the chief dreams alongside his brothers Morpheus, who appears in human form, and Phantasos, who takes the form of inanimate objects.

Hyginus and Cicero say that both the Dreams and Sleep himself were born from the union of Night and Erebus. Virgil, for his part, describes a gloomy giant elm at the centre of the underworld, its ancient branches hung with empty dreams clinging to all its foliage.

Legacy

Modern scholars debate his origins. Tripp regards Morpheus, Phobetor and Phantasos as literary concepts rather than myths, while Griffin suggests that the names of these deities and the division of dream forms among them may have a Hellenistic origin.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Metamorphoses is the only ancient source that names him; Hyginus, Cicero and Virgil refer to the Dreams and their lineage.

Trivia

Two names depending on the speaker. Humans call him Phobetor, while the gods call him Icelus or Icelos, according to Ovid.
Brothers with no earlier record. The names Morpheus and Phantasos appear nowhere before Ovid, so some scholars believe he may have invented them.
