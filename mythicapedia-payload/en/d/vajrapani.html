bodhisattva and dharma protector of Mahayana Buddhism

Vajrapani

Vajrapani is a bodhisattva and dharmapala in Mahayana Buddhism, protector and guide of Gautama Buddha and a symbol of the Buddha's power. His name means 'holder of the thunderbolt', and he has roots in the Indian cultural sphere.

The myth

In early Buddhist legends, Vajrapani is a minor deity who accompanied Gautama during his years as a wandering mendicant. Some texts identify him with Śakra, king of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven, and say he was present at the Tathagata's birth. When Gautama left the palace to renounce the world, it was Vajrapani who helped him escape.

Other tales say that, on his return from Kapilavastu, the Buddha was escorted by eight forms of devas. The Chinese traveler Xuanzang recounts that Vajrapani vanquished a large serpent at Udyana. In another version, to protect the nagas who came to hear the Buddha from their deadly enemies, the garudas, Vajrapani took the form of a bird to deceive them. At the Buddha's parinirvana, a despairing Vajrapani dropped his vajra and rolled in the dust.

Worship and religion

Vajrapani is venerated in Tibetan Buddhism, in Pure Land Buddhism, where he is known as Mahasthamaprapta, and at the Shaolin Monastery. In China he appears as the paired guardians at temple gates, known as vajra warriors or Benevolent Kings. In Japan he is represented as the protectors called Niō.

Legacy

In Greco-Buddhist art of Gandhara he was depicted with features of Heracles, and some scholars believe the figure may even be Zeus, whose classical attribute is the thunderbolt. This muscular wrestler-like iconography influenced guardian statues in East Asia. His name is also linked to Acala, known in Japan as Fudō Myō-ō.

Ancient sources: He is the only Buddhist deity mentioned in the Pali Canon, though there he appears as a secondary figure.

In art

Trivia

Temple guardian. In China his figures flank temple and monastery gates as guardians.
Heracles in Gandhara. In Gandhara art he was depicted in the likeness of Heracles, a Greek hero.
