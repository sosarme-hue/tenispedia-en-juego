guardian dragon of the east in Chinese mythology

Azure Dragon

The Azure Dragon (青龍, Qīnglóng) is one of the Dragon Kings of Chinese mythology and the guardian of the east, linked to spring and wood. It is one of the Four Symbols of the constellations and, in Japan and Korea, it also guards cities and tombs.

The myth

In Chinese tradition the Azure Dragon has a composite body: the head of a camel, a deer-like horn over its snout, bull's ears, a serpent's neck, tiger's paws, eagle's talons and fish-like scales. Its mouth, full of sharp teeth, spits sea foam, and its serpent eyes flash with fire.

It is often shown with a red pearl in its mouth, symbolizing the Moon, which gives an unfading light and makes whatever is nearby grow and multiply. Chinese dragons are benevolent rather than evil, although in anger they can bring typhoons and floods. They breathe clouds instead of fire and rule the waters: rain, lakes and rivers. They are serpentine beings with four legs that can become invisible or take human form.

The dragon is linked to spring: according to tradition, it wakes from its winter sleep in the mud with the first spring rains, much like crocodiles. Some scholars suggest the Chinese dragon may have been inspired by large crocodilians and gharials that lived in southern China into historical times.

Worship and religion

As the guardian of the east, the Azure Dragon belonged to a system of four symbols representing the cardinal directions. In Kyoto, Japan, a statue in front of Kiyomizu Temple is regarded as protecting the city, and each year a ceremony is held to honour the eastern dragon. It remains an object of devotion within Buddhism and Taoism.

Legacy

The dragon appeared on the Chinese national flag during the Qing dynasty (1644–1912) and as a door god warding off evil spirits from homes. In the novel Romance of the Tales of the Tang, its star is reincarnated as General Shan Xiongxin. The character 朧 (lóng), meaning pale or faintly lit, combines the Moon with the dragon.

Ancient sources: Ancient Chinese sources place it among the Dragon Kings and the celestial emblems; the Kitora Tomb murals in Japan and the Goguryeo tombs in Korea depict it.

Trivia

A crocodile in the sky. On some ancient Greek and Egyptian star charts, the constellation Scorpius is shown as a crocodile, and Schlegel linked it with the Azure Dragon.
A statue that drinks. In front of Kiyomizu Temple in Kyoto stands a statue of the dragon, said to drink at night from the waterfall in the temple grounds.
