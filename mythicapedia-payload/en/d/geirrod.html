Geirröd

Geirröd is a jötunn in Norse mythology and the father of the giantesses Gjálp and Greip. His story, in which Thor confronts him in his own hall, is one of the best-known tales of the god's clashes with the giants.

The myth

According to the tradition, Loki was flying in Frigg's falcon coat when Geirröd captured him at his abode, Geirrödargarðar, and locked him in a chest for three months. To save his life, Loki promised to bring Thor to the giant's castle without his belt of strength and his hammer.

On the way, Thor and Loki (or Thjálfi, in the Þórsdrápa) stop at the home of the giantess Gríðr, who warns Thor of the plot and lends him a new belt of strength, a pair of iron gloves and a staff called Grídarvöl. On arrival, Geirröd challenges Thor to a game and hurls a piece of red-hot iron at him. Thor catches it with the gloves, and when the giant hides behind a pillar, the god throws the iron through it and kills him. He then also kills the frost giants he meets, among them Gjálp and Greip, Geirröd's daughters.

The versions differ in the details. In Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, the hero Thorkillus finds in the hall of the dead the pierced body of Geruthus and three daughters with broken backs, and is told that Thor killed them with a glowing ingot and with thunderbolts. The Grímnismál mentions a different Geirröd, a jötunn king whom Odin kills as punishment for his cruelty, with no connection to the first.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons, Geirrod, is named after him. The scholar John Lindow has noted that the myth shares features with other Thor stories, such as the dangerous journey to the otherworld and the giantesses' special enmity, and that it introduces ideas linked to smithing.

Ancient sources: The story appears in Eilíf Godrúnarson's skaldic poem Þórsdrápa, cited in the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, and in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Geirröðr is usually translated as 'spear-reddener', from the Old Norse geirr, 'spear'.
An ally of Thor. In the sources, it is the giantess Gríðr who warns Thor of the trap and lends him her magical objects.
