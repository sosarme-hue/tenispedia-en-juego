Vedic sky god, the Father of Heaven

Dyaus Pita

Dyaus Pita is the ancient Vedic sky god, known as the Father of Heaven. His name is the root behind Zeus, Jupiter and Tyr, which makes him a key figure for understanding the links between Indo-European religions.

The myth

In the Rigveda hymns he appears mainly as a father, married to Prithvi, the Earth, who is the mother. Their children include Agni, the fire, and Indra, lord of the sky. Ushas, the dawn, is named as their daughter. According to the Purusha sukta hymn, he was created from the head of the primordial being Purusha.

The best-known episode is his overthrow. According to the Rigveda, Indra dragged him by a foot and made him fall from the sky. The details are unclear, but the story is read as a myth of the separation of heaven and earth. The scholar Thomas Oberlies identifies him with an asura of the pre-Vedic religion, since both are defeated by Indra.

Worship and religion

In the Vedic religion he held a prominent place as father of the gods, but the sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to him. With the arrival of Puranic Hinduism he ceased to be worshipped in India.

Legacy

His name is the etymological root of Zeus, Jupiter, Tyr and the Slavic Div. In the Mahabharata epic he appears as Bhishma, a human incarnation of the god who is born on earth after a curse.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda (hymns 1.89, 1.90, 1.164, 1.191 and 4.1, among others), the Purusha sukta hymn and the Mahabharata.

Trivia

A bull and a horse. In early Indian religious art he appears either as a red bull spewing lightning or as a black horse adorned with pearls, which symbolize the stars.
A god who becomes a man. In the Mahabharata, Dyaus is reborn as Devavrata, later known as Bhishma, after a curse made several Vasus live as humans.
