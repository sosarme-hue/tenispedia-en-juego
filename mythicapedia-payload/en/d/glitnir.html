hall of justice of Forseti in Norse mythology

Glitnir

Glitnir is the hall of Forseti, the Norse god of justice and reconciliation, and the seat of justice among gods and men. Its name means 'the shining one', and it stands for the idea that discussion is preferable to violence in settling conflicts.

The myth

Glitnir is the hall of Forseti, son of Baldr and Nanna according to Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda. Its name means 'the one who shines', and it is described with golden pillars and a silver roof whose light could be seen from far away. It was there that the god sat to pass judgment for gods and men alike.

Snorri says Forseti's court was the best of all: everyone who came before him left reconciled. This sets him apart from Týr, whom the same author does not call a reconciler of men. Glitnir thus reads as an emblem of the Norse preference for talking over fighting when conflicts arise.

Some accounts also say Glitnir was a dwelling of Baldr, Forseti's father. Scholars suspect the stories of Baldr and his son may have been mixed with legends about King Guðmundr and his son Höfundr, 'the judge', who lived in the otherworldly land of Glæsisvellir.

A caution is needed. Apart from his name, nothing else in Norse mythology confirms that Forseti was a judge, as de Vries pointed out. Glitnir is therefore best seen as a picture of an ideal of justice that the texts attach to the god, rather than the setting of many stories.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which describes Forseti and his hall.

Trivia

A roof that shone. Its silver roof and golden pillars were said to radiate light visible from a great distance.
A name with modern echoes. The name Forseti means 'president' in modern Icelandic and Faroese, linking the god's name to the idea of presiding.
