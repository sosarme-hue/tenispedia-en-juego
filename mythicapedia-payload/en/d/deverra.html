Roman goddess of sweeping and protector of midwives

Deverra

Deverra was a goddess of ancient Rome linked to the broom and to sweeping, and a protector of midwives and of women in labour. Her name seems to come from the Latin verb deverro, "to sweep away", and her figure captures the idea of clearing away whatever might do harm.

The myth

Tradition counts her among three deities who watched over midwives and women in labour, alongside Pilumnus and Intercidona. Her symbol was the broom, used to sweep away evil influences.

According to Roman belief, after a child was born the house had to be swept so that the god Silvanus would not enter and torment the mother. The broom thus served as a protective barrier against a danger that lurked at the threshold.

Worship and religion

Deverra was invoked above all when a new broom was used to separate wheat from chaff and gather it into a heap. Her brooms were also used to sweep temples before various worship services, sacrifices and celebrations.

Trivia

A protective trio. Deverra was one of three deities who protected midwives and women in labour, together with Pilumnus and Intercidona.
Sacred brooms. The brooms associated with her were also used to purify temples before worship services and celebrations.
