legendary king of Tyre in the Aeneid

Belus

Belus is a legendary king of Tyre who appears in Virgil's Aeneid and other Latin works. He matters mainly as the father of Dido, the founder of Carthage, and of other figures in the Tyrian tradition.

The myth

In the Latin literary tradition, Belus ruled Tyre and was the father of Dido, the queen who founded Carthage; of Pygmalion, who later governed Tyre; and of Anna Perenna. Virgil mentions him in the Aeneid as the ancestor of the Carthaginian queen, linking Dido's story to Aeneas's arrival in Africa.

The sources also point to a historical origin. The royal father of these figures would have been King Mattan I, who reigned from 840 to 832 BC. According to the classicist T. T. Duke, the name Belus may be a hypocorism derived from Mattan, a Hebrew name meaning 'Gift of the Lord'. A legendary figure of Latin literature may thus have roots in a real monarch reshaped by tradition.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid and other Latin literary works mention him.
