Hunnic warlord of Norse legend

Hlöd

Hlöd (or Hlod) is a Hunnic warlord, the illegitimate son of the Gothic king Heidrek and the Hunnic princess Sifka. His story, told in the Hervarar saga, revolves around an inheritance claimed by force and a fratricidal war between Huns and Goths.

The myth

Raised by his grandfather Humli, king of the Huns, Hlöd grew into a handsome and brave warrior. After Heidrek's death, his half-brother Angantyr was proclaimed king of the Goths, and Hlöd travelled to Arheimar to claim his share of the inheritance. Angantyr offered him wealth, but not his father's lands or belongings. The old Gizur scorned him as a bastard, the son of a slave-girl, and the insult led Humli to muster a vast horde against the Goths.

After Hervör falls at a frontier fortress, the two armies fight for eight days by the Danube. In the end, Angantyr and Hlöd meet in combat and both die. Finding his brother's body among the slain, Angantyr laments that one brother has slain another, a cruel doom decreed by the Norns.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar saga and, probably, the Old English poem Widsith, where he appears as Hlith.
