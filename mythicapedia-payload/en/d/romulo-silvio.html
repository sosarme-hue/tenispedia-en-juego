legendary king of Alba Longa

Romulus Silvius

Romulus Silvius is, according to tradition, a king of Alba Longa, a city near the site where Rome would later rise. A descendant of Aeneas, he is remembered above all for his reputation as a tyrant and for his violent death. His story belongs to the tradition that links Aeneas to the origins of the city.

The myth

According to the myth, Romulus Silvius reigned between 873 and 854 BC in traditional chronologies, and he was a cruel ruler. He wanted his subjects to worship him as a god, so he pretended to know how to make thunder, frightening them into submission.

He was the son of Agrippa and father of King Aventinus; his great-great-great-grandson Romulus would later found Rome. Tradition also knows him as Aremulus or Alladius. He perished in a thunderstorm with torrential rain, and the story presents this as the outcome of a life built on deception.

Trivia

Feigned thunder. According to the story, Romulus Silvius posed as someone able to make thunder so that his subjects would take him for a god.
A founding great-great-great-grandson. His great-great-great-grandson Romulus, who according to tradition founded Rome, belonged to the same line of descendants of Aeneas.
