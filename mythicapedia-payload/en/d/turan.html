Etruscan goddess of love, fertility and vitality

Turan

Turan was the Etruscan goddess of love, fertility and vitality, patroness of Vulci, later identified with Aphrodite and Venus. Her name means "the lady", and she appears frequently on bronze mirrors and reliefs.

The myth

In Etruscan tradition, Turan was the wife of Laran, the god of war, although in some depictions she appears alongside other gods in mythological scenes. She was imagined as a young, beautiful, winged woman, attended by the lasas, minor winged spirits who served her.

Images show her in toilette scenes, richly robed or nude depending on the period, and in the Judgment of Paris, where she appears alongside Uni (Juno) and Menrva (Minerva). In that scene, the Trojan prince, called Elacsantre in Etruscan, chooses her as the most beautiful. She is also shown with her young lover Atunis, the equivalent of Adonis, and her son is said to have been Turnu, god of love and desire.

Worship and religion

Turan was venerated along the coast of Latium, above all at her sanctuary in Gravisca, the port of Tarquinia, where she was regarded as a protector of sailors. Votive inscriptions found there, in Greek and Etruscan, were mostly dedicated by women between the 6th and 4th centuries BCE. One calls her Turan ati, "Mother Turan".

Legacy

Turan is one of the few Etruscan goddesses to survive in Italian folklore, in Romagna, where she is known as Turanna. There she is said to be a fairy or spirit of love and happiness who helps lovers. Her name is also linked to the month of July, though we know only its Latin form, Traneus.

Ancient sources: Her name appears in votive inscriptions and Etruscan mirrors; she is not listed in the Piacenza list or in Martianus' list.

Trivia

A name tied to July. According to the sources, the Etruscan month of July bore her name, though only the Latin word Traneus is known.
A sacred swan. The swan, called Tusna, was regarded as the bird of Turan, along with the dove and the goose.
