asura demigod of Hindu mythology

Gayasura

Gayasura is an asura, a demigod in Hindu tradition, linked to the ancient region of Magadha in present-day Bihar. His body gave its name to the city of Gaya, which makes him an important figure in the sacred geography of Hinduism.

The myth

According to tradition, Gayasura was the son of Tripurasura, who was killed by Shiva, and he inherited his kingdom.

While he meditated on Mount Kolahala, his penance alarmed the gods, and Vishnu offered him a boon in exchange for stopping it: immortality, or death only at the hands of the Trimurti. Emboldened, he attacked the gods. The Trimurti, disguised as Brahmins, then asked him to serve as an altar for a sacrifice. When he agreed, his body grew and his head fell off; the rite was performed on the body, but Gayasura would not die, until Vishnu split him into three with his discus Sudarshana.

Worship and religion

His remains are venerated in three places: the head in Gaya, with the Vishnupad Temple; the navel in Jajpur, with the Biraja Temple; and the feet in Pithapuram, with the Kukkuteswara Temple.

Legacy

The city of Gaya, in Bihar, takes its name from him.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in Hindu texts such as the Bhagavata Purana and the Vayu Purana.

Trivia

One body, three temples. According to tradition, his remains were scattered across three places in present-day India: Bihar, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh.
