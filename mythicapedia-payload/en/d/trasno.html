Galician household spirit

Trasno

The trasno is a mischievous household spirit of Galician folklore, said to live in the home and play rough pranks on those who dwell there. It is one of the best-known domestic creatures of northwestern Spain.

The myth

According to Galician tradition, the trasno lives inside the house and amuses itself by causing trouble. It is described with goat-like features, including a tail, horn and hooved legs, though it is also imagined as a lame little man with a pointed cap, a long beard and a hole in his left hand. Its presence is noticed through everyday signs: objects found somewhere other than where they were left, personal items that vanish, doors and windows that suddenly slam, or pets that startle for no reason.

Unlike the poltergeist, which is tied to a single dwelling, the trasno follows its household if they move house. According to belief, it can be kept busy by leaving seeds in a corner of the home. Since it can only count up to ten, it sets about counting them, but the seeds slip through the hole in its left hand, and it must start again and again.

Trivia

The seeds. According to belief, leaving seeds around the house keeps the trasno occupied, as it spends its time trying to count them.
A spirit that follows the family. Unlike the poltergeist, the trasno does not stay in one dwelling: it accompanies its household when they move.
