old woman yōkai of Japanese folklore

Amazake-babaa

Amazake-babaa is an old woman yōkai from Japanese folklore, found mainly in Miyagi and Aomori, who visits houses at night asking for amazake. She was linked to smallpox and was at one time revered as a goddess of that disease.

The myth

She appears at the door in the middle of the night with a childlike voice, asking whether anyone has amazake, a sweet fermented rice drink. Whoever answers her call falls ill. To keep her away, a cedar leaf is placed on the path to the entrance.

In Yamanashi there is a related creature, amazake-banbaa, who goes from door to door trying to sell sake and amazake; a sign saying the household does not like sake or amazake will drive her off.

Worship and religion

Mothers offered her gifts so their children would not fall ill. In the Kohinata district of Bunkyō, Tokyo, however, the figure is a human amazake seller who died of a cold and was deified as a protector of children; the abbot of Nichirin-ji temple erected a statue of her.

Trivia

Two different visitors. Amazake-babaa only asks for the drink, while amazake-banbaa tries to sell it, so each is repelled in a different way.
Goddess of smallpox. She was known as the goddess of smallpox at a time when the disease spread widely across Japan.
