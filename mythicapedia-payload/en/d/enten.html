Sumerian god of agricultural fertility

Enten

Enten is a Sumerian god of farmers and animal fertility in ancient Mesopotamia. He matters chiefly for the mythic debate he holds with his brother Emesh, summer, over the worth of each season.

The myth

According to Sumerian tradition, Enten was the farmer and shepherd of Enlil, in charge of the fertility of sheep, goats, cattle, donkeys and birds. He and Emesh, god of vegetation, were born of Enlil's union with a hill. Both brought their gifts to Enlil's house, the E-namtila, and there began to argue over which was more valuable.

Enten claimed the title of patron of all farmers and asked Enki to grant it. Enlil judged the quarrel and awarded victory to winter, since it brought the water essential in the hot climate of the region, and he arranged the reconciliation of the brothers.

Legacy

Kramer considered this tale the closest Sumerian parallel to the Biblical story of Cain and Abel. Other scholars have compared it with the Book of Job and with an Aesop fable about winter and spring.

Ancient sources: The Debate between Winter and Summer, a Sumerian disputation written on clay tablets in the mid to late third millennium BC, many of them found at Nippur.

Trivia

Winter wins the debate. Alster explains that winter prevails because it provides the water so essential to farming in the hot climate of ancient Mesopotamia.
A biblical parallel. Samuel Noah Kramer called this myth the closest Sumerian parallel to the Genesis story of Cain and Abel.
