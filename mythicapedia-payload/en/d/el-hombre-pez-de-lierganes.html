The Fish-Man of Liérganes

The Fish-Man of Liérganes is a legendary being from Cantabria, in northern Spain, known by the nickname of Francisco de la Vega Casar, a young man who, according to tradition, drowned in Bilbao and reappeared years later as an aquatic creature in Cádiz. It is one of the best-known legends of Cantabrian folklore.

The myth

According to the tale, Francisco de la Vega Casar, a carpenter's apprentice in Bilbao, vanished in 1674 when the current swept him away while he swam with friends on the eve of Saint John's Day. Five years later, in 1679, fishermen in the Bay of Cadiz caught a creature with a human shape, scaly skin and fish-like features, which could barely speak. After days of questioning it finally uttered the word "Liérganes", a village in Cantabria that nobody in Cadiz knew.

A friar accompanied it to Liérganes, and the creature led him straight to the house of María del Casar, who recognised it as her son. It lived there for nine years, barefoot and almost mute, until it plunged into the sea and was never seen again.

Legacy

Benito Jerónimo Feijoo recorded it in his Teatro crítico universal, and Gregorio Marañón attributed it to cretinism. There is a 2009 comic, El regreso del hombre pez, and a 2017 novel, El hombre pez.

Trivia

A literary precedent. Stories of captured fish-men already circulated in Spain long before, and the second part of Lazarillo de Tormes tells how Lazaro is exhibited as a sea monster after being rescued from a storm.
A centre in a mill. Today an interpretation centre dedicated to the legend is housed in an old mill in Liérganes.
