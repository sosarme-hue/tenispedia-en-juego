Mexica goddess of night, astrology and calendars

Oxomoco

Oxomoco is a goddess of the night, astrology and the calendar in Mexica, or Aztec, tradition. She is paired with Cipactonal and, together with him, is regarded as one of the figures who gave rise to the reckoning of time.

The myth

According to the stories, Oxomoco and Cipactonal were the first human couple, a kind of Adam and Eve of the Nahua world, and they had a son called Piltzintecuhtli, who married a maiden, daughter of Xochiquetzal. In old age, Oxomoco was also known as Itzpapalotl. Her name is explained as 'first woman', from words in the Huastec language that blended into Nahuatl.

The sources do not agree on their sexes: some writers of the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries swap their roles and present them as sorcerers and magicians.

Worship and religion

In the Codex Borbonicus she appears with the tobacco gourd that priests carried on their backs. In some images she throws maize and beans from a vessel and wears a butterfly mask. In the Florentine Codex she is shown divining with knotted cords.

Legacy

A notable carved relief showing the pair survives near Yautepec, Morelos.

Ancient sources: The Annals of Cuauhtitlan mention her as keeper of the calendar, and the Popol Vuh includes her in the Quiche tradition.

Trivia

Owls in the Tonalamatl. In the Tonalamatl the pair was painted as owls, one red like the day and another black like the night.
A Huastec name. Her name may come from the Huastec language and mean 'first woman', according to researcher Rafael Tena.
