Hindu Earth goddess, mother of the Daityas

Diti

Diti is a Hindu Earth goddess, mother of the daityas, demons who opposed the gods, and of the maruts, a divine group. She appears in the Vedic texts and plays a key role in myths of the struggle between gods and asuras.

The myth

According to the Puranic scriptures, Diti is one of the sixty daughters of the patriarch Daksha. Her mother was Virni in the Mahabharata, or Asikni in other Puranas. Diti and her twelve sisters, including Aditi, married the sage Kashyapa.

From this marriage came the daityas and the maruts. Among the most famous daityas were Hiranyakashipu and Hiranyaksha. The Bhagavata Purana tells how Diti, eager for a child, did not wait for the auspicious hour her husband had named. The sage therefore foretold that her sons would be killed by Vishnu.

After her sons died in the churning of the ocean, Diti begged her husband for a child who could defeat Indra. She conceived and, following Kashyapa's advice, practised tapas and remained chaste. Indra, learning that this child would be his slayer, disguised himself as an attendant and split the foetus into many pieces with his vajra thunderbolt. From those fragments arose the maruts.

Diti is portrayed as cruel towards her husband and her sister Aditi. She is obsessed with raising the power of the asuras and is a bitter enemy of the devas, Aditi's sons. Her daughter Simhika, also called Holika, died when she tried to kill the devoted child Prahlada, and was destroyed by her own powers.

Legacy

Her sons also live on in literary tradition. The Bengali writer Krishnadas Kaviraj, in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (1582), identified Vishnu's gatekeepers, Jaya and Vijaya, with the demons who incarnated to be defeated by Vishnu's avatars. Shishupala, the king of Chedi, is also said to be an incarnation of Hiranyakashipu, and King Ashoka a reincarnation of Ashuaka.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda uses the word diti to mean generosity, the Atharva Veda names her as a goddess, a counterpart of Aditi, and the Mahabharata and the Puranas recount her story.

Trivia

A Vedic term. In the Rig Veda, the word diti means generosity and may personify that virtue, which would make it a goddess.
A foetus split into a hundred pieces. According to the story, Indra split Diti's foetus into a hundred pieces with his vajra, and from these fragments the maruts were born.
