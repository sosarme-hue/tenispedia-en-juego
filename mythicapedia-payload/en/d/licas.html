vengeful spirit of one of Odysseus's companions

Licas

Licas is, in Greek mythology, a man from Odysseus's army who was killed at Temesa; his spirit, turned into a vengeful daimon, explains an ancient annual sacrifice once practised in the town.

The myth

After a storm drove the Greek fleet to Temesa, Licas, one of Odysseus's men, raped a local maiden and was killed by the townspeople. Misfortunes followed as punishment, and the oracle of Apollo commanded them to build him a temple and sacrifice a maiden to him every year.

Many years later, during one of these sacrifices, the boxer Euthymus, a victor at three Olympiads, resolved to save the girl and faced Licas's ghost. The spectre accepted the challenge, was defeated and threw itself into the sea. Euthymus married the freed maiden, and the people of Temesa honoured him.

Worship and religion

The temple and yearly sacrifices to Licas, ordered by Apollo's oracle, shaped the religious life of Temesa. The sources do not say whether the cult survived.

Trivia

An Olympic boxer. Euthymus, the fighter in the story, won at the 74th, 76th and 77th Olympiads, according to tradition.
Another name for the sailor. Some sources give the sailor killed by the people of Temesa the name Polites, showing that the story had several versions.
