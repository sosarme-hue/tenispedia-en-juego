Mesopotamian goddess of weaving

Uttu

Uttu is a goddess of ancient Mesopotamia associated with weaving, cloth and wool. She is best known from the myth of Enki and Ninhursag, in which she is a victim of abuse by her own father.

The myth

According to tradition, Uttu was a daughter of Enki, the god of fresh waters and wisdom, and of Ninkurra. In the myth of Enki and Ninhursag, Enki pursues a series of encounters with his descendants, and Uttu is the last of them. Before this, she takes advice from Ninhursag and makes her supposed marriage conditional on Enki supplying fresh produce. Enki obtains cucumbers, apples and grapes from a farmer, then returns disguised as a gardener. This time Uttu becomes pregnant.

Ninhursag steps in, removes Enki's seed from the goddess's body and plants it in the earth, where eight new plants sprout. When Enki eats them, he falls ill, and other gods must intervene to heal him. This episode ends the cycle of incestuous relationships.

Uttu also appears in the myth Enki and the World Order, where she waits for her domain to be assigned and is called the conscientious woman and the silent one. A later version makes her a daughter of Anu, and a late god list equates her with Enki. The debate poem The Debate between Grain and Sheep places her in a time before she began to weave, that is, before civilisation.

Worship and religion

Uttu was worshipped in the E-ešgar, the house of work assignment, part of the Esagil temple complex in Babylon. A deity named dTAG.NUN, who may be the same goddess, had a temple in Umma in the Early Dynastic period. Two bilingual Sumerian-Akkadian incantations from the Neo-Assyrian period show her cooperating with Inanna in spinning yarn.

Ancient sources: The myth of Enki and Ninhursag, the myth of Enki and the World Order, the debate poem The Debate between Grain and Sheep, and two Neo-Assyrian incantations.

Trivia

A spider at the loom. The scholar Thorkild Jacobsen suggested that Uttu was imagined as a spider spinning its web. The link rests on a single text and may be a learned etymology or simply the similar sound of the Akkadian word for spider, ettūtu.
Eight plants from a single seed. In the myth, Enki's seed planted by Ninhursag gives rise to eight plants, and eating them makes the god himself fall ill.
