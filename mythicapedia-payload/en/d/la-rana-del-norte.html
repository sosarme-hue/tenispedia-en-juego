legendary creature of Estonian folklore

The Dragon of the North

The Dragon of the North is a creature from Estonian folklore, a dragon-like monster shaped like a frog that lays waste to the land until it is defeated. Today it is often reimagined as a protector of the Estonian language and culture.

The myth

In the tale "Põhja Konn", collected by Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald in 1866, a monstrous beast spreads havoc until a young man, using a signet ring, manages to defeat it. The folklorist Risto Järv classifies it as a variant of the dragon-slayer story (ATU 300), in which the frog replaces the dragon as a local version of the archetype.

Legacy

The story reached an international audience through Andrew Lang's The Yellow Fairy Book (1894), under the title "The Dragon of the North". In Andrus Kivirähk's novel The Man Who Spoke Snakish (2007), it becomes a giant sleeping protector of the Estonians against Christian crusaders. In 2025, the artist Navitrolla installed the statue Tamula konn in Võru, with spikes on its back and a soap bubble in its mouth that stands for the fragility of the Võro language.

Ancient sources: Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald, Eesti rahva ennemuistsed jutud (1866), the collection where the tale first appears in writing.

Trivia

A frog with a dragon's tradition. Although the creature is called a frog, the tale follows the pattern of dragon-slayer stories found in many European cultures.
A bubble with meaning. In the Võru statue, the soap bubble in the frog's mouth represents the fragility and transience of the Võro language.
