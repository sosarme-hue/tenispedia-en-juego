Indian goddess of protection against epidemics

Parnashavari

Parnashavari (also Parṇaśabarī or Paranasabari) is a deity of indigenous origin, linked to the Shavara people of ancient India, whom Buddhism adopted as a protector against diseases and epidemics. Her case shows how a local goddess could be absorbed into several religious traditions.

The myth

According to tradition, Parnashavari began as a deity of the Shavara people, and her worship was associated with protection from outbreaks of epidemics. She was later taken into Buddhism as a goddess of illness. Some hold that her name connects her to the Vindhya region, where the Sabaras held considerable power, though this is a hypothesis rather than an ancient account.

Worship and religion

Her worship was believed to offer effective protection against outbreaks of epidemics.

Legacy

Images from the Pala period, found in Dhaka, show her as the main goddess, flanked by the Hindu deities Jvarasura and Shitala, both associated with disease. The Kurkihar hoard contains seven bronze images of her dating from the 10th to 12th centuries CE. In Buddhism she appears as an attendant of the goddess Tara.

Trivia

Several spellings and names. In Tibetan she is called Lomagyuma, and her name is written in several forms, such as Parnashavari, Paranasavari or Parnasabari.
