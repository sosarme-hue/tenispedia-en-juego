orisha of the deep ocean in Yoruba religion

Olokun

Olokun is the orisha of the deep ocean in Yoruba religion, ruler of the sea's riches and of the depths where the secrets of life and death are kept. Depending on the region and religious tradition, Olokun is imagined as male, female or androgynous.

The myth

According to an Odu Ifa story, Olokun grew enraged and rose to the surface to drown humanity. The orishas went to Orunmila, who advised them to have Ogun forge the longest chain possible. Obatala descended into the ocean with it and bound Olokun in the depths, where tradition places a palace of ivory and precious stones.

Worship and religion

In Ifa and Santería practice, Olokun's vessel is a large clay or ceramic jar, and offerings include cooked corn, animals and ceremonial dances. Olokun is linked to the joining of earth and sea, and in Ile-Ife the cult once held great importance. In Brazilian Candomblé, Olokun is recognized as the mother of Yemoja, though without songs of their own.

Legacy

Olokun's name survives in Brazilian Candomblé and in the Festival of Yemoja. Archaeological sites at Ile-Ife, linked to the cult, reveal an ancient industry of glass bead making dating mainly from the eleventh to fifteenth centuries.

Ancient sources: The oral corpus of Ifa, in its odu, preserves the story of Olokun's punishment and binding.

Trivia

Ifa and the babalawo's Olokun. In Ifa, the babalawo's Olokun is conceived without water, in the empty space between the earth's core and the ocean waters.
