protector deities of Chinese seafarers

Xiongdi Gong

Xiongdi Gong, also known as the '108 Saints', are protector deities of seafarers in Chinese folk religion, especially strong in Hainan. They are honored at coastal temples, often alongside Shui Wei Sheng Niang.

The myth

Tradition tells that 109 men from Hainan, who had worked in Annam (today Vietnam), were sailing home carrying the savings of their fellow villagers. Pirates attacked at sea, killing everyone except the cook, who survived hidden under the deck. Soon afterward a flock of crows gathered over the king's palace, and his seer declared they foretold an unpunished crime. A captured pirate wore a ring that one Hainanese man recognized as his brother's wedding gift. Once he confessed, the king seized the rest of the gang and offered a prayer for the souls of the 108 brothers, and the crows flew away from the palace.

Worship and religion

The people of Hainan built a memorial temple for the brothers, and each year they commemorate their deaths on the 27th day of the fifth lunar month. Seafarers setting out abroad pray to them and bring a cook along as a witness. Their cult spread worldwide with Hainanese emigrants and has become part of the island's identity.

Trivia

The surviving cook. According to the story, the only one to survive the attack was the cook, who hid under the deck. This is why seafarers travelling abroad bring a cook along as a witness.
A simple altar. Their altar usually holds just one statue of a red-faced scholar, rather than many images.
