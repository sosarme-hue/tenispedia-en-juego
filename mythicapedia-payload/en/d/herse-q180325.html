Greek goddess of dew

Ersa

Ersa, also known as Herse, is a minor goddess of Greek mythology who personifies the dew produced by the Moon. She matters because she is one of the few figures of this kind that appear in early Greek poetry, where natural phenomena were often given a divine face.

The myth

According to the lyric poet Alcman, who wrote in the 7th century BC, Ersa was the daughter of Zeus and Selene, the goddess of the Moon. In this way the dew of the night was explained as a phenomenon linked to moonlight, which moistened and softened the earth. Alcman phrased it almost like a riddle: the dew was the child of air and the Moon, and what nourished Ersa was the daughter of Zeus and the divine Moon.

Plutarch preserves this fragment in an excursus and reads it through the natural philosophy of his own time. He observes that air also dissolves into dew, especially on nights of full moon, and concludes that moonlight has a moistening power everywhere. The genealogy, however, is not fixed. Other sources call the daughter of Zeus and Selene Pandia or Nemea, though these may be names for different versions of the same figure.

Her figure belongs to the family of minor nature deities that poetry liked to personify. No temples or long stories of her own have survived, so Ersa appears above all as a poetic image of dew and of the quiet influence of the Moon on the world.

Ancient sources: Alcman, the 7th-century BC lyric poet, quoted by Plutarch.

Trivia

A different Herse. There is another Herse, a heroine and daughter of King Cecrops, whom the Athenians worshipped. She should not be confused with the goddess of dew.
