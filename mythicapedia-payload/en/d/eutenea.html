Greek personification of prosperity

Euthenia

Euthenia is the Greek personification of prosperity, stability and abundance, especially the abundance of grain linked to the Nile's floods. She matters because she shows how Rome presented the food supply of Egypt as part of its rule.

The myth

In a passage that Cicero preserves in De natura deorum, ancient theologians made Euthenia a daughter of Hephaestus and Aglaia, and a sister of Eucleia (glory), Eupheme (acclamation) and Philophrosyne (welcome). This family fits a cosmological view: Hephaestus appears as the craftsman of the sky, and Aglaia as the one who adorns it with the stars.

Apart from this genealogy, there are no stories about her of her own. She has no known deeds or episodes in which she plays the lead.

Worship and religion

She had no cult of her own. Her Greek name, euthenia, meant plenty of food, and in Roman Egypt the management of the grain supply was also called euthenia, with its official known as the eutheniarchos. She was considered a consort of Nilus, the Greek god of the river, and became syncretized with the Egyptian goddess Isis.

Ancient sources: Cicero, in De natura deorum, records the genealogy that makes her a daughter of Hephaestus and Aglaia. Otherwise, there are almost no literary sources; she is known mainly from coin imagery.

Trivia

An imperial coin. Euthenia appeared on Roman coins minted in the Greek East, where she served as the Greek equivalent of the Roman personifications Annona and Abundantia.
An office named after her. The magistrate in charge of the food supply in Egypt was called the eutheniarchos, a title derived from her name.
