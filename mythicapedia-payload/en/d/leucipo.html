Messenian prince in Greek mythology

Leucippus

Leucippus is a Messenian prince in Greek mythology, son of Perieres and father of the Leucippides, Hilaeira and Phoebe, and of Arsinoe. His story is intertwined with that of the Dioscuri and with that of Apollo, which is why he appears in several tales of the household of Tyndareus.

The myth

According to a Messenian tradition, Leucippus was the son of Perieres and Gorgophone, and brother of Aphareus and Icarius. When Perieres died, Leucippus and Aphareus inherited the kingdom, but Aphareus came to hold greater power and eventually pushed his brother aside. Leucippus also took part in the hunt for the Calydonian Boar.

According to at least one author, his wife was Philodice, and she bore him Phoebe and Hilaeira, known as the Leucippides. The two girls were carried off by their cousins Castor and Polydeuces, who married them. In revenge, Idas and Lynceus, nephews of Leucippus and rival suitors, killed Castor. Polydeuces, a son of Zeus, was immortal and persuaded Zeus to grant immortality to Castor as well, so the brothers alternated between Olympus and Hades.

Tradition also holds that Apollo had romances with Leucippus and with his wife, and that Arsinoe, another daughter of the prince, was loved by the god and bore him Asclepius. According to the Cypria, the Leucippides were daughters of Apollo.

Legacy

According to a Messenian tradition, the town of Leuctra took its name from Leucippus, although the sources place it in Boeotia.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources that mention him include the Cypria, cited for the tradition about the Leucippides and their ties to Apollo.

Trivia

Two places, one name. According to a Messenian tradition, the town of Leuctra took its name from Leucippus, although it is located in Boeotia rather than Messenia.
