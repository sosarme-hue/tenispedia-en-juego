Sami goddess of the sun and spring

Beaivi

Beaivi (also Beiwe) is the Sami sun deity, worshipped by the indigenous people of Fennoscandia. She is linked to spring, the fertility of plants and animals, especially reindeer, and to sanity. Her name is the same as the sun itself.

The myth

According to Sami tradition, Beaivi travels across the sky with her daughter, Beaivi-nieida, in an enclosure covered with reindeer bones, bringing green plants back after winter so the reindeer can feed. She was also called upon to restore the minds of those driven mad by the long dark winter.

Versions vary by region. In Southern Sápmi the sun is female, while among the Skolt and Kola Sami it appears as a man. According to Samuel Rheen, she was considered the mother of all living things.

Worship and religion

White female reindeer were sacrificed to her, and their meat was cut into strips and braided into hoops decorated with ribbons. In spring, doorposts were smeared with butter so the goddess could regain strength and climb higher in the sky. Sacrifices were held on platforms behind the goahti, alongside those of another deity.

Trivia

Butter for the sun. In spring, butter was smeared on doorposts because it melts in sunshine. People believed this helped the goddess regain her strength.
The sun on drums. On Southern Sami drums the Sun appears as a central square with four lines reaching toward the four cardinal directions.
