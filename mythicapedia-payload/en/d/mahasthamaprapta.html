bodhisattva of strength and wisdom in Mahayana Buddhism

Mahāsthāmaprāpta

Mahāsthāmaprāpta, known in China as Dashizhi and in Japan as Daiseishi, is a Mahayana bodhisattva associated with wisdom and spiritual strength. His name means "arrival of great strength", and he is one of the most revered figures in the Pure Land school.

The myth

According to Buddhist texts, Mahāsthāmaprāpta forms a trinity with Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara in Pure Land belief, and accompanies the Buddha of infinite light in welcoming the souls of the dying into the paradise of Sukhāvatī. He plays a prominent role in the Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, and in the Lotus Sutra he is among the eighty thousand bodhisattvas gathered on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa. In the Śūraṅgama Sūtra he recounts how he reached enlightenment through nianfo, the continuous mindful remembrance of Amitābha, until he attained samādhi.

Tradition portrays him differently from region to region. In China he often appears with feminine features resembling those of Avalokiteśvara, while Tibetan Buddhism equates him with Vajrapāṇi, the protector of the Buddha. In Japan he is linked to temple guardians and to the rites for the dead.

Worship and religion

In Japan he is one of the Thirteen Buddhas of the Shingon school and, according to tradition, watches over those born in the Year of the Horse and presides over the first anniversary rite of a person's death. His mantra is recited in Shingon and Tendai temples. His veneration continues today in temples across East Asia.

Legacy

The Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra symbolizes him with the moon, in contrast to the sun that represents Avalokiteśvara. In Japan, the monk Shinran identified him with Hōnen, founder of the Pure Land school, in a hymn composed after his teacher's death.

Ancient sources: The Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, the Śūraṅgama Sūtra, the Lotus Sutra and the Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra.

Trivia

The moon and the sun. The Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra assigns the moon to Mahāsthāmaprāpta and the sun to Avalokiteśvara, a pairing that presents the two bodhisattvas as complementary.
A year of the horse. In Japanese tradition, Mahāsthāmaprāpta is said to protect people born in the Year of the Horse.
