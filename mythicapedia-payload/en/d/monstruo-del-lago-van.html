Lake Van Monster

The Lake Van Monster is a creature said to live in the lake of the same name in eastern Turkey. Its roots lie in the Armenian vishaps, water dragons of Armenian tradition, and today it is one of the best-known beings in modern Turkish folklore.

The myth

According to the Armenian chroniclers Movses Khorenatsi and Anania Shirakatsi, vishaps, water dragons from Armenian mythology, lived in the lake. Tradition holds that the god Vahagn, called vishapakagh ("reaper of vishaps"), would plunge into the lake to drag out any vishap that had grown large enough to devour the world.

Scholar James Russell suggests this legend may derive from Urartian myths about the god Teisheba fighting a water monster. In 1889, the Ottoman newspaper Saadet reported that a creature had dragged a man into the lake; the government sent a scientific survey that found nothing. In 1997, Ünal Kozak claimed to have filmed the monster, a video that is still debated.

Legacy

The Turkish poet and former prime minister Bülent Ecevit wrote a poem about it, and the monster appears in the series The Secret Saturdays and Destination Truth. A four-metre statue honours it in Van, and Deniz Karakurt's Dictionary of Turkish Mythology includes it.

Ancient sources: The Armenian chroniclers Movses Khorenatsi and Anania Shirakatsi wrote about the vishaps living in Lake Van.

Trivia

A much-debated video. Critics of Kozak's footage note that the camera never pans left, which could hide a boat, and that the creature moves in a straight line.
A recent monster. According to scholar James Russell, Kurdish beliefs about the monster owe more to Western lake monster stories than to Armenian traditions.
