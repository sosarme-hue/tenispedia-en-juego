Heliad of Greek mythology

Candalus

Candalus was one of the Heliadae, the children that the sun god Helios had with the nymph Rhodos. His story, tied to the island of Rhodes, is a Greek tale of sibling envy and of the punishment that follows the crime.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Candalus was a son of Helios and the nymph Rhodos, and therefore one of the Heliadae, the descendants of the sun god. Together with his mother and brothers, he co-ruled the island of Rhodes. Among them stood out Tenages, whose skills in the arts and whose intelligence stirred the jealousy of some of his brothers. Candalus, along with several of them, plotted to kill him.

The crime had consequences. Once it came to light, Candalus had to leave Rhodes, the island he shared with his mother and brothers, and settle on Cos. The myth gives no further details about his life there. The English reference names his brothers Triopas, Macar and Actis as accomplices and speaks of Tenages' skill in science, which shows that the details change from one telling to another.

The episode has the tone of a moral tale about envy and justice within a family. Candalus is not a central figure of Greek mythology, but his story shows how the stories about Rhodes linked the island's origins with the solar lineage of its first rulers.

Trivia

Candalus's brothers. In the English reference, Candalus appears alongside Triopas, Macar and Actis as brothers involved in the death of Tenages, the fifth brother.
A shared rule. The Heliadae ruled Rhodes together with their mother, Rhodos, until the crime forced Candalus to leave for Cos.
