solar god of Urartu

Shivini

Shivini was a solar god in the mythology of Urartu, the Iron Age kingdom of the Armenian Highlands. He was the third member of a divine triad alongside Khaldi and Theispas, and he is associated with the solar disc.

The myth

Surviving information about Shivini is scarce and comes mostly from Urartian sources, especially inscriptions and works of art. He was shown as a kneeling man raising a solar disc, an image that linked him to the sun and to the upkeep of cosmic order.

In the Urartian pantheon he appeared alongside Khaldi and Theispas, the kingdom's main gods, and held the third place in that triad. Some scholars suggest that his wife was most likely the goddess Tushpuea, listed as the third goddess in the Mheri-Dur inscription.

Researchers have compared Shivini with the Assyrian sun god Shamash because of their shared role. Some scholars, including Armen Petrosyan, argue that his name derives from a Hittite source and shares the Indo-European origin of the names of Zeus and Jupiter, though this remains a hypothesis rather than an established fact.

Legacy

In modern Armenia, Shivini appears on an Armenian postage stamp from 1993, showing his place in the country's cultural memory. His image also survives on Urartian bronze objects, such as a caldron with a sculpted bronze handle.

Ancient sources: His attestations come mainly from Urartian inscriptions, such as that of Mheri-Dur, and from artworks produced within Urartian culture.

Trivia

Armenian stamp. Shivini appears on an Armenian postage stamp issued in 1993.
Bronze caldron. His image appears on an Urartian bronze caldron with a sculpted handle.
