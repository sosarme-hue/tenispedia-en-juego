king of the Munster sidhe in Irish mythology

Ethal Anbuail

Ethal Anbuail is a king of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology, ruler of the Munster sidhe. He is best known for the war fought so that his daughter Cáer could marry Óengus.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Ethal Anbuail was the father of Cáer. His main role appears in the tale of the dream of Óengus, son of the Dagda.

The Dagda and Ailill mac Máta waged war on Ethal so that Cáer could marry Óengus. In the conflict, Ethal's kingdom was destroyed by the forces of the Dagda and Ailill.

Legacy

His name is little known outside Irish literature, but the same episode inspired W. B. Yeats, who evoked it in the poem The Song of Wandering Aengus, about a young man searching for the girl he saw in a dream.

Ancient sources: The medieval Irish tale Aisling Óenguso (The Dream of Óengus) preserves his story within Irish literature.
