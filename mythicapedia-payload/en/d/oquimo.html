king of Rhodes, son of Helios

Ochimus

Ochimus was the eldest of the Heliadae, the seven sons that Helios had with the nymph Rhodos, and he ruled the island of Rhodes after his brothers were expelled. He matters because he shows how power was shared among the sun god's descendants in Greek myth.

The myth

According to the tradition, the Telchines, dog-headed daimones who controlled the weather and had defied the gods, abandoned Rhodes after foreseeing a great flood. Helios, to whom the island belonged, then raised it from the waters with his rays, named it after his beloved Rhodos, and gave its rule to the seven sons he had with her, Ochimus among them.

The harmony did not last. Four of the Heliadae, envious of the skills of their brother Tenages, plotted and carried out his murder. When the crime came to light, they were expelled from Rhodes, so only Cercaphus and Ochimus remained, since they had taken no part. Ochimus, the eldest, seized the throne.

With the naiad Hegetoria he had a daughter, Cydippe or Cyrbia, who married Cercaphus, making him heir to his brother's throne. In an alternate version, Ochimus had promised Cydippe to Ocridion, but Cercaphus loved her and kidnapped her. The pair fled far away and did not return until Ochimus was an old man. From then on, the betrayal by Ocridion's herald, who had handed the young woman to Cercaphus, left a lasting mark on the place: no herald dared to enter Ocridion's shrine.

Trivia

The Telchines. Before the Heliadae, Rhodes was inhabited by the Telchines, dog-headed daimones believed to control rain and other sky phenomena.
A forbidden shrine. After Cydippe's abduction, no herald dared to enter Ocridion's shrine, in memory of the betrayal that caused it.
