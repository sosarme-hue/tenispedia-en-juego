king of the Getae in Greek myth

Charnabon

Charnabon is a king of the Getae, a Thracian people, who in Greek myth treated Triptolemus, the envoy of Demeter, with cruelty. He matters mainly for the punishment that sent him to the sky as a constellation.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Hyginus, the king welcomed Triptolemus, who was travelling the world teaching agriculture. Then Charnabon seized his guest treacherously and nearly killed him. Triptolemus could not escape, because the king had killed one of the dragons that pulled his chariot.

Demeter rescued him, returned his chariot with a replacement dragon and punished the king with a miserable life. After his death he was placed among the stars as the constellation Ophiuchus, which shows a man holding a serpent, a reminder of his crime and punishment. Sophocles' tragedy Triptolemus, known only from fragments, names him as ruler of the Getae without fixing the location of his kingdom.

Legacy

His story is linked to the constellation Ophiuchus, the figure of a man grasping a serpent.

Ancient sources: Hyginus's Poetical Astronomy preserves the story; Sophocles' tragedy Triptolemus mentions him only in fragments.

Trivia

Name in Greek. In Greek it is written Χαρναβών. Hyginus, who wrote in Latin, calls him Carnabon.
A constellation of punishment. Ophiuchus shows a man holding a serpent, interpreted here as a reminder of his crime.
