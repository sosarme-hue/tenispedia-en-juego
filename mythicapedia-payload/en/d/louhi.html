Mistress of Pohjola, witch and mother of diseases in Finnish mythology

Louhi

Louhi is the fearsome ruler of Pohjola, the North, in Finnish and Karelian mythology. A sorceress able to take the shape of birds and other beings, she is Väinämöinen's great rival in the Kalevala and is linked to the tale of the mother of the world's diseases.

The myth

In the Kalevala, Louhi rules Pohjola, a northern land known for its riches and magic. To gain the Sampo, a magical artefact that brings prosperity, Väinämöinen and his allies steal it from Pohjola and carry it off by boat. Louhi turns into a monstrous bird and pursues them. One of the heroes cuts off the toes of one of her feet, and the Sampo falls into the sea in three pieces.

Oral songs tell the story in many ways. In some, Louhi is a maiden who becomes pregnant by water and gives birth to the diseases of the world, naming them herself. In others she is the mother of Väinämöinen, or of a son who goes with a black dog to claim his inheritance. In a Kainuu song she shows compassion, taking in the wounded hero who drifts to Pohjola. As in many fairy tales, she also sets impossible tasks for suitors seeking her beautiful daughters.

Ancient sources: Her name and forms appear mainly in Finnish and Karelian folk songs collected by Elias Lönnrot and woven into the Kalevala.

Trivia

A name tied to trance. Her name seems linked to lovi, a state of ecstasy or trance, expressed in Finnish by the phrase to fall into lovi.
Bird of Pohjola. In some traditions Louhi takes the form of a kokko, an eagle that flies from Pohjola and that Forest Finns associated with the god Ukko.
