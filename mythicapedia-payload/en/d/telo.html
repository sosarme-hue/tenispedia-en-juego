Celtic god eponymous of Toulon

Telo

Telo is a Celtic god linked to Toulon, in the French department of Var, a city that takes its name from him as its eponymous spirit. He matters because he was the deity of a sacred spring around which the ancient settlement grew.

The myth

The sources preserve no extended story about Telo. Nearly everything known about him comes from his bond with the place: he was the protective spirit of a sacred spring, and the settlement grew up around it. For this reason his mythological traits remain poorly defined, and his figure is understood mainly through the territory he watched over.

Worship and religion

His veneration centred on the spring at Toulon. Several dedications to Telo survive from Périgueux, where he is invoked alongside other deities, including the goddesses Sianna and Vesunna.

Legacy

His name lives on in the history of Toulon, a city that the sources present as named after this spirit.

Ancient sources: Mainly votive inscriptions found at Périgueux, which mention him on several occasions alongside other deities.

Trivia

Dedications at Périgueux. Several inscriptions dedicated to Telo have been found at Périgueux, showing that his worship reached beyond Toulon.
Divine companions. On three of these dedications Telo is invoked together with other deities, among them the goddesses Sianna and Vesunna.
