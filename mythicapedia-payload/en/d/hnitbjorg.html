mountain home of the giant Suttung in Norse mythology

Hnitbjörg

Hnitbjörg is the mountain where the jötunn Suttung hid the mead of poetry in Norse mythology. Its name is often translated as 'colliding rocks', and the tale of Odin winning that mead over makes it one of the key settings of the tradition.

The myth

According to the Norse tradition, Hnitbjörg was the mountain abode of the jötunn Suttung, who kept the mead of poetry there. To keep it safe, Suttung entrusted it to his daughter Gunnlöð, who guarded the treasure inside the mountain.

Odin, determined to get hold of that mead, turned to Baugi, brother of Suttung. With his help, the god drilled a hole into the rock, gained access to the mountain and reached the mead, which thus came into his power.

Trivia

A name suggesting motion. The Old Norse name can be translated as 'colliding rocks', which may imply that the mountain could open and close.
