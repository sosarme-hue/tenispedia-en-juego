Mongolian earth goddess

Etügen Eke

Etügen Eke ('Mother Earth') is an earth goddess of Mongolic and Turkic tradition, ranked as the second most important deity after Tengri. People honored her as the protector of the homeland and of nature.

The myth

According to Mongolian myth, Etugen existed at the centre of the universe. She was imagined as a young, beautiful and voluptuous woman, often riding a grey bull. Her name may derive from Ötüken, the sacred mountain of the earth and fertility goddess of the ancient Turks. Some medieval sources pair her with a male counterpart, Natigai, although this is probably a mistake caused by a mispronunciation of Etugen.

The fate of people and nations belonged to Tengri, while natural forces were Etugen's domain. On Tengri's command she could punish the sins of the people, but she was generally considered benevolent.

Worship and religion

To appease her, sacrifices were offered every spring, before the cattle-breeding season and sowing, and also in autumn, after the harvest. In the time of the khaganates they took on a nationwide character, with a reddish horse sacrificed near rivers and lakes.

Trivia

A doubtful partner. Some medieval sources give her a male partner called Natigai, but this is thought to be a mispronunciation error.
A name with an ancient echo. Her name may come from Ötüken, the sacred mountain of the ancient Turks linked to earth and fertility.
