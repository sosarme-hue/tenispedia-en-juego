Viking hero and legendary king of Denmark and Sweden

Ragnar Lodbrok

Ragnar Lodbrok is a Viking hero and legendary king of Sweden and Denmark, the protagonist of Icelandic sagas and Norse chronicles from the Viking Age. He matters because his figure brings together raids, marriages to shield-maidens and a death that, according to tradition, sparked the great invasion of England.

The myth

According to the Icelandic sagas, Ragnar was the son of the Swedish king Sigurd Ring and of Alfhild, a princess of the small realm of Álfheimr. The sources do not agree on his genealogy: Saxo Grammaticus links him to the House of the Ynglings, while other traditions connect his father to the legendary Randver. His nickname Lodbrok comes from the unusual protective clothing with which, the story goes, he killed a giant serpent guarding the home of Thora Borgarhjort, daughter of King Herrauðr, whom he then married.

After Thora's death, tradition says, he met Kráka, a woman of great beauty and wisdom who lived with a poor peasant couple in Norway. The sagas reveal that she was in fact Aslaug, the secret daughter of the hero Sigurd Fafnesbane. With her he had Ivar the Boneless, Björn Ironside, Hvitserk, Ragnvald and Sigurd Snake-in-the-Eye. Saxo, by contrast, speaks of Ladgerda, a shield-maiden whom he won after killing a bear and a huge hound.

The stories portray him as a warlord who spent much of his life raiding the Christian coasts of Europe. Saxo describes campaigns in the Baltic and, around 845, an assault on the Frankish kingdom with 120 ships and some 5,000 men, during which he took part in the siege of Paris. Later, according to tradition, he tried to conquer England with just two ships. Defeated by the forces of Ælla of Northumbria, he was thrown into a pit of venomous snakes. His last words promised that his cubs would avenge him, and his sons led the Great Heathen Army.

Historians have not been able to identify him with one specific man. Candidates such as King Horik I or a leader called Reginherus have been proposed, but many conclude that Ragnar is a blend of historical figures and a literary invention. His sons, who appear in more reliable chronicles, have a firmer footing.

Legacy

His figure has had great reach in modern popular culture. He is the main character of the series Vikings, played by Travis Fimmel, and appears in the 1958 film The Vikings, with Ernest Borgnine. He is also a supporting character in the comic El Capitán Trueno, as the father of Sigrid, and an unlockable figure in the video game Rise of Kingdoms. In Assassin's Creed Valhalla, the mission «The Sons of Ragnar» recalls him alongside two of his sons, Ivar and Ubbe.

Ancient sources: Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, the chronicle of Sven Aggesen, and Icelandic sagas such as the Saga of Ragnar Lodbrok and the Tale of Ragnar's Sons; the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle also mentions him.

Trivia

The nickname's origin. Lodbrok is explained by the protective clothing he wore when facing the serpent guarding Thora. The sources describe the garment, but do not give a secure etymology for the name.
A death in a snake pit. According to the sagas, Ragnar died thrown into a pit of venomous snakes by Ælla of Northumbria, and his sons avenged him with a great invasion of England.
