Arruns of Clusium

Arruns of Clusium is a probably legendary figure who, in Roman tradition, drew the Gauls into Italy by urging them to attack his own Etruscan city of Clusium, before their sack of Rome in 390 BC.

The myth

According to the tale, Arruns was the guardian of a young aristocrat of Clusium named Lucumo, who ended up seducing Arruns's wife. Unable to confront a man from one of the city's great families, Arruns turned his anger against Clusium itself and resolved to seek revenge among the Gauls.

He travelled to Gaul carrying wine and, according to Dionysius of Halicarnassus, olive oil and figs as well. The Gauls, who had never tasted wine, were so taken with these luxuries that they resolved to invade Italy. Arruns guided them over the Alps to Clusium, and the city became the launching point of the campaign that led to the sack of Rome. Modern historians regard the story as a literary construction, perhaps Roman, and some link it to internal political conflicts in Clusium.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus and Livy recount it, and Plutarch also mentions it.

Trivia

Another account by Pliny. Pliny the Elder tells a different story about a Helvetian smith named Helico who, after returning home with dried figs, grapes, olive oil and wine, pushed his compatriots into war to obtain these goods.
Different causes according to different authors. Polybius attributes the Gallic migration to the beauty of the land, Appian to overpopulation in Gaul, and Justin to internal struggles among the Gauls.
