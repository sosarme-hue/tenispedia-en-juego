Chinese god of water control

Erlang Shen

Erlang Shen is a Chinese god linked to flood control, recognisable by a third eye in his forehead. He is most often identified with Li Erlang, son of the hydraulic engineer Li Bing and builder of the Dujiangyan irrigation system.

The myth

According to legend, Li Erlang helped his father build the irrigation system that protected the Chengdu Plain. While searching for the source of the floods, he killed a tiger, gathered seven hunters and reached a cottage where an old woman was mourning her grandson, destined to be sacrificed to a dragon. Following his father's plan, he captured the dragon in the river and secured it beneath the Dragon-Taming Temple.

In Ming novels such as Investiture of the Gods and Journey to the West, he appears as Yang Jian, nephew of the Jade Emperor.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped around Guankou and Dujiangyan, where the Two Kings Temple honours father and son. His cult spread across China during the Northern Song dynasty.

Legacy

He appears in Ming novels and in the 2009 Chinese television series Lotus Lantern.

Ancient sources: The story of Li Bing and his son appears in the Records of Guansian, and Li Erlang's first mention is in the Zhishui ji by Li Ying, of the Liang dynasty.

Trivia

A third eye. He is depicted with a third eye in his forehead, called the Eye of Heaven, which lets him see through deceptions and transformations.
A loyal dog. He is always accompanied by his hunting dog Xiaotian Quan, which can attack demons and evil spirits.
