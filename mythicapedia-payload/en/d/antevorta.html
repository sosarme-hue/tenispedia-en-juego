Roman goddess of the future and childbirth

Antevorta

Antevorta was a Roman goddess of the future, sister of Postverta and linked to Carmenta. Together the two sisters watched over childbirth and the fate of newborns.

The myth

According to tradition, Antevorta and Postverta were companions of Carmenta, though they may have been only epithets of her cult or two aspects of one goddess, one knowing the past and the other the future, much like the two-faced Janus. Ovid assigns Porrima the song of what has already happened and Postverta the song of what is to come. Augustine of Hippo mentions them as goddesses who foretell the fortune of those who are born.

At birth, Antevorta was present when the baby came head first, and Postverta when the feet came first. Aulus Gellius, citing Varro, says altars were raised to both to ward off difficult labours.

Worship and religion

They had two altars in Rome, and pregnant women invoked them as protectors against the dangers of childbirth. Expiations were offered to them during the Carmentalia.

Legacy

Her name survives as Porrima, the name of the star Gamma Virginis in the constellation Virgo.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Aulus Gellius (citing Varro) and Augustine of Hippo mention them as goddesses of the future, the past and birth.

Trivia

A star with her name. The star Gamma Virginis, in Virgo, bears the name Porrima, one of the sisters.
Altars in Rome. Rome had two altars dedicated to these goddesses, set up to protect mothers in childbirth.
