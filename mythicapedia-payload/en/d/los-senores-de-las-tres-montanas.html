Taoist earth deities worshipped by Teochew and Hakka communities

Lords of the Three Mountains

The Lords of the Three Mountains are protective Taoist deities from the Jieyang region of Guangdong. They are deified spirits of three local mountains, and they are worshipped mainly by Teochew and Hakka communities, including in Taiwan and Southeast Asia.

The myth

In one version, the three spirits were once three generals serving Emperor Wen of the Sui dynasty, venerated after their deaths for their military achievements.

Another tradition, widely told, places their intervention in the Song dynasty, when they supposedly revealed their power to help Emperor Taizong quell unrest. For this they received the title of "kings" and personal names: Lian Jie, Zhao Xuan and Qiao Jun, each shown with a distinctive face: white, red and black.

Worship and religion

They belong to the earthly deities of the Taoist pantheon and are believed to guard against evil and avert disasters. Their temples, many officially registered as Taoist institutions, hold daily offerings, jiao rites and birthday celebrations. The cult remains alive, especially in Taiwan, which has 184 temples, including the Hepolun temple in Changhua, regarded as a founding site.

Trivia

Symbolic faces. Each king is identified by face color: white for the first, red for the second and black for the third. These colors are linked to virtues and to Five Elements symbolism.
Founding temple of Taiwan. According to tradition, two farmers from Jieyang left a sachet of incense on a mound beside a lotus pond, and that night a light appeared, leading residents to build a temple there.
