Greek name of the planet Mercury

Stilbon

Stilbon is the Greek name of the planet Mercury, linked to the god Hermes and, in some sources, to Apollo. Its name means «the shining, glittering one» and it belongs to the list of planets of classical astronomy.

The myth

Stilbon is less a story of its own than the name by which the Greeks knew a heavenly body and the deity connected with it. Some traditions make it a son of Astraeus and Eos, the dawn, which would link it to the stars, although the ancient texts do not tell it this way directly.

Aristotle records its name among those of the five planets, and the Epinomis, attributed to Plato or to one of his disciples, lists the star of Hermes among them. For astrologers its influence was variable, sometimes beneficent and sometimes harmful. The Romans identified it with the star of Mercury or with that of Apollo.

Legacy

The name Mercury still designates the planet closest to the Sun, and the idea of the planet's changing influence survived in astrology, which for centuries was not clearly separated from astronomy.

Ancient sources: Aristotle and the Epinomis, which lists the star of Hermes among the planets, and the Timaeus, where the identification of Hermes with the planet first appears.

Trivia

A luminous name. Stilbon means «the shining, glittering one», an allusion to the light of the heavenly body.
A distant estimate. Pliny the Elder estimated the planet's orbit at 339 days, nearly four times the actual period of 88 days.
