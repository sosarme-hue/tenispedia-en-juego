dwarf smith of Norse mythology

Eitri

Eitri is a dwarf smith from Norse mythology, brother of Brokkr. He is known for forging, together with his brother, treasures of the gods in a famous wager with Loki.

The myth

According to the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda, Loki boasted that the Sons of Ivaldi had made admirable objects: Sif's hair, Freyr's ship Skidbladnir and Odin's spear Gungnir. He wagered his own head that Eitri and Brokkr could not match them.

Eitri got to work at his furnace while his brother worked the bellows. A fly, sometimes thought to be Loki himself, kept stinging Brokkr to spoil the work. Even so, Eitri created the golden boar Gullinbursti, the ring Draupnir and the hammer Mjölnir, which won the wager for Brokkr, although its handle turned out shorter than it should have been.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Skáldskaparmál, is the source that recounts the wager and the forging of the objects.

Trivia

An annoying fly. According to the story, a fly stung Brokkr while he worked the bellows, and it is sometimes thought to have been Loki in disguise.
A handle too short. Despite winning the wager, the hammer Mjölnir had a handle shorter than it should have been, a detail the story mentions explicitly.
