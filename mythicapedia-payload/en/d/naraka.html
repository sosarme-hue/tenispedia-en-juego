realm of torment in Buddhism, one of the six realms of existence

Naraka

Naraka is, in Buddhist cosmology, the realm of greatest suffering among the six realms of existence, an underground place of torment that beings are reborn into as a result of their karma. It is counted among the four unfortunate rebirths, together with the animal realm, the asuras and the pretas. According to Buddhist tradition, beings are born into a naraka as the direct result of their previous karma, not through a divine judgment. They stay there for a set period, which can last several kalpas, until the negative karma is exhausted and they can be reborn in higher worlds. The realm lies beneath the surface, in a network of caverns running below Jambudvīpa, the ordinary human world. The texts describe many different systems of hells. One common classification groups eight cold and eight hot narakas, and some sources speak of hundreds or even thousands. The Devadūta Sutta places a central Mahāniraya surrounded by adjacent areas, such as the Hell of Feces or the Forest of Sword Leaves, and the Saṃkicca Jātaka lists eight great hells. In the Sañjīva, the wardens hack the damned to pieces, and they come back to life again and again to suffer once more.

Worship and religion

The Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva Sutra describes the suffering of this realm and teaches how people can transfer their own merits to ease the beings who dwell there, a practice linked to Dizàng or Jizō. In Chinese Buddhism, the tale of Mulian recounts how the Buddha's disciple descended into the naraka to win a better rebirth for his mother.

Legacy

The term spread to other languages: the Malay neraka, even though Malaysia's population is predominantly Muslim, and the Khmer norok. In China, its descriptions merged with Diyu, the hell of Chinese mythology, and with an otherworld bureaucracy that mirrored the imperial administration.

Ancient sources: Pali texts such as the Kathāvatthu, the Devadūta Sutta, the Dhammapada and the Jātakas, which describe hell and the causes that lead to it.

Trivia

A name with modern echoes. The word neraka was adopted in Malay to describe hell, even though Malaysia's population is predominantly Muslim.
Lives that multiply. In the cold narakas, each life lasts twenty times longer than the one before.
