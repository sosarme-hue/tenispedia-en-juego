Buddhist deity of death, temptation and illusion

Mara

Mara is the figure who, in Buddhism, embodies the forces that stand in the way of enlightenment: death, desire and the illusion of the world. Although his name comes from Hindu tradition, Buddhism gave him the central role he still holds.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, Mara is a celestial ruler who tried to stop Prince Siddhartha Gautama from reaching enlightenment and escaping the cycle of rebirth (samsara). As Siddhartha neared awakening, Mara arrived on his elephant Girimekhala with a vast army to intimidate him. The devas who had accompanied the prince fled, and Siddhartha faced Mara alone, relying on the ten perfections (paramitas) he had cultivated for long ages. Storms, winds and thunder failed to move him, and a weapon hurled by Mara turned into a beautiful parasol that shaded the future Buddha. Siddhartha then called the earth to witness his resolve, and Girimekhala knelt before him.

Mara also tried to tempt him with his daughters, Taṇhā (thirst), Arati (aversion) and Rāga (desire), who undressed before him without success. Some accounts name five daughters, adding pride and fear. Later traditions tell that Mara eventually converted to Buddhism under the monk Upagupta, vowing never to harm the Dharma.

Worship and religion

Mara had no cult of his own like that of other deities. His role was that of an adversary, and his presence in Buddhist religious life appears mainly in iconography and in meditation practice.

Legacy

The Buddha pose in which he touches the earth with his right hand, known as the bhūmisparśa mudra, depicts the moment Siddhartha defied Mara, and is commonly called 'Buddha defying Mara'. In Buddhist philosophy, Mara serves as a metaphor for inner obstacles, harmful emotions and the ignorance that block liberation.

Ancient sources: The Atharva Veda already mentions Mṛtyu and an evil Mara; the Samyutta Nikaya records the episode of Mara's daughters.

Trivia

A parasol instead of a weapon. According to the story, the weapon Mara hurled at Siddhartha turned into a beautiful parasol that shaded him.
A giant elephant. In certain Sri Lankan traditions, Mara's mount is Girimekhala, a black elephant said to be about 250 yojanas tall.
