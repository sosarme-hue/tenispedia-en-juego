Egyptian goddesses of fate and protection

Hemsut

The Hemsut (also spelled Hemuset or Hemusut, from the hieroglyphs ḥmswt) were Egyptian goddesses of fate and protection, the female counterparts of the Ka, the male protective spirits. Their role shows how the Egyptians shared vital protection among several beings.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, the Hemsut provided nourishment, life force, power and magical protection, especially to newborn royalty. Originally they were closely tied to specific Ka and formed pairs, such as the Hemsut of Hu or the Hemsut of Heka. Over time, their concept probably widened to include other deities.

In art they appear as a shield crossed with arrows, often worn as a headdress and sometimes shown on its own. The shield symbolized the Sais nome (Sap-Meh).

Legacy

They have little presence in popular culture. They are briefly mentioned in the film The Prince of Egypt.
