ram god of Mendes

Banebdjedet

Banebdjedet was the ram god of Mendes, capital of the sixteenth nome of Lower Egypt, in the Nile Delta. His name means 'Lord of the Ba of Djedet', and he embodied the creative spirit of the sun god Ra.

The myth

According to tradition, Banebdjedet began as a local god of Mendes. When the great gods arrived, his creative spirit was linked first to Ra and then to Osiris, Set and Shu, which gave him four ram heads and eventually turned him into Khnum, god of the waters. He was the husband of the fish goddess Hatmehit, and their child was Horus the Child, forming the so-called Mendesian Triad.

In the Osirian myth he appears in the dispute between Horus and Set. Although he supports Set, he proposes that Neith should decide who deserves the throne and later suggests that Set inherit it as the elder brother. According to the Litany of Ra, Ptah, the creator god of Memphis, took his form to father the pharaoh.

Worship and religion

His main sanctuary was at Mendes, where large granite shrines were built for each of the four divine figures associated with him. He was shown with four ram heads, and in tomb KV19 Prince Mentuherkhepeshef is seen presenting offerings to him. The sources do not say whether his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: The Book of the Heavenly Cow identifies him as the Ba of Osiris, the Litany of Ra from Medinet Habu describes his role in the begetting of the pharaoh, and the Chester Beatty I papyrus records the Judgment between Horus and Set.

Trivia

Four heads. He was depicted with four ram heads, one for each of the four Bas of the sun god.
Ram and soul. In Egyptian, the words for ram and soul sounded the same, so ram deities were sometimes seen as forms of other gods.
