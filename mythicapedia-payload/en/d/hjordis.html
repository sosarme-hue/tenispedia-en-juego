wife of Sigmund and mother of Sigurd in Norse mythology

Hjördís

Hjördís is a figure of Norse mythology, wife of the hero Sigmund and mother of Sigurd. She appears in the Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda, and her story is part of the heroic lineage of the Völsungs.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Hjördís was the daughter of a king named Eylimi. She married Sigmund, whose life was marked by war and misfortune, and their union produced Sigurd, the hero who would later kill the dragon Fafnir.

Tradition presents her mainly as Sigurd's mother, so her role is chiefly to pass on her family's heroic inheritance. Her brother Grípir also appears in the lineage of the story.

Legacy

The name is used as a given name in Scandinavia, in the forms Hjördis in Sweden and Iceland and Hjørdis in Denmark and Norway, and has recently grown popular in Germany as Jördis.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda and the Prose Edda mention her among the characters of the Völsunga saga.
