Sabine goddess in the Roman tradition

Vacuna

Vacuna was an ancient Sabine goddess, worshipped around Reate, near Horace's villa and in Rome. She matters because Roman sources identified her with many other goddesses, and her exact role remains unclear.

The myth

Vacuna is a divinity whose stories barely survive. The ancient texts give her no family, no genealogy and no deeds, so her figure is known mainly through the identifications Roman authors made with her. Varro goes so far as to say she is the same goddess as Victoria, while others equate her with Ceres, Diana, Nike, Minerva, Bellona or Venus.

Her role has been reconstructed from interpretation. Her name is linked to lack and privation, and in one of his epistles Horace seems to call upon her on behalf of an absent friend. From this it has been conjectured that she was prayed to for distant people, family members or friends. Pomponius Porphyrion, in his commentaries on Horace, calls her incerta specie, of an uncertain kind, which reflects the difficulty of pinning down her nature.

Ovid describes rites with sacred fires at which attendants stood or sat, and compares them with ancient ceremonies held in honour of Vesta. His account suggests no link to farm work or to absent loved ones.

Worship and religion

She was mainly worshipped at a sanctuary near Horace's villa, in what is now the commune of Licenza, in the sacred woods at Reate and at Rome. Renaissance authors and Leonhard Schmitz stated that country people offered her sacrifices when the labours of the field were over, when they were vacui, at leisure, but this reading comes from later authors. There is no data on whether her cult survived.

Ancient sources: Pomponius Porphyrion in his commentaries on Horace, Horace's epistles, Ovid's accounts of rites with sacred fires, and Varro, who identifies her with Victoria.

Trivia

A goddess of many names. Roman sources linked her with Ceres, Diana, Nike, Minerva, Bellona, Venus and Victoria, which makes it hard to isolate her own identity.
A deity of uncertain kind. The commentator Pomponius Porphyrion called her incerta specie, of an uncertain kind, since even he was unsure what sort of deity she was.
