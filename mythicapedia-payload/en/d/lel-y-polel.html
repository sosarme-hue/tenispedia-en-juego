divine twins of Polish Slavic mythology

Lel and Polel

Lel and Polel are divine twins from Polish Slavic tradition. They appear in the sources as brother deities, related to a mother goddess, and they matter mainly because of how they were linked to the Greek Dioscuri.

The myth

According to the account recorded by Maciej Miechowita in the 16th century, the twins are sons of the goddess Łada, whom the author equates with Leda, the mother of Castor and Pollux in Greek mythology. For this reason Miechowita presents Lel and Polel as Slavic equivalents of those twins.

Their first known mention comes late, centuries after the pagan Slavic period, so the details of their deeds and their full story are not clearly documented.

Worship and religion

Specialists do not agree on whether a real cult of Lel and Polel existed in early Poland, so their veneration remains an open question.

Ancient sources: Maciej Miechowita's 16th-century work is the earliest known mention of the twins.
