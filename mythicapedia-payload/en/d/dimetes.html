Greek hero, brother of Troezen

Dimoetes

Dimoetes is a hero of Greek mythology, brother of the eponym Troezen and therefore presumably a son of Pelops and Hippodamia. His story, dark and sensational in tone, is best known from the account preserved in the Sorrows of Love.

The myth

Dimoetes married his niece Evopis, the daughter of Troezen. She secretly carried on an affair with her own brother, and when Dimoetes discovered it he reported the matter to Troezen. Ashamed, Evopis cursed Dimoetes and hanged herself.

Not long afterwards, Dimoetes found the body of an extraordinarily beautiful woman washed up on the seashore and was overcome with an uncontrollable passion. He kept the corpse for some time, until it began to decay. He then gave her a proper burial in a magnificent tomb, but, unable to bear the loss of what he loved, he took his own life at her tomb.

Legacy

Some scholars suggest that the myth may have influenced the second part of Oscar Wilde's poem Charmides, in which a dryad finds the protagonist's body and falls in love with it, having never known him while he was alive.

Ancient sources: Parthenius of Nicaea's Sorrows of Love, which attributes the tale to the historian Phylarchus.

Trivia

Another name. In some manuscripts his name is spelled Thymoetes.
A sensational tale. Phylarchus was known for his taste for dramatic myths, and this story is often cited as an example.
