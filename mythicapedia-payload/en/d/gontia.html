Celtic tutelary goddess of the River Günz

Gontia

Gontia was a Celtic goddess who protected the River Günz, near present-day Günzburg in Germany. She is known mainly from a Roman-era altar dedicated to her, showing that her worship continued in a region under Roman rule.

The myth

Little survives about Gontia beyond her name, and no stories about her origins, family or deeds have been preserved. Her identity rests mainly on her role as guardian of a river that gave the surrounding land its character and life.

Worship and religion

The main evidence is a Roman-era altar found at Günzburg, dedicated to Gontia by Gaius Julius Faventianus, a centurion of the Legio I Italica. The inscription, which opens with the formula sacrum, marks a votive offering. It shows a Roman officer honouring a local deity, a common practice in the provinces.

Ancient sources: A Latin inscription on a Roman-era altar found at Günzburg. No ancient literary texts mention her.

Trivia

A Roman dedication. The altar was commissioned by a Roman centurion, not a local worshipper. It reveals the meeting of Roman tradition with the Celtic deities of the area.
A river goddess. Gontia is one of the few Celtic deities documented as the protector of a specific watercourse in what is now German territory.
