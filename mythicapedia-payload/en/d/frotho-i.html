legendary Danish king who slew a dragon

Frotho I

Frotho I, also known as Frode Haddingsson, is one of the legendary kings of Denmark. He is remembered mainly through Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum, which gives him a lengthy biography, and above all for slaying a dragon and winning its treasure.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Saxo, Frotho succeeded his father Hadingus on the throne. The royal treasury had been drained by war, so the king set out to find wealth. He was told of a dragon guarding a hoard on an island, and was advised to cover his body and shield with bull hide to block the poison and to strike at the beast's belly. When the dragon paused to drink water on its way back, Frotho slew it and took the treasure for himself.

With this gold he financed expeditions into the Baltic, where he won victories through clever stratagems; in one of them he disguised himself as one of his own shieldmaidens. After some trouble at home, he campaigned successfully in Britain and captured London. He died in a war against the king of Sweden.

Ancient sources: Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum, which devotes a substantial biography to him.

Trivia

Disguised as a shieldmaiden. During one of his Baltic campaigns, Frotho dressed as one of his own shieldmaidens to deceive the enemy.
Protection against poison. The bull hide that covered him against the dragon was the defence he was advised to use against its poison.
