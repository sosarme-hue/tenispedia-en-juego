Greek river god of the Nile

Nilus

Nilus is the river god who personifies the Nile in Greek mythology. According to Hesiod he was a son of Oceanus and Tethys, like the other river gods, though his role in the wider Greek myths was modest and his story is understood mainly in relation to Egypt.

The myth

The Hesiodic tradition makes him a son of Oceanus and Tethys, while Pindar gives him Cronus as his father. Aeschylus praised the river's great length with the phrase "the far-flowing Nile", and Homer names it after Egypt, speaking of the waters of Egypt that come from the gods.

Nilus had many nymph daughters. Memphis married Epaphus, king of Egypt, and the city of Memphis was founded in his name; from this union came Libya, who gave her name to the country. Among his other daughters were Anchiroe, wife of Belus; Chione, said to be the daughter of Callirhoe; Anippe, mother of Busiris; Eurrhoe, wife of Aegyptus, with whom she bore fifty sons; and Europa, wife of Danaus and mother of the Danaids. Tzetzes says the river had several names: first Oceanus, then Aegyptus, then Nilus and finally Triton. Cicero, the Latin author, claims that several Olympian gods were his children, presenting them as Roman versions of Egyptian deities.

Legacy

Hyginus suggested that the constellation Eridanus might represent Nilus. Outside the ancient sources, the god's presence in later culture is slight, and he remains mainly a witness to the Greeks' relationship with Egypt's river.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony), Homer, Aeschylus, Pindar, Hyginus, Tzetzes and Cicero.

Trivia

A river with several names. According to Tzetzes, the river was successively called Oceanus, Aegyptus, Nilus and Triton.
A large family. Eurrhoe, daughter of Nilus, bore her husband Aegyptus as many as fifty sons.
