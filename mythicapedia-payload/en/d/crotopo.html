king of Argos in Greek mythology

Crotopus

Crotopus is a king of Argos, son of Agenor and descendant of Phoroneus, whose story is tied to the birth of Apollo's son and the plague that struck his city. His tale belongs to the Argive myths about royal lineages and the rites honouring Linus.

The myth

According to myth, Crotopus sentenced his daughter Psamathe to death after she bore a son fathered by Apollo. She exposed the child out of fear of her father, but the shepherd's dogs tore him apart; the boy, raised as a shepherd and named Linus, was thus lost. Apollo punished Argos with a plague, and the oracle prescribed honouring mother and child with a festival at which women and girls sang dirges for Linus.

When the plague persisted, a second oracle ordered Crotopus to exile himself and found a city in the Megarid, named Tripodiscium. In other versions, such as those of Pausanias and Statius, the penance falls to an Argive hero named Coroebus, who slays the monster Poine.

Worship and religion

The veneration of Linus, the mother and the child was prescribed by an oracle and marked by an Argive festival called the Festival of Argis in the month of Arneios, when women sang dirges and dogs were ritually killed.

Legacy

A butterfly species, Euselasia crotopus, is possibly named after him.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Statius and Conon recount his story, and Eusebius of Caesarea gives his chronology of the Argive kings, in which he reigned twenty-one years.

Trivia

Tripodiscium. According to Conon, after his exile Crotopus founded a city in the Megarid called Tripodiscium as penance for the plague.
A butterfly with his name. The butterfly Euselasia crotopus may be named after him, though this is uncertain.
