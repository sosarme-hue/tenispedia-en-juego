divine cow of plenty, mother of cattle

Kāmadhenu (Surabhi)

Kāmadhenu, also called Surabhi, is the wish-granting divine cow of Hinduism and is regarded as the mother of all cattle. She has no independent cult, yet her image underpins the Hindu veneration of cows, which are seen as her earthly embodiments.

The myth

Accounts of her origin differ. Some texts say she rose from the churning of the cosmic ocean, among the treasures the gods and demons obtained while seeking amrita, the elixir of life. Others make her a daughter of the patriarch Daksha and wife of the sage Kashyapa, father of humankind, or place her in the possession of the sages Vasishtha or Jamadagni. After the churning, the seven sages received her, and she ended up under Vasishtha's care.

One of the best-known tales concerns her theft. When the eight Vasus visited Vasishtha's hermitage, one of their wives coveted the cow and asked her husband to steal her; he did so with the others' help. The sage cursed them to be born as humans, and the most prominent of them was Bhishma. In the Ramayana, King Vishvamitra covets the cow and tries to take her by force; defeated by the warriors she summons, he resolves to earn the rank of brahmarishi through his own merit. Another version says King Trishanku killed her to feed Vishvamitra's sons.

Worship and religion

Kāmadhenu is not worshipped on her own; she is honored through the Hindu veneration of cows, regarded as her earthly embodiments. She is a popular image in Indian art, usually shown as a white cow with a woman's head and breasts, bird wings and a peacock's tail. This veneration remains alive today.

Legacy

Her name gave the title to Surabhi, a hugely popular 1990s television programme on Doordarshan, India's state broadcaster. The Philadelphia Museum of Art notes that her form influenced the iconography of the Persian Buraq, a winged creature with a horse's body and a woman's face.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, Ramayana, Harivamsa, Matsya Purana and Satapatha Brahmana, among other Sanskrit texts, mention her with differing accounts of her origin.

Trivia

Fragrant name. Surabhi means 'the fragrant one'. Indologist Jacobi linked the name to the distinctive smell of cows.
Cow of the Vedas. In iconographic tradition, her four legs are the Vedas and her horns represent the Trimurti.
