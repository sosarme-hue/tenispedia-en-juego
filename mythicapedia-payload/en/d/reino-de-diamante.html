metaphysical space in Vajrayana Buddhism

Diamond Realm

The Diamond Realm (vajradhātu, Japanese kongōkai) is a metaphysical space in Vajrayana Buddhism inhabited by the Five Tathagatas. Together with the Womb Realm it forms the Mandala of the Two Realms.

The myth

In esoteric tradition, this realm embodies the indestructible, all-knowing wisdom of the cosmic Buddha Mahāvairocana, which pervades all phenomena. It is depicted as a mandala-palace where buddhas and bodhisattvas, identified by their mudrās and attributes, preside over enlightened worlds. Its scriptural basis is the Vajraśekhara Sūtra, compiled in India from the late 7th century.

Worship and religion

In Japanese Shingon and Tendai temples, the mandala hangs on the western wall of the hall. During initiation, the blindfolded novice tosses a flower onto it, and where it lands indicates the Buddhist figure to whom they will be dedicated. These rites are still practised.

Legacy

A very popular motif in mandala painting, in China as well as Japan.

Ancient sources: The Vajraśekhara Sūtra, an Indian Tantric Buddhist scripture that underlies the Diamond Realm.

Trivia

Two mandalas, one Chinese union. The two mandalas are believed to have evolved separately in India and were first joined in China, perhaps by the master Huiguo.
Mandalas on a grand scale. In Indonesia, the Buddhist complex of Borobudur is regarded as a giant mandala, with an entire temple serving as a sacred diagram.
