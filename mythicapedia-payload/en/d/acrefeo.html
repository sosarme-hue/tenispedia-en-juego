son of Apollo, founder of Acraephnium

Acraepheus

Acraepheus is a figure of Greek mythology, a son of Apollo credited with founding the Boeotian town of Acraephnium. He matters mainly because of the versions that link him to the family of Asopus and to his descendant Ptous.

The myth

According to ancient tradition, Acraepheus founded the town of Acraephnium, a Boeotian settlement on the lake Copais.

Another version makes him a son of Orion rather than of Apollo. In this account, he is the one who reveals to Asopus a prophecy: his daughters would be carried off by the gods and would give birth to a line of heroes.

A tradition also connects him with Euxippe, by whom he could have fathered Ptous, though the sources present this only as a possibility.

Worship and religion

In Acraephnium, worshippers attached the epithets Acraephius or Acraephiaeus to Apollo, names drawn from the town. The sources do not say whether this cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Esteban de Bizancio records the foundation of Acraephium, and the prophecy to Asopus appears in other ancient versions of the story.

Trivia

Two possible fathers. According to one version Acraepheus is a son of Apollo; according to another he is a son of Orion. The ancient sources do not agree.
Apollo with a local name. In Acraephnium, Apollo received epithets taken from the town, an example of how gods were adapted to each place.
