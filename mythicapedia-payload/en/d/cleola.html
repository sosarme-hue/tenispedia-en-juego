Cleola

Cleola is a figure in Greek mythology, daughter of Dias and granddaughter of the hero Pelops. Her place in the Atreid family tree changes from one author to another, which makes her a clear example of how Greek myths allowed competing versions.

The myth

According to tradition, Cleola was the daughter of Dias and therefore the granddaughter of Pelops. Her mother's name is not known. The sources do not agree on her marriage. The Byzantine scholar John Tzetzes, citing Hesiod, Aeschylus and others, makes her the wife of her first cousin Pleisthenes, son of Atreus, and the mother of Agamemnon, Menelaus and Anaxibia.

The Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, instead makes her the wife of her uncle Atreus and the mother of Pleisthenes. A papyrus from Oxyrhynchus says that Pleisthenes married Aerope rather than Cleola, which supports this reading, since it leaves Cleola as the wife of Atreus. Homer's version, by contrast, names Atreus and Aerope as the parents of the three siblings.

Ancient sources: Cited by the scholar John Tzetzes, drawing on Hesiod and Aeschylus, and by the scholia to Euripides' Orestes; the Catalogue of Women, attributed to Hesiod, also mentions her.

Trivia

A tangled marriage. In some versions Cleola was married to her cousin Pleisthenes; in others, to her uncle Atreus. Both options place her within the same family of the Atreidai.
A papyrus to settle it. A papyrus found at Oxyrhynchus, in Egypt, helps scholars favour the version in which Cleola is the wife of Atreus.
