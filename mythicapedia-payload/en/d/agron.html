first Heraclid king of Lydia

Agron

Agron is, according to Herodotus, the first king of the Heraclid dynasty that ruled Lydia in western Anatolia. His figure marks the start of a line that claimed descent from Heracles.

The myth

According to Herodotus, before Agron the ruling family of Lydia was the Maeonian line of Lydus, from whom the country took its name. Agron started the Heraclid line, which, by the historian's reckoning, reigned continuously for 22 generations and 505 years. The dynasty ended with Candaules, whose death is dated to around 687 BC. From that date, Herodotus' computation points to an accession for Agron around 1192 BC, though this chronology is legendary rather than historical. In some accounts, Agron is the son of Ninus, the mythical founder of Nineveh.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, in Book I of his Histories, is the main ancient source that names him as the first Heraclid king of Lydia.

Trivia

A very long dynasty. Herodotus calculates that the Heraclid dynasty ruled Lydia for 22 generations, about 505 years, until Candaules.
