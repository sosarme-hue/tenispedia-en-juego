wives of the Kaurava prince in the Mahabharata

Wives of Duryodhana

The wives of Duryodhana, the principal Kaurava prince in the Mahabharata, are a group of women the epic barely describes. Their stories were expanded in later works, which gave them names and roles of their own.

The myth

The Mahabharata hardly names them. The mother of his son Lakshmana is the most prominent: during the Ghosha Yatra episode, Duryodhana sets out with his wives to inspect cattle near Dwaitavana, intending to mock the exiled Pandavas, but Gandharvas capture them and Arjuna frees them. Mortally wounded at Kurukshetra, Duryodhana laments that his wife will be left without a son or husband, and in the Stri Parva Gandhari describes her daughter-in-law in detail to Krishna.

According to the Shanti Parva, Duryodhana abducted a princess of Kalinga, daughter of King Chitrangada, from her svayamvara in Rajapura. She had passed him by, and the offended Duryodhana carried her off with Karna's help, Karna defeating the kings who pursued them single-handedly. The Bhishma Parva and the Kumbakonam edition also mention a princess of Kashi, daughter of King Kashiraja, who welcomed Draupadi on her arrival at Hastinapura and whom the text calls mahishi, or chief queen.

Legacy

Playwrights expanded their stories. Bhasa, in Urubhanga, gave him two wives, Malavi and Pauravi, and a son, Durjaya. Around the 11th century, Bhatta Narayana, in the play Venisamhara, presented Bhanumati as his only wife, a figure now very popular in traditional retellings although she does not appear in the older Sanskrit versions.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, in its Adi, Shanti, Bhishma, Shalya and Stri Parvas, and in the Kumbakonam edition.

Trivia

Bhanumati. Although she is one of the best-known wives of Duryodhana in popular tradition, she does not appear in any accessible Sanskrit version of the Mahabharata, where none of his wives is named.
Grief on the battlefield. In Urubhanga, Duryodhana feels more pain at seeing his wives dishevelled and unveiled than at the wound dealt by Bhima's mace.
