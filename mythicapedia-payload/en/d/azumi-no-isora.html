Shinto kami of the seashore

Azumi-no-isora

Azumi-no-isora is a Shinto kami of the seashore, regarded as the ancestor of the Azumi people. He is linked to seafaring and to a legend about the tide jewels within the Hachiman tradition.

The myth

In a late legend, Empress Jingū borrowed the tide jewels from the Dragon King (Ryūjin). To reach him, the envoy Takenouchi no Sukune relied on Azumi-no-isora, a lesser sea deity who served as his navigator and guided him to the dragon's realm.

The story is attested in various foundation myth documents of the Hachiman cult, where it forms part of that tradition's origins.

Worship and religion

He is worshiped at several shrines, including ones in Kitakyushu, Shikaumi Shrine on Shika Island, and a shrine on Tsushima. The sources do not describe rites or festivals.

Ancient sources: Foundation myth documents of the Hachiman cult, which record the legend of the tide jewels.

Trivia

A minor god. In the story he is a sea deity of secondary rank, hired as a navigator for a mission that is not part of his main story.
Tied to Hachiman. His story appears in foundation texts of the Hachiman cult, which place it among that tradition's origins.
