Slavic goddess of wild nature and hunting

Devana

Devana is a goddess of the Western Slavs, linked to wild nature, forests, hunting and the moon. She first appears in the 15th century, when Jan Długosz compared her to the Roman Diana; after harsh criticism her authenticity was for a time rejected, but a growing number of researchers now accept her as a genuine part of Slavic tradition.

The myth

Stories about Devana are scarce, and much of what is known comes from analysis of her name. She is associated with forests and hunting, and in some folk rituals she appears alongside Morana. Her name also appears as Zevana and, less often, Zievonia.

The origin of the name is debated. The most obvious explanation links it to dziewa, "girl" or "maiden", and dziewica, "virgin". Another proposal connects it with the old Polish dziwy, "wild". Some scholars have also suggested a link with the Proto-Indo-European sky god Dyēus, through a root meaning "to shine".

Worship and religion

In some folk rituals she appears together with Morana. The sources do not describe temples, festivals or offerings dedicated to Devana.

Legacy

Her name survives in Polish: dziewanna is the Polish name for mullein, a plant of the genus Verbascum whose etymology remains unclear. The plant was sometimes used to lighten hair, and some linguists connect its name with the goddess's.

Ancient sources: The earliest known mention is by Jan Długosz in the 15th century; the sources do not cite older texts from her own tradition that name her.

Trivia

A name with several meanings. The word dziewanna also names a plant, which has led scholars to debate whether the goddess gave her name to the plant or the reverse.
Goddesses and demons. In Slavic folklore, the divas and samodivas are female beings, sometimes demonic, whose names are linked to the root of the word for a sky god.
