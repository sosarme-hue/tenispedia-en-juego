Hindu goddess of nourishment

Shakambhari

Shakambhari is the Hindu goddess of nourishment, regarded as a form of Mahadevi and identified with Durga. Her name means "she who bears vegetables," and her story tells how she rescued humans and gods from a long famine.

The myth

According to tradition, the asura Durgamasura caused a drought that left the earth in scarcity for a hundred years. He did this by making the sages forget the Vedas, and only when they finally remembered the goddess Parvati and chanted hymns to her did she appear before them. She came with dark blue skin, four arms and a hundred eyes, carrying a lotus, arrows, a great bow, and vegetables, fruits, flowers and roots.

Moved by the people's suffering, the Devi Bhagavata Purana says she wept incessantly, her tears flowing into rivers, and offered medicines. The Uma Samhita of the Shiva Purana identifies her with Devi Satakshi and Shakambari, manifestations of Parvati, while the Skanda Purana and Devi Bhagavatam tell that Parvati slew the demon Durgamasura.

Worship and religion

Several temples are dedicated to Shakambhari. One is in Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh, another major one lies near Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan, and a third is in Badami.

Ancient sources: Accounts of her origin appear in the Devi Bhagavata, Skanda and Shiva Puranas (in the Uma Samhita of the latter).

Trivia

A telling name. Her name comes from śāka, "vegetable," and bharī, "bearer," so she is understood as the bringer of food.
Tears that become rivers. According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, her tears of sorrow for human suffering turned into rivers that brought relief to the land.
