Hindu goddess, mother of the war god Kartikeya

Skandamata

Skandamata is a form of the Hindu goddess Mahadevi, revered as the mother of the war god Kartikeya, also called Skanda. She is the fifth of the Navadurga and is honoured on the fifth day of Navaratri.

The myth

According to the Shiva Purana, after their wedding Shiva and Parvati enjoyed a thousand years of married bliss. Meanwhile the asura Taraka spread destruction, and the gods, led by Vishnu and Brahma, travelled to Kailasa to seek help. Shiva, eager to receive them, let his semen fall to the ground. Urged by the gods, Agni took the form of a dove to swallow it and prevent its loss, but he felt an unbearable burning. Following Shiva's instructions, he placed the seed in the morning baths of six of the seven sages' wives. They became pregnant, could not bear the effects, and expelled the embryo, which the Ganga carried to a reed bed, where a child of divine radiance appeared.

Parvati, angered that her privacy had been disturbed and Shiva's vīrya was being wasted, cursed the wives of the devas who had accompanied their husbands to be barren. Kartikeya's birth brought great joy to Parvati and Shiva. She nursed him, and this nurturing, maternal aspect of hers came to be called Skandamata. She gave him the divine weapon Shakti, with which he would fulfil his destiny of slaying Taraka and restoring cosmic order.

Worship and religion

Her veneration is part of Navaratri, the nine-night festival dedicated to the goddess's forms, and the fifth night is consecrated to her. She is shown with four arms, three eyes and a lion as her mount, one hand raised in a fear-dispelling gesture and another holding her son on her lap. She is often pictured seated on a lotus, which gives her the epithet Padamasani.

Ancient sources: The Shiva Purana narrates her origin and the birth of Kartikeya.
