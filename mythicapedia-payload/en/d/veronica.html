Bloody Mary

Bloody Mary, known in Spain as Verónica, is a ghost of urban legend who appears when her name is chanted before a mirror. She is central to games and divination rituals, especially among young people, and her story has spread widely across the English-speaking world.

The myth

The legend has no single ancient source; it survives in many versions that changed over time and place. It draws on the widespread belief that the souls of the dead can appear in mirrors. In the ritual, the name is repeated three times before a mirror, often in candlelight, and the apparition may kill the summoner with a sharp object nearby, such as scissors or a knife.

In the best-known American version, Mary was a vain fifteen-year-old whose long hair was cut by a masked intruder; unable to bear her disfigurement, she took her own life. Other tellings describe a gravely ill girl buried at home, whose fingernails were found torn off inside her coffin. In Spain, the figure is usually a girl who died during puberty, sometimes during a ouija session, and who is named differently from place to place.

Legacy

The character inspired Clive Barker's story The Forbidden, adapted to film as Candyman. Some have linked her origin to Saint Veronica, others to Mary I of England, nicknamed Bloody Mary by her Protestant opponents, though no firm identification exists.

Trivia

Hanako-san. In Japan, the legend of Hanako-san, a ghost haunting school toilets, closely parallels the Bloody Mary mythology.
Forty-seven times. Some modern versions require chanting her name into the bathroom mirror 47 times before she appears.
