bogeyman of children's folklore

The Sack Man

The Sack Man is a figure of children's folklore found in many countries: a wanderer who, after dark, carries away in a sack the children he finds alone or misbehaving. Its purpose was to frighten children into getting home on time and behaving.

The myth

In the recorded versions, the Sack Man has no fixed origin and is not based on any particular event, although over time some real child-kidnapping criminals have been linked to him. He is usually described as a thin, old man, or as an imposing adult who roams the streets at nightfall.

Depending on the tradition, he takes children to eat them or to sell them. The details change from place to place, but the central idea stays the same: the danger of being out after supper time.

Legacy

A children's game of the same name has existed since the 17th or 18th century. In Spain, the 1911 crime of Gádor gave rise to the term sacamantecas, because the kidnappers used a sack to carry a child. In contrast, in several countries he coexists with benevolent gift-bringers such as Father Christmas or the helpers of Saint Nicholas.

Trivia

A helper with a past. In some stories from the Netherlands and Flanders, the Zwarte Piet themselves were kidnapped as children, and those kidnapped children become the next generation of helpers.
A giant in Haiti. In Haiti, a giant counterpart of Father Christmas carries off naughty children in his knapsack.
