commander of the army of the Hindu god Vishnu

Vishvaksena

Vishvaksena is the commander-in-chief of the army of the Hindu god Vishnu and the guardian of his celestial realm, Vaikuntha. He matters because several Vaishnava traditions worship him before any ritual or festival begins.

The myth

According to the stories, Vishvaksena guards the gate of Vaikuntha, and the Kurma Purana says he was born from a portion of Vishnu. One legend tells how he failed to recognise Shiva, disguised as a mendicant, and refused him entry. Bhairava sent his attendant Kalavega, whom Vishvaksena defeated, but when he charged at Bhairava he was slain and impaled on the trident. This form of Bhairava is known as Kankala, 'the one with the skeleton'.

Worship and religion

He is central to the Vaikhanasa and Sri Vaishnava traditions, where rituals and festivals begin with his worship, as he is believed to remove obstacles, a role parallel to that of Ganesha. At the Tirumala temple, leftover offerings from the main deity are presented to him, and his procession opens the Brahmotsavam. His worship remains alive today.

Legacy

He is regarded as a symbol of the Agama scriptures, and the saint Nammalvar is seen as an incarnation of his. A taniyan places him in the teacher lineage running from Vishnu to Ramanuja.

Ancient sources: The Kurma Purana, Kalika Purana, Lakshmi Tantra and Garga Samhita describe him and recount his deeds.

Trivia

Guardian of the north. According to the Garga Samhita, he guards the northern gate of Vaikuntha day and night.
A parallel with Ganesha. Ramanuja noted that Sri Vaishnavas invoke Vishvaksena where other traditions invoke Kartikeya or Ganesha.
