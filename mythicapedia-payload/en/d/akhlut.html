composite creature of Inuit mythology

Akhlut

The akhlut is a composite creature from Inuit folklore, resembling an orca, that takes the form of a wolf on land. It matters as an example of the hybrid beings that populate the folklore of the Arctic peoples.

The myth

According to Inuit stories, the akhlut is a composite animal that lives in the sea in the shape of an orca but becomes a wolf when it comes ashore. Some accounts describe it as a wolf-orca hybrid, halfway between two worlds.

The American naturalist Edward William Nelson recorded it in 1900, alongside other mythical and composite animals of the same culture. He linked the stories of the akhlut to the orca and explained wolf tracks that seemed to lead into the sea as the result of ice breaking away from the shore. Nelson also noted a white whale that could turn into a reindeer. In his account, belief in the creature was widespread among Inuit along the coast of the Bering Sea. More recent collections of myths have adopted the name Nelson gave to the orca to describe this composite being.

Trivia

A whale that turns into a reindeer. Nelson also described a white whale able to transform into a reindeer, another composite being from the same tradition.
