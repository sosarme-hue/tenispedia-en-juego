wolf of Norse mythology who chases the Moon

Hati Hróðvitnisson

Hati is a wolf in Norse mythology who, according to the sources, chases Máni, the Moon, across the night sky. His name means "he who hates", and as a celestial hunter he helps explain lunar eclipses in Norse storytelling.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Hati is a son of Fenrir, a point supported by his patronymic Hróðvitnisson, which also appears in the Grímnismál. His mother is a giantess who lives in the Ironwood, Járnviðr, east of Midgard, and raises many giant sons in the shape of wolves. Among them is Sköll, who chases the Sun by day, so the two brothers form a pair of celestial hunters.

The Völuspá, which Snorri quotes, prophesies that one of this giantess's sons will snatch the Moon and feed on the flesh of the dead, spattering the heavens with blood. Snorri also names Mánagarmr, "Moon-Hound", as the most powerful of her offspring, who will swallow the Moon and gorge on corpses. It is probably another name for Hati or Sköll, taken from folklore, though the texts do not fully settle the matter.

The Vafþrúðnismál, by contrast, says that Fenrir himself will destroy the Sun, so the sources disagree over which wolf devours the heavenly bodies. In every account, the wolves' work culminates at Ragnarök, the end of the world.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, the Grímnismál, the Völuspá and the Vafþrúðnismál (Poetic Edda).

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Hati comes from Old Norse and means "he who hates" or "enemy".
Another name for Fenrir. Fenrir also appears as Hróðvitnir, which can be translated as "famous wolf".
