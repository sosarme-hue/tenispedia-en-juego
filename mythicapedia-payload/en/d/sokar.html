hawk god of the Memphite necropolis and of the realm of the dead

Sokar

Sokar is a hawk-headed god linked to darkness, to the Duat (the Egyptian underworld) and to the protection of the dead. He matters because his figure merged with those of Ptah and Osiris into a widely venerated funerary triad.

The myth

According to Egyptian texts, Sokar guarded the entrance to the Duat, the underworld realm through which the sun god travelled at night. He was imagined dwelling in a secret cavern called Imhet, a difficult, sandy terrain, and he was said to nourish the hearts of the dead and guide them through the regions of the afterlife.

He possessed a boat called Henu, which carried his mummiform image and, in some accounts, the pharaoh and the Sun as well. On reaching the subterranean desert, the solar barque had to turn into a serpent to move across the sand. Sokar ruled this stretch, known by the epithet 'He who is on his sand'.

In The Journey of Ra he governs the Fifth Kingdom of Night, called 'Hidden', and punishes the souls of evildoers by casting them into a boiling lake. His origins are older still: in early times he was worshipped as an earth and fertility god, and the Book of the Dead credits him with fashioning silver bowls.

Worship and religion

His worship is attested from the First Dynasty, and his cult centre was at Memphis, in the necropolis of Saqqara. As patron of the necropolises he was honoured in royal and popular tombs. His main festival was held on the twenty-sixth day of the fourth month of the akhet season. During it, devotees hoed and tilled the ground and drove cattle, and they wore strings of onions, linked to embalming and to his underworld aspect. A similar festival was held at Thebes, where it rivalled the great Opet Festival, and a statue of the god was floated on a Henu barque.

Legacy

Sokar appears in the film The Ten Commandments (1956), where the pharaoh invokes him to bring his firstborn son back to life. In the series Stargate SG-1, the Goa'uld villain Sokar takes his name. The death metal band Nile dedicated the title track of their album Annihilation of the Wicked to his domain and appearance, and he features in the role-playing game Advanced Dungeons & Dragons as a lesser god of light.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, the Book of the Dead, the Amduat and The Journey of Ra mention him.

Trivia

Onions in worship. Sokar's followers wore strings of onions during his festivals, a gesture tied to his role in the underworld, since onions were used in embalming.
A science fiction villain. The Goa'uld villain of the series Stargate SG-1 borrows the name of the Egyptian god, although the character is very different from the original deity.
