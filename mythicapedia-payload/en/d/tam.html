hell in Tengrism and Turkic mythology

Tam

Tam (also Tamag) is the hell of Tengrism and Turkic mythology, where, according to belief, criminals go to be punished after death. Fire is the feature that recurs most often in its descriptions.

The myth

According to Turkic traditions, Tam lies underground and is ruled by Erlik Khan, the deity who punishes the dead. Accounts differ from people to people: among the Khakas, the underworld is governed by Tami Han. The ancient Turks believed that once their sentence was served, souls were brought up to the third floor of the sky.

In Tuvan belief, the earth has three lower levels and beneath them lie eighteen layers of hellfire, called tamas. Tam stands opposite Uçmag, the heaven that is the opposite destination after death.

Trivia

A name with variants. The underworld is written in several forms, such as Tam, tamuk, tamu or tamag, depending on the language and the people describing it.
Hell against heaven. Tam is conceived as the exact opposite of Uçmag, the heaven, so the two places mark the final destiny of souls.
