form of Vishnu worshipped in South India

Ranganatha

Ranganatha is a form of the Hindu god Vishnu, worshipped as the chief deity of the Ranganathaswamy Temple at Srirangam in South India. He is especially important to the Sri Vaishnava tradition, which associates him with the goddess Lakshmi in her form as Ranganayaki.

The myth

The name combines the Sanskrit ranga, meaning "assembly, stage or arena," and nātha, "lord or protector," so it is often rendered as "Lord of the Stage." In Sri Vaishnava thought, ranga symbolises the universe (samsāra) as a cosmic stage on which the divine play (lilā) unfolds.

His most characteristic image shows him reclining and smiling on the serpent Ādishésha in the Ocean of Milk during pralaya, the period of cosmic dissolution. This posture is understood to show that he is accessible and ready to hear the prayers and hardships of his devotees.

Worship and religion

Five temples along the Kaveri River, known as the Pancharanga Kshetrams, are dedicated to this form of Vishnu. The three most important stand on river islands: Adi Ranga at Srirangapatna, Madhya Ranga at Shivanasamudram and Antya Ranga at Srirangam, the last considered the primary shrine among the Divya Desams. Devotion remains alive in South India.

Ancient sources: The Pancharatra Agamas and Puranas, such as the Brahmanda Purana, elaborate on the name and his reclining iconography.
