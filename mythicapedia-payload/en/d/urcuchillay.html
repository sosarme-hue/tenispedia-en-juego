Incan god of livestock and animals

Urcuchillay

Urcuchillay is the Incan god of livestock and animals, especially the llama and the alpaca. The Incas linked him to the constellation Lyra, which they saw as a cosmic llama.

The myth

According to Andean tradition, Urcuchillay took the form of a multicolored male llama who watched over the herds. Sources describe him as the incarnation of the constellation, since in the Andean world the same name designated both the deity and the star.

His role was to protect and multiply the animals on which people depended.

Worship and religion

He was revered above all by herders, who sought his favor to protect their animals and help them thrive.

Legacy

In 2026 the International Astronomical Union adopted the name Urquchillay for the star Zeta2 Lyrae, in the constellation Lyra.

Trivia

Multicolored llama. Tradition imagined him as a multicolored male llama, a very different image from that of a human-like god.
A star with his name. In 2026 the International Astronomical Union gave his name to a star in the constellation Lyra.
