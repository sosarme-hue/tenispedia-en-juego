god and legendary king of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology

Nuada

Nuada, also called Nuada Airgetlám (“silver hand”), was the first king of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology and is thought to have originally been a god. He matters for the tale of losing his arm, which cost him the throne, and for his links to British deities such as Nodens.

The myth

According to tradition, the Tuatha Dé Danann ruled before they came to Ireland. Nuada asked the Fir Bolg, the island's inhabitants, for half of the land, and when their king refused, war broke out. During the first battle of Mag Tuired, Nuada lost an arm to the champion Sreng. Because their king had to be physically perfect, the people replaced him with Bres, a half-Fomorian whose tyrannical rule bred discontent. Nuada regained the throne with a silver arm made by Dian Cécht and Creidhne, and later with one of flesh and blood made by Miach, Dian's son.

In the second battle of Mag Tuired, Bres allied himself with the Fomorian Balor. Nuada, who had handed command to the young Lugh, was killed by Balor, but Lugh later defeated Balor and led the Tuatha Dé to victory. The king's sword was one of the Four Treasures of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Maynooth in County Kildare, whose Irish name, Maigh Nuad, means “the plain of Nuada”. Tolkien linked his silver hand to that of Nodens, “the Catcher”, and proposed a connection between the name Nuada and a Germanic root meaning “acquire” or “catch”.

Ancient sources: Medieval Irish texts such as the Lebor Gabála Érenn (Book of Invasions), the Annals of Inisfallen and The Fate of the Children of Tuireann.

Trivia

A town named after him. The Irish town of Maynooth takes its name from Nuada. In Irish it is called Maigh Nuad, the plain of Nuada.
A silver arm. His artificial silver arm is one of the features that most distinguishes him in Irish tales of the Tuatha Dé Danann.
