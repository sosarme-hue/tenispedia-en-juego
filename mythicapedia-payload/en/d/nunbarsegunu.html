Mesopotamian mother goddess of barley

Nunbarsegunu

Nunbarsegunu is a little-known mother goddess of Mesopotamian mythology, associated with barley. Some texts call her 'the old woman of Nippur' and present her as a figure of note in creation accounts.

The myth

According to some texts, Nunbarsegunu appears in creation accounts from the Sumerian, Babylonian and Akkadian traditions. She is regarded as the mother of Ninlil, the goddess of the air, and some sources also name her as the consort of Haia. Her name varies between texts, which also give it as Nidaba, Nisaba or Nanibgal.

Her best-known role appears in the myth of Enlil and Ninlil. When her young daughter plans to stroll by the river, her mother advises her not to go alone, since Enlil, who sees everything, would likely notice her and want to court her. Some scholars also suggest that Nunbarsegunu may be another name for Nisaba, goddess of writing.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Sumerian poem Enlil and Ninlil and in Mesopotamian creation texts.

Trivia

A goddess of many names. The texts call her Nidaba, Nisaba or Nanibgal. It has been suggested that Nunbarsegunu was another name for Nisaba, goddess of writing.
