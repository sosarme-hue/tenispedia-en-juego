final episode of Homer's Odyssey

Slaughter of the Suitors

The slaughter of the suitors is the climax of the Odyssey, the epic poem traditionally attributed to Homer. When Odysseus returns to Ithaca disguised as a beggar, he kills, with the help of his son Telemachus, the young men who had spent years courting his wife Penelope.

The myth

In Homer's account, Odysseus left for the Trojan War, leaving his wife Penelope and a newborn son, Telemachus, on Ithaca. After the war his homecoming took ten years, and in his absence many young men from the island and elsewhere moved into his house hoping to marry Penelope, drinking his wine and consuming his stores.

Penelope bought time by promising to choose a husband once she had finished a funeral shroud for Laertes, Odysseus' father. She wove by day and unravelled her work by night for three years, until a maidservant, Melantho, revealed the trick to her lover Eurymachus. The suitors then demanded an immediate decision.

When Odysseus reaches his home, Athena helps him keep his identity hidden. The suitors mistreat him, and Antinous, the most arrogant, throws a stool at him. Finally Odysseus reveals himself, strings the bow no one else could bend, and shoots the suitors one by one, aided by Telemachus, the loyal herdsmen Eumaeus and Philoetius, and other faithful servants. Disloyal maidservants are punished too. Amphinomus, the most sympathetic, dies despite trying to dissuade the others, and Leodes, the priest, is executed although he begs for mercy.

Ancient sources: Homer, Odyssey, Book XXII, which narrates the slaughter and lists the suitors by their island of origin.
