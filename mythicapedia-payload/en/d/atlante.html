first king of Atlantis and father of the Hesperides

Atlas

Atlas is the first king of Atlantis according to Plato's Critias, and a figure of Greek mythology, son of Poseidon and the mortal Cleito. His name gave rise to the name of the island and of the Atlantic Ocean.

The myth

In Plato's account, Poseidon and Cleito had five pairs of twins, and Atlas, the eldest, received the finest part of the island as his kingdom. Diodorus Siculus, by contrast, describes him as a mythical king of Mauritania, a wise astrologer who supposedly discovered that the stars are spherical and built the first celestial globe, which gave rise to the image of a man holding up the sky.

In later tradition he married Hesperis and had the six Hesperides. When King Busiris sent pirates to abduct them, Heracles defeated the kidnappers and returned the girls to their father, who in turn rewarded him with the golden sheep that Eurystheus had asked for.

Legacy

His name lives on in the island of Atlantis and in the Atlantic Ocean, which take their name from him.

Ancient sources: Plato, in the Critias, and Diodorus Siculus, in his Bibliotheca historica.

Trivia

Sheep or apples. In Greek, the word mēlo can mean either «sheep» or «apple», so the golden sheep have also been read as golden apples.
The Atlantides. The six daughters of Atlas were also called the Atlantides, a name that recalls their father.
