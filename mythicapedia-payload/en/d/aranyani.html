Hindu goddess of the forests

Aranyani

Aranyani is the goddess of forests and of the wild animals that dwell within them, according to Hindu tradition. She is one of the few Vedic deities devoted above all to the wilderness, and her figure lives on in later forest deities.

The myth

The most descriptive hymn in the Rigveda is dedicated to her: the Aranyani Suktam, hymn 146 of the tenth mandala. In it, Aranyani appears as an elusive presence who loves quiet clearings in the thicket and fears no remote places. The worshipper asks her how she can wander so far from the edge of civilisation without feeling afraid or lonely.

She is seldom seen but often heard, her anklets with bells betraying her steps among the trees. She is also described as a dancer. What the worshipper finds most wondrous is that she can feed both people and animals although she tills no land.

Worship and religion

Her worship has declined in modern Hinduism, and temples dedicated to her are rare. One exists in Arrah, Bihar, known as the Aranya Devi Temple. She is sometimes regarded as the owner of the Kalpavriksha, the divine tree.

Ancient sources: The Aranyani Suktam of the Rigveda, repeated in the Taittiriya Brahmana, where its commentator interprets it.

Trivia

A whole hymn. Aranyani has one of the most descriptive hymns in the Rigveda dedicated to a single deity.
Ringing anklets. She is recognised by the tinkling bells of her anklets, though she is seldom seen.
