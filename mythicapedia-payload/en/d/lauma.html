fairy and birth goddess of Baltic mythology

Lauma

The Latvian lauma or Lithuanian laumė is a woodland spirit of Eastern Baltic mythology, guardian of orphans and linked to childbirth and the fate of newborns. Depending on the tradition, she appears as a sky goddess, a fairy or a dangerous being.

The myth

According to tradition, the lauma was originally a sky spirit who, moved by compassion for human suffering, came down to earth to share our fate. In Lithuania she is considered one of the oldest goddesses, appearing as a mare, a female goat, a bear or a dog, and later in human form with birds' claws and a single eye. The laumės were dangerous, killing people with tickles or pinches and devouring their bodies, much like the Greek lamia, yet they also helped hard workers and punished the lazy.

Lithuanian accounts of her origins vary. In some she is the bride of the thunder god Perkūnas but falls in love with the Moon; in others the devil Velnias steals her. In another version Perkūnas's bride is called Vaiva, and the rainbow is her ribbon, stolen by the singer Straublys. In Latvian mythology the lauma assists at births and cares for mother and child; if the mother dies, she becomes the child's spiritual foster mother and spins the thread of life.

Legacy

The nickname 'laumė's nipples' is given to belemnite fossils found on the ground, and many Lithuanian lakes bear names related to the word laumė.
