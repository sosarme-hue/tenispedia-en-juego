Irish god of Celtic myth, son of the Sun

Mac Gréine

Mac Gréine, meaning «Son of the Sun» in Irish, was one of the three High Kings of Ireland in Celtic mythology, belonging to the Tuatha Dé Danann.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Mac Gréine, whose given name was Céthur, was a son of Cermait and a grandson of the Dagda. He was married to Ériu. Together with his brothers Mac Cuill and Mac Cecht, he killed Lug to avenge their father. The three brothers ruled Ireland as joint High Kings, passing the sovereignty between them one year at a time over some twenty-nine or thirty years, depending on the source. They were the last kings of the Tuatha Dé Danann before the arrival of the Milesians.

Later, Mac Gréine and his brothers treacherously killed Íth, which led his nephew Míl Espáine and Míl's sons to invade Ireland to avenge him. In the battle against the Milesians, Mac Gréine was slain by Amergin Glúingel.

Trivia

A shared reign. The three brothers took turns holding power, each ruling for one year, in a cycle lasting between twenty-nine and thirty years depending on the source.
A name that speaks of the sun. Mac Gréine means «Son of the Sun» in Irish, although his given name was Céthur.
