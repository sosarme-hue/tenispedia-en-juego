mythical creature of Persian and Indian folklore

Karkadann

The karkadann is a legendary animal said to have roamed the grassy plains of India and Iran. It began as a description of the real Indian rhinoceros and later merged with the unicorn, which explains its place in Arabic and Persian imagination.

The myth

The first descriptions come from the Persian scholar Al-Biruni (973-1048), who described an animal with a buffalo-like build, black scaly skin, low-set eyes and a single horn curving upward from its nose. This image clearly draws on the Indian rhinoceros, and the same word in Persian and Arabic names both animals.

Over the centuries, Persian writers embellished the creature, partly because they had no firsthand knowledge of it. The horn grew long and straight and artists moved it to the forehead. In the late thirteenth century Al-Qazwini linked it with poison and healing, while in the fourteenth century Ibn Battuta used the name karkadann for a real rhinoceros he saw in India. Like the unicorn, the karkadann is said to be tamed by virgins and to be fierce toward other animals.

Legacy

The karkadann appears in One Thousand and One Nights, in the Second Voyage of Sinbad the Sailor. In Iraq a tradition of the tears of the karkadann survives, reddish beads used in Muslim prayer beads. The Syrian poet Tawfiq Sayigh wrote a long poem about it, and Peter Beagle included it in a story.

Ancient sources: The writings of Al-Biruni, Al-Qazwini and Ibn Battuta, along with medieval European bestiaries that record the name.

Trivia

Tears of the karkadann. In Iraq it was believed that the animal wept on finding water in the desert, and that its tears turned into reddish beads.
