autochthonous giant of Greek myth

Anax

Anax is an autochthonous giant in Greek mythology, son of Gaia and father of Asterius. His story is tied to Miletus and the legendary origins of the city.

The myth

According to the Milesians, their land was first called Anactoria for two generations, under the rule of Anax and his son Asterius, who succeeded him on the throne. Anax is presented as an autochthon, born from the land itself.

When Miletus arrived with a Cretan army, fleeing King Minos, he seized the territory and named it after himself, and the city took the same name. Pausanias reports that the body of Asterius, buried on a islet off Miletus, measured no less than ten cubits.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions him twice in his work, in passages about the island of Lade and the earliest history of Miletus.

Trivia

A body ten cubits long. Pausanias claims that the body of Asterius, buried on an islet near Miletus, measured no less than ten cubits.
A name meaning chief. The name Anax is interpreted as tribal chief, lord or military leader, a meaning that reflects his role as king.
