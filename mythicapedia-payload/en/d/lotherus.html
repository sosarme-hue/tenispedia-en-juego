legendary king of Denmark

Lotherus

Lotherus is a legendary king of Denmark, son of Danus and brother of Humblus, recorded by Saxo Grammaticus in his Gesta Danorum. He is remembered as the archetype of the tyrant who seizes power by force.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved by Saxo Grammaticus, the early Danes chose their kings by swearing oaths over stones set into the ground. On one occasion Humblus was chosen, but his brother Lotherus, driven by ambition, went to war to seize power.

Lotherus defeated his brother but spared his life on one condition: Humblus had to sell him the crown. As king, Lotherus ruled with arrogance and cruelty, filling his realm with violence and injustice, until his people rose in arms and overthrew him. The story adds that even his downfall brought him a grim satisfaction, as if it were a blessing. He is presented as a descendant of Danus, patriarch of all the Danes.

Ancient sources: Gesta Danorum by Saxo Grammaticus, which preserves the legendary traditions of the earliest Danish kings.

Trivia

A name with ancient echoes. The name Lotherus may share its origin with Hlǫðr in the Hervarar saga and the Hlöðskviða, or with Hliþe in the Old English poem Widsith.
