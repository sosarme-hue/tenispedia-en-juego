Egyptian god of war and hunting

Onuris

Onuris was an Egyptian god of war and hunting, worshipped mainly at Abydos and Thinis. Protector of the army and the king, he is the Greek name of the Egyptian deity Inheret, meaning “the one who brought back the distant goddess.”

The myth

According to tradition, Onuris was originally a foreign god of the hunt, tied to the desert, who later became a god of war. Myths said he had brought his wife, Mehit, from Nubia; she was his female counterpart.

In one legend, Ra sent him to retrieve his Eye, which had wandered off and had a life of its own. He bore the title “Slayer of Enemies” and was shown as a bearded man with four feathers on his headdress, sometimes with a lion’s head. The Greeks equated him with Ares.

Worship and religion

His main cult centres were Tanis and Sebennitos, where he was regarded as a god of the wind. Festivals included mock battles between priests and the people, who struck each other with sticks. Several high priests are known, from the reign of Thutmose IV to that of Ramesses III.

Legacy

He is a playable character in the battle arena game SMITE and a boss in Boss Rush: Mythology. In the 2012 novel The Serpent's Shadow he is a god revived to destroy Apophis, and the American death metal band Nile wrote two songs about him in 2000.

In art

Trivia

Tiberius's crown. In the Roman era, Emperor Tiberius appears on Egyptian temple walls wearing Onuris's four-plumed crown.
Lion head. In some depictions he appears with a lion's head, a symbol of strength and power.
