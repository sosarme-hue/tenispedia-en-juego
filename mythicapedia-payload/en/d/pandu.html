king of Hastinapura and father of the Pandavas

Pandu

Pandu is a king of Hastinapura in the ancient Sanskrit epic Mahabharata, and the legal father of the five Pandavas, who are the epic's central characters. His name means "pale", a reference to the pallid appearance he had at birth.

The myth

According to the tradition, Pandu was born to Queen Ambalika and the sage Vyasa, whom his grandmother Satyavati summoned to father an heir after the early death of Vichitravirya left the dynasty without one. Ambalika turned pale with fright at the sage's appearance, and so her son was born pale. His elder half-brother Dhritarashtra was blind and could not rule, so Pandu was crowned king of the Kuru realm at Hastinapura. He expanded his power by conquering several kingdoms, becoming supreme over the other rulers.

Pandu married Kunti and Madri. While hunting in a forest, he shot at a pair of deer that turned out to be the sage Kindama and his wife, who were making love in that form. The dying sage cursed him: Pandu would die the moment he lay with one of his wives. Stricken with guilt, the king handed his kingdom to his brother and withdrew with his wives to the Shatasringa mountains to live as an ascetic.

Because of the curse he could not father children, so Kunti revealed a mantra she had received from the sage Durvasa. Through it she called upon Dharma, Vayu and Indra, and bore Yudhishthira, Bhima and Arjuna. At Pandu's request, Madri used the same mantra to summon the divine twins Ashvins and had Nakula and Sahadeva. These five sons became known as the pious Pandavas.

After fifteen years of abstinence, Pandu, when Kunti and the children were away, felt a strong desire for Madri. She begged him not to, but he tried to embrace her and died on the spot. Consumed by remorse, Madri took her own life after entrusting her children to Kunti. In other accounts, Kunti postponed her own ritual sacrifice in order to raise them.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the great Sanskrit epic of India, where he appears as king of Hastinapura and father of the Pandavas.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His Sanskrit name, pāṇḍu, means "pale", and it is pronounced somewhat differently in Sanskrit than in several modern Indian languages.
A cursed deer. The deer Pandu shot were in fact the sage Kindama and his wife, and the sage's curse shaped the king's fate.
