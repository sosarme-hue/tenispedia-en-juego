Roman goddess of envy

Invidia

Invidia is the Roman goddess of envy, jealousy and vengeance, the counterpart of Nemesis. Romans linked her to the evil eye and to the hostility that lurked around any unusual success.

The myth

In Ovid's Metamorphoses she is a gaunt woman with a sickly pale face, rotten teeth and a tongue dripping venom, who gnaws at others and at herself.

In the story of Aglauros, Minerva sends her to possess the princess so that she envies her sister Herse, whom Mercury desires. The god turns Aglauros into a black rock.

Invidia returns in the myth of Narcissus and Echo, where she grants the wish of a rejected young woman: that the youth should know the pain of unrequited love.

Legacy

In the Renaissance, Cesare Ripa depicted her with a serpent biting her heart, and Shakespeare made envy the fatal flaw of Iago in Othello. Today her name lives on in the company Nvidia and in a battle theme from Final Fantasy XV.

Ancient sources: Ovid, Metamorphoses (Book II), which describes Invidia in detail.

In art

Trivia

A tech company. The name of the company Nvidia comes from Invidia, the Roman goddess of envy.
A video game. Invidia is also the name of a battle theme in Final Fantasy XV.
