Roman goddess of thought and the mind

Mens

Mens, also called Mens Bona, was the Roman personification of thought, consciousness and the mind, as well as of right thinking. Her temple on the Capitoline Hill made her a symbol of clear reasoning in Roman religion.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, a temple to Mens on the Capitoline Hill was vowed in 217 BC, on the advice of the Sibylline Books, after the defeat at Lake Trasimene. It was dedicated in 215 BC, and its founding day was celebrated on June 8.

In Latin love elegy, Mens Bona stands as a guardian against desire and amorous pursuits. Propertius claimed to have escaped his erotic bondage to Cynthia by devoting himself to her shrine. Ovid imagined Cupid leading Mens Bona as a captive in his triumphal parade.

Worship and religion

Her temple in Rome was vowed after a military defeat and dedicated in 215 BC; its founding was celebrated on June 8. The sources do not describe specific rituals.

Legacy

The Latin word mens, meaning mind, is the origin of English words such as mental and dementia. The high-IQ society Mensa International was originally to be called mens, but took the name Mensa (Latin for "table") to avoid confusion with "men's" in English and with mens in other languages, such as Dutch, where it means "mankind".

Ancient sources: The Sibylline Books, cited as advisers for the founding of the temple; and the Latin poetry of Propertius and Ovid.

Trivia

A name that almost was different. The society Mensa International nearly took the name mens, but chose Mensa to avoid linguistic confusion.
Cupid's captive. Ovid imagined Mens Bona as a captive paraded in Cupid's triumphal procession.
