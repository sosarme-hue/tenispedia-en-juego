warrior and treasurer of the Irish Fenian Cycle

Liath Luachra

Liath Luachra is the name of two characters in the Fenian Cycle of Irish mythology, both connected with the youth of the hero Fionn mac Cumhaill. One is a warrior woman who secretly raises him; the other is a fearsome warrior and treasurer of the Fianna.

The myth

The first is a great warrior who, together with Bodhmall, Fionn's druidess aunt, raises the boy in secret in the forest of Sliabh Bladhma after his father Cumhal is killed by Goll mac Morna. When the boy's growing fame threatens to draw his father's killers to him, his caretakers send him off to fend for himself.

The second is a tall, hideous warrior who had opposed Cumhal and struck him the first blow in the battle where he died. Goll later gave him Cumhal's treasures and made him the Fianna's treasurer; his crane bag, or corrbolg, is one of the band's great treasures. After he kills a young warrior named Glonda, Fionn defeats him easily, takes his treasure and avenges the death. His son, Conán mac Lia, becomes a raider against the Fianna until he is reconciled with them and joins the group.

Ancient sources: Their story appears in The Boyhood Deeds of Fionn, part of the medieval Irish Fenian Cycle.

Trivia

The crane bag. The corrbolg, or crane bag, is one of the Fianna's great treasures and passed to Fionn after he defeated Liath.
A mother weeping blood. According to the tale, Fionn sees Glonda's mother weeping blood over her son's murder, which leads him to avenge it.
