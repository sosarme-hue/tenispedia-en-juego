hero who founded Orchomenus, son of the river-god Peneus

Andreus

Andreus is a figure of Greek myth, son of the river-god Peneus, to whom tradition credits the founding of Orchomenus in Boeotia, a city that took his name. His story matters mainly as a mythical explanation for the origin of certain places and their organisation.

The myth

According to Pausanias, Andreus, son of the river Peneus, was the first to settle as a colonist at Orchomenus, and for this reason the region was called Andreis. When Athamas joined him, Andreus gave him land around Lafystion, the site of modern Coronea and Haliartus.

Andreus married Euippe, daughter of Leucon, and had a son, Eteocles. The people of Orchomenus, however, said that Eteocles was a son of the river Cephisus. When Eteocles became king, he allowed the region to bear Andreus's name and divided the people into two tribes: one was called Cephisias, and the other was named after Andreus.

Elsewhere, Pausanias mentions an Andreus as the first colonist of the island of Andros, though he admits he does not know whether it was the same man. Diodorus Siculus presents him as one of the generals of Rhadamanthys, to whom the island was given as a gift. Stephanus of Byzantium, Conon and Ovid, by contrast, call this colonist Andros, son of Anius.

Legacy

His name is linked to the region of Orchomenus, which by tradition remembered him in the name Andreis and in one of its two tribes. The island of Andros is connected to his figure in some versions, although the sources do not make clear whether it is the same character.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions him when describing Orchomenus and Andros; Diodorus Siculus places him among the generals of Rhadamanthys; Stephanus of Byzantium, Conon and Ovid name a colonist called Andros, son of Anius.

Trivia

Two names for one colonist. Stephanus of Byzantium, Conon and Ovid call the colonist of Andros Andros, son of Anius, rather than Andreus.
A river as grandfather. Andreus is the son of the river-god Peneus, while the people of Orchomenus credited his son Eteocles with a different father, the river Cephisus.
