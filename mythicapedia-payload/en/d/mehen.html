serpent deity of ancient Egypt

Mehen

Mehen, whose name means 'the coiled one', is a snake-shaped deity of Egyptian mythology. Despite his frightening appearance, he was regarded as a benevolent spirit who protected the solar barque of Ra during its nightly journey through the Duat, the underworld.

The myth

According to the funerary texts, Mehen coiled around Ra while the sun barque travelled through the night, and his curves formed a kind of protective shield around the god's shrine or boat. Opposing him was Apophis, the serpent embodying universal chaos, who threatened from the depths.

The earliest references appear in the Coffin Texts, and later he accompanies the sun god in the Amduat. His dwelling was Urt, near the 'Lake of Millions of Years', and tradition places him guarding Ra's barque in the night sky, within the body of the goddess Nut.

Legacy

His name gave its title to an ancient Egyptian board game, the Game of the Serpent, whose boards and pieces appear in tomb paintings and surviving objects. It is unknown whether the game took its name from the character or the other way round, and its rules are lost; those played today are modern reconstructions. The German-Egyptian dictionary by R. Hannig equates the Mehen serpent with the Ouroboros.

Ancient sources: The Coffin Texts and the Amduat, where he appears accompanying Ra on his nightly journey.

Trivia

A game with lost rules. A game called mehen existed, but nobody knows for certain how it was played. The rules used today are modern hypotheses based on surviving boards.
Compared with the Ouroboros. R. Hannig's dictionary equates the Mehen serpent with the Ouroboros, the symbol of a serpent biting its own tail.
