Greek hero, friend of Heracles

Licymnius

Licymnius is a hero of Greek mythology, the illegitimate son of King Electryon and half-brother of Alcmene, the mother of Heracles. He was a loyal companion of the hero, and his story appears mainly in the tales of the Argolid royal family and in the Iliad.

The myth

According to tradition, Licymnius was the son of Electryon, king of Tiryns and Mycenae, and of Mideia, a Phrygian woman; some sources also name Alco as his sister. He was the half-brother of Alcmene and a faithful friend of Heracles. He was the only one of Electryon's sons to return home after the unsuccessful war against the Taphians and Teleboans. He married Perimede, daughter of Alcaeus and sister of Amphitryon, and they had three sons: Melas, Argius and Oeonus.

When Amphitryon was expelled from the Argolid and fled to Thebes, Licymnius went with him. Later, when Heracles gathered his expedition against Laomedon of Troy, he asked Argius to join him. Licymnius was reluctant, since Oeonus had already died fighting alongside Heracles in his war against Lacedaemon, so he made the hero promise to bring Argius back safely. Argius died at Troy, and Heracles cremated his body so it could be returned to his father.

According to a version found in the Iliad, the aged Licymnius was killed by Tlepolemus, son of Heracles, when Tlepolemus was beating a servant with a stick and Licymnius stepped in between. Another account speaks of a quarrel between the two over an unspecified matter. Pausanias mentions his tomb in Argos.

Ancient sources: The Iliad mentions him as an elderly uncle of Heracles, and Pausanias refers to his tomb in Argos.

Trivia

A son of Ares. The Iliad describes him as a son of Ares, which can be understood figuratively as a warrior.
A promise to the hero. Licymnius forced Heracles to promise he would bring his son Argius home, and the hero kept his word by cremating his body at Troy.
