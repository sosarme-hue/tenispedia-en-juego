son of Poseidon in Greek mythology

Lycus

Lycus is a figure of Greek mythology, a son of Poseidon and the Pleiad Celaeno, also known as Lycaon. He barely appears in the stories, and his identity is often confused with that of other characters who share his name.

The myth

According to tradition, Poseidon settled him on the Islands of the Blessed, though the sources record no further deeds of his. Hyginus, a late Latin author, confuses several characters with the same name: he lists Lycus alongside Euphemus and Nycteus as sons of Poseidon and Celaeno, whom he calls the daughter of a certain Ergeus, a name that survives in corrupted form.

In the usual genealogy, the Theban Lycus, co-ruler of Thebes with his brother Nycteus, is the son of Hyrieus and the Pleiad Alcyone. Some modern writers merge the two figures and make Lycus a half-brother of Euripylus and the abductor of Antiope, though the genealogical texts usually treat them separately.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, a late Latin author in his Fabulae, who confuses several characters called Lycus.
