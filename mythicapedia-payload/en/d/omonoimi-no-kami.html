Japanese god of industry and growth

Omonoimi no Kami

Omonoimi no Kami is a Japanese deity (kami) linked to industrial growth and chiefly worshipped on Mount Chōkai, in Yamagata Prefecture. He is considered a possible counterpart of Toyouke-Ōmikami, the deity of food and grain.

The myth

The texts mention Omonoimi alongside a divine pair thought to be identical to Toyouke-Ōmikami. According to tradition, the goddess Toyouke-hime, daughter of Wakumusubi and granddaughter of Izanami in the Kojiki, was brought to Ise Shrine during the reign of Emperor Yūryaku to offer sacred food to Amaterasu.

A story from a lost fudoki of Tanba Province tells of eight heavenly women bathing in Manai Pond. An elderly couple hid one woman's robe, and she lived with them making a sake that cured all ills, until she was chased out about ten years later.

Worship and religion

Omonoimi is worshipped at Chōkaisan Ōmonoimi Shrine and at other places in the Tōhoku region. According to tradition, his rank rose each time Mount Chōkai erupted.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki mentions Toyouke-hime, with whom he is identified; the Nihon Shoki has no entry about her.

Trivia

A rank that grew with eruptions. According to tradition, each eruption of Mount Chōkai raised Omonoimi's rank.
Manai Pond. Manai Pond, in Hiji Village, appears in a lost fudoki as the setting for the story of the heavenly women.
