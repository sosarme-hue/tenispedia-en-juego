king and mythical ancestor of the Dryopians, Greek

Dryops

Dryops is a mythical king of ancient Greece, the eponymous ancestor of the Dryopians, whom tradition places near Mount Oeta. He matters because his name gave a people its identity and explains their migrations across the Greek world.

The myth

Accounts of his origin disagree. Some make him a son of the river god Spercheus or Peneus and Polydora, one of the Danaids; others make him a son of Apollo and Dia, daughter of Lycaon. In the latter version, Dia hid the newborn in a hollow tree, which gave him his name, linked to the Greek word for oak. Some authors replace him with Eurytus, king of Oechalia.

In the Arcadian version, his daughter was loved by Hermes and became the mother of Pan; in the Thessalian one, she conceived Amphissus by Apollo.

Worship and religion

In Asine, in Messenia, he was worshipped as an ancestral hero and son of Apollo. There was a temple of Apollo there, a heroon with a very archaic statue of the hero, and a festival held in his honour.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions him in his Description of Greece when discussing Asine, and Antoninus Liberalis names his son Cragaleus.

Trivia

A tree as a hiding place. In one version, his mother hid him in a hollow tree at birth, a detail that ancient writers linked to his name.
A name that travels. The Dryopians kept their name even after moving to Asine in the Peloponnese.
