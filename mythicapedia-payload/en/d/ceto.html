Greek primordial sea goddess

Ceto

Ceto is an ancient sea goddess of Greek mythology, daughter of Gaia, the Earth, and Pontus, the Sea. She is considered one of the oldest deities and was the mother of many monstrous sea and serpent-like beings, so her figure helps explain the dangers of the sea in the Greek imagination. In Hesiod's Theogony, Ceto was a daughter of Gaia and Pontus and a sister of Phorcys, Nereus, Thaumas and Eurybia. Her name means large fish, and the word cetacean derives from it. With her brother Phorcys she had a long line of offspring: the Graeae, Pemphredo and Enyo, grey-haired from birth; the Gorgons, Stheno, Euryale and Medusa, the only mortal one; and a huge serpent guarding the golden apples of the Hesperides' garden. The genealogy is not uniform. Hesiod also links Echidna to Ceto, though the text does not clearly name her mother. Pherecydes of Athens makes Echidna a daughter of Phorcys without naming a mother, while Apollodorus and Hyginus make Echidna and Typhon the parents of the dragon Ladon. Hyginus names a certain Gorgon as father of the Gorgons, and Stesichorus makes Scylla a daughter of Phorcys and Lamia. In the Odyssey, Thoosa, mother of Polyphemus, is a daughter of Phorcys, with no mother named. The Homeric poems do not describe Ceto; in them, Scylla's mother is Crataeis. In the Perseus cycle, the goddess appears as a marine threat: the hero defeats her before she can devour Andromeda. Ceto is associated with the perils of the sea and with other monster-begetting figures such as Charybdis, Scylla and Hecate.
