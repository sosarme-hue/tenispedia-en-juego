Utnapishtim's ferryman in Mesopotamian myth

Urshanabi

Urshanabi is the boatman of Utnapishtim in the Epic of Gilgamesh, the great literary work of Mesopotamia. He is the one who carries the hero across to the distant realm of the man who survived the great flood.

The myth

According to the Epic of Gilgamesh, Urshanabi is a boatman in the service of Utnapishtim, responsible for ferrying across the cosmic ocean those who wish to reach his home at the far end of the world. Siduri, the tavern keeper, first mentions him to Gilgamesh and tells him he will find him in a forest, gathering wood. The hero attacks him and smashes the so-called "Stone Ones" who accompany him, whose nature remains unclear.

Later, Urshanabi hears from Gilgamesh the story of Enkidu's death and the hero's fear of his own mortality. To continue the voyage, the boat needs three hundred punting poles, which Gilgamesh cuts and uses only once each, until he improvises a sail from Urshanabi's clothes. After three days they reach their destination. In the end, Utnapishtim curses him and relieves him of his duties, perhaps so that no mortal may ever reach his shore again.

Ancient sources: The Epic of Gilgamesh, in its Old Babylonian and Standard Babylonian versions, and in its Hittite adaptation.

Trivia

A name made of numbers. His name contains a numeral that could be read as 40. A modern proposal reads it as "servant of two thirds", alluding to Gilgamesh being two thirds god.
The first sail. Gilgamesh improvises a sail from Urshanabi's clothes. Some scholars think this episode may have been meant to explain the origin of sails.
