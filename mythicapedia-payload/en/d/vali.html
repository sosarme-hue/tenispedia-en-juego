Norse god, son of Odin and avenger of Baldr

Váli

Váli is a Norse god, son of Odin and the giantess Rindr, best known as an archer and as the avenger of Baldr's death. He appears only briefly in the myths, yet his role is pivotal in the story that leads to Ragnarök.

The myth

According to the Icelandic sources, Odin fathered Váli for the sole purpose of avenging Baldr, who had been killed by Höðr. The poems Baldrs draumar and Völuspá tell how Váli grew to adulthood within a single night, did not wash his hands or comb his hair before bringing Höðr to the pyre, and then killed him.

The Prose Edda describes him as a bold fighter and an outstanding marksman. The Vafþrúðnismál states that he will survive Ragnarök alongside his brother Víðarr. Saxo Grammaticus, in the twelfth-century Gesta Danorum, turns him into Boe, a son of Odin and the princess Rinda, who dies after avenging Balder.

A single passage in the Prose Edda mentions a Váli who is a son of Loki, probably the result of a copying or translation error, since the other sources place him as Odin's son.

Worship and religion

The sources say very little about the worship of Váli. He does not seem to have been a widely venerated deity among the people, and he is mainly a literary figure shaped by the skalds.

Legacy

In the Norwegian calendar his month, falling between mid-January and February, was marked with the sign of the bow and called liosberi, meaning bearer of light. Early Christians linked that month to Saint Valentine, who was also an archer and the patron of lovers.

Ancient sources: Völuspá, Baldrs draumar, Vafþrúðnismál and Snorri's Prose Edda, as well as Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

An avenger of a single night. According to the poems, Váli reached adulthood within a single day of his birth, a detail that emphasizes the urgency of his mission.
A translation error. The Váli who is Loki's son appears in only one line of the Prose Edda, and scholars consider it a copying or translation error.
