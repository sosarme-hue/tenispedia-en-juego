Yoruba orisha of herbs and healing

Osanyin

Osanyin is a Yoruba orisha who rules leaves, herbs and the secrets of nature. He is regarded as a healer, sage and diviner, and his knowledge is essential to the religious life of his followers.

The myth

According to tradition, Osanyin is Igbamole and came into the world by order of Olodumare, the supreme god. He knows every plant, animal and mineral, and his knowledge is used to save lives, strengthen warriors and keep death away. He is a close friend of Ogun and Oshosi, who are linked to the forest.

He is believed to embody nature itself and to be positioned on the left side of the human body.

Worship and religion

Osanyin is not directly placed on the head; his cult belongs mainly to the Babalawos, and Oloshas receive him without passing him on. No consecration is performed without him, since herbs are always used, and his followers, the adajunshe, know the properties of plants and the chants for the omieros. Tobacco and maize are offered to him. In the Americas he is syncretized with Saint Joseph, and in Santeria with Saint Sylvester or Saint Michael the Archangel, and his worship continues today.

Legacy

He guards the batá drums and obtained ashé for Orula. His image is linked to birds and to the wrought iron staff topped with birds, as well as to ventriloquism through effigies that produce tiny high-pitched voices.

Trivia

Staff with birds. According to a quote from the Odu Ifa, there are sixteen styles of wrought iron staff, depending on the number of birds on its summit, from one to sixteen. The last is the most prestigious.
Voices of birds. In some Yoruba groups, the ventriloquism of effigies is attributed to the sound of small birds that represent Osanyin.
