Slavic spirit of spring and youth

Vesna

Vesna is a female figure in Slavic mythology linked to youth and springtime, documented mainly among the South Slavs. Together with her companion Vesnik, she was associated with rural rituals of the season, and her name survives as the ordinary word for 'spring' in several Slavic languages.

The myth

According to Slovene tradition, the vesnas were beautiful women who lived in palaces high in the mountains, where they debated the fate of the harvests and of people. A magic circle kept them there all year, except in February: at midnight they rode wooden carts down the roads into the valleys, and only those with a innate gift could hear their voices.

Anyone who managed to sneak into their mountain palaces could learn what the year held in store, but if the vesnas caught them in the act, they paid dearly for the boldness. Some scholars suggest that Vesna was originally an earth goddess of spring, closely related to Mokosh.

Worship and religion

In rural areas, her figure accompanied the rituals marking the arrival of spring. According to the English author G. F. Abbott, nineteenth-century Russian peasants greeted the return of the season on March 1 by going out to the fields carrying a clay lark figure decorated with flowers, and sang songs about springtime.

Legacy

Today Vesna is the poetic word for 'spring' in Slovene, Croatian, Czech and Slovak. In Serbo-Croatian, vesnik denotes something that heralds the season, such as early-blooming flowers. Her story inspired the 1983 Czechoslovak short film A Ballad About Green Wood by Jiří Barta, and she appeared on a Slovenian postage stamp sold between 2005 and 2006.

Trivia
