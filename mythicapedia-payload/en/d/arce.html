messenger goddess of Greek mythology, sister of Iris

Arke

Arke is a messenger goddess in Greek mythology, sister of Iris, who according to tradition switched sides to the Titans during the Titanomachy. She matters mainly because, according to one ancient writer, she gave Achilles his epithet Podarces.

The myth

Daughter of Thaumas and sister of Iris, Arke also had wings. During the Titanomachy she left the Olympian camp and joined the Titans. When Zeus won, he tore off her wings and cast her into Tartarus. According to Ptolemy Hephaestion, those wings were given as a wedding gift to Peleus and Thetis, and Thetis later passed them on to their son Achilles, which is why he was called Podarces, meaning swift-footed.

The same writer tells how Thetis secretly threw her sons by Peleus into the fire, and how Peleus rescued the seventh, Achilles, who was scarred by burns on his ankle bone. Chiron then replaced it with the bone of Damysus, a giant famed for his speed. Other sources give the role of Titan messenger to Ithas, a figure identified with Prometheus.

Ancient sources: Ptolemy Hephaestion, and Eumelus of Corinth's lost Titanomachy, which named the Titans' messenger as Ithas or Ithax.

Trivia

Inherited wings. According to the account, Arke's wings passed from Zeus to Peleus and Thetis as a wedding gift, and from Thetis to Achilles, who wore them on his feet.
A messenger with two names. In Eumelus of Corinth's lost poem, the Titans' messenger was called Ithas, a figure later identified with Prometheus.
