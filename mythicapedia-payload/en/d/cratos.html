Greek god of strength

Cratos

Cratos is the personification of strength and power in Greek mythology. Son of Pallas and Styx, he dwells with Zeus and carries out the enforcement of his rule.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Styx, daughter of Ocean, joined with Pallas and bore Zelus, Nike, Cratos and Bia. When Styx came to Zeus first after the Titanomachy, he honored her and her children with high positions in his new regime, and they remain near him. Other authors differ on the siblings: Hyginus names Potestas and adds Scylla, the Springs and the Lakes among the offspring of Pallas and Styx.

The best-known episode is the punishment of Prometheus in Aeschylus' Prometheus Bound. Together with his sister Bia, Cratos leads the Titan to a remote spot in Scythia and compels the gentle Hephaestus to chain him to a rock for stealing fire for humans. Cratos is brutal and merciless, mocking Hephaestus and Prometheus and predicting that the Titan will never escape. In Plato, the theft is set at the temple of Athena and Hephaestus, guarded by feared 'guards of Zeus' whose identity remains unclear. In Aeschylus' Libation Bearers, Electra calls on Cratos and Dike to help Orestes avenge Agamemnon.

Legacy

Cratos appears in Romantic-era art and literature through the scene of Prometheus Bound, which writers of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries used to reflect on revolution, tyranny and freedom. Eighteenth-century English translations of Aeschylus helped bring the scene to British readers.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Aeschylus' Prometheus Bound and Libation Bearers, Plato's Protagoras, and the Library attributed to Apollodorus.
