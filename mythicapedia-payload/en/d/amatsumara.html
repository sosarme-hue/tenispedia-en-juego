Shinto kami of ironworking and blacksmiths

Amatsumara

Amatsumara is the Shinto kami of ironworking and the patron of blacksmiths, mentioned in the Kojiki. According to tradition, he served as the smith of the gods in Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven, and his work played a key role in one of the best-known episodes of Japanese myth.

The myth

According to tradition, Amatsumara worked as the gods' blacksmith in Takamagahara. In many versions he worked with Ishikori-dome no Mikoto to make a mirror, which was used to lure Amaterasu out of the rock cave of heaven where she had hidden. Some traditions also link him to the giant yōkai Daidarabocchi.

His name, Amatsumara, is read as ma-ura, meaning «eye divination». Some believe it alludes to him being one-eyed, a reference to the hazards of the blacksmith's trade. Another of his names, Amenomahitotsu, can be rendered as «the one-eyed kami of heaven».

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in the Kojiki, the oldest chronicle of Japanese history.

Trivia

A name that speaks of the trade. Some interpretations read Amatsumara as «eye divination» and connect it with a one-eyed blacksmith, reflecting the dangers of working hot metal.
A link to a giant. Some traditions associate him with Daidarabocchi, a giant yōkai from Japanese folklore, placing him also in the realm of supernatural beings.
