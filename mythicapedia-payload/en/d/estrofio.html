king of Phocis in Greek mythology

Strophius

Strophius was a king of Phocis who ruled from Crisa, at the foot of Mount Parnassus, according to Greek tradition. He matters because he took in and raised the young Orestes, and because he was the father of Pylades, Orestes' cousin and inseparable friend.

The myth

According to tradition, Strophius was a son of Crisus, of the line of the Aeacids, and of Antiphateia, daughter of Naubolus. He married Anaxibia, sister of Agamemnon, and they had Pylades; he also had a daughter, Astydameia. The story shows him returning home with renown after winning at the Olympic Games, when he met Electra, daughter of Agamemnon.

Electra told him about the tragedy in her family. Clytemnestra had killed Agamemnon with the help of her lover Aegisthus, who had seized the palace and power in Argos. Electra feared that her younger brother Orestes would meet the same fate, and she wanted to keep him safe.

Strophius promised Electra that he would take Orestes away and raise him in his palace. Electra had hidden the boy somewhere, and Strophius brought him to Phocis. There Orestes grew up alongside Pylades, and the two cousins became inseparable friends, a bond that later retellings held up as a model of loyalty.

Trivia

Olympic victory. In the story, Strophius returns home honored after winning at the Olympic Games, a detail that underlines his prestige before he meets Electra.
Inseparable cousins. Pylades and Orestes were cousins, and tradition presents them as inseparable friends from childhood at Strophius' court.
