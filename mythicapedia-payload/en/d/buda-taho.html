Buddha of the jeweled stupa in Mahayana Buddhism

Prabhūtaratna

Prabhūtaratna (Taho in Japanese, meaning Many Treasures) is a Buddha who appears in the Lotus Sutra. Seated inside a jeweled stupa, he vouches for the teachings of Shakyamuni.

The myth

According to the eleventh chapter of the sutra, Prabhūtaratna lives in a land called Treasure Purity, far to the east. While still following the bodhisattva path, he vowed that even after entering nirvana he would appear to verify the Lotus teaching wherever it was preached.

When Shakyamuni preaches on Vulture Peak, a great stupa rises from the earth and hangs in midair. Prabhūtaratna invites Shakyamuni to sit beside him inside it, and the two Buddhas continue teaching together. Shakyamuni then lifts the whole assembly into the air, in the scene known as the Ceremony in the Air. In the twenty-second chapter, the stupa returns beneath the earth.

Worship and religion

In Japanese Buddhism, Nichiren placed him on the Gohonzon, his calligraphic image of the treasure tower.

Legacy

The scene of the two Buddhas seated together has been a frequent theme in Buddhist art. Thich Nhat Hanh reads it as the union of the ultimate and the historical.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra (Saddharma Puṇḍarīka), especially chapters 11 and 22.

Trivia

A Gohonzon with a tower. Nichiren included Prabhūtaratna in the Gohonzon, his calligraphic representation of the treasure tower.
Two Buddhas, one tower. For Akira Hirakawa, the two Buddhas seated together in the tower symbolize the union of the eternal nature of the dharma and the eternal nature of the Buddha.
