twin asuras of Hindu mythology

Madhu and Kaitabha

Madhu and Kaitabha are two asuras in Hindu scripture, born from the earwax of Vishnu. Their story, which includes the theft of the Vedas and their defeat, is linked to Hindu cosmology.

The myth

According to tradition, Madhu and Kaitabha arose from the earwax of Vishnu while he rested in the meditative state of yoganidra. Madhu emerged from a dewdrop as sweet as honey, and Kaitabha from a hard one.

After long penances to the goddess Mahadevi, they gained the boon that they could die only by their own consent. Growing powerful, they stole the Vedas from Brahma and hid them in Patala. Vishnu defeated them through cunning: he asked as a boon that they slay him, and the confident asuras agreed to die anywhere except in water. According to the Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu, in his form of Hayagriva, killed them and recovered the sacred texts.

Legacy

Their story appears in the Devi Bhagavata Purana, and the epithets Madhusudana and Kaitabhajit, meaning "slayer of Madhu" and "victor over Kaitabha", remain names of Vishnu.

Ancient sources: Devi Bhagavata Purana, Bhagavata Purana and Mahabharata.

Trivia

Twelve pieces. According to the Bhagavata Purana, the bodies of the two asuras broke into twelve pieces. These are considered to represent the twelve tectonic plates of the Earth.
An avenging son. The Mahabharata says the asuras had a son, Dhundhu, who sought revenge against Vishnu for killing his fathers. He was slain by King Kuvalāśva of the Ikshvaku dynasty and his sons.
