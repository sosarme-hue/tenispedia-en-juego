Greek hero, son of Achilles

Neoptolemus

Neoptolemus was the son of Achilles and Deidamia, known in his childhood as Pyrrhus. In Greek tradition he was a young warrior who played a decisive role in the Trojan War and became the founder of the ruling dynasty of the Molossians in Epirus.

The myth

According to the myth, Achilles hid at the court of King Lycomedes on the island of Skyros, disguised as a woman, to avoid being sent to war. There he fathered a son with Princess Deidamia, whom he called Pyrrhus, the female form of the name he had taken in disguise. Other accounts make Neoptolemus the son of Achilles and Iphigenia, or the product of a rape, according to Statius.

After Achilles died, Odysseus brought the young Pyrrhus to Troy. There he received his father's armour, killed the prince Eurypylus and earned the name Neoptolemus, "the young warrior". He helped bring Philoctetes and the bow of Heracles to the Greek side, slipped into the city inside the wooden horse and killed King Priam. He took Andromache as a captive and, by tradition, sacrificed Polyxena at the tomb of Achilles.

Afterwards he ruled Epirus, where he had children with Andromache, among them Molossus, Pielus and Pergamus. He then married Hermione, daughter of Menelaus, who had been promised to Orestes. At Delphi, Orestes killed him, although another version blames the Delphian Machaereus. His bones were scattered across the region of Ambracia.

Worship and religion

According to one tradition, Neoptolemus was buried within the sacred precinct at Delphi, and festivals in his honour were held every eight years. There is no evidence of a cult that continued in later periods.

Legacy

Several kings of Epirus bore the name Pyrrhus or Neoptolemus, and Pyrrhus of Epirus claimed descent from this hero. According to Pseudo-Apollodorus, he was an ancestor of Olympias, mother of Alexander the Great. His name gave rise to the expression "the punishment of Neoptolemus", a retribution in which someone suffers the same wrong they once inflicted. The Myrmidons who followed him were said to have founded the Illyrian city of Byllis.

Ancient sources: The Cypria, Euripides' Hecuba, Sophocles' Philoctetes, Virgil's Aeneid and Hyginus' Fabulae mention him, with versions that differ in his origin and death.

Trivia

A name from a disguise. Pyrrhus means "red" or "blond", and he received the name because Achilles, disguised as a woman, called himself Pyrrha.
A phrase from his story. The expression "punishment of Neoptolemus" comes from the story that he killed Priam at an altar and died at an altar.
