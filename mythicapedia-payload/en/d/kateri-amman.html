Tamil guardian goddess of villages

Kateri Amman

Kateri Amman, also known as Kateri, is a Tamil guardian goddess who protects certain towns and villages in South India. Her worship grew out of Dravidian folk religion and was later absorbed into mainstream Hinduism as a form of Parvati.

The myth

According to Tamil folklore, Shiva noticed that Parvati left their bed at night and returned before dawn. He followed her into a forest and discovered that she had taken the fearsome form of Kali to dig up corpses. Shiva dug a hole in the path; Kali fell into it, regretted her actions and left her terrible form there, and that form became Kateri Amman, a benevolent goddess who cures illness.

According to Dalit folklore, however, her origin lies in a curse from Shiva: she would roam the forests and attack pregnant women if she was not worshipped.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped in villages of South India and Sri Lanka, especially by the Dalit community, and also by the Tamil diaspora in Trinidad, Guyana, Jamaica, Mauritius and South Africa. Her offerings include neem leaves, limes and red flowers, and animal sacrifices of chickens and pigs are common. She is considered a kuladaivam, the family deity of a lineage.

Trivia

Iconography. She is often shown with dark blue or black skin and, in her four-armed form, holding an aruval, a bowl, a trishula or a lotus.
