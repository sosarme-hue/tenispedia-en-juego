Egyptian god of perception and intuition

Sia

Sia (or Saa) personifies perception, intuition and understanding in Egyptian mythology. Together with Hu and Heka, he forms a trio of powers that accompany Ra and make creation possible, which makes him a symbol of the mind that conceives and orders the world.

The myth

In the Heliopolitan cosmogony, Sia is the deification of perception within the Ennead, the group of gods associated with Heliopolis. The Memphite tradition links him instead to the intellectual energies of Ptah's heart, although the sources do not spell out a precise equivalence.

In the Book of the Dead, Ra cuts off his phallus and from the blood that drips from it creates two gods, Sia and Hu, personifications of the mind and of authority. In this account Sia springs from the creator himself and becomes one of his faculties.

In New Kingdom texts and tomb decorations, Sia rides in Ra's solar barque on his nightly journey through the underworld, alongside Hu and Heka. The three appear as powers that help the creator on his voyage.

Worship and religion

Sia had no cult of his own, unlike Heka, who did. His role was limited to accompanying Ra and forming part of the solar barque's company.

Legacy

Sia was connected with writing. He is often shown on Ra's right side holding a papyrus scroll, a symbol of intellectual achievement. His hieroglyph was used to write the verbs "to perceive", "to know" and "to be cognizant".

Ancient sources: He appears in the Book of the Dead and in New Kingdom texts and tombs, such as the solar barque scenes.

Trivia

A hieroglyph with a double use. The sign for Sia was used to write verbs such as to perceive or to know, so the god was tied to the writing of the very idea of knowledge.
A god without a cult. Unlike Heka, who had his own cult, Sia received no specific shrines or rituals and appears mainly in the scenes of the solar barque.
