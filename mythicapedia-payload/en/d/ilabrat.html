Mesopotamian god, vizier of Anu

Ilabrat

Ilabrat is a Mesopotamian god who served as vizier and messenger of the sky god Anu. He is best known from the Akkadian myth of Adapa, but he was also worshipped independently, mainly as a family deity.

The myth

In the story of Adapa, a priest of Ea's temple at Eridu who had gained wisdom but not eternal life, the south wind capsized his boat while he was fishing. Furious, Adapa broke the wings of the wind, which could not blow over the land for seven days.

Summoned before Anu, Adapa followed his father Ea's advice and refused the food and drink that would have granted him immortality. Ilabrat reported to Anu what had happened, and the myth thus explains the origin of death.

Worship and religion

Ilabrat was honoured as a family god who could settle personal disputes or act as a witness. Old Assyrian documents from Kanesh mention a golden sun disc to be dedicated to him, and many personal names include his, such as Ibbi-Ilabrat. By the first millennium BCE he had been eclipsed by Papsukkal.

Ancient sources: The Akkadian tablets of the Adapa myth, where he appears as Anu's messenger.

Trivia

A god perhaps seen as female. Some scholars think that in northwestern Mesopotamia Ilabrat may have been perceived as female, because of the -t suffix in some forms of his name. Most treat him as male.
A sign in the sky. The constellation Orion, known as Sipazianna, was regarded as the astral symbol of Ilabrat.
