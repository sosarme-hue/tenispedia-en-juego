charioteer of Anga and foster-father of Karna

Adhiratha

Adhiratha was a charioteer from the kingdom of Anga and the foster-father of Karna, one of the great warriors of the Mahabharata, the Sanskrit epic of ancient India. He matters because he shaped the household in which one of the poem's most famous fighters grew up.

The myth

Adhiratha was a charioteer from the kingdom of Anga, remembered mostly as the foster-father of Karna, a central figure of the Mahabharata. His wife was Radha, and together they had two biological sons, Shatrunjaya and Chitrasena. Tradition places him in a distinguished lineage: according to the Bhagavata Purana, he descended from Yayati and was therefore related to Krishna.

On his ancestral side he was a descendant of Romapada, king of Anga, and brother-in-law of Shighra, ruler of Ayodhya from the line of Dasharatha. This web of kinship connects him to the royal families of northern India, even though he worked as a charioteer rather than a king.

The Mahabharata revolves around the great struggle between the Pandavas and the Kauravas. Karna, raised in Adhiratha's home, eventually stands with the Kauravas, tying the charioteer to one of the poem's most tragic figures. Accounts of his family vary among the epic's versions, since the manuscripts differ greatly in the details of their characters. Though he is not a protagonist, his domestic role gives him a place of his own in the story.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana is the source that describes his genealogy.

Trivia

Kinship with Krishna. According to the Bhagavata Purana, Adhiratha descended from Yayati, so he was related to Krishna.
A family of his own. Besides Karna, Adhiratha had two biological sons with Radha, Shatrunjaya and Chitrasena.
