lioness goddess of Egyptian mythology

Aperetiset

Aperetiset, also Aperet-Isis and in Greek Trifis, was a lioness goddess of ancient Egypt whose main cult centre was Akhmim in Upper Egypt. She is known mainly from late inscriptions from the Saite and Greco-Roman periods, and her figure reflects the merging of several deities.

The myth

Her name means "she who furnishes the throne" or "she who has a throne," and she bore the title "Great Woman." According to the most widely held view, she arose from the combination of two goddesses: the lioness Repit and Isis, and eventually she supplanted Isis. She is also linked to Repit and Hathor, since she appears wearing the Hathor headdress, a solar disc between two horns, and sometimes a tall crown of plumes. For this reason she is thought to be a lion-headed form of Hathor, the goddess of love.

At Akhmim, the Greek Panopolis and capital of the ninth nome of Upper Egypt, she formed a divine triad with Min, which underlines her character of fertility, and with her son Colantes or, in late and Greco-Roman times, with Horus. Under the Greek name Trifis she was regarded as the mother of Colantes, and her figure merged with his to form "Aperetiset in the House of the Moon."

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was the city of Akhmim, where she shared a triad with Min and with her son Colantes or, in late times, with Horus.

Ancient sources: She is known only from late inscriptions of the Saite and Greco-Roman periods, so there are no lengthy ancient texts describing her.
