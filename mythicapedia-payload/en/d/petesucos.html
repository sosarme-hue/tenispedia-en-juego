sacred crocodile of Crocodilopolis

Petsuchos

Petsuchos is the name the Greeks gave to the sacred crocodile of Crocodilopolis, in Egypt's Faiyum region, venerated as a manifestation of the god Sobek.

The myth

According to the accounts, it was a pampered animal wearing gold earrings and bracelets on its forelegs. When it died, its body was mummified and replaced by another crocodile.

Its name is read in several ways. Hans Bonnet took it to mean 'born of Sebek', while the most widespread view treats it as the name of a sacred crocodile. Adolf Erman, by contrast, proposed that it was a deified person, the builder of the Hawara labyrinth described by Herodotus and, according to Pliny the Elder, credited to that figure.

Worship and religion

It was worshipped at Per-Sobek (Crocodilopolis), Kerkeosiris and Theadelphia, and a temple in its honour was built at Karanis. Mummified crocodiles were placed in its sanctuaries.

Legacy

It appears in the video game Age of Mythology as a mythical unit of the Egyptians, and in Assassin's Creed: Origins, where players can visit Krocodilopolis.

Ancient sources: Papyri from the time of Ptolemy Auletes and Greco-Roman authors such as Pliny the Elder mention it.

Trivia

Gold jewellery. According to the accounts, the animal wore gold earrings in its ears and bracelets on its forelegs.
A replacement. When it died, its body was mummified and another crocodile took its place.
