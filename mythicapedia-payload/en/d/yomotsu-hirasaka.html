boundary between the world of the living and the dead in Japanese mythology

Yomotsu Hirasaka

Yomotsu Hirasaka is a slope that, in Japanese mythology, marks the boundary between the land of the living and Yomi, the realm of the dead. Named in the Kojiki, it is one of the best-known mythical places linked to Izanami and to the separation of the two worlds.

The myth

According to tradition, Yomotsu Hirasaka is a slope that divides the land of the living from Yomi, the underworld. The Kojiki mentions it twice in its opening section, and tradition places it at Ifuyasaka in the former province of Izumo.

The idea of a boundary place between the realms of the living and the dead appears in many cultures, such as the Sanzu River. In Japan, the slope is thought to have been inspired by the stone passages leading to the burial chambers of kofun tombs. The word "hira" is said to mean "cliff".

In the stories of Izanagi, who tries to bring back his wife Izanami, the slope acts as an impassable barrier between the two worlds. A legend recorded in the Edo period tells how Izanagi threw peach fruits at the god of thunder at Komakaerizaka in Matsue.

Worship and religion

Nearby, Iya Shrine is dedicated to Izanami. In 1940 a stone monument was erected at Iya, Higashiizumo, to mark the traditional site of the slope, beside a large rock known as Senbiki.

Legacy

The site appears in the 2010 Japanese film Matataki, where the heroine visits her dead boyfriend there.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, which mentions it twice in its first part.
