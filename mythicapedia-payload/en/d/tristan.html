Arthurian hero of Celtic origin

Tristan

Tristan is a hero of the Arthurian tradition of Celtic origin, a Knight of the Round Table and the protagonist of the legend of Tristan and Iseult. His story of forbidden love and tragic fate is among the most influential in medieval European literature.

The myth

In the tale, Tristan is the nephew of King Mark of Cornwall, and he must bring the Irish princess Iseult from Ireland so she can wed the king. During the voyage, the pair accidentally drink a love potion and fall in love. An adulterous affair follows, leading them through numerous trials as they hide their secret, until a tragic end.

The oldest versions, of which only fragments survive, come mainly from the poems of Béroul and Eilhart von Oberge, and from the works of Thomas of Britain and Gottfried von Strassburg. In the 13th-century Prose Tristan, the story is folded into the Arthurian cycle: after fleeing the anger of King Mark, Tristan takes refuge at King Arthur's court and becomes a knight-errant. In Gottfried's version he is the son of Blancheflor and Rivalen; in later tellings, of Meliodas and Queen Elizabeth.

His name has several interpretations: the Brythonic form has been linked to 'clanking swords of iron', while Romance languages associated it with sadness. A 6th-century pillar in Cornwall inscribed with Drustanus may point to an older origin.

Legacy

Thomas Malory included the story in Le Morte d'Arthur, where Tristram, rival and friend of Lancelot, is one of the greatest knights of the Round Table. The 14th-century French romance Ysaÿe le Triste tells the adventures of a son of Tristan and Iseult, and the 1555 Italian work I Due Tristani introduces another son, Tristan the Younger.

Ancient sources: Béroul, Eilhart von Oberge, Thomas of Britain and Gottfried von Strassburg, in medieval poems of the 12th and 13th centuries; later, the Prose Tristan.

Trivia

A stone with his name. In Cornwall a 6th-century stone bears the inscription Drustanus, a Latin form of Drust, which is linked to the name Tristan.
A name with several origins. The philologist Sigmund Eisner proposed that the name comes from Drust, son of Talorc, a Pictish figure whose name may have travelled southwest through Britain.
