king of Mysia, eponym of Teuthrania

Teuthras

Teuthras is a king of Mysia in Greek mythology and the mythological eponym of the town of Teuthrania. His story is closely tied to that of the hero Telephus, whom he took in and whose destiny he changed forever, and it shows how a kingdom could pass from fathers to sons-in-law and adoptive heirs.

The myth

According to the myth, Teuthras received Auge, the ill-fated mother of Telephus, and the tradition does not agree on whether he married her or adopted her as his own daughter. No source says who his own parents were. When Idas, son of Aphareus, tried to seize his throne, Telephus arrived in time to help him. He had been told by the Delphic oracle to sail to Mysia if he wanted to learn who his mother was. Telephus defeated Idas, and then he and Auge recognized each other.

Since Teuthras had no sons, he gave his daughter Argiope to Telephus as his wife and named him successor to the kingdom of Mysia. The versions differ: in one, Nauplius brought Auge and the infant Telephus to Mysia; in another, Teuthras raised Telephus as his own son; and there was even a version that made him Telephus's biological father.

As for his descendants, Homer's Iliad calls him the father of Axylus, a warrior who died in the Trojan War. Another tradition gives him a son named Teuthranius, born to Auge, who also fell fighting at Troy.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad names him as the father of Axylus, Euripides describes how he raised Auge and Telephus, and Dictys Cretensis and Hyginus preserve other versions of his story.

Trivia

A son with his own name. Some traditions credit him with a son called Teuthranius, born to Auge, who died fighting in the Trojan War.
Conflicting versions. According to Dictys Cretensis, he also fathered Tecmessa, although another version makes her the daughter of the Phrygian Teleutas.
