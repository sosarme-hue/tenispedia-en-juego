Greek god of marriage and the wedding song

Hymen

Hymen is the Greek god who presides over the wedding procession and protects marriage. His name gave its title to the hymenaios, the wedding song sung to invoke him as the bride travelled to her new home.

The myth

Accounts of his parentage differ. The most common version makes him the son of Apollo and one of the Muses, Clio, Calliope, Urania or Terpsichore, though other writers name Dionysus, or Dionysus and Aphrodite, as his father. In a fragment attributed to Hesiod, he is the son of Magnes, a youth of remarkable beauty whom Apollo fell in love with. Other traditions make him the lover of Dionysus, Tamyris, Argynnus or Hesperus, depending on the source.

A later romance tells of an Athenian youth of humble birth who, to get close to a wealthy woman's daughter, disguised himself as a woman and joined a religious procession at Eleusis. Pirates captured the women, but Hymen encouraged them, planned with them, and together they killed their captors. As his reward he asked to marry one of them, and the marriage was so happy that the Athenians founded festivals in his honour. According to Apollodorus, the Orphics claimed he was also among those raised from the dead by Asclepius.

Worship and religion

Hymen was invoked at Greek weddings. It was believed that he had to attend every marriage, and if he did not, the union would be unhappy, so guests would call his name aloud. His song, the hymenaios, accompanied the bride's procession to the groom's house, while the epithalamium was sung at the nuptial threshold. The sources offer little information about any organised cult.

Legacy

His name entered language through the word hymen, the membrane linked to virginity, although the word does not derive directly from his name but shares its root. He appears in Shakespeare's plays as the presider over weddings, and Vivaldi composed Gloria e Imeneo in 1725 for the wedding of Louis XV of France. The Italian Renaissance established his familiar image as a young man with a garland of flowers and a burning torch.

Ancient sources: Sappho and Euripides treat him as a personification; Homer's Iliad mentions him in the scene of Achilles' shield, and he also appears in Catullus, Ovid and Virgil.

Trivia

Calling out loud. Tradition held that if Hymen failed to attend a wedding, the marriage would be unhappy, so the guests called his name aloud during the celebration.
A song that named a genre. The hymenaios was a form of Greek lyric poetry sung during the bridal procession, and his name stayed attached to that custom.
