queen of Phoenicia in Greek myth

Cassiopeia

Cassiopeia is a figure of Greek mythology, the daughter of Arabus and wife of King Phoenix of Phoenicia. Her story is of interest mainly for the varying accounts of her family tree and for the tales in which Zeus appears.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the sources, Cassiopeia was the daughter of Arabus, also called Arabius, and she married Phoenix, king of Phoenicia. From this union came Phineus and Carme, although other authors claim that Carme was in fact the daughter of Eubuleus, a Cretan. The mention of a different father shows that her family tree was never fixed, and each telling could adjust her relationships.

Some versions go further and claim that Cassiopeia was the mother of the hero Atymnius, conceived either with her own husband or with the god Zeus. Another tradition holds that Anchinos was also a son of Cassiopeia and Zeus. The god is said to have seduced her by taking the shape of Phoenix, so she believed she was with her husband while the god was posing as him.

Trivia

Several fathers. Carme is presented as a daughter of Arabus and Phoenix in some versions and of Eubuleus in others, showing that ancient authors did not agree on her family tree.
Zeus in disguise. According to one tradition, Zeus took the form of Phoenix to approach Cassiopeia, so she did not suspect the deception.
