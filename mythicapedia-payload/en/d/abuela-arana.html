Spider Grandmother

Spider Grandmother is a figure in the mythology of the Hopi and Navajo peoples of the American Southwest. Many traditions regard her as a creator of the world and a guide for humankind.

The myth

In Hopi tradition, Spider Grandmother works alongside the sun god Tawa to create the world and its creatures. Together they give life to their creations and shape the first human beings. She guides the people through successive worlds up to the present surface and teaches them to weave and make pottery.

In Navajo stories she is a constant protector of people. She taught them to spin sheep's wool, dye it and weave it, which allowed one village to survive the winter by selling rugs. For the Navajo, her home is Spider Rock, a monolith in Canyon de Chelly, Arizona.

Legacy

The playwright Murray Mednick wrote Coyote Cycles, a seven-act play with characters drawn from these myths, and the choreographer Daniel Dutton included Spider Grandmother in his dance The Highway.

Trivia

A white rock. Spider Rock is a monolith in Canyon de Chelly. According to Navajo myth, its white color comes from bones left in the sun.
A link to Teotihuacan. In 1983 the researcher Karl Taube tentatively linked the Southwestern Spider Woman motif with the Great Goddess of Teotihuacan, known from pictorial representations.
