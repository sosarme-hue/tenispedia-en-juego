bull guardian of Shiva in Hindu mythology

Nandi

Nandi is the bull who serves as Shiva's mount (vahana) and his guardian at Mount Kailash, and who is also worshipped in his own right. His seated figure, facing the sanctum, is one of the most constant presences in Shaiva temples across India and in parts of Southeast Asia.

The myth

According to tradition, Nandi was the son of the sage Shilada, who performed severe penance to obtain a child with immortality and Shiva's blessing. The Shiva Purana says he was born from a sacrificial rite (yajna) performed by Shilada. As a devotee, Nandi performed harsh austerities on the banks of the Narmada river until he became Shiva's gatekeeper and mount.

Puranic legends offer other stories. In one, Nandi cursed Ravana, the rakshasa king of Lanka, for behaving restlessly like a monkey while waiting to meet Shiva; the curse foretold that his kingdom would be burned by a monkey, and Hanuman later set Lanka alight. In the Tamil Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, Nandi is incarnated as a whale that troubles people until Shiva, disguised as a fisherman, kills it.

In the Shaiva Siddhanta tradition, Nandi is also a teacher. He passed on his teachings to eight disciples, among them the Four Kumaras, Patanjali and Tirumular, who were sent in eight directions to spread his wisdom. The Tirumantiram places him as an important link in the chain through which the Agamas are transmitted.

Worship and religion

The Shaiva Agamas, compiled between roughly the 5th and 12th centuries CE, prescribed that Nandi be placed on the central axis facing the linga, in his own pavilion, an arrangement that became standard in South Indian Shaiva temples. During the Chola period (9th to 13th centuries) large monolithic statues were carved. Devotees whisper their wishes into his ear, usually the right one, believing he will carry them to Shiva. Flowers are offered and prayers for fertility are addressed to him. His worship remains alive in many temples.

Legacy

The Nandi flag, showing a seated bull, is recognised as the emblem of Shaivism, especially among the Tamil community. It was used by Shaiva rulers such as the Pallavas and the Jaffna Kingdom. It was designed in the 1990s and declared the official Shaiva flag at the 2008 conference in Zurich.

Ancient sources: The Shaiva Agamas, the Shiva Purana, the Linga Purana, the Tamil Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam and the Tirumantiram mention Nandi in different roles.

Trivia

Curse on Ravana. In a Puranic story, Nandi cursed Ravana for his impatience, and the prophecy came true when Hanuman set Lanka on fire.
Whispers in the ear. Many devotees whisper their requests into the right ear of Nandi's statue, convinced that the bull will carry them to Shiva.
