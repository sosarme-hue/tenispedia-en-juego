Etruscan goddess of childbirth

Ethausva

Ethausva is an Etruscan deity linked to childbirth, attested in a few inscriptions and on a bronze mirror from Praeneste. She seems to have been a minor figure, since other goddesses such as Thanr and Thalna covered similar roles.

The myth

Ethausva appears in the scene of the birth of Menerva, the Etruscan equivalent of Athena, shown on a bronze mirror from Praeneste dated to around the 4th-3rd centuries BCE. She stands to the right of Tinia, the Etruscan Zeus, caressing his head with her right hand and supporting his shoulder with her left. On the other side is Thanr, who is bandaging Tinia's head while holding up Menerva, who sits on top of him, fully clothed, with a plumed helmet and a shield. All three figures have wings, and Tinia holds his lightning bolt.

Worship and religion

There is little evidence of a cult dedicated to Ethausva. Her name appears on the bronze liver of Piacenza, a model of a sheep's liver inscribed with the names of many Etruscan deities, where it is abbreviated as EO. This could suggest she held some status in religious practice, though no further evidence survives.

Ancient sources: She appears in a few Etruscan inscriptions.

Trivia

The abbreviation EO. On the liver of Piacenza her name is abbreviated as EO. Ethausva appears alongside Thalna, but Thanr is not included.
Winged goddesses. Ethausva, Thanr and Menerva are all shown with wings on the mirror from Praeneste.
