Aztec god of fishing and hunting

Opochtli

Opochtli is a god of Mexica mythology linked to fishing, hunting and water. He belonged to the Tlaloques, the companions of the rain god Tlaloc, and his name is associated with the left and with the south.

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Opochtli was one of the Tlaloque, the helpers of Tlaloc, the rain god. He was credited with inventing fishing nets and a tool for killing fish called minacachalli.

According to the more detailed accounts, he was the god of fishing and hunting, often shown riding a dolphin. He was considered a representative of Tlaloc and was credited with inventing the atlatl (spear-thrower), the net, the canoe pole and the bird snare. His name in Nahuatl means "the left" or "left-handed", because he threw his spear with his left hand. Since the Mexica viewed west as the main cardinal point, south lay on his left, so he was also linked to that direction.

Worship and religion

Fishermen and water people held festivals in his honour, offering food, pulque, maize and incense.
