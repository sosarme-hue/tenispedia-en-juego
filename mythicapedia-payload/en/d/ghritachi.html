apsara, celestial nymph of Hindu mythology

Ghritachi

Ghritachi is an apsara, a celestial nymph of Hindu mythology, famous for her beauty and for her many unions with gods, sages, celestial musicians and kings, from which illustrious sons were born.

The myth

Ghritachi belongs to the divine class of apsaras and presides over the month of Kumbha in the Indian lunisolar calendar. The stories portray her as a seductress of sages, gandharvas, gods and kings, and as the mother of many children.

According to the Vamana Purana, she lived with Vishvakarma, the architect of the gods, and had a daughter, Chitrangada. When her father forbade the girl to marry, he was cursed to become a monkey until he had a son; Ghritachi freed him by giving birth to Nala, who would later help the god Rama. In the Mahabharata, she seduces the sage Pramati and Ruru is born, and in the Adi Parva she is seen bathing by the sage Bharadvaja, whose seed falls into a basket and gives rise to Drona, future teacher of the Pandavas and the Kauravas.

Ancient sources: Mahabharata, Ramayana, Puranas (including the Shiva Purana, the Vamana Purana and the Brahma Vaivarta Purana)

Trivia

An unexpected birth. According to the Adi Parva, Drona, who later became teacher of the Pandavas and the Kauravas, was born from the involuntary union of the sage Bharadvaja with Ghritachi.
A transformation into a parrot. To attract the sage Vyasa, who wanted an heir without marrying, Ghritachi took the form of a parrot. From that encounter Shuka was born.
