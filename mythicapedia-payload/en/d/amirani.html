culture hero of the Georgian epic

Amirani

Amirani is a culture hero of the Georgian epic, often compared to the Greek Prometheus. According to the tradition, he gave humanity the use of metal, and as punishment he was chained in the Caucasus Mountains.

The myth

According to Georgian tradition, Amirani was the son of Dali, a Caucasian goddess of the hunt, but he was removed from her womb too early. He was raised in the wilderness by the hunter Sulkalmakhi and his wife Darejan, alongside their sons Badri and Usup. Wild and untamed, he fought the devs, demonic giants who lived on the edges of inhabited lands.

To avenge their uncle, the three brothers killed Baq'-Baq', a three-headed giant. From each head emerged a worm that became a dragon: white, red and black. Amirani slew the first two, but the black one swallowed him whole; from inside, he cut its belly open and emerged alive. He then crossed the seas to rescue Kamar, daughter of the demon king Kaji, and defeated his army, including the king, though he lost his brothers. Devastated, he took his own life, but Kamar restored him to life with her powers.

Wandering alone, Amirani prayed to Ghmerti, the Georgian word for God, and received supernatural strength. Swollen with pride, he challenged God to a duel, and his punishment was threefold: he was chained to a stake driven into the earth and buried beneath a dome-shaped rock. A winged dog, Q'ursha, licked the chains until they weakened, but blacksmiths beyond the Caucasus struck their anvils and restored their strength. Once a year he was allowed to see the open sky for one night, until a woman's loud voice closed the mountain again. In some versions, an eagle eats his liver by day, and it heals every night.

Legacy

The volcano Amirani on Io, Jupiter's moon, is named after this figure from Georgian mythology.

Trivia

Parallel with Prometheus. Like Prometheus, Amirani is punished for defying the divine, and in some versions an eagle eats his liver.
A volcano with his name. A volcano on Jupiter's moon Io is named Amirani.
