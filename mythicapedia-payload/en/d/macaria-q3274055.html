daughter of Hades in Greek mythology

Macaria

Macaria is a very obscure figure of Greek mythology, said to be a daughter of Hades, lord of the Underworld. Her only known mention comes from the Byzantine encyclopedia known as the Suda, written in the tenth century.

The myth

According to the Suda, Macaria was a daughter of Hades, but the entry gives no mother, and does not say whether she was a goddess or a mortal, whether she lived with her father in the Underworld, or whether she had a partner or children. No myth of her own survives.

The same entry discusses two other Macarias, apparently unconnected to the daughter of Hades. One is the daughter of Heracles who sacrificed herself to save her siblings and their city when Eurystheus attacked and besieged the hero's children.

Legacy

The ancient Greek proverb "be gone to blessedness", meaning to go into destruction, was used for those whose courage put them in danger. Its modern counterpart would be "go to hell". According to the sophist Zenobius, the phrase was originally connected to the daughter of Heracles and was said admiringly of those who sacrificed themselves with valour.

Ancient sources: The Suda, a tenth-century Byzantine encyclopedia, is its only ancient source.

Trivia

A name with two meanings. The Greek name Macaria can be translated as "happiness" or "blessed one", but it can also mean "foolishness".
The blessed ones. The Greeks traditionally referred to the dead as "the blessed ones", so dying could be expressed as a euphemism.
