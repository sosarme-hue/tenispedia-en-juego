personification of old age in Norse mythology

Elli

Elli is, in Norse mythology, the personification of old age. According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, she managed to throw Thor, the god of thunder, in a wrestling match, which makes her one of the most unusual figures in Germanic lore.

The myth

The episode takes place in the hall of the giant Útgarða-Loki, where Thor, Loki and Þjálfi arrive. There they are set difficult tests of strength and skill. Thor, who had just been humiliated in a drinking contest, wants a chance to redeem himself and challenges anyone to wrestle him. The giant replies that no man in his hall would dare, but suggests the god first wrestle his old nurse, Elli, who, he says, has thrown men no less strong than Thor.

An old woman enters the hall and the match begins. The harder Thor grips, the firmer she stands. Eventually she manages to make him lose his balance, and Thor ends up on one knee. The giant then calls a halt to the contest.

When Thor and his companions are safely outside the hall, Útgarða-Loki reveals the deception: his opponent was far more formidable than she appeared, and the fact that Thor lasted so long was astonishing. In the story, old age eventually overcomes everyone who reaches it, and no one can fully escape it. The myth rests on the idea that not even the gods are immune to aging, since they must regularly eat Iðunn's apples to stay young.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson recounts her in the Prose Edda, in the book Gylfaginning; she is not mentioned in any other known ancient source.

Trivia

An old woman against a god. Thor, one of the most powerful gods of the Norse pantheon, could not defeat an old woman in the wrestling match and only fell to one knee.
Iðunn's apples. According to the story, not even the gods escape old age, and they need Iðunn's apples to keep their youth.
