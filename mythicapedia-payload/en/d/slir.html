river of the land of the dead in Norse mythology

Sliðr

Sliðr is a river in Hel, the land of the dead in Norse mythology. According to the Eddas, its waters are full of knives and swords.

The myth

In Norse mythology, the Sliðr flows through Hel, the land of the dead. According to the Eddas, its waters come from glaciers pouring in from the freezing well of Hvergelmir, which sends cold into the realm of the dead.

The river is no welcoming place: its current carries knives and swords, and it winds through so-called “valleys of venom”, a landscape of constant threat. The account thus portrays a hostile, desolate realm where even the water is a danger.

The sources do not explain in detail what role the river plays in the lives of the dead, so its function remains suggested rather than defined. The image of a cutting river fits the Norse view of the afterlife as a cold and perilous territory.

Ancient sources: The Eddas, which describe the river and its cutting waters in the realm of Hel.
