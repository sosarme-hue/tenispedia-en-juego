Greek personification of Invention

Euresis

Euresis, also written Heuresis, is a figure of Greek mythology who represents Invention and appears in literature as a nymph or goddess. Her best-known image comes from a manuscript of Dioscorides' medical work, where she offers a mandrake root.

The myth

According to tradition, the mandrake was thought to have a human shape, since its roots looked like two legs, and it was regarded as a remedy that could kill and was linked to magical rituals. Its scream was said to drive anyone who heard it mad, so the myth held that a black dog should pull it up, and the dog would die in the process.

In the illustration, Euresis appears as a child who presents this root to a bearded man seated on an elegant throne, who stretches out his arm to take it. At the man's feet lies the dead black dog.

Legacy

The only known image of Euresis is found in a manuscript of Dioscorides' De materia medica, a work by a military surgeon of the Roman Empire that is considered one of the most influential pharmacological texts of antiquity. In English she is also referred to as Discovery.

Trivia

Root with a human shape. The mandrake was described as a plant with human features, since its roots resembled a pair of legs.
A dog to pull it up. According to the legends, only a black dog could pull up the mandrake without losing its mind, and the animal died afterwards.
