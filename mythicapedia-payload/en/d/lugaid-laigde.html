legendary high king of Ireland

Lugaid Laigde

Lugaid Laigde, son of Eochu mac Ailella, is in medieval Irish tradition a High King of Ireland who gained the throne by killing his predecessor. His figure blends legend and chronicle, and medieval writers used him to order Ireland's early kings.

The myth

According to tradition, Lugaid helped Dui Ladrach overthrow Airgetmar, but later killed Dui to take the crown himself. He ruled for seven years until Áed Rúad, Airgetmar's grandson, killed him.

The medieval Lebor Gabála synchronises his reign with that of Artaxerxes III of Persia, while Geoffrey Keating's Foras Feasa ar Éirinn dates it to 537-530 BC and the Annals of the Four Masters to 738-731 BC. Keating also recounts how Lugaid, while hunting, met a hideous hag wearing a magic mask; when he slept with her he removed the mask and dreamed she became a beautiful young woman, an allegory, in Keating's reading, of Ireland's toil and later pleasure.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Lebor Gabála and in Geoffrey Keating's Foras Feasa ar Éirinn, as well as in the Annals of the Four Masters.

Trivia

Two Lugaids. The Cóir Anmann tells of Lugaid Loígde, one of five sons of Dáire Doimthech, all named Lugaid. A druid prophesied that one would rule, and the winner was the son who caught a fawn appearing at Tailtiu. Keating doubts this is the same man, since he has a different father.
