Lithuanian goddess of forests, trees and animals

Medeina

Medeina (or Medeinė) is one of the main deities of Lithuanian mythology, mistress of forests, trees and animals. She resembles the Latvian Meža māte and, in her traits, Artemis and Diana.

The myth

According to tradition, Medeina rules the forests, trees and animals, and her sacred animal is the hare. Her name probably comes from Lithuanian words meaning «tree» (medė) or «forest».

According to the research of Algirdas Julius Greimas, Medeina is a beautiful and seductive huntress who remains single and unwilling to marry. She is depicted as a young woman and also as a she-wolf, accompanied by wolves. Greimas describes her as having both divine and demonic traits. Her duty is not to help hunters but to protect the forest.

Worship and religion

In eastern Lithuania, the former Duchy of Lithuania, Vykintas Vaitkevičius identified five «Hare Churches» (sacred stones, hills and forests) and ten «Wolf-footprints» (stones with hollows shaped like footprints) linked to her cult. After the baptism of Lithuania, the cult declined.

Legacy

In the official pantheon, Medeina represented the military interests of warriors, but she was later replaced by Žemyna, goddess of the earth linked to peasants. In the 15th century Jan Długosz compared her with Diana, and her name appears in texts by Jan Łasicki, Mikalojus Daukša and the Bychowiec Chronicle.

Ancient sources: A Slavic codex describing events of 1252 mentions pagan gods still worshipped by King Mindaugas, including Medeina and an unnamed hare goddess. The Slavic transcription of John Malalas' Chronicle, dated 1261, mentions Žvorūna and three other gods.

Trivia

A Lithuanian Diana. Jan Długosz compared Medeina with the Roman goddess Diana, and she was sometimes also called Diana.
Stones shaped like footprints. The «Wolf-footprints» are stones with hollows that resemble a footprint and are linked to the cult of Medeina.
