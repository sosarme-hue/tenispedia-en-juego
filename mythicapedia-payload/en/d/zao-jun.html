Chinese kitchen god

Zao Jun

Zao Jun, also called Zao Shen or the Kitchen God, is the most important of the many domestic deities that protect the hearth and family in Chinese folk religion. He matters because he watches over the conduct of each household and reports it yearly to the Jade Emperor.

The myth

According to the most popular version, which dates back to around the 2nd century BC, Zao Jun was a mortal named Zhang Lang. He left his virtuous wife for a younger woman and, as punishment, went blind and was abandoned. Begging for food, he arrived at the house of his former wife, who fed him and cared for him without recognizing him. When he confessed and apologized, his sight was restored, but out of shame he threw himself into the kitchen hearth. She managed to save only one of his legs and built a shrine above the fire in his memory.

Another version says he was so poor that he sold his wife. Later he became a servant in her new husband's house and, unaware, sold some cakes in which she had hidden money. When he realized his mistake, he took his own life. In both tales Heaven takes pity on him, makes him the god of the kitchen and reunites him with his wife.

A third tradition from the Han dynasty tells of a poor farmer, Yin Zifang, who saw the god during the Lunar New Year, sacrificed his only yellow sheep and became rich.

Worship and religion

Traditionally, every Chinese household kept a paper image or plaque of Zao Jun and his wife above the kitchen fireplace. On the twenty-third or twenty-fourth day of the twelfth lunar month, depending on the region and profession, offerings of food and incense are made before he returns to Heaven. Honey is sometimes smeared on the image's lips to sweeten his report. The image is then burned and replaced at New Year. The custom remains widespread, and the god is also venerated in Vietnam.

Legacy

In China, the iron poker used to stir the fire is sometimes called "Zhang Lang's Leg".

Trivia

Honey on the lips. Before he returns to Heaven, the paper image of Zao Jun is sometimes smeared with honey on its lips, to sweeten his report or keep his mouth shut.
One rescued leg. In the Zhang Lang legend, his former wife could only save one of his legs from the fire.
