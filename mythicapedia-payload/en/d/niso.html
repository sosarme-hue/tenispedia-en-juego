king of Megara in Greek mythology

Nisus

Nisus was a king of Megara in Greek mythology and the grandfather of Bellerophon. His story, centred on a lock of purple hair that protected him and on the betrayal by his daughter Scylla, is one of the best-known tales of the rivalry between Megara and Crete.

The myth

According to tradition, Nisus was one of the four sons of Pandion II, king of Athens, and Pylia. Sources disagree about his father: some make him a son of Ares, others of a man named Deion. After Pandion's death, Nisus and his brothers Aegeas, Pallas and Lykos drove out the sons of Metion, restored their family's power and divided the government into four. Aegeas received Athens and Nisus became king of Megara.

Nisus was married to Abrota, and after her death he ordered the women of Megara to dress as she had. His daughters included Eurynome, who bore Bellerophon to Poseidon, and Iphinoe, who married her maternal uncle Megareus. The third, Scylla, brought about her father's downfall. When Minos, king of Crete, attacked Megara during a war with Athens, Nisus was kept safe by a lock of purple hair. Eros made Scylla fall in love with Minos, and in one version she cut off the lock and gave it to him. Minos was disgusted by her betrayal and sailed away, but Scylla tried to climb aboard one of his ships. Nisus, transformed into a sea eagle, attacked her, and his daughter was also turned into a bird. Another account says Nisus killed himself after losing the hair that was his lifeline.

Ancient sources: Hyginus and other ancient authors record his genealogy, and his story appears in accounts of Megara and Athens.

Trivia

A magical lock. His strength lay in a lock of purple hair. As long as he kept it, no harm could reach him.
Father of Bellerophon's mother. His daughter Eurynome bore Bellerophon to Poseidon, the famous hero who tamed Pegasus.
