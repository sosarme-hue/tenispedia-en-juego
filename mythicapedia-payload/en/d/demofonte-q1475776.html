Demophon

Demophon was the son of King Celeus of Eleusis and Queen Metanira, and a central figure in the story that explains the origins of the Eleusinian rites. His myth shows Demeter's failed attempt to grant him immortality.

The myth

According to the myth, Demeter arrived at Eleusis while searching for her daughter Persephone, who had been abducted by Hades. Disguised as an old woman, she was welcomed into King Celeus's palace and offered to nurse the infant Demophon. At night she anointed him with ambrosia, breathed sweetly upon him and placed him in the fire to burn away his mortal part, all without his parents' knowledge.

One night Metanira walked in, saw her son amid the flames and screamed. The goddess, angered, rebuked mortals for their folly and left the palace, so Demophon remained mortal. Some authors say he was consumed by the fire.

In the Homeric Hymn to Demeter, Demophon never gained immortality but received a lasting honour: each year the sons of the Eleusinians fought a ritual battle in his memory. Some traditions make him the elder brother of Triptolemus, whom Demeter later taught the art of agriculture.

Worship and religion

At Eleusis, the ritual battle in Demophon's honour was part of a set of funeral games known as the Eleusinian Games, held on a regular basis and linked to the cult of Demeter.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Demeter describes his upbringing with the goddess and the episode of the fire in detail.

Trivia

A ritual battle. The Ballêtus was a mock battle held at Eleusis in memory of Demophon.
