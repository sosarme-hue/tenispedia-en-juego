chef of the gods and the einherjar in Norse mythology

Andhrimnir

Andhrimnir is the chef of the Æsir and the einherjar, the fallen warriors who dwell in Valhalla, in Norse mythology. Although his role is domestic, his daily work places him at the heart of life in Odin's great hall.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, every day Andhrimnir slaughtered the cosmic boar Sæhrimnir and cooked it in Eldhrimnir, his cauldron of magical powers, to feed the inhabitants of Valhalla. The most striking feature of the story is that every night the boar came back to life, so that it could be eaten again the next day, in a cycle without end.

Andhrimnir also brewed the mead of the Æsir from the milk of Heiðrún, the goat who, according to the accounts, fed on the branches of Valhalla. In this way, the cook was responsible for both the solid food and the drink of the gods.

The sources say little more about him: his origins, family and fate are not recorded. He remains an almost anonymous servant in the divine world, and his everyday job, repeated day after day, gives the story a homely tone that is unusual in mythology.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Poetic Edda, in the poem Grímnismál, which describes his work in Valhalla.

Trivia

A name meaning soot. His Old Norse name means 'the one exposed to soot', formed from the elements and- and hrīm.
A cauldron with its own name. The cauldron in which he cooks is called Eldhrimnir, and it is as well known as the cook himself.
