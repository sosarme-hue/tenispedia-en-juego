Sumerian goddess, wife of Ningishzida

Azimua

Azimua, also known as Ninazimua, is a goddess of Sumerian Mesopotamia regarded as the wife of the god Ningishzida. She matters mainly for her role in the story of her husband's temporary death.

The myth

According to the myth known as Ningishzida and Ninazimua, the goddess exchanges messages with her husband, who has died temporarily. The story survives in very few copies, some with possible scribal errors, so its full plot cannot be reconstructed today.

The sources do not agree on the god's marriage. God lists also name Ekurritum or Geshtinanna as his wives, and at times Azimua and Geshtinanna are confused with one another. According to a list from Susa, they are two names of the same deity, the sister of Dumuzi. Azimua was also said to serve as scribe of the underworld.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in Ur, where a temple dedicated to her stood, and also in Umma. Her name may appear in very early texts from Tell Fara, though the full name of the deity in them has been lost.

Ancient sources: God lists such as An = Anum and a list from Susa, along with the myth of Ningishzida and Ninazimua, preserved in few copies.

Trivia

Possible meaning of the name. Wilfred G. Lambert proposed that the element a-zi in her name could mean «water of life».
Confusion with Geshtinanna. In inscriptions of King Gudea of Lagash, Azimua and Geshtinanna appear conflated as a single figure.
