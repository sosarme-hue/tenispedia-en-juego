Inuit god of the sea, death and the underworld

Torngarsuk

Torngarsuk is a god of Inuit religion, master of whales and seals and linked to the sea, death and the underworld. He is one of the most important deities in their pantheon, and his figure remains present in the imagination of the Arctic.

The myth

According to Inuit traditions from Greenland and northeastern Canada, Torngarsuk is the leader of the Tornat, a group of protective gods. Accounts of his form contradict each other: he appears as a bear, as a one-armed man, or as a great human figure like one finger of a hand. Only the angakkuit, the shamans, can see him.

Fishermen call on him, and he is also invoked when someone falls ill. Each angakkuq keeps a familiar spirit in a leather bottle, which seeks Torngarsuk in a cave and brings good fortune and healing power.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped with offerings in Greenland and northeastern Canada, mainly to ask for good hunting and health.

Legacy

H. P. Lovecraft mentions him in “The Call of Cthulhu”, where an Eskimo diabolist cult worships Cthulhu as an avatar of Torngarsuk. In Marvel Comics he is merged with Anguta and Tulugaak under the name Hodiak.

Trivia

A god in horror fiction. H. P. Lovecraft used the name in his story “The Call of Cthulhu”, though he presented it briefly within his own invented mythology.
Hodiak in Marvel. In Marvel Comics, Torngarsuk appears merged with other Inuit figures under the name Hodiak, chief deity of the northern gods.
