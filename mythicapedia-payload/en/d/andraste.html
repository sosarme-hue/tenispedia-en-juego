war goddess of the Iceni

Andraste

Andraste is a war goddess of the Iceni, a Celtic people of Roman Britain. According to Dio Cassius, Boudica invoked her in her revolt against the Roman occupation in AD 60.

The myth

According to the Roman historian Dio Cassius, Boudica called on Andraste before facing Rome. The same account describes a divination rite: Boudica released a hare from under her gown and read the omen from the direction in which it ran, a method similar to Roman augury from the flight of birds, where the left was unfavourable and the right favourable.

Her name has been rendered as 'indestructible' or 'unconquerable'. Dio later mentions Andate, which he says was their word for victory, and some scholars suggest it may be the same goddess, linked to the Roman Victoria, Nike or Bellona.

Legacy

Many modern neopagan sources regard the hare as sacred to Andraste, an idea that seems to be extrapolated from Dio Cassius's account.

Ancient sources: Dio Cassius, a Roman historian who recounts Boudica's revolt in AD 60.
