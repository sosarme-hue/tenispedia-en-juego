Irish god of the Tuatha Dé Danann, son of the Dagda

Midir

Midir is a figure of Irish mythology, son of the Dagda and a member of the Tuatha Dé Danann. He is the central male character of the tale Tochmarc Étaíne, one of the best-known stories of the Irish Mythological Cycle, in which his love for Étaín sets off a chain of transformations and losses.

The myth

According to the tradition, Midir lived in the sidh of Brí Léith after the Milesians defeated the Tuatha Dé Danann. He was married to Fuamnach, but fell in love with Étaín and, with the help of his foster-son Aengus, made her his new bride. Fuamnach took revenge by turning Étaín into water, a worm and a fly, until she was reborn after drinking from another woman's cup.

Étaín later married Eochaid Airem, High King of Ireland. Midir challenged the king to several games of fidchell and, after winning the last one, asked for a kiss from Étaín as his prize. In the end he flew off with her in the form of swans through the smoke hole. Eochaid pursued them to the sidh, where Midir offered him a choice among fifty women resembling Étaín; the king mistakenly chose his own daughter and lost his wife for good.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources place him in the Lebor Gabála and the Tochmarc Étaíne, and he also appears in the Ulster Cycle and the Metrical Dindsenchas.

Trivia

A wounded eye. An old story tells that Midir lost an eye in a quarrel between groups of youths, and that Dian Cecht or Étaín healed it.
A video game. The hidden boss Darkeater Midir appears in the second DLC of Dark Souls III, named after this figure.
