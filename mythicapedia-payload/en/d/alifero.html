Arcadian prince, son of Lycaon

Alipherus

Alipherus (or Halipherus) is an Arcadian prince in Greek mythology, one of the fifty sons of the impious king Lycaon. He is traditionally credited with founding the town of Aliphera in Arcadia, which is said to have taken its name from him.

The myth

According to myth, Alipherus and his brothers were notorious for their wickedness and their careless disregard of the gods. To test them, Zeus visited their home disguised as a peasant. Convinced by signs of the stranger's divinity, the brothers decided to find out whether he was truly a god: they killed one of their own, Nyctimus, and served his flesh mixed with animal entrails.

Zeus recognised the abomination at once and flung the meal off the table. Accounts differ on the punishment: some say he restored Nyctimus to life and struck the guilty brothers with lightning, while others say he turned them into wolves. In one version, Alipherus perished by the god's bolt along with his brothers and their father.

Trivia

Fifty brothers. Alipherus was one of the fifty sons of Lycaon, though sources give different mothers: the naiad Cyllene, the naiad Nonacris, or an unknown woman.
A town named after him. Tradition credited this figure with founding Aliphera in Greece and derived the town's name from him.
