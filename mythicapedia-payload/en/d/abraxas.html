Mystical word and name of the Great Archon in Gnosticism

Abraxas

Abraxas is a word of uncertain origin that Gnostic followers of Basilides used for the "Great Archon," ruler of the 365 heavens. Its fame owes much to the Abraxas stones, engraved gems worn as amulets or charms.

The myth

According to the system described by Irenaeus, the Unbegotten Father produced Nous, Nous produced Logos, and so on, until Sophia and Dynamis gave rise to principalities, powers and angels. Each series created a new heaven until there were 365, and the angels of the last, visible heaven were said to be the makers of our world. The great archon of those heavens is Abraxas, and for this reason he contains the number 365 within himself.

Hippolytus of Rome records that the Basilidians gave him this name because its letters add up to 365, the days of the year. According to Epiphanius he was the power above all and the first principle, and the Pseudo-Tertullian claims that the world and the heavens were created in his honour. In the Nag Hammadi texts he appears lower in the hierarchy: the Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit mentions him as an aeon, and his identity remains unclear.

Art shows him as a cock-headed anguiped, with serpents in place of legs, a whip in his hand and a shield bearing the name IAO. He is often linked with Osiris, Harpocrates, Pantheus and Hecate. Modern scholars have proposed very different origins for the name, from Egyptian or Hebrew roots to a link with Abracadabra, which remains unproven. Some have identified him with Mithras or the Sun, and his seven letters have been related to the seven classical planets.

Legacy

Hermann Hesse made him a god who joins the divine with the infernal in his novel Demian, and much of the plot revolves around him. In the Harry Potter series, Abraxas Malfoy is Draco's paternal grandfather. Carlos Santana released an album called Abraxas in 1970, and the Swedish band Therion wrote a song of the same name in 2004. He also appears in video games such as Genshin Impact and MapleStory, and the series The Foundation mentions the Abraxas Conjecture.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned by heresiologists such as Irenaeus, Hippolytus of Rome, Epiphanius and Jerome, as well as in the Gnostic Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit and the Greek Magical Papyri.

Trivia

A name made of numbers. In the Greek numerical system, the letters of Abraxas add up to 365, the number of days in the solar year.
Surviving stones. About forty Abraxas stones are held by the Cabinet des Médailles of the French National Library.
