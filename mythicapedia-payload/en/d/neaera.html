minor Greek goddess, lover of Helios

Neaera

Neaera is a minor goddess in Greek mythology, a lover of Helios, the sun god, and the mother by him of Phaethusa and Lampetia. Her own figure barely appears, but her name and those of her daughters link her to the episode of the Oxen of the Sun.

The myth

In Homer's Odyssey, Circe tells Odysseus that Neaera raised her daughters and then sent them to the island of Thrinacia, where Helios kept his sacred cattle, to tend their father's flocks. Homer calls her "divine" without giving her parentage. Hesychius of Alexandria wrote that Neaera was also the name of an Oceanid nymph, though it is unclear whether this is the same figure.

Her name, roughly "the younger," suits both Helios and his daughters, since the sun seems young again each morning.

Ancient sources: She appears in Homer's Odyssey, where Circe mentions her, and in the lexicon of Hesychius of Alexandria.

Trivia

A name meaning "young". Neaera's name is linked to the idea of youth, as are those of her daughters, alluding to the sun that is reborn each morning.
A possible namesake. Hesychius of Alexandria links her to an Oceanid of the same name, but the sources do not clarify whether it is the same figure.
