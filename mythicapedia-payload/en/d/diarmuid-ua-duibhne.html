semi-divine hero of Irish Fenian Cycle

Diarmuid Ua Duibhne

Diarmuid Ua Duibhne, also known as Diarmuid of the Love Spot, is a hero and demigod of the Fenian Cycle of Irish mythology. He is best remembered as the lover of Gráinne, the intended wife of Fionn mac Cumhaill.

The myth

Son of Donn and, in most versions, Cochrann, he was raised by Aengus Óg, who gave him the sword Moralltach. A woman who embodied youth marked him with a love spot near his right eye, making every woman who saw him fall instantly in love with him.

Gráinne fled with him rather than marry Fionn, and Aengus persuaded Fionn to forgive him. Years later, a giant boar mortally wounded him on Ben Gulbain. Fionn let the healing water slip through his fingers, and Aengus carried the body to Brugh, where he brought Diarmuid back to life whenever he wished to talk with him.

Legacy

The Scottish Clan Campbell claims him as its founder, and its crest bears a boar's head, a reference to his death. In 1999 he was a character in the Irish dance show Dancing on Dangerous Ground.

Trivia

A boar on a crest. The Campbell coat of arms shows a boar's head, which is read as a nod to Diarmuid's death.
A fatal taboo. According to some accounts, his son Eachtach died after breaking a taboo tied to a geis, as his father had.
