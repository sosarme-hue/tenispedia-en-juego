Greek primordial god of darkness

Erebus

Erebus is, in Greek mythology, the primordial personification of darkness, the gloom that spreads after sunset. The name also designates the underworld region through which the shades of the dead pass, so he matters both as a cosmic force and as a place of the afterlife.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Erebus arises from Chaos together with Nyx (Night). With her he begets Aether and Hemera (Day), who stand as the personified opposites of their parents. Other authors give him different offspring: Acusilaus makes him the father of Aether, Eros and Metis, Aristophanes places him among the first beings beside Chaos, Night and Tartarus, and an Orphic theogony has him born of Chronos (Time), in serpent form.

Roman writers expanded his family the most. Cicero credits Erebus and Nox with a long roll call of abstractions, from Love and Fear to Old Age, Death and Dreams, while Hyginus adds the Parcae, the Hesperides and Nemesis. Boccaccio even gives him twenty-one children.

As a place, the name appears mostly in epic. Homer describes souls gathering out of Erebus at the shore of Oceanus, and from there Heracles must fetch Cerberus. In the Theogony Zeus casts the Titan Menoetius into its depths, and in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter it is the abode of Hades and Persephone. Euripides places the home of Nyx there, and Ovid calls Proserpina the 'queen of Erebus'. Over time the word became a synonym for Hades, Tartarus or the underworld itself.

Legacy

Erebus has found new life in modern popular culture. In the young-adult saga Percy Jackson and the Olympians, a sign at the entrance of the Underworld reads 'Welcome to Erebus'. In the DC Comics miniseries DCeased, he is the being behind the Anti-Life Equation and the main villain. The God of War saga features an enemy called the Minotaur of Erebus and a section titled 'Trials of Erebus', while in Persona 3 he is the main antagonist of the bonus chapter The Answer. In Hades II, the protagonist must cross Erebus as the first zone of the game.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, and works by Aristophanes, Cicero, Hyginus, Ovid and Euripides mention him.

Trivia

A word with distant relatives. Erebus is etymologically linked to words in Sanskrit, Armenian and Old Norse, all tied to darkness or evening.
A wind egg. In Aristophanes' comedy The Birds, Night lays a wind-egg in the boundless bosom of Erebus, from which golden-winged Eros springs.
