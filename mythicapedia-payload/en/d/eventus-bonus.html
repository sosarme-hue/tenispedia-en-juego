Roman god of agriculture and good outcomes

Bonus Eventus

Bonus Eventus, or Eventus Bonus, is a figure of ancient Roman religion linked at first to agriculture and later to the broader idea of success. His image appears often on coins, gems and other everyday objects.

The myth

According to ancient sources, Bonus Eventus was a benevolent deity who distributed goods and brought good omens to the fields. Over time, his name, meaning "good outcome", came to stand for good moments in general, so he became more a personification than a god with his own stories.

Varro lists him among the deities who presided over agriculture, paired with Lympha, the goddess of the water supply. Pliny the Elder mentions statues in the Capitol that were in fact renamed images of Greek gods, one a marble by Praxiteles that stood beside a statue of Bona Fortuna.

Worship and religion

His first altars stood in the fields, and farmers made vows to him for a good harvest. He was invoked only for particular matters, unlike Fortuna, who was believed to influence every event of life. According to the sources, a horse was sacrificed to him on the Ides of October. He had a temple in the Campus Martius, mentioned by Ammianus Marcellinus, and a mosaic in Roman Britain reminds worshippers to honour him.

Legacy

His image, nude and holding grain and poppies, appeared on coins of Galba, Vespasian, Titus, Antoninus Pius and Septimius Severus. He also appears often on engraved gems, suggesting a protective role for his devotees.

Ancient sources: Varro names him among the agricultural deities, Pliny the Elder describes the Capitol statues, and Ammianus Marcellinus records his temple in the Campus Martius.

Trivia

A statue with another name. Pliny describes statues called Bonus Eventus that were actually renamed images of Greek gods, one by Praxiteles.
Gems and seals. In a jeweller's hoard from Snettisham, Bonus Eventus was the most common motif on intaglios, appearing on 25 percent of the 127 found.
