Titan of Arcadian Greek myth

Anytos

Anytos is a Titan cited by Pausanias as part of a local Arcadian legend, in which he was said to have raised Despoina. His cult was prominent in Lycosura, and fragments of his statue are preserved in the National Archaeological Museum of Athens.

The myth

According to the legend the Arcadians told Pausanias, Anytos was one of the Titans and the one who raised Despoina, a deity linked to the mysteries of Arcadia. Pausanias does not name his parents and implicitly links him to the Curetes, who were also represented in the sanctuary.

Pausanias notes that Homer was the first to bring the Titans into poetry, describing them as gods in the so-called Tartarus. Later, Onomacritus took the name of the Titans from Homer, composed a mystery festival for Dionysus, and made the Titans responsible for the god's sufferings.

Worship and religion

The cult of Anytos was prominent in Lycosura, home to the Temple of Despoina, dedicated to a chthonic pantheon of Artemis, Demeter and Despoina. There he was shown beside Despoina in an acrolith statue carved around 180 BC by Damophon, with head and limbs of local marble and a wooden body. Anytos appeared as an armed man standing beside the image of Despoina.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, is the main source mentioning him; Homer is also cited as the reference point for the Titans.
