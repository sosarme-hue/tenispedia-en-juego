nymph of Samos, daughter of the river-god Imbrasus

Ocyrhoë

Ocyrhoë is a nymph from the Greek island of Samos, daughter of the river-god Imbrasus. Her story of pursuit by Apollo survives through references by authors of the Roman imperial era.

The myth

Ocyrhoë is a young nymph of Samos, daughter of the river-god Imbrasus and the nymph Chesias. The Horae gave her great beauty. Apollo fell in love with her and tried to seize her at a festival of Artemis in Miletus, across the strait.

Fearing him, she fled to Pompilus, an old friend of her father's and a ferryman, who agreed to row her back to Samos. When they reached the island, Apollo was waiting on the shore. He carried her off, turned the boat to stone and transformed Pompilus into a pilot fish for trying to thwart him.

Worship and religion

Chesia and Imbrasia were cult epithets of Artemis and Hera, the patron goddess of Samos. Near the source of the river Imbrasus, in the eastern lowlands, stood a shrine to Apollo Nymphegetes, 'leader of the nymphs'.

Ancient sources: Athenaeus (Deipnosophistae) and Aelian (On the Nature of Animals) record the tale, which both attribute to Apollonius of Rhodes.

Trivia

Origin of the pilot fish. According to the tale, the sailor Pompilus was transformed into a pilot fish, a marine species known today as Naucrates ductor.
A blend of genres. Because it combines myth with Greek romance, the story is considered an unusual mixture within mythology.
