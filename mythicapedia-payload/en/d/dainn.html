dwarf of Norse mythology

Dáinn

Dáinn is a dwarf in Norse mythology, whose name means 'dead' in Old Norse. Some traditions present him instead as a king of the elves.

The myth

According to the Hyndluljóð, Dáinn made Freyja's magical boar Hildisvíni together with his brother Nabbi. The Völuspá lists him among the dwarfs, while the Hávamál calls him an elf who carved runes. He is also one of four deer living beneath the branches of Yggdrasil.

King Högni's sword, Dáinsleif ('Dáinn's legacy'), carries a curse: it must kill a man each time it is drawn, and its wounds never heal.

Legacy

A constellation shaped like a deer bears his name and is considered an elven name. J. R. R. Tolkien later reimagined him as Dáin II Ironfoot, a dwarf king who helps his cousin Thorin Oakenshield at the Battle of the Five Armies in The Hobbit.

Ancient sources: The Hyndluljóð, Völuspá and Hávamál of the Poetic Edda, where he appears in different roles.

Trivia

Runes. In the Hávamál, Dáinn appears as an elf who carved runes, alongside Odin, Dvalin and Ásvid.
