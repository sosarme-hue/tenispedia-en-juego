personified demon of intoxication and pride in Hindu tradition

Mada

Mada is, in Hindu theology, one of the six enemies of the mind (arishadvarga), a form of madness, pride or intoxication that blocks the attainment of moksha. In the Mahabharata he appears personified as an asura, a demon of enormous size able to swallow the entire cosmos.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, the sage Chyavana regained his eyesight thanks to the Ashvinidevas, and in return they asked that the ban Indra had placed on drinking soma be lifted. Chyavana held a yajna and invited all the devas. Indra objected to the Ashvinidevas drinking soma and a fight broke out. Chyavana then called upon Mada, who rose out of the sacrificial fire. When Indra surrendered, the sage released Mada and tore him into four pieces.

The texts describe him as a being able to swallow the whole universe, and they count him among the krtya, the products of sorcery. His form is compared to Krishna's visvarupa at the climax of the Bhagavad Gita.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, which presents him as a personified asura and a product of sorcery.

Trivia

Cosmic form. His image is compared to Krishna's visvarupa, the revelation of the god's universal form in the Bhagavad Gita.
A violent end. After Indra surrendered, Chyavana tore him into four pieces, and that is how his appearance in the story ends.
