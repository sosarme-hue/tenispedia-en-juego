Eurydice of Thebes

Eurydice is the wife of Creon, king of Thebes, and the mother of several of his children, among them Haemon. Though her part is brief, she embodies the grief of a mother who loses her sons and sinks into despair.

The myth

According to the tradition, Eurydice was the wife of Creon and the mother of several of his children, among them Haemon and Megareus. Ancient sources do not fully agree on the number or names of her offspring. She is described as a loving mother who, after the death of Haemon, gradually withdrew from public life.

In Sophocles' Antigone she appears only briefly. When Creon imprisons Antigone for defying his order, a messenger tells Eurydice that Haemon and his betrothed have both taken their own lives. Eurydice then thrusts a sword into her body and, before dying, curses Creon for the deaths of her two sons.

Statius, in the Thebaid, tells a different scene. The seer Tiresias declares that the city will be saved if a son of Creon throws himself from the wall, and Menoeceus sacrifices himself in this way. When his body is carried through the streets, Eurydice rushes into the town, lamenting and reproaching her son, the gods and her own fate. Afterwards, shut away in her chamber, she tears at her face and pulls out her hair for months, until she loses her voice, turns away from the light and finally her reason.

Ancient sources: Sophocles mentions her in Antigone, and Statius describes her lament in the Thebaid.

Trivia

A meaningful name. Eurydice roughly means « wide justice », from the Greek words for « wide » and « justice ». Some sources also call her Henioche.
A mother in tragedy. In Sophocles' play, Eurydice serves as an older counterpart to Antigone, embodying a mother's grief set against the young woman's determination.
