Oceanid nymph of Greek mythology

Pasithoe

Pasithoe is one of the Oceanids, the three thousand water nymphs born to the Titans Oceanus and his sister and wife Tethys. She belongs to Greek mythology, and her interest lies in showing the vast family of water beings who, according to tradition, inhabited rivers, springs and seas.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Pasithoe was a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys, the Titans who gave rise to the rivers and the waters that surround the earth. She belonged to the large group of Oceanids, nymphs who, according to myth, dwelt in seas, lakes, springs and streams.

The best-known reference is Hesiod's Theogony, which lists the Oceanids in a long catalogue. There Pasithoe appears simply as one sister among many, and the poem gives her no deeds, loves or episodes of her own. She is known mainly through her lineage and her place within the great watery family of the Titans.

Ancient sources: Hesiod names her among the Oceanids in his Theogony.
