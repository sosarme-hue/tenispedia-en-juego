father-in-law of Ixion in Greek myth

Eioneus

Eioneus, also known as Ioneus or Deioneus, is a figure of Greek myth from Perrhaebia who became the father-in-law of Ixion. His story, preserved in fragments by ancient authors, centres on a deception that ended in murder.

The myth

According to the tradition, Eioneus was the father of Dia, whom Ixion married after promising her father many gifts as a bride price. Ixion never handed them over, so Eioneus kept his mares as a pledge.

Ixion then invited him to collect his payment, assuring him that he would meet all his demands. When Eioneus arrived, Ixion threw him into a fiery pit. The crime was so monstrous that no one would purify Ixion, until, according to the myths, Zeus purified him.

Ancient sources: Pindar and Diodorus Siculus mention him in connection with Ixion and the Lapiths, and a scholium links him with Magnes, eponym of the Magnesians.

Trivia

An uncertain kinship. Pindar says Ixion was the first to shed the blood of a kinsman, which has led some to suggest Eioneus may have been his brother.
A shifting genealogy. The name of Eioneus's wife is unknown, and the sources do not agree about his parents, since one scholium makes him a son of Magnes.
