Armenian warrior god, dragon-slayer

Vahagn

Vahagn is the warrior god of Armenian mythology, linked to war and victory and, according to scholars, to thunder, the sun or fire. His fame as a dragon-slayer made him one of the central figures of the pre-Christian Armenian religion.

The myth

According to tradition, Vahagn had a miraculous birth and, as an adult, fought the vishaps, dragons of Armenian myth, and defeated them. This gave him the title Vishapakagh, 'reaper of vishaps'. The historian Movses Khorenatsi records a song about his deeds, which were compared to those of Heracles.

In Khorenatsi's genealogy, Vahagn is a son of Tigranes, a mythologized king, and he married Astghik, goddess of beauty and personification of the Moon. Anania Shirakatsi, a seventh-century author, tells how Vahagn stole straw from Barsham and dropped it on his way back, creating the Milky Way.

Worship and religion

Vahagn was worshipped in a triad with Aramazd and Anahit. His chief temple, at Ashtishat on the slopes of Mount Karke, was served by priests from the noble house of the Vahevuni, who claimed descent from him. After Tiridates III converted to Christianity, Gregory the Illuminator destroyed the temple, and a church was built on its site. Today he is the central deity of Armenian neopaganism, and his birth festival, on 21 or 22 March, is held at the temple of Garni.

Legacy

Some scholars regard him as the national god of Armenia. Vahagn remains a very popular male given name and appears in poems such as Hovhannes Hovhannisyan's 1904 work. Several statues in Yerevan and a monumental one in the Lori province, erected in 1973, commemorate him. The Hercules constellation is named after him in Armenian, and the ancient Armenians called Mars Atraher, 'fire-hair', in his honour.

Ancient sources: Agathangelos, Movses Khorenatsi (History of Armenia) and Anania Shirakatsi, among other ancient Armenian authors.

Trivia

The Milky Way. According to Anania Shirakatsi, the Milky Way formed when Vahagn dropped the straw he had stolen. In Armenian it is popularly known as 'the way of the straw-thief'.
A god in a Japanese light novel. In the Japanese light novel series Campfire Cooking in Another World with My Absurd Skill, Vahagn appears as the God of War, wearing a skull helmet and covered in tattoos.
