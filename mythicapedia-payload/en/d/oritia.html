Athenian princess abducted by the north wind Boreas

Orithyia

Orithyia is a figure of Greek myth, a princess of Athens and daughter of Erechtheus and Praxithea, carried off by Boreas, the north wind. She became his wife in Thrace and mother of the winged twins Zetes and Calais. Her story explains the cold northern winds and the winged sons of the god.

The myth

In the myth, Orithyia was the daughter of King Erechtheus of Athens and Praxithea. While she played by the river Ilissos, the north wind Boreas fell in love with her. Her father refused the match, partly because of the cold of Thrace, the suitor's homeland, and the bad memory left by its kings in Athens. Boreas answered with violent whirlwinds, seized her and carried her off to Thrace, where she came to reign beside him.

Accounts differ on the place of the abduction. Apollonius places her at Sarpedon's Rock, near the river Erginos, where Boreas wrapped her in a cloud. With him she bore the winged twins Zetes and Calais, and the daughters Chione and Cleopatra. A late mythographic tradition adds a son, the eponym Haemus. Plato offered a rationalizing version in which she fell from the rocks in a gust of wind and was later said to have been taken by Boreas. Aeschylus wrote a satyr play about her, now lost. According to Quintus Smyrnaeus, she gave the Amazon Penthesilea a swift horse.

Worship and religion

Herodotus reports that during the Persian Wars the Athenians, waiting at Chalcis in Euboea, offered sacrifices to Boreas and Orithyia, asking them to destroy the barbarian fleet, as they had done earlier near Mount Athos. Her name has been linked by some scholars to mountain rites.

Legacy

The abduction of Orithyia was a popular subject in Baroque and Neoclassical painting, with works by Rubens, Solimena and Foggini, and it appears in later drawings, prints and porcelain.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, Plato, Apollonius of Rhodes and Quintus Smyrnaeus mention her, and Aeschylus wrote a now-lost satyr play about her.

Trivia

Winged sons. According to the myth, her sons Zetes and Calais were born with wings like their father Boreas and joined the Argonauts.
Abduction from the Areopagus. Plato records another version in which she was seized from the Areopagus, a rock near the Acropolis where murderers were tried.
