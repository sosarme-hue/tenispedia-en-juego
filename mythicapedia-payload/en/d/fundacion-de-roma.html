founding myth of the city of Rome

Founding of Rome

The founding of Rome is the tale Roman tradition used to explain the origins of its city: Romulus, son of Mars, founded it after the death of his twin brother Remus. It is the most widespread origin myth of Rome and the one that links the city to the Trojan hero Aeneas.

The myth

According to the main version, Trojan survivors reached the shores of Latium led by the hero Aeneas. His son Ascanius, also called Iulus, founded Alba Longa, and after a long line of kings the lineage reached Rhea Silvia, a descendant of Aeneas, who bore the twins Romulus and Remus to the god Mars. Abandoned on the Tiber, they were suckled by a she-wolf at the Lupercal and raised by the shepherd Faustulus.

As adults, the brothers overthrew the usurper Amulius and restored the throne to their grandfather Numitor. They then decided to found a city where they had been found, but quarrelled over the site and over who would lead it. Romulus chose the Palatine and Remus the Aventine, and each claimed the gods favoured him with omens. During the tracing of the wall, Remus leapt over the furrow, a sacrilege that led to his death at his brother's hands. Romulus became the first king of Rome and vanished in a storm, carried off by his father Mars.

Modern historians regard the story as fiction. Excavations show the area was inhabited since the Bronze Age and that several hilltop villages merged around the 8th century BC.

Worship and religion

The Parilia, held on 21 April, was regarded in the late Republic as commemorating the anniversary of the city's founding; under the Empire its importance grew until, in AD 121, it was fully transformed into the Romaea. Earlier, on 11 December, the septimontium was celebrated, which may originally have referred to federations of villages rather than to the founding. These celebrations belong to the past and have no living cult.

Legacy

The story was reworked by Virgil in the Aeneid, which unified it into a coherent narrative. The date of 753 BC, fixed by Varro's chronology, remains the basis of modern calculations of the ab urbe condita era.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid and the work of Dionysius of Halicarnassus, which records the line of kings of Alba Longa linking Ascanius with Romulus.

Trivia

A sacred tree. The spear Romulus threw onto the Palatine was said to have turned into the Corniolus, the sacred tree of Rome.
Two possible dates. Ancient chroniclers gave different dates for the founding; the two official ones were 753 and 752 BC, and the first still marks the modern count.
