Egyptian goddess of the west and the necropolises

Imentet

Imentet, whose name means "She of the West", was an ancient Egyptian goddess representing the necropolises west of the Nile, where the dead were welcomed. Her title refers both to geography and to her role, as she accompanied the setting sun and death. Although she was never officially worshipped, she appears in hymns and passages of the Book of the Dead.

The myth

According to the tradition, Imentet lived in a tree at the edge of the desert, looking toward the entrance to the Duat, the underworld. Her main task was to offer food and drink to the newly dead, restoring their spirits enough to reach the Field of Reeds, which in Egyptian belief amounted to a paradise. She was also considered a minor fertility goddess.

Her role in the afterlife linked her so closely with Hathor and Isis that she may not have been an independent deity, but an alternate form of those two goddesses. Some accounts make her the daughter of Hathor and Horus. She was shown as a woman dressed as a queen, holding a sceptre and an ankh, with the hieroglyph for "west" on her head: a feather or a falcon above a loaf of bread and a folded cloth. Painted on tomb walls, she welcomed the deceased; on coffins she might appear winged or as a kite, because of her link with Isis and Nephthys.

Worship and religion

She was never officially worshipped, and the sources offer no details on popular devotion.

Ancient sources: She appears in various hymns and passages of the Book of the Dead.

Trivia

A goddess in the form of a kite. On some coffins she appears as a kite, a bird of prey that linked her to Isis and Nephthys.
The hieroglyph for west. She wore on her head the sign for west, a feather or a falcon above a loaf of bread and a folded cloth.
