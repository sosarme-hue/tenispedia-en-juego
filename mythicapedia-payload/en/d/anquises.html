Trojan prince, father of Aeneas and lover of Aphrodite

Anchises

Anchises is a Trojan prince of Greek mythology, the father of the hero Aeneas after his union with the goddess Aphrodite. He becomes especially prominent in Virgil's Aeneid, where he accompanies his son in the escape from Troy and on the journey toward Italy.

The myth

According to the tradition, Anchises belonged to the royal house of Troy. He was the son of King Capys and Themiste, daughter of Ilus. While he was herding his cattle on the slopes of Mount Ida, Aphrodite appeared to him disguised as a Phrygian princess, sent by Zeus in some versions. The two made love, and the goddess told him they would have a son, Aeneas, who would be raised by nymphs until the age of five.

Anchises, however, boasted of the affair to his companions, perhaps after drinking. Aphrodite had warned him that Zeus would punish him if he revealed it, and in some accounts the god struck him with a thunderbolt. Some versions say he was killed, others that he was left crippled for life, since the goddess partly deflected the blow. The Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite tells the seduction in detail.

Anchises had other children, including Lyrus with Aphrodite and more with the mortal Eriopis, according to the scholiasts. Homer also names his daughter Hippodamia, wife of Alcathous. In the Aeneid, he is an old man when Troy burns, and Aeneas carries him out on his back, with young Ascanius at his side, while Aeneas' wife Creusa is lost during the flight. Along the journey his advice guides the Trojans. He dies in Sicily, and in the underworld Aeneas sees him again in the Elysian Fields.

Legacy

The rescue of Anchises has been a recurring subject in painting, and a famous version by Federico Barocci hangs in the Galleria Borghese in Rome. Shakespeare alludes to the episode in Julius Caesar, when Cassius tries to persuade Brutus to murder Caesar. His lasting presence owes most to the Aeneid, which made him one of the most memorable figures of Latin epic.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Aphrodite, Homer's Iliad, Virgil's Aeneid, and the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus.

Trivia

A father who returns from the underworld. In the Aeneid, Aeneas sees his father again in the Elysian Fields, where Anchises encourages him to continue his journey to Italy.
A namesake in the Iliad. Homer's Iliad mentions another Anchises, a wealthy man from Sicyon and father of Echepolus.
