daughter of Shukra, preceptor of the asuras

Devayani

Devayani is a character in Hindu literature, the daughter of Shukra, preceptor of the asuras. Her story with the young Kacha and with King Yayati makes her one of the most memorable female figures in these tales, where love, jealousy and curses are closely intertwined.

The myth

According to the tradition, Devayani fell in love with Kacha, the son of Brihaspati, who had been sent by the devas to learn from Shukra the Mṛtasañjīvanī mantra, which restores life. The asuras, suspicious of his intentions, killed him on three occasions. Shukra revived him at his daughter's pleading in the first two, and in the third, after Kacha had ended up inside his belly, brought him back from within using the mantra. When his studies were complete, Kacha turned down Devayani's proposal of marriage, saying that she was like a sister to him. Wounded, Devayani cursed him never to be able to use the mantra, and Kacha cursed her so that no son of a sage would ever marry her.

Later, Devayani became friends with Sharmishtha, a princess of the asuras and daughter of King Vrishaparvan. After a bitter quarrel by a forest stream, Sharmishtha threw her into a well and left her for dead. She was rescued by King Yayati, of the chandravamsha dynasty, whom she eventually married. She had two sons with him, Yadu and Turvasu, but Yayati also fathered three sons with Sharmishtha. When Devayani discovered this, she left him and returned to her father's house, and Shukra cursed his son-in-law with premature old age. Yayati exchanged his old age for the youth of his youngest son, Puru, and after a thousand years of pleasure he returned the curse and retired to the forest with his two wives.

Trivia

Three deaths. The asuras killed Kacha three times: they fed him to wolves, pounded his body into paste mixed with seawater, and in the third attempt burned him and gave his ashes, mixed with wine, to Shukra to drink.
Clothes taken by Indra. In the story, Indra takes the form of a wind that blows away the garments of Devayani and Sharmishtha by the stream, and when they dress in each other's saris, the quarrel between them begins.
