Sybaris

Sybaris, also called Lamia, is a gigantic beast that, according to Greek legend, lived in a cave on Mount Cirphis, near Krisa and Delphi. Her story explains the origin of a spring and the name of the Italian city of Sybaris.

The myth

According to the Greek tradition recorded by Antoninus Liberalis, an enormous creature lived in a cave on Mount Cirphis and emerged every day to prey on livestock and people. The locals asked the Oracle of Delphi for help, and the answer was that a young man must be offered to the beast to end the attacks.

The chosen victim was Alcyoneus, but the hero Eurybatus, son of Euphemos and moved by divine inspiration, fell in love with him, took his place and hurled the beast from the mountainside. Where it struck the rocks a spring gushed forth, which the locals named Sybaris.

Legacy

The spring gave its name to the city of Sybaris, founded by the Locrians in what is now Italy.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis gives her names Sybaris or Lamia, and Pausanias describes an ancient painting showing a young man called Sybaris about to be sacrificed to a monster called Lycas.

Trivia

A monster without a description. The ancient sources do not describe her appearance, and modern commentators suppose she was a dragoness because of her resemblance to the serpent Python, who also plagued the Delphi region.
A painting with another name. The geographer Pausanias described a copy of an ancient painting from Temesa in which the monster is called Lycas, meaning wolfish.
