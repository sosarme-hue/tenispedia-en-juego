Chinese god of fire

Zhurong

Zhurong is the god of fire in Chinese mythology and folk religion, associated with the south and summer. Some sources also present him as a historical official, Minister of Fire, and his figure is tied to several of China's principal ancient myths.

The myth

According to popular legend, Zhurong lived in a palace on Mount Kunlun and taught humans to use fire, sending firewood down from the sky. Gonggong, the water god, resented the devotion Zhurong received and sent four lakes and five seas against his residence, extinguishing the sacred fire and plunging the world into darkness. Angered, Zhurong mounted a fire dragon and battled Gonggong. Defeated, Gonggong smashed his head against Mount Buzhou, one of the pillars of the earth, which Nüwa later repaired.

The Classic of Mountains and Seas gives alternative genealogies for Zhurong, with descent from both the Yan Emperor and the Yellow Emperor. In Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian he appears as a historical Minister of Fire and son of Gaoyang, and tradition also made him an ancestor of the eight lineages of the royal families of the state of Chu.

Worship and religion

On Mount Heng in Hunan province, the highest peak, about 1,300 metres tall, bears his name. At its summit stands his palace or temple, a stone structure with an iron roof built during the Ming dynasty in the Wanli era.

Legacy

His name became linked to the rulers of Chu, who claimed descent from him. His association with fire also connects him with the planet Mars.

Ancient sources: The Classic of Mountains and Seas, the Huainanzi, Qu Yuan's poem Heavenly Questions and Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian mention him.

Trivia

A name with a vessel. His character combines the sign for a tripod ritual vessel with three hollow legs, typical of the Bronze Age in northern China. David Hawkes linked the name to ancient Chinese ceramic technology.
A contest of strength. In one version of the myth, Zhurong and Gonggong fought for days to see who was stronger, and the struggle ended with Gonggong crashing into Mount Buzhou.
