hamadryad nymph of Greek mythology

Chrysopeleia

Chrysopeleia was a hamadryad nymph of Arcadia, linked to an oak tree and to the hunter Arcas, from whom the tradition traces the origin of the Arcadians. Her story explains the bond between a pastoral people and the region.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Tzetzes, the nymph lived in an oak when a river swollen by winter rains threatened to sweep it away. Arcas, who was hunting nearby, diverted the river and secured the tree with a mound of earth. Grateful or drawn to him, Chrysopeleia became his lover.

According to Eumelus, she was his wife and the mother of Elatus and Apheidas, from whom, according to Apollonius, the Arcadians descended. Tzetzes gives Zeus or Apollo and Callisto, daughter of Lycaon, as Arcas's parents, following Charon of Lampsacus. The Library attributed to Apollodorus mentions her only as a possible wife, alongside Leanira, daughter of Amyclas, and Meganira, daughter of Crocon, without settling the genealogy.

Ancient sources: Tzetzes, in his commentary on Lycophron, also cites Charon of Lampsacus, Eumelus and Apollonius; the Library attributed to Apollodorus names her as a possible wife of Arcas.
