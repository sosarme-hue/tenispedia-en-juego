Greek and Berber hero, guardian of the Berber lands

Sufax

Sufax is a hero of Greek and Berber mythology, son of Heracles and the goddess Tingis. He is regarded as a guardian of the land of the Berbers and, according to some accounts, the founder of Tangier, a city said to be named after his mother.

The myth

According to the myth, Sufax was born from the second marriage of Tingis to Heracles, making him a grandson of Zeus and of the mortal Alcmene. He replaced Antaeus, his mother's first husband, as guardian of the land of the Berbers, or Imazighen. Other accounts make him the founder of Tangier, in memory of his mother.

He had a son, Diodorus, who ruled many North African Berber tribes with the help of the Olympian gods. Berber tradition holds that many of their kings were descended from Sufax.

Legacy

According to Plutarch, many of these myths were created to lend legitimacy to the Numidian king Juba II, who considered himself a descendant of Diodorus and Sufax, showing how the hero's figure was used politically in ancient North Africa.

Ancient sources: The Greek historian Plutarch mentions him in connection with the Numidian king Juba II.

Trivia

A city named for his mother. According to some accounts, Tangier was named in memory of Tingis, the mother of Sufax.
A hero with many names. The hero appears under several spellings, including Sophax, Syphax and Sufaqs, and his name is also linked to the modern Tunisian city of Sfax.
