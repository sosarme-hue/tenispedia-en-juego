Maya Moon Goddess

The Maya Moon Goddess was a major deity of the Classic Maya, linked to the moon, the calendar and eclipses. She is recognized by the lunar rabbit and the crescent moon that usually accompany her.

The myth

According to the Classic-period story, the Moon Goddess and the lunar rabbit take part in the theft of the regalia of God L, an underworld deity. The scene is painted on polychrome vessels such as Kerr 5166. The rabbit may be a trickster figure, while the goddess acts as the guardian of those objects.

A variant replaces her with the sun god K'inich Ahau as the thief, as shown on Kerr 1398.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to her. Her importance appears mainly in the calendar: she was patroness of the month Ch'en, 'well', and of one of the Venus years.

Legacy

Her name is still debated among specialists, and the reading Hun Uh, 'One Moon', is not generally accepted. In popular culture she appears as a playable character named Awilix in the video game Smite.

Ancient sources: She appears mostly on Classic-period polychrome ceramics, and her importance is reflected in the eclipse tables of the Dresden Codex.

Trivia

Expression for the new moon. The Maya expression 'the Moon has gone to her well' refers to the new moon.
A disputed name. The reading Hun Uh, 'One Moon', was proposed as hieroglyphic decipherment advanced, but most scholars still simply call her the Moon Goddess.
