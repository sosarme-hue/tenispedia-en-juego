Abyss

The abyss is a bottomless place in biblical imagery that later became a central concept in Christian theology and philosophy. In the Bible it appears as the primordial waters of chaos, the depths of the earth, or the realm of the dead and of rebellious spirits.

The myth

In the Hebrew Bible, the word tehóm refers to the primordial waters over which darkness lay before creation. As a place of immense depth, it could also mean the sea, underground springs or the interior of the earth. The Book of Jonah describes the prophet sinking down to the abyss.

In later Jewish literature, the abyss became the underworld, either the home of the dead (Sheol) or a prison for rebellious spirits, equivalent to hell. The New Testament takes up this idea: according to the Gospel of Luke, Jesus sends the Gadarene swine into the abyss, and Revelation places it as the source of locusts and a sea beast, and as the prison of the dragon during the millennium. There appears Abaddon, called «the angel of the abyss». In Gnostic texts such as On the Origin of the World, the archons are cast into the abyss by Sophia for their injustice.

Worship and religion

The abyss had no cult of its own. Its role was doctrinal: Christian theology used it to explain the relationship between the Old and New Testaments, and Pope Gregory XVI invoked it to criticise liberty of conscience.

Legacy

The concept moved from the Bible into philosophy. Augustine of Hippo understood it as formless matter, lacking light, and Meister Eckhart translated it into German as Abgrunt. Later, in speculative mysticism and philosophical Romanticism, the abyss became an image of the hidden ground of being, invisible and beyond the reach of human understanding.

Ancient sources: Biblical Hebrew (tehóm), the Septuagint, the New Testament (Luke and Revelation) and the Gnostic text On the Origin of the World.

Trivia

A pope and liberty of conscience. Gregory XVI drew on the image of the bottomless pit from Revelation to criticise the idea of liberty of conscience, which he considered an absurd and erroneous proposition.
The Gnostic archons. In the Gnostic text On the Origin of the World, at the end of time the archons are cast into the abyss, where they fight one another until only the chief archon remains, and he turns against himself.
