apotropaic guardian of Mesopotamian mythology

Lahmu

Lahmu and Lahamu are a pair of beings from Mesopotamian mythology, linked to water and to the protection of homes and temples. Although they appear in divine genealogies, the sources present them more as guardians than as full gods, and their name, of Semitic origin, already appears in Sumerian sources.

The myth

According to Babylonian tradition, Lahmu and Lahamu were siblings, children of Apsu and Tiamat. In the Enuma Elish, Lahmu is the firstborn of the pair and, with his sister, the parent of Anshar and Kishar, the horizons of sky and earth, from whom Anu descends and, through him, Ea and Marduk. After Marduk's victory, both give him three names.

Their best-known role was as guardians: according to the texts, they served Enki/Ea and guarded the doors of his temple at Eridu, and some lists speak of as many as fifty Lahmu in that task. In the war against Marduk, however, they also appear among Tiamat's monsters, presumably of the same kind as the protective ones.

A fragmentary Assyrian rewrite of the Enuma Elish replaces Marduk with Ashur, and puts Lahmu and Lahamu in the place of Ea and Damkina, with a result that Wilfred G. Lambert described as mainly confused.

Legacy

The name of the town of Bethlehem, known from the Bible, originally Beth-Lehem or Bit-Lahmi, meant 'house of Lahmu or Lahamu'.

Ancient sources: They appear in the Enuma Elish and in some versions of the Atra-Hasis, as well as in Mesopotamian god lists.

Trivia

A discarded theory. Authors of the 19th and early 20th centuries claimed Lahmu represented the zodiac, parent stars or constellations, an idea now disproven.
Naked heroes. Artistic depictions, showing a bearded man in a red garment, are sometimes called 'naked heroes' in the literature.
