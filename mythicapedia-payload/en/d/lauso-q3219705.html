son of King Numitor in Roman legend

Lausus

Lausus is a minor figure in the Roman legend of Rome's origins, the son and heir of the Alban king Numitor. His killing by his uncle Amulius, who wanted the throne, is part of the story that precedes the founding of the city.

The myth

According to the tradition, Amulius seized the Alban throne from his brother Numitor and, to secure his power, killed Lausus while he was out hunting. Amulius blamed the death on bandits, but many Albans did not believe him. Dionysius of Halicarnassus records that this character was called Aegestus, and Ovid tells his story briefly.

Lausus does not appear in Livy or in Plutarch's Life of Romulus and Remus. The scholar Paul Marius Martin argues that he was invented to reinforce the tyrannical image of Amulius's reign, since Amulius resorts to the classic ploy of eliminating his nephew during a hunt.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus, who gives the name Aegestus, and Ovid, who tells the story briefly.

Trivia

Another name. Dionysius of Halicarnassus calls him Aegestus, a name different from the one usually used for this character.
Absent from Livy. Neither Livy nor Plutarch mention him, which makes his role in the story less clear than that of other protagonists.
