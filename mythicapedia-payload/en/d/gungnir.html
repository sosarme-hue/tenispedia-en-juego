Odin's spear in Norse mythology

Gungnir

Gungnir is the spear of the god Odin in Norse mythology, a weapon so perfectly balanced that it never missed its target. Recorded in the Eddas, its story makes it one of the most famous objects of the Scandinavian pantheon.

The myth

According to the Eddas, the spear was forged by the Sons of Ivaldi, under the direction of the dwarf smith Dvalinn. Loki obtained it as partial compensation for cutting the hair of Sif, Thor's wife, and had to promise the dwarves a golden hair that would grow like the rest.

Gungnir became Odin's weapon. In the Prose Edda, at Ragnarök the god will ride ahead of the einherjar, wearing a gold helmet and mail coat with the spear in hand, and will use it to attack the wolf Fenrir. The runes carved on its tip and its unfailing accuracy make it a symbol of the god's will.

Worship and religion

Oaths were sworn over the spear's tip, and when Odin hurled it at someone it meant that person belonged to him. According to the tale, Odin wounded himself with it and thereby dedicated himself to the sky. When he hands the spear to a warrior, he watches over that warrior and guides his deeds of valour. There is no record of a separate cult devoted to the spear itself.

Legacy

The spear appears in Richard Wagner's opera cycle Der Ring des Nibelungen, where it is carved from the wood of the ash tree Yggdrasil and inscribed with the contracts that underpin Wotan's power; Siegfried breaks it at the end. An image on the Böksta Runestone may show Odin hunting an elk with it. Its name is linked to the rune Tiwaz, associated with the god Týr, who also carried a spear.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Völuspá and Sigrdrífumál) and the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál).

Trivia

The name. Gungnir is usually read as the rocker or the swaying one, a reference to the violent shudder it was said to cause in whoever it struck.
A sure strike. According to the Prose Edda, the spear hits any target regardless of the skill or strength of the one who wields it.
