son of Bhima, half-demon figure of Hindu epic

Ghatotkacha

Ghatotkacha is a character in the ancient Indian epic Mahabharata, the son of the Pandava Bhima and the rakshasi Hidimbi. He matters chiefly for his role in the Kurukshetra War, where his death becomes a turning point in the conflict.

The myth

According to the epic, Ghatotkacha was born to Bhima and Hidimbi, sister of the rakshasa Hidimba, whom Bhima had slain. Through his mother he was half human and half rakshasa, and he had magical powers, including flight. His name refers to his bald, pot-shaped head.

As a young man he lived with his mother and gave a pearl to his cousin Abhimanyu. He was loyal and humble toward his father: Bhima need only think of him and he would appear in the air. Like Bhima, he fought with the mace. He married Ahilavati, and the sons attributed to him include Barbarika, though some versions mention him only in folklore rather than in the main text.

At Kurukshetra he fought for the Pandavas, wrought great destruction among the Kauravas and faced several great warriors. After sunset, when his powers peaked, Duryodhana turned to Karna, who hurled at Ghatotkacha Indra's secret weapon. Ghatotkacha enlarged his body before falling, crushing many Kaurava soldiers. The Pandavas mourned him, yet Krishna smiled, since his sacrifice allowed Arjuna to survive his rivalry with Karna.

Worship and religion

A temple dedicated to Ghatotkacha stands in Dieng, in Central Java, Indonesia. Another, in Manali in the Indian state of Himachal Pradesh, lies near the shrine of Hidimba Devi.

Legacy

He lives on in the wayang shadow theatre of Java and Bali, where the Kurukshetra War is called Bharatayuddha and Ghatotkacha is a much-loved character.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata, one of the two great Sanskrit epics of India.

Trivia

Name explained. His name comes from Sanskrit: ghata means pot or jar, and utkacha means bald, referring to his head.
A death that changed the war. As he fell, he crushed an akshauhini, the largest military unit of the tradition, a turning point in the battle.
