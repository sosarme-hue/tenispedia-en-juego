Greek hero, father of Amphiaraus

Oecles

Oecles, also known as Oicles or Oicleus, is a hero of Greek myth best remembered as the father of the seer Amphiaraus. He joined Heracles on his campaign against Troy, where he died defending the ships that the hero had entrusted to him.

The myth

According to Homer, Oecles was the son of Antiphates, who was in turn the son of Melampus. Diodorus Siculus adds that his mother was Zeuxippe, daughter of Hippocoon. He married Hypermnestra, daughter of Thestius, and they had Amphiaraus and Iphianeira, along with Polyboea according to Diodorus, while the Catalogue of Women names Endeus instead of Polyboea. Later authors claimed that Apollo was Amphiaraus's true father, and that Oecles had only raised him as his putative father.

Some traditions say that Oecles spent time in Arcadia. According to the mythographer Apollodorus, after the Epigoni captured Thebes, his grandson Alcmaeon, son of Amphiaraus, came to visit him there. Pausanias reports seeing, near Megalopolis, what was said to be his tomb.

Oecles accompanied Heracles in the war against Troy. While the hero led the main force against the city, Oecles was left in charge of the fleet. Laomedon, king of Troy, attacked the camp to set the ships on fire. Oecles resisted and was killed in the fighting, but his men managed to take the ships out to sea. According to the Library, it was Laomedon himself who killed him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey names him as the son of Antiphates; the Catalogue of Women and Diodorus Siculus record his descendants, while the Library and Pausanias describe his death and tomb.

Trivia

A lost tragedy. The tragedian Sophocles may have written a play titled Oicles dealing with this episode, but it has not survived.
A tomb in Arcadia. Pausanias says he saw near Megalopolis what was said to be the tomb of Oecles.
