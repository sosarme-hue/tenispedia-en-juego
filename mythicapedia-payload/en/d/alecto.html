Fury of Greek and Roman mythology

Allecto

Allecto is one of the Erinyes, or Furies, avenging spirits of classical myth who punished moral crimes, especially anger and violence against others. Her image became famous in Virgil's Aeneid, where she stirs up war between the Trojans and the Latins.

The myth

According to Hesiod, the Erinyes were born from the blood spilled when Cronus castrated Uranus, which places them among the oldest figures of avenging justice. Later sources imagined them as daughters of Acheron and Nox, and presented them as three sisters: Allecto, Tisiphone and Megaera. According to Tzetzes, her name reflects that Allecto never ceases in her task of punishing the guilty.

In the Aeneid, Juno sends her up from the Underworld to sow discord between the Trojans and the Latins and to stain the wedding of Turnus and Lavinia with blood. Allecto poisons Queen Amata's mind by slipping one of her serpents into her, then appears to Turnus in a dream disguised as a priestess of Juno. When he mocks her, she reveals her true form and drives a burning torch into his chest, inflaming his rage. She later provokes an incident involving Iulus, son of Aeneas, which pushes the conflict further. In the end, Juno orders her to withdraw, since the goddess will handle the rest of the war herself.

Legacy

Dante mentions her briefly in Canto IX of the Inferno, with her sisters before the gates of Dis. She also appears in Torquato Tasso's Jerusalem Delivered, where she brings false visions and discord to the Christian captains, and in John Dryden's adaptation of Oedipus Rex. Miklós Zrínyi includes her in The Siege of Sziget, and Henry Purcell mentions her in the song Music for a While. The minor planet 465 Alekto is named in her honour.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, who places the origin of the Erinyes in the blood of Uranus, and Virgil's Aeneid, the source that describes Allecto in most detail.

Trivia

An asteroid with her name. The minor planet 465 Alekto is named in her honour, showing how her figure lives on well beyond literature.
An Irish Fury. In the Irish epic Táin Bó Cúailnge she is equated with the Mórrígan, a figure linked to battle and death.
