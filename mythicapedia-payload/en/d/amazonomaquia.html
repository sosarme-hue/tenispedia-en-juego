mythical battle between the Greeks and the Amazons

Amazonomachy

The Amazonomachy is the mythical battle between the ancient Greeks and the Amazons, a nation of all-female warriors. It was a popular subject in Greek and Roman art, appearing on pottery and on reliefs of temples, altars and sarcophagi.

The myth

According to tradition, the Amazons were fiercely independent women, skilled at hunting, riding, archery and warfare, who were regarded as descendants of Ares and worshipped Ares and Artemis. They were associated with Scythia and Asia Minor, and in Greek epic they appeared as non-Greek warriors who challenged the heroes on the battlefield.

Among the best-known episodes, Achilles kills Penthesilea, queen of the Amazons, after she comes to aid Troy following the death of Hector; this story belongs to the lost epic Aethiopis, widely attributed to Arctinus of Miletus. During Heracles' ninth labour he must obtain the girdle of the Amazon queen Hippolyta; a misunderstanding stirred up by Hera leads to a battle in which the Amazons are defeated. In other versions, Theseus captures Hippolyta or her sister Antiope, which sparks a war against Athens that ends in the Amazons' defeat.

Legacy

The theme was very popular in Greek and Roman art, appearing on reliefs such as the metopes of the Parthenon, the temple of Apollo at Eretria, the Athenian Treasury at Delphi, the temple of Apollo Epicurius at Bassae, the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus and the Altar of Zeus at Pergamon. Modern scholars have read the myth in many ways: as a symbol of Greek civilization against barbarism, as a metaphor for the Persians, or as a reflection of gender tensions, though other critics have challenged these readings.

Ancient sources: Epics such as the lost Aethiopis, attributed to Arctinus of Miletus, and Quintus Smyrnaeus's The Fall of Troy, which narrates Penthesilea's arrival at Troy.
