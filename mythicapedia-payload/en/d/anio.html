king of Delos and priest of Apollo

Anius

Anius was a king of Delos, son of Apollo and the mortal Rhoeo, and also served as priest of the god. His story links the sacred island of Apollo with the Trojan War and with Aeneas's arrival in Italy.

The myth

According to the myth, Rhoeo, daughter of Staphylus, was locked in a chest by her father after he discovered her pregnancy, believing a mortal to be responsible. The chest washed up on Delos, where Rhoeo gave birth and left the baby on Apollo's altar, asking the god to save him if he was his son. Apollo raised the child, taught him divination and gave him the gift of prophecy, and Anius later became king of the island. Another version says that Rhoeo married Zarex, who became the child's legal father.

With Dorippe he had three daughters, the Oenotropae: Elais, Oeno and Spermo. Dionysus had granted them the power to turn whatever they wished into oil, wine or wheat. When the Greeks landed on Delos on their way to Troy, Anius prophesied that the war would not be won until the tenth year and kept them there for nine years. When the Greeks tried to seize the women to supply their army, they prayed to Dionysus, who turned them into doves.

Anius also had three sons: Andros and Mykonos gave their names to the islands of Andros and Mykonos. Thasos was devoured by dogs, and ever since, keeping dogs on Delos has been forbidden. Later, Aeneas, Anchises and other refugees fleeing Troy reached Delos and were welcomed by Anius.

Worship and religion

As priest of Apollo, Anius looked after the worship of the god on Delos, the island sacred to him. The sources do not describe rites or festivals devoted specifically to Anius.

Legacy

In a rare version of the myth, Aeneas marries Anius's daughter Lavinia, who also had prophetic powers, and they have a son who bears his grandfather's name. The islands of Andros and Mykonos still carry the names of two of his sons.

Trivia

Banned dogs. According to tradition, after Thasos was devoured by dogs, keeping dogs on Delos was prohibited.
Daughters turned into doves. The Oenotropae, pursued by the Greeks, asked Dionysus for help, and he turned them into doves.
