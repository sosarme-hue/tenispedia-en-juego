asura king in Hindu mythology

Vajradatta

Vajradatta is an asura king in Hindu mythology, son and successor of King Bhagadatta and third ruler of the Naraka dynasty of Pragjyotisha. He is best remembered for his clash with Arjuna during Yudhishthira's horse sacrifice.

The myth

Texts portray him as a learned prince who, according to tradition, mastered the four Vedas, the Angas and the political treatises of Brihaspati and Shukra. Epics compare him to Indra in power and speed, and say his lustre was like a thunderbolt.

His best-known episode appears in the Ashvamedha Parva of the Mahabharata. When Yudhishthira's sacrificial horse reached Pragjyotisha, Vajradatta tried to hold it to avenge his father's humiliation at Kurukshetra. After a long battle he was defeated by Arjuna. In the story, he was still a child during the war and never fought in it.

Ancient sources: The Ashvamedha Parva of the Mahabharata, the Kalika Purana and the Harshacharita; Kamarupa inscriptions also name him among the ancestors of their kings.

Trivia

Brothers in the Kalika Purana. The Kalika Purana presents him as a son of Bhagadatta alongside Pushpadatta, who shares the same lineage.
Ancestor of kings. Kamarupa inscriptions, such as the Nidhanpur inscription of Bhaskara Varman, name Naraka, Bhagadatta and Vajradatta as ancestors of their kings.
