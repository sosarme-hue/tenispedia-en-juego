queen of Corinth, wife of Polybus

Merope

Merope is the queen of Corinth in Greek mythology and the wife of King Polybus. She raised Oedipus as her own son, and her role is key to understanding how the hero comes to his tragic fate.

The myth

According to the myth, King Laius of Thebes abandoned his newborn son on Mount Cithaeron to avoid a prophecy. Servants of Polybus found the baby and handed him to Merope, who named him Oedipus because of his swollen feet and raised him as her own.

As a young man, Oedipus doubted his origins after taunts from other young men and consulted the oracle at Delphi, which told him he would kill his father and marry his mother. Believing Polybus and Merope were his parents, he never returned to Corinth and went to Thebes, where the prophecy came true. After Polybus died, Merope told him the truth about his birth.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Her name is linked to the Greek meros, “part”, and ops, “face” or “eye”.
