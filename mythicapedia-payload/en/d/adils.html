Adils

Adils was a semi-legendary king of the Swedes, ruling from Uppsala and belonging to the Yngling dynasty, traditionally placed in the sixth century. He survives in the Old English epic Beowulf and in Norse sources, which portray him as a wealthy and greedy ruler.

The myth

According to Beowulf, Adils, also called Eadgils, was the son of King Ohthere and the brother of Eanmund. After Ohthere's death his uncle Onela seized the throne, and the two brothers had to seek refuge among the Geats. With Geatish and, in Scandinavian sources, Danish help, Adils defeated Onela, a battle that the Norse sagas place on the frozen Lake Vänern.

Scandinavian sources link him with the legendary Hrólfr Kraki, in an uneasy mix of alliance and rivalry. In the Ynglingatal his death is accidental: he fell from his horse while performing sacrifices at Uppsala.

Ancient sources: He appears in Beowulf, the Ynglingatal, the Ynglinga saga, the Skjöldunga saga and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A very rich burial. His presumed tomb at Gamla Uppsala, in the Adils mound, held a man wrapped in a bearskin, with two dogs and rich grave goods, including a Frankish sword and Roman ivory gaming pieces.
