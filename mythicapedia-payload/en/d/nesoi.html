personifications of islands in Greek mythology

Nesoi

The Nesoi were the personifications of islands in Greek myth. They are barely mentioned in ancient texts, and only Callimachus names them as goddesses.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Callimachus, islands were not rooted to the sea floor but drifted like loose pieces of land. Poseidon struck the mountains with his trident, a work of the Telchines, tore the islands from their bases and hurled them into the sea, where he anchored them deep down so they would forget the mainland. Only one kept drifting: Delos, formerly called Asteria. As the story goes, Asteria fled Zeus's amorous pursuit by throwing herself into the abyss, and so became an island.

In his hymn to Delos, Callimachus presents her as the nurse of Apollo, the first to bathe him, wrap him in swaddling clothes and praise him as a god, and claims she rightly claims the first fruits of the Muses. The Cyclades are the holiest of the islands, and Delos, guarded by Apollo, is the firmest of them all.

Ancient sources: Callimachus, in his Hymn to Delos.

Trivia

Asteria, the island that fled. Asteria threw herself into the sea to escape Zeus, and the myth says that her leap gave birth to the island of Delos.
A procession of islands. After Delos, Callimachus names Corsica, Euboea, Sardinia and Cyprus as islands following behind her in line.
