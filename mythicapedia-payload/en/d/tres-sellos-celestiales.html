Three Heavenly Seals

A set of three bronze objects, a sword, a mirror and a bell, which Korean tradition regards as symbols of authority. Their meaning lies mainly in Korean shamanism and native beliefs.

The myth

According to the Samguk yusa, written in the 13th century, the god Hwanin gave these three seals to his son Hwanung as tokens of authority when he descended from heaven. They are known as the Heavenly Blade, the Heavenly Mirror and the Heavenly Bell.

The account belongs to the founding legend that links Hwanung with Dangun, associated with the Yemaek people of northern Korea. In the tradition, the objects work more as symbols than as practical tools.

Worship and religion

The objects and their variants belong to the shamanic tools called mugu, which Korean shamans still use today, although many have been replaced by other ritual devices such as the mengdu.

Legacy

The 20th-century historian Ch'oe Namsŏn linked them to the spread of shamanic beliefs across Northeast Asia, comparing finds from Manchuria, Korea and Japan. The relics also help scholars study migration in Korean history.

Ancient sources: The Samguk yusa (13th century), which recounts Hwanin's gift of the seals to Hwanung.

Trivia

An eight-armed bell. The bronze bell has eight arms, with a small bell at each end. According to the description, it produces a majestic sound when shaken.
A lute-shaped dagger. The bronze dagger has a lute-shaped blade and a detachable hilt, a feature found in Manchuria, Korea and Japan.
