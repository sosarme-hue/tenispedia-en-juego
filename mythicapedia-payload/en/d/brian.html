Gaelic hero, one of the Sons of Tuireann

Brian

Brian, also known as Uar, is one of the three Sons of Tuireann in Gaelic mythology, son of Lugh and the goddess Dana. His story of revenge and punishment has been compared to the Greek Argonautica.

The myth

According to the Irish tale Oidheadh Chlainne Tuireann (The Tragedy of the Sons of Tuireann), the three brothers, Brian, Iuchar and Iucharba, killed Cian, their father's enemy. Cian, father of Lugh, had shapeshifted into a pig to escape; the brothers, turned into dogs, cornered him, killed him and hid the body.

As punishment, Lugh sent them across the known world to fetch magical weapons. They obtained them, but returned badly wounded and begged Lugh to heal them, and he refused. In some versions he had set them eight impossible tasks; they failed the last one, and Lugh killed them.

Legacy

Their tale has been likened to an Irish Argonautica, given its long quest for magical objects across the world.

Ancient sources: Oidheadh Chlainne Tuireann, an Irish tale preserved in manuscripts.

Trivia

Uar or Uair. In many surviving manuscripts the character appears as Uar or Uair, and modern editors have changed the name to Brian.
The clever brother. In at least one version, Brian is the clever and subtle one of the three, while Iuchar and Iucharba are bumbling and easily overawed.
