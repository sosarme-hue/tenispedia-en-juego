Amazon queen in Greek mythology

Penthesilea

Penthesilea is an Amazon queen of Greek mythology, the daughter of Ares and Otrera. She comes to Troy to help the Trojans in the war against the Greeks, and her story as a tragic warrior who arrives too late has inspired poets, painters and composers for centuries.

The myth

According to the tradition, Penthesilea was an Amazon warrior, daughter of the god Ares and Otrera. The sources disagree about why she came to Troy. Helanicus and Lysias, cited by John Tzetzes, say that her love of war drove her. Diodorus Siculus and Apollodorus tell that she had accidentally killed her sister Hippolyta during a hunt and went to Troy to be purified by King Priam, who had fought the Amazons in his youth.

She arrived with twelve other Amazons. In Quintus of Smyrna, where her story is told as a tragedy of grief and a wish to die honorably, she fought with devastating fury, killing many Greeks until Achilles brought her down with a spear. As she died, Achilles was struck by her beauty. When Thersites mocked him for this feeling, Achilles killed him. Diomedes, Thersites' cousin, threw the Amazon's body into the river Scamander, although other versions say Achilles buried her on its banks. Some traditions credit Neoptolemus rather than Achilles with her death. Antianira succeeded her as queen.

Legacy

Virgil presents her in the Aeneid as a tragic figure who arrived too late to save the city. The asteroid 271 Penthesilea, discovered in 1887, bears her name. In music, Othmar Schoeck composed the opera Penthesilea and Hugo Wolf wrote a symphonic poem with her name. In art, the Bassae frieze shows her dying before Achilles, a motif later repeated on the Temple of Zeus at Olympia and on black-figure vases.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Posthomerica of Quintus of Smyrna, in the Bibliotheca historica of Diodorus Siculus and in the Epitome of the Bibliotheca attributed to Pseudo-Apollodorus, as well as in the epic Aethiopis, attributed to Arctinus of Miletus.

Trivia

An asteroid named after her. The asteroid 271 Penthesilea, discovered in 1887, was named in her honor.
A recurring artistic motif. The scene of Achilles supporting the dying Amazon appears on the Temple of Zeus at Olympia, in ancient Roman art and on a black-figure vase from about 510-500 BC.
