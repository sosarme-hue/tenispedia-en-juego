The Tarasque

The Tarasque is a creature of French legend that, according to tradition, terrorised the banks of the Rhône until Saint Martha tamed it. Its name comes from the town of Tarascon, which uses it as its emblem, and its effigy still parades through streets in France and Spain.

The myth

According to medieval tradition, the beast lived in the forested banks of the Rhône between Arles and Avignon, in a place then called Nerluc, or 'black place'. It lurked in the river, attacked those crossing it and sank their boats. Texts from the 12th and 13th centuries describe it as a half-animal, half-fish dragon with sword-like teeth and a poisonous breath. Some versions say it came from Galatia and was a cross between the biblical Leviathan and a creature called Onachus, which hurled its dung like arrows.

The Golden Legend tells how Saint Martha found the monster devouring a man. Using holy water and a cross, she subdued it and led it back on her girdle to the villagers, who stoned and speared it to death. Later versions added a turtle-like shell, six bear-like feet and a scorpion-tipped tail.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, King René of Anjou introduced the Tarasque into the Pentecost festival at Tarascon in the 15th century. Today a large float carried by four to a dozen men hidden inside parades through the town at the yearly celebration in late June. In Spain, the figure appeared in Corpus Christi processions in cities such as Granada, Seville, Valencia, Toledo and Zamora, although Charles III banned secular elements from these feasts in the 18th century. UNESCO declared the Tarascon festivities part of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2005.

Legacy

The Tarasque appears on the coat of arms of Tarascon and in Alphonse Daudet's novel Tartarin de Tarascon. The name was given to a French 20 mm anti-aircraft chain gun. In tabletop roleplaying, the Tarrasque of Dungeons & Dragons is one of the most powerful creatures, and video games such as Starcraft and Fate/Grand Order have drawn on its figure.

Ancient sources: The earliest versions appear in the Golden Legend (Legenda aurea), the pseudo-Marcella and the Speculum historiale of Vincent de Beauvais, all from the 12th and 13th centuries.

Trivia

Name in video games. In Dungeons & Dragons the monster is spelled with two r's, Tarrasque, and is one of the most powerful creatures in the game.
The devouring scene. In medieval art the Tarasque is often shown devouring a human, with the victim's legs still dangling from its jaws.
