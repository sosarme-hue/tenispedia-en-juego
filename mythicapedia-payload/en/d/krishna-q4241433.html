avatar of Vishnu and hero of the Mahabharata

Krishna

Krishna is one of the central figures of the Mahabharata, the great Indian epic of war and duty. Tradition identifies him as the human avatar of the god Vishnu, and he appears in the story as counselor, warrior and teacher of the Pandavas.

The myth

According to the story, Krishna was born in Mathura as the eighth child of Devaki and Vasudeva. A prophecy warned that Devaki's eighth son would kill Kamsa, her brother and the usurping king, so the king imprisoned the couple. Vasudeva carried the newborn across the Yamuna to Gokul, where the cowherds Nanda and Yashoda raised him. As an adult he returned to Mathura, killed Kamsa and later founded the city of Dvaraka.

In the Mahabharata he is the cousin of the Pandavas, since his aunt Kunti was the sister of Vasudeva. He backed the brothers at Draupadi's svayamvara and at the Rajasuya sacrifice, where he killed Shishupala. He was absent during the dice game, because he was fighting King Salva, although a later tradition places him protecting Draupadi's honour. Later he visited the Pandavas in exile to reaffirm his loyalty.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata itself, in its books Adiparva, Sabhaparva and Vanaparva, and its appendix, the Harivamsha.

Trivia

A disputed episode. The scene in which Krishna saves Draupadi with an endless supply of cloth appears in several versions of the text. The scholar Franklin Edgerton concluded that it is a later interpolation.
An intriguing absence. Krishna does not appear in the dice game that leads to the fall of the Pandavas. The researcher Walter Ruben reads this absence as a sign that the original narrative may not have included him.
Ancient divine companions. According to the epic, Arjuna and Krishna are rebirths of the divine pair Nara and Narayana, and this shared identity is why Arjuna calms Krishna's anger during the visit in exile.
