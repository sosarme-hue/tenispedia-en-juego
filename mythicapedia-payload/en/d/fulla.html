Norse goddess of abundance and Frigg's confidante

Fulla

Fulla is a goddess of Norse and Germanic mythology, servant and confidante of Frigg, wife of Odin. She is linked to abundance, gold and fertility, and scholars debate whether she was an independent deity or an aspect of another goddess.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Fulla is one of Frigg's handmaidens, along with Gna and Hlin. She is described as a virgin with loose hair and a golden band around her head, who carries her mistress's jewel box, tends her footwear and shares her secrets. Some ancient authorities call her Frigg's sister, though not all do.

Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda lists her among the goddesses and mentions her in several passages. In the prologue to Grímnismál, Frigg sends Fulla to warn King Geirröd that a magician, in truth Odin in disguise, is coming to visit him. In the story of Baldr's death, Baldr sends gifts from Hel to Frigg and Nanna, and among them is a finger-ring for Fulla. Tradition also portrays her advising Frigg on how to help those who seek her protection.

In the Old High German Merseburg Incantation, a goddess named Volla, Frigg's sister, sings a charm to heal the injured foal of Balder, alongside other figures, a passage many scholars connect with Fulla.

Legacy

The name Fulla is related to the Old Norse adjective fullr, 'full', which explains her link to abundance. Scholars have studied her figure as a possible forerunner of other fertility and harvest goddesses in Scandinavia.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Grímnismál), Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning, Skáldskaparmál), skaldic poetry, and the Old High German Merseburg Incantation.
