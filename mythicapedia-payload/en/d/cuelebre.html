serpent-dragon of Asturian and Cantabrian folklore

Cuélebre

The cuélebre is a legendary creature from northwestern Iberia, described as a winged serpent with dragon features. It guards treasures in caves, springs and woods, and Asturian and Leonese tradition links it to many stories in which villagers tricked it into leaving them alone.

The myth

According to tradition, the cuélebre has burning eyes, scaly skin and bat-like wings. Its main role is guarding hidden treasures, the ayalgues, whose location is described in guides called lliendas. It troubles those who live near its lair, hisses terribly and feeds on people, living or dead, so villagers offered it food to appease it.

The legends tell of people defeating it through trickery. Monks gave it a loaf full of pins, and at Brañaseca it was struck with a red-hot stone. In the story of La Vid, Saint Lawrence killed it with a loaf filled with heated iron. Its weak point is said to be the underside of the neck, and it loses power with age, though Saint Bartholomew's Night makes it more dangerous.

Legacy

Víctor Manuel wrote a song bearing its name. Many place names in Asturian and Leonese toponymy are linked to the cuélebre.

Trivia

Food offerings. Villagers were said to feed the cuélebre with boroña and rye bread so it would not attack their livestock.
Red-hot stone. In the Brañaseca story, villagers killed it by throwing a red-hot stone into its mouth.
