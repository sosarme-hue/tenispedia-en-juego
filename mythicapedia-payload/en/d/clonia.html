nymph of Greek mythology

Clonia

Clonia is a nymph in Greek mythology, the consort of Hyrieus and mother of Nycteus and Lycus. The name is also borne by an Amazon who fought in the Trojan War.

The myth

According to tradition, Clonia married Hyrieus, with whom she had two sons, Nycteus and Lycus. Her profile also lists a daughter, Histiaea, although the reference texts give no details about her. Stories about Clonia as a nymph are scarce, and little else survives about her life or deeds.

The name also belongs to Clonie, an Amazon described as fair as a goddess, who joined Queen Penthesilea at the Trojan War. She killed the Achaean warrior Menippus, but was struck by a spear thrown by his comrade Podarces, which pierced her between the hips and tore through her belly, and she died on the battlefield.

Trivia

Two namesakes. Sources distinguish between the nymph who was the wife of Hyrieus and an Amazon with the same name who fought at Troy.
