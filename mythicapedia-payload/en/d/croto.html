satyr of Greek mythology, inventor of applause

Crotus

Crotus is a satyr from Greek mythology, son of Pan and Eupheme, the nurse of the Muses. He is credited with inventing archery and applause, which makes him a model of devotion to the arts.

The myth

According to the tradition, Crotus lived on Mount Helicon in the company of the Muses, whom his mother had nursed. He was an excellent hunter, and he is said to have been the first to use light to hunt animals. When the Muses sang, he clapped his hands, a sign of acclaim they valued above any spoken praise.

To reward his devotion, the Muses asked Zeus to place him among the stars, and he became a constellation. Details of his celestial image reflect his virtues: the lower body of a horse for his riding skill, arrows for his keenness as a hunter, and a satyr's tail, since he delighted the Muses as satyrs delight Dionysus.

Legacy

His transformation into a constellation links him to Sagittarius, whose archer-on-horseback figure recalls his skills.

Trivia

The inventor of applause. Crotus clapped when the Muses sang, and others copied the gesture. For this he is considered the creator of applause.
A constellation with symbolic traits. Each part of his star figure represents a virtue: the horse's body for his riding skill, and the arrows for his speed as a hunter.
