Lydian autochthon of Greek mythology

Tyllus

Tyllus is an autochthon of Greek mythology, a figure who «sprang from the earth itself». He matters because he appears as an ancestor of the early kings of Lydia in the genealogies handed down by ancient authors.

The myth

According to the tradition, the first king of Lydia was Manes, son of Zeus and Gaia. His son Cotys married Halie, daughter of Tyllus, the one born from the earth, and their children were Asies and Atys. After his father died, Atys became king of Lydia and had two sons, Lydus and Tyrrhenus, who were said to give their names to the Lydian people and to the Tyrrhenians, that is, the Etruscans.

The accounts do not fully agree. Herodotus presents Atys as the son of Manes, while Dionysius of Halicarnassus makes him the grandson and names Callithea as his mother. Strabo, for his part, claims that Atys was a descendant of Heracles and Omphale.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, in the first book of his Histories, and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, in his Roman Antiquities, record his family tree; Strabo adds the claim that Atys descended from Heracles.

Trivia

A continent with a Lydian name. According to the Lydians, the continent of Asia took its name from Asies, grandson of Tyllus through his mother. Herodotus passes on this tradition.
Born from the earth. The Greek word autochthon literally means «sprung from the earth itself». It was used for peoples who regarded themselves as native to their own land.
