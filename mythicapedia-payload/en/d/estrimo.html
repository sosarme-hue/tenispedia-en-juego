nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of Scamander

Strymo

Strymo is, in Greek mythology, a nymph daughter of the Oceanid Scamander, linked to the Trojan world. She matters mainly as the mother of Priam, and therefore an ancestor of the royal line of Troy.

The myth

According to the Homeric tradition, Strymo was the wife of Laomedon, king of Troy. From this union came Priam and Astyoche, who would later hold a central place in the city's story. Her father, the river Scamander, was one of the river deities associated with the plain of Troy, which places the family in the watery landscape of the region.

The account is brief. Strymo works mainly as a link in a genealogy, in the line of descent running from Scamander to Priam, rather than as a protagonist of deeds of her own. That is why she appears mostly in genealogies rather than in the narrative episodes.

Ancient sources: The Homeric tradition presents her as the wife of Laomedon and mother of Priam.

Trivia

A name that gets confused. Her name resembles that of the river god Strymon, who gives his name to a river in Thrace. They should not be confused: Strymo is a nymph, daughter of Scamander.
