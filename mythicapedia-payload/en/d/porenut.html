Slavic god of unknown function

Porenut

Porenut is a Slavic god whose role is unknown, recorded in only two medieval sources. He is best known for a description of his statue, which had five faces, as reported by Scandinavian chroniclers.

The myth

What little is known about Porenut comes from two medieval works written far from his original setting. The first is the Gesta Danorum, a Latin history of the Danes by Saxo Grammaticus, and the second is the Knýtlinga saga, an Old Norse chronicle of the Danish kings. Neither explains what this god did, what stories were told about him, or who his companions were, so there is no myth in the strict sense that we can reconstruct.

What both texts do describe is an image. According to these sources, Porenut's statue had four faces on its head and a fifth on its chest. The figure held that chest face with its right hand, resting on the chin, while its left hand touched the forehead. Many scholars have read this posture as a ritual gesture, although the texts do not explain it.

Therefore, the story of Porenut amounts to an object and the memory of a cult that Danish and Scandinavian chroniclers knew only secondhand. We do not know the names of his worshippers, his cycle of tales, or how his story ends. This lack of information is exactly what makes this god so hard to place within the Slavic pantheon, and it makes him a case study for those who research the religion of the Slavic peoples before Christianization.

Ancient sources: He appears in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum and in the Knýtlinga saga, two medieval chronicles that describe his statue.

Trivia

Five faces. The described statue had four faces on its head and a fifth on its chest, an unusual image in the sources about Slavic gods.
A precise gesture. The figure held the chest face by its chin with its right hand and touched its forehead with its left.
