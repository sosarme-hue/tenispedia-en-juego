princess and Shinto deity of Japanese myth

Yamatohime-no-mikoto

A princess of Japanese myth, daughter of Emperor Suinin, to whom tradition credits the founding of the Inner Shrine of Ise, dedicated to the sun goddess Amaterasu Ōmikami. Today she is venerated as a kami within the shrine itself.

The myth

According to the legend, Emperor Suinin asked his daughter to find a permanent place to worship Amaterasu, who had until then been honoured in the Imperial Palace at Yamato and later at a temporary site in the Nara Basin.

Yamatohime set out from Mt. Miwa and wandered for twenty years through the provinces of Ōmi and Mino. On reaching Ise she heard the goddess's voice, saying she wished to stay there forever, and she founded Naiku. Later, under Emperor Keikō, she gave her dress and the sacred sword Kusanagi to Yamato Takeru.

Worship and religion

In the 1920s a shrine of her own, Yamatohime no miya, was built within the Ise complex. On May 5 and November 5 a Shinto ceremony is held there in her honour.

Ancient sources: She first appears in the Kojiki, compiled in the early 8th century.

Trivia

Comparison with Himiko. Some scholars link her with Queen Himiko, mentioned in 3rd-century Chinese sources, since both were princesses or priestesses and their names are associated with the sun. Other authors propose different historical figures.
A legendary figure. Since her earliest written mention is in the 8th-century Kojiki, it is difficult to reconstruct a historical figure behind the princess.
