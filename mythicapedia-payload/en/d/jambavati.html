wife of Krishna, daughter of the bear king

Jambavati

Jambavati is one of Krishna's principal wives in Hindu mythology and the only daughter of Jambavan, king of the bears. Her story is intertwined with the theft of the Syamantaka jewel and the birth of Samba, a key figure in the fate of the Yadavas.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Jambavati is the second of Krishna's eight principal wives (Ashtabharya) and the only daughter of Jambavan, king of the bears. Her name means "daughter of Jambavan."

The connection begins with the Syamantaka jewel, which the sun god Surya had given to the Yadava nobleman Satrajit. After Satrajit's brother Prasena was killed by a lion, rumour held that Krishna had murdered him to steal the gem. To clear his name, Krishna followed the trail to a cave, where he found a child playing with the jewel. After a long combat, Jambavan recognised Krishna as Rama, his former benefactor, returned the jewel and offered his daughter in marriage.

Back in Dvaraka, Krishna returned the gem to Satrajit, who, ashamed, gave him his daughter Satyabhama, whom Krishna also married. Jambavati, who had no children, asked Krishna for help, and after a penance before Shiva he obtained a son, Samba. According to the Bhagavata Purana, she had nine more sons. Samba's mischief brought a curse from sages that ended the Yadava line. After Krishna's disappearance, Jambavati ascended the funeral pyre with Rukmini and other women.

Legacy

The Vijayanagara emperor Krishnadevaraya composed the drama Jambava Kalyanam, and Ekaramantha wrote the poem Jambavati Parinayam, both about her marriage. The fight between Jambavan and Krishna over the jewel is one of the most prominent episodes in Puranic literature.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Harivamsa, the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Two honorific names. The sources give her the epithets Narendraputri, "daughter of a king", and Kapindraputra, "daughter of the king of the apes".
An incarnation of Lakshmi. According to some sources, Jambavati is an incarnation of the goddess Lakshmi, as are Krishna's junior wives.
A birth after penance. Samba was named after the form of Shiva that appeared to Krishna, Ardhanarishvara, half male and half female.
