son of Krishna and hero of Hindu mythology

Samba

Samba is a hero of Hindu mythology, son of the god Krishna and his wife Jambavati. His birth and his pranks were tied to the destruction of the Yadu clan, and he is best remembered for his curse of leprosy and his link to the worship of the sun god Surya.

The myth

According to the tale, Jambavati, one of Krishna's wives, wished for a son as handsome as Pradyumna. Krishna knew that this son would bring about the destruction of the Yadus, so he meditated for a long time until Shiva granted him a son with the same destructive energy: thus Samba was born.

Samba grew into an arrogant and mischievous young man. According to the Mausala Parva of the Mahabharata, some Yadava youths dressed him as a woman to trick a group of sages, pretending that she was pregnant. The enraged rishis announced that she would give birth to an iron mace that would destroy their clan. The mace was ground into powder and thrown into the sea, and from the powder grew a grass as strong as iron, with which the Yadavas ended up killing one another.

In another version, the sage Narada, offended by Samba, made Krishna's wives, under the effects of liquor, sleep with him. Krishna cursed him with leprosy. Samba atoned for twelve years by penance beside the Chandrabhaga river and was cured by the sun god Surya.

Worship and religion

Samba is linked to sun worship. He is credited with founding temples to Surya, such as the one at Konark in Odisha and the one at Multan. Every year in Odisha, the Samba Dashami is celebrated, when mothers and fathers pray to Surya for the health of their children.

Legacy

An inscription from Mora, near Mathura, dating to the 1st century BC, seems to group Samba with five other Vrishni heroes in early worship.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Harivamsa, the Bhagavata Purana, the Samba Purana and the Devi Bhagavata Purana tell his stories.

Trivia

Samba Dashami. In Odisha, Samba Dashami is celebrated on the tenth day of the waxing fortnight of the month of Pausha.
Attributed temples. Both the Konark Sun Temple and the Multan Sun Temple, once known as Kashyapapura, are attributed to Samba.
