hero of Greek mythology, son of Aeolus

Deion

Deion (also Deioneus, in Greek Δηίων) is a hero of Greek mythology, one of the seven sons of Aeolus and Enarete, who according to tradition ruled in Phocis. He matters mainly for his place in genealogy: through his son Cephalus and other descendants he links several heroic families.

The myth

According to the Mythological Library, Deion was one of the seven sons of Aeolus and Enarete, and he ruled in Phocis. He married Diomede, daughter of Xuthus, and they had a daughter, Asterodia, and several sons: Eneto, Actor, Phylacus and Cephalus. Cephalus married Procris, daughter of Erechtheus, and is the best known of his descendants. Another daughter, Philonis, is not usually counted among his children, although she already appears in epic poetry.

The sources do not fully agree about his identity. It seems that the Aeolid Deion of Phocis and a possibly different Athenian Deioneus were confused at an early date as the father of Cephalus of Thoricus. Other writers name Nisus, king of Megara, as a son of Deion.

After the death of his brother Salmoneus, Deion took in his daughter Tyro and brought her into his house, until she was married to Creteus, another brother of Deion. His descendants were also noted for multiple births, as happened with his two daughters.

Ancient sources: The Mythological Library and epic poetry, which attests his daughter Philonis.

Trivia

Twin births. Deion's descendants were said to have frequently given birth to twins, as happened with his two daughters.
An early confusion. An Athenian Deioneus and the Aeolid Deion of Phocis may have been equated as the father of Cephalus from a very early date.
