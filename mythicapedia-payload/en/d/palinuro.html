helmsman of Aeneas's ship in Roman myth

Palinurus

Palinurus is the helmsman of Aeneas's ship in Virgil's Aeneid, and his death at sea is said to explain the name of a cape on the Campanian coast. He matters for the human sacrifice the voyage demands and for his role in Aeneas's journey to the underworld.

The myth

In Virgil's account, Palinurus steered Aeneas's ship after the Trojans left the ruins of their city. Venus asked Neptune for a safe passage to Latium, and the god agreed on condition that one human life be given: Palinurus's. During a night crossing, Somnus, the Roman counterpart of Hypnos, put him to sleep, and he fell into the sea.

The waves carried him to a shore, where bandits killed him and left him unburied. The prophecy was thus fulfilled, though the Trojans still faced hardships on land before reaching Italy. When Aeneas descends to the underworld, he meets Palinurus's spirit, who asks for help to cross the river, since without burial he cannot rest. The Sibyl of Cumae refuses, because this would go against the fates, but promises that his killers will raise a tomb for him and that a cape will bear his name. Servius and Dionysius of Halicarnassus record accounts of his death that predate the Aeneid, so the story is understood as a legend explaining a geographical feature.

Legacy

Dante, in the Purgatorio, is seen as identifying an unnamed figure with Manfred of Sicily, a parallel with Palinurus, while Martial built an obscene pun on his name. Cyril Connolly used the name as a pseudonym for The Unquiet Grave, and H. P. Lovecraft alluded to his legend in The Tomb.

Ancient sources: Virgil, Aeneid (books 3, 5 and 6); Servius and Dionysius of Halicarnassus in later commentaries.
