demon of European demonology, Great President of Hell

Buer

Buer is a demon from early modern European demonology, described as a Great President of Hell with fifty legions of demons under his command. He matters because he is one of the names that Renaissance and later grimoires placed within the infernal hierarchy.

The myth

The texts present him as a spirit who appears when the Sun is in Sagittarius, which is why he is linked to that zodiac sign. According to the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, he teaches natural and moral philosophy, logic, and the properties of all herbs and plants, much like Chiron, the chief centaur of Greek mythology. He also heals human ailments and grants good familiars, meaning helpful attendant spirits.

A darker detail is that, according to these accounts, he can cure a sick loved one in exchange for the soul of the person who summons him. The Book of St. Cyprian places him under the command of the demon Agaliareth. The grimoires add that he was set in the world by order of the supreme ruler of Hell, and that he awaits a great day of revelation when he will inherit the staff of fire. His appearance varies between authors: sometimes a centaur with bow and arrows, other times a wheel with several legs or a lion's head on goat legs.

Legacy

The most widespread image of Buer comes from an illustration by Louis Le Breton, later engraved by M. Jarrault, which shows him with a lion's head and five goat legs around his body, arranged to walk in any direction. His name became known mainly through The Lesser Key of Solomon, whose 1904 edition was published by Aleister Crowley, where he appears as the tenth spirit of the text.

Ancient sources: The Pseudomonarchia Daemonum, a 16th-century grimoire traditionally linked to Johann Weyer, describes him among the demons of Hell; he later appears in the Book of St. Cyprian and in The Lesser Key of Solomon.

Trivia

Sign of Sagittarius. In the usual astrological order, the Sun enters Sagittarius between November 22 and December 21, and this is when Buer is said to appear.
Lion head and goat legs. Louis Le Breton's illustration shows him with a lion's head and goat legs surrounding the body, one of the most recognizable images of the demon.
