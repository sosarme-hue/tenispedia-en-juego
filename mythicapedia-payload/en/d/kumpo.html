ritual spirit of the Diola people

Kumpo

The Kumpo is a ritual figure of the Diola people, living in the Casamance region of Senegal, Gambia and Guinea-Bissau. It is regarded as a spirit that animates the community festivals and reminds villagers of their duties to the village.

The myth

According to Diola tradition, the Kumpo is not a person but a ghost or spirit, closely associated with the bois sacré, the sacred grove of the village. It appears covered in palm leaves, usually from the ronier palm, and carries a stick on its head to which a young woman ties a coloured flag at the start of the dance.

It dances for hours and speaks a private, secret language, so it communicates with the spectators through an interpreter. Touching it is forbidden, and looking inside the palm leaves would be sacrilege. To deter intruders it strikes the ground with its stick and makes sharp, dangerous gestures. At the end of the celebration it says goodbye to the community and withdraws into the sacred grove.

Worship and religion

Its role is to encourage community life. It is seen as a social stimulus, and skipping the festival is regarded as antisocial behaviour.

Trivia

Ronier leaves. The costume is usually made from ronier leaves, a palm whose fronds completely cover the person performing the Kumpo.
A secret language. The Kumpo speaks a private, secret language that only an interpreter can translate, which reinforces its mysterious nature.
