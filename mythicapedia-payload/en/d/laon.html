supreme creator deity of the Visayan peoples (Philippines)

Laon

Laon, whose name means 'the ancient one', is the supreme creator deity in the animist anito beliefs of the Visayan peoples of the Philippines. Associated with creation, agriculture, the sky and divine justice, she is often identified with Kanlaon volcano on the island of Negros.

The myth

Although usually spoken of as female, Laon also has a male aspect, Makapatag, 'the leveler', a destructive deity of punishment and vengeance, while the female form is milder and more just. As supreme creator she is linked to Makaako, who dwells on the uppermost level of the universe.

In the Visayan creation myths, Laon creates the first creature, a bird (manaul), which finds the first islands and indirectly brings about the first man and woman, found inside a bamboo or rattan stem. In the Hinilawod epic of the Suludnon people she appears as Laonsina, a sky goddess, alongside Tungkung Langit.

Worship and religion

In ancient times, shamans (babaylan) climbed the volcano to perform rituals after every good harvest or during special ceremonies, and offered gifts as a sign of respect. According to Miguel de Loarca, people invoked her for good harvests and feared the locust plagues she sent when displeased.

Legacy

Her name varies among Visayan groups: Lahon, Lalaon, Lauon, Malaon, Raom or Laonsina. Due to a mistranslation in a 1903 book, she is sometimes wrongly presented as a goddess of fire.

Ancient sources: Miguel de Loarca records her in the Relación de las Yslas Filipinas (1582), Pedro Chirino describes her in 1604 and Francisco Ignacio Alcina in 1668; the Hinilawod epic remembers her as Laonsina.

Trivia

A fire goddess by mistake. She is sometimes confused with a fire goddess due to a 1903 mistranslation: where the original says the volcano hurls fire, it was read as her hurling it.
The suffix «sina». In Laonsina, «sina» means 'foreigner', suggesting the deity arrived from neighbouring Visayan peoples.
