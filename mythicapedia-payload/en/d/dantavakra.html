king of Karusha in Hindu mythology

Dantavakra

Dantavakra is, in Hindu tradition, the king of Karusha and an enemy of Krishna. His story appears in the Puranas and the Mahabharata.

The myth

According to the myth, he is the third birth of Vijaya, Vishnu's gatekeeper, whose brother Jaya is reborn as Shishupala. The Puranas do not agree on his lineage, and his name, meaning crooked teeth, refers to a dental deformity present from birth. He was an ally of Jarasandha, Kamsa and Shishupala.

The Harivamsha portrays him more sympathetically: on learning that Krishna planned to elope with Rukmini, he recognises the god in him and wishes to become his friend.

In the Mahabharata, after the Rajasuya sacrifice, which he skipped in protest at Jarasandha's death, he attacks Krishna to avenge Shishupala. Krishna strikes his chest with the mace Kaumodaki, and he dies spitting blood. His brother Viduratha falls in the same battle.

Ancient sources: The Puranas (Padma, Vishnu and Bhagavata), the Harivamsha and the Mahabharata.

Trivia

A sympathetic portrait. In the Harivamsha, Dantavakra recognises Krishna as God and wishes for his friendship, very different from his role as an enemy in other texts.
A name from his teeth. His name means crooked teeth, a feature he was said to have had since birth.
