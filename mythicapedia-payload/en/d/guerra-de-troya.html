Trojan War

The Trojan War is the great epic conflict of Greek mythology: a coalition of Achaean kings besieged the city of Troy, in Asia Minor, for ten years after Paris of Troy took Helen from her husband Menelaus. It is the core of the Greco-Roman epic tradition and the subject of Homer's Iliad and Odyssey.

The myth

According to the myth, the war was set in motion by a quarrel among the gods. Zeus had seized power by overthrowing his father Cronus, and a prophecy warned him that one of his own sons would depose him. Another prophecy foretold that a son of the nymph Thetis would be greater than his father, so Zeus arranged her marriage to the mortal king Peleus. Their son Achilles, according to yet another prophecy, would die young at Troy.

At the wedding of Peleus and Thetis, Eris, who had not been invited, left a golden apple inscribed "for the fairest." Hera, Athena and Aphrodite all claimed it, and Zeus named Paris, a Trojan prince raised as a shepherd, as judge. Each goddess tried to win him over: Athena offered victory in battle, Hera power, and Aphrodite the love of the most beautiful woman. Paris chose Aphrodite, who promised him Helen, daughter of Zeus according to Homer and wife of Menelaus, king of Sparta.

Many princes had courted Helen, and Odysseus persuaded them all to swear to defend whichever suitor she chose. When Paris carried her off with Aphrodite's help while Menelaus was away, the Greek kings gathered under Agamemnon, Menelaus's brother, to honour the oath. Odysseus feigned madness to avoid the war, but was exposed when he refused to harm his son Telemachus. The seer Calchas declared that Troy could not fall without Achilles, whom his mother had hidden disguised as a woman on Scyros.

The Achaean fleet of more than a thousand ships besieged Troy for ten years. The Iliad covers only a few days in the tenth year, centred on Achilles' wrath and the death of Hector, while the Odyssey follows Odysseus on his long voyage home.

Legacy

The Trojan War inspired a vast literary and artistic tradition, from Greek tragedy to the Roman poets Virgil and Ovid. The Romans regarded themselves as descendants of the Trojans who survived the war, and the story of Aeneas fleeing Troy became their mythic origin.

Ancient sources: The Iliad and the Odyssey, attributed to Homer, and a cycle of lost epic poems preserved only in fragments; also the Cypria, attributed to Stasinus, which recounts the origins of the war.

Trivia

A debate that remains open. Heinrich Schliemann's excavations at Hisarlık, in Turkey, place Troy where most scholars now accept the city stood. However, there is no proof that a Greek expedition like the one in the poems ever took place.
