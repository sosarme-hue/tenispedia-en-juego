Greek sea goddess, daughter of Poseidon

Benthesikyme

Benthesikyme is a sea goddess of Greek myth, daughter of Poseidon and Amphitrite and sister of Triton. She appears in a single story, that of Eumolpus, whom she raised as a foster mother.

The myth

According to the mythographer Apollodorus, Poseidon and Amphitrite, daughter of Oceanus, had Triton and Rhode, who married Helios. Benthesikyme appears later, in the myth of Eumolpus.

Chione, daughter of Boreas, bore a child to Poseidon and, to hide the birth from her father, threw the infant into the sea. Poseidon rescued him, took him to Ethiopia and entrusted him to Benthesikyme to raise. Once grown, Eumolpus was given one of the couple's two daughters in marriage by Benthesikyme's husband, but he tried to force himself on the other daughter and was banished. He left with his son Ismarus for the court of Tegyrius, king of Thrace, who gave his daughter in marriage to Eumolpus's son.

The sources do not name Benthesikyme's husband or daughters, nor say whether she was herself a sea nymph. A late source links her to Daira, one of the Oceanids, and adds that Eumolpus and Daira had Eumaras, identified with Ismarus. In a modern reading, Robert Graves saw Amphitrite's children as a lunar triad: Triton as the new moon, Rhode as the full moon and Benthesikyme as the dangerous old moon.

Legacy

The name comes from the Greek root benthos, "depth of the sea", a term biologists use for the sea floor. Benthesikyme also gave her name to the Benthesicymidae, a family of shrimp.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, in his Library, is the only ancient source that mentions her.

Trivia

A sea name. Her name means "wave of the deep sea", formed from the root benthos, which biologists use for the sea floor.
From myth to shrimp. The shrimp family Benthesicymidae takes its name from this goddess.
