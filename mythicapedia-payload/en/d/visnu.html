Hindu god of preservation

Vishnu

Vishnu is one of the principal deities of Hinduism, regarded in Vaishnavism as the Supreme Being and as part of the Trimurti alongside Brahma and Shiva. His role is to preserve the universe and protect dharma, and he does so by descending in avatars such as Rama and Krishna.

The myth

The earliest Vedas barely mention him: in the Rig Veda he appears as a minor god, though scholars suggest he was more important than those texts show. Over the centuries, in the Puranas, he grew into one of the most powerful deities.

Tradition says Vishnu rests on the ocean of milk (Kshira Sagara) on the cosmic serpent Shesha, and that as Mahavishnu he sleeps in the causal ocean, exhaling universes and inhaling them back. When the world is threatened by evil, he descends as an avatar to restore order. The Dashavatara are his ten main avatars, among them Rama, hero of the Ramayana, and Krishna, central figure of the Mahabharata and the Bhagavad Gita.

His consort is Lakshmi and his vehicle is Garuda. He is shown with blue skin and four arms holding a lotus, a conch (shankha), a mace and the Sudarshana Chakra discus, which he uses to defeat demons. He dwells in Vaikuntha, and the Ganges is said to flow from his feet. As a son, Kamadeva is attributed to him, though accounts of that lineage vary across texts.

Worship and religion

Vishnu is worshipped in countless temples across India and in the diaspora, and his cult remains active within Vaishnavism and the Smarta tradition. Among the best-known shrines are those dedicated to Krishna and Rama, and in South India, Venkateshwara at Tirupati and Ranganathaswamy at Srirangam.

Legacy

His figure has shaped Hindu religious art, where his iconography is instantly recognisable, and devotional literature in the Puranas and epics. As a public deity, he remains one of the most revered figures in contemporary Hinduism.

Ancient sources: The Rig Veda mentions him as a secondary god; the Atharva Veda describes his son Kamadeva; the Bhagavata Purana and the Mahabharata develop his role as preserver and avatar-bearer.

Trivia

Vishnu's names. The texts give him hundreds of epithets. The Vishnu Sahasranama, included in the Mahabharata, lists over a thousand names describing his qualities.
