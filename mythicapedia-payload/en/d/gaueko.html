spirit of the night in Basque mythology

Gaueko

Gaueko is the spirit of the night in Basque mythology, a lord of darkness who reigns from midnight until dawn. His name literally means “of the night”.

The myth

According to tradition, Gaueko could take many forms: a black wolf, sometimes standing on two legs, or a cow, an owl or a sheep. He was feared for devouring shepherds and sheep, and some tales link him to the goddess Mari, who could master him.

In one story, people asked Mari for help. She first gave them the light of the moon, then that of the sun, but the night remained dangerous. So she blessed homes with the eguzkilore, a flower whose countless bracts forced any evil spirit that found it to keep counting until daylight caught it. Gaueko's night law forbade people to leave the house, boast or steal. A charcoal burner from Escoriaza once met a bull blocking his path; it was Gaueko, who chased him until the man managed to escape.

Worship and religion

There is little evidence of temples or festivals devoted to him; his role was mainly protective. The eguzkilore, a thistle-like plant common in the region, was placed at house entrances as an amulet against night spirits.

Trivia

A name that is a realm. Gaueko literally means “of the night” in Basque, and his refrain proclaims that the night belongs to those of the night.
The thistle that guarded homes. The eguzkilore is a thistle-like plant, Carlina acaulis, abundant in the region and used as a protective amulet.
