Japanese god of wind

Shinatsuhiko

Shinatsuhiko is the kami of wind in Japanese mythology. He appears in both of the country's great ancient chronicles and is honoured at the Ise Shrine, one of Japan's most important sanctuaries.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Shinatsuhiko is the direct son of Izanagi and Izanami, the divine couple who gave birth to many islands and deities. The Nihon Shoki tells a different story: he was born from the breath of the morning mist, breathed out by Izanami, and arose together with Shinatobe, whom the text regards as his sister.

Shinatobe may originally have been a separate goddess of wind, and the two were later linked as a single pair of wind deities.

Worship and religion

Both deities are venerated together at the Kaza-hi-no-miya shrine, within the Naikū complex of the Ise Shrine, and at the Kaza-no-miya shrine, within its Gekū complex.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, the two great ancient texts of Japanese mythology.

Trivia

Two versions of his origin. The Kojiki presents him as the direct son of Izanagi and Izanami, while the Nihon Shoki says he was born from the morning mist.
A name in two texts. His name is written differently in each ancient work: 志那都比古神 in the Kojiki and 級長津彦命 in the Nihon Shoki.
