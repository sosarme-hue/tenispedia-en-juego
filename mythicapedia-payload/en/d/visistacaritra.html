bodhisattva of the Lotus Sutra

Viśiṣṭacāritra

Viśiṣṭacāritra is a bodhisattva of Mahayana Buddhism who appears in the Lotus Sutra. He is one of the four great perfected bodhisattvas who attend Gautama Buddha and protect the scripture and those who follow it.

The myth

According to the sutra, Viśiṣṭacāritra appears in chapters 15, 21 and 22 and is associated with the Superior Practice. Together with Anantacaritra, Visuddhacaritra and Supratisthitacaritra, he watches over the Buddhist teaching.

Some texts link him to the "true self", a quality of buddhahood that corresponds to the selflessness of nirvana.

Worship and religion

In Nichiren schools, especially Nichiren Shōshū, Soka Gakkai and Kempon Hokke, Nichiren is believed to have fulfilled this bodhisattva's vow by revealing the practice of daimoku, chanting the title of the sutra, Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō. According to Anesaki, Nichiren identified himself with him after his exile to Sado Island. These groups still keep this chanting as a living practice of faith.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra (Saddharmapuṇḍarīka), in its chapters 15, 21 and 22.

Trivia

A jewel wrapped up. The Object of Devotion for Observing the Mind says the Buddha wrapped the gem of the doctrine in the five characters of Myōhō Renge Kyō and hung them as an adornment on the necks of ignorant people of the latter age.
