sacred river of the Rigveda, later goddess of knowledge in Hinduism

Sarasvati

Sarasvati is a river mentioned in the Vedas, the oldest sacred texts of Hinduism, and later also a goddess. Its identification with a real watercourse remains debated, but it is considered one of the most important rivers of the Vedic tradition.

The myth

In the Rigveda, the Sarasvati appears as a great sacred river of north-western India, mentioned around seventy times and present in nearly all books of the text, except the fourth. It is listed among the seven main rivers, and in the Nadistuti hymn (10.75) it lies between the Yamuna to the east and the Sutlej to the west. The earliest hymns describe it as mighty and purifying, while in the later books it appears as a small river ending in a lake or inland sea.

Later Vedic texts, such as the Tandya Brahmana, the Jaiminiya Brahmana and the Mahabharata, tell that the river dried up in a desert. Tradition gives different reasons for this; the Bhagavata Purana, written many centuries later, links it to the heat produced by the austerities and sacrifice of the sage Kardama. Over time the river came to be personified as a goddess, later associated with speech, wisdom and learning.

Worship and religion

The Sarasvati held an important place in Vedic religion, and its course was regarded as the birthplace of Vedic Sanskrit and of sacred texts. According to tradition, the river meets the Ganges and Yamuna in a metaphysical form at the Triveni Sangam, a Hindu pilgrimage site. The goddess Sarasvati is still worshipped today as the patron of knowledge, the arts and music.

Legacy

Sarasvati is the name of a river in modern India and of the goddess of music, speech and wisdom in present-day Hinduism. Some scholars have linked the river to the now-dry Ghaggar-Hakra, and others to the Helmand in Afghanistan; these hypotheses remain unconfirmed.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda, the later Brahmanas, the Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana mention the river, and the Rigveda also addresses the goddess in several hymns.

Trivia

Mythical confluence. In tradition, the Sarasvati joins the Ganges and Yamuna invisibly at the Triveni Sangam in Prayagraj.
A river celebrated in hymns. It is the only river that has hymns dedicated entirely to it in the Rigveda.
