Greek hero, son of Alcmaeon and Manto

Amphilochus

Amphilochus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Alcmaeon, one of the Epigoni, and Manto, daughter of the Theban seer Teiresias. His story centres on a childhood marked by his family's separation and on the founding of the city of Amphilochian Argos.

The myth

According to a story ascribed to Euripides' lost play Alcmaeon in Corinth, Manto was sent first to Delphi and then to Caria. Alcmaeon entrusted his young son and his daughter Tisiphone to King Creon of Corinth, but Creon's queen sold the girl into slavery. Alcmaeon eventually discovered this and recovered both children.

According to Apollodorus, Amphilochus founded Amphilochian Argos, although this is usually credited to his uncle of the same name.

Ancient sources: Euripides, Alcmaeon in Corinth (lost), preserved in the Bibliotheca and by Apollodorus.

Trivia

A disputed founding. Although Apollodorus credits Amphilochus with founding Amphilochian Argos, the city is usually attributed to his uncle, who bore the same name.
A sister sold. In Euripides' lost play, the queen of Corinth sells Tisiphone, Amphilochus' sister, into slavery until their father recovers her.
