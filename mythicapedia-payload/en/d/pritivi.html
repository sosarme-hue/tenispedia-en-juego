Hindu goddess of the Earth

Prithvi

Prithvi (Sanskrit pṛthvī, "the Vast One") is the Earth in Sanskrit and, in Hinduism, the goddess who personifies it, a great Mother Goddess. She appears in the Vedas alongside the Sky, Dyaus, and in later tradition she remains a symbol of the ground that supports and feeds all beings.

The myth

In the Rigveda, Prithvi is usually paired with Dyaus, the Father Sky. Together they form the dual deity Dyavaprithivi, the universal parents who gave rise to the world and the gods. Some hymns suggest that the two were once inseparable, until Varuna parted them by decree. Dyaus makes the Earth fertile with rain, and she brings forth food. According to one tradition, Prithvi is the mother of Indra and Agni. Another story tells that when Indra killed Dyaus, she applauded and afterwards married him.

In the Atharvaveda, Prithvi gains a more independent role and is celebrated as the queen of all creation. One hymn says she was born from Aditi, the boundless. The Prithvi Sukta, the longest praise of her in Vedic literature, shows her as a gentle mother who receives the dead, covering them lightly as a mother covers her child with her garment. In classical Hinduism her figure gives way to the goddess Bhumi, and Prithvi becomes one of her epithets. She is associated with Vishnu, and linked to his avatars Varaha and Prithu. In some versions, Narakasura is her son.

Worship and religion

Vedic hymns invoke her for wealth, prosperity, strength and protection from harm. In the Atharvaveda, the dangers linked to the Earth, such as death and disease, were averted through prayers, sacrifices and amulets, in order to keep harmony with the goddess. Her association with the cow, a symbol of nourishment and motherhood, reinforced her role as provider.

Legacy

Prithvi remains the Sanskrit name for the Earth. In early Buddhism, according to the Pali Canon, she appears at the moment of Gautama's enlightenment, when she bears witness to the Buddha's power to awaken and drives away Mara. As a mother Earth, she is comparable to Gaia, Tellus, Jörð and Žemyna in other mythologies.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda, the Atharvaveda (especially the Prithvi Sukta, Atharvaveda 12.1) and the Pali Canon for her role in Buddhism.

Trivia

A kiss at the centre of the world. According to the Rigveda, Dyaus and Prithvi are described as kissing the central point of the world, an image of the union between sky and earth.
A name meaning "the vast one". Her name traces back to a Proto-Indo-European epithet meaning "the Broad One", which gave rise to the Vedic idea of Mother Earth.
