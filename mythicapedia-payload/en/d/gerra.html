Mesopotamian god of fire

Gerra

Gerra, also called Girra, is the god of fire in Babylonian and Akkadian mythology, inheriting the role of the older Sumerian god Gibil. He could embody fire as a useful force, linked to furnaces and metalworking, but also as a cause of destruction.

The myth

According to Babylonian tradition, Gerra was a son of Anu, the sky god and "father of the gods", and of the goddess Antu. Other texts make Enlil his father, or name other deities, and the genealogies vary between authors and periods.

His main role was to ward off malevolent magic and ominous portents revealed in nightmares, a function described in incantation series such as Maqlû and Šurpu. He also played a central part in ritual purification. He was linked to the sun god Shamash, and in some rites he was believed to take the sun's place at night, and to the god Nuska.

Worship and religion

Gerra is sparsely attested, but he appears from the Early Dynastic period in offering lists from Shuruppak and was worshiped in Nippur during the Kassite period. Texts associate him with the city of Eridu. No representations of him are known; his symbol was a torch.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Maqlû incantation series, in the god list An = Anum and in various literary and ritual texts from Mesopotamia.
