legendary king of Munster in Irish mythology

Mug Nuadat

Mug Nuadat is a legendary king of Munster in medieval Irish tradition, a rival of the High King Conn of the Hundred Battles. His name, meaning «servant of Nuada», has led some scholars to suggest he may originally have been the god Nuada himself.

The myth

According to medieval tradition, he was the de facto ruler of the southern half of Ireland from the year 123. That territory became known as Leth Moga, «Mug's half», while the north was Leth Cuinn, «Conn's half». Conn drove him from Ireland, and Mug took refuge in Spain, from where he returned with an army. He was defeated and killed by Conn at Mag Léna, though some versions say Conn treacherously murdered him in his bed.

Mug was the father of Ailill Ollamh and the grandfather of Éogan Mór, whom tradition credits with founding the Eóganachta dynasty.

Legacy

According to John O'Hart, the name Magh-Nuadhad, now Maynooth, comes from Éogan Mór, whom he calls «Mogha Nuadhad».

Trivia

A modern hypothesis. The scholar T.F. O'Rahilly suggested that Mug Nuadat might really be the god Nuada rather than a historical person. It is also possible that Éogan Mór was the earthly representation of that deity.
A name that endures. The name Magh-Nuadhad survives in Maynooth, an Irish town whose modern name derives from this older form.
