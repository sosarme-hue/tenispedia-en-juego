cosmogonies of ancient Egypt

Ancient Egyptian creation myths

A set of ancient Egyptian accounts of how the world took shape from a primordial sea, with versions that differed according to the city promoting them. According to the reference text, they are the earliest religious compilations in the world.

The myth

The Egyptians held that the world arose from the lifeless waters of chaos, called Nun, when the sun first rose in a distant time known as zp tpj, "the first time". The first thing to emerge was the benben, a pyramid-shaped mound, an image probably inspired by the fertile land left behind as the Nile's annual flood receded.

The cosmogonies varied from sanctuary to sanctuary. At Hermopolis, the Ogdoad, eight deities representing the qualities of the primeval waters, divided into male frogs and female snakes; their convergence caused a great upheaval from which the mound and then the sun arose. At Heliopolis, Atum self-engendered and gave rise to Shu and Tefnut, who in turn produced Geb and Nut; their children Osiris, Isis, Set and Nephthys formed the Ennead. At Memphis, Ptah created the world through his heart and tongue, naming things into being. At Thebes, Amun was the hidden force behind all things, and one myth likened his act of creation to the call of a goose breaking the stillness of the waters.

Worship and religion

Each account was tied to the cult of a god in one of the great cities: Hermopolis, Heliopolis, Memphis and Thebes. Amun became the supreme god of the pantheon, and his cult drove the growth of Thebes as a major religious centre, with columned halls, obelisks and colossal statues.

Ancient sources: Pyramid Texts, tomb wall decorations and funerary inscriptions from the Old Kingdom.

Trivia

Zep Tepi. The term zp tpj, "the first time", denotes the moment of transition from primordial chaos to the world as it exists.
Atum's metaphors. In the Heliopolitan myth, Atum produced Shu and Tefnut through a metaphor of masturbation, and also "sneezed" and "spat" them out, puns on their names.
