Hindu goddess, sister of Krishna and form of Yogamaya

Ekanamsha

Ekanamsha is a Hindu goddess associated with Vishnu's illusory power, known as Yogamaya. She is linked to the Krishna cycle and to his sister Subhadra, and her figure appears in early Mathura iconography.

The myth

According to the Krishna cycle, Ekanamsha is the daughter of Nanda and Yashoda, born at the same time Krishna enters the world. To protect him from the tyrant Kamsa, her place is swapped with the baby's, and Kamsa believes the newborn is Devaki's ninth child. When the goddess leaves her infant body, she reveals herself, warns Kamsa of his impending death, and then vanishes.

Her name, meaning "the single, portionless one", is explained in several ways: because she alone protected Krishna, or because she was born from a part of Vishnu himself. In the Harivamsa she is a shakti of Vishnu linked to the Ekadasi day, while the Brahmavaivarta Purana describes her as an avatar of Durga. In Puri tradition she reincarnated as Subhadra, daughter of Vasudeva and Rohini, although the scriptures do not clarify whether Yogamaya becomes Subhadra once her role is done.

Worship and religion

The Vrishnis and Abhiras are said to have worshipped her as an ancient goddess. In Puri, Odisha, the name Yogamaya is practically synonymous with Subhadra, who receives prominent worship, and the triad appears in the later Jagannath tradition.

Legacy

Her figure appears in many kinship triads found in the Mathura region, dated to the early centuries of the Common Era.

Ancient sources: The Harivamsa, the Vishnudharmottara Purana and the Brahmavaivarta Purana describe her with differing identities and roles.
