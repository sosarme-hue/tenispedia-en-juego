vampire-like hag of Cantabrian legend

Guajona

The Guajona, also called Lumia, is a monster of Cantabrian oral tradition, a vampire-like hag who drains the blood of healthy children and young people while they sleep.

The myth

According to popular belief, the Guajona is a thin old woman with gnarled hands, bird's feet, a yellowed face covered in warts and coarse hair, and tiny eyes that shine like stars. Her most feared feature is a single long, black, razor-sharp tooth that reaches below her chin.

She appears only at night. She slips into houses unnoticed and, while healthy children and young people sleep, approaches them in silence to sink that tooth into a vein and suck their blood. She does not kill them; she leaves them almost bloodless, so they wake up tired, pale and drained. Adults and the elderly are not targeted. Where she spends the day is a mystery, though she is thought to hide underground.

Legacy

Her name comes from Guaja, also found in Asturian folklore as Guaxa, and its origin is debated: some link it to Arabic wahsh, 'beast', others to Latin hostem, 'enemy'. She is one of the few vampire legends in Spain, alongside the Conde Estruch. Manuel Llano recorded her in his writings, and Miguel de Unamuno quoted her.

Trivia

Two possible roots. Scholar Xosé Lluis García Arias proposes the Arabic wahsh, 'beast', while Xulio Concepción links the name to Latin hostem, 'enemy'.
An augmentative name. Her name is an augmentative of Guaja, the same word that appears in Asturias as Guaxa.
