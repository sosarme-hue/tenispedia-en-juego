Son of the Dagda and one of the Tuatha Dé Danann

Cermait

Cermait is one of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology and a son of the god Dagda. His killing by Lugh and the revenge taken by his three sons place him at the heart of one of the best-known stories of the Irish cycle.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cermait had an affair with Buach, the wife of Lugh, and Lugh killed him for it. His father, the Dagda, wept tears of blood. The Dagda carried his son's body across the world until, in the east, he met three men carrying their fathers' treasures. Among them was a magic staff that gave death from one end and life from the other, and with it the Dagda brought Cermait back to life.

His three sons, Mac Cuill, Mac Cecht and Mac Gréine, avenged his death and went on to rule together as joint High Kings of Ireland. Cermait was known by the epithet Milbél, meaning honey-mouth.

Ancient sources: The Yellow Book of Lecan, a medieval Irish manuscript, and the Dindsenchas, which also mention Conan Honey-mouth, son of the Dagda, who may be the same figure as Cermait.

Trivia

Tears of blood. The texts say that the god Dagda wept tears of blood over the death of his son.
Kings by inheritance. Cermait's three sons, who avenged his death, ended up as joint High Kings of Ireland.
