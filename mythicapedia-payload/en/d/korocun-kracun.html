Slavic winter solstice festival and spirit of sudden death

Koročun (Kračun)

Koročun, or Kračun, is the Slavic name for the winter solstice, a pre-Christian festival that today gives its name to Christmas in several countries. It also refers to an evil spirit that shortens life and brings sudden death.

The myth

Tradition placed around December 20 or 21, the longest night of the year, the moment when the old sun weakened and died while spirits of darkness, above all the progenitor deity, usually Veles, reached their greatest power. In Christianized beliefs the sun died on December 24 and was reborn around December 21 or 25 as a young god, associated with Svarozhits or Dazhbog.

The origin of the name is debated. Max Vasmer derived it from a Slavic verb meaning to take steps, a idea that suits the transitional character of the solstice. Others link it to Slavic words for short, and some propose a Latin or Romanian origin.

Worship and religion

Modern scholars often link the festival to ancestor worship. Western Slavs lit fires in cemeteries to keep their dead warm, held feasts in their honour and lit wooden logs at crossroads.

Legacy

The word is the common name for Christmas in Romania and Hungary. In some Slavic languages it came to mean the unexpected death of a young person and the spirit that shortens life.

Ancient sources: The Novgorod First Chronicle mentions it in 1143, referring to the winter solstice as Koročun.

Trivia

Festival and demon. The same word names both the festival and a spirit that shortens life, showing how closely the solstice was linked to death.
Logs at crossroads. Western Slavs lit wooden logs at local crossroads during this night.
