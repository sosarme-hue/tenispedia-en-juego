Greek goddess of truth

Aletheia

Aletheia is the Greek personification of truth, the goddess of what is not hidden. Her name literally means "unconcealment" or "disclosure", and her Roman equivalent is Veritas.

The myth

Texts describing Aletheia are scarce, and the ancient accounts of her are thin. According to some versions, she is a daughter of Zeus, although genealogies vary depending on the tradition consulted.

Most of the details known come from her Latin counterpart, Veritas, to whom different parents are assigned: Saturn, Jupiter or, in other versions, Prometheus. The elusive goddess was said to have hidden at the bottom of a holy well. In art she appears as a young virgin dressed in white or as the "naked truth" (nuda veritas), holding a hand mirror.

Legacy

The concept echoed in philosophy. Martin Heidegger distinguished the Greek aletheia, understood as "unconcealment", from the Roman veritas, linked to rightness and justice. Today the Latin motto Veritas appears on the crests of many universities, such as Harvard.

Trivia

Heidegger and truth. The German philosopher Martin Heidegger argued that the Greek idea of truth, aletheia, means "unconcealment" and differs from the Roman veritas, which is linked to rightness.
A university motto. The Latin word Veritas is the motto of Harvard University and of many other colleges and organizations around the world.
