Māori god of cultivated plants

Rongo

Rongo is a god (atua) in Māori mythology of New Zealand, protector of cultivated plants, above all the kūmara or sweet potato. He was also an agricultural and war god in the southern Cook Islands, especially on Mangaia.

The myth

In the creation story of the Arawa canoe tribes, Rongo was a son of the primordial parents Rangi and Papa. Together with his brothers he agreed to separate them so that light could enter the world. Tāwhirimātea, god of storms, opposed this and furiously attacked his brothers; Rongo and Haumia, god of wild food, sheltered inside Papa, mother earth, until the storm passed.

In some traditions he is the father of the kūmara, a crop that needed peaceful times to grow, which is why rongo can also mean 'peace'.

Worship and religion

On Mangaia he had two marae with hereditary high priests, Orongo and Akaoro. Cooked taro parcels were offered there to secure victory in war and the fertility of the land, and human sacrifices were sometimes made. Both sites were destroyed after Christianity arrived in the early 19th century.

Legacy

The waiting room of Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room tells his legend of flying the first kite, with Rongo voiced by Ernest Tavares.
