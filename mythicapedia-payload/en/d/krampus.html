Krampus

Krampus is a horned creature from Alpine folklore who accompanies Saint Nicholas on the eve of his feast to punish badly behaved children. Documented since the 16th century, he is one of the most recognizable figures of the Central European Christmas season.

The myth

According to the tradition of Austria, Bavaria, Slovenia and other Alpine regions, on the night of 5 December, known as Krampusnacht, Krampus roams the streets, sometimes alone and sometimes beside Saint Nicholas. While the saint rewards good children with small gifts, Krampus deals with the naughty ones: he swats them with birch rods and, in the older versions, stuffs them into a sack or basket strapped to his back to carry them off to Hell.

He is usually shown as a hairy, horned man with a long tongue, fangs and goat's hooves. He rattles heavy chains, sometimes with bells attached, which are read as a symbol of the Devil being bound by the Church. Local people believe he descends from a pre-Christian supernatural being later merged with the Christian devil, though historians consider such an early origin unlikely.

In the 17th century he was given the role of helper to Saint Nicholas, since a saint was not considered fitting for delivering punishments. Similar companions, such as Knecht Ruprecht, Schmutzli, Père Fouettard and Zwarte Piet, play comparable roles elsewhere in Europe.

Worship and religion

The tradition remains alive. In many Alpine villages, young men dressed as Krampus take part in processions known as Krampus runs, where they scare spectators with their antics. In Styria, Krampus presents families with bundles of birch rods that are painted gold and displayed year-round as a reminder to children. It is customary to offer Krampusschnaps, a strong fruit brandy.

Legacy

Since the 19th century, Europeans have exchanged Christmas cards featuring Krampus, often with humorous rhymes. He has inspired films such as Krampus (2015) by Michael Dougherty and appears in Roma (2018) by Alfonso Cuarón. Television has taken him into series such as Grimm and Supernatural, and he appears in video games such as Fortnite, Overwatch and Dead by Daylight.

Trivia

Golden rods. In Styria, the birch rod bundles Krampus gives to families are painted gold and displayed throughout the year.
A worrying pamphlet. In the 1950s, the Austrian government distributed pamphlets titled Krampus Is an Evil Man, fearing that encounters with him could harm children's mental health.
