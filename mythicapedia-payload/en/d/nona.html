Roman goddess of pregnancy and one of the Parcae

Nona

Nona is one of the Roman Parcae, the personifications of destiny, and the goddess associated with pregnancy and childbirth. She is the Roman counterpart of the Greek Clotho.

The myth

According to tradition, Nona spins the thread of each person's life from birth to death, and decides when the child should come into the world. Her name is linked to the ninth month of pregnancy, when the child was due.

Together with her sisters Decima and Morta, she controls the metaphorical thread of life. According to Aulus Gellius, she also determined a person's life expectancy on the dies lustricus, the day of purification when the baby's name was chosen: the ninth day after birth for boys and the eighth for girls.

Worship and religion

Pregnant women called upon her during their ninth month, when the birth was expected.

Ancient sources: Aulus Gellius mentions her when explaining her name among the Parcae.

Trivia

The baby's name. On the dies lustricus the child's name was chosen, a rite tied to the life that Nona was said to measure.
Parcae and Norns. The Greeks called them Moirai and the Germanic peoples Norns, figures with similar roles.
