legendary lost city of Inca tradition

Paititi

Paititi is a legendary Inca lost city or utopian rich land, said to lie in the Amazon rainforest east of the Andes, within Peru, Bolivia or Brazil. Its search has drawn explorers for a century, and its existence remains debated.

The myth

According to the legend, Paititi was a refuge of the Incas, a land rich in gold, silver and precious stones beyond the mountains. Early colonial texts link it to the conquests of the Inca Pachacuti, who supposedly drew jungle peoples into his empire, and to the expeditions of Túpac Yupanqui toward the Chunchos and the Musus or Moxos.

A Peruvian version connects Paititi with Inkarri, a culture hero who, after founding Q'ero and Cusco, withdrew to the jungle of Pantiacolla to spend his last days in that city. In the account of Diego Felipe de Alcaya, Paititi was a region of fertile plains and pearl-rich lagoons, where the Inca Manco ruled after crossing a river and leaving a stone figure as a sign of his realm.

Legacy

The name Paititi has inspired real expeditions since the twentieth century and appears in Arthur Conan Doyle's novel The Lost World. It also features in films, in the video game Shadow of the Tomb Raider and in Civilization VI.

Ancient sources: Colonial chronicles of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, including those of Vaca de Castro, Garcilaso de la Vega and Diego Felipe de Alcaya.

Trivia

A stone map. In 2005, explorers studied the Pusharo petroglyphs and nearby geoglyphs, which some thought might show where Paititi lay.
An expedition that vanished. In 1971, a French-American expedition went up the Pantiacolla river in search of Paititi and never returned.
