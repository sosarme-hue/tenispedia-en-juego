Lithuanian goddess of love and freedom

Milda

Milda is the goddess of love and freedom said to have been worshipped in Lithuania, also known as Alexota or Aleksota. Her existence is widely debated, since little evidence supports it.

The myth

Milda was first mentioned in 1835, in Teodor Narbutt's history of Lithuania. According to him, she had temples in Aleksotas, near Kaunas, and in Antakalnis, in Vilnius, and April was dedicated to her. Narbutt linked her to bronze figurines found in several places.

Other scholars, such as Aleksander Brückner and Norbertas Vėlius, doubt she ever existed. Vėlius argues that Narbutt may have borrowed the idea of a Roman nymph called Alexothe from a Polish author, and that the name fitted a suburb of Kaunas.

Worship and religion

According to Narbutt, her cult centred on the Kaunas and Vilnius area. No clear documentary or archaeological evidence confirms it. Today, neo-pagan communities such as Romuva hold events in her honour in May.

Legacy

Her modern popularity owes much to Józef Ignacy Kraszewski, who featured her in his 1840 epic poem Witolorauda. Wincenty Smokowski's woodcuts for the 1846 Polish edition have shaped her image. Milda is also a common given name in Lithuania, and a mountain on Venus is named after her.

Trivia

A mountain on Venus. Milda Mons, a mountain on Venus, is named after this Lithuanian goddess.
A given name. After her popularisation, Milda became a common female given name in Lithuania.
