Nubian god of incense and prosperity

Dedun

Dedun, also spelled Dedwen, was a god of southern Egypt and Nubia, attested as early as 2400 BCE. He was linked to incense, a precious luxury that came from Nubia, and so became a protector of wealth and good fortune.

The myth

Little is known about Dedun's original nature. Early depictions show him as a young man from the south and as a falcon; from the New Kingdom onward he appears as a lion, a role usually given to the son of another deity. Nothing survives of the earlier Kushite myths from which he may have emerged.

The earliest Egyptian writings that mention him already present him as a god of incense. In the Middle Kingdom, when Egypt ruled Kush, he was regarded as protector of deceased Nubian rulers and linked to funerary rites. His main role was burning incense at royal births to drive away harmful spirits. According to some documents, a great fire in a temple complex shared by several gods may have started in a temple of Dedun, which is why he is associated with an attempt to destroy other gods.

Later he was seen as the personification of the South, just as Ha represented the West, Sopedu the East and Sobek the North. He appears in the myth of the Distant Goddess, accompanying Shu to fetch Sekhmet and bring her back to reason, and he belongs to the lesser Ennead.

Worship and religion

The Pyramid Texts call him Lord of Nubia and Lord of Punt, but there is no evidence that he was worshipped north of Aswan. He protected the fortress of Semna. Thutmose III of the 18th Dynasty ordered temples built in his honour at el-Lesiya and Uronarti. A small chapel to him was rescued from the waters of Lake Nasser and moved to the new Kalabsha.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Pyramid Texts, which name him as a Nubian deity.

Trivia

A luxury commodity. In his time incense was extremely expensive, and the trade that came from Nubia enriched the region and made Dedun a god of prosperity.
A temple at Jebel Barkal. The Kushite ruler Atlanersa of Napata began a temple to the syncretic god Osiris-Dedun at Jebel Barkal.
