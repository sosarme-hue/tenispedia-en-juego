giant demoness of Hindu mythology

Pūtanā

Pūtanā is a giant demoness in Hindu mythology, famous for trying to kill the infant god Krishna with poisoned milk. Her story matters because it brings together danger, motherhood and redemption in a single tale.

The myth

According to the tradition, the wicked king Kamsa, Krishna's uncle, sent her to kill the child, who was only a few months old. Pūtanā appeared as a young, beautiful wet nurse in Gokul, the cowherd village where Krishna lived, and his foster-mother Yashoda let her take the baby into her lap. She had smeared poison on her breasts, although in later versions the milk itself is poisoned.

Krishna, however, drew out not only her milk but also her life. She regained her giant form, fell to the ground screaming, and her body crushed a forest; her cremation took several days. According to the Bhagavata Purana, having nursed the god, Pūtanā attained liberation and a place in the spiritual world as Krishna's eternal nurse. Another version tells of an earlier birth as Ratnamala, daughter of the demon king Bali, who wanted Vamana as her son before deciding to kill him.

Worship and religion

Ancient Indian medical texts prescribe her worship to protect children from disease, and she is counted among the Matrikas, the powerful mother goddesses. The sources do not describe a living cult.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions her as a protective rakshasi, and the story is told in the Harivamsa, the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Uncertain etymology. A popular explanation links her name to pūta, meaning pure, and na, meaning not, but scholars do not consider it certain. Another connects it to a word for a hell associated with parents and children.
A death that saves her. Although Krishna kills her, the sources say that the milk he drank cleansed her of sin and granted her the same heaven that Yashoda attained.
