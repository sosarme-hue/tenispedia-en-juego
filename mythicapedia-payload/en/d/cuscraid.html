hero of the Ulster Cycle, son of King Conchobar

Cúscraid

Cúscraid, also known by the epithet Mend Macha (the "stammerer" of Macha), is a hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology and a son of Conchobar mac Nessa. His story explains the origin of his speech impediment and how he came to be king of Ulster.

The myth

According to the tale, Cúscraid led his first armed raid against Connacht. On the border he was met by the Connacht hero Cet mac Mágach, who wounded him with a spear in the throat or, in another version, on the tip of the tongue. This is said to be the source of his stammer.

The women of Ulster used to imitate the traits of the hero they admired most: Cúscraid's admirers stammered, those of Conall Cernach twisted their necks, and those of Cúchulainn squinted one eye, in imitation of his battle spasm.

After Conchobar's death, the men of Ulster offered the throne to his eldest son, Cormac Cond Longas, but he died before he could take power. The crown then passed to Conall Cernach, who refused it and recommended his foster son Cúscraid, who eventually became king.

Trivia

Admirers who stammered. According to the tale, young women of Ulster copied the quirks of their favourite heroes. Cúscraid's followers took to stammering just like him.
A spear through the tongue. The wound that explains his stammer is placed in the throat or on the tip of the tongue, depending on the version told.
