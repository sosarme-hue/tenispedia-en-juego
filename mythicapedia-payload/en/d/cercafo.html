Heliad of Greek mythology, king of Rhodes

Cercaphus

Cercaphus is one of the Heliadae, sons of the sun god Helios and the nymph Rhodos, in Greek mythology. Ruler of Rhodes alongside his brother Ochimus, his story is tied to the origins of the island's three main cities.

The myth

According to myth, Cercaphus was one of the seven Heliadae, children of the sun god and the nymph Rhodos. Helios raised the island from the waters and gave its rule to his sons, after the expulsion of the Telchines, dog-headed daimones who defied the gods and whom Zeus tried to destroy with a flood.

The brothers were renowned as astrologers and seafarers. Four of them, envious of Tenages, murdered him and fled to other lands. Cercaphus and his brother Ochimus, who had taken no part in the crime, stayed on Rhodes. Ochimus, the eldest, became king, and Cercaphus married Cydippe, daughter of Ochimus and Hegetoria, and so inherited the throne.

In one version, Cydippe was promised to Ocridion, but a herald handed her over to Cercaphus instead. In another, Cercaphus, smitten with her, kidnapped the girl and did not return her until Ochimus was an old man. With her he had three sons, Camirus, Lindus and Ialysus, who ruled together after his death. During their lifetime a great deluge struck, burying their mother Cydippe beneath the flood. Afterwards the brothers divided the island and each founded a city named after himself. Pindar, however, says these three sons were born to Rhodos herself and were thus brothers of Cercaphus.

Ancient sources: Pindar and Nonnus of Panopolis, in his Dionysiaca, record variants of his lineage, and Pindar makes Rhodos the mother of Cercaphus's sons.

Trivia

A treacherous herald. In one version, the herald whose task was to bring the bride handed Cydippe over to Cercaphus instead of Ocridion. From then on, no herald dared enter Ocridion's small shrine.
The Telchines. The Telchines, expelled from Rhodes, were dog-headed daimones who defied the gods. Zeus caused a flood to destroy them, though most of them managed to escape.
