boy turned into a gecko in Greek myth

Ascalabus

Ascalabus is a boy in Greek mythology who was turned into a gecko by Demeter after mocking her. His story, preserved mainly by Ovid and Antoninus Liberalis, explains the origin of the Latin name Stellio.

The myth

According to the tradition, Demeter was wandering the earth searching for her daughter Persephone, abducted by Hades, when she came to Attica and was welcomed by Misme, Ascalabus's mother. Exhausted and thirsty, the goddess drank down in one gulp the kykeon, a drink of water, mint and barley meal, that Misme offered her. Ascalabus laughed at her and ordered a whole cask to be brought. Indignant, Demeter sprinkled the leftover drink on him, and the boy was changed into a spotted gecko, hated by gods and men.

Ovid tells it differently: the goddess is received by an old woman, and the unnamed boy mocks her. After the punishment, Demeter shrinks him to the size of a small lizard, and his new name, stellio, plays on stellatus, meaning starred or spotted. According to Antoninus Liberalis, the creature lives near canals, and whoever kills it wins Demeter's favour.

Legacy

The name survives in several gecko species, such as Stellio fimbriatus and Stellio tetradactylus, which carry the root stellio in their scientific names.

Ancient sources: Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, and Antoninus Liberalis, in his Metamorphoses, which draws on the lost work of Nicander.

Trivia

A play on words. Ovid plays on the resemblance between stellio, the lizard, and stellatus, meaning starred, alluding to the creature's spots.
Dangerous bites. Aristotle claims that the bites of the stellio are very harmful, which helps explain why the animal was so feared.
