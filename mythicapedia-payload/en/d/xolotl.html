Aztec god of fire, lightning and twins

Xolotl

Xolotl is the Aztec (Mexica) god of fire, lightning and twins, usually shown as a man with a dog's head. He guides the dead through Mictlan and is the twin brother of Quetzalcoatl, with whom he shares the figure of Venus as evening and morning star.

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Xolotl accompanied Quetzalcoatl to Mictlan, the land of the dead, to retrieve the bones of earlier humanity and create life for the present world. He was also charged with protecting the sun from the dangers of the underworld. When the Fifth Sun was created but would not move, Ehecatl, the wind god, sacrificed the other gods to set it in motion. Xolotl refused to die and transformed into a two-stalked young maize plant, a double maguey and an amphibian, the axolotl, yet he was eventually found and killed.

Another account, linked to Teotihuacan, tells that Xolotl refused to sacrifice himself for the newly made sun and wept so hard that his eyes fell from their sockets, explaining his empty eye sockets.

Worship and religion

Dogs accompanied their owners in life and in death. Dog remains and sculptures have been found in Aztec burials, some near the Templo Mayor in Tenochtitlan, and dogs were sometimes sacrificed so they could guide the dead across a deep river in Mictlan. Xolotl was also the patron of the Mesoamerican ballgame. The xoloitzcuintli, the Mexican hairless dog, is a living breed that carries the name of this god.

Legacy

The axolotl, an aquatic salamander, is named after him, and its name is often translated as "water monster". The xoloitzcuintli remains closely tied to his image as a spirit-animal form.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Florentine Codex, which records the creation myth of the Fifth Sun, and in the Codex Mendoza, where he plays ball against the moon god.

Trivia

Name of the axolotl. The axolotl takes its name from this god. The Nahuatl word atl, water, combined with xolotl, is often translated as "water monster".
The two faces of Venus. Xolotl is the evening star and Quetzalcoatl the morning star. Together they represent the two phases of the cycle of life and death.
A dog with a torch. In the Maya codices he is sometimes shown carrying a torch, referring to the belief that the dog brought fire to humanity.
