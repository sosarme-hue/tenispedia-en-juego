Etruscan god of war

Laran

Laran, also called Larun, is the Etruscan god of war, the equivalent of the Greek Ares and the Roman Mars. He was shown as a naked young man in armour, and sources also link him with the underworld and the guarding of boundaries.

The myth

Laran appears in Etruscan art recognised by his attributes: helmet, cuirass, spear and shield. According to scholars, his role was not limited to war, since he was also charged with maintaining peace and has a connection with the underworld.

The scenes showing him are interpreted by comparison with Greek and Roman myths, as Etruscan literature has not survived. On a mirror from Orvieto he appears with Turms and a young Tinia, and on another mirror he stands behind Menrva holding a spear. On a mirror from Populonia he fights the giant Celsclan, son of the earth goddess Cel. His wife is Turan, and he appears alongside Lurs, with whom he has been associated since the archaic period.

Worship and religion

Inscriptions attest to his worship, and priests prepared meals for the Etruscan gods in utilitarian ware, though one inscription indicates meals were made in his honour. The Tabula Capuana records his festival on the Ides of May. Boundary cippi found at Bettona bear his name.

Legacy

Earlier scholars believed the Etruscan war god was Maris, due to the similarity of the name to Mars, but this has been disproven and Laran is now identified as the god of war.

Ancient sources: The Tabula Capuana, which records his festival and the spelling variant of his name, and Etruscan mirrors and vases bearing inscriptions.

Trivia

Gender-neutral name. Like many other Etruscan gods, the name Laran is gender neutral.
A vase with his name. A 5th century BCE vase from a sanctuary near a bridge at San Giovenale connects Laran with the god Lurs.
