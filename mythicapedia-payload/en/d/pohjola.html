northern mythical realm in Finnish mythology

Pohjola

Pohjola is, in Finnish mythology, the frozen northern realm ruled by the sorceress Louhi and the rival of the land of Kalevala. It matters because it is the central setting of the Kalevala, Finland's national epic, where the Sampo is forged and fought over.

The myth

In the oldest runic songs, Pohjola is the realm of the dead, a distant place in the far north that had to be reached by crossing waters and that was identified with Tuonela. When agriculture spread and cemeteries became places for honouring ancestors, the idea of the realm of the dead gradually became linked to specific geographical places.

In the Kalevala, Pohjola appears mainly as the home of women whom the heroes of Kalevala want to marry. Its mistress is Louhi, a powerful and wicked sorceress who sets tests for the suitors. Louhi demands that Ilmarinen forge the Sampo, a magic artefact that produces abundance, in exchange for her daughter's hand. Väinämöinen and Lemminkäinen also seek the maiden and must face trials. Lemminkäinen, while trying to catch the swan of Tuonela, is killed by a shepherd, and his mother restores him to life with spells. Later, the heroes of Kalevala launch an expedition to recover the Sampo, which breaks during the fighting. Louhi, in revenge, hides the sun and the moon, until the heroes win them back and return home.

Scholars have tried to locate Pohjola in the real world. Elias Lönnrot, who compiled the Kalevala, placed it in places such as Lapland, while other writers suggested Karelia, the White Sea, Gotland or even Constantinople. Yet a remote, icy, dreadful north is a common motif in classical and medieval European literature, so many see Pohjola as a symbolic space, the source of evil and disease.

Legacy

Jean Sibelius composed the symphonic poem Pohjolan tytär (Pohjola's Daughter). The metal band Sentenced took the frozen land as inspiration for the albums Journey to Pohjola (1992) and North from Here (1993), and Ensiferum and Moonsorrow wrote songs on the theme. In the video game Genshin Impact, the Nod-Krai arc draws on Finnish folklore, and there Pohjola is used as a synonym for the Abyss. In modern Finnish, the word pohjola also refers to the Nordic countries, and the episode 'Pohjola's Ordeal' of the anime series Little Witch Academia (2017) takes its name from it.

Ancient sources: The Finnish runic songs, collected in the Kalevala by Elias Lönnrot, are the main source for the stories about Pohjola.

Trivia

A shifting north. According to scholars, Pohjola may have referred to different territories depending on the era and the people imagining it, from Lapland to regions of the Baltic.
A name for the Nordic countries. In modern Finnish, pohjola is also used to name the Nordic countries, the equivalent of Norden in the Scandinavian languages.
