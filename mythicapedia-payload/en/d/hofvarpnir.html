horse of the goddess Gná in Norse mythology

Hofvarpnir

Hofvarpnir is, in Norse mythology, the flying horse of Gná, one of the errand-runners of the goddess Frigg. Its name means roughly "hoof-thrower", and it is recorded in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, Gná travelled between other worlds on Frigg's errands, riding Hofvarpnir, a horse able to ride through the air and to tread on the sea. Once, some Vanir saw her path through the sky and asked her what was flying there. Gná replied that she was not flying but riding through the air on Hofvarpnir, the offspring of the horses Hamskerpir and Garðrofa.

The account ends by explaining Gná's name: from it comes the custom of saying that something gnaefir, that is, looms high up.

Legacy

Some nineteenth-century scholars linked Gná to Fama, the Roman personification of rumour, and Jacob Grimm suggested that Hofvarpnir, like Pegasus, may have been a winged horse. Today it is cited, alongside Sleipnir, as a prime example of horses that bridge earth and sky.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (13th century), in chapter 35 of Gylfaginning, which describes Gná and her horse.

Trivia

Transcendent horses. Hofvarpnir and the eight-legged horse Sleipnir are cited as examples of horses that can move between worlds and link earth and sky.
A verb from her name. Norse tradition linked Gná's name to the idea of something rising up, expressed by the verb gnæfir.
