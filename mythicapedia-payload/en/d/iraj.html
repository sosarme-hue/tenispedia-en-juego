legendary hero of ancient Iran, son of Fereydun

Iraj

Iraj is, in Iranian tradition, the youngest son of Fereydun and the mythical ancestor of the Iranians. His story, whose fullest telling appears in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, explains the origins of the peoples of Iran and their link to kingship.

The myth

Iraj was the youngest son of Fereydun, the king who divided the world among his three sons. His name appears in the Avesta as Airiia, and in later Zoroastrian tradition as Ērič, which is generally understood to mean “Iranian”, making him the legendary progenitor of the Iranians. The texts do not agree on his family line: in the Avesta he is the father of Manuchehr, while in later sources he is Manuchehr's grandfather.

The central episode is the division of the world. According to the Chihrdad, a book of the Sasanian Avesta now lost but summarized in the Denkard, the division gave Iraj a share alongside his brothers. That share became the source of conflict, and the brothers ended up killing him. The Bundahishn recalls that his death was later avenged by his grandson Manuchehr.

Ferdowsi's Shahnameh brings all these elements together in one account: the division, Iraj's murder and the revenge of his grandson. Earlier sources offer fragments and variants, but it is in this epic that the story takes its definitive form.

Legacy

The Shahnameh is the national epic of Greater Iran and has been praised for its eloquence and picturesque language. Yarshater presented a modernized prose version of Iraj's story, which makes it readable today without the original poetic language.

Ancient sources: The Avesta (as Airiia), the Chihrdad nask (lost, summarized in the Denkard), the Bundahishn, the Ayadgar ī Jāmāspīg and Ferdowsi's Shahnameh.

Trivia

A name with meaning. His name is generally understood to mean “Iranian”, pointing to his role as the legendary progenitor of the Iranians.
A possible trace at Persepolis. He may appear in the Elamite tablets of Persepolis as Harriyazza, the Elamite form of a hypothetical Old Persian Airya-ča.
A shifting family tie. In the Avesta he is the father of Manuchehr, but in later texts he becomes his grandfather.
