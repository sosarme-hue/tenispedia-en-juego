god of business fortune in the city of Sendai

Sendai Shirō

Sendai Shirō (c. 1855–c. 1902) was a man with an intellectual disability from the Japanese city of Sendai, worshipped as a hitogami of commercial prosperity.

The myth

According to tradition, Shirō was born around 1855 as the fourth son of the Haga family, former gunners of the Date clan. Some say he was born with his disability; others say it followed a fall into the water at age seven while he watched fireworks.

As an adult he wandered the city, was loved by children, and never harmed anyone. Shops he visited were said to prosper mysteriously, so owners offered him free meals. He loved trains and, after Sendai Station opened in 1887, visited almost daily. He is said to have died around 1902.

Worship and religion

Many shops in Sendai place his image on altars and beside the cash register, ahead of traditional charms such as the daruma doll.

Legacy

His photograph, sold as a postcard since the Taishō era, fueled the 1993 boom and today he is the city's mascot, appearing in corporate advertisements.

Trivia

Train visits. He reportedly visited Sendai Station almost daily and rode trains to Shiroishi and Shirakawa, though sometimes he could not pay the fare when getting off.
Sightings after death. Alleged sightings of him continued into the Shōwa era, even as far away as the port of Busan in Japanese-occupied Korea.
