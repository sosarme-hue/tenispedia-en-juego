supreme god of the Etruscan pantheon

Voltumna

Voltumna is a chthonic deity of the Etruscan religion, of uncertain gender, who according to Varro became the supreme god of the Etruscan pantheon, the deus Etruriae princeps. Her cult was centred in Volsinii, modern Bolsena, a city in central Italy.

The myth

The sources portray Voltumna with contrasting traits: at times as a maleficent monster, at times as a chthonic vegetation god of uncertain sex, and at times as a mighty war god. According to tradition, the volcanic activity of Lake Bolsena was caused by her destructive aspects.

Her figure therefore remains wrapped in several images, with no single version that the ancients settled on definitively.

Worship and religion

The bond of the twelve Etruscan peoples was renewed every year in the sacred grove of Fanum Voltumnae, the sanctuary of Voltumnus near Volsinii, which Livy mentions. During the assembly a long nail was ritually driven into a cell of the temple, marking the passage of time in the Etruscan sacred calendar. Games, ludi, were also held there, though it is unknown whether they were athletic or artistic. In Rome, near the Temple of Castor and Pollux in the Vicus Tuscus, a shrine was dedicated to Voltumna.

Legacy

Associated with the Roman Fortuna, the deity was eventually adopted into Rome as Vertumnus, a figure who kept some of her links to change and the seasons.

Ancient sources: Varro presents her as the supreme Etruscan deity, and Livy mentions her sanctuary near Volsinii.

Trivia
