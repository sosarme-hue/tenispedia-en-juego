seer and suitor of Penelope in the Odyssey

Leodes

Leodes was a seer from Ithaca and a minor suitor of Penelope. In the Odyssey he darkly predicted that Odysseus would return to avenge the suitors' abuse of hospitality under his own roof.

The myth

The son of Oenops, Leodes served as the sacrificial priest of the suitors. Although he belonged to their circle, the tradition presents him as someone who loathed their wicked deeds and was indignant with his companions.

According to the story, he was the first to test Odysseus' bow, and he warned that the weapon would break the heart of anyone who tried to string it and would bring death to many. When Odysseus returned and began the slaughter in the palace, Leodes was the last of the suitors he killed. While pleading for his life, he was beheaded, closing the hero's revenge.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Odyssey, where he is described as the suitors' seer.
