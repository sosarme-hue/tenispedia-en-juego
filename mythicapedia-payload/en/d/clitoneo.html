son of Alcinous and the finest runner of the Phaeacians

Clytoneus

Clytoneus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of King Alcinous and Arete and brother of Nausicaa. Homer's Odyssey presents him as the best runner among the Phaeacians.

The myth

According to Homer's Odyssey, Clytoneus was one of the children of King Alcinous and Queen Arete, and his siblings included Nausicaa, Laodamas and Halius. His role is brief, but he appears in an episode that shows life at the Phaeacian court.

When Odysseus reached Scheria after his ship was wrecked by the storms sent by Poseidon, Nausicaa guided him from the shore to her father's palace. Alcinous resolved to help him continue his journey home, and before the departure he arranged an assembly, a feast in the stranger's honour and a day of games. In the contests of leaping and running, Clytoneus stood out above the others, and Homer presents him as the best runner of the Phaeacians.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Odyssey, in the book devoted to Odysseus's stay among the Phaeacians.
