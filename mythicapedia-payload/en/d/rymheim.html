giants' home in Norse mythology

Þrymheim

Þrymheim is, in Norse mythology, the home of the giant Þjazi and later of his daughter Skaði, located in Jötunheim, the territory of the giants. Its name is often translated as "crash-home" or "noisy home".

The myth

According to the myth, Þrymheim was the home of the giant Þjazi. He once abducted the goddess Iðunn, whose apples kept the Æsir youthful. Without her, the gods began to age and arranged a rescue. Þjazi was killed while trying to recapture her.

After his death, the house passed to his daughter Skaði, a giantess and goddess, who lived there. During her marriage to Njörðr, the couple separated because Skaði preferred her home in Þrymheim, and she is said to have grown melancholy there after the wedding.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda, whose manuscripts preserve spelling variants such as Þrumheimr and Þruþheimr.

Trivia

A name with an echo. Þrymheim is usually translated as "noisy home" or "thunder home". Some manuscripts of the Prose Edda alter the spelling slightly.
A reading of power. The scholar Rudolf Simek translates the variant Þruþheimr as "power house", an interpretation he considers fitting for a giant's home.
