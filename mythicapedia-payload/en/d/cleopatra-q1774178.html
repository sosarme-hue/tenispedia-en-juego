wife of Meleager in Greek mythology

Cleopatra

Cleopatra, sometimes called Alcyone, is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of Idas and Marpessa and wife of the Calydonian prince Meleager. She matters for her role during the war between the Calydonians and the Curetes, and for the tragic end she shares with her husband.

The myth

According to tradition, Cleopatra was the daughter of Idas and Marpessa, and she married Meleager, a hero who took part in the Calydonian boar hunt. After the beast was killed, Meleager gave its skin to Atalanta, which angered his uncles Plexippus and Agenor. Meleager killed them, and the ending varies by version: in some accounts, Althaea throws into the fire the log on which her son's life depended, and he dies instantly.

In other versions, the war between the Calydonians and the Curetes, sons of Thestius, leads Meleager to refuse to fight, enraged after his mother's curse. He stays at home with Cleopatra, and neither his family nor his friends can persuade him. Only when Cleopatra begs him to defend the city does he agree to return to battle. Calydon wins, but Althaea had prayed to the gods for her son's death, and he falls in combat. Cleopatra dies of grief or hangs herself, depending on the account. According to Hyginus, she threw herself onto the funeral pyre. Their daughter was Polydora.

Ancient sources: Homer tells the story of Cleopatra and Meleager in the Iliad, and Apollodorus and Pausanias preserve later versions of it.

Trivia

Two names. Besides Cleopatra, she was also called Alcyone, a name recalling the abduction of her mother Marpessa by Apollo.
A famous daughter. According to Pausanias, her daughter Polydora was the wife of Protesilaus, the first soldier to leap ashore at Troy and therefore the first to die in the Trojan War.
