Mexica god of storms, war and the hunt

Mixcoatl

Mixcoatl is a god of Mexica (Aztec) mythology associated with storms, war and the hunt. His name means «cloud serpent» and he is linked to the Milky Way, which makes him a bridge between the sky, fire and the origins of humanity, as the Nahua peoples told it.

The myth

According to the stories, Mixcoatl was a child of the creator couple and, according to Mendieta, he was identified with the Milky Way, which was called Iztac-Mixcoatl, «white cloud serpent». After a destruction by water, he created four men and a woman to feed the Sun, but they fell into the water and never fulfilled their role.

He is also credited with the invention of fire, whether with a celestial fire drill or with flint. Father of four hundred sons, the Centzon Huitznahua, they conspired with their sister Coyolxauhqui to kill their mother Coatlicue; she gave birth to Huitzilopochtli, already armed, who defeated them. Some traditions make him the father of Quetzalcoatl, and in other versions he appears as an aspect of Tezcatlipoca.

Worship and religion

In Michoacán he was worshipped under the name Taras, and people offered him snakes, birds and rabbits. During Quecholli, the fourteenth twenty-day month of the calendar, dedicated to him, hunters dressed like the god and kindled a new fire to roast their game. A man and a woman were also sacrificed at his temple.

Legacy

In modern science his name has been given to Camaxtli Patera, a crater on Io, the moon of Jupiter; to a salamander, Pseudoeurycea mixcoatl; and to two Mexican vipers, Mixcoatlus barbouri and Mixcoatlus browni.

Ancient sources: Nahua and colonial chronicles and codices mention him, such as the Codex Mendieta, which records his genealogy and deeds.

Trivia

A crater on Io. One of the craters on Jupiter's moon Io is named Camaxtli, one of the forms under which this god was known.
Hunting as festival. During Quecholli, hunters imitated the god by dressing like him and kindled a new fire to roast their game.
