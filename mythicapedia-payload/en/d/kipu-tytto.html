Finnish goddess of pain

Kivutar

Kivutar, whose name means roughly "pain maiden", is a spirit or goddess of Finnish mythology who takes the pains and injuries of humans upon herself. She appears mostly in runic songs, which were chanted as spells to ease suffering.

The myth

According to tradition, she lives in Tuonela, the land of the dead, alongside her sister Loviatar. People asked her to take their pains onto herself. She sits on a pain rock on a hill where three rivers meet, gathering pains into a container or a glove, or throwing them into the pitch-black sea. She is described as frightening, with a dark face scarred by smallpox, and she is said to sing the final sleep to the dying. She is the mother of nine children, with names such as cancer, gout and scabies.

In some songs she was blended with the Virgin Mary under Christian influence, and in others she is called the daughter of Väinä.

Worship and religion

No temples or festivals dedicated to her are known. Her name was invoked in sung spells to relieve burns, wounds and the pains of childbirth.

Legacy

Scholars have compared her with Lilith, the Norse Menglöð and the Virgin Mary. It has been proposed that the pain rock with a hole in it was once an ancient cup-marked stone used for offerings.

Ancient sources: Finnish runic songs from Kainuu and North Ostrobothnia, and Seto poetry.

Trivia

The pain rock. The stone she sits on is said to have a hole through which pains can be banished.
Pain and iron. A Kainuu song about the origin of iron states that pain belongs to Kivutar, the daughter of injury.
