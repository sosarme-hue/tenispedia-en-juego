Mesopotamian underworld goddess or demon

Dimpemekug

Dimpemekug, also read as Dimmeku, is a Mesopotamian figure linked to the underworld, presented in the texts as either a goddess or a demon depending on the source. She matters because she appears in some of the earliest funerary tales from the region.

The myth

According to the god list An = Anum, Dimmeku was the daughter of Namtar and Hušbiša, and belonged to the court of Ereshkigal, queen of the underworld. In the oldest texts, however, she appears beside Ningishzida, which has led some scholars to suggest that her name first referred to a divine statue.

In the poem The Death of Ur-Namma, the ruler, on arriving in the underworld, makes offerings to several deities and gives Dimmeku an object called tudida, probably a garment. A similar scene recurs in The Death of Gilgamesh, from the Old Babylonian period.

Worship and religion

No temples or palaces dedicated to her are known. In the stories, visitors to the dark realm brought her gifts.

Ancient sources: The Death of Ur-Namma, The Death of Gilgamesh, the god list An = Anum, the Udug-hul incantation series and the Nippur god list.

Trivia

A mysterious object. The gift she receives in the poem is the tudida, a term presumed to mean a garment, although its exact meaning is not fully clear.
A daughter with two fathers. According to different sources, she is also linked to Enki through a similarly named deity, Ḫedimmeku, although not all scholars accept that they are the same.
