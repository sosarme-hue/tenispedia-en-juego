king of Orchomenus, father of Amphion

Iasus

Iasus is a king of Orchomenus in Greek mythology and the father of the musician Amphion. He matters mainly for the genealogy that links several royal houses of ancient Greece.

The myth

According to a scholion, Iasus (also called Iasios) was a powerful king of Orchomenus, son of a Persephone, who was herself a daughter of Minyas; the same text does not say who his father was. Other versions make him a son of Eleuther.

In the epic genealogy, Iasus is the father of Amphion, and Amphion is the father of Chloris, the wife of Neleus. Some accounts add that Amphion also fathered Phthia's rival Philomache, the wife of Pelias, a link that would tie Neleus and Pelias to the same father-in-law.

Another tradition says that Iasus also had a daughter, the beautiful Astreis, with whom Apollo had a relationship. Pausanias also records that the people of Tanagra held Pemander, grandson of Iasios through Chersilaus, to be their founder.

Ancient sources: An ancient scholion, the epic genealogy and Pausanias, who mentions him in connection with Tanagra.
