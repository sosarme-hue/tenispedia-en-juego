Japanese urban legend about a fictional railway station

Kisaragi Station

Kisaragi Station is a Japanese internet urban legend about a nonexistent railway stop where paranormal incidents take place. It was first posted on the 2channel forum in 2004 and remains part of popular internet culture, film and music.

The myth

The story began with a 2004 post on the 2channel forum. A woman calling herself Hasumi is riding a train that, for no clear reason, fails to stop for a long time. She finally gets off at an unmanned station named Kisaragi, which no one online recognizes and which appears on no map. Trying to walk away along the tracks, she hears a voice warning her it is dangerous; when she turns, she sees a one-legged man who vanishes. Fleeing into a tunnel, she hurts herself, and on the far side a man offers help. Together they return to the station and board another train bound for a remote area of the Japanese Alps, where the man begins to talk gibberish. Hasumi writes her final post, saying her battery is dying, and then disappears completely.

Readers have searched for the real station in Shizuoka Prefecture and suggested Saginomiya Station as the model, though a reporter who visited found little resemblance. Others place the story in Fukuoka. According to the legend, time is distorted and GPS malfunctions in the area.

Legacy

The legend went viral on Twitter and prompted calls and letters to the Enshū Railway, which has promoted Saginomiya Station as its birthplace. A 2022 film was released on the story, and Saginomiya Station was briefly renamed Kisaragi. A 2020 song by a YouTuber and a 2021 track by the artist Camellia also draw on it.

Trivia

A name without kanji. The original post never gave the kanji for the name, so it is written in hiragana. In Chinese it is often written 如月車站.
A station on a pond. In 2014, an anonymous Google Maps user created a spot called Kisaragi Station on a pond near the University of Tsukuba.
