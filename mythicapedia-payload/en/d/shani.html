Hindu god of the planet Saturn and lord of karma

Shani

Shani is the divine personification of the planet Saturn in Hinduism and one of the nine Navagraha of Hindu astrology. He is a male Hindu deity linked to karma, time and retribution, feared as a bringer of hardship yet also seen as a teacher who rewards righteous conduct.

The myth

In the Puranic tradition, Shani is the son of the sun god Surya and Chhaya, although some medieval accounts also make him a son of Balarama and Revati. His sister is Tapati. He is depicted with a dark complexion, dressed in blue or black, carrying a bow, arrow, axe and trident, and riding a crow or vulture that follows him everywhere. Some texts show him on a horse, a snake or a buffalo, and Buddhist texts from northeastern India and Nepal show him on a tortoise.

The Brahma Vaivarta Purana tells that Shani was a great devotee of Krishna. While he was absorbed in worship, he ignored his wife, daughter of the gandharva king Chitraratha, and in anger she cursed him so that anyone upon whom his gaze fell would suffer misfortune. The same text presents him as an incarnation of Krishna, who declares that he is 'Shani among planets'.

His name means 'the slow mover', since Saturn takes about thirty years to circle the Sun. Shani rules the zodiac signs Capricorn and Aquarius and, according to tradition, grants the fruits of one's actions in line with one's thoughts, words and deeds.

Worship and religion

On Saturdays, the day named after him in the Hindu calendar, devotees worship him with prayers and offerings to guard against his harmful effects and ease hardship. He is believed to favour those who give to the poor without expecting anything in return. According to some texts, the pipal fig tree is his abode. Shrines to him exist across India, such as Shani Shingnapur in Maharashtra. The worship remains alive and forms part of the religious life of millions of Hindus.

Legacy

In Hindu astrology, Shani stands for discipline, duty and patience. The name of the planet Saturn and of Saturday in several Indian languages derives from him. In Vedic astrology he is also linked to the blue sapphire, which is recommended as a ring for those under his influence.

Ancient sources: He appears in Sanskrit astronomical texts such as Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya (5th century), Brahmagupta's Khandakhadyaka (7th century) and the Surya Siddhanta, as well as in the Brahma Vaivarta Purana.

Trivia

A day of the week. Shanivara, Saturday in the Hindu calendar, takes its name from Shani, just as the Western Saturday takes its name from Saturn.
A planet with several names. In Sanskrit astronomical texts the planet also appears as Āra, Koṇa and Kroḍa.
