werewolf of Castilian folk tradition

Enchanted Wolf

The lobo hechizado is a kind of werewolf from Castilian popular folklore. Unlike other shapeshifters, his identity was usually known to the community, which lived alongside him even while fearing him.

The myth

According to Castilian tradition, a man turned into a wolf on nights of the full moon, either under a curse or after rubbing on ointments or swallowing herbs. He often went on all fours, with overgrown nails, fangs and fur, or became an enormous wolf, sometimes accompanied by real wolves. At dawn, tufts of hair would be found on doorsteps, and the man himself would warn his family not to open the door.

In a legend recorded in Robledo de Corpes (Guadalajara), a wolf attacked the wife who was bringing food to her husband and tore her clothes. When he refused to eat, she noticed threads from her stockings caught between his teeth and reported him. This time, the tale says, the wolf was executed.

Legacy

In Albaladejo (Ciudad Real), the Loberico, a carnival figure in the danzas de las ánimas wearing a grotesque mask, recalls the werewolf in its traits.

Trivia

A court case. In 1852, a reaper who claimed to be cursed with lycanthropy was arrested by the Guardia Civil near Escalona (Toledo), after years of crimes across several regions.
Tolerated thefts. In rural communities the wolf was tolerated when it stole or killed an animal to satisfy its hunger, almost as a donation to an unfortunate being.
