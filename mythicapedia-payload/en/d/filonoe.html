wife of Bellerophon in Greek myth

Philonoe

Philonoe is a figure of Greek myth, daughter of the Lycian king Iobates and wife of the hero Bellerophon. Her story matters mainly because it places her at the centre of a web of kinship and tales linking Lycia, Argos and Corinth.

The myth

According to tradition, Philonoe was the daughter of the Lycian king Iobates and sister of Antea, also called Stheneboea. Bellerophon, who had fled his homeland after accidentally killing a man named Belerus, reached Argos, where Antea, married to King Proetus, fell in love with him. Rejected, she accused him before her husband, who would not kill his guest outright and gave him a sealed letter for Iobates asking for the bearer's death.

Iobates set Bellerophon three tasks, each meant to end in his death: slaying the Chimera, fighting the Amazons and facing an ambush by his best soldiers. The hero overcame them all with the help of Pegasus. Impressed, the king showed him the letter, offered him a place in his kingdom and gave him Philonoe as his wife, along with part of the royal wealth and the promise of succession. When Antea learned of this, she took her own life.

Sources differ on the couple's children. According to the Iliad there were three: Isander, Hippolochus and Laodamia, the last becoming by Zeus the mother of Sarpedon. Other authors change the names, such as Pisander, or Deidamia and Hippodamia.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions Bellerophon and his children Isander, Hippolochus and Laodamia, and through her the lineage of Sarpedon.
