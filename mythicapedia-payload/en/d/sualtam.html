mortal father of the hero Cúchulainn in Irish myth

Súaltam

Súaltam is the mortal father of the hero Cúchulainn in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. He is best remembered for his death, which sets the defence of Ulster in motion.

The myth

According to the Ulster Cycle, Súaltam was the son of Róich and brother of Fergus mac Róich. He was married to Deichtine, sister of King Conchobar, and raised Sétanta, the future Cúchulainn, as his own son. The stories disagree on the boy's parentage, and Lugh is named as a possible biological father.

When Queen Medb of Connacht launches the Táin Bó Cúailnge against Ulster, Súaltam and his son guard the border. He sets off to warn the king, but takes months to arrive. When he denounces the invasion without observing court etiquette, the king and his druids sentence him to death. As he runs out, he falls onto the sharpened rim of his shield and beheads himself. His head, still on the shield, keeps crying out about the raids until Conchobar finally rouses the army to fight.

Trivia

A head that keeps shouting. After his death, his severed head returns to the court on his shield and repeats his warning about the raids. Only then does the king decide to act.
Several versions of an origin. Irish stories do not agree on who fathered Cúchulainn. In one version the child is Lugh's son and Súaltam raises him; in another, the boy is born after Deichtine is found in Lugh's house.
