Cypriot princess of Greek mythology

Arsinoe

Arsinoe is a princess of Salamis, on Cyprus, who was turned to stone by Aphrodite after rejecting a suitor. Her story belongs to Greek mythology and survives mainly in the work of Antoninus Liberalis, though it is considered a variant of a better-known tale.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved by Antoninus Liberalis, Arsinoe was the daughter of King Nicocreon of Salamis, on Cyprus, and a descendant of Teucer. A wealthy man of Phoenician descent, Arceophon, son of Minnyrides, fell in love with her. The king refused to let him marry her, judging his origins unworthy.

Arceophon persisted with night serenades and with bribes to the girl's wet nurse, but Arsinoe kept spurning him. When her parents discovered the scheme, they punished the nurse by cutting off her tongue, nose and fingers. In despair at losing her, Arceophon starved himself to death and received a widely attended funeral.

Arsinoe looked out of the window to watch the funeral procession, and Aphrodite, angered by her coldness, turned her into stone. The story is a variant of the tale of Iphis and Anaxarete, which Ovid recounts in his Metamorphoses.

Ancient sources: Her story survives mainly in the work of Antoninus Liberalis, a writer of the Roman imperial era, and Plutarch mentions it when comparing it with other tales of scorned lovers.

Trivia

A stone with meaning. In Greek metamorphosis myths, stone usually does not reflect a person's character. In Arsinoe's case, however, the petrification does express the coldness and cruelty she showed before being transformed.
A real king. Nicocreon was a historical king of Cyprus who lived in the time of Alexander the Great, which gives the story a historical setting.
