phantom land of the Weddell Sea

New South Greenland

New South Greenland, also known as Morrell's Land, was a coastline that the American captain Benjamin Morrell claimed to have sighted in 1823 in Antarctica, in the Weddell Sea. Later expeditions proved it did not exist, so it is now regarded as a phantom land.

The myth

The land appears in the account of Benjamin Morrell, captain of the schooner Wasp, who was hunting seals in Antarctic waters. In his account, on 15 March 1823 he saw land and sailed along a coastline of more than 480 kilometres, which he described with precise coordinates.

The name came from Captain Robert Johnson, who had discovered and named the land two years earlier, and Morrell gave him the credit with notable modesty. However, his reputation as a fabulist and several errors in his figures raised doubts from the start.

The disproof came in 1912, when Wilhelm Filchner, with his ship Deutschland trapped in the ice, passed through the area and found no land. A sounding showed about 1,500 metres of water, impossible near a coast. Three years later, Ernest Shackleton, aboard the Endurance, confirmed the same.

Several explanations have been proposed: a calculation error, a memory lapse when writing the account nine years later, icebergs mistaken for coastline, or mirages. Intentional deception has also been suggested.

Ancient sources: The details come from the account of four voyages written by Benjamin Morrell, published as A Narrative of Four Voyages.
