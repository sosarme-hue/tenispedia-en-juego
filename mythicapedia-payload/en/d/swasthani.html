Hindu goddess of Nepal

Swasthani

Swasthani is a Hindu goddess worshipped mainly in Nepal and the central deity of the Swasthani Vrata, an annual month-long observance based on reading the Swasthani Vrata Katha. She matters because her worship is linked to protection, prosperity and family well-being.

The myth

The sacred narrative places the goddess within Shaiva mythology: she is connected with Shiva, Sati, Parvati and Vishnu, who instructs Parvati to perform the Swasthani vow in order to win Shiva as her husband.

A more human-centred story follows Goma, the daughter of a Brahmin couple, and her family, Navaraj and Chandravati. According to the tradition, devotion to the goddess changes their fortunes.

Worship and religion

According to the sources, her devotion has roots in the Newar culture of the Kathmandu Valley and later spread among Nepali-speaking Hindu communities. The annual vow combines reading or listening to the text, offerings and, for some participants, fasting. It continues today.

Legacy

Her image surrounded by the Ashtamatrika, the Eight Mother Goddesses, spread through printed books in Nepali. A statue of Shiva and Swasthani, consecrated in 1674 by King Pratap Malla, stands near Tana Bahal in Kathmandu.

Ancient sources: The oldest known manuscript of the text dates to 1573 CE, although this does not establish when her worship began.

Trivia

A dated statue. A statue of Shiva and Swasthani was consecrated in 1674 by King Pratap Malla and is identified by its inscription.
A book at the centre of worship. For much of her documented history, the sacred book rather than a freestanding image was the main physical focus of worship.
