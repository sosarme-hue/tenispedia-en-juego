Japanese kami of agriculture

Wakumusubi

Wakumusubi is a Japanese kami of agriculture, linked to grains and to the sustenance of communities. His figure connects the world of the deities with the cultivation of the land.

The myth

In many versions, Wakumusubi was born from the urine of Izanami when she died, an origin that places him among the first kami to emerge after the goddess's death. Another version in the Nihon Shoki presents him as a son of Kagutsuchi and Haniyasu-hime, showing that ancient authors did not agree on his lineage.

In the most common genealogy he is a son of Izanagi and Izanami. He is the brother of Mizuhanome and the father of Toyouke-Ōmikami, and his spouse is Yosatsura.

Worship and religion

He is venerated at Aiki Jinja, where he is worshipped as a god of agriculture. The available material does not describe his festivals or rituals in detail.

Ancient sources: The Nihon Shoki, which offers an alternative account of his birth as a son of Kagutsuchi and Haniyasu-hime.
