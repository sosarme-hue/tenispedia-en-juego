exiled prince of Ulster, son of Conchobar

Cormac Cond Longas

Cormac Cond Longas is a hero of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, the eldest son of King Conchobar mac Nessa and raised by Fergus mac Róich. Known as the 'Exiled Prince', his story shows how heroic taboos, the geasa, could decide the fate of a character.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cormac was the eldest son of Conchobar, king of Ulster. The accounts disagree about his mother: some name his wife Clothru, others his own mother, Ness. After the Deirdre affair, he followed Fergus mac Róich into exile in Connacht.

During the Táin Bó Cuailnge, the Cattle Raid of Cooley, he fought for Ailill and Medb against his own father. In the heat of battle, he stopped Fergus from killing Conchobar.

When Conchobar died, the men of Ulster asked Cormac to return and rule them. On the journey home he was forced to break his geasa, and he was killed when the hostel where he and his men were staying was raided.
