Viking chief and legendary king of Sweden

Björn Ironside

Björn Ironside is a Norse Viking chief and semi-legendary king of Sweden. The sagas present him as a son of Ragnar Lodbrok and the ancestor of the Munsö dynasty, which is why he matters as a link between Scandinavian heroic legend and the origins of the Swedish royal line.

The myth

According to the sagas, Björn was the son of Ragnar Lodbrok and Aslaug, and brother of Hvitserk, Ivar the Boneless, Sigurd and Ubbe. After leaving Sweden with his brothers to conquer Zealand, Reidgotaland and other islands, they settled at Lejre under Ivar's leadership. When Eric and Agnar, Björn's half-brothers, rebelled against the jarl Eysteinn Beli, Agnar died in battle and Eric was captured. He asked to be impaled on spears.

Aslaug, who called herself Randalin, led the cavalry and killed Eysteinn in a great battle. After Ragnar's death, the Saga of Hervarar says Björn inherited Sweden and fathered Erik Björnsson, ancestor of the kings of Munsö. Other sources also credit him with sons such as Refil.

Medieval chronicles link this figure with the Björn of William of Jumièges's account, who places a chief of that name on a great Mediterranean expedition around 859 to 861 alongside Hastein. In that story they took Pisa, and when they believed Luni was Rome and could not enter it, Hastein feigned a deathbed conversion to gain access to the city.

Ancient sources: Ragnarssona þáttr (14th century), the Saga of Hervarar, the Saga of Ragnar Lothbrok and William of Jumièges's chronicle (c. 1070).

Trivia

A barrow on Munsö. In the early 18th century, antiquarians attributed a burial mound on the island of Munsö to Björn, a claim that still links his name to the site.
The trick at Luni. Hastein pretended to convert on his deathbed and asked for burial on consecrated ground, and once inside his men opened the gates for the army.
