eponymous king of the Daunians (Greek myth)

Daunus

Daunus, also called Dauno, is the eponymous king of the Daunians in Greek myth, a people of the Adriatic coast of Italy. His story links the origin of that people with the arrival of Diomedes after the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Antoninus Liberalis, Daunus was a son of Lycaon and had two brothers, Iapyx and Peucetius. The three were part of a group of Illyrian settlers led by Mesapus who sailed to the Adriatic coast of Italy. There they defeated the Ausonians, the people who lived in the area, and divided the land among themselves. Daunus received the region around the Gargano peninsula, and its inhabitants took their name, Daunians, from him.

Later, after the fall of Troy, Diomedes arrived in the land. Daunus asked him for help against the Messapians and offered in return his daughter's hand in marriage and a plot of land. Diomedes won the war, took the territory and shared it among the Dorians who had come with him. Two sons were born from his marriage to Daunus's daughter.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis preserves the tradition about Daunus and his brothers.

Trivia

Name of a people. According to the tradition, the inhabitants of the Gargano region took their name, Daunians, in honor of Daunus.
A famous son-in-law. The hero who married his daughter was Diomedes, who had reached Italy after the Trojan War.
