divine couple of Vaishnava Hinduism

Radha-Krishna

Radha-Krishna is the divine couple formed by the god Krishna and his chief consort and shakti, Radha. In several Vaishnava traditions they are regarded as the feminine and masculine realities of God, and they are widely worshipped within Hinduism.

The myth

According to tradition, Radha is Krishna's most beloved companion, and later literature presents her as the source of his love and divine energy. Texts describe her as the complete incarnation of Mahalakshmi and say that while Krishna enchants the world, Radha enchants even him. Some accounts claim that Radha is Krishna himself, split into two so that he could enjoy his divine sport.

The stories are set mainly in Vrindavan and Goloka and recount the couple's divine pastimes (lila). In some schools, Radha is said to be the origin of all the gopis and of the figures who take part in the rasa dance. Many devotees call on her as a way to reach Krishna.

Worship and religion

In Krishnaite Vaishnava traditions the couple is worshipped as the complete form of the divine, and in some branches Radha is venerated as the supreme goddess. Her cult is especially rooted in Bengal and the Vrindavan region, and it forms part of traditions such as Gaudiya Vaishnavism, the Nimbarka Sampradaya, Pushtimarg and the Swaminarayan Sampradaya. Her devotees keep this worship alive today.

Legacy

Jayadeva portrayed them as a couple in the twelfth-century Gita Govinda, a work that strongly shaped devotional poetry. Poets such as Surdas, Meera Bai and Chandidas, along with the movement of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, spread her devotion.

Ancient sources: She is mentioned in the Gatha Saptasati, the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, the Garga Samhita and the Padma Purana, among other texts.

Trivia

Earliest literary mention. The Gatha Saptasati, written in Prakrit in the 1st century CE, contains the earliest known literary mention of Radha alongside Krishna.
Meaning of her name. The name Radha means "the pleasing one", while Krishna is associated with "all attractive" or "dark blue".
