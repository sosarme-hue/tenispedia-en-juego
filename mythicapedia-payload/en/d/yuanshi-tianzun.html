Yuanshi Tianzun

Yuanshi Tianzun, the Celestial Venerable of the Primordial Beginning, is one of the highest deities of Chinese Taoism. He is one of the Three Pure Ones and the source of all that exists.

The myth

According to Taoist cosmogony, he existed before anything else and created himself from the purest qi. As a creator deity he brought forth Heaven and Earth, and all things arise from him. He later handed the administration of Heaven to his assistant, the Jade Emperor.

At the start of each age he delivers the Scriptures of the Magic Jewel to his disciples, lesser deities who pass them on to mankind to teach the doctrines of the Tao. In the sixteenth-century novel Investiture of the Gods he appears as the master of Jiang Ziya on Mount Kunlun.

Worship and religion

Taoists claim that kings offered sacrifices to him before the Xia dynasty. For centuries, the emperor offered a bull each year at the Temple of Heaven in the capital, on a three-tiered altar. He is never shown in images; instead his name is written on a spirit tablet.

Legacy

He appears as a wise master, loyal to heaven's will, in the novel Investiture of the Gods.

Ancient sources: The Scriptures of the Magic Jewel (Lingbao jing) and other Taoist texts; the novel Investiture of the Gods is later, from the sixteenth century.

In art

Trivia

No image. He was never represented with images or idols. His name was written on a tablet that the emperor carried to the Temple of Heaven.
Three-tiered altar. On the Temple of Heaven altar, the highest tier was reserved for Yuanshi Tianzun and the Son of Heaven.
