Euboean prince, son of Nauplius in Greek mythology

Oeax

Oeax is a Euboean prince, son of King Nauplius and brother of Palamedes, who appears in accounts of the Trojan War and in later stories about Agamemnon's household. Ancient sources give his mother under different names: Clymene, Hesione or Philyra.

The myth

According to the tradition, Oeax accompanied his brother Palamedes to Troy. After Palamedes was killed by Odysseus, Oeax carved a message on oar blades for their father Nauplius, who came to the Greek camp and threatened Agamemnon, though he left empty-handed. The Greeks then tried to throw Oeax into the sea, but the Nereids rescued him.

Angry at the Greeks, Oeax lied to Clytemnestra, telling her that Agamemnon was bringing back Cassandra, a Trojan concubine. This pushed her to plot her husband's death. Dictys adds that he also turned Aegiale, the wife of Diomedes, against him.

Later, Oeax tried to banish Orestes after Clytemnestra's murder. In the end, he and his brother Nausimedon were killed by Pylades, after helping Aegisthus in his fight against Orestes.

Ancient sources: Dictys Cretensis, in his account of the Trojan War, credits Oeax with turning Aegiale against Diomedes.

Trivia

A variable mother. Ancient sources do not agree on Oeax's mother, who appears as Clymene, Hesione or Philyra.
