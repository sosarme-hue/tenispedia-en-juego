Arcadian princess in Greek mythology

Alphesiboea

Alphesiboea is a princess of Psophis, an Arcadian kingdom of ancient Greece, and the daughter of King Phegeus. Her story, tied to the myth of Alcmaeon, makes her a key figure in the curse of the necklace of Harmonia.

The myth

According to tradition, Alphesiboea lived at her father's court with her brothers Axion and Temenus when Alcmaeon of Argos arrived in Psophis, seeking purification for killing his mother Eriphyle. Phegeus welcomed him, purified him and gave him his daughter as a wife. Alcmaeon gave her the cursed necklace and robe of Harmonia, objects that brought misfortune to their owners. The couple had a son, Clytius.

Alcmaeon's curse did not fade. An oracle of Apollo ordered him to leave Psophis, and he settled in Acarnania by the river Achelous, where he married the nymph Callirrhoe, daughter of the river god. Callirrhoe wanted the necklace and robe that Alphesiboea still kept, so Alcmaeon returned to Psophis, where his brothers-in-law, acting on Phegeus' orders, ambushed and killed him. When she reproached them, her brothers locked her in a chest and sold her as a slave to the Tegean Agapenor, claiming she had slain her husband. Her ultimate fate is unknown.

Ancient sources: Her story appears in lost tragedies by Achaeus, Chaeremon, Timotheus, Sophocles, Euripides and Theodectes, of which only fragments survive.

Trivia

A version of revenge. Propertius, in his Elegies, is the only source saying that Alphesiboea avenged Alcmaeon by killing her brothers. No other surviving source confirms this.
Several plays bearing her name. At least three ancient tragedies were titled Alphesiboea, though all are now lost.
