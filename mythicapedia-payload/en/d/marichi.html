Hindu sage, mind-born son of Brahma

Marichi

Marichi is a sage in Hindu mythology, the mind-born son of the creator god Brahma and one of the Saptarishi, the Seven Sages. His importance lies in being the father of Kashyapa, a key figure in the genealogies of mythic peoples.

The myth

According to the tradition, before creation began Brahma needed helpers to take charge of the subcreations. From his mind he produced ten Prajapatis, rulers of the people, among them Marichi, one of his manasputras or mind-born sons. Married to Kala, he became the father of Kashyapa, who inherited his father's fertility and is sometimes also counted as a Prajapati.

In a well-known episode, Marichi cursed his wife Dharmavrata to turn to stone. While her husband slept, she received the visit of Brahma and chose to honour the god rather than obey her husband. Dharmavrata proved her innocence by performing penance amid fires. Vishnu, pleased, could not reverse the curse, but ruled that the stone should be revered as the sacred Devashila.

In Jainism, Marichi was an earlier incarnation of Mahavira, the twenty-fourth tirthankara. As a disciple of Rishabhanatha he could not bear the strict discipline and founded his own doctrine.

Legacy

In some parts of India people believe that seven stars of the Big Dipper bear the names of the Saptarishi, Marichi among them, and that a nearby faint star is Arundhati.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions him among the sages, and the Rig Veda contains hymns attributed to his descendant Kashyapa.

Trivia

Stars named after him. According to beliefs in some parts of India, seven stars of the Big Dipper carry the names of the Saptarishi, Marichi included.
Meaning of his name. His name is explained as a speck of light or a dust mote that shines in the sun, according to the Rig Veda and the Atharva Veda.
