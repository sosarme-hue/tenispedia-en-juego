fierce creature of Chinese mythology

Qiongqi

Qiongqi (窮奇, roughly "exhaustively strange") is a creature of Chinese mythology and one of the Four Perils, alongside Hundun, Taowu and Taotie. It is remembered above all as a symbol of lies, slander and wickedness.

The myth

According to the Records of the Grand Historian, Qiongqi was a failed son of Shaohao, lacking any talent, who acted in bad faith, scorned loyalty and spoke only lies. He caused chaos during the age of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors until Emperor Shun of the Youyu clan banished him to the far corners of the land. The Zuo Zhuan adds that he elevated falsehood and slandered people in silence. Tradition places him in Yunmeng, in the State of Chu.

The Classic of Mountains and Seas gives two conflicting descriptions. In the Classic of the Western Mountains he is an ox-like beast with hedgehog-like fur, whose cry resembles a howling dog, and who eats people. In the Classic of the Northern Seas' Inner Realm he is a winged tiger who devours his victims from the head, or from the feet according to another account. The Book of Gods and Strange Things adds that he understands human speech: he punishes the wicked by providing them with a beast, and he eats the nose of the loyal.

Legacy

He appears in the Shin Megami Tensei franchise, especially the Devil Children games, where he can be recruited as a demon, using the description from the Classic of the Northern Seas' Inner Realm.

Ancient sources: Records of the Grand Historian, Zuo Zhuan, Classic of Mountains and Seas and Book of Gods and Strange Things.

Trivia

One of the Four Perils. Qiongqi belongs, together with Hundun, Taowu and Taotie, to a group of four evil beings banished in the classical Chinese stories.
A name that describes its nature. Its name literally means "exhaustively strange", a phrase that points to the abnormality of its character.
