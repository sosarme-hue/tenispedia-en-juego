celestial generals of the Chinese zodiac

Tai Sui

The Tai Sui are the stars directly opposite Jupiter in its roughly twelve-year orbit, personified as deities. They are part of Chinese astrology, Taoism and Feng Shui, and each year is governed by one of them.

The myth

According to tradition, the Tai Sui help the Jade Emperor watch over the mortal world. There are sixty celestial generals, many with military backgrounds, which is why they are also called the Military Generals or the Cyclical Gods. Each one presides over a year, and his features foretell its fortune: a general holding a pen signals political unrest, while one armed with a spear or sword calls for hard work and excellence.

The legends often describe people who suffer disaster after disrespecting or ignoring them.

Worship and religion

In Taoism, people whose zodiac sign clashes with the year's Tai Sui hold a prayer session with a Taoist priest to seek peace and good fortune. In Taiwan, at the start of the lunar year, worshippers visit temples for this rite and receive a protective talisman. The custom continues in overseas Chinese communities.

Legacy

They shape Chinese astrology and the traditional taboo against building a house facing the year's Tai Sui.

Ancient sources: The earliest record of their worship is in Wang Chong's first-century Lunheng.
