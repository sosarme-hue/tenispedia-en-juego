mythical king of the Minyans of Orchomenus

Clymenus

Clymenus is a king of the Minyans, a people of Orchomenus, a city in Boeotia, in Greek mythology. His story is tied to the war his son ErgINUS waged against Thebes.

The myth

Accounts differ on his parentage: some make him a son of Presbon, while others make him a direct son of Orchomenus, in which case he would have been the brother of Aspledon and Anphidocus. He inherited the throne, either as the king's son or because Orchomenus, having no children of his own, passed the kingdom to him. Clymenus married Budeia, daughter of Lycus, and had several children: Erginus, Stratius, Arrhon, Pyleus and Azeus.

One day, during a festival of Poseidon held at Onchestus, he quarrelled with a group of Thebans over a trifling matter. In the fight, Perieres, the charioteer of Menoeceus, hurled a stone that struck him mortally. As he lay dying, he asked his heir Erginus to avenge his death, and Erginus then led a war against Thebes.

Trivia

A quarrel at a festival. The dispute broke out during a festival of Poseidon at Onchestus, over a matter of little importance.
A revenge that became a war. Clymenus's death led to the war that Erginus waged against Thebes.
