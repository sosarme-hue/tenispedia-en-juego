supreme deity of the Yoruba pantheon

Olorun

Olorun is one of the manifestations of Olodumare, the supreme deity of the Yoruba religion, and the name means “lord of the heavens.” Credited with creating the universe and all living things, Olorun is seen as distant and detached from human affairs.

The myth

In Yoruba tradition, Olorun is the supreme being who created the universe and all living things. From this divine energy came the Ìrúnmalé, emanations that keep the universe in balance. The sources divide them into two groups, linked to Changó and Oggun, which they describe as masculine and feminine energies.

In the Ifá literary corpus, Olorun has no gender and exists only in spiritual form. When Samuel Ajayi Crowther translated the Bible into Yoruba in the late nineteenth century, he used the name for “God,” which led to the deity being associated with the male gender.

Worship and religion

Olorun has no temple or cult of its own, and no shrines, images or sacrifices are dedicated directly to this deity. Worship happens through the orishas, although believers may direct prayers toward Olorun. The name is spoken with restraint, and an offering called ñangareo is made to the sun when an itá is to be held or an iyawó is born.

Legacy

The name appears in Paul Simon’s song “The Rhythm of the Saints” (1990), in Sérgio Mendes’s “Lua Soberana” (1992) and in the video game Smite (2014). The Afro-Brazilian percussion group Olodum takes its name from Olodumare.

Ancient sources: The Ifá literary corpus mentions Olorun as a genderless being who exists only in spiritual form.

Trivia

A name in pop music. Paul Simon mentions Olorun in the song “The Rhythm of the Saints,” from his 1990 album of the same name.
From name to percussion. The popular Afro-Brazilian percussion group Olodum takes its name from Olodumare.
