princess of Kashi in the Mahabharata

Amba

Amba is a princess in the Hindu epic Mahabharata, the eldest daughter of the king of Kashi, whose rejection and vow of revenge drive one of the epic's best-known storylines. Her fate becomes entangled with that of Bhishma and the great battle of Kurukshetra.

The myth

In the Mahabharata, Amba is the eldest daughter of the king of Kashi and the sister of Ambika and Ambalika. At their svayamvara, Bhishma abducted the three princesses to marry them to his half-brother Vichitravirya, king of Hastinapura. Amba had secretly chosen King Salva, but when she was sent to him he refused her, since Bhishma had already taken her.

When she returned to Bhishma and asked him to marry her, he refused, bound by his vow of celibacy. Furious, Amba blamed him for her misfortune and swore to kill him. After Parashurama tried to mediate and the two fought to a draw, Amba performed austerities, and Shiva granted her that she could take her revenge in her next life. She then threw herself onto a funeral pyre.

She was reborn as Shikhandi, son of King Drupada. At the battle of Kurukshetra, Bhishma lowered his weapons when he saw Shikhandi before him, recognizing in him the injustice done to Amba, and Arjuna shot him down with arrows.

Legacy

The word amba, meaning mother in Vedic Sanskrit, survives in many Indian languages as amma, a respectful term added to the names of goddesses and women. The name Amba also refers to other figures and concepts in Hindu tradition.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, especially the Adi Parva and Udyoga Parva books, where her story is told.

Trivia

A name with several meanings. Amba is also a name of the goddess Parvati or Durga, and in astrology it denotes a condition arising from a conjunction of planets.
