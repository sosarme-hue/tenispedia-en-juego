daughter of Autonous, turned into a goldfinch in Greek myth

Acanthis

Acanthis is a young woman from Greek mythology, daughter of King Autonous and Hippodamia. Her name means "little thorn," and her story ends with her transformation into a bird, as happens to her whole family.

The myth

According to Antoninus Liberalis, Autonous, son of Melaneus and Hippodamia, had four sons, Erodius, Anthus, Schoeneus and Acanthus, and a daughter, Acanthis, whom the gods had blessed with great beauty. Because he neglected farming, nothing grew on his lands but rushes and thistles, so he named his children after those plants. Erodius, whose name recalls the erosion of the land, took special care of the horses.

One day, when Anthus drove the mares from their pastures, the animals turned on him and devoured him while he cried out to the gods. Neither his father nor the servant could drive them off, and his mother, weakened, could not prevent the tragedy. Zeus and Apollo, moved with pity, turned the whole family into birds: Autonous into a quail, Hipodamia into a crested lark, and the children kept their names. Acanthis became a goldfinch.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis, Metamorphoses, 7, which tells the whole story of the family.

Trivia

A name from thistles. The names of Autonous's children come from plants that grew on his neglected land, such as rushes and thistles.
A heron that mimics neighs. Anthus's servant became a heron, which even today flies away from horses while mimicking their neigh.
