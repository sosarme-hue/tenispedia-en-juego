hero of Greek mythology, son of Poseidon and namesake of the Aeolian Islands

Aeolus

Aeolus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Poseidon and Arne, whose name was given to the Aeolian Islands, a group of islands in the Tyrrhenian Sea near Sicily.

The myth

According to the tradition, Arne, daughter of Aeolus (son of Hellen), told her father she was pregnant by the god Poseidon. He did not believe her and handed her over to Metapontus, king of Icaria. There she gave birth to the twins Boeotus and Aeolus, who were raised by Metapontus. When his wife, named Autolyte or Theano depending on the version, quarrelled with Arne, the two brothers killed her and fled Icaria.

Boeotus travelled to southern Thessaly and founded Boeotia, while Aeolus reached a group of islands in the Tyrrhenian Sea, which took his name. Some accounts credit him with founding the town of Lipara. His home is traditionally placed on one of the Aeolian Islands, though there is little agreement on which, and an alternative location has been proposed at Gramvousa, off the northwest coast of Crete.

Trivia

The name of the archipelago. According to some accounts the Aeolian Islands took their name from this figure, and they still bear it today.
An alternative location. Although tradition places him near Sicily, Gramvousa on Crete has been proposed as his possible home.
