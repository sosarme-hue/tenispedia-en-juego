Mesopotamian god of the underworld and the primordial deities

Enmesharra

Enmesharra is a Mesopotamian god linked to the underworld, belonging to the oldest generation of deities, whose name means "lord of all the me". He matters mainly because texts place him among the ancestors of Enlil and in ancient stories of struggles for divine power.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian tradition, Enmesharra belonged to a very old generation of gods, so he was often described as a ghost or a resident of the underworld. He appears mostly in lists of primordial deities, such as the so-called "theogony of Enlil", and in fragmentary myths describing clashes with Enlil, Ninurta or Marduk.

In the myth of Enlil and Namzitara he is Enlil's paternal uncle and, it seems, once ruled the universe in the distant past, perhaps after seizing the throne from his nephew. His children, the "seven sons of Enmesharra", resemble the Sebitti. In one story, when facing an opponent, he handed his sons over to save his own life. Some texts describe him as dead and buried after a battle at Shuruppak, while others picture him as a ghost.

Ancient sources: He appears in lists from the theogony of Enlil, in the myth of Enlil and Namzitara, in Enmesharra's Defeat and in the Old Babylonian Death of Gilgamesh.
