Shiva's pillar-of-light shrines, the twelve sacred lingam temples

Jyotirlinga

A jyotirlinga ('lingam of light') is a Hindu shrine where Shiva is worshipped in the form of a lingam, a stone symbol linked to his power. Tradition counts twelve particularly sacred shrines across India, and they remain the most revered for his devotees.

The myth

According to the Shiva Purana, Brahma and Vishnu argued over which of them was supreme. Shiva appeared before them as an endless column of light and challenged them to find its end. Vishnu admitted he could not, while Brahma claimed success and offered a ketaki flower as proof. Shiva punished the lie: the flower was barred from his worship, and Brahma was left with very few devotees and a single temple, at Pushkar. Vishnu, by contrast, was honoured for his honesty.

Each of the twelve shrines has its own legend. At Somnath, Shiva is shown with the moon on his head; at Nageshwar, he defeated the demon Daruka. Tradition holds that reciting their names frees one from all sins.

Worship and religion

Devotees visit these temples to worship the lingam. At Srisailam, every visitor may touch the deity, and Kedarnath, reachable only on foot, is open just six months a year because of snow. At Kashi Vishwanath in Varanasi, a black stone lingam stands on a square silver altar.

Legacy

The scholar Adi Shankara composed his Shivananda Lahari at Srisailam, and the names of these twelve sites appear in a Sanskrit hymn, the Dvādaśa Jyotirliṅga Stotram.

Ancient sources: The Shiva Purana lists the names of the twelve shrines in its Shata-rudra-samhita, and gives detailed stories in the Kotirudra-samhita.

Trivia

A temple to Brahma. According to the legend, Brahma received only one temple in his honour, at Pushkar, as punishment for his lie.
Twenty-two sacred springs. Rameswaram, the southernmost of the twelve, has 22 sacred springs regarded as medicinal.
