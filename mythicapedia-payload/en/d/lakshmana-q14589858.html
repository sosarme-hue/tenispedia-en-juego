wife of Krishna in Hindu tradition

Lakshmana

Lakshmana is the seventh of the Ashtabharya, the eight principal consorts of Krishna, an avatar of the god Vishnu and king of Dvaraka in the Dvapara Yuga. Her story appears in Hindu texts such as the Bhagavata Purana and the Harivamsa.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, Lakshmana was the daughter of an unnamed ruler of the kingdom of Madra, and she was praised for her good qualities. Her father held a svayamvara, the ceremony in which a bride chooses her husband from among the assembled suitors. Krishna carried her off from the ceremony, much as the divine eagle Garuda stole the jar of amrita, the elixir of life, from the gods.

Another version says Krishna won her in an archery contest. The kings Jarasandha and Duryodhana missed the target, and Arjuna, the finest archer, deliberately missed so that Krishna could win her hand. Bhima refused to take part out of deference to Krishna. In the end Krishna hit the mark.

The Bhagavata Purana credits her with ten sons. After Krishna's death, his wives threw themselves onto his funeral pyre, according to the same text.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana, the Padma Purana, the Vishnu Purana and the Harivamsa mention Lakshmana, although they differ on her lineage and her children.
