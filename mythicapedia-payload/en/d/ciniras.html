king and priest of Aphrodite on Cyprus

Cinyras

Cinyras is a king and priest in Greek mythology, linked to the island of Cyprus and the cult of Aphrodite. He is considered the founder of the city of Paphos and, in some accounts, the father of Adonis.

The myth

According to the myth, Cinyras arrived on Cyprus from Asia, founded Paphos and ruled so prosperously that his wealth became proverbial. Accounts of his ancestry differ: the Bibliotheca makes him a descendant of Eos and Cephalus, while other authors call him a son of Apollo or of Paphos.

In the Trojan War, according to Homer, he gave Agamemnon a corselet. Later tradition adds that he promised fifty ships but sent only one, commanded by his son Mygdalion, while the rest were made of clay.

His end is also told in different ways. Some say Apollo defeated him in a musical contest and that he took his own life; others say his daughters threw themselves into the sea and were turned into halcyon birds.

The best-known story is that of his daughter Myrrha who, according to Ovid, conceived Adonis with her father. When he discovered it, Cinyras wanted to kill her; she begged the gods to change her, and she became the tree whose resin is myrrh.

Worship and religion

In Paphos he was revered as the founder and first priest of Aphrodite Paphia. The office became hereditary within his line, the Cinyradae. Tacitus notes that in his time only a priest of Cinyras's family was consulted at the sanctuary. There is no evidence that this cult survives today.

Legacy

His name may derive from kinnor, a stringed instrument of Eastern origin, and tradition remembers him as a musician and maker of instruments. The Cypriot city of Cinyreia is said to have taken its name from him. The story of Myrrha and Adonis enjoyed wide circulation in later literature.

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad), Pindar, Ovid (Metamorphoses), the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus, Tacitus (Histories) and Clement of Alexandria.

Trivia

Proverbial wealth. Cinyras's riches became famous in antiquity, to the point of being compared with those of Croesus.
Clay ships. According to some sources, the ships he did not send to the Trojan War were clay models, with clay figures of men as crews.
