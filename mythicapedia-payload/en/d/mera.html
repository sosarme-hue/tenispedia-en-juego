Erigone's hound in Greek mythology

Maera

Maera is the hound of Erigone, daughter of Icarius of Athens, in Greek mythology. Her story of loyalty and shared death explains how she, her mistress and Icarius were placed among the stars.

The myth

Icarius, a follower of Dionysus, had learned how to make wine and shared it with some shepherds who, intoxicated, believed he had poisoned them and killed him. Erigone set off to look for her father with her dog Maera, who led her to the grave. Overcome with grief, Erigone hanged herself and Maera leapt off a cliff.

Dionysus punished Athens with a plague that drove the unmarried women mad, and they hanged themselves in imitation of Erigone. According to the story, the plague ceased only when the Athenians established honorific rites for Icarius and Erigone. Afterwards, the god, or Zeus in other versions, placed the three in the sky.

Worship and religion

According to the story, the Athenians instituted rites honouring Icarius and Erigone to end the plague. Maera has no cult of her own in the sources.

Legacy

In the sky, Erigone became the constellation Virgo, Icarius became Boötes and Maera became the star Procyon.

Trivia

A plague as punishment. According to the story, Dionysus drove the unmarried women of Athens into madness, and they hanged themselves in imitation of Erigone.
Three figures in the sky. The three figures of the story give their names to a constellation, Virgo, to another, Boötes, and to a star, Procyon.
