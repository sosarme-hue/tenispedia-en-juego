Greek hero of the Trojan War

Podarces

Podarces is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Iphiclus and younger brother of Protesilaus, who led the Phylacian troops in the Trojan War. He matters mainly because his name is linked to Priam, king of Troy, who was originally called Podarces.

The myth

According to tradition, Podarces was the son of Iphiclus, grandson of Phylacus, and of Diomedeia. Some accounts instead make his mother Astyoche and his father Phylacus, so the genealogy varies among ancient authors. He and his brother Protesilaus had been suitors of Helen, and so they swore to defend the marriage rights of her husband Menelaus when Paris carried her off.

The brothers sailed for Troy with forty Thessalian ships. In Homer's Iliad, Protesilaus was killed by Hector, and Podarces then took command of the Phylacian troops fighting on the Greek side. According to the Posthomerica of Quintus of Smyrna, he himself fell to Penthesilea, queen of the Amazons.

His story shows a warrior who inherits a family duty: keeping a sworn promise and leading his people when his brother could no longer do so.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, where he appears as a former suitor of Helen and leader of the Phylacian troops, and the Posthomerica of Quintus of Smyrna, which recounts his death at the hands of Penthesilea.

Trivia

A shared name. Podarces was also the original name of Priam, king of Troy. The same name thus belongs to two different figures connected with the war.
Killed by an Amazon. According to Quintus of Smyrna, Podarces was killed by Penthesilea, queen of the Amazons, an encounter recounted in the Posthomerica.
