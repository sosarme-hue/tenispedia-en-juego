Nereid, sea nymph of Greek mythology

Hipponoe

Hipponoe is a Nereid, a sea nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and the Oceanid Doris. Her name links her to the marine world that surrounded her father.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Hipponoe was one of the Nereids, the sea nymphs born to Nereus, the "Old Man of the Sea," and the Oceanid Doris. Ancient texts describe her with the epithet "rosy-armed," a common image for sea divinities.

Her name, Hipponoe, can be rendered as "the temper of horses," a reference to the waves that rush forward like galloping steeds. Like the other Nereids, she appears in the genealogical lists compiled by ancient poets, which illustrate the large marine family of Nereus. Stories barely narrate episodes featuring her, so she figures mostly as part of the retinue that accompanies her father in the sea.

Ancient sources: Hesiod lists her among the Nereids in his Theogony, where the marine offspring of Nereus and Doris are enumerated.
