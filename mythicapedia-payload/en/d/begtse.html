Mongol god of war, protector in Tibetan Buddhism

Begtse

Begtse is a dharmapala and lord of war in Tibetan Buddhism, originally a pre-Buddhist war god of the Mongols. He shows how a local war deity was absorbed into Buddhist tradition as a protector.

The myth

The name Begtse comes from the Mongolian begder, meaning "coat of mail". He is also called Jamsaran, "Great Coat of Mail", a Tibetan rendering of the Mongolian name.

He is shown with red skin and orange-red hair, with two arms, unlike other Mahākālas who have four or six. He has three bloodshot eyes, wields a sword in his right hand and holds a human heart in his left. He wears a chainmail shirt and a Mongolian helmet crowned with five skulls. He is accompanied by his consort, Rikpay Lhamo, and his chief general, Laihansorgodog, as well as the twenty-nine butchers, his attendants.

Worship and religion

He appears in Mongolian Cham dance and, to a lesser extent, in Tibetan Cham dance.

Legacy

His presence in Cham dance keeps his image alive in ritual celebrations in Mongolia and, more modestly, in Tibet.
