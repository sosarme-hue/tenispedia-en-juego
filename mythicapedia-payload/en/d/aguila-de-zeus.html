sacred eagle of Zeus in Greek mythology

Eagle of Zeus

The Eagle of Zeus, known in Greek as Aetos, was one of the chief attributes of Zeus, head of the Olympian pantheon. Three different origin stories about it survive, all recorded by later authors, and its image was later adopted by rulers and Roman legions.

The myth

According to Antoninus Liberalis, Periphas was a just king of Attica and a dutiful priest of Apollo. The people revered him as if he were Zeus himself, and the god, indignant, wanted to destroy him and his entire household. Apollo interceded, and Zeus transformed Periphas into an eagle, making him king of all birds and guardian of his sacred sceptre.

In Fulgentius' account, the eagle was an ancient creation of the goddess Gaia, who appeared before Zeus at the start of the Titanomachy. The god took this as a good omen of victory and used the eagle as the emblem of his war standards.

According to Servius, Aetos was a handsome youth born from the earth who, when Zeus was a child hiding in Crete from his father Cronus, became his friend and swore loyalty to him as the new king. Years later Hera turned Aetos into an eagle, fearing that Zeus loved him.

Legacy

The eagle became a symbol of several rulers, from the Achaemenids to Alexander the Great and the Diadochi, and finally of the Roman emperors. Since Zeus was equated with Jupiter, the eagle holding the lightning became the chief symbol of the Roman legions (aquila). It gave its name to the constellation Aquila, placed alongside Lyra. Napoleon revived it in 1804 as the emblem of his house and the Grande Armée, and the House of Bernadotte also adopted it. The Great Seal of the United States and several federal agencies show a bald eagle.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis (Metamorphoses), Fulgentius (Mythologiarum Libri III) and Servius.

In art

Trivia

Eagle on coins. The image of the Eagle of Zeus was widely used on coins of the Hellenistic period.
A bird with special fame. In classical antiquity eagles were believed to have unique qualities, such as immunity to lightning or the ability to look directly at the sun without their eyes watering.
