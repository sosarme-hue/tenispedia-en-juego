wrathful goddess of Tibetan Buddhism

Vajravārāhī

Vajravārāhī, also known as Dorje Pakmo, is a female buddha in Tibetan Buddhism, regarded as the root of all emanations of the dakinis. She is the wrathful form of Vajrayoginī and one of the most popular female tantric deities in that tradition.

The myth

According to Buddhist tantric texts, Vajravārāhī and Cakrasamvara defeat Bhairava and Kali, embodiments of ego, in a battle in which each side pits twenty-four emanations of itself against the other across twenty-four sacred sites.

Iconographically, she appears naked, often red-skinned, dancing on a corpse, with a kapala in her left hand and a curved knife in her right. Her distinguishing feature is the sow's head, a symbol of victory over ignorance.

Worship and religion

She is widely venerated in all schools of Tibetan Buddhism, and in the Kagyu school she is one of the main yidam practices. Her incarnation lineage, the Samding Dorje Phagmo, began at Samding Monastery in 1717 to tame Yamdrok Lake; she is said to have halted the Dzungar invasion. Her worship continues today.

Ancient sources: Buddhist tantric texts such as the Cakrasamvara Tantra, in which she appears alongside Cakrasamvara.

Trivia

Sows and raiders. According to tradition, the abbess Samding Dorje Phagmo turned 80 novices into wild sows, prompting the Dzungars to flee and leave their plunder at the monastery.
