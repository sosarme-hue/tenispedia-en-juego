Greek nymph of the Parnassus springs

Corycia

Corycia is a nymph of Greek mythology linked to the springs of Mount Parnassus, in Phocis. She is remembered as the mother of Lycorus, from whom the city of Lycoreia took its name.

The myth

According to Pausanias, Corycia bore a son, Lycorus, to Apollo, and the city of Lycoreia took its name from him. The same author records another explanation for the city's name: after the flood of Deucalion, survivors took refuge on the heights of Parnassus, guided by wolves, and founded there the city they called Lycoreia.

The sources do not agree on her parentage. Some make her a daughter of the river Kephisos, others of the Pleistos, and the Coryciae, mentioned in the plural, are described as daughters of the latter. She is closely associated with the nymphs Kleodora and Melaina, also tied to Parnassus, and with Castalia, who presided over the sacred springs at Delphi.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, mentions her when explaining the origin of the name Lycoreia and of the Corycian Cave.

Trivia

A cave named after her. According to tradition, the Corycian Cave and its springs took their name from this nymph.
Nymphs in the plural. The Coryciae, in the plural, are described as daughters of the river Pleistos, showing that the name could refer to a group of nymphs.
