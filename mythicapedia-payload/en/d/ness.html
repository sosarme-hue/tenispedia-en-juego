princess of the Ulaid, mother of Conchobar

Ness

Ness is a princess of the Ulaid and the mother of King Conchobar mac Nessa in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. Her story, marked by violence and a suggested incest, makes her one of the most complex female figures in that cycle.

The myth

In one version, Ness asks the druid Cathbad what day is auspicious; he replies that it is good for conceiving a king, and she takes him to bed. Another tradition says she was raised by twelve foster-fathers, whom Cathbad, leader of a band of fianna, killed. When he finds her alone bathing, Cathbad demands her as his wife.

Ness gives birth to Conchobar beside the river that gives him his name. Later, when Fergus mac Róich asks to marry her, the boy, advised by his mother, keeps the throne after the one-year reign Fergus granted him. According to some traditions, Ness was also the mother of Cormac Cond Longas, born of incest with Conchobar.

Legacy

Cartoonist Patrick Brown adapted her story as a webcomic in 2007-2008.

Trivia

A drink with worms. Cathbad makes Ness drink river water in which two worms are floating. The story denies that this causes the pregnancy, although in many Irish legends heroes are conceived when their mothers swallow a tiny creature.
A birthday prophecy. Cathbad prophesies that if Ness can hold off giving birth until the next day, her son will share a birthday with Jesus Christ.
