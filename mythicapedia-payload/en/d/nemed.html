legendary chieftain of the Nemedians in Ireland

Nemed

Nemed is the chieftain who, according to medieval Irish tradition, led the third wave of settlers to Ireland. His story explains the origin of the Nemedians, a people who fought the Fomorians and whose fate shaped those who came after them.

The myth

According to the tradition, Nemed reached Ireland with his wife Macha and his four sons after a long voyage from the Caspian Sea. Macha died twelve days after landing and was buried at Ard Mhacha, today's Armagh. The Nemedians cleared plains, built royal forts and won four battles against the Fomorians, beings that modern scholars read as destructive forces of nature.

Nemed died of plague nine years after his arrival. His descendants fell under Fomorian rule, which demanded two thirds of their children, grain and milk every Samhain. After many years they rose up and attacked Conand's Tower, but nearly all of them perished in the counterattack. The survivors scattered: some went into the north of the world, others to Britain and others south to Greece.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Lebor Gabála Érenn, compiled in the 11th century, and the Historia Brittonum also lists the Nemedians among the early settlements of Ireland.

Trivia

A name with meaning. In Old Irish, nemed means something like privileged or holy, and it may be related to a Celtic root meaning sky.
