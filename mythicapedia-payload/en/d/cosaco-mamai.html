legendary hero of Ukrainian folklore

Cossack Mamai

Cossack Mamai is one of the most popular characters in Ukrainian folklore and a symbol of the people's affection for the Cossacks. He has no fixed narrative; his importance lies mainly in folk painting, where he embodies the ideal of the free Cossack.

The myth

Cossack Mamai appears in legends, folk tales, parables and proverbs. Rather than a biography, he is a figure who gathers idealized traits: bravery, independence and a love of music and free life.

His image was fixed above all in Ukrainian folk painting, from the late 17th century to the present. The typical portrait shows a Cossack with a shaved head, long moustache, thick eyebrows and rosy cheeks, wearing a coat, red boots and blue trousers. He is usually seated with his legs crossed, playing the kobza, an instrument that has become a symbol of the Ukrainian soul. A horse, representing freedom and loyalty, and an oak, suggesting the strength of the people, accompany him. Some paintings also show a sword with a red flag, a bottle and a chato, objects linked to the Cossack's death.

The paintings were made on cloth, walls and doors, often with inscriptions such as "I am Cossack Mamai, don't touch me". Each inscription spoke of the household's owners, their character and way of life.

Legacy

The figure has inspired novels and poetry, including Olexandr Ilchenko's novel No End to Cossack Kin, Leonid Gorlach's verse novel Mamay and Ihor Kalynets's collection The Cossack Mamay Myth. In 2003 director Oles Sanin made the film Mamay. Ukrainian bands Komu Vnyz and Vopli Vidoplyasova have referenced him in their songs. During the Revolution of Dignity, artist Mykola Goncharov reworked him in graffiti and posters, replacing the kobza with a rifle. He also appears on a 1997 Ukrainian coin.

Trivia

National coin. In 1997 the National Bank of Ukraine minted a coin showing Cossack Mamai smoking a pipe and playing the kobza.
Painted homes. His images were painted on walls, cloth and doors, and the inscriptions described the character of the owners.
