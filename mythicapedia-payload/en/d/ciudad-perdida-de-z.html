The Lost City of Z

The Lost City of Z is the name British surveyor Percy Fawcett gave to a supposed indigenous city hidden in the jungle of Brazil's Mato Grosso state. His disappearing search made it one of the most famous exploration legends of the twentieth century.

The myth

Fawcett theorized that a complex civilization had once existed in the Amazon region and that isolated ruins might have survived. His main support was Manuscript 512, held at Brazil's National Library and attributed to the Portuguese bandeirante João da Silva Guimarães. According to the document, in 1753 a group of bandeirantes found a city with arches, a statue, and a temple with hieroglyphs, though it gave no location and its veracity is doubtful.

Fawcett planned to seek "Z" first and then this city, but World War I interrupted his plans. In 1920 he set out on an expedition that he abandoned after fever and having to shoot his pack animal. In 1925, on a second expedition, he vanished in the jungle with his son Jack and Raleigh Rimell, and what happened to them was never established.

Legacy

The story inspired David Grann's 2005 New Yorker article, later a 2009 book of the same title and a 2016 film by James Gray starring Charlie Hunnam, Robert Pattinson, Tom Holland and Sienna Miller. It also features in the 1991 novel Indiana Jones and the Seven Veils.

Trivia

Kuhikugu. After Fawcett's presumed death, Westerners discovered the Kuhikugu site in 1925, near the headwaters of the Xingu River. It holds the ruins of about twenty cities and villages that may have housed up to 50,000 people.
Casarabe in Bolivia. In 2022, settlements of the Casarabe culture were found in the Llanos de Moxos savannah in Bolivia, surveyed by lidar. Though not in Brazil or the Amazon, they support the idea of ancient settlements in the region, yet they do not match the description in Manuscript 512.
