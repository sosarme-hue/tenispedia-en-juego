Roman goddess of safe return

Fortuna Redux

Fortuna Redux was a form of the goddess Fortuna who watched over the safe return from a long or perilous journey. Her cult, linked first to Augustus's homecoming and later to the imperial cult, made her one of the most common guises of Fortuna in imperial Rome.

The myth

The sources present Fortuna Redux less as the protagonist of stories than as a specific aspect of Fortuna, the goddess of luck. Her role was to guard the journey home, above all that of emperors who left Rome and came back to the city.

Her religious origin dates to 19 BC, when a festival on October 12 was created to mark Augustus's return from Asia Minor. After his death, the holiday became known as the Augustalia. Domitian later built a temple to the goddess after his triumphal return from Germany.

Worship and religion

Annual sacrifices were offered by the pontiffs and Vestals at an altar dedicated to her, the Ara Fortunae Reducis, probably beside the Temple of Honor and Virtue near the Porta Capena. Her cult spread through the Western Empire, and at Cirta in Numidia a dedication uses the epithet Augusta. Individuals also made vows of thanks for safe returns, such as a veteran of the Legio XXI Rapax at Glanum.
