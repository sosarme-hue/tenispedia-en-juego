Hindu sage, husband of the serpent goddess Manasa

Jaratkaru

Jaratkaru is a rishi, an ascetic sage in Hinduism, best known for his marriage to the serpent goddess Manasa and for being the father of Astika, the youth who saved the serpents from Janamejaya's sacrifice. He appears as a secondary character in the tales of Astika and Manasa.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Jaratkaru belonged to the Yayavara Brahmana lineage and was the last heir of his clan. His name is explained by his huge body, which he reduced through austerities until it was consumed: jara means "consumption" and karu, "monstrous".

The tradition tells that, wandering as an ascetic, he found his ancestors hanging upside down over a pit leading to hell, while a rat gnawed the grass root that held them. They reproached his vow of celibacy and begged him to marry. Jaratkaru agreed on conditions: he would take only a wife with his own name, given to him by her family. After a long search, Vasuki, king of the nagas, offered his sister Manasa, on Brahma's advice, to fulfil a prophecy that would save the serpents.

The marriage was cold. Jaratkaru left after waking in anger when Manasa woke him for his ablutions and ritual fire. Before leaving, he made her pregnant, and she bore Astika, who stopped Janamejaya's sacrifice. Jaratkaru died after a long life and reached heaven.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata, the Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, and in the Manasa Vijaya by Bipradas Pipilai, a medieval Bengali work.

Trivia

The name explained. The Mahabharata explains his name by his huge body, which the ascetic consumed through austerities.
An unconsummated marriage. In the Brahma Vaivarta Purana, Jaratkaru refuses to touch Manasa and sleeps apart under a fig tree.
