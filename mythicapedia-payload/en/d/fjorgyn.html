personification of the earth in Norse mythology

Fjörgyn

Fjörgyn is a personification of the earth in Norse mythology and, according to the sources, the mother of the thunder god Thor. Her masculine form, Fjörgynn, appears as the father of Frigg, wife of Odin.

The myth

Norse sources present Fjörgyn in the feminine as the mother of Thor. In poems of the Poetic Edda, such as Hárbarðsljóð, she is named this way, and in Völuspá Thor appears as «Fjörgyn's son» in a kenning, a poetic periphrasis. Her masculine version, Fjörgynn, is the father of Frigg according to the Gylfaginning and the Skáldskaparmál in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

The name is also a poetic synonym for «land» or «earth», sharing its meaning with Jörð, so many scholars suspect Fjörgyn may be another name for this goddess. Some researchers see her as part of a divine pair about which little has survived. Others link her to an ancient Indo-European thunder or rain god, *Perkwunos, given her kinship with Perkūnas, Perun and perhaps Parjanya.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Poetic Edda, compiled in the 13th century from earlier traditional sources, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A name linked to mountains. Its name comes from a root meaning «mountain», the same one found in the Old High German Firgunnea, referring to the Ore Mountains.
Two forms, one name. The same figure appears in the feminine as Fjörgyn and in the masculine as Fjörgynn, which scholars interpret in different ways.
