Shinto god of wind and water

Takeminakata

Takeminakata is a Shinto deity, son of Ōkuninushi and the main god of Suwa Shrine in Nagano. He was revered as a god of wind, water, agriculture and war.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Takeminakata is a son of Ōkuninushi, a deity of Izumo. When the heavenly gods sent by Takemikazuchi demand the land, he appears lifting a huge boulder with one hand and challenges the newcomer to a trial of strength. Takemikazuchi crushes his hand like a reed; tradition regards this contest as the first sumo bout. Takeminakata flees to Suwa and surrenders there.

Local traditions tell a different story, presenting him as an invader who defeats the mountain god Moreya. According to legend, when the ice on Lake Suwa cracks, Takeminakata is crossing the water to visit his wife Yasakatome, enshrined on the opposite shore.

Worship and religion

He is the main deity of the great Suwa Shrine, where he shares worship with Yasakatome. In the medieval period, samurai clans such as the Takeda venerated him fervently, and their war banners bore his titles.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Sendai Kuji Hongi present him as a son of Ōkuninushi who is defeated by Takemikazuchi.

Trivia

A founding contest. His contest with Takemikazuchi is considered the first sumo bout, according to the tradition recorded in the Kojiki.
Many names. He is also known as Suwa Daimyōjin or Suwa Myōjin, and the origin of his name has several possible explanations.
