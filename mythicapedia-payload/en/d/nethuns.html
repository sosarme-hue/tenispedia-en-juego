etruscan god of waters

Nethuns

Nethuns is the Etruscan god of water, first of wells and springs and later of all waters, the seas included. He is often linked to the Roman Neptune and, through syncretism, to the Greek Poseidon.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Nethuns was the brother of Tinia, the supreme god. Accounts present him as a god who could make water rise from the ground, and his domain grew over time until it took in the seas, by the middle of the Etruscan period. His attributes were the trident, the anchor, the hippocampus and dolphins, and as a patron god he wears a ketos, a sea monster, as a headdress.

His name points to an ancient shared origin: it may be related to Nechtan, the Celtic god, and to Apam Napat, figures in Vedic India and Persia, all perhaps tied to a Proto-Indo-European word meaning nephew or grandson. The Etruscans may have borrowed it from the Umbrian *Nehtuns, and its root neptu- refers to moisture.

Worship and religion

His cult is known mainly from ritual texts. His name appears three times on the Piacenza liver, a bronze model used in haruspicy to read signs, and on the linen wrappings of the so-called Zagreb mummy, where he is invoked as a benevolent deity. The sources give no data on temples or festivals.

Legacy

His presence survives mostly in objects: a coin from Vetulonia shows him with his trident between two dolphins, and his name is engraved on a bronze mirror in the Gregorian Etruscan Museum in the Vatican. His name is also the linguistic precursor most often cited for the Roman Neptune.

Ancient sources: The surviving sources are mainly Etruscan: the Piacenza liver, the Zagreb mummy, a coin from Vetulonia and a bronze mirror in the Vatican.
