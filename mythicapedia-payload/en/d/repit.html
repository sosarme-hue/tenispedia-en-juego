Repyt

Repyt, also spelled Repit, was an ancient Egyptian goddess usually shown with a lioness head. She was worshipped mainly in Upper Egypt, at Athribis, and the Greeks and Romans knew her as Trifis.

The myth

According to tradition, Repyt was the wife of Min, a fertility god, and the mother of Kolanthes, a child deity. Images often show her behind her husband, her hand touching the nekhekh sceptre, which suggests closeness and a share in Min's power.

In the myth of Horus, the child is stung by a scorpion while his mother Isis hides him from Seth in the marshes. He needs magic and spells to be healed, and a "distinguished lady" living nearby can cure him. Because of the story's setting, this lady is identified with Repyt, whose main shrine was in the Akhmim and Athribis region.

Worship and religion

Her main shrine stood at Athribis in Upper Egypt. In the Greek period a shared shrine to Repyt, Pan, Kolanthes and other gods existed at Ptolemais Hermiou. Excavations in the area, begun by Flinders Petrie in 1900 and resumed since 2012, have uncovered traces of her cult.
