Egyptian god of the horizon and guardian of the Duat

Aker

Aker is an ancient Egyptian god of the horizon, linked to the earth and the underworld. He guarded the gates of the Duat, the realm of the dead through which the sun travelled by night, and was often shown as two lions set back to back, looking in opposite directions.

The myth

Aker is one of Egypt's oldest deities. He already appears in the Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom, where the inscriptions of King Teti describe him as one of the earth gods guarding the 'gate to the yonder site'. According to these texts, he shielded the dead king from three demonic serpents named Hemtet, Iqeru and Jagw, and by enfolding the king in the earth he sealed him away from their poisonous breath. In this role he worked alongside Geb, another earth god, and the two became closely linked.

In the Middle Kingdom, the Coffin Texts made him the ferryman of Ra in the sun god's nocturnal barque, replacing the god Kherty. His task was to protect Ra on his journey through the caverns of the underworld. In the Book of the Dead, he carries the sarcophagus of Khepri safely through those caverns and then gives birth to Khepri, the young sun rising as a scarab beetle. He is also asked to hide the body of Osiris beneath his womb, under the protection of Geb.

His best-known image is the double lion, with the sign for horizon between the two animals and a sun disc above. The lions had names: Duaj, meaning 'tomorrow', and Sefe, meaning 'yesterday'. That is why he was called 'He who looks forward and behind'. Sarcophagus texts from the tombs of Ramesses IV and others describe Ra passing through the underworld 'like Apophis going through the belly of Aker', and in such passages Aker seems to stand for the underworld itself.

Worship and religion

The location of his main cult centre is unknown. His protection also reached everyday life: in the Magic Ivories of the Middle Kingdom he appears as a guardian of children and the family, and he was believed to ward off the venom of certain dangerous animals.

Ancient sources: Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead, the Book of Aker and sarcophagus texts from the tombs of Ramesses IV, Djedkhonsuiusankh and Pediamenopet.

Trivia

Two lions with names. Aker's lions were called Duaj, meaning 'tomorrow', and Sefe, meaning 'yesterday'. They represented the past and the future that the sun passed through each day.
A handover in Ra's barque. In the Coffin Texts of the Middle Kingdom, Aker replaces the god Kherty as the ferryman of Ra in his nocturnal barque.
Guardian of children. He appears on the Middle Kingdom Magic Ivories as a protector of children and the family, and he was believed to guard against the bites of some venomous creatures.
