human king in Norse mythology

Geirröð

Geirröð is a human king in Norse mythology, the central figure of the story told in the poem Grímnismál from the Poetic Edda. He matters because his cruel treatment of a stranger ends with the discovery that the man is Odin himself.

The myth

According to the poem, Odin and Frigg watched the worlds from Hliðskjálf and noticed Geirröð, whom they had raised as a child while disguised as peasants. Frigg claimed the king was so stingy and inhospitable that he tortured his guests when there were too many of them, and Odin disagreed; their wager set the test in motion.

Frigg sent her maid Fulla with a false warning: a magician would soon arrive at court, and no dog would dare attack him. When the dogs left the stranger unharmed, the king had him seized. The man called himself Grímnir and was tortured between two fires for eight nights, until the king's son Agnarr offered him a drink. Grímnir then revealed his identity and foretold the king's doom. As Geirröð rushed to pull him from the flames, his sword slipped from his knee and he impaled himself on it. Odin vanished, and Agnarr took the throne.

Legacy

He appears in the twelfth album of the comic Valhalla, loosely based on the poem, and in the 2017 television series American Gods, where the character Mad Sweeney calls Mr. Wednesday Grimnir.

Ancient sources: Grímnismál, in the Poetic Edda (Codex Regius manuscript).

Trivia

An Odin disguise. The name Grímnir suggests 'mask' or 'hood', one of the many guises in which Odin travels the world.
