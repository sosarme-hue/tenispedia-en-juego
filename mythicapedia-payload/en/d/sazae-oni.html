marine yokai shaped like a giant snail in Japanese mythology

Sazae-oni

The sazae-oni is a yokai from Japanese mythology that resembles a giant mollusk. Popular belief holds that it forms when a turban snail, especially Turbo sazae, reaches thirty years of age.

The myth

The best-known tale tells of a group of pirates who rescued a drowning woman from the sea and brought her aboard. The men competed for her attention, but soon discovered she was willing to sleep with every one of them and then cut off their testicles.

Enraged, the pirates threw her into the ocean, where she revealed her true form and bargained with the captain for the return of the men's testicles in exchange for a large amount of pirate gold, which she took with her. In Japanese, testicles are sometimes called kin-tama, or "golden balls", which gives the story its pun: gold was bought with gold.

Other legends place it in coastal inns, where it takes human form, devours the innkeeper at night and escapes before dawn.

Trivia

Origin in a snail. The sazae-oni is believed to originate from turban snails that lived around thirty years.
A play on gold. The term kin-tama for testicles makes the story's ending a pun on words.
