figure of Greek mythology, father of Aeolus

Hippotes

Hippotes is a figure of Greek mythology best known as the father of Aeolus, lord of the floating island of Aeolia. He matters because he forms a link in the family tree of Aeolus and his descendants, who appear in several ancient accounts.

The myth

Homer mentions Hippotes only indirectly, in the Odyssey, through a patronymic: Aeolus is called "the Hippotades", meaning son of Hippotes. The poem presents Aeolus as a man beloved of the gods who ruled an island defended by a wall of bronze, with sheer cliffs rising smooth from the sea.

The fullest genealogy comes from Diodorus Siculus, who makes Hippotes a son of Mimas, himself a descendant of Hellen and Deucalion. In his account, Mimas ruled in Aeolis while his other sons settled elsewhere, and Hippotes fathered Aeolus with Melanippe. Diodorus adds that Arne, a daughter of Aeolus, was made pregnant by Poseidon and bore Boeotus.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey and Diodorus Siculus's Library of History.
