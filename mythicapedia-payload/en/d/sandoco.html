Greek hero, ancestor of Cinyras

Sandocus

Sandocus is a hero of Greek mythology and ancestor of King Cinyras of Cyprus. His story links the Syrian origins of the Cypriot royal line with the founding of the Cilician city of Celenderis.

The myth

According to the Library of Mythology, Sandocus was the son of Astynous, who descended from Eos and Cephalus. Other versions make Astynous a son of Phaethon, so the genealogies do not fully agree.

Sandocus left his native Syria and reached Cilicia, in Asia Minor, where he founded the city of Celenderis. There he married Pharnace, daughter of King Megassares of the Hyrians. From their union came Cinyras, who would later rule Cyprus. According to another version, Cinyras was the father of Adonis by the nymph Metharme.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned only in the Library of Mythology, attributed to Apollodorus.

Trivia

Two spellings. The name also appears as Sandacus in some Greek sources, differing by a single letter.
Adonis's great-grandfather. Through his son Cinyras, Sandocus was the great-grandfather of Adonis, a famous figure of Greek mythology.
