Epeian king of Olenus, father of Capaneus

Hipponous

Hipponous was a mythical Epeian king of Olenus in Greek tradition and the father of Capaneus and Peribaea, both linked to the Theban cycle. His story shows how Greek myths connected kingdoms and families through war and marriage.

The myth

In Hesiod's account, Peribaea was seduced by Hippostratus, son of Amarynceus. Hipponous, furious, sent her away from Olenus to Aetolia as a gift for Oeneus, with orders that she be killed.

The Thebaid gives another setting: war broke out between Oeneus's Aetolians and Hipponous's Epeans, perhaps because Calydon and Olenus were close neighbours. Hipponous was defeated and handed Peribaea over as tribute.

Traditions disagree about his ancestry: some make him son of Ioxus, a descendant of Peneus, others son of Anaxagoras and a descendant of Proetus. Only one author names his wife, Astynome, daughter of Talaus.

Ancient sources: Hesiod and the Thebaid, which narrate the episode of Peribaea and the war with Oeneus.

Trivia

A rock beside a river. Stephanus of Byzantium explains that Hipponous lived on the Olenian rock of a river called Peiros, and that Olenus was a city in Aetolia.
A river with disputed names. Strabo identifies that river with the Teuteas, a tributary of the Achelous that flows through Acarnania, showing that ancient authors did not agree on its location.
