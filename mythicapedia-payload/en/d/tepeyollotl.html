Aztec god of caves, earthquakes, echoes and jaguars

Tepeyollotl

Tepeyollotl is a deity of the Mexica (Aztec) world, known as the "Heart of the Mountain", linked to echoes, earthquakes and the jaguar. He matters because he is often read as a form of Tezcatlipoca and because his figure shows how the Mexica connected the sounds of the mountain with fortune and death.

The myth

According to the myth, Tezcatlipoca ruled the first world, the sun of earth, inhabited by giants so strong that they tore up trees with their bare hands. Quetzalcoatl struck him with a staff and cast him into the sea. Rising from the depths of the ocean, Tezcatlipoca became a huge jaguar, and tradition holds that he can still be seen in the form of the Big Dipper. On his return, jaguars devoured the giants.

Tepeyollotl is identified with this feline form of Tezcatlipoca. He bears his attributes, such as the smoking mirror and the characteristic pectoral, and he is linked to the night sun and the fall of day, which the Mexica read as the end of a cycle, the fall of Tezcatlipoca. He had no fixed home and could be in the night sky or in the depths of the earth. According to the interpreter of the Codex Telleriano-Remensis, his name meant something like "sacrifices of earth" and "god of beasts".

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to Tepeyollotl, so his specific cult is poorly documented.

Legacy

In Chiapas, according to some traditions, he is called Uotan, "Heart", and is associated with the rumbling of the earth during earthquakes. In Mayan languages and in Totonac, the jaguar and the stars share a single word, which reinforces the symbolic link between the cat and the starry sky.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Codex Telleriano-Remensis and the Codex Vaticanus Latinus 3738, which describe him as the echo of a voice in the mountain.
