village goddess of smallpox in Andhra Pradesh

Poleramma

Poleramma is a village goddess of Hindu tradition, mainly worshipped in the villages of Andhra Pradesh, where she is seen as the protector of the community against smallpox and other diseases. She is regarded as a regional consort of Shiva.

The myth

According to local legend, Poleramma is one of seven sister goddesses revered by rural communities in Andhra Pradesh. Her brother Pothuraju was tasked with guarding them.

The story goes that Parvati once drank seven mouthfuls of water from a lake that held the energy of Lakshmi. Seven drops fell from her mouth, and from them the seven sisters were born: Poleramma, Ankamma, Mutyalamma, Pochamma, Bangaramma, Maramma and Yellamma.

Worship and religion

Her shrine usually stands outside the village, often a rough structure of mud and stones, and she is worshipped as a small stone shaped like a linga. In Yanadi belief, she sends smallpox to those who neglect her, and once the community recovers, it honours her on a fixed day in a special hut. The three-day Poleramma Jatara festival includes the sacrifice of a buffalo, whose blood is collected in a pot and ritually sprinkled around the village. She is also believed to watch over cattle, guard against drought and promote general health.
