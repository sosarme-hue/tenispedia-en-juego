minor Hawaiian god

Kāne Milohai

Kāne Milohai is a minor god in Hawaiian mythology, the son of Haumea and the brother of the shark god Kāmohoaliʻi. He matters mainly for his role in stories of a deity's journey along the island chain, and for his link to the great god Kāne, with whom he is sometimes confused.

The myth

According to Hawaiian tradition, Kāne Milohai is the son of Haumea and the brother of Kāmohoaliʻi, the shark god, along with other siblings. He appears mostly in the account of a deity's journey along the island chain to Hawaiʻi. He is often seen as a terrestrial counterpart to his brother, who rules the sea: while Kāmohoaliʻi represents the waters, Kāne Milohai is linked to solid land. The surviving texts give him little independent space, so his role is understood mainly through his family ties and his place in that journey.

Trivia

A name with a double meaning. The word “kāne”, on its own, means “man”, and it is also the name of one of the four major Hawaiian gods.
A frequent confusion. For this reason, Kāne Milohai is occasionally confused with the great god Kāne.
