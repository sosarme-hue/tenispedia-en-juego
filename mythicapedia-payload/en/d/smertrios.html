Gaulish god of war, equated with Mars

Smertrios

Smertrios, also spelled Smertrius, was a god of war worshipped in Gaul and Noricum, equated with Mars in Roman times. His name shares a root with that of the goddess Rosmerta and may mean something like "the provider" rather than a true personal name.

The myth

Very little of his myth survives. He is shown as a well-muscled, bearded man confronting a snake that rears up before him, and he brandishes an object usually taken to be a club, though it looks more like a torch. That reading led modern scholars to identify him with Hercules.

The geographer Claudius Ptolemy is the only historical mention of him that survives. He links him with the Smertae people, for whom he may have been a protective god, associated with abundance and fertility.

Worship and religion

A spring sanctuary at Möhn, near Trier, was dedicated to Mars Smertrius and his consort Ancamna. Coins found there suggest the site was already sacred before the Roman period.

Ancient sources: Only Claudius Ptolemy's geography mentions him in any detail; the rest of what is known of his cult comes from inscriptions and works of art.

Trivia

Pillar of the Boatmen. Smertrios appears on the Pillar of the Boatmen, a monument discovered in Paris that brings together several Gaulish gods.
An inscription in Austria. The god is also known outside Gaul, through a fragmentary inscription found at Grossbach in Austria.
