Persian demon of wrath

Aeshma

Aeshma is the demon of wrath in Zoroastrian Persian tradition, a daeva serving Angra Mainyu. His name means "wrath", "rage" or "fury", and his usual epithet is "of the bloody mace".

The myth

In the Avestan texts, Aeshma opposes Asha Vahishta, the embodiment of truth, and his chief adversary is Sraosha, the principle of religious obedience. According to the Yasna, he distorts sacrifice through cruelty to cattle, violence in war and drunkenness. According to Yasht 11, Ahura Mazda created Sraosha to counter him. At the renewal of the world, Sraosha will overthrow him and Aeshma will flee before the saoshyant, though in the present he flees before Mithra.

In later Zoroastrian writings, Angra Mainyu makes him his commander, and he is linked to Az, the demon of avarice, who will eventually swallow him. The Mēnog ī xrad says that after Jamshid fell, Dahāg took the kingship so that the immortal Aeshma would not rule the earth.

Worship and religion

He had no cult of his own. Believers repelled him by reciting prayers, which according to the Vendidad were enough to drive him away.

Legacy

His name entered Hebrew as Asmodeus, a demon of Jewish tradition, and the Georgian word for devil, eshmaki, derives from it.

Ancient sources: Avestan Yasna, Yasht and Vendidad; Pahlavi texts such as the Denkard and the Mēnog ī xrad.

Trivia

A name that travelled far. The name Aeshma is linked to Asmodeus, the demon of Jewish tradition, a connection often proposed from the Avestan aeshma.daeva.
A shared Indo-European root. His name shares a root with Latin īra, meaning wrath, and with Greek oîma, meaning rush or spring.
