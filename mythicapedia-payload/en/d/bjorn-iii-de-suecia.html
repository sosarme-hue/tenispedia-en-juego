semi-legendary Swedish king of the Yngling dynasty

Björn III of Sweden

Björn III Eriksson, known as the Old, is a semi-legendary king of Sweden of the Yngling dynasty, placed at Gamla Uppsala in the late ninth and early tenth centuries. He matters because the Norse sagas make him the father of Eric the Victorious and Olof, linking the genealogy of Sweden's earliest kings.

The myth

According to the Hervarar saga and the saga of Harald Fairhair, Björn was the son of a King Eric who had clashed with Harald, and he ruled for a long time. The sagas credit him with around fifty years on the throne, a period of peace and prosperity, as Snorri Sturluson recalls when quoting the lawspeaker Thorgny.

After his death, his sons Olof and Eric were chosen as co-rulers, and Eric planned to disinherit his nephew Styrbjörn. Other sources complicate the picture: Adam of Bremen names only Emund Eriksson as Eric's predecessor, and many modern historians doubt that Björn was a historical figure.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar saga, the saga of Harald Fairhair, Snorri Sturluson's saga of Olaf Haraldsson, the Landnámabók and Adam of Bremen's chronicle mention him.

Trivia

A plaque in a cathedral. A plaque in Linköping Cathedral names a King Björn as founder in 813, a date considered too early, so it may refer to this king or to a purely legendary figure.
A prosperous reign. The lawspeaker Thorgny, quoted by Snorri Sturluson, recalled that in Björn's time the kingdom was very powerful and no one suffered any want.
