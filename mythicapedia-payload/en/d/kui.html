legendary musician of ancient China, inventor of music

Kui

Kui is a legendary musician of ancient China, credited in classical texts with inventing music and dance. The name is shared with a separate figure, a one-legged mountain demon, so the two should not be confused.

The myth

According to the Shujing (Book of Documents), the ruler Di appointed Kui as Director of Music and charged him with teaching the sons of the nobility, so that straightforwardness would be softened by gentleness and strength tempered by moderation.

In the text, Kui says that when he strikes the sounding stone, the animals lead one another in a dance and the ancestors come to the service. In another passage, the male and female phoenix arrive at the court once the ceremony is complete. Tradition also links him to the state of Kui, which Chu destroyed in 634 BCE, and to a lineage descended from the fire god.

Legacy

Kui is an uncommon surname among the Hundred Family Surnames. The character that writes it combines five elements and conveys awe and reverence. Early Chinese-English dictionaries mistranslated it as 'walrus', an error that persisted as a ghost word.

Ancient sources: Shujing (Book of Documents), Zuozhuan, Guoyu and Shuowen Jiezi.

Trivia

A walrus by mistake. Several early Chinese-English dictionaries translated the name as 'walrus', although the Chinese term does not mean that. The mistake was copied from one dictionary to another.
A character with five parts. The modern character for Kui combines five elements simplified from ancient pictographs that showed a creature with a demon's face, arms, belly, tail and two feet.
