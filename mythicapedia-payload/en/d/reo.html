Lusitanian god of rivers and the sky

Reo

Reo (also Reve, Reue or Rebe) is a deity of the Lusitanians and of Roman Gallaecia, known almost only from inscribed dedications. He was one of the most widely worshipped gods in north-western Iberia.

The myth

No narrative about Reo survives. His figure is reconstructed from some twenty inscriptions, almost all in Latin or the Lusitanian language, in which he appears with epithets tied to places, such as Reo Paramaeco, found in Lugo.

According to some scholars, his name may derive from a Proto-Indo-European word for the sky, which would link him to Zeus or Jupiter. Others connect him with rivers and with a root meaning 'flow' or 'current'. Both readings remain hypotheses.

Worship and religion

Dedications were made at specific places, often near mountains or watercourses. An inscription from Ribeira da Venda records the sacrifice of ten sheep, and the one from Cabeço das Fráguas mentions a bull offered to Reue.

Ancient sources: He does not appear in ancient literary texts; he is known from Latin and Lusitanian inscriptions, such as those from Lugo, Ourense and Cabeço das Fráguas.

Trivia

A very popular god. Because so many dedications survive, some researchers believe Reo was the most popular deity in the Lusitanian pantheon.
A possible parallel with Zeus. The Polish scholar K. T. Witczak proposed that his name comes from a root that also gave rise to the Indo-European sky god.
