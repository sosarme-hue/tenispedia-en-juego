mythical homeland of the early Iranians

Airyanem Vaejah

Airyanem Vaejah, whose name roughly means 'the Arya Expanse', is in Zoroastrianism the homeland of the early Iranians and the place where Zarathustra received the religion from Ahura Mazda. Its real location remains debated among scholars.

The myth

The Avesta names Airyanem Vaejah as the first of the sixteen perfect lands that Ahura Mazda created for the Iranians. It is described as an excellent place, yet the Vendidad adds that it endured harsh winters, with only two months of summer and ten of winter, and that it flooded at the end of the cold season.

In the Yashts, Zarathustra and Ahura Mazda himself offer sacrifices there to deities such as Anahita, Drvaspa and Ashi. In the Vendidad, Ahura Mazda meets Yima in this land and instructs him to build a shelter against the winter that Angra Mainyu would soon unleash upon the material world.

In Middle Iranian sources the Bundahishn calls it Eranwez, places there the creation of the first cattle and the first revelation of the religion, and links it to the river Daitya.

Legacy

The name of the region lies behind the term Iran, through Middle Persian Ērān-Shahr and Ērān in the Sasanian period.

Ancient sources: The Avesta (Vendidad, Yashts, especially the Aban Yasht) and, in its Middle Persian form, the Pahlavi Bundahishn.
