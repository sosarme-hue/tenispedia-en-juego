Hindu goddess, form of Mohini and consort of Khandoba

Mahalasa

Mahalasa, also written Mhalsa, is a Hindu goddess venerated mainly in Goa, along India's west coast and in Maharashtra. She is regarded as a form of Mohini, an avatar of Vishnu, and in the Khandoba tradition she appears as his consort and as an incarnation of Parvati.

The myth

According to a popular legend, Mhalsa was born as the daughter of a wealthy Lingayat merchant named Timmaseth. On Khandoba's divine command, revealed in a dream to her father, she was married to him on the full moon of Pausha, in Pali (Pembar). Two Shiva lingams appeared at the wedding, and each year Pali holds a festival to commemorate the day.

In her Mahalasa Narayani form she has four arms and carries a trident, a sword, a severed head and a drinking bowl. She wears the sacred thread, usually reserved for male deities, and stands on a prostrate man or demon while a tiger or lion laps the blood dripping from the head. Goud Saraswat and Daivajnya Brahmins identify her with Mohini, call her Narayani and Rahu-matthani, slayer of Rahu, as told in the Bhavishya Purana.

Worship and religion

Her chief temple is at Mardol in Goa, where she is worshipped as a form of Vishnu. She is the kuldevi, or family goddess, of many Hindu families in western and southern India, and her temples have multiplied along the west coast, in Karnataka and elsewhere as her followers migrated. In Maharashtra she is worshipped alongside Khandoba at all his centres, including Jejuri.
