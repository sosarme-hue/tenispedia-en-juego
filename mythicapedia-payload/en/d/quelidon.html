daughter of Pandareus, turned into a swallow

Chelidon

Chelidon (Greek for 'swallow') is a minor figure of Greek myth from Asia Minor, daughter of Pandareus and sister of Aëdon. Her story is an Anatolian variant of the myth of Procne and Philomela, in which the victim ends up transformed into a swallow.

The myth

Chelidon was the sister of Aëdon, who was married to Polytechnus. After losing a wager to his wife, Polytechnus had to find her a slave, and on the pretext of visiting Aëdon he took Chelidon along and raped her on the way, while she prayed to Artemis for help. He then cut her hair, dressed her as a slave and threatened her into silence. Unaware of anything, Aëdon worked her sister without rest until she overheard her lamenting and resolved to avenge her.

Together, the sisters killed Itys, the son of Aëdon and Polytechnus, and served him to his father. When Polytechnus pursued them, Pandareus tied him up, smeared him with honey and left him to the flies, though Aëdon chased them away out of pity. Pandareus and his family then attacked Aëdon, and Zeus turned them all into birds: Chelidon into a swallow with a tuneless twitter, and Aëdon into a nightingale.

Ancient sources: Hesiod and Sappho made her a daughter of Pandion, father of Philomela; Antoninus Liberalis and Pausanias place her in the family of Pandareus, and Eustathius of Thessalonica names her mother Harmothoë.

Trivia

An ancient relief. A sixth-century BC metope from the temple of Apollo at Thermos shows Chelidon and Aëdon plotting together. It is one of the oldest depictions of the episode.
A tuneless song. According to the myth, the swallow has a tuneless twitter, unlike the melodious song of the nightingale into which Aëdon was transformed.
