Acarnanian hero linked to the Salii of Rome

Salius

Salius is a figure of Greek and Roman mythology, a native of Acarnania who, in one alternative tradition, appears as the legendary founder of the Salii, an ancient Roman priesthood. He is best known from Virgil's Aeneid.

The myth

According to Varro, Salius came to Italy with Evander, the Arcadian king to whom several Roman religious institutions were credited. In Book 5 of the Aeneid he lives in Segesta and competes in the funeral games held for Anchises. In the footrace, when the favourite Nisus falls, Salius takes the lead, but Nisus trips him deliberately so that his friend Euryalus can win. Salius protests angrily and receives a lion skin as consolation. He later stays with Aeneas in Latium and, in Book 10, is killed by Nealces during the war against the local population.

His Latin name matches the Greek Halios, the Phaeacian dancer in the Odyssey who loses his athletic contest. Plutarch reports that a Salius from Samothrace or Mantinea was reputed to be the founder of the Salian priests, though he argues the brotherhood took its name from the Latin salire, to leap, after the jumps of their armed dance.

Worship and religion

The Salii were priests who performed an armed leaping dance, as Plutarch describes when explaining the origin of their name. The sources give no further details about their rites.

Legacy

The footrace episode is given a comic treatment, especially in John Dryden's translation.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid (Books 5 and 10), Varro and Plutarch.

Trivia

Consolation prize. In the Aeneid, Salius receives a lion skin as a consolation prize for losing the race after being tripped.
