Hindu king of the solar dynasty

Trishanku

Trishanku is a king of the solar dynasty (Suryavamsha) who appears in Hindu texts, above all in the Ramayana. His story, marked by a curse and a failed attempt to reach heaven in his own body, explains in tradition the meaning of his name, 'three stumps'.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Satyavrata, son of Trayyāruṇa, was an impious prince who abducted a Brahmin bride from her wedding and was banished. Blaming the sage Vasishtha for his punishment, he killed Nandini, Vasishtha's cow, and offered her meat to the family of Vishvamitra, Vasishtha's rival. Vasishtha cursed him to become a chandala and named him Trishanku, 'three sins'.

In the Ramayana, Trishanku wishes to ascend to heaven in his own body. Vasishtha and his sons refuse, and the sons curse him to become a chandala. Abandoned by all, he seeks refuge with Vishvamitra, who performs the sacrifice out of pity. On reaching Svarga, Indra casts him out, and Vishvamitra halts his fall midair, leaving him suspended head downwards while creating new stars for him. Finally, Indra carries him to the true heaven on his golden vimana.

Legacy

His name is associated with the stars of the Southern Cross, according to the interpretation of 'three stumps'.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana (Bala Kanda), the Puranas and the Taittiriya Upanishad, whose Siksha Valli includes teachings attributed to him.

Trivia

Three stumps. The name is read as 'three stumps', possibly referring to the alignment of stars in the Southern Cross.
A parallel heaven. According to the Ramayana, Vishvamitra created a new Indra and new devas to give the king a heaven of his own.
