legendary European culture hero, icon of beer

Gambrinus

Gambrinus is a legendary European culture hero and an icon of beer, brewing and joviality. Often portrayed as a duke or king of Flanders and Brabant, he is neither a saint nor a tutelary deity.

The myth

In tradition, Gambrinus is the inventor of beer or the one who sends it to people. Storytellers picture him as a king, duke or count of Flanders and Brabant with an enormous capacity for drink, and their tales borrow common European folklore motifs, such as trial by ordeal. The legends give him no power to bless brews or make crops grow, although some narrators have added that trait.

The origin of the figure is uncertain. Possible models include John I, Duke of Brabant, and John the Fearless, Duke of Burgundy and Count of Flanders, both linked to beer through the Brussels brewers' guild and the adoption of hops. Another theory points to Gambrivius, a mythical Germanic king, and to a possible corruption of the name Jan Primus.

The most elaborate version comes from the German historian Johannes Aventinus in his Annals of Bavaria (1523). There Gambrivius learns brewing from Osiris and Isis and descends from Noah and from Tuisto, the ancestor of the Germanic peoples mentioned by Tacitus. Aventinus blended history with myth, and his account claimed a glorious ancestry for the Germans.

Legacy

Gambrinus has become a symbol of beer and good living. In 1543 Hans Guldenmundt published a series of prints of early German kings, including Gambrivius, titled «Gampar, King of Brabant and Flanders», with verses by Burkard Waldis. English poet Peter Mews also alludes to him in the drinking ode «The Ex-ale-tation of Ale».

Ancient sources: Tacitus, in Germania, mentions Tuisto and cites Strabo as the source listing the Gambrivii; the legendary Gambrinus figure itself comes mainly from the sixteenth-century historian Aventinus.

Trivia

Cities with similar names. It has been proposed that Cambrai and Hamburg derive their names from Gambrivium, although this remains an unconfirmed hypothesis.
The Order of the Hop. According to Jean-Jacques Chifflet, John the Fearless founded an order called Ordo Lupuli, whose members drank beer at the celebration.
