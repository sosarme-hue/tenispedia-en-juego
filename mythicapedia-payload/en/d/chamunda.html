fearsome form of the goddess Chandi in Hinduism

Chamunda

Chamunda is a terrifying form of Chandi, the Hindu mother goddess, and one of the seven Matrikas. Her name combines the demons Chanda and Munda, whom she slew, and she is closely linked to Kali and Durga.

The myth

According to the Devi Mahatmya, Chamunda emerged from the brow of the goddess Kaushiki, who was herself born from the sheath of Durga. She was tasked with destroying Chanda and Munda, servants of the demon kings Shumbha and Nisumbha, and after a fierce battle she killed them.

Another episode of the same text tells how Durga created the Matrikas to defeat the demon army. There Kali drinks the blood of the demon Raktabija, from whose dripping blood new demons arose, and she receives the epithet Chamunda, identifying her with that figure. In the Varaha Purana, however, Chamunda and Kali are two separate goddesses. Another tradition makes her spring from the frown of Parvati to kill the same demons. The Matsya Purana places her among the Matrikas created by Shiva to defeat Andhakasura, whose spilled blood spawned copies of himself; Chamunda drank that blood until her complexion turned blood-red.

Worship and religion

In Tantric tradition, Chamunda was worshipped with animal sacrifices and offerings of wine, and she was associated with cremation grounds and sacred fig trees. The practice of animal sacrifice has declined under Vaishnavite influence.

Legacy

Her image appears in the cave sculptures of Ellora and Elephanta, where she is among the groups of Matrikas.

Ancient sources: Devi Mahatmya, Varaha Purana, Matsya Purana, Devi Purana, Mahabharata and Vishnudharmottara Purana.

Trivia

Always last. In carved groups of Matrikas, Chamunda is always shown last on the right, though she is sometimes considered the leader of the group.
Unique independent worship. She is the only Matrika who receives independent worship; the others are always worshipped together.
