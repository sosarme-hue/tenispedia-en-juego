hunter in Greek myth, brother of the Hyades

Hyas

Hyas is a hunter in Greek mythology, son of the Titan Atlas and brother of the Hyades. He was regarded as the ancestor of the Hyantes, an ancient people of Boeotia, so his figure links the myth to the origins of the Boeotians.

The myth

According to the most common version, Hyas was a skilled archer, son of Atlas and of either Aethra or Pleione, depending on the author. As a youth he hunted stags and hares, but as he grew up he took on boars and lions. Once, while trying to seize the cubs of a lioness, he was killed by a wild beast. Most accounts say a boar gored him, though others name a lion or a venomous serpent in Libya.

His sisters, the Hyades, mourned him so intensely that they wasted away with grief. Zeus, moved by their devotion, changed them into stars and set them in the head of Taurus, where their rising and setting were said to bring heavy rain. Some authors claim the Hyades took their name from Hyas, while others say they were daughters of Hyas and a certain Boeotia. In another tradition, Hyas is the father of the Hyades rather than their brother.

Legacy

The name of the Hyades, the star cluster in the constellation Taurus, comes from this brother according to the myth. Hyas has little existence apart from his role as ancestor of the Hyantes and as the cause of his sisters' grief.

Trivia

The Hyantes. The Hyantes were the former name of the Boeotians. According to tradition, they were driven out of Boeotia by Phoenicians led by Cadmus.
A flight to Phocis. Some Hyantes emigrated to pastoral Phocis, where they founded Hyampolis. Others fled to Aetolia.
