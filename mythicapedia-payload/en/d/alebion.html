son of Poseidon, warrior of Liguria

Alebion

Alebion, also known as Albion, is a son of Poseidon whom Greek tradition places in Liguria, in north-western Italy. Together with his brother Dercynus, he fought against Heracles.

The myth

According to the myth, Alebion and Dercynus attacked Heracles as he passed through their land on his way back to Mycenae, having taken the cattle of Geryon, his tenth labour. The brothers commanded a large army, and the battle became very hard for the hero and his men.

Heracles prayed to his father Zeus, who helped him with the aegis, and the hero won. Both brothers died in the fight. Some sources call Alebion Ialebion, and Dercynus Bergion or Ligys.

Legacy

The kneeling posture Heracles took while praying gave its name to the constellation of Hercules, called Engonasin, meaning 'on his knees'.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Dionysius and Strabo allude to the episode.

Trivia

A constellation named for a kneeling pose. The Greek name Engonasin comes from a phrase meaning 'on the knees'. It refers to Heracles' posture while praying to Zeus.
