celestial weapon of Hindu mythology linked to Varuna

Varunastra

The Varunastra is the signature celestial weapon (astra) of Varuna, the Hindu god of water and the hydrosphere. Indian scriptures describe it as a water weapon, a kind of storm that can take any shape and unleash torrents capable of sweeping away entire troops.

The myth

According to Indian texts, the Varunastra has no fixed form, like water, so it can take the shape of any other weapon. When used, it summons torrential floods that can wash away large numbers of infantry. It is often deployed against the fire-based Agneyastra, and the only counter usually mentioned is the Visoshanastra, an astra that Indra, king of the gods, can obtain to dry its waters.

The Puranas credit great warriors and heroes such as Rama, Lakshmana, Hanuman, Ravana, Arjuna, Karna and Krishna with mastering it. Tradition holds that it was gained through meditation on Varuna or Shiva and demanded great care: an inexperienced warrior who made a slight mistake could be destroyed by his own weapon. Its use also depended on mantras that had to be intoned exactly as prescribed.

Ancient sources: Indian scriptures and epics, such as the Puranas, list the Varunastra among the celestial weapons obtained by heroes.
