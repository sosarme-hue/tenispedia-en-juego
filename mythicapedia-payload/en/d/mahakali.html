Hindu goddess of time and death

Mahakali

Mahakali is a fierce goddess shared by Hinduism and Buddhism, associated with time, death, rebirth and liberation. Her name is the feminine form of Mahakala, or 'Great Time', and in Shaktism, the tradition that venerates the Goddess as supreme, she is often regarded as ultimate reality.

The myth

In Shakta tradition, Mahakali is the primeval force of the universe, identical with Brahman. In Samkhya philosophy she embodies tamas, the force of inertia, and she is the consort of Mahakala, identified with Shiva.

The Devi Mahatmya, included in the Markandeya Purana, places her in its first episode. She is usually shown with blue or black skin, four armed hands, a lolling tongue stained with the blood of the demons she has defeated, and standing on the inert body of Shiva. The most widespread reading is that Shakti gives life to a passive consciousness: without her, Shiva would be a corpse (shava), a play on the short vowel i.

In her ten-headed, ten-armed form, each hand holds the weapon of a god, expressing that their powers come from her.

Worship and religion

In Jainism she is a yakshini, a guardian deity. The Achalgaccha order, founded at Pavagadh in the 12th century after a reported appearance of the goddess, regards her as its protector. Her worship is central to Shaktism, which honours her as the supreme form of the Goddess.

Legacy

Her fierce blue image is common in Indian popular art, and she is counted among the Jain goddesses of learning.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya, included in the Markandeya Purana, assigns her to one of its three episodes.

Trivia

One hundred and eight heads. Her garland is often said to hold 108 demon heads, a sacred number in Hinduism and the same number of beads on a japa mala, the rosary used to repeat mantras.
A saving apparition. According to Jain legend, the goddess appeared to a monk who had resolved to die by ritual fasting and asked him not to.
