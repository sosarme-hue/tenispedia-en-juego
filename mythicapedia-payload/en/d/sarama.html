divine bitch of Hindu mythology

Sarama

Sarama is Indra's dog and one of the oldest figures in Hindu mythology. In the Rig Veda she helps Indra recover the cows stolen by the Panis, and later texts describe her as the mother of all dogs.

The myth

According to the Rig Veda, the Panis, a group of asuras, stole the cattle of the Angirasas, the ancestors of humankind, and hid them in a cave. Sarama followed the thieves' tracks, found the hideout and led Indra to the cows, which he recovered.

In hymn 10.108 the Panis try to win her over, offering to make her their sister and share the booty, but she refuses. Some versions add that she asked for milk in return, which secured nourishment for her puppies, the Sarameyas, and for humankind. In the Brahmana and the Brhaddevata, however, she is less loyal: she drinks the milk and lies to Indra, who kicks her until she vomits it up.

Legacy

Her name came to mean any female dog, and the dogs of the myth are called Sarameya, offspring of Sarama.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Rig Veda, the Atharvaveda, the Mahabharata and the Puranas, and in later texts such as the Brahmana and Yaska's Nirukta.

Trivia

A name for any dog. Over time, sarama came to mean any female dog, not just Indra's.
