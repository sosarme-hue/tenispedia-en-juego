king of Thebes, father of Oedipus

Laius

Laius is a king of Thebes in Greek mythology and the father of Oedipus. His story, shaped by a prophecy and a killing, belongs to the Theban cycle and to the founding myth of the city.

The myth

According to tradition, Laius was the son of Labdacus. After his father's death he was raised by the regent Lycus, but his cousins Amphion and Zethus seized the throne and drove him out of Thebes. He found refuge with King Pelops of Pisa, who entrusted him with his son Chrysippus so that Laius could teach the boy to drive a chariot.

Some accounts say Laius abducted and raped the young man and carried him off to Thebes. After Amphion and Zethus died, he returned and became king. He married Jocasta, daughter of Menoeceus, but the couple had no children. An oracle at Delphi warned him against fathering a child, because the son would kill him and marry his wife. One night, drunk, he fathered Oedipus. To escape the prophecy, he ordered the baby abandoned, but a shepherd carried him to Corinth, where Polybus and Periboea raised him.

Years later, Oedipus, unaware of his origins, was leaving Corinth for Thebes to avoid the fate announced at Delphi. At a crossroads known as the Cleft Way he met Laius, who was travelling to consult the oracle. Oedipus refused to step aside, a quarrel broke out, and he killed Laius, fulfilling the prophecy. Thebes was then struck by a plague, because the killer had never been punished.

Legacy

In Greece the phrase “the crime of Laius” came to describe the abduction and rape of Chrysippus, and Laius is credited with originating pederastic love between an adult man and a noble youth. Euripides is thought to have written a lost tragedy about the episode.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus in the Bibliotheca, Hyginus, Aeschylus and Epimenides mention him, with differing versions of his marriage and descendants.

Trivia

The Nemean Games. According to Hyginus, the abduction of Chrysippus took place during the Nemean Games, while Laius was teaching him to drive a chariot.
The four Cretan youths. Another figure named Laius appears in Greek myth: one of four young Cretans who tried to steal honey from the cave on Mount Ida where Zeus was born. The god turned them all into birds.
