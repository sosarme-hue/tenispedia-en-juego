Taoist god of fortune linked to Jupiter

Fu (Fuxing)

Fu is the god of fortune in Chinese folk religion and Taoism, linked to the planet Jupiter. Together with Lu and Shou he forms the Sanxing, or Three Stars, whose figures embody the three attributes of a good life.

The myth

In folk belief, Fu personifies Jupiter, which Chinese astronomers called Suìxīng, the Year Star, because its orbit of roughly twelve years matches the cycle of the Earthly Branches. A Ming dynasty Taoist tale instead links him to Yang Cheng, governor of Daozhou in the Tang dynasty, who risked his life to free court dwarfs held as slaves. After his death the people built him a temple, and over time he came to be seen as the embodiment of good fortune.

He is usually shown as a scholar in golden-trimmed robes, holding a scroll with the character Fu, sometimes surrounded by children. He is often confused with Caishen, the god of wealth.

Worship and religion

His statues stand on folk temple facades, ancestral shrines, homes and many Chinese-owned shops, on small altars with a glass of water or an orange, especially during Chinese New Year. They are considered auspicious presences and are linked to feng shui, and their veneration remains alive in Chinese communities.

Legacy

The term sanxing is commonly used in Chinese culture to name the three attributes of a good life: fortune, prosperity and longevity.

Ancient sources: Sima Qian's Shiji records the longevity star of the south polar region, which corresponds to Canopus, the star of Shou.

Trivia

Three gods, three stars. Each god personifies a celestial body: Jupiter, Mizar and Canopus.
Order of display. The statues are arranged right to left, as Chinese characters are traditionally written: Fu on the far right and Shou on the left.
