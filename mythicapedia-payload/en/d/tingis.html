wife of Antaeus and mother of Sufax in Greek and Berber myth

Tingis

Tingis is a figure of Greek and Berber myth, the wife of the giant Antaeus and, according to tradition, the mother of Sufax. Her name was given to the ancient city of Tingis, today Tangier.

The myth

According to the myth, Antaeus, son of Poseidon and Gaia, forced travellers to wrestle him. After Heracles killed him, Heracles joined with Tingis, as Plutarch recounts in his Parallel Lives, and their son was Sufax. Berber tradition credits Sufax with founding the city and naming it after his mother.

The sources do not fully agree. Some name Antaeus's daughters, Alceis or Barce, and the Berber account presents Tingis more as a Libyan queen than as the wife of a Greek hero.

Legacy

The historian Mustapha Ouachi has pointed to the link between Tangier's geography and its myth. The name of Sufax recalls that of Syphax, king of the Masaesyli of western Numidia.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, in his Parallel Lives, records Heracles' union with Tingis, and Herodotus regarded Poseidon as an ancient Libyan god.

Trivia

Poseidon in Libya. Herodotus regarded Poseidon as an ancient Libyan god that the Greeks adopted, as they did with Athena.
A myth tied to geography. The historian Mustapha Ouachi noted that the city of Tangier is geographically related to its myth.
