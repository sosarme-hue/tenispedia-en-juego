Egyptian goddess of moisture

Tefnut

Tefnut is a goddess of Ancient Egyptian religion linked to moisture, dew and rain, and the twin sister and wife of Shu, the dry air. She matters because, together with him, she forms the first couple of the Heliopolitan Ennead, and because she could take on the role of the fiery Eye of Ra.

The myth

According to the Heliopolitan myth, Tefnut came from Atum, the creator god. In the best-known versions, Atum brought her forth without a partner: sometimes by sneezing, sometimes by spitting her out, which connects with her name, whose first sound resembles the verb "to spit". Together with her twin brother Shu, she became the mother of Nut, the sky, and Geb, the earth, who in turn gave birth to Osiris, Isis, Set and Nephthys.

The stories also tell that Tefnut, angered, withdrew to Nubia, perhaps jealous of the worship given to her grandchildren. Shu and Thoth went to bring her back, and they succeeded in returning her to Egypt, an episode linked to the arrival of the Nile flood. In other traditions she appears as a flame-bearing protector and as the wife of Geb.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Heliopolis, where she had a sanctuary called the Lower Menset and took part in the purification rites of the wab priest. She was also worshipped at Leontopolis, where she was honoured together with Shu as a pair of lions, and at Oxyrhynchus. At Dendera, part of the temple complex was called the House of Tefnut. At Buto she was depicted in the form of a flamingo.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts and the Coffin Texts mention her as a daughter of Atum, and later texts such as the Bremner-Rind Papyrus and the Memphite Theology describe her origin.

Trivia

Flamingo goddess. At Buto, alongside Shu, she was worshipped in the form of a flamingo, one of her most unusual depictions.
Born from a sneeze. In some versions of the myth, Atum produced Tefnut and Shu by sneezing, an origin linked to the wordplay in her name.
