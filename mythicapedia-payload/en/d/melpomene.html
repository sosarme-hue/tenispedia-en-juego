Greek Muse of tragedy

Melpomene

Melpomene is the Muse of tragedy in Greek mythology. Daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, she is usually shown holding a tragic mask, the emblem of a dramatic genre whose name still carries her idea today.

The myth

According to tradition, Melpomene is one of the nine Muses, daughters of Zeus and Mnemosyne, the goddess of memory. Her name means roughly "the songstress". At first she presided over song and musical harmony, but over time she came to be known as the Muse of tragedy. She is described as a solemn woman with a severe gaze and rich garments, and she is imagined singing songs of mourning for people of note, especially poets. She is associated with Dionysus, who inspires the theatre.

Hesiod, Apollodorus and Diodorus Siculus agree that she was the daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne. Apollodorus, Lycophron and Hyginus further say that she was the mother of the Sirens, although some ancient writers gave that role to other figures. In the best-known version, Pisinoe, Aglaope and Telxiepeia are the daughters of Achelous and Melpomene. One played the lyre, another sang and the third played the flute, and together they lured sailors to stay. They had the form of birds from the thighs down. The story connects with Odysseus, who after his time with Circe sailed past the island of the Sirens.

Legacy

Melpomene is shown with a tragic mask, buskins, a crown or a bloody dagger. Since Horace, invoking her was common in Greek and Latin poetry, and in Ode 3.30 Horace asks her to crown him with laurel. A first-century mosaic shows Virgil between Melpomene and Clio, the Muse of history. In the 1780s and 1790s the tragic actress Sarah Siddons posed as Melpomene for several paintings, and the best known, by Joshua Reynolds in 1784, is considered a high point in the careers of both. The minor planet 18 Melpomene was named in 1852 by the British Astronomer Royal, George Biddell Airy.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Apollodorus and Diodorus Siculus name her as a daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne, and Apollodorus, Lycophron and Hyginus make her the mother of the Sirens.

In art

Trivia

An asteroid with a tragic name. George Biddell Airy chose a name linked to tragedy for the asteroid 18 Melpomene because his daughter Elizabeth died on the day it was discovered. That day was also the anniversary of the death of an earlier son.
A statue in a Roman bathhouse. In 2018 excavators uncovered a Roman bathhouse in the Decapolis with six mostly well-preserved statues of the Muses. The statue of Melpomene, made of Pentelic marble and 62 centimetres tall, had lost its head, but it was identified by the tragic mask resting on its thigh.
