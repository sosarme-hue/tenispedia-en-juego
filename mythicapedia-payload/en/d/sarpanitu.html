mother goddess of Babylon, wife of Marduk

Sarpanitu

Sarpanitu is the mother goddess of Babylon and the wife of the chief god Marduk, with whom she had a son, Nabu, god of writing. She belongs to the Mesopotamian tradition and was linked to pregnancy, childbirth and the protection of women.

The myth

Texts present her as the spouse of Marduk, with whom she is paired from very early times. Sources say little about her character. Later traditions describe her as a motherly figure connected to birth, and she was often portrayed pregnant. Her name has two possible explanations: "the lady of the city of Zarpan", a settlement near Babylon, or "creatress of seed". Today it is thought most likely that the name comes from the place name.

A lesser-known story, preserved in the Love Lyric, shows Marduk chasing the goddess Ishtar through the streets of Babylon while his jealous wife Sarpanitu follows him. She was also believed to intercede with Marduk on behalf of petitioners, although some texts portray her as ambivalent, capable of praising or slandering those who asked for her help.

Worship and religion

In Babylon, her marriage to Marduk was enacted every year during the Akitu festival at the New Year. On the tenth day, Marduk returned to the city at night to be united with her. The king played the god's role and symbolically married the chief priestess of the Esagila, who represented the goddess. The two sat on a throne before the population while poems composed for the occasion were recited. Around 1780 BCE, Hammurabi had a throne built for her worship.

Ancient sources: She appears in god lists such as the Weidner list and An = Anum, in the Archive of Mystic Heptads, which calls her "mistress of the goddesses", and in texts relating to the Akitu festival and the Love Lyric.

Trivia

A name with several readings. Her name has been read as "the lady of Zarpan" or "creatress of seed", although today it is thought to come from the name of the town.
A shared doorkeeper. The uridimmu, a Sumerian name meaning "mad lion", was regarded as the doorkeeper of both Sarpanitu and Marduk.
