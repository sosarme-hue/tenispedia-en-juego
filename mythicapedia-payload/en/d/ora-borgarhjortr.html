Norse saga heroine, wife of Ragnar Lodbrok

Þóra Borgarhjörtr

Þóra Borgarhjörtr is a heroine of the Norse sagas, daughter of Herrauðr, earl of Götaland, and one of the wives of the legendary Ragnar Lodbrok. Her story centres on the episode in which Ragnar slays the serpent guarding her.

The myth

According to the tradition, her father gave her a small lindworm, though some versions speak of two. As it grew, the creature became a jealous serpent that coiled around the bower where Thora lived and kept her cut off from all outside contact. Herrauðr promised her hand to whoever could kill it.

Ragnar came to Västergötland dressed in breeches treated with tar and sand, which protected him from the venom the serpent spat without pause. He drove a spear through its heart and then cut off its head. From these hairy breeches he gained the nickname Lodbrok. Thora and Ragnar married and had two sons, Eiríkr and Agnar. According to the Ragnarssona þáttr, Thora died of an illness, and the sons fell in battle against Eysteinn Beli, earl of Sweden. Ragnar later married Aslaug.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Norse sagas, especially the Ragnarssona þáttr (the Tale of Ragnar's Sons).

Trivia

Meaning of the name. Borgarhjörtr literally means "town hart", a name combining a reference to a town with the word for a male deer.
