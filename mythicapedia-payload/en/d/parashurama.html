sixth avatar of Vishnu, the Rama with the axe

Parashurama

Parashurama is a hero-god of Hinduism, regarded as the sixth avatar of Vishnu among the ten principal avatars (some traditions count him as the sixteenth). He is remembered for his axe and for the long campaign he waged against the warrior caste of the Kshatriyas, which makes him a key figure in Hindu myth.

The myth

According to the myth, Parashurama was the son of the sage Jamadagni and his wife Renuka, who lived in a hermitage beside a river. Renuka was a woman of great purity, and as long as she kept it her powers over the elements let her draw water from the rivers as solid ice. One day, when she glimpsed celestial musicians bathing in the water, she felt desire, and the water turned liquid for good. Jamadagni, discovering the lapse, ordered his sons to kill her. Only Rama obeyed, and with his axe he beheaded his mother and his brothers. His father later granted him a boon, and the family was restored, though Renuka's head was mistakenly joined to the body of an outcast.

Later, the thousand-armed king Kartavirya Arjuna killed Jamadagni to steal the celestial cow Surabhi. Enraged, Parashurama challenged the king and defeated him. He then swore to destroy the warrior class, and according to tradition he slew many generations of Kshatriyas. Once his vengeance was complete, he gave the earth to the Brahmins and withdrew to perform penance.

Worship and religion

His worship remains alive in several regions of India, especially on the western coast, where he is credited with reclaiming the land from the sea. Temples dedicated to him stand in Maharashtra and Goa. In some traditions his mother Renuka receives a separate cult.

Legacy

He appears in the Ramayana, where he confronts Rama after his marriage to Sita and ends by acknowledging him as an avatar of Vishnu, and in the Mahabharata, as the teacher of Bhishma, Drona, Karna and other warriors. His figure has inspired coastal legends about the origin of the land and is linked to the founding myths of Kerala.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Bhagavata Purana, the Garuda Purana, the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the Vishnu Purana, the Vayu Purana and the Padma Purana, as well as in the Ramayana and the Mahabharata.

Trivia

Land recovered from the sea. According to a coastal legend, Parashurama threw his axe into the sea and the waters receded, creating the western strip of India.
What the name means. His Sanskrit name, Paraśurāma, means 'Rama with the axe', distinguishing him from King Rama, who appears in a different legend many years later.
