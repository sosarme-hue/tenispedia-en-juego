elemental water spirit of European occult tradition

Undine

The undine is a water-linked being that, according to tradition, lacks an immortal soul and can gain one by marrying a human. It first appears in the alchemical writings of Paracelsus in the 16th century and later became the heroine of stories, legends and artworks across Europe.

The myth

In the thinking of Paracelsus, undines form one of four classes of elemental spirits, alongside gnomes, sylphs and salamanders, which inhabit earth, air and fire respectively. They resemble humans but have no immortal soul. Marriage to a man allows them to acquire one, and the children of such a union are born with a human soul and a watery mark on their bodies. If the husband insults an undine in the presence of water, she returns to her element, yet the marriage remains valid and she will keep her soul on the Day of Judgment.

In an Alsatian legend, Undine is a girl whom the fairies endow with gifts at birth. A young nobleman abducts her, and when she refuses to visit her sick mother, her grandmother, a fairy, condemns her to love him forever. Tired of her, the nobleman pretends she has been unfaithful and demands a huge jar of water from the Niddeck river. After three days of travel, Undine collapses into the water, and her grandmother turns her into a guardian nymph of that river. Since then, on stormy days, her reflection appears in the river's waterfalls.

Legacy

The occult Grimoire of Pope Honorius, attributed to Antonio del Rabino, describes undines as protectors of water and includes a prayer to avoid drowning. Friedrich de la Motte Fouqué's novella Undine (1811) popularised the character, and Hans Christian Andersen drew on it for The Little Mermaid (1837). Aloysius Bertrand wrote a poem about her, which Maurice Ravel turned into the first movement of Gaspard de la nuit. In the video game Secret of Mana a water spirit called Undine appears, and the character Undyne in Undertale takes her name.

Trivia

A given name. Ondine is also used as a female given name, especially in French.
From ballet to Margot Fonteyn. The ballet Ondine by Hans Werner Henze and Frederick Ashton starred Margot Fonteyn as Undine.
