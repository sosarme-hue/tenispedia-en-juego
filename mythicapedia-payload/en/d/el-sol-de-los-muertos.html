Cantabrian folk belief about the sunset

The Sun of the Dead

The Sun of the Dead is a folk belief from Cantabria, in northern Spain, according to which the dead sent a last sun of the afternoon, toward which people prayed while facing the sunset. It matters as an example of the region's oral tradition and of how people explained natural phenomena through the dead.

The myth

According to tradition, the last ray of afternoon sun was not an ordinary natural event but a sign from the dead. In other parts of the region, the sun that peeked through on drizzly days marked the moment when the dead returned to life.

The belief thus had an ambiguous and intimate character: the dead were not far away but briefly reappeared to the living at a specific time of day or weather.

Worship and religion

People honored it by saying a prayer while facing the western sky when the sun appeared. The practice was held in high regard and survived in certain areas of Cantabria until the early twentieth century.

Legacy

The scholar of Cantabrian mythology Manuel Llano wrote his first book in 1928 under this same title, El Sol de los Muertos, giving the belief a place in writing about the region's folklore.
