malevolent god and demiurge of Gnosticism

Yaldabaoth

Yaldabaoth is, in various Gnostic sects, the malevolent god and demiurge who created the material world and keeps souls trapped within it. He is sometimes depicted with a lion's head and a serpent's body, and his figure shows how Gnostics reinterpreted the God of the Hebrew Bible.

The myth

According to Gnostic myth, Yaldabaoth is the son of Sophia, born from her alone without a father. Sophia had instilled a divine spark in human beings, but Yaldabaoth works to suppress it and demands that people acknowledge him as God.

Some sects regard him as malevolent, while others see him as incompetent rather than evil. In these traditions, biblical events such as the expulsion of Adam and Eve from Eden, the Flood and the crucifixion of Jesus are punishments he imposes on those who reject him. His sons, including Sabaoth, Astaphaios and Yao, appear in other accounts as his helpers.

Legacy

His name, whose origin is hotly debated, has inspired etymological theories since François Feuardent proposed one in 1575. The image of Yahweh as a donkey-headed god, born in Ptolemaic Egypt, also fed accusations of donkey worship that spread throughout the Greco-Roman world. Yaldabaoth is also known by the alternative names Ariel and Samael.

Ancient sources: Gnostic texts that describe his lineage and deeds, and the Greek Magical Papyri, which attest the formula Iao Sabaoth.

Trivia

A much-debated name. The origin of Yaldabaoth has generated decades of hypotheses. In 1974 Gershom Scholem refuted the etymology that dominated from the late 19th to the mid-20th century.
A donkey-headed god. In Ptolemaic Egypt Yahweh was identified with the god Set, who was depicted with a donkey's head. This association was used to mock the Jews.
