solar symbol of divinity and kingship in Egypt

Winged sun

The winged sun is a solar emblem of Egyptian origin: a sun disk with two wings that stood for protection, divinity and kingship. It spread across the Ancient Near East and became a royal emblem for pharaohs, Assyrian rulers and Hebrew kings.

The myth

In Egypt the symbol is linked to Behdety, the Horus of Edfu, later identified with Ra-Horakhty. According to the myth of the winged sun carved on the temple of Edfu, Ra-Horakhty, while in Nubia, faced rebellions across his realm. With his followers, among them Horus, son of Isis, he defeated Set, who was beheaded but escaped transformed into a serpent. Afterwards the winged disk became a popular emblem.

Later the motif appears in Mesopotamia, the Levant and Anatolia, as a royal symbol on Assyrian reliefs and Hittite hieroglyphs, where it meant something like 'His Majesty'. The Hittite god Istanu wore it on his headdress, and the storm god Teshub was often shown beneath it.

Worship and religion

Its main role was protective: it was placed above the doors of Egyptian sanctuaries as an apotropaic device. In Judah it appears on royal seals of Hezekiah inscribed l'melekh ('belonging to the king'), and Malachi 4:2 speaks of a winged 'sun of righteousness' who brings healing. It is an emblem rather than a living cult.

Legacy

A winged sun forms the knob of Hermes' caduceus. Freemasonry, Rosicrucianism, Thelema, Theosophy and the Unity Church have used it, as have carmakers such as Bentley, Aston Martin, Chrysler and Harley-Davidson. It appears on the cover of Charles Taze Russell's Studies in the Scriptures and, in games, in the Sonic the Hedgehog series and Civilization VI.

Ancient sources: Inscriptions from the temple of Edfu, which record the myth of the winged sun, and the Book of Malachi (4:2).

In art

Trivia

A sun on a coffin. The earliest known winged sun appears on the coffin of Queen Hetepheres I, of the Fourth Dynasty, placed above the cartouche of Sneferu.
A presence in video games. The motif appears on the title screens of Sonic the Hedgehog and in Undertale, where a stylised version forms part of the Delta Rune.
