Hindu goddess of misfortune and bad luck

Jyeshtha

Jyeshtha is a Hindu goddess of misfortune, poverty and inauspicious places. Her name means 'the eldest', and in tradition she is the elder sister of Lakshmi, goddess of good fortune.

The myth

According to myth, Jyeshtha was born from the churning of the cosmic ocean, when the poison emerged that Shiva swallowed, while Lakshmi rose with the elixir of life. The gods ordered her to dwell in inauspicious places, where quarrels, lies and sin prevail.

In the Linga Purana she is married to the sage Dussaha, who eventually abandons her. Vishnu then decrees that she will be sustained only by the offerings of women.

Worship and religion

Women were the ones who made offerings to her, invoking her to keep her away from their homes. Her worship peaked in South India in the 7th and 8th centuries but declined by the 10th century. Today she is seldom worshipped; her ancient images survive, mostly on village outskirts, and are often regarded with fear.

Legacy

Her elephant face links her to Ganesha, and in some places she is identified with Shitala Devi, goddess of smallpox. Her name also gives a title to a constellation, Jyeshtha, believed to bring misfortune.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Baudhayana Grihyasutra, the Padma Purana, the Linga Purana and Kamba's Ramayana.

Trivia

An elephant face. Because of her long, prominent nose, she is sometimes called elephant-faced, and has been seen as a possible precursor of Ganesha.
A name meaning 'the eldest'. Jyeshtha means 'the eldest' or 'the elder', and her role as Lakshmi's elder sister explains her other name, Mudevi.
