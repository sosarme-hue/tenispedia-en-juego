Stentor

Stentor was a herald of the Greek forces during the Trojan War, famous for a voice so powerful that his name gave us the word «stentorian». He belongs to Greek mythology and is known mainly from a brief passage in Homer's Iliad.

The myth

In the Iliad, Homer mentions him only briefly: Hera takes his form to encourage the Greeks in the middle of battle, and the poet stresses that his voice was as powerful as the voices of fifty men. After that he disappears from the poem.

According to the ancient scholia, the commentaries on Homer, Stentor died after challenging Hermes to a shouting contest and losing. Other traditions make him a Thracian, and some versions place him in the Catalogue of Ships as an Arcadian, showing that the story was never fixed.

Legacy

His name entered the language: «stentorian» describes a loud, resonant voice. Aristotle recalls him in Book VII of the Politics, when discussing how large a city should be: who could lead such a vast multitude and make himself heard, he asks, unless he had the voice of Stentor? In Spanish music, a member of the Manchegan rap group BMEM alludes to the hero, and the humorous Spanish heavy metal band Gigatrón dedicated a song to him on their album Atopeosis 666.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, where he appears briefly; Aristotle's Politics, which cites him as the model of a powerful voice; and the ancient scholia on Homer, which recount his death after the challenge to Hermes.

Trivia

Origin of a word. The adjective «stentorian», used for a loud, booming voice, comes directly from this herald's name.
An Aristotelian comparison. Aristotle uses his voice as the ideal measure for a herald who must command a huge crowd in his work on politics.
