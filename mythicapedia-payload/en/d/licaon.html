Trojan prince in Greek mythology

Lycaon

Lycaon is a Trojan prince in Greek mythology, a son of King Priam and Laothoe and a half-brother of Hector. He appears in the Iliad among Priam's many sons, and his death at the hands of Achilles makes him one of the most memorable victims of the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the Iliad, Laothoe was a daughter of Altes, king of the Lelegians, and Lycaon lived a fairly quiet life among Priam's sons. He lent his cuirass to his brother Paris when Paris duelled with Menelaus, husband of Helen, and on another occasion Apollo took his shape to address Aeneas.

During the war, Achilles captured him while he was cutting branches in Priam's orchard and sold him as a slave to Euneus of Lemnos. Eetion, ruler of Imbros, bought him, took him back to Troy and returned him to his father. Only twelve days later, Lycaon met Achilles again in battle, while Achilles was consumed by terrible wrath after the death of Patroclus.

Lycaon grasped Achilles' knees and begged for mercy, offering a ransom or appealing to Patroclus' gentle nature. Neither argument moved him, and Achilles killed him without pity.

Legacy

The Jovian asteroid 4792 Lykaon is named after him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad is the main ancient source that mentions him, as a son of Priam and Laothoe.

Trivia

A borrowed cuirass. Lycaon lent his cuirass to Paris for the duel against Menelaus, a detail that shows his ties to his Trojan brothers.
An asteroid bearing his name. The asteroid 4792 Lykaon, a Jovian asteroid, is named in his honour.
