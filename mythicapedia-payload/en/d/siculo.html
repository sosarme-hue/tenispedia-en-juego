Siculus

Siculus is a legendary king in the Greek tradition, regarded as the ancestor of the Sicels, an Italic people who, according to these accounts, settled Sicily. Sources disagree on his parentage: some make him a son of Italus, others a son of Poseidon.

The myth

According to tradition, Siculus was the son of Italus, a legendary figure of ancient Italy. The stories present him as a king and the forefather of the Sicels, an Italic people who supposedly reached Sicily around 1050 BCE, some three hundred years before the Greeks. Other versions, however, make him a son of Poseidon, the god of the sea, which would explain his link to the island.

Before this people arrived, the island was known as Sicania, after another legendary king, Sicanus. The tradition thus suggests a succession of kings and peoples that gave Sicily its later name, although the ancient accounts do not always agree on the details.

Ancient sources: Greek historians, including Thucydides, mention Siculus as the legendary ancestor of the Sicels.

Trivia

Sicania. According to tradition, Sicily was first called Sicania, after the legendary king Sicanus, before taking the name linked to the Sicels.
