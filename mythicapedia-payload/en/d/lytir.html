ancient Norse god, of the Vanir

Lytir

Lytir is a god of Norse mythology, probably very ancient, regarded as one of the Vanir, the first gods of the Norse pantheon. He is known mainly from a story in which a Swedish king consults him.

The myth

In the tale Hauks þáttr hábrókar, preserved in the Flateyjarbók, a Swedish king consults the god. Lytir's ceremonial wagon was taken to a sacred place, where the god entered it, and then brought back to the king's hall, where it was used to answer questions.

Some scholars think Lytir may be the same as Lóðurr, one of the three gods who created the first two humans, Ask and Embla. Others, such as Anatoly Liberman, argue that Lytir was a cognomen of Freyr.

Worship and religion

His cult seems to have belonged to the völvas, the seeresses of Norse tradition. His name may be linked to Old Norse hlutr, meaning lot or share, which suggests a connection with divination.

Ancient sources: The Flateyjarbók, a medieval Icelandic manuscript that contains the tale Hauks þáttr hábrókar.

Trivia

Swedish place names. The Swedish place names Lytisberg and Lytislunda may contain elements of the god's name.
