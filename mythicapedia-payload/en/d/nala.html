king of Nishadha and legendary charioteer of the Mahabharata

Nala

Nala is a legendary king of the ancient kingdom of Nishadha and the central protagonist of the Nalopakhyana, an episode in the Vana Parva of the Mahabharata. He is remembered for his courage, wisdom and skill as a charioteer, and for his love story with Princess Damayanti.

The myth

According to the tale, Nala is the son of King Virasena and grows into a virtuous young man, admired for his righteousness and his skill with chariots. Damayanti, princess of Vidarbha, hears of him and the two fall in love without ever having met. A golden swan that Nala sets free carries his words to the princess.

When Damayanti chooses Nala at her svayamvara, the gods Indra, Agni, Varuna and Yama, who had also wanted her, grant him blessings. However, Kali, the deity of discord, resents the match and, after twelve years, takes advantage of Nala forgetting to wash his feet before prayer to take hold of him. Nala loses his kingdom and fortune to his brother Pushkara in a game of dice, and abandons Damayanti while she sleeps.

In exile he rescues the serpent Karkotaka, who in return bites him and transforms him into a dwarf named Bahuka. Serving King Rituparna of Ayodhya as a charioteer, Nala eventually regains his true form and his kingdom after his reunion with Damayanti.

Legacy

His story was retold in Sanskrit literature and in regional languages such as Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Bengali. The poet Sriharsha composed the Naishadhiya Charita in the twelfth century, one of the five great mahakavyas. He is also credited with the cookbook Pakadarpanam.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, in its Vana Parva, where it is narrated in the Nalopakhyana.

Trivia

Legendary cook. He is credited with the cookbook Pakadarpanam, and he is said to have been able to prepare a full meal without lighting a fire.
The messenger swan. The golden swan Nala sets free flies to Vidarbha and speaks of his virtues to Damayanti, who begins to admire him.
