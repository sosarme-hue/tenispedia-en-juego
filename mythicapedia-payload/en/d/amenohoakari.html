Japanese kami of the sun and agriculture

Amenohoakari

Amenohoakari is a kami of the sun and agriculture in Japanese mythology. His name evokes sunlight and heat, and he is linked to the ripening of rice.

The myth

According to the Kojiki and volumes six and eight of the Nihon Shoki, Amenohoakari was born to Amenooshihomimi and Takuhatachijihime, daughter of Takamimusubi, and his younger brother is Ninigi-no-Mikoto. However, in other volumes of the Nihon Shoki, Ninigi is named as his father, so the genealogies differ from text to text.

The Kujiki identifies him with Nigihayahi, ancestor of the Hozumi and Mononobe clans, an equation that modern scholars question. The Harima no Kuni Fudoki, by contrast, makes him the child of Ōkuninushi and Notsuhime.

Worship and religion

At Kono Shrine, the main deity is Amenohoakari, while Amaterasu, enshrined in the same place, is considered a separate kami. There, a member of the Kaifu clan, who according to tradition descends from him, has long held the post of head priest. Descendants of the Owari clan also held the head priesthood of Sumiyoshi-taisha, and many Amateru Mitama shrines are scattered across Japan.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, the Nihon Shoki and the Kujiki, along with the Harima no Kuni Fudoki and the Shinsen Shōjiroku, which lists his descendants.

Trivia

Warming the grain. In the Kojiki-den, an alternative spelling of his name means that he warms the grain so that it ripens.
