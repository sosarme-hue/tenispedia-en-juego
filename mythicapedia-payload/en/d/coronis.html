Thessalian princess, lover of Apollo and mother of Asclepius

Coronis

Coronis is a princess of the Lapiths in Greek mythology, the lover of Apollo and the mother of Asclepius, god of medicine. Her story, told in variants by Hesiod, Callimachus, Ovid and Pausanias, explains why the raven is black and is a key episode in the myth of Asclepius.

The myth

According to tradition, Coronis was the daughter of Phlegyas, king of the Lapiths, and Cleophema. Apollo fell in love with her and got her pregnant, but while the god was away she secretly took up with Ischys, son of Elatus. A raven, which had once been white and had been set to guard her, told Apollo, and the god punished it by turning it black.

Blinded by anger, Apollo turned on the pair. According to Ovid, the god himself shot them with his arrows; in other versions, Artemis did it. Before dying, Coronis confessed that the child she carried was Apollo's. The god tried to heal her without success and, on the funeral pyre, took the baby from her womb. He raised the boy and later entrusted him to the centaur Chiron, who taught him medicine and hunting. In other versions, Hermes was the one who rescued the child from the flames.

The accounts differ on the lover's name: Hesiod, Pindar and Callimachus call him Ischys, while Antoninus Liberalis calls him Alcyoneus.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes sacrifices of a bull, a lamb and a pig to the god at Titane, in Sicyon. Afterwards, a wooden cult image of Coronis was carried to a sanctuary of Athena, where she was honoured.

Legacy

Her name is linked to the Greek word korōnē, meaning "crow", because of the curve of its beak. According to Istrus and others, Coronis was turned into the constellation Corvus, named after the bird of her myth.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Pindar, Callimachus, Ovid, Hyginus, Pausanias and Antoninus Liberalis all mention her, with variants about her lover and about who killed her.

Trivia

A raven that was once white. According to Ovid, the raven was once silver, with feathers as white as doves. Its punishment for telling tales was to turn black for good.
A constellation. Istrus and several other writers said Coronis was placed among the stars as the constellation Corvus.
