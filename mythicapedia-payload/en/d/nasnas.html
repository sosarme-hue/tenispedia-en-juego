monstrous creature of Arab folklore

Nasnas

The nasnas is a monstrous creature of Arab folklore, a being with half a body, half a head, one arm and one leg, which moves by hopping. It is one of the most unusual figures in Arab tradition and also appears in Shiʿi accounts.

The myth

According to the sources, the nisānīs, the plural of nasnas, lived on earth alongside the jinns, while the angels dwelt in heaven. After seventy thousand years, God lifted the veil between the seven heavens and the earth, and the angels saw that the earthly beings were committing injustice and bloodshed. They protested, and God decided to replace them with humans, in a story linked to Surah 2:30 of the Quran.

Sunni versions tell similar stories but mention only jinns. In Shiʿi sources, the nisānīs are portrayed as a prototype of the Shiʿi opponents, while the jinns are believed to obey the Imamate. A character in "The Story of the Sage and the Scholar", a tale from One Thousand and One Nights, is turned into a nasnas after a magician applies kohl to one of his eyes.

Legacy

Gustave Flaubert mentions it in The Temptation of Saint Anthony.

Ancient sources: Shiʿi accounts and the tale "The Story of the Sage and the Scholar" in One Thousand and One Nights; it is not found in Sunni interpretations of the Quran.

Trivia

A similar being in Somalia. Somali mythology has a similar creature that can kill with a single touch. It was believed to be the offspring of a jinn called shiqq and a human being.
A spell with kohl. In the tale from One Thousand and One Nights, the transformation into a nasnas comes from kohl applied to one eye by a magician.
