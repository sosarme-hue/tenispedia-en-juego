Sumerian myth of the birth of Suen

Enlil and Ninlil

Sumerian creation myth in which the gods Enlil and Ninlil beget Suen, the moon god, and several deities of the underworld. It is one of the oldest surviving stories about the origins of Mesopotamian gods.

The myth

The story opens in Nippur, with its walls, river, canals and well, portrayed as the home of the gods before humans were created. The goddess Nun-bar-she-gunu warns her daughter Ninlil that Enlil may approach her if she strays from the river. After an initial refusal, Enlil, helped by his minister Nuska, crosses the water and meets her on the bank; together they conceive Suen, the moon god.

The gods seize Enlil in the Ekur and exile him for ritual impurity. On his way to the underworld he speaks to the gatekeeper, the guardian of the Id-kura, the river of the dead, and the ferryman Si.lu.igi, asking each not to reveal where he is. Ninlil follows him, and Enlil, disguised, misleads her until she offers to lie with him; each union produces a god: Nergal-Meclamta-ea and Ninazu, rulers of the underworld, and Enbilulu, the "inspector of canals". The myth ends with praise for the couple's fertility.

Ancient sources: Sumerian cuneiform tablets from the mid to late third millennium BC, with later Akkadian versions preserving part of the same story.

Trivia

An inspector of canals. Jeremy Black links Enbilulu to the management of irrigation, a vital matter in Mesopotamian agriculture.
The first city. A. R. George describes Nippur as the uru-sag, the "city-head" of Sumer, a city inhabited at its origin only by gods.
