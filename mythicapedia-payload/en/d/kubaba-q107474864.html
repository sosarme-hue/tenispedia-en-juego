goddess of Carchemish, worshiped in Syria and Anatolia

Kubaba

Kubaba was a goddess of uncertain origin worshiped in northern Syria, with her main cult center at Carchemish. She was absorbed into Hurrian and Hittite religion and, during the first millennium BCE, was venerated by Luwians, Arameans and Lydians, which makes her a good example of how cults spread between neighboring peoples.

The myth

Little narrative material survives about Kubaba, and the local traditions linking her to other gods do not always agree. She first appears in documents from Kanesh and Alalakh and, in the Hurrian sphere, in some texts from Ugarit. Depending on the region, she was associated with different deities, and no single story reconciles them.

In the Hurrian pantheon of Kizzuwatna she formed a pair with Adamma, a goddess already attested at Ebla. Earlier, Adamma had instead been considered the consort of Resheph. Information about her deeds or ending is practically nonexistent.

Worship and religion

Her main sanctuary was at Carchemish. Hurro-Hittite ritual texts say little about her cult, although Luwian sources from Carchemish describe her as benevolent. Alfonso Archi links her with lawsuits, and Ian Rutherford calls her a civic deity. In Lydia she had a temple at Sardis, and its burning has been linked to the decline of her worship. There is no evidence that her veneration survives today.

Legacy

Her name has often been confused with that of the legendary queen Kubaba of Kish and with the Phrygian goddess Cybele, although she is now regarded as a distinct figure. Some Greek and Roman authors blended her with Cybele, and it has been suggested that she may have shaped Cybele's iconography, though this remains speculative.

Ancient sources: She appears in texts from Kanesh and Alalakh, in Hurrian tablets from Ugarit, and in Hittite, Luwian and Greek sources.
