tyrant king of Egypt in Greek myth

Busiris

Busiris is, in Greek myth, a cruel king of Egypt who was killed by Heracles. His story was a popular subject on Athenian vases in the early 5th century BC, and it is mainly told as a tale of a tyrant punished for breaking the code of hospitality.

The myth

According to the Greek tradition, Busiris was a son of Poseidon and either Libya or Lysianassa, although other authors name Anippe, daughter of the river-god Nile, as his mother. He ruled Egypt cruelly, had driven out Proteus and tried to abduct the Hesperides. To appease the gods, he sacrificed every foreigner who set foot in his land.

When Heracles arrived in Egypt, he was seized to be sacrificed, but he broke his shackles at the last moment and killed Busiris, freeing the Egyptians. Isocrates later treated the episode in his speech Busiris as a comic and unserious tale. Eratosthenes, however, doubted that Busiris had ever existed and thought the story was a slander against the Egyptians.

Worship and religion

His name comes from Dyed (Dyedu), an Egyptian city that was a centre of the worship of Osiris. The Greeks called it Busiris, and the term also came to designate the city's main god, a form of Osiris. Some scholars think the sacrifice story distorts the Egyptian myth of Osiris, who was killed by Set and then resurrected.

Legacy

Busiris appears in Lucian's True History as the leader of a revolt. John Milton uses the name in Paradise Lost for the Pharaoh of the Exodus, and in Don Quixote (II, 60) the bandit Roque Guinart calls himself a 'cruel Osiris', meaning Busiris.

Ancient sources: Isocrates, in his speech Busiris; Diodorus Siculus, who presents him as the founder of the Theban dynasty; and Lucian, in his True History.

Trivia

A king who may never have existed. Eratosthenes argued that no king named Busiris ever existed and that the story was a mockery of the city bearing his name.
A pharaoh in Paradise Lost. Milton chose the name Busiris for the Pharaoh of the Exodus, suggesting a comparison between Heracles' escape and the flight of the Israelites.
