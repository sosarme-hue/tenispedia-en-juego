popular Hindu goddess of Gujarat

Mogal Maa

Mogal Maa is a Hindu folk goddess widely worshipped in Gujarat, especially by the Charan, Rabari and Ahir communities. She is regarded as a protector who grants favours, especially children, and cures illness.

The myth

Tradition places her birth in a Charan family in the village of Bhimrana, around the middle of the 7th century AD. She is depicted wearing a red ghagra choli and a black odhani, a sword raised in a ready-for-war stance, while holding by the tail a nāg, a black cobra that serves as both ally and weapon. Her wide, red eyes show her anger.

Worship and religion

Her chief temples are at Bhimrana, her birthplace, and at Bhaguda and Kabrau, all in Gujarat. Believers trust that she grants favours and cures diseases. At Bhaguda, according to local tradition, people leave their shops and homes unlocked out of awe of the goddess, much as happens at Shani Shingnapur.

Trivia

Unlocked doors. In Bhaguda, it is said, residents do not lock their shops or homes out of respect for the goddess.
A cobra as ally. The black cobra she holds in one hand is not just a symbol: she is depicted using it both as an ally and as a weapon.
