Hindu goddess of love, chief consort of Krishna

Radha

Radha, also called Radhika, is a Hindu goddess and the chief consort of the god Krishna. Vaishnava devotees honour her as the goddess of love, tenderness, compassion and devotion, and in many traditions she is regarded as inseparable from Krishna. According to the Vaishnava tradition, Radha was born in the village of Varshana or Rawal, near Vrindavan, daughter of Vrisha Bhanu, one of the chiefs of the cowherds, and of Kirti. She is mentioned in the Mahabharata as one of the gopis, the milkmaids with whom Krishna played as a child, though her role grows in later texts. The best-known story tells of her love for Krishna in Vrindavan. Each night she meets him through clever stratagems, and the gopis, guided by Vrinda, arrange the encounter. Among the leading gopis are Lalita and Vishakha. Theology distinguishes two forms of their bond: parakiya-rasa, love outside marriage, since Radha is said to have been married to another cowherd while Yogamaya kept her bond with Krishna pure; and swakiya-rasa, the married union fulfilled when both ascend to Goloka. Later, when Krishna became a king, his principal wife was Rukmini.

The myth

Later, when Krishna became a king, his principal wife was Rukmini.

Worship and religion

In the Gaudiya branch of Vaishnavism, Radha is the centre of doctrine and, for many devotees, she matters even more than Krishna himself. She is considered the adi-shakti, the primordial energy, and the supreme goddess. Nimbarka declared that Radha and Krishna together constitute the absolute truth, and the Radha Vallabha and Haridasi traditions worship her as the supreme being. In Vrindavan, people greet each other with the cry Radhe Radhe, and her birthday is celebrated at Radhashtami. Images of the divine couple are worshipped in temples across India, and her devotion continues today.

Legacy

Her figure has inspired poetry, painting and music. The Raslila, the dance of Radha and Krishna, has inspired many forms of performance art. Poets such as Jayadeva, Vidyapati, Chandidas, Surdas and Mirabai wrote about their love story.

Ancient sources: She appears in Jayadeva's Gita Govinda (12th century), the Brahma Vaivarta Purana and the Garga Samhita, and is already mentioned in the Gatha Saptasati, a Prakrit anthology from around the 1st or 2nd century AD.

Trivia

A greeting in Vrindavan. In the city of Vrindavan, people greet each other in the street with the cry Radhe Radhe, a custom that keeps her devotion alive.
A shared image. Some images show one half of the figure as Radha and the other as Krishna, in the Ardhanari form, which symbolises their inseparable union.
