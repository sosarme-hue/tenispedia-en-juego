asura in Hindu mythology

Rambha

Rambha is an asura, a demon of Hindu mythology, brother of Karambha and father of Mahishasura. His story links asceticism, the pursuit of power and the origin of one of the great enemies of the goddess Durga.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Rambha and Karambha, descendants of Danu and Kashyapa, set out to gain powers that would let the Danavas rule the universe. To obtain offspring, they performed tapas at Pañcanada: Rambha, standing amid five fires, meditated on Agni, while Karambha, standing in the river, meditated on Varuna.

Indra, fearing their power, attacked the brothers. Disguised as a crocodile, he dragged Karambha away by the feet and drowned him. He then went after Rambha, but Agni saved him. Grief-stricken, Rambha decided to offer his own head as a sacrifice, and Agni appeared to stop him and promised him any wish. Rambha asked for a son more radiant than Agni himself, unconquerable even by gods and asuras.

On his way home, Rambha met a she-buffalo named Shyamala, really a princess turned into an animal by a sage's curse, and married her. She became pregnant, and Rambha took her to Patala to keep her safe. A rival buffalo killed him by impaling him with its horns. Shyamala threw herself onto Rambha's funeral pyre, and from the fire Mahishasura was born.

According to other legends, Rambha was reborn as Raktabīja, army commander of Shumbha and Nishumbha. He had received a boon from Shiva: every drop of his blood that fell to the ground would produce a new Raktabīja. Kaushiki, the goddess sent by the devas against them, took the form of Kali, wounded him and drank his blood before it touched the earth, until he was defeated.

Ancient sources: The Puranas, which narrate the origin of Rambha and his son Mahishasura.
