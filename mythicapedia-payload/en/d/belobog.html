Slavic god of light and the sun

Belobog

Belobog, whose name means «White God», is a deity of light and the sun in Slavic mythology, presented as the counterpart of Chernobog, the «Black God». Whether he was a traditional deity at all is debated by researchers, since he barely appears in ancient sources and almost always alongside his rival.

The myth

Belobog is understood mainly through his opposition to Chernobog. The first written account of Chernobog appears in the twelfth-century Chronicle of the Slavs, which describes him as a god of misfortune worshipped by the Wagri and Obodrites, Slavic peoples of the western Baltic. Belobog, by contrast, is absent from such early sources. He is first noted in sixteenth-century literature, already set in opposition to his rival.

In this way, the myth of Belobog works as the luminous half of a dualism: light against darkness, good against evil. The sources, however, give no details of his deeds, his genealogy or his main episodes. Researchers disagree about whether both gods were really part of Slavic folklore. Many consider the names authentic, while others regard them as pseudo-deities that emerged in later sources.

Legacy

Both gods appear in later sources, although Belobog's presence is very limited. Chernobog, in those sources, is associated with the Christian devil, a change that shows how his figure transformed over time. Outside scholarly texts, Belobog has left little trace in art or language.

Ancient sources: Chernobog appears in the twelfth-century Chronicle of the Slavs. Belobog is not found in any known ancient source and is documented only from the sixteenth century onward.

Trivia

A god almost always paired. Belobog appears in the sources almost always alongside Chernobog, which makes him hard to study on his own.
The Christian devil. In later sources, Chernobog is identified with the Christian devil, a figure with no direct equivalent in the known Slavic traditions.
