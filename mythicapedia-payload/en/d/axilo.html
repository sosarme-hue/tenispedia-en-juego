hospitable Trojan warrior in Greek mythology

Axylus

Axylus is a Trojan warrior in Greek mythology, son of Teuthras and lord of the city of Arisbe. He appears mainly in the Iliad, where his generosity and his death at the hands of Diomedes make him a memorable example of hospitality that could not save him.

The myth

According to the tradition, Axylus was a wealthy man who lived beside a road and welcomed every traveller into his home. His hospitality won him many friends, but, as Homer tells it, none of them came to his aid when the moment of danger arrived.

Axylus lived in Arisbe, a city in the Troad that took its name from one of Priam's wives. When the Trojan War broke out, Asius, lord of the city, came to help King Priam and brought with him his brother Nisus and many subjects, Axylus among them.

In battle, Diomedes killed Axylus and his attendant and charioteer Calesius. Both went down to the underworld with no one to shield them from their fate.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions him in the Iliad, Book 6, where his death at the hands of Diomedes is recounted.
