supernatural battle between angels in Revelation

War in Heaven

The War in Heaven is a supernatural conflict described in the Book of Revelation, in which the archangel Michael and his angels defeat the dragon, identified with Satan, and his followers. It is the basis of the Christian story of the fallen angels and has deeply shaped Western ideas of evil.

The myth

According to Revelation, a battle takes place in heaven between Michael's angels and the forces of the dragon, who is cast down to earth along with his followers. Chapter 12 also mentions the dragon's tail sweeping a third of the stars to the earth, a detail commentators have read in many different ways.

Christian tradition filled out the story with explanations of Satan's fall. Among the motives attributed to him are pride, his refusal to bow before newly created humanity, a motif that also appears in Islamic tradition with Iblis, and his drift away from God. In Milton's Paradise Lost, the rebellion follows God's declaration that his Son would rule over the angels.

Biblical parallels have been sought in Isaiah 14, with its fallen son of the morning, and in Dead Sea Scrolls texts such as the War Scroll, where angels of light fight angels of darkness. Many modern commentators read the scene as a symbol of the end of time or of the spiritual struggle within the Church, rather than as the literal origin of evil.

Legacy

The War in Heaven appears often in Christian art and frames John Milton's Paradise Lost, which sets the battle over three days. Ellen G. White expanded the idea in Seventh-day Adventist thought through her book The Great Controversy.

Ancient sources: The Book of Revelation (chapter 12), with possible parallels in Isaiah 14 and the Dead Sea Scrolls.

Trivia

Three days of battle. Milton places the war in heaven over the course of three days, following the proclamation of Jesus Christ as the Son of God.
A morning star. Lucifer means 'light-bearer' in Latin and was the name of the morning star. It was later applied to Satan based on the passage in Isaiah 14.
