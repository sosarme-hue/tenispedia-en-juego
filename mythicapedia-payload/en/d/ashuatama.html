hero of the Mahabharata, son of the teacher Drona

Ashvatthama

Ashvatthama is a warrior of the Mahabharata, son of the teacher Drona and one of the seven chiranjivi, beings said to be immortal in Hindu tradition. His story is tied to the Kurukshetra War, where he fought alongside the Kauravas and took part in one of the epic's darkest episodes.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the Mahabharata, Ashvatthama was born to the teacher Drona and his wife Kripi, sister of the brahmin Kripa. At birth he let out a cry that sounded like a horse neighing, and a voice from the heavens gave him his name. As a child, Drona was so poor that he could not even afford a cow. When the boy wept after seeing his friends drink milk, his father felt deeply humiliated. That wound eventually led Drona to accept the post of teacher to the Kuru princes.

Ashvatthama trained alongside them and became an exceptional warrior, able to face tens of thousands of men at once. He fought on the side of the Kauravas, whose leader Duryodhana was his close friend. When his father was beheaded by Dhrishtadyumna, he swore revenge. On the last night of the war, sitting sleepless under a tree, he watched an owl pick off a sleeping group of crows one by one, and the sight gave him his plan. He entered the Pandava camp, killing Dhrishtadyumna and the sons of Draupadi. When the Pandavas pursued him, he unleashed the Brahmashirastra. Unable to recall it, he aimed it at the Pandava line, and it struck Uttara, who was pregnant with Parikshit. The child died, but Krishna brought him back to life.

As punishment, Krishna cursed Ashvatthama to wander the earth for three thousand years, afflicted by loneliness, suffering and decay. According to the story, a gem on his forehead gives him extraordinary powers, and he withdrew into the forests. In the epic, his fate is left hanging there.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, especially the Adi Parva, Udyoga Parva and Drona Parva.

Trivia

A name with a horse's voice. According to the Adi Parva, at birth he let out a cry resembling the neighing of the celestial horse Uchchaihshravas, which gave rise to his name.
An elephant with his name. To deceive Drona, the Pandavas spread the rumor that Bhima had killed Ashvatthama, when in fact he had killed an elephant bearing that name.
