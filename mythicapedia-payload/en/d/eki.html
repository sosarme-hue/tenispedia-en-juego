Basque goddess of the Sun

Eki

Eki (also Ekhi, Eguzki, Iuski or Iguzki) is the Basque name for the Sun and the solar goddess of Basque mythology. Regarded as the protector of humanity and the enemy of all evil spirits, she marked the passage from day to night with her daily journey.

The myth

In Basque tradition, Eki is a daughter of Mother Earth, to whom she returns every day. At sunset the goddess travelled beneath the ground through Itxasgorrieta, "The Reddish Seas", into the womb of her mother, Amalur or Lurbira, where she spent the night before rising again. In the account, her sibling is Ilargi, the Moon.

Worship and religion

The ancient Basques called her "grandmother" and held rites in her honour at sunset, when they believed she vanished beneath the earth. The sources do not describe temples or festivals, and they do not say whether her cult survives today.

Trivia

Many names. The Sun has several Basque forms, such as Ekhi, Eguzki, Iuski or Iduzki, all referring to the same goddess.
The grandmother Sun. The ancient Basques called her "grandmother", a familiar title that reflects the trust they placed in her.
