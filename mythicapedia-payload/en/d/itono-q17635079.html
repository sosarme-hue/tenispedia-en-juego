Greek hero, son of Boeotus

Itonus

Itonus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Boeotus and ancestor of the Boeotian leaders who fought at Troy. He matters mainly for the lineage tradition attributes to him.

The myth

According to Diodorus Siculus, Itonus was the son of Boeotus, the eponym of Boeotia and brother of Aeolus, eponym of the Aeolian Islands. Together they had four sons: Hippalcimus, Electryon, Archilycus and Alegenor.

Their descendants led the Boeotians to Troy. Peneleos was the son of Hippalcimus, Leitus of Electryon, Clonius of Alegenor, and Protoenor and Arcesilaus of Archilycus. The Iliad names these five leaders at the opening of the Catalogue of Ships. In the stories, Itonus appears mainly as a link in a genealogy.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus, in his Historical Library, presents him as the father of four sons; the Iliad, in the Catalogue of Ships, names his grandsons as Boeotian leaders.

Trivia

A willow name. The Greek name Ἴτωνος can be translated as "willow", a tree associated with damp ground and riverbanks.
First names in the Catalogue. The Boeotian leaders descended from Itonus are the first ones named in the Catalogue of Ships in the Iliad.
