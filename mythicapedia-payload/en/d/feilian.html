Chinese wind spirit and mythical beast

Feilian

Feilian is a wind spirit from the southern Chinese tradition, later identified with Fengbo, the chief wind deity. It kept a separate identity as a mythical creature with traits of a deer, a bird and a serpent.

The myth

Feilian first appears in the poem Li sao, attributed to Qu Yuan, where it helps the poet during part of his mystical journey. The poem comes from the state of Chu, on the edge of the Zhou cultural world. Centuries later, the commentator Wang Yi noted that Feilian is Fengbo, showing that the various wind spirits had been merged under one identity.

As Fengbo, the god carries the wind in a bag and stirs up trouble, until the heavenly archer Houyi keeps him in check. Another tradition held that Feilian could grant immortality, and Emperor Wu of Han ordered buildings dedicated to its worship for that purpose. As an individual, a minister of the last Shang king Di Xin was pursued by the Duke of Zhou to the coast and killed there; according to the Shiji, his descendants later founded the state of Qin.

Worship and religion

Feilian was worshipped in the south, and Emperor Wu of Han ordered several buildings built for its cult in pursuit of eternal life.

Legacy

Its figure has been reinterpreted as a phoenix, as a winged type of qilin and as a creature with a deer's body. It appears as a divine beast in the video game Wo Long Fallen Dynasty.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Li sao and other texts of the Chu ci anthology, in the Shiji, in the Huainanzi and in the Mengzi.

Trivia

A name with several readings. Some scholars propose that feilian was a dialectal variant of feng, 'wind', and that its characters were used interchangeably with those of the phoenix.
A creature with many descriptions. Han and Jin commentators described Feilian differently, from a deer with a bird's head to a winged beast with long fur.
