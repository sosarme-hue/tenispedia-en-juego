bull-and-serpent monster of Greek mythology

Ophiotaurus

The ophiotaurus is a monster of Greek mythology with the front of a bull and the tail of a serpent. Its name combines the Greek words for snake and bull, and its story belongs to the Titanomachy, the war between the gods and the Titans.

The myth

In Ovid's Fasti, Cronus had been driven from his throne by Zeus and, seething with resentment, roused the Titans to war. Among his resources was a bull born of Gaia, whose hindquarters were those of a serpent. A prophecy claimed that whoever burned its innards would defeat the eternal gods.

The Styx had shut the creature away in a gloomy grove, but Briareus killed it with an adamantine axe. As he was about to cast the entrails into the flames, Jupiter ordered birds to steal them. A kite carried them to Zeus, who rewarded it by raising it to the stars as a constellation.

Legacy

The ophiotaurus appears in Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson and the Olympians series, where Percy finds it, calls it Bessie and protects it. It also appears in the My Little Pony series and the animated comedy Krapopolis, and in the Dungeon Crawler Carl books, where a ophiotaurus bolt can strip a deity of its invulnerability.

Ancient sources: Ovid's Fasti, the only known ancient work that mentions it.

Trivia

A monster in a list. In Ovid's poem, the ophiotaurus is one of the lesser monstra that threaten both men and gods, set apart from the more terrifying monsters.
A mosaic in York. The only other known depiction of the ophiotaurus is a mosaic excavated in York, England.
