Etruscan goddess of the earth

Cel

Cel is the Etruscan goddess of the earth, the Etruscan counterpart of Gaia in Greece and Terra in Rome. Her figure shows how central the mother earth was in Etruscan religion.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Cel was the mother of a giant called Celsclan, whose name means "son of Cel". A bronze mirror from the 5th century BC shows a theomachy in which Laran, the god of war, attacks this giant. Another mirror depicts serpent-legged giants alongside a goddess, possibly Cel, whose lower body is formed of vegetation.

Worship and religion

At a sanctuary near Lake Trasimeno, at Castiglione del Lago, five small bronze votive statuettes dedicated to Cel Ati, "Mother Cel", were found. Their inscriptions state that each was offered to the mother goddess in that sacred place. Cel also appears on the Liver of Piacenza, a bronze model of a liver used in haruspicy, in section 13. Her cult did not survive.

Legacy

Her name lives on in the month Celi of the Etruscan calendar, which probably corresponds to September.

Trivia

A giant with a telling name. Celsclan, whose name means "son of Cel", appears on a 5th-century BC bronze mirror confronting Laran, the god of war.
Cel on the Liver of Piacenza. Etruscan diviners studied the Liver of Piacenza, a bronze model divided into sections named for gods. Cel occupies section 13.
