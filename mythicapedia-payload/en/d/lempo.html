Finnish god of love and demon

Lempo

Lempo is a demon in Finnish tradition who, according to some scholars, was once the personification of love. He appears in runic songs and in the Kalevala, and his image turned evil after Christianity arrived in Finland.

The myth

In folk tales Lempo is a capricious and dangerous spirit, able to take hold of a person and lead them to ruin. According to the Kalevala, he joined the demons Hiisi and Piru in an attempt to bring down the hero Väinämöinen.

Another account tells that when a wife committed adultery, the Lempo within her was driven out by a spell.

Worship and religion

Some researchers believe he was invoked in love spells in pre-Christian times, and that his name, linked to fire, may come from an old word for sacred fire. There is no evidence of an organized cult.

Legacy

In the Finnish language, lempo and hiisi are mild swear words. In Costa Rica, lempo is used colloquially to refer to someone with dark skin.

Ancient sources: Finnish and Karelian runic songs, and the Kalevala, where he appears as an enemy of Väinämöinen.

Trivia

A cave bearing his name. In Lempäälä there is a cave and a hollow called after Lempo, and local folklore says he was worshipped there before Christianity. Both were destroyed when a railway was built in the area.
A name that is also lightning. The word lempo means ball lightning, and lemmes means a meteorite that makes a howling sound as it falls.
