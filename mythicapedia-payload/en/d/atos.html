Athos

Athos is a giant of Greek mythology, from Thrace, who fought against the gods. His name gave rise to Mount Athos, on the Chalcidice peninsula in northern Greece.

The myth

According to the story, Athos was one of the giants who rose up against the gods. Some versions make him a son of Uranus and Gaia, while another makes him a son of Poseidon and the naiad Rhodope, daughter of the river-god Strymon.

Tradition holds that Poseidon attacked him and that the mountain took its name from the giant. According to Nicander, in a lost work, the god hurled two huge blocks of stone at Athos with his hands. The Byzantine commentator Eustathius of Thessalonica adds that Poseidon eventually trapped him under the mountain.

Legacy

Today Mount Athos, called Agion Oros in Greek, or 'Holy Mountain', is an important centre of Eastern Orthodox monasticism, with monasteries still living their religious life.

Ancient sources: Nicander, in a lost work cited by Stephanus of Byzantium, and Eustathius of Thessalonica, a Byzantine commentator.

Trivia

Holy Mountain. In modern Greek the mountain is known as Agion Oros, meaning 'Holy Mountain'.
Mountain and peninsula. Athos is both a mountain and a peninsula in Chalcidice, northern Greece.
