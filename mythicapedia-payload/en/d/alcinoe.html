princess of Corinth, foster sister of Oedipus

Alcinoe

Alcinoe is a princess of Corinth, daughter of King Polybus and foster sister of Oedipus, and the heroine of a Greek tale about divine punishment and a fatal passion. We know her story through the grammarian Parthenius of Nicaea.

The myth

Alcinoe, married to Amphilochus, hired the spinner Nicandra for a year to make textiles for her. When it was time to pay her, she drove Nicandra out of the house without the agreed wage. Nicandra prayed to Athena, goddess of weaving, asking for revenge.

Soon after, a stranger from Samos named Xanthus arrived. By Athena's wrath, Alcinoe fell so deeply in love with him that she eloped, leaving behind her husband and children. Halfway through the voyage she came back to her senses and grasped the gravity of what she had done. Despite Xanthus's promises, she threw herself into the sea and drowned.

Legacy

Her story has been compared with Leo Tolstoy's Anna Karenina: a woman who abandons her husband and children for a lover and ends her own life. There is no proof that Tolstoy was inspired by this myth, although in his notes he mentions reading Parthenius in 1878, and it is unknown whether he knew this tale before writing the novel.

Ancient sources: Parthenius of Nicaea preserves it in his Erotica Pathemata, attributing the story to the Hellenistic poetess Moero.

Trivia

An unusual goddess. Athena is a virgin goddess, yet the punishment she imposes on Alcinoe resembles the methods of Aphrodite, as scholars have noted.
A echoing name. Her name is similar to Alcyone, another heroine who also died by drowning.
