Harpy of Greek mythology

Aello

Aello is one of the Harpies of Greek mythology, winged creatures linked to winds and storms. Her name, Aellṓ, means "stormy wind", and her figure is connected to Boreas, the north wind.

The myth

According to tradition, Aello was a daughter of Thaumas and Electra and a sister of Iris. Ancient authors disagree about her name: Apollodorus hesitates between Nicotoe and Aelopus, while Hyginus calls her Aelopus.

Nonnus of Panopolis tells that her union with Boreas produced the horses Xanthus and Podarge, who belonged to the Athenian king Erechtheus. During the voyage of the Argonauts, the Boreads Calais and Zetes chased Aello and Ocypete. Harried, the Harpies fled, and one of them fell in the Peloponnese, into the river Tigris, which has since been called Harpis after her; some identified her with Aello.

Legacy

The Tigris river in the Peloponnese came to be called Harpis in memory of one of these creatures, whom some called Aello.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus and Nonnus of Panopolis.

Trivia

A name of wind. Aello means "stormy wind" in Greek, a name that fits her connection with the winds.
Horses of the wind. According to Nonnus of Panopolis, her children with Boreas were the horses Xanthus and Podarge, belonging to the Athenian king Erechtheus.
