Yoruba twin deities, protectors of children

Ibeji

The Ibeji are a pair of twin orishas in the Yoruba religion, a boy and a girl, venerated as protectors of children and of multiple births.

The myth

According to Yoruba tradition, they are the children of Shango and Oshun, although in some areas their mother is said to be Oya; they were raised by Yemoja. Their names are given as Taewo and Kainde. In one story they saved humankind from the Devil: they annoyed him with their drums until he promised to leave and remove the traps he had set to catch people.

Worship and religion

They are invoked above all when children fall ill. When one twin dies, a carved figure represents the dead child, and the survivor shares all possessions with it so the Ibeji do not take the other twin as well. Their feast falls on 27 September, when sweets are handed out to children. Their symbols are paired objects such as drums, bells or pots.

Legacy

In Latin America they were syncretized with the Catholic saints Cosmas and Damian, and their wooden figures, dressed and adorned by families, attract tribal art collectors. The largest collection in the world is held by the British Museum in London.

Trivia

A twin who judges the world. In Yoruba belief, Kehinde sends his twin Taiwo ahead to check whether the world is fit and beautiful. For this reason the second-born is regarded as the elder.
The largest collection. The British Museum in London holds the largest collection of Ibeji figures in the world.
