hybrid creature of Hindu mythology, half lion and half elephant

Gajasimha

The gajasimha is a hybrid animal from Hindu mythology that combines the body of a mythical lion (sinha or rajasiha) with the head or trunk of an elephant. Its image spread through Indian and Sinhalese art and became a heraldic emblem in Southeast Asia.

The myth

In Hindu tradition, the gajasimha appears as a composite creature that joins the strength of the lion with the stateliness of the elephant, two animals long linked to royal power in India. The sources available describe its form, but they do not record a detailed story about its origin or specific deeds.

Its presence in iconography, rather than in narrative, is what sets it apart: it is shown as a guardian-like, protective being, though the written tradition says little more about these traits.

Legacy

In pre-modern Siam, the gajasimha was the emblem of the kalahom, one of the king's two chief chancellors. It appears as a supporter in the coat of arms of Siam, used from 1873 to 1910, and in the royal arms of Cambodia, officially adopted in 1993. It also decorates ivory work from the Eastern Ganga dynasty, a gateway at Gwalior Fort, temples in Cambodia and Thailand, and official buildings in Thailand.

Trivia

Emblem of an office. In old Siam, the gajasimha identified the kalahom, one of the king's two chief chancellors.
From ivory to coats of arms. One of its earliest known images is an ivory throne leg from the Eastern Ganga dynasty, dating to the 13th century.
