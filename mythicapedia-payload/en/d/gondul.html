Göndul

Göndul is a valkyrie of Norse mythology, one of the maidens in the service of Odin who decided who fell in battle and who went on to Valhalla. Her name, Gǫndul, is usually translated as "wand-wielder".

The myth

Göndul appears in the poem Völuspá among the valkyries and in the Darraðarljóð. In the Hákonarmál, preserved in Snorri's Heimskringla, Odin sends her with Skögul to choose the king who will join him in Valhalla. When King Haakon dies in battle, Göndul is seen leaning on a spear shaft and announces that the gods welcome the warrior with honour.

In the Sörla þáttr, a tale from the Flateyjarbók of around the 14th century, Göndul sits in a chair in the forest and becomes the instigator of the rivalry between the kings Hedinn and Hogni. She offers Hedinn a horn of a drink that makes him forget his oath of brotherhood and advises him to kill Hogni's bride. In the end, while Hedinn sleeps in her lap, she curses him, and he wakes to see her shadow, now black and enormous.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Völuspá, in the name lists of the Prose Edda, in the Darraðarljóð, in the Hákonarmál of the Heimskringla, and in the Sörla þáttr of the Flateyjarbók.

Trivia

A magical name. The name Gǫndul is usually translated as "wand-wielder". Rudolf Simek connects it with the Old Norse word gandr, meaning magic.
A medieval charm. In 1324, a witchcraft trial in Bergen, Norway, recorded a spell that mentions Göndul being sent out as a spirit.
