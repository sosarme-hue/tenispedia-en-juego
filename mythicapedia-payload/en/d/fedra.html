Cretan princess, wife of Theseus

Phaedra

Phaedra is a Cretan princess, daughter of Minos and Pasiphaë, wife of the hero Theseus, and the central figure of some of the most famous tragedies in Greek literature. Her story matters because tradition turns her into a figure of desire, guilt and death that playwrights and poets reinterpreted again and again.

The myth

In Euripides's version, Theseus married Phaedra after the death of the Amazon queen Hippolyta. Theseus already had a son, Hippolytus, by her, and the boy was raised in Troezen. Hippolytus was a devotee of Artemis, had sworn chastity, and rejected both love and the worship of Aphrodite, which offended the goddess. To punish him, Aphrodite made Phaedra fall in love with her stepson.

Phaedra hid her passion for a while, until her nurse, without her consent, confided it to Hippolytus. He refused her because of his oath of chastity. Fearing Theseus's reaction, Phaedra wrote a letter accusing Hippolytus of assaulting her, and then hanged herself. When Theseus read the letter, he prayed to Poseidon to kill his son. The god sent a sea bull that frightened Hippolytus's horses, and they dragged him to his death. Artemis later revealed the truth and the curse of Aphrodite to Theseus.

Other versions, some of them older according to certain authors, present Phaedra as solely responsible for her actions, with no divine excuse. In these accounts Hippolytus still dies because of his stepmother's false accusation.

Worship and religion

In Athens there was a shrine to Aphrodite « for Hippolytus » on the south slope of the Acropolis. Pausanias says that Phaedra and Hippolytus were buried close to each other, near a racetrack where he trained, and that a myrtle grew over her tomb. In Troezen a myrtle was shown with all its leaves pierced with holes, which according to tradition had been made by the pin in Phaedra's hair while she watched him.

Legacy

Her story has inspired Racine's Phèdre, Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz's work, Miguel de Unamuno's Fedra and Sarah Kane's Phaedra's Love, as well as works by Salvador Espriu and Raúl Hernández Garrido. It has been the subject of paintings, sculptures and films, and of the concert opera Phaedra (2007) by the German composer Hans Werner Henze. The asteroid Fedra also bears her name.

Ancient sources: Euripides wrote Hippolytus, Seneca wrote Phaedra, and Ovid included her in his Heroides; Homer and Virgil place her among the shades of the underworld.

Trivia

An asteroid named after her. The asteroid Fedra was named in her honour after the Cretan princess.
The pierced myrtle. In Troezen a myrtle was displayed with its leaves full of holes, which tradition attributed to a hairpin of Phaedra's.
A name meaning bright. Her name derives from a Greek word meaning bright.
