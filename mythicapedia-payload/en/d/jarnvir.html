Ironwood, forest of Norse mythology

Járnviðr

Járnviðr, the Iron Wood, is a forest east of Midgard in Norse mythology, inhabited by troll women and giant wolves. It matters because the offspring of Fenrir are raised there, including the one destined to swallow the moon.

The myth

According to the Völuspá, found in the Poetic Edda, an old woman sits in the Ironwood to the east, nurturing the offspring of Fenrir. One of them, in monstrous form, will snatch the moon. The stanza identifies him as Mánagarmr, also called Hati, and the old woman may be Angrboða, Fenrir's mother.

Snorri Sturluson expands the passage in the Gylfaginning of the Prose Edda. There the witch bears many giants as sons, all in wolf shape, and the wolves descend from this line. The women of the wood are called járnviðjur, and the moon-hound will be filled with the flesh of the dead before he swallows the moon.

Ancient sources: It appears in Völuspá (stanza 40) of the Poetic Edda, in Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning in the Prose Edda, and, in singular form, in Eyvindr Skáldaspillir's Háleygjatal.

Trivia

A troll wife. In the þulur, a list of poetic names, Járnviðja appears in the singular as a troll wife.
Skaði in the Háleygjatal. The skald Eyvindr Skáldaspillir uses the form járnviðja in his Háleygjatal when referring to the goddess Skaði.
