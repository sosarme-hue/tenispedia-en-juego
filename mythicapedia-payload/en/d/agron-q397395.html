son of Eumelus in Greek myth

Agron

Agron was a son of Eumelus in Greek mythology, a minor figure linked to the island of Kos. His story, shared with his sisters Byssa and Meropis, serves as a warning against impiety toward the gods.

The myth

The family lived at Meropis on Kos and worshipped Gaia, who rewarded them with all kinds of goods. Yet they were impious toward the other gods and never joined religious festivals. Agron was especially scornful of Athena, Artemis and Hermes, refusing every invitation to their rites and mocking them.

One night the three gods visited in disguise: Hermes as a shepherd, Athena and Artemis as country maidens. Hermes invited Eumelus and Agron to a ritual feast in his honor and suggested sending Byssa and Meropis to the sacred grove where other girls gathered. Meropis mocked Athena's name, and the goddess turned her into an owl. Byssa became a bird called byssa, sacred to Leucothea. Hermes changed Agron into a plover. Eumelus scolded Hermes and was turned into a night raven, a bird believed to foretell trouble.

Trivia

An owl for mockery. According to the myth, Meropis was turned into an owl for ridiculing the name of Athena, the goddess who punished her.
A raven that foretold trouble. Eumelus ended up as a night raven, a bird believed to announce trouble.
