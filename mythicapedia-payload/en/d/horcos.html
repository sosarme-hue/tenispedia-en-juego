Greek personification of oaths

Horkos

In Greek mythology, Horkos personifies the curse that falls on anyone who swears a false oath. Alongside Dike, Justice, he enforced oaths, a key part of the ancient Greek system of justice.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, he was a son of Eris, Strife, and the Erinyes attended his birth. Hyginus makes him a child of Aether and Earth, while Sophocles calls him a son of Zeus. His punishment could reach the offspring of the oath-breaker.

In an Aesop fable, a man who had denied holding a deposit asked Horkos how often he returned to punish oath-breakers. Hearing every thirty or forty years, he swore falsely the next day, only for Horkos to hurl him off a cliff, since he could come back the same day if provoked.

Worship and religion

At Olympia stood a statue of Zeus Horkios, an epithet of the god who punished perjury, holding a thunderbolt in each hand. Before it, athletes swore over a sacrificed boar that they would not cheat, and judges swore they had not been bribed. The Romans worshipped him as Jusjurandum.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony), Aesop, Herodotus, Sophocles (Oedipus at Colonus) and Hyginus.
