nymphs of the Amnisos river in Crete

Amnisiades

The Amnisiades are nymphs of the Amnisos river in Crete, whom ancient texts make companions of Artemis. They matter because they link the goddess to a cave on the same river dedicated to Eileithyia.

The myth

In Callimachus's Hymn to Artemis, the goddess asks her father Zeus for twenty of the Amnisiades to join her retinue, alongside sixty Oceanids. She then travels to Crete to collect them, and later in the hymn they are shown tending the goddess's deer.

Apollonius of Rhodes, following Callimachus, says that some of her companions come from the very source of the Amnisus.

Worship and religion

Along the river stands a cave dedicated to Eileithyia, goddess of childbirth, who was already venerated there in the Mycenaean era. According to Ivana Petrovic, Artemis and Eileithyia may have been assimilated at this site, or Eileithyia's worship was eventually superseded by Artemis's. For Jennifer Larson, this identification is likely why the goddess is connected with these nymphs.

Ancient sources: Callimachus's Hymn to Artemis, Apollonius of Rhodes's Argonautica, and Stephanus of Byzantium, who calls them Naiads.

Trivia

Twenty nymphs. In Callimachus's hymn, Artemis asks Zeus for twenty Amnisiades and sixty Oceanids for her retinue.
Naiads. Stephanus of Byzantium calls them Naiads, that is, nymphs of rivers and springs.
