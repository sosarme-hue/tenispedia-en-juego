forms of the Hindu elephant god

The Thirty-two Forms of Ganesha

The thirty-two forms of Ganesha are variants of the elephant god of Hinduism, each with its own iconography and attributes. They matter because they give devotees specific images on which to meditate.

The myth

Devotional texts dedicated to Ganesha present these forms as aspects of a single deity. The Mudgala Purana, a text centred on Ganesha, is the first to list them.

According to tradition, detailed descriptions appear in the Shivanidhi section of the nineteenth-century Kannada Sritattvanidhi, where each form is paired with a short Sanskrit meditation verse written in Kannada script. The scholar Martin-Dubost observes that this recent South Indian text does not describe the two-armed forms of the fourth century, nor the fourteen- and twenty-armed forms of central India in the ninth and tenth centuries.

Worship and religion

The forms serve as objects of meditation and devotion. Sculptural representations survive in the temples of Nanjangud and Chāmarājanagar, in Mysore district, Karnataka.

Ancient sources: The Mudgala Purana is the first text to list them.
