apsara, celestial nymph of Hindu mythology

Menaka

Menaka is one of the apsaras, celestial nymphs of Hindu mythology, famous for her beauty and for her role in the seduction of the sage Vishvamitra. Her story, and that of her daughter Shakuntala, gave rise to some of the best-known tales of ancient India.

The myth

According to the Shad-vimsa-brahmana, Menaka was the daughter of Vrishanashva, also called Mena. Indra, fearing the power that the sage Vishvamitra had built up over ten thousand years of austerities, sent her down to earth to seduce him and break his meditation. She succeeded, and the sage, forgetting his goal, lived with her for many years.

When her daughter Shakuntala was born, Menaka left the infant on the banks of the river Malini in the Himalayas and returned to heaven. Vultures protected the girl until the sage Kanva found her and raised her as his own. Years later, Shakuntala married King Dushyanta and bore Bharata, the mythical emperor after whom India takes its name, Bharat. In another Mahabharata episode, Menaka courts Vishvavasu, king of the gandharvas, and has a daughter, Pramadvara, whom she also abandons.

According to the kavya literature, Menaka married Jimavat, king of the Himalayas, and had several mountain children. Among them were Mainaka, the only mountain who kept his wings when Indra clipped those of all the mountains, and Parvati, who would become the wife of Shiva.

Legacy

The poet Kalidasa, active in the 4th century CE, made Menaka a character in his drama Abhijnanashakuntalam, where he highlights her maternal side, largely absent from earlier versions. Her figure also appears in later Indian literature as the model of the celestial seductress.

Ancient sources: Rig Veda, Yajur Veda (Vajasaneyi Samhita), Ramayana, Mahabharata, Harivamsha and Puranas such as the Skanda Purana and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

An incomparable name. One interpretation of her name splits it into parts meaning 'not to be measured' or 'incomparable', alluding to her exceptional beauty.
The mountain that kept its wings. According to the myth, Mainaka is the only mountain that did not lose its wings when Indra cut off those of all the mountains.
