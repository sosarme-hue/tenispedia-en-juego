Mesopotamian god of wisdom and fresh waters

Enki (Ea)

Enki, also known as Ea, was the Mesopotamian god of wisdom, crafts, fresh subterranean waters and magic. His cult began in Sumer, with the city of Eridu as its main center.

The myth

In Mesopotamian stories, Enki appears above all as a crafty counsellor who finds solutions to difficult problems. Tradition credits him with creating humankind from clay.

In Enki and Ninmah he devised the plan to fashion a being who would take over the labour of the gods. In Atra-hasis he works with Belet-ili to make humans from clay and divine blood, and his advice lets humanity survive Enlil's attempts to wipe it out, which culminate in the flood. In the later Babylonian Epic of Creation, Enuma elish, the making of man is instead a collaboration between Ea and his son Marduk.

Worship and religion

His main center was Eridu, regarded as his home, where his temple the E-Abzû stood. His cult was especially influential in the Ur III and Old Babylonian periods, when he formed part of a triad with Anu and Enlil, and it spread to Anatolia and to Assyrian cities such as Assur, Nineveh and Kalhu. His symbols included the goat-fish, the ram-headed staff and the turtle.

Ancient sources: Enuma elish, Atra-hasis, Enki and Ninmah and the Kumarbi cycle, Mesopotamian and Hurrian texts that present him as counsellor and creator.

Trivia

A god with a goat-fish form. Among his primary symbols was the goat-fish, a hybrid figure that identified him in Mesopotamian art and religion.
A name with several readings. Edmond Sollberger proposed translating Enki as Lord Benevolence, an idea that is not generally accepted by specialists.
