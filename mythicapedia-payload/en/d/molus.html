Cretan hero of Greek mythology

Molus

Molus is a figure of Greek mythology, the illegitimate son of Deucalion and father of Meriones, who fought beside Idomeneus in the Trojan War. He appears only briefly in the stories, yet he links the royal house of Crete with the expedition against Ilium.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Molus was a bastard son of Deucalion, brother of Idomeneus. Hyginus names him as the father of Meriones, born to Melphis, who sailed to Troy with forty ships. Diodorus Siculus makes him a son of Minos and says Meriones joined Agamemnon with ninety ships.

According to Diodorus, both returned home and died with great honours. At Cnossus their tomb was pointed out, bearing an inscription in which Meriones calls himself the son of Molus. Another account tells that Molus tried to violate a nymph and was later found beheaded.

Worship and religion

At a festival in Crete, an image of a headless man called Molus was displayed, linked to his fate.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus and Diodorus Siculus, who record his genealogies and his tomb at Cnossus.

Trivia

Tomb with epitaph. Diodorus Siculus quotes an inscription that the Cretans said stood on Meriones' tomb at Cnossus, beside that of Idomeneus.
