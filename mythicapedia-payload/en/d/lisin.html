Mesopotamian deity, first a goddess and later a god of fire

Lisin

Lisin is a deity of ancient Mesopotamia, first regarded as a goddess and addressed as ama, "mother", and later as a god with links to fire. Her name was also given to a star associated with Nabu, presumably Antares.

The myth

According to the texts, Lisin was married to Ninsikila, whose gender also changed over time, and the couple was believed to have eight children. Her mother was Ninhursag and her father Šulpae; she also had a brother, Ashgi.

The only literary text focused on her is a lament in which she mourns the death of one of her sons and blames her mother Ninhursag for it. In some compositions she appears as a male god, and in esoteric glosses he is described as "he who burns with fire".

Worship and religion

The original cult centre is uncertain, with Abu Salabikh, Adab and Kesh often proposed. She is attested in texts from Umma, Lagash, Nippur and Meturan. She seems to have held a high position in the early pantheon, but references to her worship after the third millennium BCE are uncommon, suggesting she lost importance in the Old Babylonian period or earlier.

Legacy

In first-millennium BCE Mesopotamian astronomy, her name designated the star now called Antares, also known as "the breast of the scorpion". According to the MUL.APIN compendium, praying to this star could bring good luck.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Early Dynastic Zame Hymns, in Udug Hul incantations and in the god list An = Anum.

Trivia

A star named after her. In Mesopotamia, Lisin's name was used for the star now identified with Antares, in the constellation of Scorpius.
A change of gender. Although she began as a goddess, in later periods she was regarded as a god, and her spouse Ninsikila's gender was also switched.
