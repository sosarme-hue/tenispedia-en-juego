son of Priam, seer and seabird of Greek myth

Aesacus

Aesacus is a son of King Priam of Troy in Greek mythology, gifted with the power to interpret dreams. His story of lost love ends with his transformation into a seabird, which makes him one of the more unusual figures of the Trojan royal family.

The myth

According to the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus, Aesacus was the son of Priam's first wife, Arisbe, daughter of Merops. His grandfather had taught him to read dreams, so it was Aesacus who interpreted the dream Hecabe had before Paris was born, in which she gave birth to a burning brand that set Troy alight. He warned that the child would bring ruin on the city and had to be killed at birth.

Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, presents him differently: an illegitimate son of Priam, secretly born to the nymph Alexirhoe, daughter of the river Granicus. Aesacus avoided Ilium and preferred the countryside, far from the court and his father's world. There he caught sight of the nymph Hesperia, daughter of the river Cebren, fell in love and pursued her. As she fled, a venomous snake bit her and she died.

Desperate, Aesacus leapt from a tall cliff into the sea, but Tethys changed him into a diving bird. In Ovid he kept trying to dive into the depths. In Apollodorus, by contrast, the dead girl is his wife, Asterope, not a would-be lover. The exact identity of the bird, called mergus, remains uncertain: it has been linked to a cormorant or to Scopoli's shearwater.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus in the Bibliotheca, Tzetzes and Ovid in the Metamorphoses.

Trivia

A bird without a certain name. The Latin term mergus, later used as the name of a genus of diving ducks, does not reveal with certainty which bird was meant. A cormorant or Scopoli's shearwater have both been proposed.
Wife or lover?. Apollodorus presents the dead girl as Aesacus's wife, named Asterope. Ovid describes her as a nymph he pursued without ever having married her.
