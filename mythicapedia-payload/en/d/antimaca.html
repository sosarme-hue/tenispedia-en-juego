queen of Tiryns, wife of Eurystheus

Antimache

Antimache is the queen of Tiryns, wife of King Eurystheus, the ruler who set Heracles his Twelve Labors. She belongs to the Greek tradition and is best known for the tragic fate of her children.

The myth

According to the tradition, Antimache was of Arcadian descent as the daughter of Amphidamas and the sister of Melanion, husband of the heroine Atalanta. With Eurystheus she had several children: Admete, Alexander, Iphimedon, Eurybius, Mentor, Perimedes and possibly Eurypylus. When Athens refused to hand the Heracleidae over to Eurystheus, a war broke out in which the Athenians killed all of her sons.

Another version tells that, during a sacrificial meal held after Heracles completed his Twelve Labors, the hero himself slew Eurybius, Perimedes and Eurypylus, because they had served him a smaller portion of meat than they had kept for themselves.

Trivia

A fatal banquet. One version holds that Heracles killed three of Antimache's sons during a sacrifice, over a dispute about the size of the meat portion served to him.
Disputed children. The sources do not agree on the list of children: Eurypylus is only possibly her son, and not all accounts include him.
