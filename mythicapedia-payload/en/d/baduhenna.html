Germanic goddess of war of the Frisii

Baduhenna

Baduhenna was a goddess of war in the ancient religion of the Frisii, a Germanic people of the northern Low Countries. She is known almost only from a passage in Tacitus's Annals, which is the sole ancient source that mentions her.

The myth

The sources tell us very little about her. According to Tacitus, the Frisii kept a sacred grove dedicated to Baduhenna, and near it, in 28 AD, 900 Roman soldiers were killed in her honour, in a battle known as the Battle of the Baduhenna Grove.

Her name is usually read as derived from badwa-, 'battle', which suits her role as a warrior goddess. The second part of the name may be linked to the Germanic matronae, figures honoured on votive stones and altars. Rudolf Simek notes that sacred groves were a common feature of Germanic peoples.

Worship and religion

Her worship centred on a sacred grove dedicated to her, and the sacrifice of Roman prisoners that Tacitus describes was made in her honour. No temples or later rituals are recorded, and there is no evidence about whether her cult survived.

Ancient sources: Tacitus's Annals (book 4, chapters 73 and 74), the only ancient source that mentions her.

Trivia

Sling bullets. Roman sling bullets have been found in Kennemerland in the Netherlands, the same type as those found near the Roman fortified harbour at Velsen, which suggests where the battle may have taken place.
A name with a possible sacred meaning. One theory places the battle at Heiloo. Its name may contain hei-, perhaps meaning 'holy', and -loo, meaning 'grove' or 'wood'.
