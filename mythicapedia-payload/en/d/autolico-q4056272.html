Thessalian prince, hero and founder of Sinope

Autolycus

Autolycus is a hero of Greek mythology, the son of the Thessalian king Deimachus and brother of Deileon and Phlogius. He is remembered mainly as the founder of the city of Sinope, where he was worshipped as a god.

The myth

According to tradition, Autolycus was a prince of Thessaly, son of King Deimachus and brother of Deileon and Phlogius, and in some accounts also of Phronius. Together with his brothers he joined Heracles in his expedition against the Amazons. After going astray, the three brothers settled at Sinope and remained there until they joined the voyage of the Argonauts.

He was later regarded as the founder of Sinope. His name, in Greek, means something like "the wolf itself", a detail the stories do not fully explain.

Worship and religion

At Sinope he was worshipped as a god and had an oracle in his honour. According to the sources, after the Roman conquest of the city, Lucullus carried his statue to Rome.

Ancient sources: Hyginus confused Autolycus and his brothers with the sons of Phrixus and Chalciope, Argus, Melas, Phrontides and Cylindrus, who were also rescued by the Argonauts on the island of Dia.

Trivia

Statue taken to Rome. After the Roman conquest of Sinope, Lucullus carried Autolycus's statue to Rome.
Confusion with other heroes. Hyginus confused Autolycus's brothers with the sons of Phrixus and Chalciope, who were also rescued by the Argonauts.
