Aztec goddess of beauty, flowers and love

Xochiquetzal

Xochiquetzal is a goddess of Aztec (Mexica) mythology associated with beauty, flowers, love and the arts. Her name means "precious flower", and she was seen as a protector of weavers, artisans and pregnant women.

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Xochiquetzal was born from the hair of the mother goddess. Her first husband was Tlaloc, although in another version Tezcatlipoca kidnapped her and forced her to marry him. In some creation myths she is the wife of Piltzintecuhtli, son of the first human couple, Cipactonal and Oxomoco. With him she had Centeotl, the god of maize, and, in some accounts, Nanahuatzin, who threw himself into the divine fire to become the Fifth Sun, and Xochipilli, her twin brother and god of flowers and love.

She was said to live in Tamoanchan, a paradise in the Tlalocan filled with fountains, rivers and flowering trees. Whoever picked one of her flowers would be happy and faithful in love. She was surrounded by other goddesses and by dwarves, hunchbacks and jesters who entertained her with music and dance. Her nature was dual, solar in her association with the butterfly and lunar, as the Codex Borgia shows, where she is sometimes equated with Tlazolteotl.

Worship and religion

People invoked her for beauty, fertility, safe childbirth, good harvests, skill in weaving and stable marriages. Offerings included feathers, daisies and small tiles bearing her image, and marigolds were sacred to her. At the Hueypachtli festival, held alongside Tlaloc, flowers were offered to her and a young woman was chosen to embody the goddess, though the details of the rite vary between sources.

Legacy

The anthropologist Hugo Nutini identifies her with the Virgin of Ocotlan in his study of patron saints in Tlaxcala, an example of syncretism between Mexica religion and colonial Catholicism.

Trivia

Festival every eight years. According to the sources, her festival was held every eight years, and worshippers wore animal and flower masks.
Unusual attendants. Her attendants were dwarves, hunchbacks and clowns, who also acted as messengers to the other gods.
