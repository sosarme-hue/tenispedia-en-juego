Japanese goddess of food and grain

Toyouke Ōmikami

Toyouke Ōmikami is a Shinto deity linked to food, grain, trade and industry in Japan. She is best known for her role at Ise Shrine, where she is honoured as the protector of the sacred meals offered to the sun goddess.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Toyouke was the daughter of Wakumusubi and therefore a granddaughter of Izanami, the creator of heaven and earth alongside her husband. Some accounts of the lineage are confusing, since Wakumusubi was born from Izanami's urine rather than from Izanagi. After the tenson kōrin, the goddess was enshrined at the outer shrine in Watarai.

Local traditions say that, during the reign of Emperor Yūryaku, Amaterasu appeared to him in a dream and asked for Toyouke to be brought from Manai Pond in Tanba Province, since she alone could not supply enough food. A lost fudoki tells of heavenly maidens bathing in that pond. An elderly couple hid the robe of one of them, and she lived for years in their home brewing a sake that cured every ailment, until she was driven out and settled in Nagu Village, where she is identified with Toyouke.

Worship and religion

She was once worshipped in Tanba Province. She was later moved to the Gekū complex within Ise Shrine, where she purifies the food offered to Amaterasu. She is also honoured at branch shrines of Ise and at Inari shrines. At Mineyama there is a well and an ancient rice paddy linked to the planting of the first rice. Her worship remains alive at Ise today.

Legacy

In the 14th century, Watarai Ieyuki placed her at the centre of Ise Shintō in his treatise Ruiju Jingi Hongen, where Gekū was considered superior to the shrine of Amaterasu. Her association with Inari and Ukanomitama links her to Japan's agricultural tradition.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, which names her as the daughter of Wakumusubi; she also appears in lost fudoki and the Engishiki, though the Nihon Shoki has no entry about her.

Trivia

Miraculous sake. According to an old local story, the woman who lived with the elderly couple brewed a sake said to cure every ailment.
Water against fire. Ise Shintō linked Toyouke with water and Amaterasu with fire, a pair of opposites that also included the sun and the moon.
