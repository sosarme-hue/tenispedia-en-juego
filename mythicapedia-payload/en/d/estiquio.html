Athenian warrior at Troy

Stichius

Stichius was an Athenian warrior who fought in the Trojan War, according to the tradition preserved in ancient texts. Though his role is modest, he illustrates how minor leaders in the Iliad gained prominence in battle alongside the great heroes.

The myth

During the siege of Troy, Stichius and Menestheus, another Athenian leader, carried the body of Amphimachus, commander of the Epeians, off the battlefield. Stichius later supported Menestheus against the attacks of Hector, but the Trojan hero struck him down together with Arcesilaus, a trusted comrade of Menestheus.

Homer describes Stichius as a leader of the Boeotians, wearing bronze coats of mail, which shows that the tradition did not always agree on his exact origin. His name appears in only a few lines, yet it is enough to place him among the defenders of the Achaean fleet. His death is presented as one more episode of the war, without further development in the surviving texts.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him as a leader killed by Hector.
