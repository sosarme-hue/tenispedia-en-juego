son of Odin in a late list of Norse mythology

Hildólfr

Hildólfr is a name that appears in Norse mythology as one of the supposed sons of Odin. His role in the stories is very minor, and his link to the god rests on a late source.

The myth

Hildólfr appears in the list of Odin's sons in the Nafnaþulur, a section of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda that is not found in all manuscripts and is probably a later addition to the original work. There he is named alongside Thor, Baldr, Váli, Hermóðr and Heimdallr, among others.

In the Eddic poem Hárbarðsljóð the name comes up only indirectly: the ferryman Hárbarðr addresses his supposed master with it. Since Hárbarðr is actually Odin in disguise, the name does not prove that the figure is a son of the god. The family tie therefore rests almost entirely on that late list.

Ancient sources: The Nafnaþulur of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and the Eddic poem Hárbarðsljóð.

Trivia

A ferryman in disguise. In Hárbarðsljóð, the name is applied to a supposed master who turns out to be Odin disguised as a ferryman.
A list that does not quite fit. The list where he appears omits Týr, Höðr and Bragi, whom Snorri names as Odin's sons elsewhere.
