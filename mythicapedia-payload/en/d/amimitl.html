Mexica god of fishing and lakes

Amimitl

Amimitl was a Mexica god of lakes and fishermen, believed to calm storms. According to Torquemada's account, he was especially worshipped on Cuitlahuac, an island in Lake Chalco.

The myth

According to the accounts gathered by colonial chroniclers, Amimitl protected those who lived by fishing. When the lake was stirred by storms, he was called upon to calm the waters so that fishermen could work safely.

The sources also describe a stern side to him: he showed his displeasure toward his subjects through dropsy or other watery illnesses. In some contexts, Clavigero identifies him with Opochtli, god of craftsmen and net fishermen, though that equivalence appears only occasionally.

Worship and religion

Amimitl was especially venerated on Cuitlahuac, an island in Lake Chalco, according to Torquemada. He was also associated with a hymn of Chichimeca origin, preserved with a Nahuatl gloss, which seems to have been sung for dances. Its verses are cryptic and were never fully translated.

Ancient sources: The references come from colonial chroniclers such as Torquemada and Clavigero, who recorded Mexica worship; the hymn survives with its Nahuatl gloss.

Trivia

A hymn for dancing. A hymn associated with Amimitl survives with a Nahuatl gloss. Its message is cryptic, but it is understood as a song for dances.
