queen of Calydon, mother of Meleager

Althaea

Althaea is the queen of Calydon and the mother of the hero Meleager in Greek mythology. Her name is tied to one of the most famous tragedies of the epic tradition: a burning log that decided her son's life and the revenge that led her to destroy him.

The myth

Althaea was the daughter of Thestius, king of Pleuron, and of Eurythemis, and sister of Leda. Married to Oeneus, king of Calydon, she bore several sons and daughters, among them Meleager, the most admired hero of the family. Some accounts make him the child of a night she spent with the god Ares, which would give Meleager a father other than the king. When the infant was born, the three Moirai appeared before Althaea and announced his fate. Clotho said he would be noble and Lachesis that he would be brave, but Atropos pointed to a burning log in the hearth and declared that the boy would live as long as that brand remained unconsumed. Althaea pulled it from the fire and hid it away.

Years later, Oeneus forgot to make an offering to Artemis, who punished Calydon with a giant boar. Meleager organized the hunt and dealt the killing blow, but he gave the pelt to the huntress Atalanta, whom he loved. Althaea's brothers, Meleager's maternal uncles, were outraged and seized the prize, and in the scuffle Meleager killed them. In Homer's Iliad, the quarrel leads to a war between the Curetes and the Calydonians, and Althaea, consumed by grief, prays to Hades and Persephone for vengeance, while Meleager falls in battle, perhaps through the work of the Erinyes.

When Althaea learned of her brothers' deaths, consumed by grief and anger, she took the log from its hiding place and threw it into the fire, and Meleager died. Other versions deny that any brand existed and say that the mother's curse, granted by the gods, caused the son's death. Afterwards Althaea took her own life, hanging herself or stabbing herself depending on the author, and with her the house of Oeneus came to an end. Her sisters, the Meleagrides, mourned without rest at the tomb until Artemis turned them into birds and carried them to the island of Leros.

Ancient sources: Hesiod called her 'of gloomy gaze', Aeschylus remembered her as 'the wretched daughter of Thestius', and Homer's Iliad recounts her grief and the war with the Curetes.

Trivia

A log that decided a life. According to the Latin poets, the brand appeared in the palace rooms at the very moment Meleager was born, a detail absent from the older Greek accounts.
Birds instead of sisters. After Meleager's death, his sisters were transformed into birds and carried to the island of Leros, where they became known as the Meleagrides.
