wise giant of Finnish mythology

Antero Vipunen

Antero Vipunen is a giant of Finnish mythology, known as the guardian of spells, who lies buried underground with ancient magical knowledge. He matters because he is the central figure of one of the best-known episodes in the runic songs and the Kalevala.

The myth

According to tradition, Vipunen sleeps beneath the earth and guards very valuable spells. Väinämöinen needs three magic words (luotes) to finish building his boat, and his brother Joukahainen tells him that the sage has been dead and buried for a long time.

Väinämöinen therefore travels to the underworld, as a fellow shaman could, and falls into the giant's mouth. Inside him he builds a forge and starts hammering, causing such pain that Vipunen spits him out and hands over the words he came for.

Legacy

In 1938 the Finnish composer Uuno Klami wrote the symphonic poem Vipusessa käynti (In the Belly of Vipunen), which depicts this episode musically. His name also gave its title to a book of games, puzzles and varied knowledge edited by Yrjö Karilas in 1950.

Ancient sources: The Finnish runic songs, passed down orally and later collected in the Kalevala.

Trivia

A possible human origin. Some researchers link Vipunen to Ikämieli, a Sámi shaman of the 16th or 17th century who appears in Finnish folktales. This is a hypothesis, not an established fact.
A different protagonist. In some versions of the runic songs the protagonist is not Väinämöinen but Lemminkäinen. According to Matti Kuusi, he was likely the original hero.
