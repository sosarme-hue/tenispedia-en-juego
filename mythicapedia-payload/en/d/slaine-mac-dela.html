legendary first High King of Ireland

Sláine mac Dela

Sláine mac Dela is, according to Irish tradition, the first High King of Ireland, a Fir Bolg leader said to have cleared the forest around Brú na Bóinne. He matters because he opens the legendary history of the island.

The myth

According to the story, the Fir Bolg arrived in Ireland with five thousand men and came ashore, so the account goes, at Wexford Harbour, where the River Slaney meets the sea. Sláine and his four brothers, descended from one of the sons of Nemed, divided Ireland among themselves. Sláine, the youngest of the five, took Leinster, while the others took Gann, Sengann, Genann and Rudraige. His companions then chose him as ruler over them all. His wife was called Fuad. The people of his portion of the Fir Bolg, known as the Gailióin or Gaileanga, were named after their spears, in Old Irish gáe.

The stories say he died at Dind Ríg in County Carlow and was buried at Slane in County Meath. He was succeeded by his brother Rudraige mac Dela. Modern estimates based on archaeological evidence from the transition between the Neolithic and the Irish Bronze Age suggest his reign may have taken place around 1238 to 1237 BCE, though this is only an estimate.

Trivia

Arrival at Wexford. Tradition places his landing at Wexford Harbour, at the mouth of the River Slaney in the southeast of the island.
A people named after spears. The Gailióin, or Gaileanga, took their name from their spears, in Old Irish gáe.
