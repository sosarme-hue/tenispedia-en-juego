folk deity of Rajasthan, India

Pabuji

Pabuji is a folk deity of Rajasthan in northwestern India, worshipped mainly in the historical region of Rajputana. He is regarded as a hero who became a god, and his deeds have been sung for centuries.

The myth

According to tradition, Pabuji was the son of the Rajput Dhandal, of the village of Kolu, but he had been born of a celestial nymph who promised to return to him as a mare when he turned twelve.

His first exploit was to fight the Khinchi clan, whose chief, Jindrao, coveted the black mare Kesar Kalami, which was in fact Pabuji's own mother. He then protected the goddess Deval, who had given him the mare, and at Pushkar he was saved from drowning by the snake-god Gogaji, to whom he promised his niece Kelam in marriage.

To fulfil his promise of bringing her camels, he raided Lanka, defeated Ravana and seized the animals. As he prepared to marry the princess Phulvanti, Deval warned him of stolen cattle; Pabuji set off after the Khinchis, and the loyal Dhebo, after offering his entrails to vultures, rode on to catch them.

Worship and religion

He is venerated in Rajasthan and the historical region of Rajputana. According to tradition, the bhopa, a community of poet-singers, perform his narrative in song, based on the text Pabuprakasa.

Ancient sources: The narrative appears in the seventeenth-century Nainsi ri Khyat under the title Vata Pabujiri, and the Pabuprakasa is the text on which the bhopa base their performances.

Trivia

Camels from Lanka. Pabuji promised Kelam camels seized from the demon king of Lanka, although camels were unknown in Rajasthan and Lanka was believed impregnable.
The calf in the opium box. When Deval demanded her favourite bull-calf back, it was found hidden in Dhebo's opium box, where he had put it as a joke.
