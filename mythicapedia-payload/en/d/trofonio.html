oracular hero of Lebadea, in Boeotia

Trophonius

Trophonius is a figure of Greek myth whom some sources treat as a hero and others as a minor deity or daimon. His oracle at Lebadea, in Boeotia, was one of the most famous in ancient Greece.

The myth

In the Homeric Hymn to Apollo, Trophonius and his brother Agamedes are sons of Erginus, though Pausanias names Apollo as their father. The two built Apollo's temple at Delphi. When it was finished, the oracle granted them six days of anything they wished; on the seventh they were found dead. The saying that the gods love those who die young is said to come from this tale.

According to Pausanias, the brothers stole King Hyrieus's treasure through a secret entrance. Agamedes was caught in a trap, and Trophonius cut off his head so he would not be recognised. He then fled to a cave at Lebadea, where the earth swallowed him and he became a god beneath the ground. Much later, a plague led the Lebadeans to his hidden tomb, which a shepherd found by following a trail of bees to a hole in the earth.

Worship and religion

Anyone who wished to consult the oracle spent several days in a special house, bathed in the river Hercyna and ate only sacrificial meat. By day he made offerings to several gods; at night he drank from the rivers Lethe and Mnemosyne and went down into the cave. Most visitors came back terrified and remembered nothing. Seated on the chair of Mnemosyne, the consultant described what he had seen, and the priests shaped his words into an oracle. The sources do not say whether the cult survived antiquity.

Legacy

"To descend into the cave of Trophonius" became a proverb for a great fright, alluded to by Aristophanes in The Clouds. The rivers Lethe and Mnemosyne have been compared with the Myth of Er in Plato's Republic and with passages on memory in Hesiod's Theogony. Kierkegaard and Nietzsche both refer to him in their writings.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Apollo, Pindar, Cicero, Pausanias, Aristophanes (The Clouds) and Plutarch (De Genio Socratis).

Trivia

A secret club's cave. The Hellfire Club built a "Cave of Trophonius" with obscene wall paintings in which to hold its revels.
A Danish philosopher's cave. Søren Kierkegaard refers to "the cave of Trophonius" in Either/Or when discussing his childhood and later philosophical insights.
