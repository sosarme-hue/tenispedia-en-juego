creature of Japanese folklore, the Iron Rat

Tesso

Tesso, the Iron Rat, is a Japanese yōkai born from the legend of the monk Raigo of Miidera temple, who after his death becomes a giant rat with a stone body and iron teeth. His story shows how the rivalry between two Buddhist temples fed Japanese folklore.

The myth

According to the Heike Monogatari, Emperor Shirakawa promised Raigo great rewards if his prayers brought the imperial family an heir. Prince Taruhito was born, but when Raigo asked to build an ordination platform at Miidera, the powerful rival temple Enryaku-ji blocked it and the emperor broke his word.

Raigo starved himself to death, cursing the emperor, and the young prince died soon after. In later versions, the monk is reborn as a monstrous rat that ravaged Enryaku-ji, devouring scriptures and Buddha statues, until it was appeased with a shrine.

Worship and religion

To pacify him, the Nezumi no Hokura shrine was built at Hiyoshi Taisha in Ōtsu, and at Miidera the Nezumi no Miya, which is still associated with his spirit.

Legacy

Toriyama Sekien gave him the name tesso in his Gazu Hyakki Yagyō, and the novelist Kyogoku Natsuhiko revived him in the mystery novel Tesso no Ori.

Ancient sources: The Heike Monogatari, in versions such as the Engyo Hon, and the Taiheiki, which describe his attack on Enryaku-ji.

Trivia

A rat of the twelve signs. According to the Keizai Zasshisha, written in the Azuchi-Momoyama period, the Nezumi no Hokura shrine at Hiyoshi Taisha actually honors the Rat of the Twelve Earthly Branches.
A name given by an illustrator. The name tesso was coined by Toriyama Sekien in his illustrated yōkai collection, though the character was much older and was known as Raigo Nezumi.
