daughter of Aeolus in Greek mythology

Perimede

Perimede is one of the daughters of Aeolus and Enarete, a member of the Aeolid line in Greek myth. Her union with the river god Achelous places her among the female figures who gave rise to heroic lineages.

The myth

According to the tradition, Perimede, whose name means 'very cunning', joined with Achelous, the deep-flowing river, and from that union were born Hippodamas and Orestes. The Library places her among her many brothers, including Creteus, Sisyphus, Athamas and Salmoneus, and among her sisters Calyce, Canace and Alcyone.

Hesiod, in the Catalogue of Women, presents her as the last of the Aeolids to be born, yet the first named for her descendants. Like her sisters, she was destined for a famous husband, a 'king rich in sheep' whose name has not survived.

Ovid follows the Hesiodic version but reverses the roles: he makes Hippodamas the father of Perimede, whom he calls Perimela.

Ancient sources: The Library attributed to Apollodorus, Hesiod's Catalogue of Women and the verses of Ovid.

Trivia

A reversal of roles. Ovid reverses the Hesiodic genealogy and makes Hippodamas the father of Perimede, whom he calls Perimela.
A name with meaning. Perimede means 'very cunning' in Greek, a name the sources explicitly highlight.
