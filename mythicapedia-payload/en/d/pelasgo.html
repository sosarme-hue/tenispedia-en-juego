hero and ancestor of the Arcadians in Greek myth

Pelasgus

Pelasgus is a hero of Greek mythology, regarded as the eponymous ancestor of the Pelasgians of the Peloponnese and the first king of Arcadia. He matters because the Arcadians saw themselves as his direct descendants.

The myth

According to the Arcadian tradition recorded by Pausanias, Pelasgus was the first settler of Arcadia, and during his reign the region was called Pelasgia. With no one else there, he was chosen as king for his outstanding physical and intellectual gifts.

Other sources offer different origins. Pseudo-Apollodorus records two versions: according to Acusilaus he was a son of Zeus and Niobe, while according to Hesiod he was autochthonous, sprung from the earth itself.

As king, he taught his people how to shelter from the cold, build huts, weave wool and eat fruit, especially acorns. Some say Lycaon was his son by Meliboea, and Temenus, who raised Hera, was born to Deianira.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, who also quotes verses by the poet Asius of Samos; Pseudo-Apollodorus; and Hesiod.

Trivia

Food of the Arcadians. Pelasgus taught people to eat acorns from oak trees, the food by which the Arcadians were known.
Older than the Moon. Some said that Pelasgus, one of the first men born from the earth, was older than the Moon.
