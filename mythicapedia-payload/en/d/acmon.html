primordial deity in Greek mythology, father of Uranus

Acmon

Acmon is one of the primordial deities of Greek mythology, linked to the sky and its highest point. In some traditions he is the father of Uranus, though other accounts give different parents and at times identify him with Uranus himself.

The myth

According to tradition, Acmon is one of the beings that emerged at the beginning of the world. His link to Uranus, the personified sky, is the best-known part of his story: the poet Alcman, in the 7th century BC, gives the earliest attestation of Acmon as Uranus's father, with no mother named.

The versions differ. A passage attributed to Hesiod makes him the son of Gaia, while in the Titanomachy Uranus's father is Aether, and some scholars think the two may be the same figure. Some ancient authors also identify him with Uranus himself or connect him with Charon, the ferryman of the underworld, though this genealogy is uncertain.

Legacy

His name comes from Proto-Indo-European and is related to Sanskrit, Avestan and Lithuanian words. Ancient writers explained his role as Uranus's father through the tireless motion of the heavens, since akámatos means 'unwearying'.

Ancient sources: Alcman, Antimachus, Callimachus and Simmias of Rhodes mention him; the Titanomachy names him in its genealogy, and a work attributed to Hesiod is associated with his name in some passages.
