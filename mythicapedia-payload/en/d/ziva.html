Slavic goddess of life and fertility

Živa

Živa is a mother goddess linked to one of the tribes of the Polabian Slavs, part of the Obodrite confederation. Her name means 'alive', and she is known mainly from the Chronicle of Helmold of Bosau, the only ancient source that mentions her directly.

The myth

The surviving texts say very little about Živa's myths. The most solid information comes from Helmold, who names her while describing how the pagan cult grew stronger during the reign of Niklot. Her name comes from the Slavic root *živa, a feminine adjective meaning 'alive' or 'living'. Scholars think it may have begun as a personal name, a wish for long life, and later became an epithet of a goddess whose original traits are lost.

Broader hypotheses link her with life, love and fertility, with Żywie, a Polish deity mentioned by Jan Długosz, and with the Norse goddess Sif. These remain unconfirmed. She should not be confused with the Hindu god Shiva.

Worship and religion

Helmold notes that paganism grew stronger under Niklot, whose main stronghold was Ratzeburg. In one copy of the chronicle, Živa appears as the 'Goddess of Ratzeburg'. The sources give no details about temples, rites or festivals, and there is no evidence of a living cult today.

Legacy

Her name survives as a personal name in several Slavic languages, including Serbo-Croatian and Bulgarian, and as an old Polish surname. The scholars Vyacheslav Ivanov and Vladimir Toporov considered her an epithet of the goddess Mokosh.

Ancient sources: The Chronicle of the Slavs by Helmold of Bosau, the only ancient text that mentions her directly.

Trivia

Different spellings. Copies of Helmold's chronicle spell her name in different ways. Some manuscripts keep the letter w, which helps scholars reconstruct the original form.
From personal name to goddess. Živa was a personal name before it became a divine epithet. Names attested in several Slavic languages suggest this origin.
