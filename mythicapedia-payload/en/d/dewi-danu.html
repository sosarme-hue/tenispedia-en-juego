Balinese goddess of freshwater

Dewi Danu

Dewi Danu is the goddess of the lakes, rivers and irrigation waters of the Balinese Hindus, and one of the supreme deities of their tradition. Linked to fertility and the irrigation system, she is central to many of the island's local rites.

The myth

According to tradition, the god Dewa Pasupati moved the peak of Mahameru from India to Java, and as it swayed it split in two. Shiva then created a second mountain on Bali: the part that reached the island became Mount Batur, the goddess's home, while Tohlangkir became the abode of her brother Dewa Putranjaya.

Another version, from the folklore of Terunyan, tells how the fragrance of the taru menyan drew the goddess Uma down to earth. The god Surya forced himself upon her, and from their union were born the twins Dewi Danu and Dewi Putranjaya, who became the waters and rocks of the place. When they had grown, Uma returned to her heavenly throne.

Worship and religion

Dewi Danu is worshipped mainly at the temple Pura Ulun Danu Batur, beside Lake Batur, and in the village of Terunyan, where the triad of the goddess, her husband Pancering Jagat and their son Dalam Dasar is venerated. She is regarded as the protector of the traditional subak irrigation system, and her cult remains alive among the Balinese.

Legacy

Her image also appears in other temples on the island, such as Luhur Uluwatu.

Ancient sources: Inscriptions at the temple Pura Ulun Danu Batur recount the transfer of the sacred mountain and the creation of the Balinese peaks.
