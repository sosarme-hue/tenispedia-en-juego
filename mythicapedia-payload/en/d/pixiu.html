hybrid creature of Chinese mythology

Pixiu

The pixiu is a hybrid creature of Chinese mythology, with the head of a dragon, the body of a lion and feathered wings. It is regarded as a protector against evil spirits and a symbol of wealth and good fortune.

The myth

Accounts of its origin vary. In one of the most widespread versions, the pixiu broke a law of Heaven by defecating in the gardens of the Jade Emperor, who punished it with a spanking so severe that it sealed its rectum. Ever since, it devours gold, silver and jewels without being able to expel them, which is why it is linked to wealth.

Another story makes it the well-behaved, youngest son of the Dragon King, who accidentally broke a seal representing his father's power. Furious, the Dragon King turned it into an animal, punished it in the same way and decreed that it could eat only things representing wealth. It is said to patrol the heavens, that its fangs devour demons, and that it even carried its masters up to heaven after death.

Worship and religion

During the Han dynasty it was used as a symbol on battle flags, and Emperor Wu declared it the "Treasure of the Emperor," a possession reserved for the royal family. Stone statues of it guarded imperial tombs and decorated the roof corners of palaces and important halls. Today many people place pixiu figures in homes and offices to attract wealth according to feng shui, and it is still regarded as an auspicious creature for those going through a bad year.

Legacy

It appears in the film Mulan, in the design of Fa Mulan's sword, and in the video game Overwatch, where the character Zenyatta has a cosmetic costume inspired by it. It is also a common design on jade pendants.

Ancient sources: It is mentioned in the Shān hǎi Jīng (Classic of Mountains and Seas) and in chapter 96 of the Book of Han.
