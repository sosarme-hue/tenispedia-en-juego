land-spirit kami in Japanese Shinto

Three Pioneer Kami

The Three Pioneer Kami, known in Japanese as Kaitaku Sanshin, are a group of kami said to embody the spirit of the land itself, watching over it alongside human beings. The concept was used in the overseas territories of the Japanese Empire to deify local spirits and ancestors.

The myth

According to the idea described in the sources, these kami embody the spirit of the ground itself and share with people the task of caring for it. The relationship it proposes is one of mutual care between the land and those who inhabit it, rather than a narrative of deeds or genealogies.

Worship and religion

The concept was applied in the overseas territories of the Japanese Empire as an initial step toward settling them. By deifying local spirits and ancestors, newly occupied land received a religious framework that eased its colonization.
