queen of Thebes in Greek mythology

Jocasta

Jocasta is the queen of Thebes in Greek mythology, first the wife of Laius and then, unknowingly, of her own son Oedipus. Her story is at the heart of Sophocles' tragedy Oedipus Rex and of the other versions that surround it.

The myth

According to the tradition, Jocasta was the daughter of Menoeceus and sister of Creon. While still very young she married Laius, king of Thebes, and they had a son. An oracle warned Laius that this son would kill him and marry his mother, so he ordered the infant exposed on Mount Cithaeron, with his ankles pierced. A shepherd spared the child and took him to Corinth, where the royal couple raised him as their own and named him Oedipus, after his swollen feet.

When Oedipus learned the prophecy from the oracle at Delphi, he fled Corinth, believing he could avoid his fate. On a narrow road in Phocis he killed a man without knowing that it was Laius, his real father. In Thebes he solved the riddle of the Sphinx, which earned him the throne and the hand of Jocasta, whose kinship he did not know either. With her he had four children: Antigone, Ismene, Eteocles and Polynices.

The accounts differ about the ending. In Sophocles, a plague leads to the truth; Jocasta understands what has happened and hangs herself. In Euripides' Phoenician Women, she remains in Thebes after the discovery and takes her own life only after her sons Eteocles and Polynices kill each other in a struggle for the throne. In the Odyssey, Odysseus sees her in Hades. Some sources name Euryganeia as the mother of Oedipus' children instead of Jocasta.

Legacy

Her story gave rise to Sophocles' tragedy Oedipus Rex, one of the best-known works of Greek theatre. In the Middle Ages, Giovanni Boccaccio included her in De mulieribus claris, composed between 1361 and 1362, regarded as the first collection in Western literature devoted exclusively to biographies of women. Psychoanalysis uses the term Oedipus complex, linked to this same story.

Ancient sources: The Odyssey (Book XI), the tragedies of Sophocles and Euripides, Pherecydes, Hyginus, the Bibliotheca and Pausanias mention her, with differing versions.

Trivia

A visit in the Underworld. According to the Odyssey, Odysseus saw Jocasta during his journey to Hades, after her death.
The first collection of women. Boccaccio devoted a chapter to Jocasta in De mulieribus claris, considered the first collection of biographies of women in Western literature.
