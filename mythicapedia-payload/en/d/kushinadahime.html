rice goddess of Japanese mythology

Kushinadahime

Kushinadahime is a goddess (kami) of Shinto and Japanese mythology, wife of Susanoo and a central figure in the Gion cult, linked to Yasaka Shrine. Her name is usually read as "princess of the rice fields".

The myth

According to the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Susanoo, banished from Takamagahara, arrived in Izumo and met an elderly couple, Ashinazuchi and Tenazuchi, who had already lost seven of their eight daughters to the serpent Yamata no Orochi. Susanoo promised to kill the monster in exchange for marrying their last daughter, Kushinadahime.

After defeating the serpent, Susanoo built a palace at Suga and composed a poem that later tradition regarded as the root of waka poetry. Their child is named Yashimajinumi in the Kojiki, and Ōkuninushi in the main account of the Nihon Shoki. Some local traditions say the goddess hid in a forest or in the mountains of Hōki during the fight.

Worship and religion

She is venerated at many shrines across Japan, usually alongside Susanoo, such as Inada Shrine in Okuizumo, Yaegaki Shrine in Matsue and Hikawa Shrine in Ōmiya. Her worship continues today.

Legacy

Susanoo's poem is regarded as the origin of Japanese waka poetry, and several local shrines preserve her memory.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, which name her in various ways, along with the Izumo Fudoki.

Trivia

A birth-bath pond. Near Inada Shrine in Okuizumo lies a pond called Ubuyu-no-Ike, where she is said to have received her first bath after birth.
A name shared with a comb. The prefix "kushi" sounds like the word for "comb", which appears in her story in both the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki.
