Finnish mythological figure linked to Ukko, the thunder god

Rauni

Rauni is the name of a figure in Finnish mythology, associated with Ukko, the god of thunder. Its identity remains heavily debated, which makes it one of the most discussed cases in Finno-Ugric folklore.

The myth

The name first appears in 1551, in a text by Mikael Agricola, in lines that have proved hard to interpret. For a long time scholars thought Rauni was Ukko's wife, perhaps a thunder goddess or the earth mother Maaemä. Others saw a fertility god influenced by Scandinavian traditions and related to Freyr.

Martti Haavio proposed a different reading: Rauni was part of a god called Rauni-ukko, 'Old Man Rauni', with an unnamed wife who matched the Finnish equivalents of Freyr and Freyja. In this view, the couple's union made the plants grow. Unto Salo, by contrast, argued that Rauni was an epithet of Ukko himself rather than of his wife, and that the name may come from Latin and Greek words linked to lightning.

Legacy

The name survives mainly in lexicography. Elias Lönnrot recorded it in his dictionary as 'Rauni, Mistress of Pohjola', linking it to Louhi.

Ancient sources: Mikael Agricola mentions it in 1551 in a poem about pre-Christian Finnish beliefs, and runic songs record related names such as Röönikkä or Ryönikkä.

Trivia

A name with many readings. Scholars have proposed that Rauni is a goddess, an epithet of Ukko, a fertility god, or even a name coined by Agricola.
The rowan and the rainbow. In Sweden and Estonia, the word for rowan has also been used for the rainbow, a link some scholars have used to interpret Rauni.
