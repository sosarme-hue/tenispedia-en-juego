Eastern Pure Land of the Buddha Akshobhya in Mahayana Buddhism

Abhirati

Abhirati, whose Sanskrit name means "The Joyous", is the eastern Pure Land associated with Akshobhya, one of the Five Dhyani Buddhas of Mahayana Buddhism. Though it emerged in the earliest phase of Mahayana thought, it is far less known than Sukhavati, the paradise of Amitabha.

The myth

Abhirati is described in the Akshobhya Sutra (Akshobhyatathagatasyavyuha). It is a land where plants such as jasmine and palm trees sway in the breeze and produce mystical songs unlike any earthly music. Food and drink are abundant, there is no illness, and the women are beautiful. Whoever is reborn there attains the state of non-return.

According to the tradition, in Abhirati only the third and fourth noble truths are real, that is, the cessation of suffering and the Noble Eightfold Path leading to it. Akshobhya, in the account, was once a monk who made a sacred vow to the Buddha who then ruled this land; after long effort and keeping his vows, he became a Buddha and the sovereign of this paradise, and so he would never feel aversion, hatred or selfishness toward any being.

Ancient sources: The Akshobhya Sutra, first translated into Chinese by Lokakṣema by 186 CE according to tradition, is the text that describes this land.
