Trojan leader, son of Merops

Adrastus

Adrastus is a Trojan leader in the Iliad, son of the seer Merops and brother of Amphius. He is remembered mainly for the account of his death, which Homer tells in two different ways.

The myth

According to the Iliad, Adrastus and Amphius went to the Trojan War at the head of troops from Adrasteia, Apesus, Pityeia and the steep mountain of Tereia, although their father had warned them that death awaited them there.

In the thick of battle, Menelaus knocked Adrastus down and took him prisoner, hoping to win a good ransom. But Agamemnon reproached him for showing pity to a Trojan. Menelaus changed his mind, released the captive, and Agamemnon ran him through with his spear.

Later, however, the same poem credits Diomedes with killing Adrastus and his brother Amphius, so the episode has two different endings.

Legacy

The town of Adrasteia, on the Propontis, took its name from this hero.

Ancient sources: Homer, in the Iliad, lists him among Troy's allies and describes his death in two separate passages.

Trivia

Two deaths for one hero. The Iliad describes Adrastus's death in one passage at the hands of Agamemnon, and in another credits Diomedes with the fatal blow.
A town named after him. The town of Adrasteia, on the Propontis, owes its name to this Trojan leader.
