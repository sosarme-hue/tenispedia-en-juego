tree yōkai from Japanese folklore

Jubokko

The jubokko is a tree-shaped yōkai from Japanese folklore, associated with old battlefields. Its image has made it a familiar figure in books about yōkai, above all in the work of Shigeru Mizuki.

The myth

According to tradition, the jubokko grows where many people died in battle. It looks like an ordinary tree, but after absorbing the blood of the dead it becomes a yōkai that feeds on human blood. If someone walks past it, its branches turn into tubes that draw the blood out of the victim, who keeps a fresh, healthy look.

The story adds that cutting it makes it bleed, and that one of its branches can heal or purify an injured person. As for its origin, folklore scholars such as Kunio Yanagita and Iwao Hino found no earlier yōkai behind it. A group of experts concluded that it may be a creature invented by Mizuki.

Legacy

It appears in many books about yōkai and in Shigeru Mizuki's work, especially his manga GeGeGe no Kitaro.

Trivia

Mizuki's inventions. Mizuki said he created around 30 yōkai for his manga GeGeGe no Kitaro and other works, but he did not specify which ones.
Bleeding tree. According to the story, a cut jubokko bleeds, and one of its branches can help heal an injured person.
