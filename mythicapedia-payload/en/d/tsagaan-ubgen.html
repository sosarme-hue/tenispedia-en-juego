Mongolian god of longevity and fertility

Tsagaan Ubgen

Tsagaan Ubgen ("the White Elder") is the Mongolian guardian of life and longevity, a symbol of fertility and prosperity. His figure predates Buddhism and was later absorbed into the Buddhist pantheon of Mongolia and Buryatia.

The myth

Before Buddhism reached Mongolia and Buryatia, he was already a deity of longevity, wealth and fertility. To explain his continued veneration within Buddhism, tales of his conversion were added. In one, the Buddha and his disciples meet him, and his wisdom impresses the Buddha so much that he declares him a "saint". In another, he is one of two hunters who, together with Hara Ubgen, meet Milarepa in his cave and give up hunting to follow the Buddhist teaching.

Worship and religion

He is venerated in so-called white shamanism, a tradition shaped by Tibetan Buddhism. In the Tibetan cham dance, introduced in the 20th century on the orders of the 13th Dalai Lama, he is the central figure of the Tiger Dance: he enters weak and staggering, then, after symbolically killing a tiger, regains his strength and collects donations from the audience.

Trivia

The Tiger Dance. In some versions of the dance, the character starts drinking alcohol and keeps dancing until he is too drunk to continue.
A dream of the Dalai Lama. The 13th Dalai Lama had a dream during his exile in Mongolia, and the figure later entered the Tibetan cham dance because of it.
