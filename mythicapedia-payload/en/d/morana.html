Slavic goddess of death and winter

Morana

Morana is a female figure of Slavic mythology, associated with winter, darkness and death, and set in opposition to her sister Živa, goddess of life and summer. Her image survives mainly in folk custom across several Slavic countries, where she is still ceremonially sent off each spring.

The myth

Ancient sources on the Slavic gods are scarce, so the image of Morana is largely reconstructed from later traditions. Popular culture presents her as an old woman who embodies winter, set against Živa, who stands for summer, fertility, harvest and abundance.

According to the common reading, this pair expresses the winter-summer duality that gave rise to many rites among the Slavic peoples. Over time the goddess's figure became simplified and was reduced to the symbolic doll linked with winter and death in folklore.

The names Morana and Živa appear in the thirteenth-century Latin dictionary Mater Verborum, but the Czech linguist Antonín Frinta warned that these terms are not attested in Czech texts before the nineteenth century and may be forgeries added to the manuscript. Even so, scholars such as Ján Kollár and Josef Růžička defined her as a goddess of death, and that idea has become dominant in popular culture.

Worship and religion

Morana's rites survive mainly as folk custom. In the Czech Republic, Slovakia and Poland the farewell to winter is still held, known as the Drowning of Morana (Topienie Marzanny), Vynášení smrti or Vynášanie Moreny. Around the spring equinox, a straw figure dressed in white cloth and decorated is carried out of the village in a procession with songs and dances, then thrown into a river or pond. It is often undressed or burned before being thrown into the water. Participants then walk back to the village without looking behind, as looking back is believed to bring illness or death.

The custom has changed over the centuries. The synods of Prague in 1366 and Poznań in 1420 tried to forbid it, but the rites continued under Christian pretexts. Today it is usually celebrated in small towns and villages as a cheerful event, often led by folk groups.

Legacy

Morana has given her name to many regional variants, including Marzanna, Morena, Mařena and Smrtka. Her figure remains a symbol of the farewell to winter and the arrival of spring, often shown alongside the goddess Vesna.

Ancient sources: Ancient sources mentioning her are scarce and disputed; the thirteenth-century Latin dictionary Mater Verborum is the most cited, although its authenticity is doubtful.

Trivia

Link to Mars. Some authors have suggested that her Slovak form, Marmuriena, may be related to the Roman god of war Mars, although the idea is debated.
Sunday tradition. In Slovakia the festival was once held on the fourth Sunday of Lent; today it is set near 21 March.
