Trojan princess, wife of Aeneas

Creusa

Creusa is a Trojan princess, daughter of Priam and Hecuba, and the first wife of Aeneas, with whom she had a son, Ascanius. Her disappearance during the fall of Troy gave rise to several ancient versions of her story.

The myth

In the best-known version, told in Virgil's Aeneid, Creusa flees the burning city behind her husband, who leads Ascanius by the hand and carries his father Anchises. At the gates, the princess falls behind and is lost in the confusion. When Aeneas returns to look for her, he meets her shade, which tells him that her fate is to remain in Troy, foretells his long journey to Hesperia in Italy and his future marriage to another woman, and asks him to look after their son. Aeneas tries three times to embrace her, without success.

Other traditions said that she had been carried off by the Mother of the Gods (Cybele) or by Aphrodite. Pausanias claims that both goddesses saved her from slavery because she was the wife of Aeneas, a mortal son of Aphrodite. According to Polygnotus's paintings in the Lesche of the Cnidians at Delphi, however, Creusa was one of the Trojan captives led off to Greece, and her later fate is unknown. Homer does not mention Aeneas having a wife. Pausanias, the poet Lesches and the Cypria call her Eurydice, and only in the 1st century BC do Virgil, Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus present her as Aeneas's wife.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Apollodorus, Lycophron and Pausanias, as well as the poet Lesches and the author of the Cypria.

Trivia

A different name for his wife. Pausanias, Lesches and the Cypria call Aeneas's wife Eurydice, not Creusa.
A second son. An ancient scholion on the Aeneid calls him Eurybates, while another on Lycophron names him Euryleon.
