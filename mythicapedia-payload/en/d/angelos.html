daughter of Zeus and Hera, messenger of the underworld

Angelos

Angelos, whose Greek name means “messenger,” was a daughter of Zeus and Hera in Greek myth. Her story survives almost only in a scholion, and scholars often read her as an aspect of Hecate, linked to both the upper world and the underworld.

The myth

According to the story preserved in a scholion on Theocritus’ second Idyll, attributed to Sophron, Hera conceived Angelos after lying with Zeus. Nymphs raised her under their father’s care. One day she stole her mother’s ointments, which made the face shine, and gave them to Europa, daughter of Phoenix. Furious, Hera sought to punish her, and Angelos hid first in the house of a woman who had just given birth, then among men carrying a corpse.

Since Hera refused to go near such places, Zeus ordered the Cabeiroi to purify Angelos in the waters of Lake Acherusia in the Underworld. After the rite, she received the realm of the dead as her sphere of influence and was given the epithet katachthonia, “of the underworld.”

Ancient sources: A scholion on Theocritus’ second Idyll, attributed to Sophron, and the lexicon of Hesychius.

Trivia

Epithet of Artemis. According to Hesychius, Angelos was also an epithet of Artemis in Syracuse, and Artemis, as a moon goddess, was identified with Hecate.
Hermes, messenger of Persephone. In a Greek expression, Hermes is called the angelos of Persephone, linking him to the mediating role that Angelos also held.
