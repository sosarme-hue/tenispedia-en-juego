region and kingdom in Hindu myth linked to the Dandaka forest

Danda

Danda is a region of Hindu mythology that names both a kingdom and a forest called Dandaka. It matters because it is the setting for episodes of the Ramayana, above all the clash between Rama and the rakshasas.

The myth

Tradition places Danda as a colonial state of Lanka during the reign of Ravana, ruled by Khara, who made the region a stronghold for the rakshasa tribes living in the Dandaka forest. From there, according to the story, Khara launched an attack on Rama of Kosala, who lived with his wife and brother at Panchavati.

The Ramayana also presents a demon named Danda, son of Sumali and maternal uncle of Ravana. The region is believed to have covered today's Nashik District in Maharashtra, with its capital at Janasthana, now the city of Nashik.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana mentions Danda, the Dandaka forest and Janasthana as settings of the story.

Trivia

A modern city. Janasthana, the region's former capital, is identified with the present-day city of Nashik in Maharashtra.
A demon uncle. In the Ramayana, Danda is the son of Sumali and maternal uncle of Ravana, the king of Lanka.
