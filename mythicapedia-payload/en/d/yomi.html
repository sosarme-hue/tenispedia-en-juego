underworld in Japanese mythology and Shinto

Yomi

Yomi is the underworld of Japanese mythology, where the dead go according to Shinto. The word is also used in a broader sense for the realm of the dead as a whole.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, Yomi, or the Land of Darkness, is a subterranean realm whose entrance is the slope of Yomotsuhira, on the border between the world of the living and the dead. The goddess Izanami went there after her death, having eaten in that realm, and could no longer return.

Her husband Izanagi went to fetch her. When he saw her decaying body, crawling with maggots, he fled in terror. She sent the Furies of the underworld after him. Izanagi threw three peaches at them, and they retreated. Then he sealed the entrance with a huge boulder, called Chigaeshi no Ōkami. On either side of the rock, the two parted with bitter words: she vowed to kill a thousand people of the living world each day, and he vowed to build a thousand five hundred birthing huts. That is why, the story goes, more people are born each day than die.

The sources do not agree on every detail. The Kojiki places Izanami's tomb in Izumo, while the Nihongi places it in Kumano. Yomi is not a paradise, nor a hell of punishments: all the dead lead a gloomy, endless existence, whatever their conduct in life. Izanagi described it as a polluted land.

Legacy

The music group Ronin's Dojo released an album called The Sons of Yomi, and the Yu-Gi-Oh! franchise includes cards that refer to Yomi, especially in their original names.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihongi describe Yomi as the land of the dead, and the Izumo Fudoki records a cave with that name in Izumo province.

Trivia

Protective peaches. Izanagi gave the peaches he used to drive off the Furies the name Augusto Ōkamuzumi, or great divine fruit. In ancient China, peaches were also used to repel evil spirits.
A cave with an underworld name. The Izumo Fudoki mentions a cave called Slope of Yomi and Entrance of Yomi. It may be the Inome Cave, where human remains and grave goods from the Yayoi and Kofun periods were found in 1948.
A name with unclear meaning. The Chinese characters used to write Yomi are used only for their sound, not their meaning. The original meaning of the Japanese word is unknown.
