son of Jason and Medea in Greek mythology

Mermerus

Mermerus is a figure in Greek mythology, the son of Jason and Medea, whose fate appears in differing versions of the Argonaut legend and of the story of Corinth. He is remembered mainly for the traditions about his tragic death.

The myth

According to tradition, Mermerus and his brother Pheres were the sons of Jason and Medea. Accounts differ about how they died. In one version, Medea herself killed them in Corinth to punish Jason's infidelity. In another, the Corinthians stoned them to death for carrying the poisoned gifts their mother had prepared for Glauce, daughter of King Creon. The gods then punished Corinth by killing every newborn child, until an oracle ordered sacrifices in honour of Medea's children.

A third tradition says that Mermerus, exiled to Corcyra after the killing of Pelias, was killed by a lioness while hunting in Epirus with his father.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey mentions Ilus, son of Mermerus, the host of Odysseus and a skilled maker of poisons.

Trivia

A statue of Fear. According to one of the traditions, the Corinthians dedicated to Medea's children a statue representing Fear.
Ilus, Odysseus' host. Ilus, son of Mermerus, appears in the Odyssey as the host of Odysseus and was skilled in preparing poisons.
