Japanese Buddhist god of obstacles

Matarajin

Matarajin (摩多羅神) is a Buddhist deity venerated mainly in the Tendai school of Japanese Buddhism. He began as a wrathful god who obstructed rebirth in the Pure Land and later became a protector of believers, of the performing arts and of destiny.

The myth

According to tradition, Matarajin was first a demonic figure who blocked rebirth in the Pure Land of anyone who failed to placate him. Accounts of how his worship reached Japan link him to an encounter with a monk, identified in different versions as Enchin or Saichō, set on Mount Tiantai in China or on Mount Hiei.

Over time he was credited with protecting Tendai followers and driving away demons, especially tengu, and epidemics. He was identified with figures such as Mahakala and Susanoo, and his image shifted from a many-armed, many-headed form to that of a smiling old man dressed as a Japanese aristocrat.

Worship and religion

He was enshrined in Tendai monasteries such as Enryaku-ji. In the tengu odoshi rite he was calmed through frantic dancing, shouting and the recitation of randomly chosen scriptures. His worship declined in the Edo period, yet the ox festival at Kōryū-ji still honours him, and a hidden statue at Mōtsū-ji can be seen only once every thirty-three years.

Legacy

He is the protector of the noh and sarugaku performing arts. At Tōshō-gū in Nikkō he is a tutelary deity of the Tokugawa clan.

Ancient sources: The tale of his origin in Japan is preserved in the Keiran shūyōshū, composed around 1311-1347.

Trivia

A rarely visited shrine. The hidden statue at Mōtsū-ji is displayed only once every thirty-three years.
A god of dreams. Documents from Togakushi Shrine describe him as a dream king, although this role appears to be a late development.
