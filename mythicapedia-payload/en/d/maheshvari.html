Hindu goddess, Shakti of Shiva, one of the Matrikas

Maheshvari

Maheshvari is one of the Matrikas, a group of mother goddesses in Hinduism, and is identified as the Shakti (feminine energy) of Shiva. She belongs to the goddess-centred traditions of Shaktism and Tantrism.

The myth

According to tradition, each Matrika emerges from a god: Maheshvari arises from Shiva as his feminine energy. Texts describe her as a white-complexioned, three-eyed goddess who rides the bull Nandi and carries a trident, a drum and a rosary.

Her name derives from those of Shiva, and she is also known as Rudri, Rudrani, Maheshi or Shivani. In the Devi Mahatmya she is counted among the eight Ashta-Matrikas, and in Shaktism she helps the goddess Durga fight demons.

Worship and religion

The Matrikas have been worshipped in temples since the medieval period, and their veneration is especially widespread in South India and Nepal.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya, part of the Markandeya Purana, describes the Ashta-Matrikas and their attributes.
