legendary hero of Estonian mythology

Kalev

Kalev is a legendary hero of Estonian mythology, father of King Kalevipoeg and husband of Linda. He matters because he lends his name to Estonia's national epic, gathered by Friedrich Reinhold Kreutzwald.

The myth

According to Estonian tradition, Kalev is the father of King Kalevipoeg, whose name means 'son of Kalev'. After his death, his wife Linda is said to have built him a grave on Toompea hill, in central Tallinn. In the story, the tears Linda shed for him, while sitting on the so-called Linda rock, formed Lake Ülemiste.

Finnish and Karelian tales tell the lineage of Kaleva differently. His sons appear as giants who built castles and lived in various regions before humans arrived. They are often blamed for oddities in nature, such as strangely shaped stones. Genealogies vary between authors and traditions, so there is no single version.

Legacy

His name appears in the title of the Estonian epic Kalevipoeg. The Finnish epic Kalevala has also been linked to his figure, though its name refers more to a house or a small area than to a region. Several theories explain the origin of his name, such as Elias Lönnrot's proposal linking it to a Russian word for 'chief', but none is confirmed.

Ancient sources: He appears in a list of deities published by Mikael Agricola in 1551, which describes the sons of Kaleva as giants. A mention in the Old English poem Widsith has also been suggested, though it is uncertain.

Trivia

An old city with his name. In Old Russian chronicles and songs from 1223 onward, Tallinn appears as Kolyvan, a name theorized to come from Kalevanlinna, 'the burg of Kaleva'.
An armed birth. In a runic song from Ostrobothnia, the old man Kave is born fully armed, with a sword and a saddled horse, after freeing himself from the womb by lifting a single finger.
