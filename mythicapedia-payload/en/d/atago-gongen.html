Japanese kami of war and protector against fire

Atago Gongen

Atago Gongen is a Japanese deity born from the fusion of Shinto and Buddhist beliefs, associated with Mount Atago in Kyoto. Considered the local avatar of the bodhisattva Jizō, it is revered as a protector against fire and as a god of war and victory, especially among samurai.

The myth

Tradition holds that Mount Atago was infested with tengu during the reign of Emperor Monmu. One of them, particularly powerful, was named Tarōbō. The sages En no Gyōja and Taichō were tasked with clearing the mountain, and when Tarōbō surrendered he became the mountain's protector. For this reason he is often seen as a guardian or avatar of Jizō.

Some sources relate Tarōbō to Shinto deities such as Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, Hiruko and Susanoo. The philosopher Hayashi Razan counts him among the greatest daitengu, alongside Sōjōbō of Kurama and Jirōbō of Hira.

Worship and religion

The cult grew out of shugendō ascetic practices on Mount Atago. The main shrine enshrined Izanami and Katsugun Jizō, and during the medieval period the Hakuun-ji temple flourished. In the Edo period devotion spread across Japan. After the Shinto–Buddhist separation of 1868, many shrines were closed or turned to Shinto deities.

Legacy

He is depicted as a warrior on a white horse, carrying a pilgrim's staff and a wish-granting jewel. Wild boars, symbols of courage and perseverance, are said to accompany him.

Ancient sources: Fujiwara no Yorinaga's diary, the Daiki, the Heike monogatari and the Gempei Seisuiki refer to Tarōbō as a great tengu.

Trivia

Mountain of the tengu. Mount Atago and Kurama are counted among the most famous places associated with tengu.
