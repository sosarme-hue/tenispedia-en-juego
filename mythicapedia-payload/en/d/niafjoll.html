mountains in the northern underworld of Norse myth

Niðafjöll

Niðafjöll, meaning «dark mountains» in Old Norse, is a location in Norse mythology lying in the northern underworld, the place from which the dragon Níðhöggr comes. It matters mainly for its role in accounts of the end of the world and of the life that follows Ragnarök.

The myth

In Norse tradition, the mountains of Niðafjöll lie in the northern underworld and are the place where the dragon Níðhöggr comes from. The Völuspá, one of the poems of the Poetic Edda, mentions them in stanzas 37 and 66, where they also bear the name Niðvellir.

Stanza 37 describes a golden hall that is home to the family of Sindri, one of the dwarfs. Many readers have mistakenly taken this reference to place Sindri's hall on Niðafjöll, a very common misunderstanding.

Snorri Sturluson adds that after Ragnarök, good and virtuous people will live here in a golden palace, even though the place lies close to Hel.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá in the Poetic Edda mentions it in stanzas 37 and 66, and Snorri Sturluson records it in his works on Norse mythology.

Trivia

A name of uncertain origin. The prefix niða- is unclear and has no known direct relative. It has been suggested that it may relate to the waning face of the Moon, which would give something like «waning mountains», though this remains only a hypothesis.
A palace in the wrong place. The golden hall of Sindri's family appears in the Völuspá, but many readers wrongly place it on Niðafjöll, although the poem does not say so.
