the Big Grey Man of Ben MacDhui

Am Fear Liath Mòr

Am Fear Liath Mòr, or “the Big Grey Man”, is a presence or cryptid that, according to Scottish tradition, haunts the summit and passes of Ben MacDhui, the second highest peak in the British Isles. It is one of the best-known cases in Scottish mountain folklore and in cryptozoology.

The myth

According to the accounts, it is a very tall, thin figure, usually unseen in the mist, perceived more through its footsteps and a sense of unease than through its appearance. Climbers hear crunching gravel behind them, as if someone were walking with enormous strides.

The most famous testimonies date from the twentieth century. In 1891, Professor J. Norman Collie fled from the summit in terror, and other climbers described pressure, footsteps and strange humming. Some cryptozoologists compare it to the yeti or Sasquatch, although there is no solid physical evidence.

Legacy

The figure is part of Scottish mountain folklore. The poet James Hogg, who had a similar experience with a Brocken spectre in 1791, turned it into a scene in his novel “The Private Memoirs and Confessions of a Justified Sinner”.

Trivia

Rain-made footprints. Photographer John A. Rennie published in a book a series of giant footprints he had found in the Spey Valley, but later discovered that rain eroding the snow had made them.
His own shadow. In 1791, the poet James Hogg was terrified by a giant figure until he removed his hat and saw that it copied his gestures: it was his own shadow.
