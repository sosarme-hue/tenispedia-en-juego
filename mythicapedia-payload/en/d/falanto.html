leader of the Partheniae and founder of Taranto

Phalanthus

Phalanthus is the semi-legendary leader of the Spartan Partheniae and the founder of Taranto, a Spartan colony in southern Italy. His story blends myth with historical tradition.

The myth

According to the tradition, during Sparta's long war against Messenia, the Spartan women feared a shortage of men, so the Perioeci were allowed to father children with Spartan maidens. These "sons of virgins", the Partheniae, claimed citizenship under Phalanthus. Their revolt failed, and they were sent into exile.

Phalanthus consulted the oracle at Delphi, which promised him a land and city when rain fell from a cloudless sky. In Italy he won battles but could not take a city, until his wife Aethra wept onto his head, and her tears wet him. Since her name means "sky", he understood the oracle, took the city from the natives and named it Taranto after the hero Taras. He later fled to Brindisi and died there, but he asked that his ashes be scattered in Taranto, securing the city.

Worship and religion

Taranto honored him as its oikistes, or founder. The Tarentines sent tithes to Delphi, and among their offerings were statues showing Phalanthus lying beside the hero Taras.

Ancient sources: Strabo records the account of the Partheniae's revolt and the founding of Taranto.

Trivia

Rescued by a dolphin. According to a tradition in the sources, Phalanthus was shipwrecked before reaching Italy and a dolphin carried him ashore, just as it had done for the hero Taras.
An oracle from Delphi. The prophecy that guided his conquest called for rain under a clear sky, something he thought impossible until his wife, named Aethra, provided the key.
