mythical creature of Spanish folklore, a unicorn variant

Oricuerno

The oricuerno is a mythical being from Spanish folklore, a unicorn variant also called alicornio, unigaso or pegacornio. Its horn was regarded as a talisman able to purify poisoned waters.

The myth

Traditions describe it as a white horse with deer's hooves, a lion's tail, a purple head and blue eyes, with a long, twisted horn in the middle of its forehead and small wings above its hooves. It was said that it could turn women into men on Saint John's night, cure impotence and cure poisonings.

In Brañosera (Palencia), a tale tells of a creature of great ferocity that could only be tamed if a young virgin offered it one of her breasts. In a story collected in Cuenca and in ballads of several versions, a maiden disguised as a man, fleeing justice or driven by warlike zeal, is discovered in a tight spot, and the animal appears to grant her the male sex.

Worship and religion

Its horn was a true talisman. With it, people made crosses and blessings, or prepared remedies, over the waters of a river poisoned by witches or devils. This custom is still practised in some villages in the mountains of Palencia and León.

Ancient sources: André Thevet describes the power of its horn against any natural or supernatural poison in his Cosmografía.

Trivia

The Water of the Alicornio. In Brañosera (Palencia) there is a tale with this title, which recounts the animal's fierce reputation and the condition set by the virginal young woman.
Espinosa records it. A story from Cuenca, included in Aurelio Macedonio Espinosa's Cuentos populares españoles, mentions this fantastic creature.
