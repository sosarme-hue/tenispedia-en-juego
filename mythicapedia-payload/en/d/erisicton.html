king of Thessaly punished with insatiable hunger by Demeter

Erysichthon

Erysichthon is a king of Thessaly in Greek mythology, known for his impiety: cutting down a sacred tree of Demeter earned him an insatiable hunger that ended up consuming him. His story illustrates the punishment for arrogance against the sacred and gave rise to one of the best-known tales about greed.

The myth

According to tradition, Erysichthon was the son of Triopas, although some accounts make him the son of Myrmidon. He scorned the gods and offered them no sacrifices. One day he wanted to extend his house for banquets and, with twenty men, felled a poplar in a sacred grove of Demeter where tree nymphs dwelt. One of them, dying, cursed him.

Demeter, enraged, sent Hunger, who settled in his belly: the more he ate, the hungrier he became. Erysichthon sold his possessions and then his own daughter, Mestra, who could change shape thanks to a gift from Poseidon and whom he repeatedly sold to buy food. Impoverished and reduced to beggary, he ended up devouring himself. Some accounts add that Demeter punished him even after death with a serpent. Palaephatus, seeking rational explanations, argued that he was a rich Thessalian who became poor through the gifts of suitors for his daughter.

Legacy

He appears on the Pergamon Altar, where he is depicted in the battle of the gods against the giants. In the sky, some accounts link his punishment to the constellation Ophiuchus.

Ancient sources: Callimachus, Ovid (Metamorphoses), Hyginus and Palaephatus, who preserve differing versions of the story.

Trivia

A daughter turned into merchandise. Mestra could take different shapes thanks to a gift from Poseidon, which allowed her father to sell her again and again.
A giant on an altar. On the Pergamon Altar a giant labelled with his name is shown fighting Demeter.
