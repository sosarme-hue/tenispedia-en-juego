king of the Fomorians in Irish mythology

Elatha

Elatha is a king of the Fomorians, a race of beings linked to the sea and darkness in Irish mythology. He matters because he fathered Bres and, according to some traditions, the Dagda too, placing his bloodline at the heart of the conflict between generations of gods.

The myth

According to the tradition, Elatha came over the sea to the shore where Ériu, a goddess of the Tuatha Dé Danann, was standing, sailing in a vessel that seemed to be made of silver. He was a handsome young man with yellow hair, dressed in gold and wearing five gold torcs. Their union produced Bres, and Elatha left Ériu a ring that was to be given only to the man whose finger it fitted.

Years later, after Bres had been deposed as king, he went to his father for help. Elatha acknowledged him as his son but refused to support him, urging him to win back the throne through justice. Bres then sought the backing of Balar, and the dispute led to the Second Battle of Magh Tuireadh, in which the Fomorians were defeated and forced to leave Ireland.

Some texts add that Elatha was also the father of gods such as the Dagda and Ogma, which blurs the line between the two sides.

Ancient sources: The medieval Irish compilation Lebor Gabála Érenn presents him as a king of the Fomorians and the father of several of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Trivia

Many names. His name appears in several spellings, such as Elotha, Elier or Elada. Some sources write Elathan, a form that is actually the genitive and means 'of Elatha'.
A name with meaning. According to one proposed etymology, his name could relate to art or bardic knowledge, which would fit with Ogma, the inventor of the ogham script, being regarded as his son.
