Sicilian giant of Greek mythology

Alpos

Alpos is a giant of Greek mythology, son of Uranus and Gaea, who according to Nonnus of Panopolis terrorized the shepherds of Sicily until Dionysus defeated him during the Gigantomachy. He appears only in this late source, which makes him an unusual case among the giants.

The myth

According to Nonnus of Panopolis, Alpos was a giant born of the Earth and an enemy of the gods. When his brothers rose against Olympus, they were encouraged by a prophecy promising victory as long as the gods did not gain the help of a mortal.

Alpos had a hundred serpent heads, so tall that he could touch the Sun, drag the Moon down and whip the nearby stars with his hair. He lived in a lair in Sicily, from which he devoured anyone who came near, whether travellers or shepherds. His terror was such that even Pan dared not play his pipes while tending his flocks.

During the war he hurled boulders and whole mountains at the gods, but Dionysus overcame him by thrusting his thyrsus down his throat. Mortally wounded, he fell into the sea and caused a wave that covered the mountain where his brother Typhon lay buried, calming for a time the burning peaks of Etna.

Ancient sources: He appears only in Nonnus of Panopolis's Dionysiaca.

Trivia

A giant from a single source. Alpos appears only in Nonnus of Panopolis's Dionysiaca and had no known cult, so some scholars consider him an invention of the poet himself.
Pan falls silent. According to Nonnus, Alpos inspired such terror that the god Pan stopped playing his pipes while tending his flocks in Sicily.
