Lithuanian goddess of fire and the hearth

Gabija

Gabija is the spirit of fire in Lithuanian mythology and the protector of the home and family. Her name comes from gaubti, meaning “to cover” or “to protect.”

The myth

According to Lithuanian folklore, Gabija could take the form of a cat, a stork or a rooster, or appear as a woman dressed in red. She was treated like a living being: people offered her bread and salt, and every evening women covered the embers with ashes so the fire would not “go for a walk.”

If she was angered or mistreated, the flames could spread out of control until she was satisfied. Many tales describe the misfortune of those who offended her by stomping on her, spitting on her or urinating on her. Mama Gabija, goddess of the home, most likely descends from her.

Worship and religion

A bowl of clean water was sometimes left by the hearth so Gabija could wash herself, and the mistress of the household guarded the fire, just as Gabija guarded the house. The vaidilutės, Lithuanian fire priestesses, served either Gabija or Praurimė, and modern scholars disagree on which.

Legacy

In microbiology, the name Gabija, shortened to Gaj, has been given to a genome.

Ancient sources: The Christian theologian Jan Łasicki lists her among Lithuanian gods in his 1615 treatise on idolatry.

Trivia

Fire that goes for a walk. A household fire was blamed on an angry Gabija, whose flames spread until she was satisfied.
A genome with her name. In microbiology, a genome bears the name Gabija and is shortened to Gaj.
