Aztec god of astrology and calendars

Cipactonal

Cipactonal is an Aztec deity of astrology and the calendar, paired with Oxomoco. He personifies the day, and together with his wife he parallels Adam and Eve in Nahua tradition.

The myth

According to Mexica tradition, Cipactonal and Oxomoco were created together with the first sun to populate the world. After that sun fell to Tezcatlipoca and his army of jaguar-shaped demons, the pair were deified. They were tasked with inventing the calendars at the command of Quetzalcoatl. Oxomoco spun thread and handled divination, weaving and midwifery, while Cipactonal worked the land and could not stay idle.

Quetzalcoatl brought them maize from Mictlan, confronting Mictlantecuhtli to do so. Oxomoco sowed, tended and harvested it, and Cipactonal ground and cooked it so that both would grow strong by eating it. Afterwards, the lord of Mictlan and the god Tlaloc hid the remains of the maize on Mount Tonacatepetl.

Legacy

His name derives from Cipactli, the first day of the Mexica sacred calendar, which the stories associate with beginnings and origin. In Maya mythology the pair are linked to Itzam Na and Ix Chel, another primordial couple.

Ancient sources: The Annals of Cuauhtitlan mention them as keepers of the calendar, and the Quiché Popol Vuh also includes them.

Trivia

A day with a god's name. Cipactli is the first day of the Mexica sacred calendar, and its name also means 'crocodile'. This is why it is linked to the origin of all things.
