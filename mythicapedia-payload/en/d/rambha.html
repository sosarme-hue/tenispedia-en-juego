apsara of Indra's heaven in Hindu mythology

Rambha

Rambha is one of the apsaras, the celestial nymphs of Svarga, Indra's heaven in Hinduism, admired for her beauty and her skill in dance and music. Her story is tied to the seduction of sages and to the violence of Ravana, so she appears in several of the great Indian epics.

The myth

According to the Adi Parva of the Mahabharata, Rambha is a daughter of the sage Kashyapa and his wife Pradha, and counts among a group of sister apsaras. Other Puranic accounts say she emerged from the ocean of milk during the churning of the sea. The tradition presents her as one of Indra's emissaries, sent to seduce sages so that they would not gain divine powers through austerity.

In the Bala Kanda of the Ramayana, Indra orders her to break the penance of Vishvamitra, a sage who had already fallen earlier to the apsara Menaka. Realising the trick, the furious sage curses her to turn into stone for ten thousand years, until a Brahmin frees her.

In the Uttara Kanda, Rambha is the wife of Nalakuvara, son of Kubera. Ravana sees her on a mountain, desires her and assaults her, even though she reminds him that she is his daughter-in-law. She goes to her husband, who curses Ravana so that his head will burst into seven pieces if he ever forces a woman again. This curse explains why Ravana could not violate Sita when he abducted her, though Rama eventually defeats him.

Worship and religion

In southern India, especially in Kerala, she is venerated under the name Rembha, a female personal name pronounced rembja.

Ancient sources: The Adi Parva of the Mahabharata, the Bala Kanda and Uttara Kanda of the Ramayana, and the Bhagavata Purana, which names her among the daughters of Kashyapa.

Trivia

Rambha, the name of a banana. According to the Katha Sarit Sagara and kavya literature, rambha also means banana plant. In the same literary tradition it is the name of a courtesan.
An encounter with Shuka. In the poem Srngarajnananirnaya, Rambha is sent to seduce the young sage Shuka, but ends up in a conversation with him about love and wisdom.
