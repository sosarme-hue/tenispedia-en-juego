asura king and father of Prahlada in Hindu mythology

Hiranyakashipu

Hiranyakashipu is the asura king of Hindu mythology and the father of Prahlada, a devotee of Vishnu. His story, told in several Puranas, explains how Vishnu protects his devotees against the arrogance of a tyrant who believed himself invincible.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hiranyakashipu was the younger brother of Hiranyaksha, and both were born to Diti and the sage Kashyapa, in a union held at dusk, a time considered inauspicious. When Vishnu, in his boar avatar Varaha, slew Hiranyaksha, Hiranyakashipu swore revenge and resolved to defeat the god. He withdrew to the valley of Mount Mandara and performed extreme austerities for thousands of years, standing motionless with his arms raised, until the heat of his penance shook the three worlds. Brahma granted him a boon: he could not die inside or outside a house, by day or by night, on earth or in the air, nor by any weapon, human or animal.

On his return he conquered the three worlds. While he was meditating, Indra and other devas had attacked his palace, and the sage Narada protected his wife Kayadhu, who was expecting a child. The boy, Prahlada, heard the sage's teachings from the womb and grew up a devotee of Vishnu, to his father's dismay. Hiranyakashipu tried to kill him several times without success, until Prahlada declared that Vishnu was everywhere. The king struck a palace pillar with his mace, and from it emerged Narasimha, half man and half lion, who killed him at dusk, on a threshold, placing him on his thighs and using his claws, thus respecting the terms of the boon. After the death, Prahlada calmed the avatar's fury and asked for his father's salvation.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Bhagavata Purana (Canto 7), the Harivamsa, the Kurma Purana, the Matsya Purana, the Shiva Purana and the Skanda Purana, among other Puranic texts.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Hiranyakashipu literally means one who desires wealth and material comforts, from hiranya, gold, and kashipu, soft bed. In the Puranas the name is also linked to a golden throne.
A reign of millions of years. According to the Skanda Purana, Hiranyakashipu ruled the universe for 107.28 million years, or 24.8 yugas.
