primordial king of Sicyon in Greek mythology

Aegialeus

Aegialeus is, in Greek mythology, a primordial king credited with the first settlement of the Peloponnese and the founding of the city of Aegialea, later known as Sicyon. The early name of the region, Aegialia, is traced back to him, and his son Europs, in some traditions, is the eponymous hero of the European lands.

The myth

According to tradition, Aegialeus was a son of the river Inachus and the Oceanid Melia, and a brother of Phoroneus. Pausanias and the local tradition of Sicyon present him as the first inhabitant and king of their land, the founder of the city of Aegialea on the plain, where the citadel with the sanctuary of Athena later stood. Castor of Rhodes gave him a reign of fifty-two years, earlier than the founding of Argos by Inachus, and stated that the Peloponnese was named after him. Eusebius placed the start of that reign in the fifteenth year of Belus in Assyria, though Jerome calculated it slightly later.

Apollodorus offers a different genealogy: Aegialeus died childless, and the whole region was called Aegialia, while Phoroneus ruled the entire territory, which later took the name Peloponnese. A scholion on Euripides, by contrast, makes him a son of Phoroneus and Peito, and a brother of Apis. According to Pausanias, Aegialeus begat Europs, Europs begat Telchis, and Telchis begat Apis, so the line of succession changes depending on who tells it.

Ancient sources: Castor of Rhodes, known through citations in later authors; Apollodorus's Library; Pausanias's Description of Greece and a scholion on Euripides.

Trivia

A citadel with a sanctuary. According to Pausanias, the citadel of Aegialea stood where the sanctuary of Athena at Sicyon was later built.
From Aegialus to Achaea. Pausanias tells that the land between Elis and Sicyonia was once called Aegialus, and later took the name Achaea from its inhabitants.
