bathhouse spirit of Slavic folklore

Bannik

The bannik is a spirit that lives in the banya, the Slavic steam bath. He is imagined as a tiny, naked old man with a long beard, covered in birch leaves, who watches over a place where births and divinations also took place.

The myth

According to popular belief, the bannik could take many forms: sometimes he looked like a local villager met on the road, and other times he hid in a stone or a coal in the oven that heated the bath. Because the banya was considered an unclean place and a site of spiritual danger, he was said to react violently against anyone who disturbed him while washing: he might pour boiling water over the intruder or even strangle him.

He was said to enjoy inviting demons and forest spirits to share his bath, which is why no Christian images were allowed inside. He reserved for himself the third steaming session, or the fourth depending on local custom. When something went wrong in the bathhouse, he was blamed, and if the building burned down, which happened often, people believed the spirit had been offended in some way.

Worship and religion

To keep him content, people left fir branches, water and soap, together with a thank-you spoken aloud, at the end of the steam session reserved for him or when the banya was closed for the night. When a new banya was built, a black hen was suffocated, left unplucked and buried beneath the threshold, and those present backed away while reciting suitable incantations. Many births took place inside the banya, because the bannik was believed not to be truly settled until a child was born within his domain. To learn what the future held, a person stood with their back exposed in the half-open door: if all was well, the spirit gently stroked their back; if trouble lay ahead, he struck with his claws.

Trivia

Black hen. When a new banya was raised, a black hen was suffocated, left unplucked and buried under the threshold as an offering to appease the spirit.
Fortune by the back. Someone who wanted to know their fortune stood with their back to the half-open bathhouse door: a caress foretold good luck and a scratch, trouble.
