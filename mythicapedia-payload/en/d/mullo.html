Celtic god associated with Mars in Gaul

Mullo

Mullo is a Celtic god known mainly from inscriptions in Gaul, where he was worshipped under the name Mars Mullo, identified with Mars. His name may be linked to horses or mules.

The myth

Ancient texts say very little about Mullo, so his character is known almost only through his cult and the dedications made to him. His name is the Latin word for 'mule', which suggests a connection with these animals.

No stories survive about his origin, his family or his deeds.

Worship and religion

His cult was popular in northern and north-western Gaul, especially in Brittany and Normandy. He had a circular temple at Craon in Mayenne and an important centre at Rennes, with statues and an official public cult. At Allonnes in Sarthe he was invoked as a healer of eye ailments, and pilgrims offered him coins and votive images of the afflicted parts of the body.

Ancient sources: Latin and Gaulish inscriptions found in Gaul; no ancient literary texts are known to mention him.

Trivia

A mule's name. Mullo is the Latin word for 'mule', which is why a link with horses or mules has been proposed, though it remains a hypothesis.
Coins and votive offerings. Pilgrims at Allonnes left numerous coins and figures of the afflicted body parts, which clearly show the eye problems they sought cured.
A link with Augustus. A dedicatory inscription connects him with Augustus, which suggests he held considerable importance.
