eponymous hero of Phthiotis in Greek mythology

Phthius

Phthius is the eponymous hero of Phthiotis, a region of southern Thessaly that took its name from him. Son of Poseidon and Larisa, he belongs to the lineage of the Pelasgian and Achaean peoples who, according to tradition, settled that land.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved by Dionysius of Halicarnassus, Phthius was the son of Poseidon and Larisa, and brother of Achaeus and the younger Pelasgus. The three left Achaean Argos with a Pelasgian contingent bound for Thessaly. On arrival they drove out the peoples living there and divided the land into three parts named after their leaders: Phthiotis, Achaia and Pelasgiotis.

The settlers prospered for several generations on the Thessalian plains. Around the sixth generation, however, the Curetes and Leleges, later known as Aetolians and Locrians, together with other inhabitants near Parnassus, drove them out. Their chief opponent was Deucalion, son of Prometheus and Clymene. Phthius left descendants: his daughter Amphictyone bore Dotius to Asterius, a figure who may have given his name to Dotion.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus, in his Roman Antiquities, describing the migration of the Pelasgian race; he is essentially the only ancient source for him.

Trivia

A single source. Almost everything known about Phthius comes from one ancient author, Dionysius of Halicarnassus, who mentions him while describing the Pelasgian migration.
Several Phthii. The name recurs in other stories: one Phthius was a son of Lycaon, and another was the child conceived by Hermione and Neoptolemus.
