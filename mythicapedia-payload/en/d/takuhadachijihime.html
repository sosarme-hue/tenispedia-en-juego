Japanese goddess of weaving

Takuhadachijihime

Takuhadachijihime is a goddess in Japanese mythology associated with weaving and textiles. She appears in the Kojiki, Japan's oldest chronicle, and her story is woven together with that of her husband and her son, Ninigi.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, she is the daughter of Takamimusubi and the wife of Amenooshihomimi, Amaterasu's eldest son. Their union produced Ninigi no Mikoto, who in the tradition is Amaterasu's grandson.

Early texts give different genealogies, so her exact family ties shift from one source to another. What stays constant is her link to spinning and cloth. The Kojiki also refers to her by another name, though the sources do not specify it.

Worship and religion

She is venerated at three shrines: Shiozawa in Fukushima Prefecture, Tsubaki Grand Shrine in Mie Prefecture, and Izuenashi in Osaka. There she is honored as a protector of weaving and of those who work with cloth.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, Japan's oldest chronicle, is the main early source that mentions her.
