Hindu goddess, queen of the yakshas and wife of Kubera

Bhadrā

Bhadrā is the queen of the yakshas and the first wife of Kubera, the god of wealth, in Hindu tradition. Her name, meaning auspicious, links her to prosperity and good fortune.

The myth

In Hindu tradition, Bhadrā was the queen of the yakshas, beings tied to nature and hidden treasures, and she became the first wife of Kubera. With him she had three sons, Nalakuvara, Manigriva and Mayuraja, and a daughter called Minakshi. Some lists name Manibhadra among their children instead.

Texts also know her as Yakshi, Chhavi, Riddhi, Manorama, Nidhi, Sahadevi and Kuberi, names that reflect different aspects of her role as a giver of abundance. In Buddhism she is called Bhuñjatī. Since Bhadra is a Sanskrit word meaning good, fortunate or auspicious, many figures in Hindu tradition share it, so this Bhadrā should be told apart from namesakes such as Chandra's daughter or Krishna's wife.

Trivia

A widely shared name. Bhadra means good, fortunate or auspicious, and several kings, sages and women in Hindu tradition carry it, including a Bhadrā who was the wife of Krishna.
A different name in Buddhism. In Buddhism she is called Bhuñjatī, and Hindu tradition gives her at least seven alternative names.
