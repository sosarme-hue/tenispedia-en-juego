Hindu goddess of deliverance from troubles

Bipodtarini Devi

Bipodtarini, also written Bipattarini or Bipadtarini, is a goddess (devi) worshipped in eastern India, especially in West Bengal, Jharkhand, Odisha and Assam. She is counted among the 108 manifestations of Durga and is invoked to overcome misfortune; her name literally means "the one who delivers from troubles".

The myth

According to tradition, the legend is set in Vishnupur, West Bengal, capital of the Mallabhum kingdom between the 7th and 19th centuries, under Malla kings of the Bagdi caste. The queen had a friend from the Mochi caste who ate beef. Curious, the queen asked to see the meat, and the friend, fearing the wrath of the devout king, finally showed it to her.

The king discovered the betrayal and rushed to kill the queen. She hid the meat in her clothes and prayed to Durga for help. When the king tore her garments, he found only a red jaba flower (hibiscus). In this way the goddess saved the queen from disgrace and death.

Worship and religion

Her worship is a vow (vrata) kept mainly by women, who ask her intervention in family crises or illness, which is why she is also honoured as a healing deity. The Bipadtarini Vrata is observed each year in the month of Ashada, with fasting, the tying of a red thread called taga on the wrist and, at Asansol, the dandi, in which devotees prostrate themselves along the route to the shrine. In Assam the festival lasts four days, with folk songs and an immersion of the image in water. Her worship remains alive today.
