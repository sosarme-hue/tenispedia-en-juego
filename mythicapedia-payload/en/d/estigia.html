Greek goddess of the river and of the sacred oath

Styx

Styx is the personification of the river of the same name, one of the waters of the Greek underworld, and a goddess tied to the solemn oaths of the gods. She matters because her water sealed the most binding promises in the divine order, and her name gave English the adjective «stygian».

The myth

According to the most common account, Styx was the eldest of the Oceanids, the daughters of the Titan Oceanus and his sister-wife Tethys. The Roman mythographer Hyginus instead makes her a daughter of Nox and Erebus. She married the Titan Pallas, and with him she had Zelus, Nike, Kratos and Bia, personifications of rivalry, victory, power and force. According to Epimenides of Crete, reported by Pausanias, she was also the mother of the monster Echidna, by a otherwise unknown father named Perias.

In the war between Zeus and the Titans, Zeus summoned the deathless gods and promised to honor whoever joined him. Styx, advised by her father Oceanus, was the first to side with him, bringing her children. In return, Zeus made her the great oath of the gods and decreed that her children would always live with him. Hesiod places her home in a cave at the entrance to Hades, propped up by silver pillars, and says that Iris fetched the cold water of Styx for the gods to swear by.

The Styx was also a river of the underworld. According to Hesiod, it received a tenth of Oceanus's water and rose from a high rock. In the Iliad it marks the boundary of Hades, and in Virgil's Aeneid it winds nine times around the realm of the dead, where the boatman Charon ferries souls across. Ovid has Jupiter swear by its waters, and in Statius's Achilleid Thetis dips the infant Achilles in them to make him immortal, though the heel she holds remains dry.

Worship and religion

Swearing by the Styx was the most solemn oath of the gods, as the Homeric Hymns record. Herodotus locates a waterfall in Arcadia that he calls the water of Styx, near Nonacris, and reports that the Spartan king Cleomenes made men swear by it. Pausanias visited the site in the second century AD, when it was already a partly buried ruin. There is no evidence that this cult survives today.

Legacy

The adjective «stygian» came to mean anything dark, gloomy or forbidding. In Dante's Inferno, Phlegyas ferries Virgil and Dante across the foul marsh of the Styx, where the angry and sullen are punished. Its name remains in the language as a byword for the infernal.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Theogony; Homer, Iliad and Odyssey; Homeric Hymns; Ovid; Virgil, Aeneid; Apollodorus; Herodotus.

Trivia

The most feared oath. Homer's gods regarded an oath sworn by Styx as the gravest of all, and even Hera had to swear by it when she denied meddling in the Trojan War.
A dry heel. According to Statius, Thetis dipped Achilles in the Styx, but the heel by which she held him stayed dry, and that heel was later his weak point.
