goddess or female yoga practitioner in the Hindu tradition

Yogini

Yogini is a Sanskrit term for a female practitioner of yoga and tantra, and also a formal title of respect for female Hindu and Buddhist spiritual teachers. In Indian mythology, yoginis are groups of goddesses, usually sixty-four, who embody the sacred feminine energy.

The myth

According to the Puranas, the demon Raktabija was so powerful that every drop of his blood that touched the ground produced a new copy of himself. The gods turned to Adi Shakti, Mother Parvati, who took the fierce form of Durga and fought a bloody battle. To defeat him, Durga's energy split into sixty-four forms, the yoginis, who drank the demon's blood before it reached the ground. As a reward for their valour, Durga gave each of them a shrine of her own, the origin of the tradition of the sixty-four yogini temples.

According to the Indologist David Gordon White, yoginis first appear in Indian literature in the sixth-century Agni Purana, and their roots lie in the Vedic tradition. Vidya Dehejia, however, argues that their worship began outside Vedic religion, with the local village goddesses who protected their communities, and that Tantra grouped them into a number considered powerful.

Worship and religion

They were worshipped in austere stone temples, round or rectangular and roofless, which housed sculptures of goddesses; often a statue of Bhairava stood at the centre. Only three such temples survive with their sculptures, and the Chandella kings, who supported the temples of Khajuraho, also built temples dedicated to them.

Legacy

The term is still used as a title of respect for Hindu and Buddhist women teachers. In the medieval Nath tradition, female yogis were highly popular in rural areas, and many seventeenth- and eighteenth-century paintings depict them. Some yogini sculptures are now held in museums in India, Europe and North America.

Ancient sources: The Agni Purana, the Puranas, the Brahmanda Purana, the Jnanarnava Tantra, the Kathasaritsagara and the Hatha Yoga Pradipika mention yoginis.

In art

Trivia

Animal forms. In statuary they often have animal heads, and many have a bird as their vahana, or animal vehicle. They are associated with the power of flight.
Curse on the indiscreet. The Brahmanda Purana and the Jnanarnava Tantra warn that passing secret knowledge to non-initiates brings the curse of the yoginis.
