Son of Horus who guarded the liver in the canopic jars

Imsety

Imsety is one of the four sons of Horus, Egyptian deities who protected the internal organs of the dead. He is the only one of the four shown in human form, and he is best known for his link with the liver and the canopic jars.

The myth

The sons of Horus first appear in the Pyramid Texts, written in the late Old Kingdom. There they are usually described as children of Horus, although some passages make them children of Atum, Geb or Nut. According to the Coffin Texts of the Middle Kingdom, they were the offspring of Isis and an older form of Horus known as Horus the Elder.

Their main task was to help the deceased in the afterlife. In the Pyramid Texts they help the king climb into the sky and shield him from hunger and thirst. In the Book of the Dead they protect Osiris, and in the Book of Gates they hold chains that bind malign beings called snakes.

The Egyptologist James P. Allen translates his name as 'He of the Dill', while other scholars see him as a personification of that herb. Maarten Raven argues that the four sons originally were celestial deities.

Worship and religion

Their worship was almost entirely tied to funerary practice. Imsety guarded the liver of the deceased, and from the First Intermediate Period his human head appeared on the lids of the canopic jars. His cult disappeared in the fourth century AD, with the end of the ancient Egyptian funerary tradition.

Ancient sources: Pyramid Texts (spells 541, 688 and 338), Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead and Book of Gates from the New Kingdom.

Trivia

A name linked to the liver. In Egyptian, Imsety's name resembled the word for 'liver', which may explain why he became linked with that organ.
Chains against serpents. In the Book of Gates, the four sons of Horus hold chains that bind malign beings called snakes.
