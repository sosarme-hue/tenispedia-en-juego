Cretan hero of the Trojan War

Meriones

Meriones is a Cretan hero of Greek mythology, son of Molus and loyal companion of King Idomeneus. Although his role in Homer's Iliad is secondary, he is one of the most active Greek warriors of the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the tradition, Meriones was of royal blood. His father Molus was an illegitimate son of King Deucalion of Crete, and his mother is named as Melphis or Euippe. Through Deucalion's line he was regarded as a descendant of the gods, including Zeus and Helios. He was said to have inherited from his father the helmet of Amyntor, which Autolycus had stolen and which he later gave to Odysseus.

In the Iliad he takes part in the night council and on the battlefield, where he kills several Trojan heroes, among them Adamas, Acamas, Harpalion, Morys, Hippotion and Laogonus, and wounds Deiphobus. He is among those who contest the body of Patroclus and is charged with gathering the wood for his pyre. At the funeral games he competes in chariot racing, archery and javelin throwing, and excels above all in archery. After the war he returns to Knossos without further incident.

Later legends send him to Sicily, where Cretan colonists welcome him, and credit him with founding Cresa in Paphlagonia. Quintus of Smyrna also places him among the Greeks who enter Troy inside the Trojan Horse.

Worship and religion

Meriones had a presence in sanctuaries. According to the Lindos Chronicle, he is credited with dedicating a silver quiver in the temple of Athena at Lindos, with an inscription naming him son of Molos and recording spoils from Troy. Beyond this offering, the sources do not document a cult devoted to him.

Legacy

Christoph Willibald Gluck gave him a role in his 1765 opera Telemaco, where he is linked to Odysseus's wanderings after the war, a detail not found in Homer's Odyssey.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, where he appears in the Catalogue of Ships and several books, and the Posthomerica of Quintus of Smyrna; Hyginus also lists him among Helen's suitors.

Trivia

The helmet of Amyntor. Meriones inherited from his father the helmet of Amyntor. He later gave it to Odysseus when Odysseus set out as a night spy into the Trojan camp.
A red-haired portrait. Dares Phrygius describes him as red-haired, of medium height and sturdy build, while the chronicler Malalas imagines him with black, curly hair.
