Finnish goddess of the Sun

Päivätär

Päivätär is the goddess of the Sun in Finnish mythology. She is linked to Kuutar, the Lady of the Moon, and the stories treat them as one goddess with two aspects. She appears in the runic songs and in the epic poem Kalevala.

The myth

According to the runic songs, Päivätär owns the silver of the Sun, spins silver yarn and weaves clothes from it. She is also known as the mother of wasps, and charms ask her to tell her children not to sting. Another name for a closely related figure is Auringotar, who some songs credit with creating fire.

In several versions she is the one who freed the Son of the Sun, an epithet that in the songs usually refers to Väinämöinen. In Christianized songs her figure merges with the Virgin Mary, who sometimes frees the Sun or even Jesus. In the Kalevala, young maidens ask her for silver jewellery and clothes, and she is described as a woman of great beauty.

Legacy

Päivätär appears in the Kalevala, the epic poem that gathers folk songs from Finnish tradition. In Christianized songs her figure blended with that of the Virgin Mary, showing how the old goddess survived in changed form in oral poetry.

Ancient sources: Finnish runic songs from oral tradition, some collected in the 1700s, and the epic poem Kalevala, which compiles them.

Trivia

The Sun from an egg. According to the Kalevala, a teal laid its eggs on the knee of Ilmatar, the daughter of the air. When the egg fell, the white became the Moon and the yolk became the Sun.
Mother of wasps. In runic charms, Päivätär is invoked as the mother of wasps and asked to command her children not to sting.
