Japanese god linked to court rituals

Futodama

Futodama is a god in Japanese mythology, regarded as the ancestor of the Inbe clan, whose role as court ritualists seems to be reflected in his character. His name is usually understood to mean "great gift" or "offering".

The myth

According to the myth, Susanoo accidentally killed one of Amaterasu's attendants in her weaving hall. The angered goddess shut herself in the Ama-no-Iwato cave, plunging the world into darkness. After almost a year of chaos, Omoikane and the other gods devised a plan to coax her out, and Futodama and Ame-no-Koyane were tasked with performing a divination.

When Amaterasu left the cave, Futodama used a shimenawa to keep her from returning. Tradition regards this episode as the mythical origin of the shimenawa. In the Kogo Shūi, Futodama leads the rituals performed, and he is recorded as the son of Takamimusubi and brother of Takuhadachi-hime and Ame-no-Oshihi.

Worship and religion

He is believed to be enshrined at Awa shrine, where a festival in his honour is held every year on August 10. He is also enshrined at Amatsu Shrine alongside Ninigi and Ame-no-Koyane. The Engi Shiki lists several shrines dedicated to him in Izumi Province.

Legacy

He appears as a demon in the Japanese role-playing game Shin Megami Tensei IV.

Ancient sources: The Kogo Shūi presents him as leader of the rituals and gives his genealogy, while the Engi Shiki records his shrines in Izumi Province.

Trivia

Origin of the shimenawa. The straw rope that marks sacred spaces in Japan is, according to the myth, traced to Futodama's gesture of closing off Amaterasu's cave.
A disputed name. His name is usually rendered as "great gift" or "offering", though this is a speculative interpretation rather than a certain meaning.
