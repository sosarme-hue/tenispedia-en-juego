Mesopotamian guardians of the sun god's gates

Scorpion-men

Scorpion-men are beings from Mesopotamian myth, usually shown with a human head, torso and arms and the body and tail of a scorpion. They stand guard at the mountains of Mashu, where the sun god Shamash passes each day.

The myth

In the Enuma Elish, Tiamat created them to wage war on the gods after the death of her mate Apsu.

In the Standard Babylonian Epic of Gilgamesh they guard the way into Kurnugi, the land of darkness. They open the doors for Shamash at dawn and close them behind him at night. When Gilgamesh reaches Mashu, they warn him that their terror is awesome and their glance is death. One of them tells his companion that Gilgamesh carries the flesh of the gods, and she replies that he is two-thirds god and one-third human.

Legacy

They appear as Egyptian mythological units in the video game Age of Mythology, as the Stingers in Dungeons & Dragons, as figure number 55 in Monster in My Pocket, and in The Mummy Returns, where the Scorpion King takes this form.

Ancient sources: The Enuma Elish and the Standard Babylonian Epic of Gilgamesh, Tablet IX.

Trivia

Very early images. They appear in the art of Mesopotamia and ancient Iran before they appear in literature, including an example from Jiroft and the Bull Lyre from Ur.
A toy figure. Figure number 55 in Monster in My Pocket was based on an ancient carving of a scorpion-man aiming a bow and arrow.
