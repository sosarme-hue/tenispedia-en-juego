Shinto kami, father of Emperor Jimmu

Ugayafukiaezu

Ugayafukiaezu is a kami in Japanese mythology and, in the tradition, the father of Japan's first emperor, Jimmu. His name refers to a roof of cormorant feathers that was not finished at the moment of his birth.

The myth

According to the myth, he was the son of Hoori and Toyotama-hime, and the grandson of Ninigi and of Ryūjin, the dragon kami of the sea. Toyotama-hime became pregnant at the undersea palace of Ryūgū, but she chose to give birth on the shore. There, she and Hoori built a hut with a roof of cormorant feathers instead of thatch, but she went into labour before the roof was finished, which is how the child got his name.

Toyotama-hime had asked Hoori not to look at her while she gave birth, since on earth she would return to her original form. He disobeyed and saw that she was a shark; she fled to the sea and left the baby, who was raised by her younger sister, Tamayori-hime. Another version holds that the infant's crying, as he lay abandoned on the beach, reached his aunt's ears, and she decided to take him in.

When he grew up, Ugayafukiaezu married Tamayori-hime, and they had four children. One of them, Mikeirinu, travelled to Tokoyo, the "Everworld", while another, Inai, returned to the ocean to be with his mother. The eldest and youngest brothers set out to rule the land, and after the eldest died, the youngest became the first emperor, known as Jimmu.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki both record him as the father of Japan's first emperor.

Trivia

An unfinished roof. The child's name is explained by the cormorant-feather roof that was not complete when he was born. The image sums up the episode of the birth on the beach.
An aunt who raises him. In one version, Tamayori-hime, the younger sister of Toyotama-hime, raised the boy after his mother fled. He later married her, so tradition presents her as his aunt.
