Trojan hero, ancestor of the Elymians of Sicily

Elymus

Elymus is a Trojan hero of Greek and Roman legend, an illegitimate son of Anchises and half-brother of Aeneas. He was regarded as the mythical ancestor of the Elymians, an indigenous people of Sicily.

The myth

According to legend, Elymus fled from Troy to Sicily with the fellow Trojan Acestes before Aeneas emigrated. They settled on the banks of the river Crinisus, in the land of the Sicani. They briefly returned, with Priam's blessing, to fight in the Trojan War, but fled back to Sicily as the city was about to fall, using three ships that Achilles had earlier lost.

When the Trojan refugees led by Aeneas arrived, Elymus founded the towns of Segesta and Elyme. The Trojans who settled in that part of the island called themselves Elymi after him. Strabo calls him Elymnus and says he sailed to Sicily with Aeneas and that together they took the cities of Eryx and Lilybaeum. He was also credited with founding Asca and Entella.

Virgil has him compete in the funeral games held in Sicily for Anchises, in the footrace where Nisus and Euryalus appear. Hyginus lists him as Helymus, who finished second to Euryalus and won an Amazonian quiver as his prize.

Ancient sources: Virgil mentions him in the Aeneid (Book V), Strabo includes him in his Geography, and Hyginus lists him among the competitors in the funeral games.

Trivia

An Amazonian quiver. According to Hyginus, Elymus, under the name Helymus, finished second in the funeral footrace and received an Amazonian quiver as his prize.
A name for a people. The Trojans who settled in western Sicily were said to have called themselves Elymi after Elymus, according to tradition.
