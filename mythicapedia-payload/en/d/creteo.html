king of Iolcus, son of Aeolus

Cretheus

Cretheus was a king of Iolcus, son of Aeolus and its founder, and he is the ancestor of several of the most famous heroes of Greek myth, such as Jason. His figure links the Aeolid dynasties of Thessaly with some of the great stories of the heroic age of Greece. According to myth, Cretheus was the eldest of the sons of Aeolus and Enarete, and he founded Iolcus, where he ruled as a king who administered justice. Some authors, however, name Laodice, daughter of Aloeus, as his mother. Cretheus took in his orphaned niece Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus, and when she came of age he married her. They had Aeson, Pheres and Amythaon, whose descendants reigned as Aeolids: Jason, leader of the Argonauts; Admetus, king of Pherae; and the twins Melampus and Bias, who shared the rule of Argos. Tyro had earlier borne Pelias and Neleus to Poseidon, and Cretheus raised them as his own. After Cretheus died, Pelias seized the throne of Iolcus and ruled as a tyrant, although the sources do not clearly explain how the succession happened. Other accounts give him several daughters: Astydameia (or Hippodameia), the treacherous wife of Acastus, son of Pelias; Myrina, queen of the Amazons on Lemnos when the Argonauts arrived and wife of Thoas; and a daughter known only as Cretheis, mother of Asterion of Crete if Cres is emended to Cretheus. A late tradition makes him married to Demodice or Biadice, who, rejected by Phrixus, falsely accused him of assault; Cretheus persuaded his brother Athamas to kill the young man. This version is not the most common one in the Argonaut saga.

The myth

According to tradition, Cretheus was the son of Aeolus and Enarete, the eldest of their children, and he founded Iolcus, where he ruled as a king who administered justice. Some authors, however, name Laodice, daughter of Aloeus, as his mother.

Cretheus took in his orphaned niece Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus, and when she came of age he married her. Tyro had earlier borne Pelias and Neleus to Poseidon, and Cretheus raised them as his own. After his death, Pelias seized the throne of Iolcus and ruled as a tyrant, though the sources do not clearly explain how the succession happened.

Other accounts give him several daughters: Astydameia (or Hippodameia), the treacherous wife of Acastus, son of Pelias; Myrina, queen of the Amazons on Lemnos when the Argonauts arrived and wife of Thoas; and a daughter known only as Cretheis, mother of Asterion of Crete if Cres is emended to Cretheus. Ancient sources: Homer and Hesiod mention him, and other ancient authors record his daughters and descendants.

Trivia

Sons of Poseidon. Tyro had Pelias and Neleus with the god Poseidon before marrying Cretheus, who raised them as his own sons.
An Amazon queen. Myrina, a daughter of Cretheus according to some authors, was queen of the Amazons on Lemnos when the Argonauts arrived.
