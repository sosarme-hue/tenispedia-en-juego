crocodile god of ancient Egyptian mythology

Sobek

Sobek is the crocodile god of ancient Egyptian mythology, linked to fertility, vegetation, the Nile and royal power. His cult, one of the most widespread in the Nile valley, lasted from the Old Kingdom into the Roman period.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Sobek emerged from the primeval waters of chaos at the creation of the world, and some accounts say the Nile came from his sweat. The source gives Seth as his father and Neit as his mother, while Renenutet is named as his consort. Rather than a man-eater, he is described as a protector who defended the innocent and warded off evil.

He also had a fierce side. In his negative aspect he was depicted as a demon of the Duat, and he was associated with Seth because both brought danger and disorder. One version of the Osiris myth says Seth hid in a crocodile's body to escape punishment for his crime. Even so, Sobek helped at the birth of Horus, helped destroy Seth and, at Ra's command, rescued the four sons of Horus from the waters of Nun.

Later traditions also linked him with Isis, as a healer of the body of Osiris, and with the sun, in the form of Sobek-Ra. His dwelling lay east of the mountain of Baju, which gave him the title "Lord of Baju".

Worship and religion

His cult goes back to the earliest dynasties. His main centre was Shedet (Crocodilopolis) in the Faiyum, where he was worshipped alongside Neit and priests cared for sacred crocodiles. Other important sites were Kom Ombo in southern Egypt, where he was the consort of Hathor or Heket, and Lake Moeris. Crocodiles were raised as living embodiments of the god and, after death, were mummified as offerings, sometimes with mummified crocodile eggs. In the Hellenistic period he was worshipped as Soknopaios, an oracular god with a falcon's head. His festival was held on the fourth day of the month of Khoiak.

Legacy

His name formed part of the royal titles of thirteenth-dynasty pharaohs called Sebekhotep ("Sobek is satisfied"), and of seventeenth-dynasty rulers such as Sobekemsaf ("Sobek is our protection"). A queen-pharaoh, Neferusobek, also bore a name derived from his. Mummified crocodiles and his statues are now common exhibits in museums worldwide.

Ancient sources: Old Kingdom Pyramid Texts mention him, especially spell PT 317, which presents the pharaoh as the living incarnation of the crocodile god. In the Faiyum, the Book of the Faiyum presents him as a creator who rose from the waters of Lake Moeris.

Trivia

Mummified crocodiles. Sacred crocodiles were mummified in large numbers, and some mummies have been found with baby crocodiles in their mouths or on their backs, interpreted as a symbol of protection.
A name meaning "to unite". Many scholars connect his name with the verb meaning "to impregnate", while others propose a root meaning "to unite", alluding to the limbs of Osiris.
