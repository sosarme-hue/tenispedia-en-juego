king of Elis in Greek mythology

Amphimachus

Amphimachus was, according to Greek tradition, king of Elis and father of Eleius, his successor. He is mainly notable for his name and for the friendship it recalls with another hero who bore it.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Pausanias, Polyxenus returned safely from Troy and had a son, whom he named Amphimachus. Pausanias himself suggests that the father chose this name to honour his friendship with another Amphimachus, son of Cteatus, who had died at Troy.

Amphimachus fathered Eleius, who inherited the throne of Elis. The sources do not describe his reign in detail, so his role is mainly to link two generations of the royal house of Elis.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, records Amphimachus's genealogy and the origin of his name.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Amphimachus comes from the Greek amphi, 'on both sides', and mache, 'battle'. The name can be roughly rendered as 'one who fights on both sides'.
