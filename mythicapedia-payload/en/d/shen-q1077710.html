shapeshifting sea dragon of Chinese mythology

Shen

The shen (or chen) is a shapeshifting sea monster from Chinese mythology, part dragon and part shellfish, believed to cause mirages. The word also refers to large clams and to the Dragon star constellation.

The myth

Early Chinese classics use shèn in two senses: first, a large shellfish linked to funerals; later, an aquatic monster that could change its shape. According to the tradition, sparrows, swallows and bats could turn into shellfish after a number of years, and pheasants entering great waters became large shellfish in winter.

The shèn belonged to the family of water dragons, and folklore held that eating swallow meat meant one should not cross rivers, since dragons would devour those who did. As a mirage, the creature was said to conjure towers and cities above the sea.

Worship and religion

Shèn shells were used in funeral rites and in sacrifices to earth spirits. A special government official was responsible for obtaining them for royal ceremonies and tombs.

Legacy

The word shèn survives in everyday East Asian terms for mirage or illusion. The name appears in the manga Naruto and in the light novel Tsukimichi: Moonlit Fantasy.

Ancient sources: Classical Chinese texts such as the Zuo Zhuan, the Rites of Zhou, the Huainanzi, the Zhuangzi and the Shuowen Jiezi.

Trivia

A constellation. The chen, or Dragon star, is an asterism in traditional Chinese constellations, associated with the east and spring.
Mirages in Japan. In Japanese and Korean, shinkirō or singiru is the usual word for mirage.
