Amaracus

Amaracus is a young Cypriot in Greek mythology who, according to tradition, was transformed into marjoram, a fragrant herb closely linked to Aphrodite.

The myth

Amaracus served as royal perfumer at the court of his father, King Cinyras, on the island of Cyprus. One day he accidentally tripped while carrying the ointments, and the mixing of the scents produced an even stronger fragrance. He was then transformed into the herb amarakon, marjoram, said to have a sweet smell and considered sacred to Aphrodite, goddess of love and beauty.

As Cinyras's son, he was the brother of Smyrna, another mortal turned into an aromatic plant connected to Aphrodite.

Worship and religion

The Greeks linked marjoram with Aphrodite, as they believed she had created it. Cyprus, the setting of the myth and a major cult center of the goddess, was famous in antiquity for its large marjoram production. Greeks and Romans decorated bridal wreaths with the herb. Today Cyprus still produces aromatic and therapeutic marjoram oils.

Trivia

Aphrodisiac and remedy. The ancients believed marjoram was a strong aphrodisiac and that it could also cure snakebites.
Bridal wreath. Both Greeks and Romans decorated brides' wreaths with marjoram.
