Athenian prince in the Erechtheid genealogy

Eupalamus

Eupalamus is a minor figure in Greek mythology, linked to the royal line of Erechtheus in Attica. His name, meaning something like "inventor", echoes that of his descendant Daedalus, whom sources make either his son or his grandson.

The myth

Eupalamus appears almost only in genealogical lists and has no stories of his own. In one version he was the son of Metion and grandson of Erechtheus; in another, Erechtheus was the son of Praxithea and Metion was the father of Daedalus, which leaves Eupalamus out of the direct line.

According to the genealogy in the sources, Eupalamus was married to Alcippe, and their children were Daedalus, the daughters Perdix and Metiadusa, the latter the wife of King Cecrops. Diodorus Siculus presents Daedalus as a member of the Erechtheidae, the Athenian royal family.

Legacy

His best-known role is as a genealogical link to Daedalus, the legendary craftsman. His telling name hints at the inventive character later attributed to his line.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Erechtheid genealogy preserved by Diodorus Siculus, in reference to Daedalus.
