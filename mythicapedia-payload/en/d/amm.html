moon god of ancient Qataban

ʿAmm

ʿAmm was a moon god worshipped in Qataban, a kingdom in ancient southern Yemen. His name comes from the Arabic word for paternal uncle, and the people of the kingdom called themselves the Banu Amm, or “Children of Amm.”

The myth

Surviving sources offer few extended stories about ʿAmm, so he is known mostly through his attributes. Beyond the moon, he was linked to the weather: lightning bolts were among his symbols, which made him a storm god as well. His consort was the goddess Asherah.

Worship and religion

He was served by the oracle-judge Anbay, and he was venerated throughout the period of the Qataban kingdom. Little is recorded about specific rites. A hypothesis by scholar Daniel E. Fleming suggests that the name may have begun as a title for a forgotten deity once central to an earlier people, whose true name was lost.

Trivia

A kinship name. “Amm” means paternal uncle in Arabic, so his name points to a family bond rather than a divine title.
A possibly forgotten god. According to Fleming, “Amm” may originally have been a title for a deity whose true name was lost.
