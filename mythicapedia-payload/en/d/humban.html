Elamite god of kingship

Humban

Humban was the chief god of Elam, an ancient region in southwestern Iran. His cult grew mainly in the Neo-Elamite period, when many kings bore names honouring him.

The myth

The texts hardly narrate episodes about Humban; his traits are known mostly from inscriptions and administrative documents. He was linked to kitin, the divine protection granted to rulers, and was called the greatest of the gods.

Worship and religion

He already appears in the treaty of Naram-Sin, around 2200 BCE, and Untash-Napirisha built him a temple at Chogha Zanbil. In the Achaemenid Persepolis archives he receives more offerings than any other god, including Ahura Mazda, and he had priests and a high priestess.

Legacy

Several Neo-Elamite kings took names including his, such as Humban-haltash III, and the place name Til Humba, "hill of Humban", recalls his presence. Neo-Assyrian scholars equated him with Enlil.

Ancient sources: He appears in the treaty of Naram-Sin, in the inscription of king Shilhak-Inshushinak I, and in the Persepolis fortification archives, as well as in Neo-Assyrian commentaries on the Šurpu series.

Trivia

More than Ahura Mazda. In the Persepolis archives, Humban appears twenty-six times, compared with ten for Auramazdā, the Old Persian form of Ahura Mazda.
Not to be confused with Jumban. Another Elamite god, Jumban, is often confused with Humban, but scholars treat them separately.
