Chinese god of marriage and love

Yue Lao

Yue Lao is the Chinese god of marriage and love, depicted as an old man who appears beneath the moon. His story gave rise to the image of the red thread of fate, which binds predestined couples together.

The myth

According to tradition, the old man secretly binds predestined couples with a silken cord, and nothing can prevent their union afterwards. He is said to live either on the moon or in the obscure regions (Yue ming), the Chinese equivalent of Hades.

A legend from the Tang dynasty tells how a young man named Wei Gu came across the old man reading a book of marriages by moonlight. The old man pointed to a little girl carried by a blind woman and declared she would be Wei Gu's future wife. Doubting him, Wei Gu ordered a servant to stab the child. Fourteen years later, the girl, now bearing a scar on her back, married him.

Legacy

He has inspired modern works: he appears in Robert W. Chambers' short story The Maker of Moons (1896), in the TV series Ashes of Love, and in Grace Lin's novel Where the Mountain Meets the Moon. He is also a playable character in the mobile game Dislyte.

Trivia

An invisible cord. In the legend, the thread that binds couples is made of silk and stays invisible, yet nothing can break it.
An old man with a book. In the tale, the old man is seen reading by moonlight a register of who will marry whom.
