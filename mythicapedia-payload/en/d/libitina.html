Roman goddess of funerals and death

Libitina

Libitina was a goddess of Roman mythology responsible for the duties owed to the dead, including burial and funerals. Her name came to be used as a synonym for death, a sign of how central her role was in Roman life.

The myth

Libitina has no story of her own. No legends or heroic deeds are attributed to her, and her role is defined by her care for the dead and their funeral rites.

Tradition credited Servius Tullius with establishing the first tombs, the equipment needed for funerals, the undertakers, and the first temples dedicated to the goddess.

The origin of her name is debated. Varro linked it to the Latin verb lubere, "to be pleasing", and to libido, which would explain her connection with Venus, sometimes called Venus Libitina or Lubentina. Some modern scholars believe the name comes from the Etruscan lupu-, "to die", and that the goddess had Etruscan roots, perhaps linked to the deity Alpanu.

Worship and religion

Her sanctuary was a sacred grove, or lucus, where the guild of funeral directors, the libitinarii, gathered. Her temple collected a small coin for each death, a death tax that was also attributed to Servius Tullius. The anniversary of the temple's founding was celebrated on August 19, the day of the Vinalia Rustica. During a plague in 65 AD, some 30,000 deaths were recorded there.

Legacy

Her name became a synonym for death, as Horace recalls, and gave rise to the term libitinarii for funeral directors. Some authors also associated her with Venus, and in certain sources her name appears as an epithet of that goddess.

Ancient sources: Horace (Odes, III, 30) refers to her name as a synonym for death, Livy records occasions when the dead exceeded the temple's capacity, and Varro offers a Latin etymology for her name.

Trivia

Death tax. According to tradition, Libitina's temple collected a coin for every death, a kind of funeral tax.
The gate of the dead. In the Roman Colosseum, the Porta libitinaria was the exit through which the bodies of gladiators and animals were carried out.
