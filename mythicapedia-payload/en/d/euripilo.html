king of Cos, son of Poseidon

Eurypylus

Eurypylus was, in Greek myth, a king of the island of Cos and a son of Poseidon. His story is tied to Heracles' arrival on the island, which ended with his death.

The myth

According to the myth, Eurypylus was the son of Poseidon and Astypalaea, though some accounts name Mestra as his mother. He led his subjects on Cos when Heracles' ships, driven off course by a storm, approached the island. The Coans, mistaking them for pirates, threw stones to stop them landing. Heracles nonetheless reached the shore, entered the city at night and killed Eurypylus.

Eurypylus was married to Clytie and was the father of Chalciope, Antagoras and Chalcon. Chalciope became the mother of a son by Heracles, whose name is given as Thessalus in some tellings and as Tetalus in others.

Trivia

A mistaken attack. The Coans took Heracles and his companions for pirates and pelted them with stones as they tried to land. The confusion ended in battle.
Another version of the attack. According to an alternative account, Heracles planned the assault on Cos because he was attracted to Eurypylus' daughter Chalciope and meant to abduct her.
