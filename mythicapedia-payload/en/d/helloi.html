female nature spirit of Meitei mythology

Helloi

Helloi are female nature spirits in Meitei mythology, from Manipur in north-eastern India. They are usually depicted as beautiful young women, are associated with seducing men, and are considered the most powerful of the female spirits.

The myth

According to Meitei tradition, hellois dwell in dense thickets, dark forests, rivers and meadows. They can spread diseases, especially sexually transmitted infections, and cause mental disorder. Attacks are believed to peak in March and April, during the spring festival of Yaoshang.

Unlike the hingchabi, who live inside some women, hellois are wandering spirits, and they may not easily leave a victim until their demands are met.

Worship and religion

Maibas and maibis, ritual specialists, perform rites to prevent attacks and the attraction of men. To appease the spirits, offerings of animal faeces and specially prepared ritual foods are made.

Legacy

They appear in theatre in Lairembigee Eshei, directed by Ratan Thiyam, and in film in HELLOY, directed by G. Narayan Sharma, and Dr. Hemogi Helloi (2013), directed by Homen D' Wai.

Trivia

Two kinds of spirit. Hellois are distinguished from hingchabis: the former wander freely, while the latter live inside the bodies of some women.
A love story. In the 2013 film Dr. Hemogi Helloi, a helloi has a love affair with a man named Dr. Hemo.
