phantom island in the North Pacific

Ganges Island

Phantom island in the North Pacific that appeared on maps of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, supposedly between Midway and Japan. Its story shows how a mapping error can remain a claimed territory for decades.

The myth

The island first appeared on nautical charts in 1826 under the name Ganges, probably after the Nantucket whaler that sailed the Pacific between 1815 and 1851. Several positions were assigned to the same land, and some navigators listed it as doubtful, until an 1875 revision by the U.S. Admiralty removed it from the charts.

In the early twentieth century, Teizaburo Yamada of Tokyo claimed to have discovered an island about 560 nautical miles from the Ogasawara Islands, and in 1908 the Japanese government incorporated it into its territory as Nakanotori. However, later expeditions, such as the 1927 survey, failed to find it, and in 1946 a Japanese hydrographic notice declared it nonexistent.

Trivia

Millions of albatrosses. Yamada's report described several million albatrosses on the island, which was odd, since albatrosses did not fly there at that time of year.
Strategically valued phosphate. According to the report, the island was covered with phosphorite, a mineral formed from accumulated guano, which was then considered valuable as a gunpowder material and fertilizer.
