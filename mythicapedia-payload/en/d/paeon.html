hero of Greek mythology, lord of Messenia

Paeon

Paeon, also called Paion, is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Antilochus and lord of Messenia. He matters because, according to Pausanias, his descendants gave their name to the Attic deme of the Paeonidae.

The myth

According to tradition, Paeon was the son of Antilochus, one of Helen's suitors who fought in the Trojan War alongside his father Nestor, king of Pylos, and his brother Thrasymedes. Paeon appears as a lord of Messenia, within the line of Neleus, the Neleidae.

The second-century geographer Pausanias explains that Paeon's sons were among the Neleidae driven out of Messenia by the descendants of Heracles, in the legendary Return of the Heracleidae. Together with other Neleidae, such as Alcmaeon and Melanthus, they fled to Athens. Pausanias adds that the Attic clan and deme of the Paeonidae took their name from Paeon. Herodotus places the Paeonia below the Attic fortress of Leipsydrium, probably the same deme.

Ancient sources: Pausanias (2nd century AD) mentions him in his account of the Neleidae, and Herodotus places the Attic Paeonia near Leipsydrium.

Trivia

An Attic deme with a heroic name. The deme of the Paeonidae in Attica is said by Pausanias to have taken its name from this figure.
