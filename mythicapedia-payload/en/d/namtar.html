underworld servant and bringer of disease in Mesopotamian myth

Namtar

Namtar is a being of the Mesopotamian underworld, the trusted messenger and attendant of the goddess Ereshkigal, and was held responsible for disease and death. Feared yet rarely worshipped, he shows how the peoples of Mesopotamia pictured the power of sickness and fate.

The myth

According to tradition, Namtar was the sukkal, or trusted attendant, of Ereshkigal, queen of the underworld. In the myth Nergal and Ereshkigal, the goddess could not climb to heaven, so she sent him in her place to a banquet of the gods. Most of them showed him respect, but Nergal refused, and Ereshkigal demanded that he be punished by being sent down to the underworld. Later Namtar returned to heaven to bring Nergal back, after Nergal had escaped.

In Ishtar's Descent, Namtar inflicts sixty illnesses on the goddess, affecting every part of her body, and then leads her back to the world of the living so that she can find a substitute. Texts also link him to headaches and heart pain, and incantations pair him with the demon Asag as one of the most dangerous sources of disease. Accounts of his parentage differ: god lists give his mother as Mardula'anki, and his wife is Hušbišag, called the "stewardess of the underworld."

Worship and religion

Namtar had little active cult, as he is absent from the offering lists. Offerings to him appear only in a few literary texts, such as The Death of Gilgamesh and The Death of Ur-Namma. To counter his influence, the healing goddess Ninisina was invoked. No modern religious practice connected to him survives.

Legacy

His name, meaning "fate" in Sumerian, makes him a personification of unavoidable death, comparable to Mot in the Ugaritic texts. Today he appears in the thriller novel Namtar: El Juicio Final by J. Germán Salazar, and as a concept in Tibetan spiritual biography.

Ancient sources: He appears in Ishtar's Descent, in the myth of Nergal and Ereshkigal, in Atrahasis, in Enki and Ninmah and in the Udug-hul incantations.

Trivia

A name meaning fate. In Sumerian, namtar means "fate". Texts distinguish him from the ordinary word by the divine sign placed before his name.
