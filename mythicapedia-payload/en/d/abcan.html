dwarf poet and musician of the Tuatha Dé Danann

Abcán

Abcán is a dwarf poet and musician in Irish mythology, serving the Tuatha Dé Danann, the early Celtic divinities of Ireland. He appears in tales about the goddess Ruad and the hero Cúchulainn.

The myth

The myth first places him in the story of the death of the goddess Ruad. Abcán, a dwarf, ferries her from the Otherworld to the real world so that she can seduce a mortal, Aed Srónmár. For this journey he has a bronze boat with a tin sail. According to the tradition, the song of mermaids or, in some versions, music from a fairy mound (Aes Side) makes Ruad leap into the water and drown.

In another episode, Abcán is captured by the hero Cúchulainn. He frees himself by playing lullabies so irresistible that the warrior falls into a deep sleep. Some scholars point to his strong similarities with the dwarf musician Fer Í and suggest that the two may be the same figure under different names.

Trivia

A bronze boat. Abcán is described with a bronze boat and a tin sail, an unusual detail in stories of this kind.
Possible double of Fer Í. His resemblance to the dwarf musician Fer Í has led some scholars to see him as a variant of the same character.
