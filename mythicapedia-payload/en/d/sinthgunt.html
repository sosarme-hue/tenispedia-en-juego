goddess in Germanic mythology, sister of the sun

Sinthgunt

Sinthgunt is a figure of Germanic mythology who appears only in the Second Merseburg Charm, as a sister of the sun. Her role is poorly known and has led to very different scholarly interpretations.

The myth

In the charm, Sinthgunt and Sól, described as sisters, are named as the ones who sang healing charms for the horse of Phol, a figure mentioned nowhere else. Friia and Uolla follow, also sisters, with alliterative names. The mention is so brief that her real role remains unknown.

Her name has been much debated. Sophus Bugge's reading in the nineteenth century suggested «the one who walks by night», which led to a link with the moon, although the moon is masculine in Norse tradition. Other proposals connect her with war or with heavenly bodies. Some scholars see her as an aspect of Sól, while others regard her as a subordinate goddess or even a valkyrie.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Second Merseburg Charm, an Old High German text from the ninth or tenth century.

Trivia

A valkyrie according to Kauffmann. Friedrich Kauffmann classified her as a valkyrie in the nineteenth century, because the elements -gund and -hild are common in such names.
A name of war. According to Stefan Schaffner's reconstruction, her name could mean something like «raid» and «fight», like other Germanic female names.
