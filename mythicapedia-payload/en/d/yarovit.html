Slavic god of war

Yarovit

Yarovit (also spelled Iarovit, Yerovit or Jarovit) is a god of war worshipped by the Polabian Slavs, who lived along the lower Elbe in what is now northern Germany. He matters because he is one of the few West Slavic deities about whom some information survives, although the details are very limited.

The myth

Little is known about Yarovit's mythology. Sources barely describe his cult, and no stories survive about his origins, family or deeds, so what we know comes down to his warlike character and a few details of his worship. Most references come from Christian writers, who did not always describe pagan beliefs accurately.

A Christian monk identified him with Mars, the Roman god of war, a sign of how clergy tried to translate the deities of the peoples they converted into familiar gods. His name has been read in several ways: the root jar- is linked either to the Proto-Slavic adjective meaning "vigorous, strong" or to the one meaning "vernal", and the suffix -vit may mean "lord, ruler" or "hero". On these readings the name has been rendered as "young master" or "strong, mighty lord".

Worship and religion

His main temple stood at Vologošč, among the Circipanians, where a golden shield belonging to Yarovit was kept. The sources do not explain its role or describe his rites, festivals or offerings in detail, and there is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Trivia

A golden shield. His temple at Vologošč housed a golden shield that was regarded as belonging to the god.
A name with several spellings. Latin and German records spell his name in different ways, and scholars explain the variation by changes in how the Slavic element jar- was written.
