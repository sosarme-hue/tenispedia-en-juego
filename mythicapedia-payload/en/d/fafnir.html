dragon of Norse and Germanic legend, once a dwarf

Fáfnir

Fáfnir is a dwarf who turns into a dragon out of greed in Norse and Germanic tradition. His story, tied to a cursed hoard and his death at the hands of Sigurd, is one of the best-known heroic legends of northern Europe.

The myth

According to the Norse sources, Fáfnir was the son of the dwarf Hreiðmarr and brother of Regin and Ótr. When Loki kills Ótr, who was in otter form, the gods must pay weregild with the treasure of the dwarf Andvari, which carries a curse. Hreiðmarr receives the gold, but his sons demand their share. Fáfnir kills his father and, greedy, refuses to share the hoard with Regin.

Fáfnir retreats to Gnitaheath and there becomes a dragon. Regin persuades the young Sigurd to kill him: the hero digs a trench on the path the beast takes to the water and stabs it from below. Before dying, the dragon speaks with Sigurd and warns him that the gold will bring his death.

Regin then asks Sigurd to cook the dragon's heart. Tasting it, Sigurd burns his finger and sucks it; the blood lets him understand birds that reveal Regin's treachery. Sigurd kills Regin and takes the treasure.

Legacy

Richard Wagner makes Fafner a character in Der Ring des Nibelungen, where he is the brother of Fasolt. J. R. R. Tolkien drew on the tale for elements of Smaug and Gollum. The Swedish band Amon Amarth dedicated the track «Fafner's Gold» to the opening of their 2019 album Berserker. He also appears often in manga, anime and video games.

Ancient sources: The story appears in the Reginsmál and Fáfnismál of the Poetic Edda, in the Völsunga saga, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

Trivia

A name with a double meaning. His name is often translated as «the embracer», from a verb meaning to embrace. It has also been read as «the one of many fathoms», referring to his enormous length.
An inspiration for Tolkien. J. R. R. Tolkien drew on the tale of Fáfnir when shaping his dragon Smaug and elements of Gollum.
