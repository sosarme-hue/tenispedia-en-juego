Armenian god of strength and the arts

Tork Angegh

Tork Angegh is a god of strength, courage, craftsmanship and the arts in Armenian mythology, known for his rough appearance and enormous power. He is considered one of the important deities of the Armenian pantheon from before Iranian and Hellenic influence reached it.

The myth

According to tradition, Tork was a shepherd unlike any other, so strong that lions and tigers took him for their king. One spring he was overcome with sadness, convinced that no one could love a giant as ugly as he was. The goddess Astghik saw him from the sky, came down to him and told him about Haykanush, a beautiful maiden who could love him. They fell in love and his sadness vanished.

Tork used neither sword nor arrows; his appearance alone terrified his enemies. When invaders came by ship to the shore of the Pontic Sea and attacked Armenian settlements, they fled at the sight of him. Furious, he tore off rocks the size of hills and hurled them into the water, and the surges they raised sank the enemy fleet.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned by the Armenian historian Movses Khorenatsi, author of a history of Armenia.

Trivia

Origin of the name. His name is thought to combine Tarku, an ancient Anatolian deity of fertility, and Angegh, a god worshipped mainly in the province of Angegh in south-western Armenia.
A name in the Bible. In the ancient Armenian translation of the Bible, the name of the Babylonian god Nargal is rendered as Angegh.
