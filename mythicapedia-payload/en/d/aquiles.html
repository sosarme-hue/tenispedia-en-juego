Greek hero of the Trojan War

Achilles

Achilles is the most famous hero of the Trojan War in Greek mythology, the central character of Homer's Iliad and regarded as the greatest warrior among the Greeks. His story matters because it embodies the Greek heroic ideal, a tension between glory, anger and a tragic fate.

The myth

According to the tradition, Achilles was the son of Peleus, a mortal king of the Myrmidons in Phthia, and of the Nereid Thetis. Zeus and Poseidon had both sought Thetis's hand, but a prophecy attributed to Themis warned that her son would surpass his father, so the goddess was married off to Peleus. In Homer, Achilles grew up in Phthia alongside his childhood companion Patroclus, and the centaur Chiron educated them both. The seer Calchas foretold that the boy would have to choose between a short, glorious life and a long, obscure one, and Achilles chose glory.

The Iliad focuses on his quarrel with Agamemnon near the end of the war, which keeps him away from battle and leads to events that culminate in the death of the Trojan hero Hector outside the walls of Troy. The poem does not narrate Achilles's own death, but other sources agree that he fell near the end of the war, shot by an arrow from Paris. His ghost appears in the Odyssey, where he speaks about his own death.

The famous invulnerability of Achilles, except for one heel, appears only in later sources. According to Statius's unfinished epic Achilleid, Thetis tried to make him immortal by dipping him in the river Styx, but held him by one heel, which the water never touched. This version is not found in sources before Statius. In the Iliad, Achilles is wounded in the arm by the Paeonian hero Asteropaeus, and the fragmentary poems of the Epic Cycle that describe his death make no mention of a heel.

Other traditions say that Thetis hid him at the court of Lycomedes on Scyros, where he lived disguised as a girl under the name Pyrrha and had a son, Neoptolemus, with Deidamia. According to this story, Odysseus exposed him by placing a shield and a spear among the gifts, and Achilles revealed himself by seizing the weapons.

Legacy

The phrase "Achilles' heel" refers to a point of weakness in someone or something otherwise strong, and the "Achilles tendon" in anatomy takes its name from the same legend. The story inspired Statius's unfinished epic Achilleid, written in the first century AD, and Achilles remains one of the most recurrent figures in Western literature and art, especially in the tradition of the Iliad.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad and Odyssey; Statius's Achilleid; Apollonius's Argonautica, Euripides's Iphigenia at Aulis and the fragmentary poems of the Trojan cycle.

Trivia

A name with meaning. According to modern analysis of his name, Achilles might mean something like "he who distresses the people", a theme repeated in the Iliad that contrasts with his fame as a hero of glory.
A wound and a spear. According to one tradition, Achilles wounded Telephus, and the wound healed only when shavings from the spear that caused it were scraped onto it, an example of sympathetic magic.
