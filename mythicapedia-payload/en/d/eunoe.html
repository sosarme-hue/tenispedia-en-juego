Naiad nymph of Greek mythology

Eunoë

Eunoë is a naiad nymph of Greek mythology, daughter of the river god Sangarius and wife of the Phrygian king Dymas, with whom she became the mother of Hecuba, later queen of Troy. Her name means good understanding, and it also lends its name to a river on Dante's Purgatory.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Eunoë was a daughter of the river god Sangarius, a river of Phrygia. She married King Dymas and bore Hecuba, who would later wed Priam and become queen of Troy.

Some accounts give a different version, in which Hecuba's mother was not Eunoë but the naiad Euagora.

Legacy

Dante includes her river in the Divine Comedy: the Eunoë crowns the summit of Mount Purgatory, and its waters make good deeds be remembered, while the waters of Lethe erase the wrongs committed. Before climbing to Paradise, the poet must drink from it.

Trivia

Twin rivers. In Purgatory, Lethe makes one forget wrongs and Eunoë makes one remember good deeds. Dante must drink from both before following Beatrice toward Paradise.
A telling name. Her Greek name means good understanding, a meaning that fits the role of her river in Dante's work.
