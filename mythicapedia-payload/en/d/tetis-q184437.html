Greek sea goddess, mother of Achilles

Thetis

Thetis is a sea nymph and one of the Nereids, the fifty daughters of the sea god Nereus and Doris, and one of the most important marine divinities of Greek mythology. She is best known as the mother of Achilles, the central hero of Homer's Iliad.

The myth

According to Hesiod, silver-footed Thetis married Peleus and bore Achilles, while other accounts show her among the Nereids who lived with their father in the depths of the sea. Her epithet Halosydne, "sea-nourished", links her to her sister Amphitrite. Ancient authors disagree about her ancestry: some make her a daughter of Chiron, and a late version calls her the daughter of the older Tethys, wife of Oceanus.

Zeus and Poseidon both courted her, but Themis, or Prometheus according to others, prophesied that her son would be greater than his father, and both gave up. Peleus won her with the help of Chiron, who advised him to hold her fast while she changed into fire, water and beasts until she returned to her true form. The wedding on Mount Pelion gathered the gods. Eris, who had not been invited, threw the golden apple of discord, which led to the judgment of Paris and the Trojan War.

Thetis tried to make Achilles immortal by burning away his mortal part in the fire, until Peleus interrupted her and she left for the sea. In Statius's version she dipped him in the Styx, but the heel she held him by stayed unprotected. In the Iliad, she begs Zeus to honour Achilles, and after his death she carries him to the White Island of Leuke. She also sheltered gods in need: she raised Hephaestus with Eurynome and gave refuge to Dionysus in the Erythraean Sea.

Worship and religion

Sanctuaries of Thetis existed in Sparta, on the coasts of Euboea and on a headland in Calabria. Pausanias documents her worship in historical times, and an archaic fragment and a hymn attributed to Alcman present her as the creator of the universe. Her name has been linked to the verb tithemi, "to establish", suggesting an early political role for Classical Greeks. Cult of Achilles on Leuke lasted into historical times.

Legacy

Her figure lives on mainly in literature through her wedding to Peleus and her motherhood of Achilles, episodes that set off the Trojan War.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony, Homer's Iliad, Alcman, Pindar, Apollonius of Rhodes's Argonautica, Statius's Achilleid and the Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus.

Trivia

The apple of discord. Eris, left out of the wedding of Thetis and Peleus, threw a golden apple marked "for the fairest". The dispute it caused led to the judgment of Paris.
A beauty contest. In one tale, Thetis and Medea argued in Thessaly over who was more beautiful. The Cretan Idomeneus gave the victory to Thetis, and Medea cursed all Cretans never to speak the truth.
