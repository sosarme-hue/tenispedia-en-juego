storm creatures of Scottish folklore

Blue men of the Minch

The blue men of the Minch are creatures of Scottish folklore who inhabit the strait between the Outer Hebrides and the mainland. They are seen as lords of the storms and sinkers of ships, and the belief is confined almost entirely to this area.

The myth

According to tradition, they are beings of human appearance and roughly human size, with blue skin, able to whip up violent storms. When the weather is fine they sleep floating just below the surface and swim with their torsos above the water, diving like porpoises. Their chief, sometimes called Shony, challenges a ship's captain to complete two lines of poetry; if he fails, the blue men try to capsize the vessel. One tale tells how sailors captured one of them, who escaped when he heard the voices of his companions.

As for their origin, several explanations have been proposed: a personification of the sea itself, the painted Picts seen from small boats, or North African slaves abandoned by the Vikings near the Shiant Isles.

Trivia

Night matches. For fun, these creatures play shinty on clear, bright nights.
The Current of Destruction. The water around the Shiant Isles is known as the Current of Destruction because of the number of shipwrecks there.
