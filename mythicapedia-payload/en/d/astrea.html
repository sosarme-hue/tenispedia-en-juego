Greek goddess of justice

Astraea

Astraea, also called Dike, is the goddess of justice in Greek mythology. According to tradition, she lived among humans during the Golden Age and eventually became the constellation Virgo, a story that explains the loss of human innocence and has served as a political and artistic symbol for centuries.

The myth

According to tradition, Dike lived among mortals during the Golden Age, when there were no wars, people did not yet sail the seas, and harvests came without effort. When greed spread, the goddess reproached humanity for its conduct and withdrew to the hills, trying in vain to correct people with threats and shame.

Her genealogy varies between authors. Hesiod makes her a daughter of Zeus and Themis, linking her to the Horae and the Moirai; Aratus and the Roman poets, by contrast, make her a daughter of Astraeus and Eos. Hesiod also prophesied that Dike and Nemesis would leave the earth at the end of the Iron Age.

In the end, weary of the wickedness of the Bronze Age, the goddess rose to the sky and became the constellation Virgo; the scales she carried gave rise to Libra. Ovid, Juvenal and Seneca tell of her departure, in some accounts accompanied by Pudicitia, the sister who left with her.

Legacy

Her name was given to the main-belt asteroid 5 Astraea, and it was also suggested for the planet Uranus. During the English Renaissance she was identified with Queen Elizabeth I, the virgin queen presiding over a new Golden Age, and in Spain with the reign of Philip IV.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony and Works and Days), Aratus (Phaenomena), Ovid, Lucan, Statius, Juvenal and Seneca, among others.
