Mesopotamian goddess of meat preparation

Ninšar

Ninšar is a Mesopotamian goddess linked to the preparation of meat, whom texts call the « butcher of Ekur ». Her cult centred on Nippur, and she had shrines in several Sumerian cities.

The myth

In Sumerian tradition, Ninšar is the daughter of Enki and Ninhursag, and with Enki she becomes the mother of Ninkurra. She takes part in the myth of Enki and Ninhursag, in which Ninhursag creates deities to heal Enki after his drunkenness and his union with Uttu.

In the myth of Enki and Ninmah she is one of the seven assistants of Ninmah, alongside Ninimma, Shuzianna, Ninmug, Ninmada, Mumudu and Ninniginna. In a text tied to temple renovation, The First Brick, Ea shapes her from clay among the beings who supply humans with goods to offer the gods. A fragment from Abu Salabikh shows her slaughtering cattle and sheep while Ninkasi brews beer.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped in Nippur, Shuruppak, Lagash and Ur, where Shulgi built a temple for her. A shrine stood in Nippur in the Kassite and Middle Babylonian periods. The Zame Hymns place her cult centre at AB.NAGAR.

Ancient sources: She appears in the god list An = Anum, the Canonical Temple List, the Zame Hymns, and in the myths of Enki and Ninmah and Enki and Ninhursag.

Trivia

A disputed name. Her name is usually rendered as « Lady Greenery », but scholars disagree on its reading, and its meaning bears no clear link to her known roles.
Not to be confused. Her name is written the same as that of another deity, dnin-šár, « Lady of All », who appears with Enšar among the ancestors of Enlil.
