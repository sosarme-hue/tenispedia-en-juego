nine-headed pheasant spirit (yaojing) from the Chinese novel Investiture of the Gods

Jiutou Zhiji Jing

Jiutou Zhiji Jing, also called Hu Ximei, is a yaojing, a spirit monster of Chinese myth, said to come from a nine-headed pheasant. She appears in the classic novel Investiture of the Gods, where she is Daji's sister and one of the story's doomed spirits.

The myth

According to the novel, Jiutou Zhiji Jing is a yaojing, a spirit monster said to derive from a nine-headed pheasant. Her name is usually rendered as roughly 'nine-headed pheasant spirit', and she is also known as Hu Ximei, 'splendor'. She was described in a large red robe, a silk sash and eyes like an autumn lake.

Zhiji Jing joined her sister Daji at the tomb of the Yellow Emperor to gather their fox companions. They planned to appear at a banquet disguised as heavenly maidens to deceive the king. When Vice Prime Minister Bi Gan exposed their true forms, the spirits returned to their original shapes and Huang Feihu set their home alight, killing nearly all of them. Only Zhiji Jing survived.

Daji returned to the tomb, found her alive and the two went back to Zhaoge. There, disguised as an even more beautiful woman, Zhiji Jing played along with her sister's scheme and spent time with King Zhou. Soon she chose to stay at the palace rather than live in the mountains, enjoying its luxuries and wine. Near the end of the novel she tried to flee with her sisters as Zhaoge fell, but was captured by Jiang Ziya's men and executed by Erlang Shen.

Ancient sources: She appears in the classic Chinese novel Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi), which places her alongside Daji and Pipa Jing under the authority of Nu Wa.

Trivia

Avian origin. Her origin is linked to the mythical nine-headed bird, from which her animal form is said to derive.
Daji's sister. She is one of three spirits under Nu Wa, alongside Daji and Pipa Jing, and joins Daji in her schemes at court.
