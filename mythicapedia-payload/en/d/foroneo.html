first king of Argos, Greek culture hero

Phoroneus

Phoroneus is the first king of the Argolid, in the Peloponnese, and a culture hero. Tradition credits him with bringing fire, founding the first city and establishing the earliest laws.

The myth

According to tradition, Phoroneus was the son of the river god Inachus and an Oceanid, called Melia or Argia depending on the source. Before him, the people of the region lived scattered in isolated families. Phoroneus gathered them into a community, taught them to make fire and introduced them to the worship of Hera. He also founded the city of Foroneus, which his grandson would rename Argos.

Accounts add that Zeus allowed him to rule over humans because of his piety. During his reign, Hermes caused the confusion of tongues, which stirred up Eris, Discord, who split mortals into nations. According to Hellanicus of Lesbos, he had three sons, Agenor, Iasus and Pelasgus, who divided the land between them. The youngest, Agenor, ended up ruling Argos. His tomb received funeral sacrifices.

Worship and religion

In Argos he was honoured as a hero. His tomb, which served as a sanctuary, received funeral offerings, and a perpetual fire burned there that Pausanias still saw in the 2nd century CE.

Legacy

His descendants Amphiaraus and Adrastus received the patronymic Phoronids, the name by which the people of Argos were known. The epic poem Phoronis called him the "father of mortals".

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Hyginus, Pausanias and Pliny mention him, along with the epic poem Phoronis.

Trivia

The oldest king of Greece. Pliny called him the oldest king of Greece, and the epic poem Phoronis called him "the first of humans".
The confusion of tongues. According to tradition, the diversity of languages arrived during his reign, the work of Hermes, and with it came discord among peoples.
