Megarian princess of Greek mythology

Pylia

Pylia is, in Greek mythology, a princess of Megara, daughter of King Pylas. She is remembered as the mother of several sons of Pandion II, among them Aegeus, father of Theseus, which places her at the origin of the Athenian royal line.

The myth

According to tradition, Pylia was the daughter of Pylas, king of Megara, though sources give other names for her father, such as Pylon or Pylos. Some authors call her Peleia. Pandion, expelled from Athens by the sons of Metion after a civil uprising, fled to Megara and, before Pylas, married her. Later, Pylas handed over the throne to him, so Pandion came to rule the city.

With Pylia, Pandion had Aegeus, Pallas, Nisus and Lycus. Tzetzes attributes the birth of Aegeus to Pandion and Peleia, and says Aegeus later fathered Theseus by another woman. Pausanias, by contrast, traces a longer lineage linking Pylas to Lelex and Sciron, and suggests that Sciron's wife, a daughter of Pandion, may also have been Pylia's daughter.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and Tzetzes record the versions of her genealogy.
