home of the elves in Norse mythology

Alfheim

Alfheim, or Álfheimr, is in Norse mythology one of the nine worlds contained within the cosmic ash Yggdrasil and the home of the elves. Its name means "home of the elves", and the sources connect it above all with the light elves.

The myth

Old Norse texts mention Alfheim only twice, so many details of this realm remain vague. In the poem Grímnismál, preserved in the Poetic Edda, it appears as the dwelling of the god Freyr, without the text making clear how it relates to the elves. Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning, instead places it first in a series of heavenly abodes and presents it as the home of the light elves, or ljósálfar.

According to these sources, the elves were divided into two groups. The light elves lived in Alfheim, while the dark elves, or svartálfar, dwelt inside the mountains. Both were blood relations, though they pursued different ends. Later, Snorri describes a hall called Gimlé, in the highest heaven, which will survive when heaven and earth have perished.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Poetic Edda (Grímnismál, stanza 5), where it is described as the dwelling of Freyr, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning, chapter 17), where it is placed as the home of the light elves.

Trivia

A rare mention. Alfheim appears only twice in the Old Norse texts, making it one of the least described worlds in the cosmology.
Two kinds of elves. The sources distinguish between light elves, who live in Alfheim, and dark elves, who dwell inside the mountains.
