sacred stone of Russian folklore

Alatyr

Alatyr is a sacred stone in East Slavic folklore, called the father of all stones and the navel of the earth. Its name appears only in East Slavic sources, though the idea of such a stone is known across much of the Slavic world.

The myth

According to the Dove Book, Alatyr stands at the navel of the world, in the middle of the ocean, on the island of Buyan, and the World Tree rises from it. The stone has healing and magical powers, and from beneath the white stone flows a spring that gives the world food and healing. It is guarded by the wise snake Garafena and the bird Gagana.

Its etymology remains uncertain. It has been compared with the word altar, with the town of Alatyr, and with the Russian word for amber, yantar.

Legacy

It appears in Russian, Latvian and Belarusian healing charms, where a raven carries the illness to the stone. Writers K. Balmont (1906) and Yevgeny Zamyatin (1914) both used it.

Ancient sources: The Dove Book and Russian spiritual verses.

Trivia

A stone with many names. It is also called the white stone or the blue stone, and some ancient tales speak of a white burning stone on Buyan, possibly the same stone.
A raven as intermediary. In some healing charms, the raven takes the patient's illness away and places it on a white or grey stone.
