Hindu god of war and the planet Mars

Mangala

Mangala is the Hindu deity of anger, aggression and war, and the Sanskrit name of the planet Mars. He links mythology with astrology and with the name of Tuesday in the Hindu calendar.

The myth

According to Vaishnavism, Mangala is the son of Bhumi, the earth goddess, and Vishnu. The story goes that the asura Hiranyaksha dragged Bhumi into the depths of the primordial waters. Vishnu, in his boar avatar Varaha, lifted her with his tusks, slew the asura and restored her to her place in the cosmos. As she rose, Vishnu recognised her as an aspect of his consort Lakshmi, and from their union Mangala was born, whose name means "the auspicious one".

In Shaivism his origin is different: while Shiva meditated on Mount Kailash, three drops of his sweat fell to Earth, and from them a reddish-complexioned child with four arms appeared. Shiva gave the child to Mother Earth, who raised him under the name Bhauma.

He is shown red or flame-coloured, with four arms, carrying a trident, mace, lotus and spear, and riding a ram. His names, such as Raktavarna, Kuja or Dharaputra, refer to his colour and his earthly birth.

Worship and religion

Mangala is among the deities who have dedicated temples at various Hindu pilgrimage sites, which devotees visit on yatra. He is part of the navagraha, the nine celestial bodies honoured and consulted in jyotisha astrology. His day is Tuesday (mangalavara).

Legacy

The Sanskrit name of the planet Mars comes from Mangala, and his day gives its name to Tuesday in the Hindu tradition. He appears in astronomical treatises such as the Aryabhatiya and the Khandakhadyaka, and the Markandeya Purana includes the Mangala Kavacha Stotram, a prayer for protection. Astrology regards him as auspicious in name but malefic in influence.

Ancient sources: He appears in Sanskrit Hindu literature such as the Markandeya Purana and in later astronomical treatises; the name Mangala is older and already occurs in the Rigveda.
