Book of Thoth

The Book of Thoth is the name given to several ancient Egyptian texts attributed to Thoth, the god of writing and knowledge. Its best-known story, preserved on Ptolemaic-era papyri, tells how a magical book fell into human hands and brought a chain of disasters.

The myth

According to the tale, the book was hidden at the bottom of the Nile near Coptos, locked in a series of boxes guarded by serpents. The Egyptian prince Neferkaptah overcame the serpents and retrieved it, but the gods punished the theft by taking his wife and son. Grief-stricken, he took his own life and was buried with the book.

Generations later, Setne Khamwas stole the volume from the tomb despite the protests of Neferkaptah's ghost. After a series of visions that ended with the deaths of his children, he discovered that everything had been an illusion created by the dead man, and returned the book to its tomb. The book held two spells: one allowed the reader to understand animals, the other to perceive the gods themselves. The story expresses the belief that divine knowledge is not meant for humans.

Legacy

From the eighteenth century the name became linked to the tarot. Etteilla founded a society under this name, and Aleister Crowley published a book on the Thoth tarot in 1944. The book appears in novels by Rick Riordan and Ishmael Reed, in the work of H. P. Lovecraft, in the video game Civilization VI and in the series American Gods.

Ancient sources: Manetho credited Thoth with 36,525 books, and Clement of Alexandria mentioned forty-two priestly books that he said were written by Hermes.

Trivia

A tarot with an Egyptian name. The Thoth tarot, designed by Crowley and Frieda Harris, takes its name from this god and the book attributed to him.
All books belonged to Thoth. According to the texts, Egyptians attributed all their writings to Thoth, as the god of writing.
