female water spirit of Slavic folklore

Berehynia

Berehynia (also Bereginia) is a female spirit from Slavic folklore, originally tied to waters and, today, to the land and the home. Her modern image, very different from the old one, has become a symbol of Ukrainian identity.

The myth

In its earliest form, according to Slavic tradition, she was a dark spirit akin to naiads and rusalkas, dwelling in rivers, lakes and ponds. She was regarded as temperamental and dangerous, and the shores where she was thought to appear were avoided, especially after dark, by young men and women alike.

Since Ukraine's independence in 1991, her image has shifted: she is now identified with a blend of Mother Earth and the rusalka. Several Ukrainian writers present her as the ideal Ukrainian woman.

Legacy

She plays a part in Ukrainian nationalism, feminism and neopaganism. In 2001 a column bearing her figure was raised in Kyiv's Maidan Nezalezhnosti, the Independence Square, on the spot where a monument to Lenin had stood.

Trivia

A name with two origins. The word is easily confused with bereg, 'river bank', and with obereg, 'protective spirit', which explains its two modern meanings.
Facing the archangel. Kyiv's historic protector is Archangel Michael, who appears on the city's coat of arms, and his monument stands on the opposite side of the city, near the Liadski Gate.
