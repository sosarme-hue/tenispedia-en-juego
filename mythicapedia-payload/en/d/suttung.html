giant (jötunn) of Norse mythology

Suttungr

Suttungr is a jötunn, a giant of Norse mythology, son of Gilling and keeper of the mead of poetry. His story matters because it explains how Odin came to steal that magical mead from him.

The myth

According to the tale, Suttungr searched for his parents, who had been killed by the dwarf brothers Fjalar and Galar. He captured them and tied them to a rock that the rising tide would soon cover. To save their lives, the dwarfs offered him the magical mead, and Suttungr hid it deep inside a mountain, guarded by his daughter Gunnlöð.

Odin wanted that mead. He worked for a whole summer for Baugi, Suttungr's brother, a farmer, and then asked for a small sip. Baugi, who could not give it to him, drilled into the mountain. Odin, turned into a snake, slithered through the hole and, inside, persuaded Gunnlöð to let him have three sips in exchange for three nights with him. He drank all the mead, changed into an eagle and fled.

Suttungr, also in eagle form, chased him and nearly caught him. Odin reached Asgard, where he spat the mead into great vessels set out by the Æsir. To divert his pursuer, he let some of the drink fall to the ground, and it became the brew that makes poets bad and their verses worse.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons is named Suttungr, a name that was originally spelled Suttung.

Trivia

A moon of Saturn. One of Saturn's moons is called Suttungr, a name originally spelled Suttung.
A drink for bad poets. According to the tale, what fell to the ground when Odin fled became a drink that produces bad poets and bad verse.
