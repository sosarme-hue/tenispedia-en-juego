Myrmidon commander in Greek mythology

Eudoros

Eudoros is a hero of Greek mythology, the son of Hermes and Polymele, and one of the leading commanders of Achilles' army in the Trojan War. He is best known from Homer's Iliad, where he appears among the captains who lead Achilles' men into battle.

The myth

According to the tradition, Eudoros was a demigod, the son of Hermes and Polymele, who danced in Artemis' choir. After Polymele married Echekles, he was raised by her father Phylas. In the Iliad, Homer presents him as the second of Achilles' five commanders, leading ten companies of fifty Myrmidons, and describes him as swift and a good fighter. In Book XVI he receives more lines than any other commander except Phoenix.

Achilles tasked him with making sure Patroclus did not advance too far against the Trojans. According to Timolaus of Macedon, Eudoros was the first to fall in that battle, slain by Pyraechmes, who in turn was the first Trojan felled by Patroclus. Patroclus would later be killed by Hector at the gates of Troy.

Legacy

He appears in the 2004 film Troy, where he is played by Vincent Regan. There he is shown as Achilles' oldest friend and main mentor, and he is the one who abducts Briseis to hand her over to his friend.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad (Book XVI), which names him among Achilles' commanders.

Trivia

Son of a god. He was the son of Hermes, the divine messenger, and was raised by his grandfather Phylas after his mother married Echekles.
A dancer in Artemis' choir. According to the tradition, he danced in Artemis' choir before joining Achilles' army.
