local name of Saint George in Georgia

Tetri Giorgi

Tetri Giorgi ('White George') is the local name of Saint George in the northeastern highlands of Georgia, especially Kakheti. It matters because it blends the Christian saint with an older cult of a local lunar deity, and because it became a national emblem on Georgia's coat of arms.

The myth

The sources do not preserve a detailed independent story about Tetri Giorgi. The figure rests on Saint George, venerated in Georgia since Late Antiquity, and on a popular association with a local lunar deity whose traditions are not recorded in detail. He is shown as an armed horseman, linking him to the iconography of the warrior saint.

Worship and religion

His cult centred on Kakheti. Every 14 August a feast separate from the saint's own day drew many pilgrims from the eastern provinces, who spent the night at the main shrine, a 14th-century church overlooking the village of Atsquri.

Legacy

The name appeared on the coat of arms of the Democratic Republic of Georgia from 1918, showing the horseman beneath the Seven Celestials, and again in post-Soviet Georgia from 1991 to 2004. Several political organizations and a 1990s paramilitary unit also adopted the name.

Trivia

Coat of arms with the planets. On the 1918 coat of arms the horseman appears beneath the Seven Celestials, that is, the seven classical planets.
A separate feast. The feast of Tetri Giorgi, called tetrigiorgoba, was celebrated separately from the Christian saint's day.
