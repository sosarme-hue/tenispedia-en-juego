first human couple in Zoroastrian cosmogony

Mashya and Mashyana

Mashya and Mashyana are, in Zoroastrian cosmogony, the first man and woman, whose offspring gave rise to humankind. Their name means "mortal being" in Avestan, and their story survives mainly in the Bundahishn.

The myth

According to the myth recorded in the Bundahishn, Ohrmazd created Gayomart as his sixth creation, a primeval being who was neither male nor female. Ahriman, the Spirit of Evil dwelling in absolute darkness, sent the demoness Jeh to kill him, and she succeeded. However, the moon, Mah, captured Gayomart's seed before the creature died, and from it all animal life grew.

From Gayomart's corpse grew a tree whose seeds became the origin of plant life, and from its branches came Mashya and Mashyana. They promised to help Ohrmazd in his battle against Ahriman, and they had fifteen sets of twins, who scattered across the Earth and became the races of mankind.

Legacy

Some researchers have proposed Indo-European connections with Ask and Embla, the first couple of Norse mythology, who were also created from trees. Others in comparative religion link them to Adam and Eve.

Ancient sources: The Bundahishn, a Zoroastrian compendium that narrates creation within the Persian tradition.

Trivia

A mortal name. The original word means "mortal being" and shares its root with Old Persian martya and Persian mard, both meaning "man".
A Norse parallel. The folklorist Jacob Grimm linked the name Embla with Meshia and Meshiane, the ancient Persian names of the first couple, who were also formed from trees.
