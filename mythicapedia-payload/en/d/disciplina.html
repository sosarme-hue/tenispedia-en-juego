minor Roman goddess of discipline

Disciplina

Disciplina was a minor deity in Roman mythology, the personification of discipline. She was worshipped above all by imperial soldiers, especially those stationed along the frontiers, and she is linked to Hadrian's effort to strengthen the army's effectiveness.

The myth

Disciplina has no extensive myth like many other Roman deities. She is defined mainly by her qualities: she embodies training, self-control, determination and an orderly way of life.

According to the available sources, she was introduced by the emperor Hadrian, who wanted to improve the effectiveness of his army. Her followers were to be guided by three virtues: frugalitas, frugality with money, energy and actions; severitas, a focused conduct that is not easily dissuaded; and fidelitas, loyalty to their unit, the army, the officers and the Roman people.

Worship and religion

Altars to the goddess have been found in Great Britain and North Africa. The fort of Cilurnum, on Hadrian's Wall, was dedicated to her, as shown by a dedicatory inscription on a stone altar discovered in 1978.
