eponymous hero of the Magnetes in Greek myth

Magnes

Magnes is the eponymous hero of the Magnetes and the first king of Magnesia in Greek tradition. He is remarkable mainly for the many conflicting genealogies that ancient authors gave him, as each source assigns him different wives and children.

The myth

In the Catalogue of Women, the oldest surviving source that names him, Magnes is the son of Zeus and Thyia, daughter of Deucalion, and brother of Macedon. The two dwelt around Pieria and Olympus, and Magnes is one of several early kings born to Zeus on earth.

The Bibliotheca places him in a later generation, as the son of Aeolus and Enarete and brother of the Aeolid ancestors. In this account Magnes married an unnamed naiad, and they had Dictys and Polydectes. The mother and sons emigrated and settled the island of Seriphos, where Polydectes became king and Dictys, a fisherman, took in Danae and her son Perseus.

Later versions differ widely. A commentator on Euripides' Phoenician Women names his wife Philodice and his sons Eurynomus and Eioneus. Eustathius instead names his wife Meliboea, after whom Magnes named a town at the foot of Mount Pelion, and mentions a son Alector. Another tradition links him with the Muse Clio, who by him bore Linus, or else Hymenaios and Yalemos. Pierus, father of Hyacinth, is also called his son.

Legacy

His name survives in Magnesia, the region of Thessaly that, according to tradition, took its name from the hero.

Ancient sources: The Catalogue of Women is the oldest source naming him; other sources include the Bibliotheca, the scholia on Euripides' Phoenician Women, Eustathius and Tzetzes.

Trivia

Different fathers. In tradition Magnes has Zeus as father in the Catalogue of Women and Aeolus in the Bibliotheca, showing that no single version was fixed.
