Greek personification of poverty

Penia

Penia is the personification of poverty and need in Greek mythology. Her best-known story, preserved by Plato in the Symposium, makes her the mother of Eros with Poros, Resource.

The myth

In the myth Plato recounts in the Symposium, attributed to Socrates via the priestess Diotima, Penia came begging during the banquet of the gods held for the birth of Aphrodite.

There, Poros, son of Metis, had fallen asleep drunk on nectar in Zeus's garden. Driven by her need, Penia lay with him and conceived Eros. That is why Eros attends Aphrodite and inherits traits from both parents: always in want, yet pursuing the beautiful and good. Other ancient writers give her different connections; Aristophanes makes her a sister of Beggary.

Legacy

She appears in philosophy, above all through Plato's use of the myth to explain the nature of love. Her Greek name also lies behind terms for poverty in economic and philosophical vocabulary.

Ancient sources: Plato's Symposium; Aristophanes' Plutus; also Alcaeus, Theognis, Herodotus, Plutarch and Philostratus.

Trivia

Mother of Eros. In Plato's account, Eros is the son of Penia and Poros, inheriting his mother's lack and his father's resourcefulness.
Altars at Gadira. Philostratus says that at Gadira there were altars dedicated to Old Age, Poverty and Art.
