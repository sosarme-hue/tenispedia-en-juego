Hindu goddess, wife of Balarama

Revati

Revati is a goddess of Hindu mythology, daughter of King Kakudmi and wife of Balarama, the elder brother of Krishna. Her figure combines an ancient mother goddess capable of great destruction with a deity linked to the protection of children, fortune and prosperity.

The myth

According to the ancient texts, Revati originated as a mother goddess capable of immense destruction. When the demon Dirghajihvi threatened the devas, Skanda asked her to fight him. Taking the form of a vixen, Shalavriki, she wreaked havoc in the demon army, until the demons sought refuge in the wombs of human women. The goddess then took the form of Jataharini and attacked the demons before they could be conceived.

The tradition also tells of her marriage to Balarama. Kakudmi, unable to find a worthy suitor for his daughter, travelled with her to Brahmaloka. While they waited for Brahma to finish a performance by the gandharvas, 27 chatur-yugas passed on Earth. Brahma advised Kakudmi to give Revati to Balarama. Since she came from an earlier age, she was far taller than her husband, so Balarama shortened her by tapping her shoulder with his plough. They had two sons, Nishatha and Ulmuka, who died in the Yadu fratricidal war. Afterwards, Revati climbed onto her husband's funeral pyre.

Worship and religion

According to the Devi Bhagavata Purana, she is associated with Shasthi, an aspect of Prakriti. She was venerated as a protector of children, worshipped by childless couples, with offerings made on the sixth day after a child's birth.

Legacy

In Hindu astronomy, Revati is the last of the nakshatras, corresponding to the star Zeta Piscium in the constellation Pisces and symbolised by a fish. She is linked to the god Pushan, protector of travellers and herds.

Ancient sources: The Vishnu Purana narrates her story, and the Bhagavata Purana, the Mahabharata, the Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Balabhadra Mahatmya also mention her.

Trivia

A corrected height. Revati was much taller than Balarama because she came from an earlier age. He reduced her to the height of people in his own time by tapping her shoulder with his plough.
Different time. Time runs differently in Brahmaloka. Kakudmi thought he had waited only briefly, but 27 chatur-yugas had passed on Earth and all the suitors had died.
