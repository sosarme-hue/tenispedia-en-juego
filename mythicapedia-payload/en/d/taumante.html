sea god of Greek mythology

Thaumas

Thaumas is a sea god of Greek mythology, son of Pontus and Gaia, and father of Iris, the messenger of the gods, and of the Harpies. His name, meaning "wonder", links him to the marvels of the sea.

The myth

According to tradition, Thaumas was a son of Gaia and Pontus, the primordial god of the sea, and a full brother of Nereus, Phorcys, Ceto and Eurybia. Hesiod gives him as his wife Electra, one of the many Oceanids, the daughters of the Titans Oceanus and Tethys, and with her he fathered Iris and the Harpies.

The names of the Harpies vary from author to author. Hesiod and Apollodorus name Aello and Ocypete; Virgil mentions Celaeno; Hyginus gives a different list, and in one version the Harpies are said to be daughters of Thaumas and Ozomene. Some later writers present them as a set of three.

Other children are attributed to Thaumas outside the Hesiodic line. They include Arke, once messenger of the Titans, and the river god Hydaspes, whom the poet Nonnus lists alongside Iris. His daughters are often called Thaumantids, especially Iris, for the dazzling range of her colours.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony is the main reference; Apollodorus, Virgil, Hyginus, Nonnus and Plato also mention him.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Plato links the name Thaumas with the Greek word thauma, meaning "wonder", in an etymological exercise.
A recurring name. Thaumas was also the name of a centaur who fought against the Lapiths in the Centauromachy.
