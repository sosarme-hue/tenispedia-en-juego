sign of the Chinese zodiac

Horse

The Horse is the seventh of the twelve animals in the Chinese zodiac, a twelve-year cycle that assigns an animal to each year. It stands for triumph, victory and energy, and in popular symbolism it is also linked to loyalty and tenderness.

The myth

Folklore explains the order of the zodiac animals through a race in which each creature competed for its place in the cycle. In the tradition, the Horse takes seventh place, and that order has been passed down ever since.

The lunar calendar behind the cycle is credited in tradition to Emperor Huangdi, the first Chinese emperor, who is said to have invented it in 2637 BC. Historically, the custom of pairing an animal with each year can be traced back at least to the Han dynasty (201 BC–AD 220), and many legends and folktales surround the twelve animals. They often appear in East Asian art; figures in the Victoria and Albert Museum's collection show them with human bodies and animal heads, a style that became popular in the Tang dynasty in the 8th century.

Each Horse year also carries one of five elements: wood, fire, earth, metal or water. So 1906 was a Fire Horse year, 1990 a Metal Horse year and 2002 a Water Horse year. In astrological belief, people born in these years share the Horse's typical traits, and the Horse is considered highly compatible with the Tiger, the Dog and the Goat.

Legacy

In the Western zodiac, the sign of Sagittarius has been linked to this animal, following Greek mythology, so the Horse appears in two different astrological systems. In Chinese astrology it remains one of the best-known signs, and each new Horse year is widely discussed in calendars and popular publications. It is considered of average compatibility with the Monkey, the Snake, the Dragon, the Rabbit and another Horse, and it is not compatible with its opposite, the Rat, or with the Ox.

Trivia

Animal heads on human bodies. Chinese figures in the Victoria and Albert Museum's collection show the zodiac animals with human bodies and animal heads.
