ghost cat of Washington, D.C.

Demon Cat

The Demon Cat is a ghostly feline that, according to legend, haunts the government buildings of Washington, D.C., especially the Capitol and the White House. It is one of the best-known urban legends in the United States.

The myth

The tradition places its origin in the mid-1800s, when cats were brought into the Capitol's basement tunnels to catch rats. One of them never left, not even after its death, and supposedly made its home in the basement crypt, originally intended as a burial chamber for George Washington. It is usually described as an ordinary-sized black or tabby cat, but when alerted it swells to the size of a tiger or an elephant and pounces on its victim, vanishing before it can be caught. It is said to appear before elections and tragedies, such as the night before the assassinations of Lincoln and Kennedy.

A modern explanation, attributed to guide Steve Livengood, points to the Capitol guards: some were unqualified relatives or friends of congressmen who patrolled while drunk. One of them, lying down in a stupor, was supposedly licked by a building cat and mistook it for a giant animal.

Legacy

The name lives on in popular culture, for example in the roller derby team DC DemonCats, founded in 2006 in Washington, D.C.

Trivia

Last sighting. The last official sighting is placed in the final days of or after World War II, in the 1940s.
Poorly prepared guards. The Capitol Police had a reputation for hiring unqualified relatives of congressmen, which fed stories about careless guards.
