Egyptian goddess of joy and celebration

Meret

Meret, also spelled Mert or Merit, was an Egyptian goddess associated with joy, singing and dancing. Her name means 'the beloved', and she was linked to celebrations and the waters of the Nile.

The myth

According to tradition, Merit was a symbolic wife occasionally given to Hapi, god of the Nile flood, and her name comes from this. She was shown as a woman with outstretched arms, crowned with the blue lotus of Upper Egypt or the papyrus plant of Lower Egypt, often holding an offering bowl as the recipient of her husband's generosity.

Later, the Book of Gates made her goddess of the eighth hour, and the Book of the Dead speaks of 'the two Merit friends', linked to Isis and Nephthys.

Worship and religion

She took part in funerary rites and the Sed Festival, and two kinds of priestesses served her cult: the 'recluses of the Luxor temple' and those known as 'the ones who love'.

Ancient sources: The Book of Gates and the Book of the Dead, where she appears as goddess of the eighth hour and as 'the two Merit friends'.
