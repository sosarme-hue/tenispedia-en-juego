Seven Lucky Gods

The Seven Lucky Gods (七福神, shichi fukujin) are a group of Japanese deities believed to bring good fortune. Their images, widely seen in woodblock prints, netsuke and shrines, bring together gods of Indian, Chinese and Japanese origin.

The myth

The seven did not arrive together. Ebisu is the only one of purely Japanese origin, reaching back to the age of the gods Izanami and Izanagi. Daikokuten, Bishamonten and Benzaiten came from Hinduism, with roots in Mahakala, Kubera and Sarasvati, while Fukurokuju and Jurojin come from China. At first merchants worshipped them, since Ebisu and Daikokuten were gods of business and trade; later other trades sought patrons of their own, such as Benzaiten for the arts and Fukurokuju for the sciences.

The legend of the Takarabune tells that during the first three days of the year the seven set sail from heaven on a ship loaded with treasures and head for human harbours. This gave rise to the custom of placing their picture under the pillow on the second day of the year to ensure good luck all year round.

Worship and religion

In ancient times each god was worshipped separately, but today they are mostly invoked when a believer wants one of them to act on their behalf. As a group they have been mentioned since around 1420 in Fushimi, where their processions imitated those of the daimyō. It is said that the Buddhist priest Tenkai chose them while serving the shōgun Iemitsu Tokugawa, seeking those who embodied virtues such as longevity, fortune and magnanimity.

Legacy

Their images appear often in netsuke and woodblock prints, and today it is common to see Ebisu in fish restaurants and household kitchens. Benzaiten is found beside the torii gates of many temples.

Trivia

Daikokuten, demon hunter. A legend tells that Daikokuten hung a sacred talisman from a branch in his garden and, using it as a trap, caught a demon.
Jurojin and his long head. Jurojin is shown with a very long head, a long white beard and a deer, and he is said to be based on a real person of ancient times.
