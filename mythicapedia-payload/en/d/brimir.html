giant and beer hall in Norse mythology

Brimir

Brimir is a name in Norse mythology that the sources link to a giant, possibly Ymir, and to a great beer hall. Its role as a hall for the souls of the virtuous after Ragnarök makes it one of the best-known images of the afterlife in Norse tradition.

The myth

In the Völuspá, Brimir appears as a possible alternative name for Ymir, the primordial giant. The gods debate who should form the lord of the dwarves from Brimir's blood and Blain's limbs. Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda, explains that the dwarves arose from Ymir's dead flesh, and that Odin and his brothers used his body to shape the earth: the blood became the sea and the bones became the mountains.

Another stanza of the same poem describes Brimir as a giant who owned a beer hall on the Never-cooled Plain, beside a golden hall of Sindri's lineage. Snorri drew on this passage in the Gylfaginning to describe a heavenly hall for good souls after Ragnarök, where plenty of good drink awaits those who enjoy it. The sources do not make clear whether the two halls are the same.

Ancient sources: The Völuspá and the Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, within the Norse Eddas.

Trivia

Blood and mountains. Snorri interprets Brimir's blood as the sea and his bones as the mountains, when the world was made from Ymir's body.
The Never-cooled Plain. Brimir's beer hall stood on a plain called Never-cooled, according to stanza 37 of the Völuspá.
