legendary Viking chieftain of Norway and Sweden

Sölve Högnesson

Sölve Högnesson is a Viking chieftain of the Nordic sagas, nicknamed the Usurper, who according to tradition seized the Swedish throne after burning its king alive.

The myth

According to the sagas, Sölve was the son of Högne, a wealthy man from Nærøy in Norway, and had a sister called Hild the Slender. He raided the Baltic coast. One night, he and his men surrounded a house where a feast was held in honour of the Swedish king Östen and set it alight with everyone inside.

He then arrived at Sigtuna and demanded that the Swedes accept him as king. They refused and resisted for eleven days of fighting before surrendering. Sölve ruled Sweden until the Swedes rebelled again and killed him. Accounts differ on his origins: the Heimskringla places him in Jutland, while the Historia Norvegiae calls him a Geat.

Ancient sources: Heimskringla, Hálfs saga ok Hálfsrekka (a c. 1300 version), Historia Norvegiae and the Ynglinga saga.

Trivia

A disputed origin. The Heimskringla places Sölve in Jutland, while the older Historia Norvegiae describes him as a Geat, from a different region.
A brother-in-law who gave him a post. His brother-in-law Hjorleiv made him jarl of Zealand, but Sölve aimed to be king of Sweden and did not stay content with that post.
