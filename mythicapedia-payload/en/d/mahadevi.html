supreme goddess of Hinduism

Mahadevi

Mahadevi, also called Mahamaya or Adi Parashakti, is the supreme goddess in Hinduism according to Shaktism, the tradition centred on her worship. For her devotees, all Hindu gods and goddesses are manifestations of her, the ultimate reality known as Para Brahman.

The myth

According to Shakta texts, Mahadevi is the Mulaprakriti, or primordial matter, and appears in five main forms known as the Panchaprakriti: Parvati, Lakshmi, Sarasvati, Gayatri and Radha. Tripura Sundari and Durga are also identified with her.

The Devi Bhagavata Purana and the Lalita Sahasranama list her many epithets, which combine protective and destructive traits: she is the mother of all, the life force in every being, the one who transcends the universe and sustains it, yet also the destroyer who slays demons. In the Devi Mahatmya she appears as Mahamaya, she who controls maya. The tradition holds that Shiva worshipped her for thousands of years, and that Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva are her subordinates, dependent on her power.

Ancient sources: The Vedas, the Devisukta of the Rigveda, the Shakta Upanishads, the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the Devi Mahatmya and the Lalita Sahasranama.
