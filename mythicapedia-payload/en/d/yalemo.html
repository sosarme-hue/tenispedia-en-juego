funeral song and minor deity of Greek mythology

Ialemus

Ialemus is, in Greek mythology, the personification of a song of lamentation that bears his name. He is considered a son of Apollo, and his figure links the funeral lament with poetry and the Muses.

The myth

According to tradition, Apollo united with one of the Muses, whom the texts call the «heavenly mothers», and from this union came the brothers Hymenaeus, the wedding song; Linus, the song of lament; and Ialemus, the song of sorrow. The sources disagree on the mother: in one version she is Calliope, while in another Ialemus and Hymenaeus are children of Clio and Magnes.

Ialemus is credited with inventing a song called ialemos, a dirge of a very serious and mournful character sung on the most melancholy occasions. Some authors suggest the figure may be Linus himself, a young man who died early and was mourned with this kind of song.

Legacy

In the Classical period the ialemos gradually lost its popularity, and comic poets ridiculed it. The name came to mean cold, frosty poetry and was used proverbially in that sense.

Ancient sources: Pindar, in his third threnos, associates him with Hymen, Linus and Orpheus and makes him a son of Apollo and Calliope; Aeschylus and Euripides mention the song in scenes of lamentation.

Trivia

Proverbial expression. The comparison «more naked than Ialemus» was used in Greek as a mockery of something poor or unadorned.
