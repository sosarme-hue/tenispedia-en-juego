Buddha before Gautama in the Theravada tradition

Koṇḍañña

Koṇḍañña is one of the Buddhas who, in Theravada Buddhism, came before Gautama, the historical founder of Buddhism. His story is told in the Pali chronicles of earlier Buddhas, where he forms part of the succession of awakened teachers.

The myth

According to the tradition, Koṇḍañña was born in Rammavati, the son of King Sunanda and Queen Sujata. For ten thousand years he lived as a layman with his wife Rudidevi and their son Vijitasena, in the towns of Ruci, Suruci and Subha.

He later left home to practise austerities. After ten months he received milk rice from Yasodhara, a merchant's daughter, and attained awakening beneath a salakalyani tree. He gave his first sermon to ten choirs of monks at Devavana, near Amaravati, and gathered three assemblies of disciples. He died at the age of one hundred thousand years at Canarama.

Worship and religion

According to the commentaries on the Buddhavamsa, his relics were not scattered but kept together as a single mass. A stupa seven leagues tall was built over them at Canarama.

Ancient sources: The Pali Buddhavamsa, a chronicle of the Buddhas before Gautama, and its commentaries record him.

Trivia

Relics kept as one mass. The commentaries on the Buddhavamsa state that his relics stayed together in a single mass rather than being scattered.
Legendary stature. Some versions of the tradition give him a height of twenty-eight cubits.
