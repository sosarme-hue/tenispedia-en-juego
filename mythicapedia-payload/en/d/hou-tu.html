Chinese goddess of earth and soil

Houtu

Houtu is the deity of land and earth in Chinese religion and mythology, regarded by many devotees as the overlord of all local earth gods. Her figure blends an older male earth god with a later earth goddess, which makes her one of the most complex deities in the Chinese pantheon.

The myth

According to early texts, Houtu was the son of Gonggong and controlled floods by raising mountains of earth. In some versions of the Great Flood, when Yu channelled the Yellow River badly, the sacred mother Houtu made the Yellow River Map and sent one of her messenger birds to tell him to open a channel to the east.

Worship and religion

In Taoism she is one of the Four Heavenly Ministers, and her daochang is on Mount Jiuhua. Emperor Wen and Emperor Wu of the Han dynasty worshipped her, the latter in 113 BC. Today she is popularly known as Houtu Niangniang.

Ancient sources: The Zuo Zhuan, the Book of Rites and the Classic of Mountains and Seas.

Trivia

Buddhist link. Some scholars connect Houtu with Bhumi, the Buddhist goddess who personifies the Earth.
Earth element. In the five-phase wuxing system, Houtu is the god of the earth element.
