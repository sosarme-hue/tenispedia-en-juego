Cleopatra (daughter of Danaus)

Cleopatra is one of the Danaides in Greek mythology, daughter of Danaus and a hamadryad. Her story belongs to the tale of the fifty sisters who, according to tradition, killed their husbands on their wedding night.

The myth

In the story, Danaus had fifty daughters by different women, and two of them were named Cleopatra. The one at the center of this account was the daughter of a hamadryad, a tree spirit who, according to the sources, was called either Atlantia or Phoebe. Like her sisters, she married one of the fifty sons of Aegyptus, Danaus's twin brother, in this case Agenor.

Tradition holds that on her wedding night Cleopatra killed her husband, as nearly all the Danaides did; only Hypermnestra spared hers. The other Cleopatra, daughter of the naiad Polyxo, married Hermus and also killed him.

Trivia

Two Cleopatras. Danaus had two daughters named Cleopatra, born to different mothers. Each belongs to a different strand of the story.
One exception. Only Hypermnestra spared her husband, which sets her apart from the fate of her sisters.
