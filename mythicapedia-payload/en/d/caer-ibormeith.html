swan maiden of Irish mythology

Caer Ibormeith

Caer Ibormeith is a supernatural maiden in Irish mythology who alternates between human form and that of a swan. She is the beloved of Óengus, of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

The myth

For more than a year, Óengus dreamed of a young woman who stood beside his bed and vanished whenever he reached for her. She was Caer, daughter of Prince Ethal Anbuail of Connacht. Every other Samhain she became human for one day, from sunset, and the rest of the time she was a swan.

Óengus searched for her in his dreams at the lake of the Dragon's Mouth, where he found 150 girls chained in pairs. He had to identify her in swan form; he succeeded, turned himself into a swan too, and they flew together to Brú na Bóinne. Their singing put everyone in Ireland to sleep for three days. Caer married Óengus and became foster-mother of Diarmuid.

Legacy

W. B. Yeats drew on the tale for his poem The Song of the Wandering Aengus.

Trivia

Different chains. In another version of the tale, Caer is bound alone with 130 chains rather than chained to 149 other swans.
Swans and cranes. Swans and cranes were both revered in Ireland for flying, walking and swimming; the upright crane was linked to shape-shifting.
