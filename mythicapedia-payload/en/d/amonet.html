Egyptian goddess of the hidden, consort of Amun

Amunet

Amunet is a primordial goddess of ancient Egyptian religion whose name means "The Hidden One". She was paired with Amun within the Ogdoad of Hermopolis and became especially important in Thebes, where she was seen as a protector of the king.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Amunet existed before creation alongside her husband Amun, as part of a group of four divine couples representing primeval forces. The four goddesses joined the four gods of the Ogdoad, whose name refers to the number eight.

The oldest texts present her as Amun's companion and, in some accounts, as both his mother and his wife, reflecting the Egyptian custom of pairing deities. Some traditions call her "the mother who is father". Accounts vary by place and period: at Hermopolis she belonged to the Ogdoad, while at Thebes she became linked to Amun as a local deity.

Other stories describe her as the great cow who gave birth to the sun god Ra, and in Graeco-Roman tradition she appears as one of the primordial creators alongside Amun, with the two often shown as a bull and a cow.

Worship and religion

Her cult grew during the Middle Kingdom and became firmly established in the New Kingdom. She was worshipped at Hermopolis, and at Thebes she received cult at Karnak alongside Amun and Mut, as well as in the temple of Luxor under the form of Opet. At Deir el-Hagar she appeared as Amun's partner. From the 18th Dynasty she had priests dedicated to her service, called prophets of Amunet, and her cult expanded notably in later periods. She took part in coronation rituals and Sed festivals, and during celebrations she was carried in her own sacred barque.

Legacy

Her most recognizable image, a woman wearing the red crown of Lower Egypt and holding a papyrus staff, appears in the colossal statue placed at Karnak during the reign of Tutankhamun. Modern Egyptology has studied her extensively as a representative example of primeval divine pairs.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, the earliest known religious texts of ancient Egypt, mention "the beneficent shadow of Amun and Amunet".
