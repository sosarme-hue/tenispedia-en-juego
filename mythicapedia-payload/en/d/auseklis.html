Latvian god of the morning star

Auseklis

Auseklis is a stellar deity of Latvian mythology, linked to the star that first appears in the east at dawn, though it may not be the same as Venus. He is the third most popular deity in Latvian belief after Saulė and Mēness, yet he survives almost only in folk songs, since paganism gave way to Christianity in Latvia in the 12th century.

The myth

According to the dainas, the folk songs, Auseklis is a young, playful god, too youthful to work alongside the other deities. His name comes from the root aus- ('dawn'), which is related to other Indo-European dawn deities. He is often described as a suitor of the Sun's daughter, Saules meita, whom he courted all the way to Germany in some versions.

The traditions disagree about his role. In some tellings he is the bride's groom; in others he is a member of the wedding procession, or he loses his bride because of the quarrels of Mēness, the Moon. He is often confused with Mēness and with the Dieva dēli, the sons of God. The Sun gives him a horse, on which the young god rides across the sky.

Legacy

Auseklis gives its name to the eight-pointed star, an isogonal octagram, which appears in ornaments, building facades, street tiles and the emblem of the Latvian National Guard. In the 1980s it became a symbol of the third Latvian National Awakening.

Ancient sources: His references come mainly from the dainas, Latvian folk songs preserved in oral tradition.
