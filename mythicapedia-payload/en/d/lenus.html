Celtic healing god

Lenus

Lenus was a Celtic healing god worshipped mainly by the Treveri of the Moselle valley, who almost always identified him with the Roman god Mars.

The myth

No ancient author describes Lenus, and he is known only from inscriptions and dedications. His native name, perhaps linked to the grove, appears before that of Mars in dedications, suggesting he was the established local god onto whom Mars was later grafted.

At Trier he was paired with the goddess Ancamna and worshipped alongside the Xulsigiae, perhaps spring nymphs. In Britain he was merged with the local gods Ocelus and Vellaunus.

Worship and religion

His chief sanctuary at Trier, beside a healing spring, had a classical-style temple, a ceremonial theatre, pilgrim baths and high-ranking priests. At the Martberg, sick pilgrims were lodged, and one building may have served for incubation, the sleep through which cures were sought. A man named Tychicus left a verse inscription thanking the god for his recovery.

Trivia

An incomplete statue. Of the statue dedicated to Lenus at Caerwent only a pair of human feet and those of a goose survive, the goose being a bird linked with war in Celtic imagery.
A thank-you in verse. Tychicus's inscription, in Latin and Greek, is one of the few signs that the god was believed to answer prayers for healing.
