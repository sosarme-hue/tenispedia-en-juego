demon of the age of decline in Hindu mythology

Kali

Kali is the personification of sin and lord of the Kaliyuga, the present age of moral decline in Hinduism. Appearing in the Mahabharata and the Puranas as a sinister gandharva who drives humans toward wrongdoing, he is destined to face Kalki at the end of time.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Kali was the fifteenth son of the sage Kashyapa and Muni, classed as a deva-gandharva, a divine being linked to celestial musicians. Jealous when Princess Damayanti chose the mortal Nala as her husband over the gods, he waited twelve years for his chance. Taking advantage of a lapse of purity in Nala, he possessed the king's soul and drove him to lose his kingdom in a rigged dice game. Nala was eventually freed through Damayanti's curse and the venom of the serpent Karkotaka, and he trapped the demon inside a vibhitaka tree.

The tradition also tells that Kali was reborn as Duryodhana, eldest of the Kaurava brothers. When King Parikshit went hunting, Kali asked to enter his realm, and the king granted him five dwelling places: gambling, drinking, prostitution, slaughter, and ill-gotten gold. Kali slipped into Parikshit's golden crown and clouded his judgment, leading to a fateful error that ended in the king's death. In the Puranas, Kali appears as a huge, sooty being with a dog-like face, and the Kalki Purana foretells his final defeat in battle against Kalki.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata describes him as one of Kashyapa's gandharva sons; the Bhagavata and Kalki Puranas expand his figure and his role at the end of the Kaliyuga.
