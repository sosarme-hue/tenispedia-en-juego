Punic moon goddess and protector of Carthage

Tanit

Tanit was the chief deity of Punic Carthage, consort of Baal Hammon and patron of the city. She combined aspects of the moon, fertility, war and the protection of homes, and her figure is linked both to Phoenician tradition and to local roots in North Africa.

The myth

The surviving sources offer more a portrait of the goddess than a long story. In Punic tradition, Tanit protected the city and its families, guided those who sought wisdom, and went to war only to defend her people, unlike the Phoenician Astarte, who was associated with wars of aggression.

Her relationship with Astarte is debated. Some scholars think the two were different faces of one goddess, while others argue that Tanit had North African origins and did not derive from Astarte. She was shown with a lion, riding it or with a lion's head, naked as a symbol of fertility, winged, and beside the dove, the palm tree and the rose. In another image she rides a bull, which linked her to Europa.

Worship and religion

Her cult spread from Carthage across the western Mediterranean, with sanctuaries on Ibiza, where the Es Culleram shrine held more than a thousand terracotta votive figures. After the Roman conquest she merged with Juno as Dea Caelestis, and her great temple in Carthage drew pilgrims from all over North Africa and Spain. In parts of modern North Africa a rain ceremony survives, rooted in ancient songs sung to the goddess during droughts.

Legacy

Her sign, a circle above a horizontal line with a triangle below, was carved on tombstones and homes as protection, and today it is the recognisable emblem of the goddess in museums and publications. On Ibiza her memory endured long after Christianization, and the neopagan Church of the Guanche People uses her symbol.

Ancient sources: Few literary texts survive from her own tradition; most knowledge comes from Punic and Neo-Punic inscriptions, such as the bronze plaque from Es Culleram, and from references by Greek writers such as Herodotus, who linked her to Athena.

Trivia

Over a thousand votive figures. At the Es Culleram sanctuary on Ibiza, worshippers left more than a thousand terracotta figurines, whole and broken, depicting the goddess.
A festival banned. Her yearly festival from antiquity still survives in parts of North Africa. Muammar Gaddafi banned it in Libya, calling it a pagan celebration.
