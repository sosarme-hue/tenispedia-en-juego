Estonian god of lightning

Pikne

Pikne is the Estonian god of lightning, worshipped by the pagan peoples of the country before Christianization. His figure, with his own relatives and episodes, shows how Finnic belief understood thunder and rain.

The myth

According to the tradition collected by Matthias Johann Eisen, Pikne is the brother of Kõu and the son of Uku. The dark underworld god Vanatühi stole his whistle or bagpipes, and without that instrument the gods could not help farmers who prayed for rain. Angered, Uku sent Pikne back to Earth, where he worked as a farmhand. At a wedding he passed himself off as a musician and regained his magic instrument, an episode comparable to the Norse myth of Trymskvida.

Worship and religion

According to the pastor Johann Gutslaff, in the Middle Ages pagan priests made animal sacrifices to Pikne. He was regarded as protector of the holy river Võhandu in Võru County, and he punished those who built mills on its waters by withholding rain.

Legacy

The composer Veljo Tormis set the priest's prayer in his 1974 choral work Litany to Thunder. Pikker was also the name of an Estonian satire and humor magazine.

Ancient sources: The pastor Johann Gutslaff recorded the punishment and the prayer in his 1644 work published in Tartu, while the story of the stolen instrument comes from later collections.

Trivia

A magazine with a mythic name. Pikker was the name of an Estonian satire and humor magazine.
