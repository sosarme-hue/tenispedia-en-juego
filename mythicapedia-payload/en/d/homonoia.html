minor Greek goddess of concord

Homonoia

Homonoia was, in Greek mythology, a minor goddess personifying concord, unanimity and oneness of mind. She belonged to a group of divinities linked to justice and civic virtues, and the Romans later portrayed her as Concordia.

The myth

According to Mnaseas, in his treatise On Europe, Homonoia was the daughter of Soter, the saviour daimon, and his sister Praxidike, the exacter of justice. Her siblings were Ctesius, a minor god of the household, and Arete, virtue, and the four were known as the Praxidikai after their mother.

Another tradition, cited by Dionysius of Chalcis, made her a daughter of the Theban king Ogygus, linking her to the myths of Thebes. The surviving accounts are scarce and recount no deeds of her own; she functions mainly as an abstract symbol of harmony among people and as the counterpart of Eris, Strife.

Worship and religion

Pausanias describes an altar of Homonoia at Olympia, placed beside those of Apollo and Hermes, Athena and the Mother of the gods. This shows she had her own place in the worship of Greek sanctuaries.

Legacy

Her image as Concordia was widely used in imperial Rome, which often depicted her on coins. The concept of homonoia, unanimity or concord, remained a frequently cited social and political value in Western culture.

Ancient sources: Mnaseas of Patras, in his treatise On Europe; Dionysius of Chalcis; and Pausanias, in his description of Olympia.

Trivia

Roman coins. She frequently appears on Roman-era coins, identified as Concordia.
An altar at Olympia. Pausanias mentions her among the altars of the Olympia sanctuary, alongside those of Apollo, Hermes and Athena.
