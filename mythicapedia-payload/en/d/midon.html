Trojan warrior in the Iliad

Mydon

Mydon is a warrior of Greek mythology, son of Atymnius, who fights on the Trojan side in Homer's Iliad. His role is minor, yet he shows how the poem gives names and deaths even to the lesser fighters of the Trojan War.

The myth

In the Iliad, Mydon fights alongside the Trojans during the siege of Troy. The poem places him in Book V, where Antilochus kills him in the midst of battle. His story takes up only a few lines, with no episodes of his own or tales of his youth, and his role is limited to adding one more name to the list of the fallen.

He should not be confused with another Mydon, a Paeonian warrior who also fights for Troy and who, according to the Homeric tradition, is killed by Achilles. The two share a name but belong to different episodes of the poem.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Iliad, Book V, as a defender of Troy.
