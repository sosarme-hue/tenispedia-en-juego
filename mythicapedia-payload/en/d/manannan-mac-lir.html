Irish sea god and lord of the otherworld

Manannán mac Lir

Manannán mac Lir is a sea god, warrior and king of the otherworld in Gaelic mythology, son of Lir. Seen as a ruler and guardian of the otherworld, he appears in Irish, Scottish and Manx legends and is linked to the Isle of Man and to the Land of Promise.

The myth

In the tradition, Manannán is a water deity who acts as a psychopomp and is closely tied to the otherworld. He is often associated with the Tuatha Dé Danann, although most scholars think he belongs to an older race of gods. Some tales make him king of Emain Ablach, the Isle of Apple Trees, a realm compared to the Land of Promise and to Avalon, and loosely linked to the Isle of Man.

Several wives and lovers are named in the sources: Fand, later known as queen of the fairies, who betrayed him with Cú Chulainn; Aífe, whose skin became his magic bag; and, in other versions, Áine, or he is said to be the father of Áine, Niamh and Clidna. After the Tuatha Dé were defeated by the Milesian humans, he is given a role as over-king, overseeing the allocation of the sídhe mounds. His possessions include a cloak that changes colour, a helmet that dazzles foes, the sword Fragarach, a self-sailing boat and the mist of invisibility féth fíada. In one tale he gives Cormac mac Airt a silver branch and the Goblet of Truth, and he lends Lugh his horse Aonbharr and his sword.

Legacy

According to tradition, his name gave rise to that of the Isle of Man, although the etymology is debated. He is the Welsh counterpart of Manawydan fab Llŷr.

Ancient sources: He appears in nearly all the cycles of Irish literature, including the Ulster Cycle and the Mythological Cycle, in texts such as the Altram Tige Dá Medar and Echtra Cormaic.

Trivia

Three legs. A folk tradition on the Isle of Man says the god moved like a wheel turning on his three legs.
Pigs that return. His magical swine provided endless food and could be revived by placing their bones back in the sty.
