Anjana

The anjana is a fairy creature of Cantabrian tradition, a protector of the poor and a foe of the ojáncanu. Her figure recalls the xanas of Asturias and other fairies of northern Iberia.

The myth

According to tales collected by the regional writer Manuel Llano in the early twentieth century, anjanas wear long braids tied with silk ribbons, crowns of wild flowers, and a long white tunic under a blue cape. They carry a staff of ash or hawthorn and can change into people, trees, animals or objects. They dwell in underground palaces hidden in sinkholes, caves and springs, full of treasure they use to reward the humble or punish the proud.

They help injured animals, trees broken by storms, lovers, and travellers lost in the forest or the snow. According to one villager from Viaña, some of them give clothes and shoes to the needy on Epiphany. There are also wicked anjanas, though the kind ones usually neutralise their power, and all of them oppose the cruel ojáncanos.

Ancient sources: Manuel Llano, a regional writer who recorded Cantabrian tales in the early twentieth century.

Trivia

Roses of the equinox. Tradition says that on the night of the spring equinox anjanas dance hand in hand until dawn, scattering roses. Whoever finds one with purple, green, blue or golden petals will be happy until death.
Heavenly or tree origin. Some believe anjanas are heavenly beings sent by God who return to heaven after 400 years, while others see them as tree spirits who guard the forests.
