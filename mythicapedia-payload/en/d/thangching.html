guardian god of the Moirang kingdom in Manipur

Thangching

Thangching (also Thangjing) is a primordial deity of Sanamahism, the traditional religion of Manipur in India. He is the ruling god of the Moirang dynasty and the guardian of the southwest, and his figure dominates the religious life of the region.

The myth

According to tradition, Thangjing is the lord of the tiger hunters and protector of both domestic and wild animals. In the epic of Khamba and Thoibi he stands for righteousness and saves Khamba. According to the stories, he sent Phouoibi to Kege Moirang to make the human world prosper.

The goddess Panthoibi, searching for her beloved Nongpok Ningthou, asked him where he was and called him the supreme lord of Moirang, using Loktak lake as his mirror.

Worship and religion

His worship is ancient, and an old shrine still stands in Moirang on the shore of Loktak lake. Every year, early in summer, the festival Lai Haraoba is held in his honour, with dances, ritual sports and the dress of the old royal lords and ladies, lasting a week. Meiteis from all over Manipur come to visit.

Legacy

His name is shared by the aquatic plant Euryale ferox, whose edible seeds, called foxnuts, are a favourite in Meitei cuisine, and by one of the four sacred peaks of the Meiteis.

Ancient sources: The traditional chronicle Moirang Ningthourol Lambuba, which presents him as the divine chief of the Koireng people and progenitor of the Kege clan.

Trivia

A plant with his name. The aquatic plant Euryale ferox bears the god's name, and its edible seeds are widely eaten in Manipur.
A sacred peak. One of the four sacred Meitei peaks carries his name and is a traditional place of worship.
