water and river goddess of northwest Iberia

Navia

Navia, also Nauia or Nabia, is a goddess of northwest Iberia, worshipped by peoples such as the Gallaeci, Lusitanians and Astures. Her name is linked to water and rivers, though her true nature remains uncertain.

The myth

Few stories about her survive. In Irish epic lore, a woman figure from certain legends acts as messenger of Death and guides young people to the afterlife by boat.

In a tale from the Cú Chulainn cycle, his son Connla climbs into a glass boat belonging to a sorceress and drifts away until he vanishes from sight.

Worship and religion

About twenty votive inscriptions mention her, mostly dedicated by local people, and many of her shrines lay beside watercourses, though not all of them.

Legacy

Several rivers and places in northern Spain, such as the Nalón and the Navia, may preserve her name. Her boat motif survives in the Virgin of the Boat at Muxía, celebrated on 8 September.

Ancient sources: About twenty votive inscriptions from northwest Iberia, with no surviving literary tales.

Trivia

River names. Several rivers in northern Spain bear similar names, such as the Navia and the Nalón, although their link to the goddess is not proven.
A name with several readings. Her name has been linked to Sanskrit navya, meaning watercourse, or to the valley, and also to the idea of a ferrywoman.
