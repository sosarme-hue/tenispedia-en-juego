ancient Egyptian goddess of justice and protection from snakes and scorpions

Mafdet

Mafdet was a goddess of ancient Egypt, shown as a feline or as a woman with a cat's head, who guarded against snakes and scorpions and was linked to legal justice and execution. She matters because she combined protection of the king and the sun god Ra with the punishment of enemies in the afterlife.

The myth

In Egyptian tradition, Mafdet defended the sun god Ra during his daily voyage and hunted by night, earning the epithet "Piercer of Darkness", until dawn arrived. Some accounts describe her protecting Osiris when his body was cut into pieces and helping bind them together.

Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom name her as slayer of snakes and closely associated with the king. In the New Kingdom she was believed to rule the judgment hall of the Duat, where the pharaoh's enemies were beheaded with her claws. Royal tombs link her with Anubis, accompanying him as hunter or executioner.

Worship and religion

She was part of the pantheon from the First Dynasty. She protected the king's chambers and other sacred places, and her image appears on stone vessel fragments from the tomb of pharaoh Den.

Legacy

The image of a cat climbing an executioner's staff is one of the most recognisable symbols of Egyptian justice in popular modern culture.

Ancient sources: She is mentioned in the Palermo Stone and the Pyramid Texts, and appears in royal tombs of the Old and New Kingdoms.

Trivia

Hearts of enemies. Egyptians compared Mafdet to cats, which leave their kills at their owners' feet. In this idea, she delivered the hearts of the pharaoh's enemies.
Piercer of Darkness. One of her epithets, "Piercer of Darkness", reflects her night hunt in defence of Ra before dawn.
