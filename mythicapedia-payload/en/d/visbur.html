legendary Swedish king of the House of Ynglings

Visbur

Visbur is a legendary king of Sweden from the House of Ynglings, whom Norse tradition presents as the son and successor of Vanlandi. His Old Norse name is usually rendered as 'certain son' or 'undoubted son', a reference to his legitimate place within the royal family. As a figure of proto-historical character, he serves as a link in the genealogy of the Swedish kings and is remembered above all for the violent death at the hands of his own sons.

The myth

According to the stories, Visbur came to the throne after the death of his father Vanlandi and ruled Sweden from his estate. The sources describe him as a king who, at one point, decided to reject his wife, the mother of his sons, and to deny them the inheritance that was rightfully theirs.

The sons, resentful of this rejection, plotted their revenge. According to tradition, they set fire to the estate with the king inside, and Visbur was burned to death within his own hall. The story thus highlights the punishment that falls upon one who turns away from his family and withholds what is due by right.

The throne passed to his son Domalde, who appears next in the same line of succession. The versions differ in their details: Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga, written in 1225, tells the story at greater length, while the 9th-century poem Ynglingatal and the Latin Historia Norwegiae mention it more briefly. Íslendingabók also cites the line of descent from Ynglingatal and places Visbur between Vanlandi and Domalde.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Ynglingatal (9th century), in Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga (1225), in the Historia Norwegiae and in Íslendingabók, where he is named as the successor of Vanlandi.
