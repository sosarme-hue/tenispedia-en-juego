Hindu lord of creatures

Prajapati

Prajapati, meaning "lord of creatures", is a name shared by several Hindu deities linked to creation and the protection of life. In the later Vedic texts he is often identified with Brahma, the creator god.

The myth

In the Vedic texts Prajapati appears late. In hymn 10.121 of the Rigveda he is born from Hiranyagarbha, the golden embryo that arose from the waters, and goes on to create the universe. His role grows in the Brahmanas. The Shatapatha Brahmana tells how he was born from a golden egg incubated for a year in the primeval sea, how his sounds formed the sky, earth and seasons, and how his breath created the devas when inhaled and the asuras when exhaled.

Other accounts credit him with creating the world together with the goddess Vac. The Aitareya Brahmana tells that, turned into a stag, he pursued his daughter Ushas, and the horrified gods created Rudra, who wounded him and drove him off. The Upanishads present him as a creative power tied to Brahman, and the Mahabharata declares Brahma to be a Prajapati, as are Vishnu and Shiva. The Manusmriti names ten prajapatis created by Brahma, among them Marichi, Atri and Daksha.

Worship and religion

In Bali, Indonesia, Hindu temples called pura prayapati or pura mrajapati are common, most closely associated with funeral rites and the ngaben cremation ceremony. The Grhyasutras invoke him at weddings, asking for prosperous offspring and harmony between husband and wife.

Legacy

Robert Graves proposed that his name is etymologically equivalent to that of Phanes, the oracular god of Colophon according to Macrobius, also called Protogonus. David Leeming links the cosmic egg concept associated with Prajapati to Greco-Roman worship.

Ancient sources: Rigveda, Shatapatha Brahmana, Aitareya Brahmana, Upanishads, Mahabharata and Manusmriti.

Trivia

Temples in Bali. Temples dedicated to Prajapati still exist in Bali, and many are linked to funeral ceremonies.
