Theban princess, maenad of Dionysus

Agave

Agave is a princess of Thebes in Greek mythology, daughter of the city's founder, Cadmus, and of the goddess Harmonia. She is best known for her role as a maenad of the god Dionysus and for killing her own son, King Pentheus, without recognizing him.

The myth

According to the myth, Agave was the eldest daughter of Cadmus and Harmonia. She married Echion, one of the Spartoi, and became the mother of Pentheus, who would rule Thebes, and of Epirus. Her sisters Autonoe, Ino and Semele belonged to the same royal family. Tradition holds that after Semele was destroyed by the sight of Zeus, the sisters spread the claim that she had only pretended Zeus was the father of her child to hide a shameful secret, and that her death was a just punishment.

Dionysus, Semele's son and Pentheus' cousin, came to Thebes to avenge his mother and drove the women of the city into frenzy, leading their rites on Mount Cithaeron. Pentheus banned his worship. In Euripides' The Bacchae, he decided to spy on the maenads disguised as a woman, but he was discovered. In her frenzy, Agave took him for a wild beast and tore him apart with the others.

Back in the city, Agave proudly showed her father the head of Pentheus on a spike, convinced it was a lion's. Cadmus made her see the truth, and her madness faded. According to Hyginus, she then fled to Illyria, married King Lycotherses and killed him to win the city for her father, though scholars regard this ending as a rewritten part of an earlier story.

Ancient sources: Euripides' The Bacchae, the Fabulae of Hyginus and the Dionysiaca of Nonnus of Panopolis mention her.

Trivia

The lion's head. Agave believed the head she carried back to Thebes belonged to a lion, and only realized it was her son's when she spoke with her father.
A conflicted family. Her sisters Ino and Autonoe also take part in the Dionysus story, showing how closely the Theban royal family is tied to this myth.
