Hindu goddess of victory, a form of Durga

Kaushiki

Kaushiki is a Hindu goddess of victory who emerges from the sheath of Parvati and is regarded as a form of the mother goddess Durga. Her emergence gave rise to the Matrikas, and she is the slayer of the demon brothers Shumbha and Nishumbha.

The myth

According to the Devi Mahatmya, part of the Markandeya Purana, the gods went to the Himalayas and asked Parvati to destroy the demon brothers Shumbha and Nishumbha. Parvati asked for whom their hymn was intended, and at that moment a dark, fierce goddess emerged from her sheath (kosha): Kaushiki. From then on, Parvati was known as Gauri. Kaushiki, depicted with eight arms and carrying weapons such as the trishula, bow and plough, fought the asuras alongside the Matrikas, after the generals Chanda and Munda spotted her and warned Shumbha.

Other traditions tell the origin differently: the Devi Bhagavata Purana describes her as a Shakti that came out of Parvati's body, while the Linga Purana calls her the daughter of Yashoda.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya of the Markandeya Purana, the Devi Bhagavata Purana, the Linga Purana and the Shaiva Ajitagama.

Trivia

A name among a thousand. The Rudrayamala-Uttaratantra lists Kaushiki among the thousand names of Kumari, and simply reciting them is said to bring spiritual rewards even without formal worship.
A Jain vidya. In Jainism, Kauśikī is the name of one of the sixteen Vidyās, associated with supernatural beings called Kauśikīpūrvaka Vidyadharas.
