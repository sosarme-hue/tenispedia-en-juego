wife of Thon, Egyptian figure from Greek mythology

Polydamna

Polydamna is an Egyptian figure from Greek mythology, wife of Thon, often called "Thon the Egyptian". She matters for a episode in the Odyssey in which she provides a potion that eases grief.

The myth

Homer mentions Polydamna in the Odyssey as the wife of Thon, a nobleman of Egyptian heritage. According to the story, she had nepenthe, a drug said to rob grief and anger of their sting and banish painful memories.

The best-known episode shows Polydamna giving this remedy to Helen, wife of Menelaus. Helen slipped it into the wine that Menelaus and Telemachus were drinking, and both forgot their sorrows. Her name, which can be read as "she who has tamed many", has led some scholars to link her to Potnia Theron, an ancient mistress of wild beasts.

Legacy

Philostratus of Athens, in his Life of Apollonius of Tyana, imagines a dialogue between Apollonius and his disciple Damis suggesting that Helen may have learned her enchantments in Egypt.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey, which names her as the wife of Thon and credits her with giving nepenthe to Helen.
