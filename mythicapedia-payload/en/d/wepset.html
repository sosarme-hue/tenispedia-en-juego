ancient Egyptian goddess of the uraeus cobra

Wepset

Wepset is an ancient Egyptian goddess linked to the uraeus cobra, a symbol of royal protection. Her name means "she who burns".

The myth

In Egyptian texts she appears as a personification of the uraeus cobra that guarded rulers, and as one of the forms of the Eye of Ra. The Coffin Texts call her simply "the Eye", and New Kingdom texts show her destroying the enemies of Osiris.

She has no extended story of her own. Her role is defined mainly by protecting the king and punishing those who threaten the divine order.

Worship and religion

Texts mention a temple of hers on the island of Biga, although no such structure has been found there. She appears in the temples of other deities, including in Lower Nubia. She is usually shown as a snake and, in the Greek and Roman period, with a lion's head or with the uraeus and sun disk.

Ancient sources: The Coffin Texts and New Kingdom texts.

Trivia

A temple without a trace. Texts place a temple of Wepset on the island of Biga, but no such structure has so far been located there.
Lioness head. In the Greek and Roman period she was also shown as a woman with a lion's head, a different form from her usual snake appearance.
