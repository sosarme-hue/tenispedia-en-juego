Greek sea god, the Old Man of the Sea

Nereus

Nereus is an ancient sea god of Greek mythology, father of the Nereids and a benevolent figure associated with wisdom and prophecy. As the Old Man of the Sea, he was one of the most venerated water deities in early tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, Nereus was the eldest of the five sons of Pontus, the primordial sea god, and Gaia. Hesiod's Theogony links him with Doris, daughter of Oceanus, with whom he had fifty daughters, the Nereids, sea nymphs. A late tradition made him a son of Oceanus or simply another name for Oceanus.

In Homer, the god appears mainly as the Old Man of the Sea, a wise counsellor. Thetis, mother of Achilles, learned from her father the decree of the Fates: her son would have either a long life without glory or a brief life full of glory. Nereus could also change shape and prophesy, much like Proteus, so heroes had to restrain him to obtain his answers.

His best-known encounter with a hero is with Heracles. In some accounts, the hero caught him while he slept and bound him until he revealed where the Hesperides and their golden apples were. Others say Nereus gave Heracles the golden cloak of Helios. Nereus was also linked to Nerites, a handsome son who was transformed into a shellfish.

Worship and religion

Nereus had no cult as widespread as that of other sea gods. Pausanias reports that the people of Gytheion in Laconia worshipped him under the name of the Old Man and believed he lived in the sea.

Legacy

His name survives in that of the Nereids, who in modern Greek folklore appear as nereides, water spirits linked to springs and the sea. Virgil gave him the trident and so associated him with Poseidon, and the epithet Old Man of the Sea is still used for wise figures of the deep.

Ancient sources: Hesiod names him in the Theogony as father of the Nereids; Homer calls him the Old Man of the Sea in the Iliad, and Pausanias records his worship at Gytheion.

Trivia

A name that may come from his daughters. Some scholars suggest the name Nereus may derive from that of the Nereids, since he is important mainly as their father.
An old man with a fish tail. Attic vase painters sometimes showed him with a long scaly fish tail, though he was always clothed and holding his staff.
