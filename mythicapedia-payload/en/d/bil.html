Norse goddess, sister of Hjúki and companion of the Moon

Bil

Bil is, in Norse mythology, a girl who travels across the sky with the Moon alongside her brother Hjúki. She appears only in the thirteenth-century Prose Edda, and her figure has been linked to the craters of the Moon and to the Bilwis of German folklore.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Bil and Hjúki were the children of Viðfinnr. While they were walking from the well Byrgir, carrying a pail hung from a pole on their shoulders, the Moon (Máni) snatched them from the Earth, and they have followed him across the sky ever since.

Bil appears again in two lists of goddesses in the same work, and her name serves as a poetic circumlocution for "woman". Hjúki is hardly mentioned in the story.

Legacy

Jacob Grimm connected the tale with the German and Scandinavian "man in the Moon", and pointed to a Christian version in which the man is punished for cutting wood on a Sunday. Bil's name is associated with the Bilwis, a folk figure of the grain fields, and with the English village of Bilsby, proposed as the origin of its name. The pair has also been compared with the English nursery rhyme "Jack and Jill", which appeared in school books.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (13th century), in the books Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál.

Trivia

An English village. The village of Bilsby, in Lincolnshire, has been proposed as a place named after Bil.
Jack and Jill. A Norse tale and the English nursery rhyme "Jack and Jill" both feature two children fetching water, which has led scholars to compare them.
