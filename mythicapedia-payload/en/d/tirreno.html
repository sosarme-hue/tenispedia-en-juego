Eponymous hero of the Tyrrhenians

Tyrrhenus

Tyrrhenus is the eponymous hero of the Tyrrhenians, the name the Greeks gave to the Etruscans, and one of the founders of the league of twelve Etruscan cities alongside his brother Tarchon. He matters because ancient sources present him as the leader who brought this people from Lydia, an origin that modern historians largely reject.

The myth

According to Strabo, Tyrrhenus was the son of Atys, king of Lydia. Another version makes him one of two brothers, with Tarchon, sons of Telephus and Hiera, from whom Tarquinia and the Tyrrhenians took their names. The Theogony, by contrast, links the rulers of the Tyrrhenians with Circe and Odysseus, through Agrius, Latinus and Telegonus. Some sources make him a son of Heracles and Omphale, and say that he drove the Pelasgians out of Italy from the cities north of the Tiber.

The best-known account, transmitted by Herodotus, places the origin in Lydia. During a famine the king divided his people by lot: some would stay and others would leave. Lydus drew the lot to remain, and Tyrrhenus led the expedition that crossed the land of the Umbrians and reached a territory he named Tyrrhenia, also called Etruria. There he founded twelve cities and placed Tarchon in charge. His wife, Sardo, gave her name to Sardis or to Sardinia, depending on the version. Tradition also credits him with inventing the salpinx, the trumpet, which his son Hegeleus taught the Dorians who accompanied Temenus to play.

Legacy

The Greeks used his name for the Etruscans, and the Romans extended it to the sea west of Etruria, the Tyrrhenian Sea.

Ancient sources: Herodotus presents him as the saviour of the Etruscans who led them from Lydia; Strabo and the Theogony offer different genealogies, and Dionysius of Halicarnassus records the objection of Xanthus of Lydia, who never mentioned Tyrrhenus in his history of the Lydians.

Trivia

A sea named after him. The Tyrrhenian Sea, between Italy, Corsica, Sardinia and Sicily, takes its name from the Romans, who extended the term from the region of Etruria.
A disputed origin. Dionysius of Halicarnassus reports that Xanthus of Lydia, a historian from Sardis, never mentioned Tyrrhenus as a ruler of the Lydians.
