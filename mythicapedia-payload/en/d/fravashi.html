personal spirit in Persian Zoroastrianism

Fravashi

Fravashi are the personal spirits of every individual, whether living, dead or yet unborn, and a class of yazata in Zoroastrianism. Their cult, of Iranian origin, had to be woven into a religion that viewed veneration of the dead with some suspicion.

The myth

According to tradition, the fravashi were originally the spirits of heroic warriors who protected their living descendants. They dwelt in the upper air and, when properly honoured, could descend like birds to help their kin, fighting invisibly beside them. They were also said to compete with one another to secure the rain that watered their descendants' fields and cattle.

In Yasht 13 they appear as swift, valiant, bloodstained beings who strike down enemies and grant victory to their worshippers. According to the Bundahishn, Ohrmazd offers them a choice between staying with him or descending to inhabit human bodies and fight the druj. The Denkard, by contrast, distinguishes them from the urvan: the urvan can descend to hell, while the fravashi remain in heaven.

Worship and religion

The last days of the year were dedicated to the fravashi, and the great annual feast of Hamaspaθmaēdaya, of Iranian tradition, came to be associated with them under the name Rōzān Fravardīgān, later shortened to Fravardīgān. During these days their names were recited and offerings of meat and clothing were made, in exchange for blessings. A month of the calendar and the 19th day of each month bear their name.

Legacy

The winged disc, the traditional Zoroastrian symbol, is interpreted as a symbolic image of the fravashi. Their name survives in modern Persian in forms such as farvardin, the first month of the Persian calendar, and foruhar or faravahar.

Ancient sources: The Avesta, especially Yasht 13, together with the Bundahishn and the Denkard from the Pahlavi tradition.

Trivia

The winged disc. The well-known Zoroastrian winged disc is traditionally read as a symbolic image of the fravashi.
A month named for them. The first month of the year and the 19th day of each month are under their protection and bear their name.
Warriors in armour. The fravashi are described as mighty warriors clad in iron armour, invoked during battle.
