one of the daughters of Rán and Ægir, personification of the waves

Bylgja

Bylgja is one of the nine daughters of Rán and Ægir, personifications of the sea in Norse mythology. She matters because she embodies the waves, and ancient poetry presents her sisters as forces that test sailors at sea.

The myth

In Norse tradition, Rán, a goddess of the Æsir, and Ægir, a jötunn, both personifications of the sea, had nine daughters, goddesses who embody the waves. Their names are poetic terms for sea waves, and they recur across several sources.

The Poetic Edda mentions them in the poem Helgakviða Hundingsbana I, where Helgi's boat crashes through fierce seas while Ægir's daughters try to overturn his ship. The passage casts them as hostile forces battering the sailors.

Ancient sources: They appear in the Poetic Edda, in the poem Helgakviða Hundingsbana I, and in the Prose Edda, in the Skáldskaparmál, where their names are listed twice.

Trivia

Possible mothers of Heimdallr. Some scholars have identified these sisters with the nine mothers of the god Heimdallr, which would mean he was born from the waves. Others question this because the names do not match.
One name changed. In chapter 61 of the Nafnaþulur section, the author lists the nine daughters again with a small variation: Dröfn is replaced by Bára.
