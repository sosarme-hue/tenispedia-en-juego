Roman god of time and agriculture

Saturn

Saturn (Saturnus) was the Roman god of agriculture, seasons and time, and in the myths he is the father of Jupiter, Neptune and Pluto. In antiquity he was identified with the Greek Titan Cronus, so many of his stories blended with those of the Greek god.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Saturn and other Titans were born from the union of Aether and Earth. Other accounts make his parents Caelus and Trivia, or Caelus and Vesta. With Ops, he fathered Vesta, Ceres, Juno, Jupiter, Pluto and Neptune, though other authors give different lists of children.

The tradition tells how Saturn, angry at his father Caelus, cut off his genitals with a sickle, and how Venus was born from the blood and sea foam. The Vatican Mythographers add that, after a prophecy from Themis warned him that a son would dethrone him, Saturn devoured his offspring. When Jupiter grew up, he castrated his father and drove him from the throne. The brothers then divided the world by lot: Jupiter took the sky, Neptune the sea and Pluto the underworld. Saturn wandered the earth, and he left his daughter Juno in the care of African nymphs.

Another tradition, preserved by Virgil and Macrobius, places him in Italy. Fleeing Jupiter, Saturn arrives and is welcomed by Janus, who shares his throne and receives farming knowledge from him. Virgil presents him as a king who gathered scattered peoples and gave them laws, and whose reign was the Golden Age, a time of peace and effortless abundance also described by Hesiod and Ovid. Macrobius explains that Janus honored Saturn's sudden disappearance with festivals, the Saturnalia, and that December was dedicated to Saturn and January to Janus.

Worship and religion

His most famous celebration was the Saturnalia in December, a time of feasting, gift-giving, free speech and a temporary reversal of social order, when slaves enjoyed great liberty. The temple of Saturn at the foot of the Capitoline Hill was one of Rome's oldest, and it housed the state treasury (aerarium) during the Republic and the early Empire. Varro linked his name to satus, meaning sowing. In Cyrene, worshippers crowned themselves with fresh figs and sent him cakes, since they considered him the discoverer of honey and fruit. The Romans also called him Sterculius, because he was said to have been the first to fertilize fields with dung.

Legacy

The planet Saturn and, in English, the day of the week Saturday both take his name. The sickle that marked him became the scythe of Father Time, and in late antiquity his figure merged with other deities of time, eventually appearing with wings. His Saturnalia also left the idea of a festival in which social hierarchies are briefly overturned.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Virgil, Macrobius, Lactantius, Aulus Gellius and the Vatican Mythographers mention him, and his name appears in the hymn of the Salian priests and in Cicero's De Natura Deorum.

Trivia

A temple with a treasury. The temple of Saturn in the Roman Forum housed the state treasury and archives of the Roman Republic and early Empire.
Two symbolic partners. Saturn had two partners representing different aspects of the god: his wife Ops, whose name means wealth and abundance, and Lua, linked to destruction and to the weapons of defeated enemies.
A planet bearing his name. The planet Saturn and the day Saturday in English are both named after this god.
