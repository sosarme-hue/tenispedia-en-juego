The Buddha (Buddha Gautama in Hinduism)

In Hinduism, the Buddha is considered the ninth of the ten major avatars of the god Vishnu. His place in the Hindu pantheon reflects the complex relationship between Hinduism and Buddhism in India.

The myth

According to the Agni Purana, Vishnu took birth on earth because the daityas, a race of asuras, kept defeating the devas in battle. To restore the natural order, the god deluded them with his teachings. The daityas abandoned the path laid out by the Vedas as they converted to Buddhism, lost their dharma, became dasyus at the end of the Kali Yuga and were sent to naraka. The Buddha also brings about the mixing of the varnas and the domination of the mlecchas, the foreigners regarded as barbarians by Vedic culture. Tradition assigns Adi Shankaracharya and later Vaishnava acharyas the task of restoring theism.

The chronology is hard to pin down. According to Wendy Doniger, the myth of the Buddha avatar appeared in the pre-Gupta period, when brahmanical traditions felt threatened by Buddhism, Jainism and foreign invaders, and Hindus came to regard him as an avatar of Vishnu between 450 CE and the sixth century. He first appears in the Vishnu Purana. The cosmic cycle completes the story: dharma is destroyed in the Kali Yuga and restored in the Satya Yuga, when Vishnu returns as Kalki. Some texts name the Buddha's father Anjana or Jina rather than Suddhodana, a confusion that reflects the influence of Jainism.

Worship and religion

Worship of the Buddha as an avatar is rare. The Varahapurana advises that whoever desires beauty should worship him, and some Hindu temples predating the fourteenth century include Buddha reliefs shown with the same reverence as other avatars. Yet he is seldom worshipped as Krishna or Rama are. Today many Hindus regard him as a holy being awakened in India, and some honour him alongside other gods during their festivals.

Legacy

Buddhist Jataka tales cast Krishna as a student of the Buddha in a past life, and the Dasaratha Jataka has the Buddha identify himself with Rama. The reformer B. R. Ambedkar rejected the idea that the Buddha was an incarnation of Vishnu, and other contemporary Hindus reject it too. In 1999, the Shankaracharya of Kanchi called for such beliefs not to be promoted.

Ancient sources: He appears as an avatar of Vishnu in the Harivamsa, the Vishnu Purana, the Bhagavata Purana, the Garuda Purana, the Agni Purana, the Padma Purana and the Skanda Purana.

Trivia

A passage in the Vishnu Purana. The Buddha appears in the Vishnu Purana, one of the oldest Puranic texts in which he is named as an avatar.
A vow against the avatar. Among his twenty-two vows, B. R. Ambedkar included a rejection of the belief that the Buddha was an incarnation of Vishnu.
