legendary land of Chinese mythology

Mount Penglai

Mount Penglai, sometimes called Penglai Island, is a legendary land in Chinese mythology, home of the immortals in the sea east of China. Its image travelled to Japan as Hōrai, where it developed a distinct myth that Japanese tradition reshaped over time.

The myth

According to the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the mountain stood on an island at the eastern end of the Bohai Sea, beside four other islands where immortals lived: Fāngzhàng, Yíngzhōu, Dàiyú and Yuánjiāo. Everything there looks pure white, the palaces are made of gold and silver, and jewels grow on the trees. There is no pain and no winter, and rice bowls and wine cups never run empty. Its enchanted fruits can cure any ailment, grant eternal youth and even raise the dead.

According to tradition, the Eight Immortals travelled there to share a feast with the illusionist Anqi Sheng. Qin Shi Huang, searching for the elixir of life, sent expeditions that failed to find it. His servant Xu Fu, sent to look for it, is said to have found Japan instead.

In the Japanese version, retold by Lafcadio Hearn in Kwaidan, Hōrai loses much of its magic: there is sorrow, death and bitter winters. Its atmosphere is made of countless souls whose knowledge is inhaled by those who breathe them, and its people are small fairies whose hearts never grow. Hearn notes that it is also called Shinkiro, meaning mirage.

Legacy

In Japanese literature and art of the Tokugawa period (1615-1868), Hōrai appears in a form closer to the original Chinese tradition than to Hearn's version. In the Japanese tale The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter, the isle of Hōrai holds trees of precious metals and gemstones, and retrieving a jewelled branch from one of them is one of the five impossible tasks Kaguya-hime sets her suitors.

Ancient sources: The Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shan Hai Jing) places the mountain in the Bohai Sea, and Xu Jing's Illustrated Account of the Embassy to Goryeo (1124) locates it on an island near Korea.

Trivia

A mirage. Lafcadio Hearn notes that Hōrai is also called Shinkiro, meaning mirage, the vision of the impossible.
A disputed island. Japan, Korea, Jeju, Taiwan and other places have been proposed as its real location, though none is certain.
