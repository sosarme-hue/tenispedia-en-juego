underworld bridge in Norse mythology

Gjallarbrú

Gjallarbrú is the bridge that spans the river Gjöll in Norse mythology and must be crossed to reach Hel, the realm of the dead. It matters mainly for its role in the myth of Baldr's death, since it is where Hermód must state his identity to the maiden who guards the crossing. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning, the bridge appears in the story of Hermód's ride to Hel to try to bring Baldr back. After nine nights through deep, dark valleys, he reaches a bridge roofed with glittering gold. There the giantess Módgud challenges him, asking his name and lineage. She tells him that five companies of the dead crossed the day before, yet the bridge trembles no more under them than under him alone, and that he does not look dead. When he explains his errand and asks whether Baldr has come that way, she confirms that he has and points him onward, down the road to the north. The scholar John Lindow notes its parallel with Bifröst, the bridge linking earth with the sky and the world of the gods. Gjallarbrú serves the same function, but toward the underworld.

The myth

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson describes it in the Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning, within the story of Baldr's death.
