minor Yoruba orisha of dual nature

Logun Ede

Logun Ede is a minor orisha in the Yoruba pantheon, associated with forests and freshwater, and known for alternating between masculine and feminine natures over the year. He remains present in Cuban Santería and in Yoruba communities in Brazil.

The myth

According to Yoruba tradition, Logun Ede is the son of Oshún and Oshosi. His most distinctive trait is his dual nature: for six months of the year he appears as a man and dwells in the forests, like his father, and for the other six months he appears as a woman, lives in rivers and freshwater lakes, and keeps his mother's charms.

Worship and religion

In Cuba, where Santería also calls him Laro or Larooye, his worship has declined considerably. Elderly olorishas used to answer that Logun Ede is 'Oshún ni', meaning 'he is Oshún herself', presenting him as Oshún's masculine counterpart, just as other orishas have complementary partners. In Brazil, by contrast, his worship is deeply rooted, especially in Rio de Janeiro and Bahia. He is greeted with 'Ea Ea Logun!', associated with blue and yellow, iron and gold, and offered red maize accompanied by an egg.

Trivia

Six months of each sex. Tradition assigns Logun Ede an alternation of six months as a man and six months as a woman, which sets him apart from most orishas.
A orisha with two nicknames. In Cuba he is called Laro or Larooye, while his honorific names Omo Alade and Oba l'oge refer to a crowned prince and a lover of fine dress.
