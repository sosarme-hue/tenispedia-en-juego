Roman goddess of security and stability

Securitas

Securitas was the Roman goddess of security and stability, both of the individual and, in a broader sense, of the Roman state. Her image on coins made her a recognisable figure throughout the imperial period.

The myth

According to tradition, Securitas was the daughter of Disciplina and sister of Humanitas, Frugalitas and Auctoritas, a group of personified virtues linked to the orderly and prosperous life of the Roman citizen. Her connection with personal and public stability made her more a figure of collective well-being than a character in stories, since the surviving sources record no adventures or narrative episodes involving her.

Most of what we know comes from coinage. She appears as a matron holding a spear, a cornucopia and an olive branch, symbols of protection, plenty and peace. On many coins she leans on a column, a detail that suggests firmness and support.

Worship and religion

The surviving sources do not describe temples, festivals or priests devoted to her. An imperial-period Latin inscription from Sardinia, however, describes the tomb of Titus Vinius as a shrine of Securitas, showing that her cult also reached funerary settings. She is identified with the Greek goddess Soteria or Soteira, whose name means 'the saviour'.

Legacy

Her image became a usual motif on Roman coins in the centuries that followed her first appearance on a coin in 62 AD.

Trivia
