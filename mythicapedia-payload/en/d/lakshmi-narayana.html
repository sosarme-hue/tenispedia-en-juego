joint form of Vishnu and Lakshmi in Hinduism

Lakshmi Narayana

Lakshmi Narayana is the joint representation of the god Vishnu (also called Narayana) and his consort Lakshmi, goddess of wealth and prosperity. It is one of the most revered images in Vaishnavism, the Hindu tradition centred on Vishnu.

The myth

In the Samudra Manthana story, found in several Puranas, the devas and asuras churned the Ocean of Milk with Vishnu's help, in his Kurma tortoise avatar. From the churning emerged many treasures, including Lakshmi, and the devas asked Vishnu to marry her, joining auspiciousness to divinity and restoring cosmic order.

In the Legend of Tirumala, the sage Bhrigu must choose which deity should receive a sacrifice. After rejecting Brahma, Indra and Shiva, he reaches Vaikuntha, where he sees Lakshmi massaging Vishnu's feet. Angered, he kicks Vishnu's chest. Vishnu receives him courteously, and Bhrigu decides the sacrifice belongs to Vishnu. Lakshmi, offended that her consort did not react, descends to earth as Padmavati, daughter of a Chola king, and Vishnu takes the form of Srinivasa to marry her again.

Worship and religion

The couple is worshipped in many temples and in household shrines by Vaishnavas, who believe their worship brings welfare, success and prosperity. In Sri Vaishnavism, devotees wear the Urdhva Pundra mark, said to represent Vishnu's feet with Lakshmi in the red streak between them. The worship remains alive today in India and Nepal.

Ancient sources: The Puranas, such as the Vishnu Purana and the Bhagavata Purana, along with the Ramayana and the Mahabharata, explore this joint representation.
