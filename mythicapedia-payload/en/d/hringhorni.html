Baldr's ship in Norse mythology

Hringhorni

Hringhorni, the "ship with a circle on the stem", is the vessel of the god Baldr in Norse mythology, described in the sources as the greatest of all ships. It matters because it serves as the setting for Baldr's funeral, one of the best-known episodes of his death myth.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, after Baldr was killed by Höðr, through the deceit of Loki, the other gods carried his body down to the shore and laid it on his ship. They meant to launch the vessel into the sea and set it alight as a funeral pyre, but the ship was so enormous that no one could move it. Only with the help of the giantess Hyrrokkin, fetched from Jötunheim, did it finally slide down the rollers, with a single mighty push that sent flames shooting up and the earth trembling, much to Thor's annoyance.

Nanna, Baldr's wife, had died of grief and was also placed on the pyre. As Thor was blessing the fire with his hammer Mjölnir, a dwarf named Litr scurried about his feet; Thor kicked him into the flames, and the dwarf burned as well. The meaning of this episode is uncertain, and some scholars have linked it to possible ritual practices. Among the other offerings on the pyre were Draupnir, Odin's gold ring, and Baldr's horse, with all its trappings.

Ancient sources: The Prose Edda of Snorri Sturluson (Gylfaginning), written in the thirteenth century, is the main source for this episode.

Trivia

The largest ship. The sources describe it as the greatest ship ever built, so heavy that not even the gods could move it without help.
A dwarf on the pyre. Thor kicks the dwarf Litr into the fire. Its meaning is debated by scholars.
