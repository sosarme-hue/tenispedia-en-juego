Zetes

Zetes is one of the Boreads, the winged twin sons of the wind god Boreas and Oreithyia, known in Greek myth for their speed and their role in the voyage of the Argonauts. His name means "seeker" or "explorer".

The myth

According to the myth, Zetes and his twin Calais, sons of Boreas and Oreithyia, were born with great beauty, and when they reached puberty, wings grew from their backs. Zetes was so fast that he won the short race at the funeral games of King Pelias. Both brothers joined the Argonauts and helped Phineus escape the Harpies, whom they chased through the air until they collapsed; Iris asked them to spare the creatures.

Another version tells that Phineus, married to Cleopatra, sister of the Boreads, took the Scythian Idaea as his wife. Idaea falsely accused his children of assault, and Zetes and Calais freed them from a pit where they were tortured. Heracles then killed Phineus. During a stop, Hylas vanished, and Jason sailed on without searching for him. When some tried to force the helmsman to change course, the brothers stepped in and steered the ship away. For this, Heracles killed them on the island of Tenos, where a funeral monument was raised for them. Moved with pity, the gods turned the twins into winds.

Trivia

Wings from puberty. Zetes and Calais grew wings when they reached puberty, a trait inherited from their father, the north wind god Boreas.
Fast without wings. Zetes was famous for running very fast even without using his wings, and he won the short race at the funeral games of King Pelias.
