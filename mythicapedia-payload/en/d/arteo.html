legendary king of the Medes

Artaeus

Artaeus is a legendary king of Media, named by the Greek historian Ctesias as the sixth ruler of that kingdom before the Persian conquest. He matters mainly for the story of his quarrel with Parsondes, which led to a lasting enmity between the Medes and the Cadusians.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Ctesias, Artaeus succeeded Arbianes on the throne of Media, not Sardanapalus. He was the father of Artachaees and Azanes.

During his reign a dispute arose with a man named Parsondes, who asked the king for the post of satrap of Babylonia. That office was then held by Nanarus, whom Parsondes disliked, partly because of his appearance. After several attempts to strip Nanarus of power with Artaeus's help, Parsondes took offence at the king's decision and fled to the Cadusian tribe with three thousand foot soldiers and a thousand horsemen. There he married the sister of one of the most influential people in the region.

At the head of the Cadusians, Parsondes defeated Artaeus and devastated Media. At the end of his life he made his followers swear that no successor would make peace with the Medes, and so the enmity endured.

Ancient sources: Ctesias of Cnidus, in his history of Assyria and Media, is the main ancient source that names Artaeus as the sixth king of the Medes.
