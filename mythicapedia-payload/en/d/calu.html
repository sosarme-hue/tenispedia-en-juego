Etruscan god of the underworld

Calu

Calu was an Etruscan underworld deity, a god of the dead linked to the wolf and, according to some sources, an epithet of Śuri. His figure shows links with wolf-related cults in Rome and the Faliscan world.

The myth

According to Etruscan tradition, Calu was an ancient god of the dead and lord of the underworld, later displaced by Aita, the Etruscan equivalent of the Greek Hades. He is recognised by his wolf traits: he appears as a wolf or as a man wearing a wolf-skin.

Another version presents him as an epithet of Śuri, the chthonic fire god in his role as lord of the underworld, and his name was also used as a synonym for the underworld itself.

Worship and religion

Although Aita was mostly a mythological figure, Calu did receive worship and popular veneration. His images seem to share features with the Roman Lupercalia and with the Faliscan cult of the Hirpi Sorani, the “wolves of Soranus”, whose name derives from hirpus, “wolf”.

Ancient sources: The Tabula Capuana mentions Calu and prescribes offering the god “perfect victims”.

Trivia

Name for the underworld. The name Calu was used as a synonym for the underworld itself, much as Hades was in Greek.
Wolves of Soranus. The Faliscan Hirpi Sorani were called “wolves of Soranus”, a name linked to the word hirpus, “wolf”.
