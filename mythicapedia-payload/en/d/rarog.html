fire demon of Slavic folklore

Raróg

The raróg is a fire demon of Slavic folklore, often pictured as a blazing falcon. Related to the fire birds of other traditions, it remains present in popular culture today.

The myth

According to Czech folklore, the raróg, also called raroh, hatches from an egg incubated for nine days and nine nights on a stove, and it may appear as a fiery falcon or as a dragon.

In Polish folklore, by contrast, it is a tiny bird that fits in a pocket and brings happiness to whoever keeps it.

Legacy

A volcanic caldera on Io, Jupiter's moon, was named Raróg Patera after a massive eruption recorded in 2013. The bird-shaped logo of the Polish video game company CD Projekt is called the Raróg, and a Ukrainian unmanned systems regiment bears the same name.

Trivia

An egg on the stove. In Czech folklore, the raróg is hatched by keeping an egg warm on a stove for nine days and nine nights.
A pocket bird. In Poland it is described as a bird so small that it can be carried in a pocket.
A name on Io. A caldera on Jupiter's moon Io bears its name after a massive eruption observed in 2013.
