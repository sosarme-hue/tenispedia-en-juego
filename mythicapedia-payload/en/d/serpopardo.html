hybrid creature of Egyptian and Mesopotamian art

Serpopard

The serpopard is a mythical hybrid animal known from ancient Egyptian and Mesopotamian art. Its name is a modern coinage blending "serpent" and "leopard", although the images are also read as lionesses with serpent-like necks.

The myth

No ancient text gives the creature a name; "serpopard" is a modern invention. Images appear on predynastic Egyptian cosmetic palettes and, most often, on Uruk-period cylinder seals of around 3500–3000 BC, where two examples usually face each other with necks intertwined. It also occurs on some birth tusks.

In Egypt it is shown subdued or restrained, as on the Narmer Palette, or attacking other animals, which suggests a chaos beyond the borders that the king had to master. In Mesopotamia, some scholars link it to the chthonic side of a god of natural vitality, present in all life rising from the earth. Its appearance on birth tusks has also led to the idea that it guarded childbirth and formed a barrier between the netherworld and the human world.

Legacy

Young-earth creationists have wrongly presented it as a surviving non-avian dinosaur. In popular culture it appears in Rick Riordan's The Kane Chronicles, where Set sends two serpopards that Bast defeats, and in the card game Magic: The Gathering, in the Egyptian-themed world of Amonkhet.

Trivia

A modern name. "Serpopard" is a modern compound of "serpent" and "leopard". No ancient text gave the creature a name.
A mistaken dinosaur. Young-earth creationists have wrongly presented it as a surviving dinosaur.
