Hindu deity of preservation of the universe

Vishnu

Vishnu is one of the principal deities of Hinduism and, within Vaishnavism, the Supreme Being. As the Preserver of the Trimurti, he sustains cosmic order (dharma) and returns to the world whenever evil threatens it.

The myth

According to tradition, Vishnu rests on the serpent Shesha in the primordial ocean of milk, Kshira Sagara, alongside his consort Lakshmi. When the world is threatened by evil, chaos or destructive forces, he descends as an avatar to restore order. His ten primary avatars, the Dashavatara, include Rama and Krishna, the most widely worshipped.

Other texts offer different accounts: in the Mahabharata, Vishnu, as Narayana, announces his incarnations to Narada, and the Padma Purana lists 108 of his names.

Worship and religion

His worship spreads across India and beyond, with temples dedicated above all to Rama and Krishna. In the south, the cult of Perumal, identified with Vishnu, centres on places such as Tirupati and Srirangam, and Vishnu is one of the five deities in the smarta panchayatana puja. For his devotees, Vaishnavism remains a living faith today.

Ancient sources: He appears only rarely in the Rig Veda, which devotes five hymns to him, and becomes prominent in the Brahmanas, the Mahabharata, the Bhagavad Gita and the Puranas. In Tamil, the Paripadal and the Tolkappiyam refer to him as Mayon.
