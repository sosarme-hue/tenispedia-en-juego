Roman goddess of salt water, consort of Neptune

Salacia

Salacia is the Roman goddess of salt water and the consort of Neptune. Often identified with the Greek Amphitrite, wife of Poseidon, her name, from Latin sal, meaning salt, denotes the open sea.

The myth

According to the tradition, Neptune wished to marry Salacia, but she, in awe of her distinguished suitor and anxious to keep her virginity, slipped out of his sight and hid in the Atlantic. The grieving god sent a dolphin to find her and persuade her to return and share his throne. Salacia agreed, and Neptune was so delighted that he rewarded the dolphin with a place among the stars, where it forms the constellation Delphinus.

The couple had three children, the most famous being Triton, half man and half fish. Varro hints at the union, while Seneca, Augustine and Servius affirm it, though some Roman authors name Amphitrite instead and leave Salacia in the background. Servius suggested she might be merely an epithet of Venus or Tethys, and others linked her with Venilia, the other consort of Neptune. Dumézil read the pair as two aspects of the god: the wild waters and the calm ones.

She is usually shown as a beautiful nymph crowned with seaweed, seated beside Neptune or riding with him in a pearl-shell chariot pulled by dolphins or sea-horses, attended by tritons and nereids. Apuleius places her in the retinue accompanying Venus to the ocean, her skirt heaped with fish.

Worship and religion

Roman priests invoked her under the attribute Salacia Neptuni, which Forsythe renders as “effervescence of Neptune”. A Portuguese legend from Alcácer do Sal tells how, after a pirate raid, the townspeople vowed to build her a temple if she heard them, and a storm then sank the raiders’ ships. The tale offers no evidence of an actual living cult.

Legacy

The trans-Neptunian object (120347) Salacia bears her name in her honour.

Ancient sources: Varro, Seneca, Augustine, Servius and Apuleius mention her, and Aulus Gellius records the invocation “Salacia Neptuni” in his Attic Nights.

Trivia

A dolphin in the sky. According to the story, the dolphin that searched for Salacia was rewarded with a place among the stars, where it forms the constellation Delphinus.
A promised temple. The Alcácer do Sal legend links the goddess to a vow to build a temple made during a pirate attack.
