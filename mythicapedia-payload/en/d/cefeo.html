king of Aethiopia in Greek myth

Cepheus

Cepheus is the king of Aethiopia in Greek mythology, father of Andromeda and husband of Cassiopeia. He matters because he is a central figure in the myth of Perseus and Andromeda, and because he gives his name to a northern constellation.

The myth

According to tradition, Cepheus was the son of Belus and Anchinoe, although other authors made him the son of Agenor or Phoenix. He ruled together with Cassiopeia over the Cepheni, a people who were placed at times in Aethiopia, at times on the Euphrates, and at times in Joppa in Phoenicia or Palestine.

The story begins with Cassiopeia's boast that her daughter Andromeda was more beautiful than the Nereids. Poseidon, offended, sent a flood and the sea monster Cetus. The oracle of Ammon, identified with Zeus at the oasis of Siwa, declared that the calamity would end only if Andromeda were offered to the monster. Cepheus chained his daughter to a rock by the shore, but before the beast arrived, Perseus appeared, flying home with the head of Medusa. He fell in love with the girl, promised to kill the monster if she would marry him, and kept his word. Later, when Phineus and his men plotted against him, Perseus turned them to stone with Medusa's head.

After a year or so at his father-in-law's court, Perseus set off for Seriphos with his wife. Since Cepheus had no heir, the couple allowed him to adopt their firstborn, Perses, who in Greek folk etymology would give his name to the Persians. When Cepheus died, the throne passed to Perses.

Legacy

Cepheus gives his name to a northern constellation, placed beside Cassiopeia, Andromeda and Perseus. The lunar crater Cepheus also commemorates this mythical figure.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, in Latin sources, names Agenor as the betrothed of Andromeda.

Trivia

A red spring. According to an apocryphal tradition, Perseus washed the monster's blood off in a spring near Joppa, which turned red as a result.
The origin of a name. Greek folk etymology made Perses, Cepheus's adopted son, the figure who would give his name to the Persians.
