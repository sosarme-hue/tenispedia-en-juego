serpentine creature of Scandinavian folklore

Bøyg

The Bøyg, also known as the "Great Bøyg of Etnedal," is a creature from Scandinavian folklore, usually described as a giant, slimy, serpent-like troll that stands in the way of travellers. It is one of the best-known legendary beings associated with the regions of Telemark and Gudbrandsdalen.

The myth

According to tradition, the Bøyg is a great troll associated with Telemark and Gudbrandsdalen, and especially with the Great Bøyg of Etnedal. It is commonly pictured as a giant, slimy serpent whose presence hinders anyone travelling the roads. Its name means something like "bend," "twist" or "curve," a reference to the winding shape of its body.

The Bøyg also appears in the folk tale of Per Gynt, the story that later became the basis for Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt. In these tales the creature works mainly as a danger that travellers must get past, rather than as a being with a detailed genealogy or fate.

Legacy

Its reach beyond local legend owes much to Henrik Ibsen. The folk tale of Per Gynt, in which the Bøyg appears, served as the basis for his play Peer Gynt, which gave the creature wider exposure in literature.

Trivia

A name that describes its body. The word Bøyg means "bend," "twist" or "curve," which fits the image of a serpent that coils and winds.
From folk tale to stage play. The Bøyg appears in the Per Gynt tale, which inspired Henrik Ibsen's drama Peer Gynt.
