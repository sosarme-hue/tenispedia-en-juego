Aetolian slave woman in the household of Athamas and Ino

Antiphera

Antiphera is an Aetolian slave woman serving Athamas and Ino, the king and queen of Boeotia. Her story, preserved mainly by Plutarch, was used to explain why slave women were barred from certain shrines of Ino.

The myth

According to the tradition, Athamas fell for Antiphera and began a relationship with her that he tried to keep secret. When Ino discovered it, the jealous queen killed Melicertes, one of her sons by Athamas, in a fit of rage. Ancient accounts preserve this episode and say little more about the slave woman's fate.

The scholar Joseph Fontenrose compared the story with the myths of Aedon and Procne, royal women of the same Attica-Boeotia region who killed their sons to take revenge on unfaithful husbands.

Worship and religion

In antiquity the tale served to justify a custom: slave women were not allowed to enter the shrine of Mater Matuta, the Roman equivalent of Ino or Leucothea. In Chaeronea, Plutarch's hometown, the guardian of Leucothea's temple would brandish a whip and shout that no slave and no Aetolian was to enter.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, a Greek philosopher of the Roman imperial era, is the main source for her story.
