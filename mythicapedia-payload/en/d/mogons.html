Celtic god of northern Roman Britain

Mogons

Mogons, "the Great One," was a Celtic god worshipped mainly in the north of Roman Britain, in the military zone of Hadrian's Wall. He is known only from Latin inscriptions and a few related divine names in Gaul and the Rhineland, so much about him remains uncertain.

The myth

Almost no myth survives for Mogons, as no texts or images describe him. His name, from a Celtic root meaning "great", suggests a powerful deity, but no source tells of his origin, family or deeds.

A later local legend from Risingham holds that the god Mogon had long defended the place against a pagan prince called Soldane. This story, recorded in the time of William Camden, is far later than the altars and tells us nothing reliable about the ancient cult.

Worship and religion

Seven British dedications survive, most of them from soldiers of the frontier garrison, at sites such as Old Penrith, Risingham, High Rochester, Netherby and Vindolanda. At Netherby he is paired with the native deity Vitiris, and at High Rochester he is invoked in the plural as the Moguntes. As the origins of the dedicators are unknown, his cult is hard to place precisely.

Legacy

His name lives on in Mogontiacum, the Roman name of Mainz in Germany, which carries the same element with a place-name suffix.

Trivia

A name with many spellings. In Britain the name is written in several forms, such as Mogons, Moguns or Mogontis, and also without the g as Mounus.
Altars found in the river. According to a tradition current in the time of William Camden, two of the Risingham altars were found in the river.
