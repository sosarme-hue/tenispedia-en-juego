Mesopotamian god of livestock

Lahar

Lahar is a Mesopotamian deity linked to livestock, especially sheep. Scholars still debate whether Lahar was male or female, and the god appears in origin stories and in a quarrel with the grain goddess Ashnan.

The myth

In the main text, Lahar and Ashnan, the two siblings were created in the creation chamber of the Sacred Mound, probably by Enki, although the poem does not say so clearly. Enlil gave them fields and farms: Lahar tended livestock and Ashnan grew crops. One day, after drinking alcohol, they quarrelled over which of them provided humans with more useful goods. The dispute was settled by Enlil, who, following Enki's suggestion, declared the grain goddess the winner.

A distinct version appears in the Theogony of Dunnu, also called the Harab Myth, known from a single tablet. There Lahar is a son of Šumugan and the personified sea. He kills his father, marries the sea and has a son whose name is damaged. Some scholars suggest he may be identified with the divine shepherd Gayu.

Ancient sources: The main source is Lahar and Ashnan, a debate between sheep and grain; the Theogony of Dunnu offers a distinct version.

Trivia

A name meaning ewe. His name was written with the logogram dU8, meaning "ewe", and derives from the Akkadian laḫru, which has the same meaning.
A passage in the Udug Hul series. According to Markham J. Geller, a passage from the poem about the origin of Lahar and Ashnan is quoted word for word in the incantation series Udug Hul.
