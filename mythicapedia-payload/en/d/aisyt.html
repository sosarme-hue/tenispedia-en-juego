Yakut goddess of fertility and births

Aisyt

Aisyt (also Ajysyt or Ajyhyt) is a fertility goddess of the Yakut people, from the Lena River region of Siberia, and an important figure in Turkic mythology. Her name means 'birthgiver', and she is also known as the 'Mother of Cradles'.

The myth

According to Yakut tradition, Aisyt looks after births. She brings the soul from heaven to the baby and records it in the Golden Book of Fate. She is considered the daughter of Yer Tanrı, and it is said that she lives on a mountain peak, in a seven-storey house, from where she controls the fate of the world.

One legend tells that she emerged from the roots of the Cosmic Tree, or from the world pillar of Yryng Aar Tojon, beside a lake of milk, and appeared to a pale young man. By suckling him, she multiplied his strength a hundredfold.

Worship and religion

Women giving birth would call on her as an intermediary, believing that this would ease the pain of labour. The Yakut also revere a variety of ajyy, deities with specific roles: Njelbey Ajyhyt presides over the births of children, Djøhøgøj Tojon over the breeding of horses, Ihegej Iejehsit over that of oxen, and Noruluya looks after dogs and foxes. Depending on the animal, the word ajysyt is understood as masculine or feminine.

Legacy

Aisyt appears in Judy Chicago's installation The Dinner Party, where her name is one of the 999 on the Heritage Floor.

Trivia

A golden book. According to tradition, Aisyt keeps a golden book in which she records each person who is born, as if noting their destiny from the very first moment.
A name that changes gender. In the Yakut language, the word ajysyt is masculine when it refers to male animals, such as stallions or bulls, and feminine when it refers to mares or cows.
