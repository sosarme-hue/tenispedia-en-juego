Hindu god of love and desire

Kamadeva

Kamadeva is the Hindu god of love, attraction, pleasure and beauty, and the personification of the concept of kama, or desire. His figure, traced from the Vedas to the Puranas, matters because it shows how desire was seen as part of creation and of the continuity of life.

The myth

In the Puranas, Kamadeva is usually a mind-born son (manasaputra) of the creator god Brahma. Other texts give different family ties: the Shiva Purana presents him as a creation of Brahma himself, while the Skanda Purana makes him a brother of Prasuti, both children of Shatarupa. All sources agree that he is married to Rati, and the Bhagavata Purana says he was reborn as Pradyumna, son of Krishna.

His best-known myth tells of his destruction. In the Kumara Sambhava, Kandarpa tries to help Parvati win Shiva by shooting his arrows of desire at the meditating god. Shiva is briefly distracted, then notices, opens his third eye and reduces Kandarpa to ashes. The loss of desire threatened to leave the world barren, and the gods, fearing that no humans would be left to make offerings, asked for his return. Shiva restored him to life, though only as a mental image, so that the continuity of life was preserved.

Worship and religion

The Holi festival, in which participants throw coloured powder at one another, is believed to commemorate this legend in part, especially through the fireworks lit that day. Celibate-leaning Hindu fundamentalists read the myth as a teaching from Shiva that values emotional love above physical lust.

Legacy

His name lies behind the Kama Sutra, the treatise on love attributed to Vatsyayana, which takes its inspiration from this god. His many epithets, such as Manmatha, "agitator of the mind", or Pushpabana, "he of the flower arrows", show the power ascribed to him in Sanskrit literature.

Ancient sources: His roots appear in the hymns of the Rig Veda and Atharva Veda, where desire is an abstract cosmic principle; he is later personified in the Puranas, the Mahabharata and the Kumara Sambhava.
