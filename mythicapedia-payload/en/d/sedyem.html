Egyptian god of creative hearing

Sedjem

Sedjem, also written Sedyem, is in Egyptian mythology the personification of hearing, one of the creative forces that accompany the god Ra. He matters because he explains, through religion, how the peoples of the world were differentiated.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, the creator god Ra relied on auxiliary forces to plan and carry out his work: Sia, the intelligence that draws up the plan; Hu, the creative word; and Heka, magic. Over time this group grew to include Sedjem, whose name means hearing, and Iri, meaning doing. Both acted as divine assistants to the scribe god Thoth.

In another version, Ra had four faculties: Hu, taste and speech; Maa, sight; Sedjem, hearing; and Sia, understanding. In the New Kingdom, Sedjem became one of the fourteen creative forces or kas of Amun-Ra. He was held responsible for the differentiation of human peoples: Egyptians, Nubians, Libyans and Asiatics. In Thebes, one of Ptah's titles was mesedjer sedjem, "the ear that hears". He is shown as a human figure with a bovine ear on his head.

Ancient sources: He appears in the temples of Seti I and Ramesses II at Abydos, where he serves as an assistant to Thoth.
