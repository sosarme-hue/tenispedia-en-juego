Korean sky god

Hwanin

Hwanin is the sky god of Korean mythology, also called Haneunim or Hananim ("Lord of Heaven"). He matters mainly as the grandfather of Dangun, the legendary founder of the Korean nation.

The myth

According to tradition, Hwanin sent his son, Prince Hwanung, to rule Earth with three Heavenly Seals and three thousand followers. Hwanung founded his holy city beneath a sacred sandalwood tree. There, a she-bear who longed to be human endured twenty-one days of confinement and became a woman, while a tiger ran away.

The woman prayed to become a mother and gave birth to Dangun, the first human king of Korea according to the tale, who founded the kingdom of Joseon in 2333 BC. Later he became Sansin, the Mountain God.

Worship and religion

Under the name Okhwang Sangje he is a deity in the Poncheongyo religion. In the more Taoist-aligned parts of these faiths he is identified with the Jade Emperor, and in the more Buddhist-aligned parts with Indra.

Legacy

Today, Haneunim and Hananim are used as Korean translations of the English word God. He is also the chief deity in the Jeusangist family of faiths.
