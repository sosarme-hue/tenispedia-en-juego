Muse of memory in Greek mythology

Mneme

Mneme, also called Mnemea, is one of the Muses of Greek mythology, associated with memory. According to Pausanias, she was one of the three original Muses of Mount Helicon, before they were replaced by the familiar group of nine.

The myth

According to Pausanias, the 2nd-century travel writer, the Aloadae believed the Muses were only three, and named them Melete, Mneme and Aoede. Mneme, whose Greek name means «memory», thus held the middle place in that trio, in a tradition that sets these goddesses on Helicon and presents them as guardians of song and learning.

Pausanias adds that the Macedonian Pierus replaced those three with the nine Muses known later. He presents this version as a local tradition rather than as historical fact. The scholar Robin Hard considers it improbable that the names Pausanias gives come from a genuinely ancient tradition, so the existence of this original trio remains a matter of debate.

Legacy

Her name lives on in modern astronomy and geography. Mneme is one of Jupiter's moons, named in honour of the Muse, and Mneme Lake in Antarctica also bears her name.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his second-century AD travel guide to Greece, is the main source that names Mneme as one of the three original Muses of Helicon.

Trivia

An Antarctic lake. Mneme Lake, in Antarctica, is named after the Muse.
A moon of Jupiter. One of Jupiter's moons bears Mneme's name as a tribute to the Muse.
