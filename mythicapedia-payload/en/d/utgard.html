stronghold of the giants in Norse mythology

Útgard

Útgard, whose Old Norse name means outer enclosures or outyards, is the stronghold of the giants in Jötunheim, ruled by the king Útgarða-Loki. It is best known for the story in which Thor and his companions are outwitted there, a tale that shows the gods defeated by greater forces.

The myth

According to the myth, Útgard was the stronghold of the giants, set in Jötunheim and surrounded by ice and snow. Sources link it with the king Útgarða-Loki, and some accounts say it was founded by the giant Narvi.

In the Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, Thor travels there with Loki and Þjálfi. On the road they meet the giant Skrymir, who becomes their guide. That night Thor cannot untie Skrymir's food bag. Once at the stronghold, he loses nearly every trial: he cannot empty the horn of ale in three swallows, he fails to throw down an old woman, and he cannot lift the king's cat off the floor. Loki loses an eating contest against Fire, and Þjálfi loses a race against Thought.

When they leave, Útgarða-Loki reveals the deception. The bag was tied with steel bands. The horn reached the Ocean, which Thor lowered but could not drain. The old woman was Old Age personified, whom no one can defeat, and the cat was Jörmungandr, the Midgard Serpent, whose body lies in the depths of the world.

Ancient sources: The Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, and the 12th-century Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A revealing name. The name combines the prefix ut-, which marks exclusion or exteriority, with garðr, meaning field, garden or district.
A chained Útgarða-Loki. In the 12th-century Gesta Danorum he is shown chained in a foul cave, very unlike the open-air arenas of the story of Thor.
