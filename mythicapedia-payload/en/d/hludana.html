Germanic goddess of fame

Hludana

Hludana is a Germanic goddess known from five Latin inscriptions from the Rhineland and Frisia, all dating from 197 to 235 AD. Her name, meaning «fame», has been linked to the Frankish war-leaders.

The myth

The inscriptions offer almost no stories: no tales survive about her origins, family or deeds, so her mythic role has been reconstructed from her name and context. It has been proposed, without proof, that she protected the first organization of the Franks, a secret group of officers in Roman auxiliary troops, which would explain the secrecy surrounding their beginnings.

Worship and religion

One dedication was made by a group of fishermen at Beetgum and accompanied a carving of a seated goddess, of which only the base survives today. The name appears in variant forms such as Hluθena, Hlucena or Hludana, and is abbreviated at Nijmegen.

Legacy

Jacob Grimm identified her with Hlóðyn, the Norse earth goddess. Some linguists connect her name with the Greek kludōn, «high waves», and with the wind Euroclydon; Walther Kuhn suggested a link with Cleito, Poseidon's wife. No proven connection to Holda exists.

Ancient sources: Five Latin inscriptions from the second and third centuries AD, from the Rhineland, Münstereifel and Frisia.

Trivia

A fishermen's dedication. The Beetgum inscription was dedicated by a group of fishermen, showing that her cult reached coastal communities too.
A name with variants. Depending on the inscription, the name could be written in several forms, such as Hluθena, Hlucena or Hludana.
