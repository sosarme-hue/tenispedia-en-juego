Egyptian bull-shaped god

Bata

Bata is an Egyptian deity shown as a bull, worshipped in the New Kingdom alongside his brother Anubis in the 17th Upper Egyptian nome. He is best known as the protagonist of a literary tale, the Tale of Two Brothers.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the texts, Bata is the hero of the Tale of Two Brothers, a story whose best surviving copy is on the New Kingdom Papyrus D'Orbiney. There he is the brother of Anubis. The Pyramid Texts and Coffin Texts do not mention him, which suggests that his prominence grew later.

His image also changed over time. Until the middle of the Eighteenth Dynasty he was depicted as a ram, and afterwards as a bull. Scholars think he may be identical with Bt, a death god of the Old Kingdom known from the Saqqara necropolis, for instance in the Mastaba of Ti.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Tale of Two Brothers, preserved on the New Kingdom Papyrus D'Orbiney, and in the Ptolemaic Papyrus Jumilhac.

Trivia

A change of animal. Until the middle of the Eighteenth Dynasty he was shown as a ram, and later as a bull.
A possible earlier identity. He may be the same figure as Bt, a death god of the Old Kingdom attested at the Saqqara necropolis.
