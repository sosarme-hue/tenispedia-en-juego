legendary mother of the founder of the Silla kingdom

Lady Saso

Lady Saso is, according to Korean tradition, the mother of Hyeokgeose, founder of the Silla kingdom. Her story comes from the Samguk Yusa and remains disputed because there is little evidence for her origins.

The myth

According to the Samguk Yusa, Lady Saso was a princess of the Buyeo royal family, also known as Seondosan Seongmo. She gave birth to Hyeokgeose, who founded Silla. Later, King Gyeongmyeong honoured her with the title of great king, or queen regnant.

Accounts of her origins differ. The Goryeo official Kim Pusik recorded that a Chinese guide told him she was the daughter of an ancient Chinese emperor, exiled for becoming pregnant without a husband, who had fled east. Kim left this story out of his Samguk Sagi, which focuses on historical facts.

Worship and religion

In Kim Pusik's account, a statue of a woman in a shrine called Wuxingwan was venerated by his Chinese guide and their companions. Beyond this episode, the sources do not describe any rituals dedicated to her.

Legacy

During the Japanese occupation of Korea, her figure was used for nationalist purposes. The pseudo-historical book Hwandan Gogi, written in 1911 and published in 1979, identified her with a Princess Paso of Buyeo. Gye Yeon-su, one of its main supporters, used this idea to claim royal ancestry. Today these claims are considered fiction, and they are thought to have fuelled the controversy surrounding the legend.

Ancient sources: The Samguk Yusa (Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms), volume 5, clause 7, is the first source to mention her.

Trivia

A Chinese guide and a statue. According to Kim Pusik, the guide who told him about Lady Saso said that he and his followers worshipped her.
A twentieth-century book. Hwandan Gogi was written in 1911, many centuries after the ancient accounts, and published in 1979.
