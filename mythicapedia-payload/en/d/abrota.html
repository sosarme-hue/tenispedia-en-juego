wife of Nisus, king of Megara

Abrota

Abrota is, in Greek mythology, the wife of Nisus, king of Megara, and mother of his daughters Scylla, Iphinoe and Eurynome. Daughter of King Onchestus of the Boeotian city of Onchestos, she is remembered for her intelligence and discretion and for the garment that bears her name.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Abrota was the daughter of King Onchestus, eponym of the Boeotian city of Onchestos, and sister of Megareus. Nisus, king of Megara, took her as his wife, and with her he had his daughters Scylla, Iphinoe and Eurynome, although some accounts say she was only the supposed mother of the girls.

The sources describe Abrota as an exceptionally intelligent and very discreet woman. When she died, the Megarians mourned her together. Nisus, wishing her memory and good name to last forever, ordered the women of the city to wear the garment she used to wear. That garment was called aphabroma in her honour.

Legacy

The name of the garment, aphabroma, remained in use in Megara until the time of Plutarch.

Ancient sources: Plutarch records her story and the origin of the garment that bears her name in his writings on Greek customs.

Trivia

A garment with a name of its own. The women's garment Nisus instituted in honour of Abrota was called aphabroma, derived from her own name.
