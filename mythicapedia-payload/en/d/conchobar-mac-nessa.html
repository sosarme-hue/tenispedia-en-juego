king of Ulster in Irish mythology

Conchobar mac Nessa

Conchobar mac Nessa is the king of Ulster in the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology, a semi-historical figure who embodies the ideal of Celtic kingship. His story centres on his capital, Emain Macha, and on his warriors, above all his nephew Cú Chulainn.

The myth

According to tradition, Conchobar was conceived in an unusual way. In one version, his mother, Ness, asks the druid Cathbad when is a good time to conceive a king, and, with no other man around, she becomes pregnant by him. In another, the father is the High King Fachtna Fáthach. The boy is born beside a river that gives him his name, falls into the water, and is lifted out and raised as a son by Cathbad.

When Conchobar is seven, Fergus mac Róich, Ness's husband, agrees to let him rule for a year. When the year ends, the nobles prefer to keep him on the throne, and Fergus allies with Eochu Feidlech to make war. After peace is made, Conchobar marries several of Eochu's daughters, including Medb, and has sons such as Cormac Cond Longas. His most tragic episode is that of Deirdre, whom he had reserved for himself and who flees with Naoise. Conchobar orders Naoise and his brothers killed, and Deirdre ends her own life.

In the account of his death, Cet mac Máach hurls a stolen petrified brain at him with a sling, striking him on the forehead. Seven years later, an eclipse moves him so deeply, according to his druid because of the death of Christ, that his brain bursts. Tradition has Christianised this ending, and some scholars note parallels with the Norse myth of Thor and Hrungnir.

Ancient sources: The Táin Bó Cúailnge, part of the Ulster Cycle, presents him as a good king, while other tales in the same cycle, such as the story of Deirdre, show him as a tyrant.

Trivia

Death at Horseleap. His wound is believed to have taken place in Horseleap, a town in County Offaly.
Born on Christ's day. In one version, his mother delayed the birth until the next day so that he would be born on the same day as Jesus Christ.
