Celtic god of craft, war and light

Lugh (Lugus)

Lugh, Lug or Lugus is one of the most prominent deities of Celtic mythology, known as Samildanach, 'the skilled craftsman', for mastering every art. His worship is attested across several Celtic regions, and his name appears in many place-names.

The myth

According to Irish tales, Lugh is the son of Cian and Ethniu, and he kills his maternal grandfather Balor with a sling stone, as a prophecy had foretold.

To gain entry to the court of King Nuada, who at first refuses him, he presents himself as a carpenter, smith, warrior and magician, and ends by beating the king at hnefatafl. His spear, which only goes out when dipped in human blood, is one of the four treasures the Tuatha Dé Danann brought to Ireland.

Worship and religion

In Iberia he is mostly invoked in the plural, as the Lugoves, in dedications from the Roman period. In Ireland the festival of Lughnasa, held in early August, is linked to him. According to some scholars, a three-night festival for Lugh in Lyon was replaced by the feast of Saint Laurence.

Legacy

Lugdunum, the origin of the name of Lyon, and place-names on the Iberian Peninsula such as Lugo and Lugones. The Romans identified him with Mercury.

Ancient sources: Julius Caesar, in his Commentaries on the Gallic War, describes an important Gaulish god he identifies with Mercury; the Irish Cath Maighe Tuireadh and the Welsh Mabinogion preserve tales of Lugh and Lleu.

Trivia

A three-night festival. In Lyon a major three-night festival honoured Lugh. According to some studies, it was replaced by the festival of Saint Laurence, held from 10 August.
A fiery spear. Lugh's spear burns constantly and loses its fire only when dipped in human blood.
