Mesopotamian warrior god, husband of Ninisina

Pabilsaĝ

Pabilsaĝ is a god of ancient Mesopotamia, associated with archery and warfare, with judicial and cadastral duties and, in his astral aspect, with the constellation Sagittarius. He was the husband of the medicine goddess Ninisina and played a minor role in the pantheon.

The myth

According to tradition, his father was Enlil, lord of the pantheon, and his mother Ninlil, although the oldest texts call him simply the «hero of Enlil», and the parental link appears clearly only in Old Babylonian sources. Closely tied to Ninurta, he came to be seen as a son of Enlil through syncretism. One Old Babylonian formula makes him a son of Anu instead, but this is an isolated witness. Alongside Ninisina he formed a couple who, it is presumed, were the parents of Damu, Gunura and Šumaḫ, though no text states this directly.

Worship and religion

His main cult centre was Larak, and he was also worshipped in Isin, Nippur and Lagash. He remained a minor deity who never received a cult across all of Mesopotamia.

Legacy

His astral aspect links him to the constellation Sagittarius, which Mesopotamian astronomy listed alongside Gula and Damu.

Ancient sources: An Early Dynastic riddle from Lagash calls him the «hero of Enlil», and Old Babylonian texts such as the incantation series Udug Hul name him as administrator of the underworld.

Trivia

A name with many spellings. His name appears in several cuneiform spellings, two of which are already attested in the Early Dynastic period.
An archer with a scorpion's tail. In later art he could be shown as a centaur-like archer with a horse's body and a scorpion's tail, perhaps linked to Sagittarius.
