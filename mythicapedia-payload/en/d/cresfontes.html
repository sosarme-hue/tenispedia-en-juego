king of Messenia in Greek myth

Cresphontes

Cresphontes was a hero of Greek myth, a son of Aristomachus and brother of Temenus and Aristodemus. As a descendant of Heracles, he took part in the Heraclidae's conquest of the Peloponnese, and after the peninsula was divided he became king of Messene.

The myth

Cresphontes was the son of Aristomachus, brother of Temenus and Aristodemus, husband of Merope and father of Aepytus. According to the tradition, he belonged to the Heraclidae, the descendants of Heracles who claimed the Peloponnese as their birthright.

The brothers consulted the oracle, which had told them to attack through the "narrow passage" when the third fruit was ripe. They understood the third fruit to mean the third generation, and the narrow passage to be the straits of Rhium rather than the isthmus of Corinth. They built a fleet at Naupactus, but before they could sail, Aristodemus was struck by lightning, or in another version shot by Apollo, and the fleet was destroyed. The disaster was blamed on one of the Heraclidae, who had slain an Acarnanian soothsayer.

The oracle told Temenus to make an expiatory sacrifice, banish the murderer for ten years, and look out for a man with three eyes to guide them. On his way back to Naupactus he met Oxylus, an Aetolian who had lost one eye and rode a horse or mule; between his two eyes and the missing one, the count reached three, so Temenus took him into his service. With their ships repaired, the Heraclidae crossed to Antirrhium and then to Rhium, where they defeated and killed Tisamenus, son of Orestes, the ruler of the peninsula.

Masters of the Peloponnese, they divided the land by lot. Argos went to Temenus, Lacedaemon to Procles and Eurysthenes, the twin sons of Aristodemus, and Messene to Cresphontes. The fertile district of Elis was reserved for Oxylus. The Heraclidae ruled Lacedaemon until 221 BC, while in the other kingdoms they disappeared much earlier.

Trivia

A guide with three eyes. The oracle asked for a man with three eyes to lead the way. Oxylus had lost one eye, so he fit the prophecy once the count of his eyes and the animal he rode were taken into account.
Two causes of one death. The death of Aristodemus was explained in two ways: by a lightning bolt or by an arrow from Apollo. Both versions are told side by side in the story.
