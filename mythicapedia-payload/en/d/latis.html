Celtic goddess and god of Roman Britain

Latis

Latis is the name of two Celtic deities worshipped in Roman Britain: a goddess, Dea Latis, and a god, Deus Latis. Each is known from a single dedicatory inscription, so very little is known about them.

The myth

Few stories about Latis survive. The sources do not describe their origins, families or deeds, so any tale told about them would be guesswork.

It has been suggested that the goddess was linked to the rivers near the Birdoswald fort, but this is a hypothesis rather than a confirmed fact.

Worship and religion

The dedication to Dea Latis was found in 1873 at the Roman fort of Birdoswald, in Cumbria, and is now kept in the Carlisle Museum. The altar to Deus Latis was discovered at the fort of Aballava, at Burgh-by-Sands, near an image of a horned god and another dedication to the god Belatucadros. Both cults seem to have been very limited, and there is no evidence that they survived.

Trivia

Possible origin of the name. The name may be related to Proto-Celtic words such as lati-, 'liquor', lat-, 'day', or *lāto-, 'lust' or 'desire'. These remain unconfirmed hypotheses.
A goddess and a god with the same name. Despite sharing a name, the goddess and the god are known only from one inscription each, found at different forts in Cumbria.
