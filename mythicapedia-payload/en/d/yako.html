fox spirit of Japanese folklore from Kyushu

Yako

The yako is a type of kitsune, or fox spirit, from the folklore of Kyushu, the largest of the southern islands of Japan. Being possessed by one is called yako-tsuki.

The myth

According to the tales, the yako is black or white, slightly larger than a mouse and smaller than a cat, although the original one is said to be invisible. In Hirado, Nagasaki Prefecture, it usually travels with a large crowd walking alongside it. In northern Kyushu, possession brings on symptoms resembling an illness. On Iki Island, where it is thought to look like a weasel, it may hide under a person's armpit in order to possess them.

A yako licking a burn or a smallpox scar was said to cause death, so the sick sheltered inside nets and kept it away with ashes or swords. In southern Kyushu, yako were passed down family lines: if a family could no longer support one, it would possess their cattle and horses. Its owners were believed to be able to send it against people they had bad relations with.

Trivia

A spirit passed down. In southern Kyushu, according to tradition, possession by a yako could pass from parents to children.
A shared name. On Iki Island, weasels are also called yako because of their resemblance to the spirit.
