evil spirit of Hindu mythology

Vetala

The vetala (also Betal or Baital) is an evil spirit in Hindu folklore that haunts cemeteries and cremation grounds and possesses corpses. Believed to know the past, present and future, it is a prized target for sorcerers who want to bend it to their will.

The myth

Vetalas haunt cemeteries, charnel grounds and places of cremation. According to tradition, they take over corpses from which they torment the living, bringing madness, sickness, miscarriage and death. They are seen as beings suspended between life and death, unbound by the laws of space and time.

The tradition ties their origin to neglect of the funerary rites (sraddha) that descendants owe their ancestors. For this reason they resemble the bhuta and the preta, wandering dead. Sorcerers try to subdue them to gain their knowledge, while ordinary people use mantras, prayers and ritual chants to drive them away or free them.

In the Vetala-panchavimshati, a collection of twenty-five tales in Sanskrit, a vetala tells stories to King Vikramaditya as the king carries a corpse slung from a tree. After answering the vetala's questions, the king uncovers the truth about a sorcerer's plans, and the vetala grants him a gift that lets him defeat the sorcerer.

Worship and religion

In the Konkan region on India's west coast, the cult of the vetalas, also known as Betal or Vetoba, is strong. According to local belief, Betal is the husband of the goddess Shantadurga, so he is often honoured within her temple complex or in the surrounding woods. There is a Betal temple in Amona, Goa.

Legacy

Vetalas appear in the Indian television series Vikram aur Betaal, broadcast from 1985, and in many later adaptations of the tales. In the West they feature in the Canadian series The Vetala (2009), Michael Scott's novels about Nicholas Flamel, and the American series Supernatural and Sleepy Hollow.

Ancient sources: The Kathasaritsagara contains the Vetala Panchavimshati cycle, and the Harivamsa, the Kalika Purana and other texts also mention them.

Trivia

A name with many forms. They are known as vetal, baital, Betal or Vetoba depending on region and language.
A vampire that tells stories. In the story collection, the vetala is not an enemy but the narrator who tests the king.
