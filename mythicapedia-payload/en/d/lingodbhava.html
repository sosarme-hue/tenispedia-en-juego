form of Shiva emerging from the linga, in South Indian Hinduism

Lingodbhava

Lingodbhava ("the emergence of the linga") is a representation of Shiva, the Hindu god, common in temples across South India, especially in Tamil Nadu. It shows the god rising from a pillar of light and captures a central episode of his worship.

The myth

In a Shaiva legend, Vishnu and Brahma argued over which of them was supreme. Shiva appeared as an endless column of light and challenged them to find its origin. Brahma, as a gander, flew toward the top; Vishnu, as the boar Varaha, dug toward the base. Neither found it. Vishnu admitted defeat, but Brahma lied, claiming he had reached the summit.

Angered, Shiva took the form of Bhairava and cut off one of Brahma's heads, condemning him to have no temples on earth. Tradition says that for this crime Shiva wandered the three worlds as a beggar until he was purified at Varanasi, on the banks of the Ganges.

Worship and religion

In many Shaiva temples, the scene is carved on the western wall of the sanctum. The Thiruvathirai festival in the Tamil month of Margazhi and the Shivaratri linked to this episode commemorate it. According to the Arunachala Purana, on that day Shiva cooled himself as the mountain Arunachala to relieve living beings. Worship continues today in South Indian temples.

Legacy

It is one of the most common forms of Shiva in South Indian temples since the Chola period, alongside Dakshinamurthy. Its image, with Vishnu and Brahma at the sides, is one of the most recognisable in Tamil iconography.

Ancient sources: The Tirukkural, from around the 5th century, and the hymns of the Shaiva saints Appar and Sambandar, from the 7th century, mention it; the Vishnu Purana tells the tale.

Trivia

A column of fire. According to the Arunachala Purana, Shiva rose as a column of fire so intense that living beings could not bear it. He then transformed into the mountain Arunachala.
Brahma without temples. As punishment for his lie, tradition says Shiva decreed that Brahma would have no temples of worship on earth.
