Mesopotamian goddess, secondary wife of Enlil

Shuzianna

Shuzianna is a Mesopotamian goddess worshiped mainly in Nippur, where she was regarded as a secondary wife of Enlil. Her Sumerian name is usually taken to mean «the just hand of heaven».

The myth

Stories place her among the minor goddesses who helped Ninmah create human beings in the myth of Enki and Ninmah, alongside six others. God lists also count her among the seven children of Enmesharra, and one text says that Enlil married her.

The An = Anum list calls her a «concubine» of Enlil and nurse of Sin, although the same text gives that role to Ninimma. A neo-Assyrian text presents her as another name of Gula, yet in Nippur the two goddesses were worshiped separately.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Nippur, where she was honoured in the Ekur complex and in Gula's temple, the Eurusaga. A shared temple with Enlil has been proposed, and marriage rites linked to him were reportedly held in the E-kurigigal. She was also worshiped in Umma, Uruk and Ur, where she had a shrine within the Ningal temple. One of the Temple Hymns is dedicated to her.

Ancient sources: She appears in the myth of Enki and Ninmah, in god lists such as the Weidner list and An = Anum, and in a Temple Hymn.

Trivia

A name with several readings. A late text offers another reading of her name: «Lady of Babylon, the saviour», based on an uncommon alternate name of that city.
Equated with foreign goddesses. In the trilingual edition from Ugarit she is identified with the local goddess Dadmish, and in god lists her group is equated with Hurrian and Ugaritic goddesses.
