Greek god of opportunity

Kairos

Kairos, the Greek personification of the opportune moment, luck and favorable occasions. In the tradition he was the youngest son of Zeus, and his image teaches that an opportunity seized can be gained, but once missed it is gone for good.

The myth

Ancient sources describe him as the youngest child of Zeus, according to a hymn by Ion of Chios (c. 490-425 BC), as Pausanias records. Kairos is less a character with a long story than an allegorical figure: he stands for what is fitting and arrives at the right instant, though sometimes for the critical or dangerous moment too.

His appearance carries the meaning. He is a beautiful youth who never grows old, because opportunity never does. He stands on tiptoe with wings on his feet, like Hermes, to run with the wind. He holds a razor or scales balanced on an edge, symbols of the fleeting instant when chances appear and vanish. His hair falls over his forehead so he can be seized as he approaches, but the back of his head is bald: once he has passed, no one can grab him.

Worship and religion

Pausanias mentions an altar of Kairos near the entrance to the stadium at Olympia, showing that he was worshipped as a divinity and not merely as an allegory.

Legacy

The image of Kairos served as a model for many ancient and medieval representations. He appears in literature, such as the epigram by Poseidippos on Lysippos's statue, and in the 2021 novel Kairos by German author Jenny Erpenbeck, which takes its title from this god.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Callistratus, Poseidippos, Phaedrus and Ion of Chios mention him, together with Poseidippos's epigram on Lysippos's statue.

Trivia

Lysippos's statue. A bronze statue of Kairos by the sculptor Lysippos stood in Sikyon and became the model for many later representations.
Bald at the back. A Latin saying in the Disticha Catonis describes Occasio as hairy in front and bald behind, so she must be grasped as she arrives.
