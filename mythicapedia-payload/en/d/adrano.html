Sicilian fire god

Adranus

Adranus was the fire god worshipped by the Sicels, the ancient people of Sicily, across the whole island and especially in the town that bears his name near Mount Etna. He is interesting because his figure reflects the meeting of indigenous, Greek and Eastern traditions on the island.

The myth

According to tradition, Adranus lived beneath Mount Etna until the Greek god Hephaestus, known to the Romans as Vulcan, drove him out. In this story, the fire inside the mountain passed to another deity, and Adranus was pushed into a secondary role.

According to the lexicographer Hesychius, Adranus was the father of the Palici, whom he had with his lover, the nymph Thalia. Ancient sources offer few concrete episodes and say little about his deeds, but they preserve the image of a god tied to the mountain, to fire and to a divine lineage.

Worship and religion

Adranus was worshipped all over Sicily, although his main centre was the town that shares his name. According to Aelian, about a thousand sacred dogs were kept near his temple there, and they formed part of his cult.

Legacy

Some modern commentators have linked his name to that of the Persian god Adar and the Phoenician god Adrammelech, both associated with the sun or with fire in general. The town of Adrano, near Etna, still keeps his name today.

Ancient sources: Aelian, who mentions the sacred dogs kept at his temple, and Hesychius, who names him as father of the Palici.

Trivia

Sacred dogs. According to Aelian, around a thousand sacred dogs were kept near Adranus's temple.
A displaced god. Tradition says that Hephaestus, or Vulcan, drove Adranus out from beneath Mount Etna.
