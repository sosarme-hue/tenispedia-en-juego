Celtic mother goddess of the River Marne

Dea Matrona

Dea Matrona, the 'Divine Mother', was a Celtic goddess who protected the River Marne in ancient Gaul. Her name, shared with the river, points to the idea of the Great Mother.

The myth

According to Celtic tradition, Dea Matrona was the protector of the River Marne, whose ancient name, Matrŏna, she shared. Her son was the god Maponos. The Gaulish theonym Mātr-on-ā means 'Great Mother', so scholars have interpreted her as a mother goddess.

In Welsh mythology, she is identified with Modron, another mother goddess, while Maponos is identified with Mabon.

Worship and religion

She was venerated in triads of mothers, or more rarely in pairs, as part of a cult of the Deae Matres attested in many parts of northern Europe. Cheap terracotta figurines, mass-produced for household shrines, survive in large numbers, showing the mothers nursing babies or holding fruit, food or small dogs in their laps.

Legacy

The River Marne keeps its ancient name, which is linked to that of the goddess.

Trivia

Triads of mothers. Many representations show her in groups of three, known as triads of divine mothers.
Modron and Mabon. In Wales, the goddess is identified with Modron, and her son Maponos with Mabon.
