Boeotian hero of Greek mythology

Alegenor

Alegenor is a figure of Greek mythology, son of Itonus and a member of the Boeotian lineage. He matters because his descendants appear among the Boeotian leaders who took part in the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the genealogy recorded by Diodorus Siculus, Itonus had four sons: Hippalcimus, Electryon, Archilycus and Alegenor. Alegenor fathered Clonius, while his brothers founded other lines, such as that of Peneleos and Leitus, or that of Prothoenor and Arcesilaus, who led the Boeotians on the expedition against Troy.

Homer, in the Iliad, mentions Promachus, son of Alegenor, lamenting that his wife will never again be gladdened by the return of her dear husband. Tradition thus credits Alegenor with two sons, Clonius and Promachus, though the ancient texts say little more about their deeds.

Ancient sources: Diodorus Siculus in his Bibliotheca historica and Homer in the Iliad.

Trivia

A warrior lineage. Diodorus states that the sons of his brothers led the Boeotians on the expedition against Troy.
Two different sons. Diodorus names Clonius as Alegenor's son, while Homer mentions Promachus.
