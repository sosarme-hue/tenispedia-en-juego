legendary first king of Attica

Actaeus

Actaeus was, according to Pausanias, the first legendary king of Attica, reigning before the flood of Deucalion. He matters because he is seen as a forerunner of the Athenian monarchy, and because his line links to the founding of Athens through his daughter Agraulos.

The myth

According to Pausanias, Actaeus ruled Attica in the ancient times that preceded the flood of Deucalion. The sources do not say who his parents were or whether, like other early kings of the region, he was one of the autochthonous people.

His daughter Agraulos married Cecrops, founder of Athens, who succeeded him on the throne. According to the Suda, Actaeus had three other daughters: Herse, Pandrosos and Phoenice. Phoenice died a virgin, and in her honour, as this tradition tells it, Actaeus named the Phoenician letters of the alphabet.

Pherecydes makes Telamon a son of Actaeus and Glauce, daughter of Cicreus, and a friend rather than a brother of Peleus.

Legacy

His name is linked to the older name of Attica, Acte or Actica, according to the Parian Chronicle, which says Actaeus gave the region its name before Cecrops renamed it. In palaeontology, an arthropod from the Middle Cambrian Burgess Shale, Actaeus armatus, was named after the king.

Ancient sources: Pausanias presents him as the first king of Attica; the Suda and Pherecydes record his daughters and family, and the Parian Chronicle mentions his role in naming the region.

Trivia

A name for the alphabet. According to the Suda, Actaeus named the Phoenician letters in honour of his daughter Phoenice, who had died a virgin.
A fossil with his name. A Middle Cambrian arthropod from the Burgess Shale bears the name Actaeus armatus in his honour.
