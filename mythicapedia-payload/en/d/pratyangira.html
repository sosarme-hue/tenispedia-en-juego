Hindu goddess who protects against black magic

Pratyangira

Pratyangira is a Hindu goddess, also known as Narasimhini, Atharvana Bhadrakali or Nikumbhila. She is described as the šakti, or female counterpart, of the god Narasimha, although in popular devotion she is often linked to Shiva. She is counted among the Matrikas, a group of mother goddesses especially venerated in the Shakta and Tantra traditions.

The myth

Ancient texts give her several origins. She appears in the Vedas as Atharvana Bhadrakali, goddess of the Atharvaveda and of magical spells. The Netra Tantra describes her as a form taken by Lakshmi to rescue those suffering from sorcery.

According to the Devi Mahatmya, the male gods manifested their female energies as goddesses to help Chandika against the asura armies of Shumbha and Nishumbha. From Narasimha's energy came Narasimhi, who resembled him in form. A lesser-known story says she emerged as the šakti of one of Sharabha's wings during his battle with Narasimha, and that, with a thousand faces and arms, she helped calm the lion god's wrath.

In the Ramayana, Indrajit began the Nikumbhila yajna to become invincible in the war in Lanka, but Hanuman interrupted the rite, and Lakshmana was able to defeat him.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped in Hinduism, Buddhism and Jainism. Her devotees perform her sadhana to counteract evil rites and magic, to seek welfare and to end suffering. Temples dedicated to her are found mainly in South India. Her iconography is fierce: a lion's head, dark skin, a garland of skulls and a lion mount. In her four-armed form she holds a trident, a noose, a damaru and a skull cup.

Ancient sources: The Vedas (Atharvaveda), Netra Tantra, Devi Mahatmya, Ramayana and Tripura Rahasya.

Trivia

A multiplied form. The Maha Pratyangira form expands her image with multiple lion faces and numerous hands holding different weapons.
A goddess with two traditions. Although the Bhavani-Sahasranama Stuti lists her as the 77th name of Parvati or Durga, in popular devotion she is mainly linked to Shiva.
