divine fruit of Chinese mythology

Peaches of Immortality

The Peaches of Immortality are sacred fruits in Chinese mythology that grant long life, or immortality, to whoever eats them. Their image has passed into Chinese art, celebrations and cooking as a symbol of longevity.

The myth

According to tradition, the immortals feast on these peaches, which grow in a garden tended in the palace of Xi Wangmu, the Queen Mother of the West. There, the Jade Emperor and his wife ensure the everlasting existence of the gods by feeding them this fruit.

In Journey to the West, Sun Wukong serves as guardian of the garden. Once he learns their effects, he eats one, then hides inside a peach during a banquet and consumes more, gaining immortality. Early texts also tell that the Queen Mother gave peaches to Emperor Wu of Han, promising that the tree bore fruit only once every three thousand years.

Worship and religion

The peaches were linked to Xi Wangmu's banquet, held in honour of her birthday on the third day of the third lunar month. Today they are carved or painted on statues and paintings, and appear on birthday cakes and pastries.

Legacy

The peach appears often in Chinese painting, ceramics and silk, frequently alongside deer or cranes, other symbols of longevity. Chinese bakers make shoutao, a bun shaped like a peach.

Ancient sources: The stories appear in Former Affairs of the Emperor Wu and Records of Diverse Matters, and in the novel Journey to the West.

Trivia

A very slow tree. According to the stories, the tree puts forth leaves once every thousand years and needs another three thousand to ripen its fruit.
Babylike fruits. In Journey to the West, the immortality fruits of a monastery look like newborn babies, complete with sense organs.
