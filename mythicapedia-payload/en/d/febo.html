epithet of the Greek gods Apollo and Helios

Phoebus

Phoebus is an epithet meaning 'the Bright One', used by Greeks and Romans, especially poets, for the god Apollo. Latin poets also applied it to the sun god, so the name came to stand for the sun itself.

The myth

Myth does not tell a separate story about Phoebus; the name accompanies Apollo in Greek mythology.

Classical poets, especially Latin ones, also gave the name to the sun god Helios, so Phoebus came to mean the sun and its chariot. That is why it often appears in poetry as a synonym for the sun, and Greeks and Romans used it interchangeably with Apollo, the standard name in Roman religion.

Legacy

The name survives in literature across languages. Cervantes uses it for the sun in Don Quixote, and Byron mentions Phoebus in his poem The Islands of Greece. It also appears in the Argentine march Marcha de San Lorenzo by Carlos Javier Benielli and in the Paraguayan National Anthem. At the 2004 Athens Olympics, the mascots were two siblings named Athenà and Phèvos.

Trivia

Olympic mascots. At the 2004 Athens Olympics, the mascots were two siblings, Athenà and Phèvos, names taken from Athena and Phoebus.
A verse about the sun. The first stanza of the Marcha de San Lorenzo opens with the line «Febo asoma», which refers to the sun by the name of the god.
