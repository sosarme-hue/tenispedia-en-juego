Gogaji

Gogaji is a folk deity of northern India, especially Rajasthan, venerated as a warrior, saint and protector against snakebites. His historical basis is thin, and he is usually placed between the 11th and 14th centuries.

The myth

According to tradition, Gogaji was born into the Chauhan clan, son of Vacha and Queen Bachhal, and his birth is credited to a blessing from the guru Gorakhnath. After fighting his twin cousins, whom he killed, his mother banished him. He returned one night to see his wife, who revealed his presence to Bachhal. Taunted by his mother, he asked the earth to take him, but it would only accept a Muslim; Gogaji converted to Islam and sank into the ground on horseback.

The versions differ: some place him in the time of Mahmud of Ghazni, others in the 13th century, and others make him a contemporary of Prithviraj Chauhan.

Worship and religion

He had many shrines, called thans, the principal one at Dadrewa, where a festival is held in Bhadrapada. He is revered as a protector of farmers and cattle, and, depending on the region, as a Muslim pir or a Nathpanthi jogi. His worship continues today.

Legacy

Over time he was reinterpreted as a Rajput hero and included in the group of the five panch pir.

Ancient sources: A 15th-century Jain text, the Sravukavratadi-atichar, warns laypeople against his worship.

Trivia

Vīr and pīr. Gogaji combines two identities: that of a vīr, or warrior, and that of a pīr, or saint.
Origin of his name. One belief links it to the gugal fruit, a resin, given to his mother by Gorakhnath; another to his devotion to cows, or gou in Sanskrit.
