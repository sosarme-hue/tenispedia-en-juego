Hesperia

Hesperia, also called Hespere, is one of the Hesperides, the nymphs of Greek myth who guarded a garden of golden apples in the far West. Her story is known for the transformation she underwent when the Argonauts arrived.

The myth

According to tradition, the Hesperides were three or four nymphs, depending on the author, who lived at the western edge of the world and tended a garden where golden apples grew.

When the Argonauts came to that place in search of water, the nymphs first turned into dust and earth and then into trees. Hesperia was transformed into a poplar.

Apollonius of Rhodes is the author who names her. Apollodorus's Library records variants such as Hesperetusa or Hesperusa, which editors read as a blend of Hesperia and Aretusa, although the manuscripts give the form Hestia Eretusa.

Ancient sources: Apollonius of Rhodes names her, and Apollodorus's Library discusses the variants of her name.

Trivia

A name with variants. Texts mention forms such as Hesperetusa or Hesperusa. Editors of Apollodorus's Library suggest splitting the name into Hesperia and Aretusa, although the manuscripts read Hestia Eretusa.
