Hindu craftsman god and divine architect

Vishvakarma

Vishvakarma is the Hindu craftsman deity and divine architect of the devas, credited with building cities and celestial palaces. His name, meaning 'maker of everything', was originally an epithet for any powerful creator god.

The myth

According to the Rigveda, he is all-seeing, with eyes, faces and arms on every side, while in the earliest texts the role of divine craftsman belonged to Tvastar. In the Mahabharata he is the son of the Vasu Prabhasa, though other texts give him different parents.

He forged the chariots of the gods and Indra's vajra from the bones of the sage Dadhichi, and built cities such as Dvaraka and Indraprastha. According to the Ramayana, the monkey Nala was his son. When his daughter Samjna fled Surya's rays, he trimmed the sun's brilliance and used the removed matter to create weapons such as the Sudarshana chakra and the trishula.

Worship and religion

His festival, Vishvakarma Puja, falls on 17 or 18 September, while Rishi Panchami commemorates his five children. The five trades of the Vishvakarma community, including blacksmiths, carpenters and goldsmiths, venerate him as their patron.

Legacy

The architectural style of northern India is named after him, and the large wooden statues of Jagannatha at Puri are attributed to him.

Ancient sources: Rigveda, Mahabharata, Ramayana, Vishnu Purana and Skanda Purana, among others.

Trivia

A golden palace. According to tradition, he built a palace entirely of gold for Shiva and Parvati. Shiva later gave it to Ravana as dakshina, and so Lanka became Ravana's residence.
A birthday without a date. Some devotees believe Vishvakarma had no birthday like mortals, only a day of commemoration when his five children, regarded as rishis, gathered to honour him.
