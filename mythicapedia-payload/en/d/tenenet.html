Egyptian goddess of childbirth, protection and beer

Tenenet

Tenenet was an ancient Egyptian goddess of childbirth and protection, also linked to beer. Worshipped mainly in Armant, where she was the consort of the god Montu, she combined maternal, creative and solar roles.

The myth

According to a text from Armant, she was the daughter of Amun and Mut. From the 11th Dynasty she was worshipped as Montu's consort, and together they were considered the parents of Harpara, a child form of Ra and Horus. She was called “mother of mothers” and “mother of the gods,” and also “the great Neith who initiated pregnancy.”

As the protective uraeus of Ra, she was said to rise to the god's brow and unleash fiery breath on his enemies. The Khonsu Cosmogony, from Karnak, places her in a Theban creation myth in which Khonsu fertilizes the primordial waters beside a chapel called Tnn.t, which some scholars read as the goddess herself.

Worship and religion

Her main cult centre was Armant, where a Ptolemaic priest held the title “he who pleases the mother of Montu, Tenenet.” She was invoked as protector of the wombs of pregnant women and appears in the Feast of Drunkenness, dedicated to Hathor.

Ancient sources: Ptolemaic-period texts, the Book of the Dead and the Khonsu Cosmogony from the Karnak temple.

Trivia

The uraeus of Ra. She is described as “the uraeus of Ra,” a great serpent on Horakhty's brow that illuminates the land.
A name linked to beer. Her name may derive from tenemu, the Egyptian word for beer.
