Nestor

Nestor is the king of Pylos who appears in the Iliad and the Odyssey as the oldest and wisest of the Greeks who fought at Troy. He is a prominent figure in Greek myth because he embodies experience and counsel, although the stories also show his advice sometimes going badly wrong.

The myth

According to tradition, Nestor was the son of Neleus and Chloris. Some accounts place him among the Argonauts, say he fought the centaurs, and took part in the hunt for the Calydonian Boar. After Heracles killed his father and brothers but spared the young Nestor, he became king of Pylos. Before the war, he led a raid against the Eleans and returned with a great herd of cattle; the Eleans struck back at the Pylians at Thryoessa, but Nestor's troops won the battle.

In the Iliad he is too old to fight himself, yet he leads the Pylian troops from his chariot and advises the other leaders. His counsel is usually respected, though it can be outdated or disastrous. The most notable case is when he urges Patroclus to disguise himself as Achilles, a step that leads to Patroclus's death at the hands of Hector. In the Odyssey he is a peaceful, hospitable king who welcomes Telemachus, lends him horses, and sends his son Peisistratus with him to Sparta, though he cannot tell him anything certain about Odysseus. Some accounts say Zeus, or in other versions only Apollo, granted him three generations of life, and his homecoming was comparatively easy because he was the only Achaean warrior who had acted justly.

Legacy

The Mycenaean palace at Pylos, excavated by Carl Blegen in 1939, is known as the Palace of Nestor, although there is no evidence that the character existed. A gold cup found at Mycenae is called the Cup of Nestor because it resembles the one described in the Iliad.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, where he is a prominent secondary character.

Trivia

A cup named after him. A gold cup from Mycenae, now in the National Archaeological Museum in Athens, bears his name because it resembles the one described in the Iliad.
A palace bearing his name. The palace complex at Pylos, excavated in 1939, is called the Palace of Nestor, though there is no evidence the character existed.
