one of the Porthaonides, mother of the Sirens

Sterope

Sterope is a figure in Greek mythology, one of the three Porthaonides, daughters of Porthaon, and the mother of the Sirens. She matters mainly because her story links the world of nymphs and rivers with the origin of these singing beings.

The myth

According to the myth, Sterope was one of the three Porthaonides, daughters of Porthaon and Laothoe, although another version makes her a daughter of Pleuron. Together with her sisters, Eurythemiste and Stratonice, she wandered the fields of Pleuron as if they were nymphs.

One day Achelous, the river god, saw her playing and lay with her. From that union the Sirens were born: Telxiope or Telsinoe, Molpe and Aglaophonos, names that allude to song.

Zeus granted them the flowery island of Antemusa, where they could live apart. It was also said that the Sirens could calm the wind and thus cause a dead calm.

Trivia

Telling names. The names of the Sirens allude to song, their most characteristic trait.
Mastery of the wind. It was said that the Sirens could calm the wind and bring about a dead calm at sea.
