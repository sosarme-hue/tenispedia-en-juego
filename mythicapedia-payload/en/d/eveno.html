eponymous hero of the Evenus river in Greek mythology

Evenus

Evenus is the hero who gave his name to the Evenus river in Aetolia, in Greek mythology. Ancient sources give him different parentage depending on the author, and his best-known story centres on the abduction of his daughter Marpessa.

The myth

The sources offer several versions of his lineage. For Hesiod, Evenus is one of the potamoi, river personifications and Oceanids, son of Oceanus and Tethys; in that context he appears only in genealogical lists and has no stories of his own. In the most familiar version he is a son of Ares and Demonice, daughter of Agenor, and brother of Molus, Pleuron and Thestius, who were also eponymous heroes.

His daughter Marpessa was courted by Idas, son of Aphareus. Evenus refused the suitor because he wanted his daughter to remain a virgin. Idas asked his divine father Poseidon for a winged chariot and carried her off. Evenus gave chase but could not catch them; in his rage he slaughtered his horses and threw himself into the river Lycormas, where he drowned, and the river has borne his name ever since. Over time he took on traits similar to Oenomaus, including competing with suitors. Another version makes his wife Alcippe, daughter of Oenomaus, but this tradition is late. Hyginus presents him as a son of Heracles who threw himself into the same river.

Ancient sources: The Iliad refers to Marpessa with the patronymic Evenina; Hesiod lists him among the children of Oceanus; Hyginus records his version as a son of Heracles.
