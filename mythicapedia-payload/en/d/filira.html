Oceanid, mother of the centaur Chiron

Philyra

Philyra is an Oceanid of Greek mythology, daughter of Oceanus and Tethys according to the sources, best known as the mother of the centaur Chiron. Her story explains the origin of one of the wisest and most unusual beings in Greek tradition.

The myth

According to the myth, Philyra was seduced by Cronus, who transformed himself into a stallion to escape the notice of his wife Rhea and galloped away. From this union Chiron was born, half man and half horse, on Mount Pelion according to the usual account.

Horrified by her son's appearance, Philyra begged the gods to transform her into anything but a human being. Zeus, in the tradition, turned her into a linden tree, whose Greek name matched her own. In some versions, Philyra and Chariclo, Chiron's wife, nursed the young Jason. Chiron's dwelling on Pelion, where his pupils were raised, was known as Philyra's cave.

Legacy

Her name became the matronymic by which Chiron was often called, Philyrides. The linden tree, which bears her Greek name, became associated with her story.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions her in the Theogony; other ancient sources record the versions about her union with Cronus and the birth of Chiron.

Trivia

A tree with her name. In Greek, the linden tree is called philyra, the same name as the nymph, which explains her final transformation.
Philyra's cave. On Mount Pelion, Chiron's pupils knew his dwelling as Philyra's cave.
