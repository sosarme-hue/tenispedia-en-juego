protector god of the Sindhis

Jhulelal

Jhulelal is the protector deity of the Sindhis, revered mainly by Sindhi Hindus in Pakistan and India. He is considered an avatar of Varuna and, for many, a symbol of harmony between communities.

The myth

According to tradition, he was born into a Lohana Hindu family during the reign of the Muslim ruler Mirkshah, who ordered Hindus to convert to Islam or die. The Hindus prayed to the Indus river, and the miraculous child changed form, rode a fish, and spoke like a sage.

When Mirkshah tried to imprison him, fire and flood engulfed the palace. The ruler relented, revoked his decree and asked forgiveness, while Jhulelal reminded him that the Quran forbids forced conversion.

Worship and religion

Sindhis celebrate Cheti Chand as their new year and Chaliya, a forty-day festival of thanksgiving. At the Odero Lal shrine in Pakistan, Hindus and Muslims pray side by side.

Legacy

From 1950, when Ram Panjwani promoted him in Bombay as a unifying icon for displaced Sindhis, his image spread through songs, statues and festivals. In Kutch, India, a complex was built around a 100-foot statue of him.

Trivia

A shared shrine. At Odero Lal, Hindus and Muslims pray in the same place, which some Muslims regard as the tomb of Sheikh Tahir.
Atop a fish. He is often shown seated on a lotus resting on a palla fish in the Indus River.
