golden boar of Norse mythology

Gullinbursti

Gullinbursti is a golden boar from Norse mythology, whose name means “golden mane” or “golden bristles.” It belongs to Freyr, the god associated with fertility and prosperity, and it was given to him by the dwarves as a treasure.

The myth

According to the account preserved by Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda, in the Skáldskaparmál, Loki boasted that the dwarf brothers could never match the works of the Sons of Ivaldi, such as Sif’s hair, Freyr’s ship Skíðblaðnir and Odin’s spear Gungnir. Brokkr accepted the wager and worked the bellows while his brother Sindri threw a pig’s skin into the furnace. From that fire emerged the boar, whose mane bristles glowed in the dark. The dwarves gave it to Freyr as a gift.

Tradition credits it with remarkable abilities: it could run through the air and over the sea, faster than any horse, and its light shone even in the darkest worlds. Other texts place it at Baldr’s funeral. The poem Húsdrápa says Freyr rode Gullinbursti there, while in the Gylfaginning Snorri states that the god travelled in a chariot pulled by the boar. It is also known by the alternative name Slíðrugtanni, “sharp tooth.”

Worship and religion

The sources describe a cult of Freyr and Gullinbursti in the British Isles as well. Accounts survive of Scottish Christmas celebrations between the 8th and 11th centuries, when a boar’s head was the main dish of the banquet and its carving was reserved for the most respected guests. After the meal, the men lit a ritual bonfire, formed a circle and danced to the sound of bagpipes, shouting “Haile, Yule, Haile” and “Thor is with us!” The sources do not say whether this cult survives today.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson’s Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál and Gylfaginning) and the poem Húsdrápa.

Trivia

Bristles that glow. According to the account, the bristles in the boar’s mane glowed in the dark, and their light lit even the darkest worlds.
Three names. The boar is also known as Slíðrugtanni, a name meaning “sharp tooth” or “fearsome tooth.”
