spring nymph and counselor of Numa Pompilius

Egeria

Egeria is a spring nymph of Roman myth, linked to the legendary beginnings of Rome as the divine companion and counselor of King Numa Pompilius. Her name has become a byword for a wise female adviser.

The myth

According to Roman tradition, Egeria dwelt in a spring and grove near the Porta Capena in Rome. Numa would go there alone, claiming he was meeting a goddess, his wife Egeria. She taught him the laws and religious rites that laid the foundations of Roman religion, helped him read difficult omens, and won him a ritual to protect against lightning from Jupiter himself.

Numa is said to have written down her teachings in sacred books and buried them with him. Some five centuries later an accident brought them to light, and the Senate, judging them unfit for the public, ordered them destroyed. After Numa died, Egeria wept without end until she was transformed into a spring. Some ancient skeptics held that Numa invented her so the people would accept his laws as coming from the gods.

Worship and religion

Egeria was associated with sacred groves such as the one at Aricia in Latium, dedicated to Diana, and with a spring near Rome whose waters were reserved for the Vestals. She was credited with oracular power, and offerings of water or milk were made to her.

Legacy

Her name is used as a common noun for a wise counselor. She appears in English literature in Nathaniel Lee's Lucius Junius Brutus, in Elizabeth Gaskell's Wives and Daughters, and in Oscar Wilde's The Importance of Being Earnest. In video games, Genshin Impact reimagines her as a former Hydro Archon.

Ancient sources: Plutarch and Ovid, in the Metamorphoses, recount her relationship with Numa, and Juvenal mentions her with irony.

Trivia

A tourist spring. The spring linked to Egeria, in the Caffarella park, was a favorite picnic spot for nineteenth-century Romans.
A name turned into a word. Her name became a common term for a wise counselor, and in eighteenth-century England it was used ironically to hint at a romantic affair.
