usurper of Thebes in Greek mythology

Lycus

Lycus is a figure of Greek mythology, son of King Lycus of Thebes and brother of Nycteus. He is best remembered as the usurper of the Theban throne, a character who appears in Euripides's Heracles.

The myth

According to the tradition, Lycus came from Euboea and seized power in ancient Thebes, in Boeotia, after killing Creon, who was acting as regent for the young Laodamas, son of Eteocles. Once on the throne, he treated Creon's family cruelly: he threw them out of their house and denied them food and clothing.

The story ends with Heracles, Creon's son-in-law. Returning unexpectedly to Thebes, he killed Lycus, and Laodamas then succeeded his father as king. The tale is essentially one of abuse of power and of order restored through the intervention of a hero.

Ancient sources: He appears in Euripides's Heracles, a tragedy that recounts his usurpation and his death at the hands of the hero.
