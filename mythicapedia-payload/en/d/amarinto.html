hunter of Artemis in Greek mythology

Amarynthus

Amarynthus is, in Greek mythology, a hunter in the service of Artemis. His name gave rise to the town of Amarynthus on the island of Euboea and to one of the goddess's epithets.

The myth

Tradition presents him as a hunter of Artemis, the goddess of the hunt. Ancient sources add few details: they do not describe his deeds or his family, and his story rests almost entirely on his link to the place that bears his name.

According to Stephanus of Byzantium, an island of Euboea was also named Amarynthus after this hunter.

Worship and religion

Strabo mentions a Euboean town also called Amarynthus, where the sanctuary of Artemis Amarynthia stood. According to tradition, the goddess's epithet, Amarynthia or Amarysia, derived from this hero, or rather from the town where Amarysia was revered. Under that name she was worshipped in Euboea and also in Attica. There is no information on whether this cult survived.

Legacy

The name survives in the Euboean town of Amarynthus, which tradition associates with the hero.

Ancient sources: Strabo mentions the sanctuary of Artemis Amarynthia in Euboea, and Stephanus of Byzantium records an island of Euboea said to be named after this hunter.

Trivia

An island with his name. Stephanus of Byzantium writes that an island of Euboea was named Amarynthus after this hunter.
An epithet of Artemis. The names Amarynthia and Amarysia are epithets of the goddess derived from this hero or from the town that commemorates him.
