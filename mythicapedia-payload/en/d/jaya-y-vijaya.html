divine gatekeepers of Vaikuntha, doorkeepers of the god Vishnu

Jaya and Vijaya

Jaya and Vijaya are two divine gatekeepers, the doorkeepers of Vaikuntha, the abode of the god Vishnu, in Hindu Vaishnava tradition. Their story, shaped by a curse and three successive births, explains why Vishnu incarnated several times to defeat them.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, the four Kumaras, mind-born sons of Brahma, wished to see Vishnu in Vaikuntha. They looked like children, but their tapas had given them an advanced age. Jaya and Vijaya, thinking them to be children, blocked their entry, and the Kumaras cursed them to be born on earth.

Vishnu could not lift the curse, but he offered them a choice: seven lives as his devotees or three as his enemies. They chose the latter, unable to bear being apart from him for so long. They were born as Hiranyakashipu and Hiranyaksha in the Satya Yuga, as Ravana and Kumbhakarna in the Treta Yuga, and as Shishupala and Dantavakra in the Dvapara Yuga. Each time Vishnu incarnated to defeat them, and in the end they returned to Vaikuntha.

Sources differ on their origin: the Brahmanda Purana makes them sons of Kali, while the Padma Purana names Kardama and Devahuti as their parents.

Worship and religion

They are shown with four arms, holding a chakra, shankha, mace and sword, and their statues stand at the entrances of many Vishnu temples. Notable examples include the temples of Venkateswara in Tirumala, Jagannath in Puri and Ranganatha in Srirangam. They remain venerated in present-day Vaishnavism.

Legacy

Their image endures mainly in the iconography of Vishnu temples, where they represent guardians who turned from service to rebellion.

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana, the Brahmanda Purana and the Padma Purana narrate their origin and curse.

Trivia

Statues in major temples. Their statues can be seen in heavily visited temples such as those at Tirumala, Puri and Srirangam.
Choosing to be enemies. Although they could choose seven lives as devotees, they preferred three as enemies of Vishnu so they could return to his side sooner.
