Italic goddess of Nemi, a form of Diana

Diana Nemorensis

Diana Nemorensis, or Diana of Nemi, was an Italic form of Diana worshipped in a sacred grove beside Lake Nemi, near Rome, who in time became identified with Artemis.

The myth

Tradition holds that her cult was founded by Orestes, who fled with his sister Iphigenia from the Tauric Chersonese (Crimea), carrying the image of the Tauric Diana hidden in a bundle of sticks.

According to the legend, in Tauris every stranger who reached the shore was sacrificed, while in Italy the rite grew milder. These are Greek founding legends with no historical or archaeological evidence linking them to the cult at Nemi.

Worship and religion

The temple was preceded by a sacred grove holding a triform cult image, in which three goddesses form one: the huntress, the Moon and the goddess of the underworld, Hecate. Votive offerings show her as a protector of motherhood and childbirth. According to legend, a runaway slave who could break a sacred bough could challenge the priest-king, the Rex Nemorensis, to single combat.

Ancient sources: Horace calls her diva triformis; Virgil and Catullus name her Trivia, and Vitruvius describes her temple as archaic and Etruscan in form.

Trivia

Mirror of Diana. Poets called Lake Nemi speculum Dianae, meaning Mirror of Diana.
A political inscription. The grove's dedication, naming several Latin cities, was copied by Cato the Elder as evidence of the Latin League.
