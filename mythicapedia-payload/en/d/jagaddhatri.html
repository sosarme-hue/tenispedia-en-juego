Jagaddhatri

Jagaddhatri is an aspect of the Hindu goddess Durga, worshipped mainly in West Bengal and also in Odisha and Jharkhand. She is revered as the protector of the world and is shown seated on a lion that tramples an elephant.

The myth

According to the Katyayani Tantra, after Durga defeated Mahishasura, the gods of heaven forgot her power. To test them she appeared before Agni, Vayu, Varuna and Chandra and asked them to move a blade of grass; none of them could. They realised she was the source of all power, their own included, and she revealed herself as Uma riding a lion. The gods' ego became an elephant that lies beneath her mount's paws.

Another tale tells of Karindrasura, a demon who had once been Indra's elephant Airavata under a curse, and who tried to seize the goddess Ganga. Ganga called on Mahamaya, who took the form of Jagaddhatri and slew him with her discus, bow and arrows, freeing Airavata from the curse. For this reason she is also called Karindrasurnishudini.

Worship and religion

Her best-known worship takes place in Bengal, especially at Krishnanagar, where tradition links its origins to Maharaja Krishnachandra. The festival falls on the ninth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Kartika. The oldest temple dedicated to her, the Mahavidya temple at Somra, dates from 1621, and her puja remains widely celebrated in many Bengali towns today.

Legacy

In Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's historical novel Anandamath, the source of the national song Vande Mataram, Jagaddhatri, Kali and Durga appear as three faces of Bharat Mata, Mother India: the mother who was, the one who is and the one who will be.

Ancient sources: The Katyayani Tantra, which names her Mahadurga, and the Shiva Purana; the story draws on the Kena Upanishad.

Trivia

A saying of Ramakrishna. The mystic Sri Ramakrishna taught that the goddess carries the world and that, if she stopped, the world would be destroyed.
A tradition taken up again. According to tradition, her worship was later resumed by Sarada Devi, the wife of Ramakrishna.
