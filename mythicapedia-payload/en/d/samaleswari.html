mother goddess of Sambalpur, Odisha (India)

Samaleswari

Samaleswari is the protective goddess of Sambalpur, in the Indian state of Odisha, revered as Jagat Janani, the mother of the universe, and as a form of Durga, Mahalaxmi or Adishakti. Her temple on the banks of the Mahanadi River is the centre of devotion for almost all of western Odisha and parts of Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh.

The myth

Tradition presents her above all as a cosmic mother who can be identified with several goddesses of the Hindu pantheon. She is also known as Samalei Maa, Mother Samaleswari, and is described as the presiding deity of Sambalpur, a divinity whose worship was revived over time.

Worship and religion

Her image is a large granite block with a trunk-like projection at the base, interpreted as a yoni, and gold features set into a naturally shaped rock. Two Navaratri festivals are held each year, in March-April and September-October, and each day the goddess is dressed differently according to the Navadurgas; the autumn one is the more splendid. The great regional festival is Nuakhai, when farmers offer the first produce of their fields to the goddess before eating it. Her worship remains alive today.

Trivia

Diamonds. According to the travellers Jean-Baptiste Tavernier and the historian Edward Gibbon, diamonds were exported from Sambalpur to Rome. The region was known since ancient times as Hirakhanda, the land of diamonds.
Changing attire. During Navaratri the image is dressed differently each day, representing each of the nine Navadurgas. On the first day she wears white attire called Dhabalamukhi.
