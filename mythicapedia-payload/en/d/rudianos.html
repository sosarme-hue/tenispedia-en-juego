Celtic war god

Rudianos

Rudianos was a war god worshiped in Celtic Gaul. In Roman times he was linked with Mars, and his name is usually interpreted as meaning «red», a nod to his warlike character.

The myth

No written stories about Rudianos survive, so his figure is known mainly through his name and a single image. At Saint-Michel-de-Valbonne a menhir-shaped stone was found, dating to around the 6th century BC, showing a horseman with an oversized head riding down five severed heads. It may depict Rudianos himself.

The scene recalls the head-hunting that classical writers attributed to the Celts, who hung the heads of defeated enemies from their saddles. The image suggests a war god associated with victory and the trophies of battle.

Worship and religion

He was invoked at Saint-Andéol-en-Quint and Rochefort-Samson in Drôme, and at Saint-Michel-de-Maurienne. The sources offer no details about his festivals, priests or rites, and his cult has not survived.

Trivia
