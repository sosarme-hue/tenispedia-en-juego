The Old Man of the Sea

The Old Man of the Sea was a title given in Greek myth to several sea divinities, chiefly Nereus and Proteus, though also Phorcys. He is a primordial figure of the sea in Greek mythology.

The myth

In the Iliad, Homer identifies him with Nereus, father of Thetis, Achilles' mother, and of the Nereids. In the Theogony, Hesiod makes him a son of Pontus and Gaia, a trusty and gentle being, and the father of the Nereids with Doris.

In Book Four of the Odyssey, however, he is Proteus. Menelaus must hold him fast while he changes shape again and again, until he reveals whether Odysseus is still alive.

Worship and religion

Pausanias records that the people of ancient Gythium worshipped Nereus under the name Old Man of the Sea.

Legacy

In the tales of Sinbad the Sailor, a monster with this name climbs onto the shoulders of its victims and rides them until they die. Sinbad defeats it by getting it drunk.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad and Odyssey, Hesiod's Theogony, and Pausanias' Description of Greece.

Trivia

A harbour named after him. According to the Odyssey, there is a harbour on Ithaca named after Phorcys, whom the poem also calls the Old Man of the Sea.
From Greek sea to the Arabian tales. The monster in the Sinbad tales shares this name with the ancient Greek title, though those tales are much later than Homer.
