first king of Argos and river god in Greek mythology

Inachus

Inachus is, according to Greek tradition, the first king of Argos and a river god, son of Oceanus and Tethys. He gave his name to the Inachus River, and his story is tied to that of his daughter Io and the origins of the Argive royal line.

The myth

According to tradition, Inachus was one of the many children of Oceanus and Tethys, and he was regarded as a river god from the pre-Olympian mythic landscape. Sources disagree about his wife: sometimes she is the Oceanid Melia, mother of Phoroneus, Aegialeus and Mycene, and sometimes the Oceanid Argia, mother of Phoroneus and Io.

The best-known episode concerns Io. In the story, Zeus seduced or abducted her, and Inachus, after consulting oracles, drove her from his house. Ovid tells how Inachus wept for his daughter once she had been turned into a cow, and how she traced her name in the dust with her hoof. Another version says Inachus angered Zeus, who drove him mad; the river formerly called Haliacmon then took his name.

In the dispute between Hera and Poseidon over the Argolid, Inachus served as judge alongside the rivers Cephissus and Asterion. When they awarded the land to Hera, Poseidon cut off their water. Inachus was said to have divided his lands between his sons Phoroneus and Aegialeus.

Legacy

The name of Inachus survives in the Inachus River of the eastern Peloponnese. Virgil places him on Turnus's shield in the Aeneid.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Ovid, Aeschylus in Prometheus Bound, Diodorus Siculus and Parthenius mention Inachus and his children.

Trivia

A river with two names. According to Plutarch, the Inachus River was previously called Carmanor or Haliacmon. It took the name Inachus after the story of his daughter Io.
Io and the goddess Isis. Callimachus says Inachus was the father of Isis, identifying her with Io in the Greek version of the story.
