river god of ancient Macedonia

Olganos

Olganos is a river god of ancient Macedonia, son of Beres. According to tradition, he gave his name to a river in the region, and his figure is linked to his siblings Mieza and Beroia, after whom two Macedonian towns were named.

The myth

Tradition presents him as the firstborn son of Beres and the elder brother of Mieza and Beroia. According to the accounts, Olganos lent his name to the river that bears it.

The sources that mention him give no detailed deeds or episodes from his story, so his myth comes down to his genealogy and his link to the Macedonian landscape.

Worship and religion

There is evidence of his worship in the Macedonian town of Mieza. At Kopanos, in the region of Emathia, a bust of Olganos bearing an inscription, dating to around the 2nd century, was found; it is now on display at the Archaeological Museum in Veria, Greece. The piece offers insight into how he was portrayed.

Trivia

Cities named after siblings. The Macedonian cities of Mieza and Beroia took their names from two siblings of Olganos. Today they are known as Nausa and Veria.
An inscribed bust. A sculpture of Olganos with an inscription, dated to around the 2nd century, was found at Kopanos and is kept in the Veria museum.
