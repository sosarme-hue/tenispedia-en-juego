heroine of Irish mythology, mother of Cú Chulainn

Deichtine

Deichtine is a figure of Irish mythology, sister of Conchobar and mother of Cú Chulainn, the great hero of Ulster.

The myth

In one version, Deichtine was Conchobar's charioteer. One day a flock of birds stripped the grass of Emain Macha, and the men of Ulster chased them in their chariots until nightfall brought snow. They sought shelter in a house, where a young man welcomed them while his wife was in labour. At dawn they found themselves at Brug na Bóinne: the house had vanished, but a baby boy and two colts born that same night remained.

Deichtine raised the boy, who fell ill and died soon after. Drinking from a cup, she swallowed a tiny creature that leapt from it into her mouth. While she slept, Lug appeared and told her that the house was his and that the child in her womb was his. Conchobar then married her to Sualtam, who owed him a great favour. Ashamed, she crushed the child within her until she was like a virgin again, and later conceived Cú Chulainn by Sualtam.

Trivia

A creature in the cup. According to the story, Deichtine drank from a cup and a tiny creature leapt from it into her mouth, an episode that foreshadows her pregnancy by Lug.
A hidden pregnancy. To hide that she carried another man's child, Deichtine crushed the baby within her so that she could return to Sualtam as if she were a virgin.
