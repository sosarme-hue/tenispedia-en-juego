home of the Vanir in Norse mythology

Vanaheim

Vanaheim (Old Norse Vanaheimr, 'home of the Vanir') is the place associated with the Vanir, one of the two groups of gods in Norse mythology alongside the Æsir. Its place within the Norse cosmos is debated, and it matters mainly as the origin of gods such as Njörðr.

The myth

According to the stories, Vanaheim is where the god Njörðr was raised or created. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the section called Gylfaginning, the enthroned figure of High explains that Njörðr was raised in Vanaheim but was later sent as a hostage to the Æsir.

The only passage in the Poetic Edda that names it appears in Vafþrúðnismál. There, Odin in disguise as Gagnráðr tests the jötunn Vafþrúðnir in a contest of wits. Asked where Njörðr came from, Vafþrúðnir replies that he was created in Vanaheim by 'wise powers', that he was exchanged as a hostage during the war between the Æsir and the Vanir, and that after Ragnarök he will return as a 'wise Vanir'.

In the Heimskringla, within the Ynglinga saga, Snorri gives a euhemerized version in which the gods appear as heroes of a real past. Vanaheim is there a land around the Don River, which Snorri says was once called Vanakvísl, and the land of its people was called Vanaland. The Æsir sent the god Hœnir as a hostage, and he was immediately made chieftain.

Modern scholars debate whether Vanaheim belonged among the Nine Worlds. Rudolf Simek argues that Snorri invented the name as a counterpart to Asgard, while others place it probably in the underworld.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Vafþrúðnismál), Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning) and the Heimskringla (Ynglinga saga).

Trivia

A river with a divine name. Snorri identifies the Don River with the ancient Tanais, which he says was once called Tanakvísl or Vanakvísl.
A son named after a land. According to the Heimskringla, Sveigðir married a woman named Vana in Vanaland, and they had a son named Vanlandi, meaning 'man from the land of the Vanir'.
