Trojan hero of the Aeneid, ancestor of the Memmii

Mnestheus

Mnestheus is a hero of Roman mythology who appears in Virgil's Aeneid. He is presented as the ancestral hero of the Memmii and as the most senior captain among Aeneas's lieutenants.

The myth

According to Virgil, Mnestheus belongs to the house of Assaracus, the same Trojan lineage as Aeneas. Among the hero's lieutenants, who are only vaguely sketched by the poet, he holds the highest rank: when Aeneas is away, Mnestheus takes charge, as he does in Book IX, when he leads the Trojans in his absence.

In Book V, during the funeral games held for Anchises, Aeneas's father, Mnestheus competes in the boat race and finishes in second place. His role is that of a loyal and capable captain rather than a leading protagonist.

Ancient sources: Virgil, Aeneid, Books V and IX.

Trivia

Ancestor of the Memmii. Virgil describes him as the ancestral hero of the Memmii, a family of ancient Rome.
Second place in the boat race. At the funeral games for Anchises he finishes second in the boat race, one of the most discussed episodes of the Aeneid.
