Latin personification of horror in Seneca's tragedy

Horror (Frice)

Horror is a personification of dread who appears in Seneca's tragedy Oedipus, the only work where it figures as a divine being. Its Latin name conveys the same idea as the Greek phríkē, the shudder and trembling that terror brings.

The myth

In Seneca's tragedy Oedipus, a scene of summoning brings a terrible company out of the depths. First come the warriors born from the dragon and the brothers sprung from the teeth of the monster of Dirce, along with the plague, eager to devour the people of Ogyges.

Then the Erinys is heard, together with blind Fury and Horror. They are followed by Grief, tearing at her hair, Sickness, barely able to hold up her failing head, harsh Old Age, and anguished Fear: everything that eternal darkness produces and hides.

Ancient sources: Seneca, Oedipus, a Latin tragedy in which Horror is listed among the powers rising from the realm of the dead.

Trivia

A root in trembling. The Greek phríkē shares its root with the verb phríttō, 'to tremble'.
No divine status in Greece. In Greek tragedy phríkē is a common noun, not a deity; the personification appears only in Seneca's Latin version.
