Russian bogatyr of the Kievan cycle

Dobrynya Nikitich

Dobrynya Nikitich is one of the most popular bogatyrs, or epic knights, of Russian folklore from the Kievan cycle, which grew out of the byliny, epic songs from the area around Kiev, the capital of Kievan Rus. The character is loosely based on a real warlord who served Sviatoslav the Great and tutored Vladimir the Great.

The myth

According to the byliny, Dobrynya is the son of Nikita, the voivode of Ryazan. His mother warns him to avoid the Saracen Mountains, not to trample dragon pups, not to rescue Russian captives, and not to bathe in the Puchai River. Dobrynya ignores all four warnings.

In the Puchai he meets a dragon with twelve trunks, which some versions identify with Zmey Gorynych. Unarmed, he defeats it using a hat of Greek make. The dragon pleads for her life and the two agree on a non-aggression pact, but she breaks it at once, flies to Kiev and abducts Zabava Putyatishna, the niece of Prince Vladimir. The prince orders him to rescue her on pain of death, so Dobrynya receives the horse Burko and a silk whip from Shemakha. During the fight a dragon pup bites the horse's leg and immobilizes it, but the whip restores it. After three days of combat in the Saracen Mountains, a voice from Heaven urges him to keep fighting, and he finally kills the dragon. Its blood does not soak into the earth until he drives his spear into the ground and speaks an incantation.

Because he is a peasant, he cannot marry Zabava, who is given to Alyosha Popovich. Instead, Dobrynya marries Nastasia, a polyanitsa he meets along the way.

Legacy

He appears alongside Ilya Muromets and Alyosha Popovich in Viktor Vasnetsov's painting Bogatyrs (1898). Alexander Grechaninov wrote an opera about him in 1901, and his story inspired animated films such as Dobrynya Nikitich and Zmey Gorynych (2006). A female version of the character, later revealed to be his wife in disguise, appears in the video game Fate/Grand Order (2021). An icebreaker bears his name, and in 2024 a seal linked to one of his descendants was found in Oryol.

Ancient sources: The byliny, oral epic songs recorded in the nineteenth century, such as the version from the Povenets District collected by A. F. Gilferding in 1871.

Trivia

A seal in Oryol. In November 2024, a seal was found in central Oryol that, according to archaeologist Oleg Radyush, belonged to Putyata Vyshatich, a grandson of Dobrynya. His link to Prince Sviatoslav II, who ruled Kiev from 1093 to 1113, made it possible to date the find.
A dog with his name. In 2015, Russian police gave their French counterparts a dog named Dobrynya in solidarity after Diesel, a French police dog, was lost in a raid following the Paris attacks of November 2015.
A hat instead of a sword. In the version collected by Gilferding, the hero defeats the dragon with a hat of Greek make, an unusual object that takes the place of the usual weapons.
