suitor of Penelope in the Odyssey

Amphimedon

Amphimedon is one of Penelope's suitors in Homer's Odyssey. The son of Melaneus and a native of Ithaca, he dies in the final slaughter in Odysseus's hall and later recounts what happened in the Underworld.

The myth

According to Homer's account, the suitors occupied Odysseus's palace during his absence, consumed his wealth and courted Penelope. When Odysseus returned and revealed himself, with the help of his son Telemachus, the hall became a battlefield. While retreating from the fight, Amphimedon gave a glancing blow to Telemachus's carapace, but shortly afterwards he fell to him.

In the Underworld, Amphimedon told the story of the suitors' slaughter at the hands of Odysseus and Telemachus.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Odyssey, where he recounts the slaughter of the suitors.

Trivia

A genus of sponges. The name Amphimedon also designates a genus of sponges.
