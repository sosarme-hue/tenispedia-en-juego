naiad of Greek mythology

Strophia

Strophia is a naiad, a nymph of freshwater springs in Greek mythology, associated with a spring near Thebes. She matters because she appears in a account by Callimachus of rivers and springs fleeing the pregnant goddess Leto.

The myth

Strophia was a daughter of the river-god Ismenus, the god of the dark pebbles, and sister of Dirce. According to Callimachus, when Leto wandered through Aonia, Ismenus feared Hera's wrath and, holding his two daughters by the hand, refused to shelter the goddess, so Strophia and her sister joined the flight.

The spring that bore her name lay on Mount Cithaeron, near Thebes and the river Ismenus, which was named after her father. Together with Dirce's spring, it was one of the two main fountains of the region.

Ancient sources: Callimachus mentions her in his Hymn to Delos, in the episode where rivers and springs flee from Leto.

Trivia

Sister of Dirce. Strophia shared a father with Dirce, and the two presided over the region's two main springs near Thebes.
A possible famous brother. Some sources suggest that the younger Linus, music teacher of Heracles, may also have been a son of Ismenus and therefore her brother.
