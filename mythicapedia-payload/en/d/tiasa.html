river of Laconia, daughter of Eurotas

Tiasa

Tiasa is a river near Amyclae, close to Sparta, personified as a daughter of the river Eurotas. Her name appears in ancient sources alongside a sanctuary of the Charites.

The myth

According to Pausanias, tradition held that Tiasa was a daughter of the Eurotas, the great river of Laconia. Her waters ran down from Sparta toward Amyclae, and beside them stood a sanctuary of the Charites Phaenna and Cleta, the goddesses of grace whom the poet Alcman celebrated in his verses.

Tradition credited the founding of that sanctuary to the hero Lacedaemon, who was also said to have given the goddesses their names. Hesychius records her as a spring or a river, and Athenaeus mentions a spring of Tiasus, showing that the name was used in both senses.

Worship and religion

The sanctuary of the Charites beside the river was a local place of worship, traditionally linked to Lacedaemon. The sources do not describe how it was celebrated or whether the cult survived beyond antiquity, so nothing more can be said with confidence.

Ancient sources: Pausanias (Description of Greece) and Hesychius; Athenaeus also mentions her.
