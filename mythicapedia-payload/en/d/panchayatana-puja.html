Hindu worship of five deities in a square (Smarta tradition)

Panchayatana puja

Panchayatana puja is a system of worship in the Smarta tradition of Hinduism in which five deities are honoured in a quincunx pattern: Ganesha, Mahadevi, Shiva, Vishnu and Surya. It is attributed to the philosopher Adi Shankara, although archaeological evidence shows the practice is much older.

The myth

The sources describe a doctrine rather than a single origin myth. According to the Smarta tradition, all murtis (idols) are icons of Saguna Brahman, the divine with form, which serves as a path to realising the formless Nirguna Brahman. The five deities are seen as multiple manifestations of one divinity rather than as separate beings.

The Puranas express this idea in various ways. In the Narada Purana, each central deity is accompanied by the other four in the corners. In the Skanda Purana, Surya declares that he is one yet has become five, like a stage manager playing several roles. In the Padma Purana, Krishna says he is one, born in five ways.

Worship and religion

Worshippers arrange the five deities in a square, with one at the centre and four at the corners. Images, aniconic forms or a mix of both may be used. Often five sacred stones are employed, such as the banalinga of Shiva or the salagrama of Vishnu, and sometimes just five marks drawn on the floor. Smarta temples with five shrines, one central and four at the corners, also exist. The practice was widespread in medieval India and remains alive in Smarta households.

Ancient sources: Puranas such as the Narada, Skanda, Padma and Devi-Bhagavata describe this form of worship.
