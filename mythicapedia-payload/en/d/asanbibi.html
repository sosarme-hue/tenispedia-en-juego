folk goddess of southern Bengal

Asanbibi

Asanbibi is a folk deity worshipped mainly in southern Bengal, alongside her six sisters in the group known as the Satbibis, or seven ladies. Some modern scholars link them to the Saptamatrikas of Hinduism, although the similarities between the two groups are very limited.

The myth

The reference texts do not record a detailed story about Asanbibi's origin or deeds. She appears mostly as part of a group of seven goddesses. Her companions include Olabibi, associated with cholera, Ajgaibibi, Chandbibi, Bahadabibi, Jhetunebibi and Jholabibi.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped mainly in southern Bengal, almost always together with her sisters rather than as a solitary goddess. The sources do not describe particular temples, festivals or rites. A terracotta seal found at Mohenjo-daro, a city of the Indus Valley Civilisation, shows seven women standing together, which may suggest that the collective worship of seven goddesses is very ancient.

Trivia

A goddess of cholera. Among the sisters is Olabibi, who in these beliefs is the goddess of cholera, a disease that for centuries struck the riverside areas of Bengal.
Seven women on an ancient seal. A terracotta seal found at Mohenjo-daro shows seven women standing together, one of the oldest images linked to the worship of seven goddesses.
