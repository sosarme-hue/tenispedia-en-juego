Greek spirit of words of good omen

Eupheme

Eupheme is, in Greek mythology, the personification of acclaim, praise and shouts of triumph. She was the counterpart of Momus, criticism, and her name means "proclamation". She appears in two different traditions: as nurse of the Muses and, according to the Orphics, as a daughter of Hephaestus.

The myth

According to myths about catasterism, Eupheme was the nurse of the Muses and lived on Mount Helicon, although her parents are not specified. From her union with Pan was born Crotus, foster brother of the Muses, whom the stories turn into the constellation Sagittarius.

In the other version, recorded by the Orphics, Eupheme is one of the four daughters of Hephaestus and Aglaia, alongside Eucleia ("glory"), Euthenia ("prosperity") and Philophrosyne ("welcome"). The Neoplatonist philosopher Proclus transmits this genealogy, which Martin Litchfield West includes in his reconstruction of the Orphic Rhapsodies and describes as a new idea.

Ancient sources: The Orphics make her a daughter of Hephaestus and Aglaia, as transmitted by Proclus, and other accounts place her as nurse of the Muses.
