humanoid creature of modern American folklore

Mothman

Mothman is a towering humanoid creature with wings and glowing red eyes, linked since 1966 to Point Pleasant, West Virginia, in the United States. It belongs to modern American folklore and, over the years, its legend has spread to other countries.

The myth

According to the accounts, the first witness report dates from the night of November 15, 1966, in Point Pleasant. Two young couples said they had seen, beside a former military site, a figure about two metres tall with wings and bright red eyes, which took flight and chased their car to the edge of town.

After the Silver Bridge collapsed in December 1967, killing 46 people, the idea spread that the creature had foretold the tragedy. Writer John Keel turned it into a prophetic being tied to UFOs and men in black. Tradition holds that the name "Mothman" may have been inspired by Batman's villain Killer Moth, who was popular at the time.

Similar beings have since been reported in Cornwall (England, 1976-1978), Chihuahua (Mexico, 2009), Santiago de Chile (from 2013) and Chicago (2017). Sceptical investigators attribute many of these cases to real birds, such as cranes, herons or owls, misidentified by witnesses.

Legacy

John Keel's book The Mothman Prophecies (1975) shaped the legend and inspired the film The Mothman Prophecies (2002), starring Richard Gere. The Mexican TV series El Hombre Polilla premiered in 2015, and the character appears in the video game Fallout 76 (2018). Point Pleasant has held an annual festival devoted to the legend since 2002, which is an important source of local tourism.

Trivia

A borrowed name. A journalist named the creature after the couples' press conference. Many believe the name drew on the Batman comics, whose villain Killer Moth did not appear in the TV series.
A stuffed owl. In 1966 a snowy owl was shot in Point Pleasant and described in local papers as a "giant owl". It has been suggested it may have started some sightings, and it is now displayed stuffed at the Mothman Museum.
