bodhisattva of moonlight, attendant of the Medicine Buddha

Candraprabha

Candraprabha, the Moonlight Bodhisattva, is a figure of Indian-origin Mahayana Buddhism who appears beside his brother Sūryaprabha as servant of the Medicine Buddha. His veneration spread across East Asia, where he is linked with the moon and with the Mid-Autumn Festival.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, in a distant past a Brahman and his two sons made a shared vow to relieve the suffering of all beings. Dipankara encouraged them and renamed them: the father became the Medicine King and the sons Sunlight and Moonlight. Through their spiritual path, the father attained buddhahood and the sons became bodhisattvas.

Other versions hold that Candraprabha was a former incarnation of Shakyamuni, or that he has a parent-child bond with the Medicine Buddha.

Worship and religion

His birthday is celebrated on the 15th day of the eighth lunar month, coinciding with the Mid-Autumn Festival among Han Chinese Buddhists, who also commemorate the harvest moon. Devotees invoke him in mantra recitation for protection from obstacles and illness.

Legacy

In Chinese tradition he appears as the moon god Taiyin Xingjun and as the Yuehui Bodhisattva in Yiguandao. In the Vajradhātu and Garbhadhātu mandalas he has his own place, marked by the half-moon symbol.

Ancient sources: Buddhist texts such as the Scripture of the Medicine Buddha and the Empowerment Sutra, plus Chinese commentaries on the Medicine Buddha scripture.
