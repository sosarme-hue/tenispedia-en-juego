Japanese yōkai with a stretching neck

Rokurokubi

The rokurokubi is a yōkai from Japanese folklore that looks like an ordinary person by day but can stretch its neck to startling lengths at night. It is one of the best-known creatures in Japanese ghost tales (kaidan).

The myth

According to tradition, many rokurokubi live among humans unnoticed and may even marry mortals. They guard their secret carefully, yet feel an almost irresistible urge to frighten and spy on people, and some go much further, devouring their victims or drinking their blood.

A variant, the nukekubi, has a head that detaches from the body and drifts freely through the night. Edo-period tales say the body must stay still while the head is away, since if it moves, the head can no longer reattach. In the Sorori Monogatari, a woman whose head flees from a swordsman turns out to be her soul wandering while she sleeps. Other texts, such as the Kasshi Yawa, treat the phenomenon as an illness. Later stories of the stretching neck seem to have grown from misreading pictures of the nukekubi, in which a cord linked head and body.

Legacy

The creature appears in many modern works: as a side character in the manga Rosario + Vampire, as a student who transforms by accident in Jigoku Sensei Nube, and as an inspiration for the elastic neck of Orochimaru in Naruto. It also turns up in One Piece, Yo-Kai Watch, the Touhou video game series through the character Sekibanki, and the 2005 film The Great Yokai War.

Ancient sources: Edo-period collections of ghost stories, including the Sorori Monogatari (1663), the Shokoku Hyaku Monogatari (1677), the Kokon Hyaku Monogatari Hyōban (1686) and the Kasshi Yawa (c. 1821).

Trivia

Origin of the name. The name may come from rokuro, a word for a potter's wheel, a well's pulley or an umbrella handle, all of which lengthen.
A village of rokurokubi. The Churyō Manroku describes a village in the mountains of Yoshino where all residents, children included, wore scarves to hide a line around their necks.
