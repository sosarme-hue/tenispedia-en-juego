location in Jotunheim in Norse mythology

Glæsisvellir

Glæsisvellir, meaning "Glittering Plains", is a place in Jotunheim, the world of the giants, in Norse mythology. It is best known for Odinsaker, a field where no one died, and for the realm of the giant Gudmund in the Hervarar saga.

The myth

According to the legends, Glæsisvellir held a place called Odinsaker, also written Údáinsakr, meaning roughly "Deathless Acre". Anyone who went there became healthy and young again, so no one ever died in that place.

The Hervarar saga places in Glæsisvellir the kingdom of Gudmund, a friendly giant who was popular in the later sagas, and of his son Höfund. The Eireks saga víðförla tells of a man who searched for Údáinsakr and eventually found it. In his Latin Gesta Danorum, Saxo Grammaticus mentions Odainsaker as the retreat of the Scanian governor Fialler, who withdrew there after being attacked by the Danish king Wiglek.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar, Bósa, Eiríks saga víðförla and Þorsteins þáttr bæjarmagns sagas, and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A field without death. Odinsaker, the name of this place, is linked to the idea of a land where old age and death do not reach.
A Latin mention. Saxo Grammaticus, the Danish chronicler, mentions it as the refuge of Fialler after his defeat by Wiglek.
