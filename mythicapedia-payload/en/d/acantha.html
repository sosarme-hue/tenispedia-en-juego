Acantha

Acantha is a minor character often presented as part of Greek mythology, although her story does not appear in any classical source. It is a modern legend that explains the origin of the acanthus plant.

The myth

According to the tale that circulates in books and encyclopedias, Acantha was a nymph loved by Apollo. She rejected his persistent advances and scratched his face, and the god, as punishment, turned her into the spiny-leaved plant called acanthus. Later compilers have repeated the story often, but rarely cite ancient sources.

The first edition of Lemprière's dictionary gave no references; the 1839 edition cited Pliny, Dioscorides and Hesychius, but on inspection none of them tells the story. It is also absent from the Thesaurus Linguae Latinae and the Thesaurus Linguae Graecae. It is thought to borrow elements from the myth of Oenone, a nymph who scratched Apollo's face in Ovid's Fasti, although those lines have later spurious interpolations.

Legacy

Its presence is mostly editorial: the story has been reprinted in books, encyclopedias and journals, which has made it pass for an ancient tradition.

Ancient sources: It does not appear in any known classical text; she is only indirectly linked to ancient authors such as Ovid, through Oenone.

Trivia

Mother of the fourth sun. Cicero mentions a woman named Acantho who became the mother of the so-called fourth sun in Rhodes.
