Maya god of the sky and creator

Itzamná

Itzamná is the great creator and sky god of Maya mythology, one of the most important deities of the Classic and Postclassic pantheon. He is linked to wisdom, writing, medicine and agriculture.

The myth

According to tradition, Itzamná was the son of Hunab Ku, the invisible supreme god, and the creator of humankind. He was also the father of the Bacabs, fourfold deities who dwell within the Earth.

Some colonial chronicles present him as the inventor of priestly writing and as the sage who named the lands of Yucatán. His figure blends with that of Zamná, a priest who founded Chichén Itzá around 525 CE and was later raised to divine status.

In the codices he sits on his celestial throne, governing agriculture and hunting. Classic scenes show him in more dramatic situations: clinging to the back of a peccary, or shot in his bird form, the Principal Bird Deity, linked to the world trees and the passage of time.

Worship and religion

Diego de Landa notes that in the month Uo the ritual books were sprinkled in his name, in Zip he was invoked as a god of medicine, and in Mac the elders venerated him alongside the Chaacs, the rain gods. Some kings of Yaxchilán, Dos Pilas and El Naranjo carried his name in their titles. No living cult is recorded, though twentieth-century Lacandon lore tells of a creator god who may be a late successor.

Legacy

He is credited with teaching people to cultivate henequen, the agave that still shapes farming in Yucatán. For years, archaeologists called him God D.

Ancient sources: The chronicles of Diego de Landa, Diego López de Cogolludo and Bartolomé de las Casas, and the Dresden and Paris Codices.

Trivia

A disputed name. For a long time his name was read as 'lizard house', but that reading has been abandoned. Some studies propose a link to roots meaning secretions such as sap or dew.
A god without a known name. Before Maya names were deciphered, researchers knew him as God D, and some still use that label.
