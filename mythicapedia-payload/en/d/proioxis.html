Greek personification of pursuit in battle

Proioxis

Proioxis is, in Greek mythology, the personification of onrush or pursuit in battle, the surge that drives warriors after their enemies. She appears among the figures wrought on Heracles' shield, alongside her counterpart Palioxis, the backrush.

The myth

Proioxis has no independent stories. Her most famous appearance is in the epic poem The Shield of Heracles, attributed to Hesiod, where the hero's shield is decorated with scenes of war. There Proioxis and Palioxis appear alongside Homados (tumult), Phobos (panic), Androktasia (slaughter) and Ker, the violent death that drags off a newly wounded warrior.

Eris, the strife that stirs men to fight, and Kydoimos, confusion, are also shown on the shield. Together they present the chaos of battle as a sequence of abstract forces surrounding the fighters.

Ancient sources: Appears in The Shield of Heracles, an epic poem attributed to Hesiod, where she is wrought on the hero's shield.
