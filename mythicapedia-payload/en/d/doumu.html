Taoist goddess, mother of the Big Dipper stars

Doumu

Doumu is a goddess of Chinese folk religion and Taoism, mother of the stars of the Big Dipper and mother of the God of Heaven. She combines the roles of wife, mother and feminine aspect of a celestial deity, and she matters for her link to star worship and to Taoist esoteric teaching.

The myth

According to tradition, Doumu is the feminine aspect of the God of Heaven, at once his wife and his mother. The seven main stars of the Big Dipper, together with two more invisible to the naked eye, are considered her sons, who are themselves seen as a ninefold manifestation of another name of the God of Heaven.

In some Taoist accounts she is identified with Xiwangmu, an ambiguous goddess of life and death. In Taoist esoteric teaching she is equated with Jinling Shengmu and seen as mother of the immortal "red infant", a symbol of the Dao enshrined in the centre of the human body. This links her to myths of the births of Laozi and the Yellow Emperor, whose mother conceived after seeing lightning from the Big Dipper, as recorded by Ge Hong (283-343).

In Chinese Tantric Buddhism (Tangmi) she was merged with the bodhisattva Marici, also described as mother of the Way and of the Dipper. Her Taoist scripture uses the same longer dharani associated with Marici, with eight Chinese verses added at the start in her praise.

Worship and religion

Her cult belongs to folk religion and Taoism, with shrines and altars in China, Taiwan and Chinese communities in Southeast Asia. Statues are venerated in temples such as the Green Ram Temple in Chengdu.

Legacy

Her image survives in statues from several dynasties and in modern shrines and paintings, and she appears in depictions of the Big Dipper.

Ancient sources: Ge Hong (283-343) records the myth of the Yellow Emperor's birth linked to the Big Dipper.
