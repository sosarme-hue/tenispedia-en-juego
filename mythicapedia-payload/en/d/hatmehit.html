fish goddess of ancient Egypt

Hatmehit

Hatmehit was the fish goddess of ancient Egypt, worshipped mainly in Mendes, in the Nile Delta. She was linked to fish, the Nile's annual flood and the primordial waters, and she is one of the most distinctive deities of Lower Egypt.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Hatmehit was the chief goddess of Mendes during the Old Kingdom and the wife of the ram god Banebdjedet, with whom she formed a triad alongside a little-known child who was later identified with Harpocrates, the child Horus. Her name is usually translated as Foremost of Fish, and she was connected with the first fish to emerge from primordial chaos, with the god Khnum and with the Nile. Another of her titles, She who presides over the Inundation, links her to the floodwaters and the fish that came with them.

Over time, Isis and Hathor took her place in various contexts. In the local cult of Mendes, Isis came to be identified with Hatmehit as a form of the goddess particular to the city, a development supported by the association between Banebdjedet and Osiris. Some Egyptologists also connect her with Hathor, and epithets that show her searching for the members of her brother suggest a link with Nephthys.

Worship and religion

Her cult was centred on Nome 16 of Lower Egypt, around Mendes, although she also appears in temples at Behbeit el-Hagar, Dendera and Edfu. Stelae decorated with fish and jars containing the remains of young schilbe fish have been found, votive offerings accompanied by beer jugs and bread moulds. In the calendar, the day IV Akhet 28 commemorated her departure from Mendes in fish form, and eating or offering fish was forbidden. There is no living cult today.

Ancient sources: The Cairo Calendar and the inscriptions of the temples at Dendera and Edfu, Egyptian texts that mention her.

Trivia

A day without fish. The Egyptian calendar forbade eating or offering fish on IV Akhet 28, in memory of the goddess leaving Mendes in fish form.
Goddess of perfume. Mendes was a centre of perfume production, and Hatmehit became associated with good scents; one of her epithets was She who creates everyone's scent.
