Peisistratus

Peisistratus is a figure of Greek mythology, the youngest son of King Nestor of Pylos. He appears in the Odyssey as the travelling companion of Telemachus, who accompanies him in his search for Odysseus.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the Odyssey, Peisistratus was still a small boy when his father Nestor and his elder brothers, Antilochus and Thrasymedes, left for the Trojan War. It was then that he became a close friend of Telemachus, son of Odysseus, who was roughly his age.

When Telemachus set out from Ithaca to seek news of his father, Nestor sent Peisistratus along as his escort. Together they travelled from Pylos to the palace of Helen and Menelaus in Sparta, where they heard about Odysseus's fate. On the return journey both went back to Pylos. Peisistratus plays no heroic role of his own; he serves as the loyal young companion of the main character.

Ancient sources: He appears in Homer's Odyssey, in books III, IV and XV.
