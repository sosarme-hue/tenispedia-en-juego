merchant and gusli musician of Novgorod in Russian folklore

Sadko

Sadko is the main character of the Russian byliny, oral epic poems, a gusli musician and merchant from Novgorod who bargained with the Sea Tsar. His story is best known outside Russia through Rimsky-Korsakov's opera.

The myth

According to the tale, Sadko was a poor musician who played the gusli on the shores of Lake Ilmen. The Sea Tsar, who enjoyed his playing, offered to help him: Sadko made a wager with the local merchants that he could catch a gold-finned fish, and when he did, he became a rich man.

Wealth brought pride. Sadko tried to control all trade in Novgorod and stopped keeping his promise to play for the Sea Tsar. The Tsar raised a storm that stopped his ships. After gold failed to appease him, the crew drew lots, and the lot fell on Sadko, who was thrown overboard. Beneath the waves he played for the Tsar, whose dancing stirred up storms that sank ships. Saint Nicholas advised him to break his gusli and, among the maidens the Tsar offered as a bride, to choose the last one and not consummate the marriage. He did so and woke on the lake shore beside his family in Novgorod, where he was remembered as a generous man.

Legacy

The story inspired Alexei Tolstoy's poem (1871-1872) and Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov's opera Sadko, for which he also wrote the libretto. In 1953 Aleksandr Ptushko adapted it into a film of the same name.

Ancient sources: It is a Russian medieval oral epic (bylina), known through many recorded variants.
