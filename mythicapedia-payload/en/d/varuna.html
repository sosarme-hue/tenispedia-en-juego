Vedic god of waters and moral order

Varuna

Varuna is a Vedic god of cosmic and moral order, first linked to the sky and later to the waters. His name is often read as 'that which encloses', and as guardian of truth and of ṛta he was one of the principal deities of Vedic religion and early Hinduism.

The myth

According to the hymns of the Rigveda, Varuna rules the sky from a palace with a thousand gates and watches over human conduct through his spies, the stars. He punishes lies and sin, yet he also grants forgiveness and protection to those who repent. He is almost always paired with his brother Mitra: together they represent the whole day, with Varuna as night and darkness and Mitra as daylight and morning. Jointly they guard oaths and contracts.

Over time his domain moved from the firmament to the waters. In the Puranic period he became lord of seas, rivers and lakes, living in an underwater palace and attended by the river goddesses Ganga and Yamuna. He rides a makara, a creature resembling a crocodile, and holds a noose and a pitcher. His standing declined, and he became a guardian of the west, one of the dikpalas.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or festivals devoted to Varuna. In Japanese Buddhism, however, he is known as Suiten and ranks among the Twelve Devas (Jūniten), showing that he was still honoured within another tradition.

Legacy

In the Tamil grammar Tolkāppiyam he appears as Kadalon, god of the sea and rain. Georges Dumézil once linked his name to the Greek Ouranos, although that derivation is now widely rejected in favour of a root meaning 'to moisten'. In Zoroastrian scholarship, Varuna and Mitra are connected with the origins of Ahura Mazda, but scholars disagree about the exact nature of that relationship.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda (hymns 1.25, 7.86–88 and 8.8, among others), the Atharvaveda, which describes him as all-knowing, and the Ramayana, which makes him master of the western elephant Saumanasa.

Trivia

A crocodile-like mount. His vahana, or vehicle, is the makara, a creature shaped somewhat like a crocodile.
An elephant of the west. According to the Ramayana, Varuna owned Saumanasa, the elephant of the west, one of the four that hold up the universe.
A golden-winged messenger. Rigveda 10.123 names his messenger, the golden-winged bird Hiraṇyapakṣa, which some interpreters identify with the flamingo.
