primordial Sumerian goddess of the sea and creation

Nammu

Nammu is a primordial goddess of Sumerian Mesopotamia, regarded in the local theology of Eridu as a creator deity and mother of Enki. She matters because she took part in the creation of humankind and, as mother of An (Heaven) and Ki (Earth), she stands at the origin of the cosmos.

The myth

According to the stories, Nammu embodies the primeval ocean, the deep waters from which everything emerged. As a goddess of birth, she is named mother of Enki and, in a single inscription, wife of An, although she was usually not believed to have a spouse.

In the Old Babylonian myth Enki and Ninmah, Nammu joins that pair and a group of seven minor goddesses in creating mankind. The tradition tells that creatures called sig-en-sig-du gathered clay, which she shaped and brought to life. Her presence sets this account apart from other texts on the same theme, such as Atra-Hasis. She was believed to remain an active figure, unlike most primordial deities.

Worship and religion

Her worship was modest in every period. She belonged to the local pantheon of Eridu, where she was honoured as the divine mother of the city. A temple dedicated to her existed in Ur in the Old Babylonian period, with clergy and a field named after her. Lugal-kisalsi, king of Uruk, also built a temple in her honour. She appears in the god list from Nippur and, according to a boundary stone inscription, a temple of hers stood in the Sealand region. There is no living cult.

Ancient sources: The myth Enki and Ninmah, the god list An = Anum, the Early Dynastic zame hymns, the Nippur god list and Old Babylonian incantation texts.

Trivia

A king with her name. Her name appears in that of King Ur-Nammu, which for a long time was the only known theophoric name invoking her.
A disputed name. The usual translation, «creatrix», rests on an etymological hypothesis that not all scholars accept.
