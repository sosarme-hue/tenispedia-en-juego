sea creature of Maliseet and Passamaquoddy mythology

Apotamkin

The Apotamkin is a creature from the mythology of the Maliseet and Passamaquoddy peoples of northeastern North America. According to the tradition, it is a giant fanged sea monster that lives in Passamaquoddy Bay, at the mouth of the St. Croix River, and pulls careless children under to eat them.

The myth

Accounts vary: sometimes it is humanoid, sometimes it has a fish body with human hair, but it almost always has long red hair. In 1888, the Journal of American Folklore compared it to a mermaid.

It is not always evil. Its presence keeps children from entering the water unsupervised and, according to the Passamaquoddy, stops them from falling through thin ice. The Pacific Northwest version, by contrast, is a tall, apelike being with orange-brown fur whose cry was said to be terrifying enough to cause a heart attack. In both regions it works as a bogeyman, keeping children away from dangerous places.

Legacy

The Pacific Northwest version appears in Stephenie Meyer's Twilight series, which is why it is sometimes assumed to be a vampiric creature. In 2017, writer-director Carly Sappier of the Tobique First Nation released the short film APOTAMKIN, based on stories about the creature she had been told by elders.

Trivia

Compared to a mermaid. In 1888, the Journal of American Folklore described the Apotamkin as similar to a mermaid.
Premiere in Santa Monica. The short film APOTAMKIN by Carly Sappier premiered at the Laemmle Monica Film Center in Santa Monica.
