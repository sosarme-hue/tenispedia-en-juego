Maya god of the ballgame and father of the Hero Twins

Hun-Hunahpu

Hun-Hunahpu is a deity of Maya mythology, best known from the Popol Vuh, and the father of the Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque. His story explains the ballgame as a passage to the underworld of Xibalba.

The myth

According to the Popol Vuh, Hun-Hunahpu was the son of the deities Ixpiyacoc and Ixmucane. He played ballgame with his brother Vucub-Hunahpu, and the noise disturbed the lords of Xibalba, Hun Came and Vucub Came. They invited the brothers down to the underworld, where they were deceived, tortured and sacrificed.

The brothers were buried, and from the grave grew a calabash tree that bore skulls as fruit. One of them was Hun-Hunahpu's head, which spat into the hand of Ixquic, daughter of a lord of Xibalba, and left her pregnant with the twins. His son Hun-Batz later honoured him.

Worship and religion

Some remains and a Late Classic vessel have been linked to him as a maize god, although the Popol Vuh does not present him that way and the identification is still debated.

Ancient sources: The Popol Vuh, the early colonial K'iche' sacred book, recounts his story in its opening chapters.
