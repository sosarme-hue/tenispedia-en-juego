cruel king of Norse and Germanic legend

Níðuðr

Níðuðr, also known as Niðhad or Niðung, is a cruel king of Norse and Germanic legend, best remembered for holding the smith Völundr captive. He appears in the Völundarkviða, the Þiðrekssaga and Anglo-Saxon poems such as Deor.

The myth

In the Völundarkviða, Níðuðr is king of Närke in Sweden. He captures the smith Völund, has his tendons cut so he cannot flee, and forces him to forge whatever the king demands. Völund takes revenge after his ring ends up with the king's daughter Bodvild: he kills the king's sons when they visit him in secret and turns their remains into goblets, jewels and a brooch sent to the royal household. He then seduces Bodvild, who becomes pregnant, and escapes on wings he has made.

In the Þiðrekssaga the story is similar, but the king, called Niðung, rules Jutland in Denmark. The version includes the sword Mimung, a deception over a stolen knife and a contest between smiths. Velent uses a disguise and a trick devised with his brother Egil to escape.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Old Norse Völundarkviða, the Þiðrekssaga and the Old English poems Deor and Waldere, and possibly on the Ardre VIII image stone and the Franks Casket.
