minor goddess of dusk in Greek mythology

Hesperis

Hesperis is a figure of Greek mythology whose name means "dusk". According to the tradition, she was either one of the Horae or the mother of the Hesperides, depending on the author.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Hesperis was one of the Horae, daughters of Zeus and Themis, minor goddesses who guarded the hours of the day, from just before dawn until just after nightfall. Diodorus Siculus gives a different version: Hesperus and his brother Atlas, men of great renown, owned sheep with yellowish fleece and a golden sheen, which is why poets called the sheep mêla, or "golden apples".

Hesperus had a daughter, Hesperis, whom he gave in marriage to Atlas. Seven daughters were born of this union, called Atlantides after their father and Hesperides after their mother, and the land of Hesperitis took its name from her.

Legacy

In the numismatics of the Iberian Peninsula, some gold dinars and solidi with bilingual Latin-Arabic inscriptions bear on the reverse a star that probably refers to Hesperis, the evening star of the West, the name by which Hispania was identified since classical times.

Ancient sources: Hyginus lists her among the Horae, and Diodorus Siculus makes her the mother of the Hesperides.

Trivia

Golden apples. The poets' name for the "golden apples" would come from the golden sheep owned by Hesperus and Atlas, according to Diodorus.
Evening star of the West. Since classical times Hispania was identified with the evening star of the West, the star that gives Hesperis her name.
