hero founder of Taras, son of Poseidon

Taras

Taras is, in Greek mythology, the hero who founded the colony of Taras, today Taranto in southern Italy. As a son of Poseidon, he explains the city's sea connection and the dolphin that became its emblem.

The myth

According to the myth, Taras was the son of Poseidon and Satyrion, whom some accounts describe as a Tarentine nymph and others as a daughter of Minos. He sailed toward southern Iapygia with a fleet from Crete, but was shipwrecked before reaching land. His father rescued him by sending a dolphin, on which he rode across the sea from the promontory of Taenarum to the coast of southern Italy.

Once ashore, while making sacrifices to Poseidon, a dolphin suddenly leapt into a river. Taras took this as a good omen and resolved to found a city bearing his name. Some versions say he dedicated it to his mother or his wife, and a nearby harbour, still called Torre Saturo, preserves the memory of Satyrion.

Another tradition, recorded by Justin, credits the founding to exiled Spartans, while Servius clarifies that Phalanthus only restored the city. Around 706 BC, Parthenian colonists from Sparta seized the territory from the Iapygians.

Worship and religion

Pausanias reports that the Tarentines honoured Taras as a hero, and their statues showed scenes from his story, including his part in a battle against King Opis.

Legacy

Ancient coins of Taras show the hero riding a dolphin and holding a trident, the symbol of Poseidon. The same image appears today on the emblem of the modern city of Taranto.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Justin and Servius record versions of his story and of the city's foundation.

Trivia

A name that survives. A harbour near Taranto is still called Torre Saturo, a name derived from Satyrion, the mother or wife of the hero according to some accounts.
A dolphin on the crest. The image of Taras riding a dolphin still appears on the emblem of the modern city of Taranto.
