Maya god of merchants and cacao

Ek Chuah

Ek Chuah is a Postclassic Maya deity, patron of traveling merchants and of cacao, one of the most valuable goods in Maya trade. He appears often in the codices, a sign of his importance in Maya religion.

The myth

Ek Chuah is shown with a thick, drooping lower lip, often painted black, and with a pack of goods tied to his head by a rope. His character is ambivalent: as a god of war he carries a spear and is sometimes shown fighting, and even defeated, by Buluk Chabtan, the god of war, violence and sacrifice; as a protector of merchants he is benevolent and guides travelers.

He is linked to the North Star, known as the "Guide of the Merchant", perhaps because ek can mean both "black" and "star". In the Postclassic codices he is often nearly indistinguishable from the older God L, whom he eventually supplanted.

Worship and religion

Owners of cacao groves held ceremonies in his honor during Muwan, a month of the Maya solar calendar. Travelers stacked three stones and offered incense to him at night to ensure a safe return home.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Dresden Codex and the Madrid Codex, two Postclassic Maya manuscripts.

Trivia

Double meaning of his name. The word ek can mean either "black" or "star", which may explain his link to the North Star.
A god defeated in combat. In some scenes he fights Buluk Chabtan and is even defeated by him, which is interpreted as merchants' need for protection on their journeys.
