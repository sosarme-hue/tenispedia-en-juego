legendary sword of King Arthur

Excalibur

Excalibur is the sword of King Arthur in the Arthurian legend. In many versions it is forged by the wizard Merlin and linked to the rightful kingship of Britain. Its fame comes above all from medieval European literature, which made it one of the most recognisable symbols of the Arthurian legend of Celtic origin.

The myth

The first reliable mention of the sword appears in Geoffrey of Monmouth's History of the Kings of Britain, written around 1130. There it is said to have been forged on Avalon, and its name is Latinised as Caliburnus. Over time, the tradition added episodes about how Arthur came to wield it. In Robert de Boron's Merlin, from the early thirteenth century, the sword is fixed in an anvil set on a stone in a churchyard, and only the true king can pull it out. Arthur succeeds, and the feat proves his lineage as the true heir of Uther Pendragon.

In the Vulgate Cycle and the works that followed it, the story becomes more complicated. Arthur breaks his first sword in a duel with King Pellinore, and Merlin takes him to a lake, where a Lady of the Lake gives him a new sword, also called Excalibur. Its scabbard keeps the king from losing blood, but Merlin warns him that a woman he trusts will one day steal it. In the final version, the dying Arthur orders a knight to throw the sword into the water. After hesitating, the knight obeys, a hand rises from the surface to take the blade, and Arthur is carried off to Avalon. Depending on the version, the knight is Griflet, Bedivere or another. In the fifteenth century, Malory brought both swords together under the same name.

In the Welsh tale Culhwch and Olwen, the sword appears as Caledfwlch, one of Arthur's most prized possessions. There his warrior Llenlleawg uses it to kill the Irish king Diwrnach and steal a magical cauldron.

Legacy

The sword has remained a strong presence in popular culture. It inspired John Boorman's film Excalibur in 1981 and Walt Disney's The Sword in the Stone in 1963. It also appears in comics, animated series and television programmes such as The Simpsons. In video games its name and silhouette recur in series such as Final Fantasy, Fire Emblem and Soulcalibur, and in other titles it appears as a main weapon or as a hidden form of another blade. Its name has also inspired many variants used for fictional weapons and characters.

Ancient sources: Geoffrey of Monmouth's History of the Kings of Britain (c. 1130); Robert de Boron's Merlin (c. 1200); the Welsh tale Culhwch and Olwen, from the Mabinogion.

Trivia

A sword in an Italian abbey. In the Abbey of San Galgano, near Siena, a sword is fixed in the ground and no one has ever pulled it out. It is protected by a glass case, and only its hilt sticks out, close to the tomb of the saint.
Many names. The sword's name changed form across ancient texts. Caledfwlch in Welsh, Caliburnus in Latin and forms such as Calibourn or Escalibor in French eventually led to the modern spelling.
