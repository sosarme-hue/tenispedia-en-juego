primordial bovine of Zoroastrian cosmogony

Gavaevodata

Gavaevodata, known in Avestan as Abudad, is the primordial bovine of Zoroastrian cosmogony, one of Ahura Mazda's six material creations and the mythical progenitor of all beneficent animal life. Its Persian myth explains how the world was repopulated after its death.

The myth

According to Zoroastrian tradition, Ahura Mazda created the bovine as one of his six material creations, and the beast lived on the river Veh. Daiti, while Gayomart, the first human, lived on the opposite bank.

When Angra Mainyu attacked the world, the Creator placed the bovine in the sphere of the moon. After Ahriman assaulted the sky, Ormuzd gave it medicinal mang to ease its suffering, but the animal died. From its marrow, organs and body grew fifty-five kinds of grain, twelve medicinal plants and other species, while its soul, Goshorun, lamented until Ormuzd showed her the future Zoroaster.

Ancient sources: Its myth appears fully developed in 9th to 11th century Middle Persian texts, above all the Bundahishn; the Avesta only alludes to it, naming it in two hymns.
