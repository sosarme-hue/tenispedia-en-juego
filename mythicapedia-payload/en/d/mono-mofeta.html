Skunk ape

The skunk ape is a hairy, humanoid cryptid of American popular culture that, according to reports, lives in the forests and swamps of the Southeast, especially Florida. It is often seen as a cousin of Bigfoot, and it takes its name from the foul smell said to accompany it.

The myth

According to the reports, it is a shaggy creature between 1.5 and 2 metres tall, with reddish-brown or nearly black fur, that walks upright and is often described as smaller than the northern Bigfoot. As early as 1818, a local newspaper told of a “man-sized monkey” raiding food stores on the coast near Apalachicola. Sightings multiplied from the 1950s through the 1970s, with a wave in Broward County between 1971 and 1975 and a 1929 incident at the Perky bat tower in the Florida Keys.

No conclusive evidence has ever been produced. In 2000, two photos sent to the Sarasota County Sheriff’s Office showed a creature stealing apples from a back porch. Sceptic Joe Nickell attributes many reports to Florida black bears, some with mange, or to hoaxes, and the United States National Park Service considers it a hoax.

Legacy

The skunk ape has become an unofficial mascot of Florida’s rural and roadside culture, appearing in businesses, television adverts and signs. During the COVID-19 pandemic, Orlando’s Gatorland park used its reputed stench to promote social distancing. Conferences and events are held in its honour.

Trivia

Perky bat tower. In 1929 a creature resembling an ape was reported to have shaken the newly built Perky bat tower in the Florida Keys, scattering the bats before running into the woods.
A failed bill. In 1977 a bill was proposed in the Florida state legislature to make it illegal to take, possess, harm or molest anthropoids or humanoid animals, but it did not pass.
