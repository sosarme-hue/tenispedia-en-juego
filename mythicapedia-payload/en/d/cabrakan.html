Maya god of earthquakes and mountains

Cabrakan

Cabrakan is a Maya god associated with earthquakes and mountains. He appears in the Popol Vuh as an arrogant and violent being who is defeated by the Hero Twins.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cabrakan was a son of Vucub-Caquix, the Seven Macaw, and Chimalmat, and the brother of Zipacna. The stories portray him as proud and destructive, tearing down mountains alongside his brother, and he is often depicted as a massive crocodile or caiman.

In the Popol Vuh, the Hero Twins, Hunahpu and Xbalanque, find him wrecking mountains and tell him of a great mountain in the East that no one could fell. Cabrakan takes the bait and boasts that no mountain can stand before him. On the way, the twins shoot birds with blowguns and cook them underground in magic earth; Cabrakan eats one, grows weaker, and fails to destroy the mountain. He finally collapses, and the twins bury him beneath the earth.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Popol Vuh, the sacred book of the K'iche' people, where he is a minor character.

Trivia

An ironic ending. Cabrakan is defeated after eating magic earth, which scholars read as an irony for a god of earthquakes.
