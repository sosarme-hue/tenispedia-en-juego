goetic demon, Great President of Hell

Caim

Caim (also Caym) is a demon of the Ars Goetia, a grimoire of early modern ceremonial magic. He is listed as a Great President of Hell and is associated with understanding the voices of animals and with knowledge of the future.

The myth

According to the goetic grimoires, Caim is a Great President of Hell who commands thirty legions of demons. He is credited with understanding the voices of birds, oxen, dogs and other creatures, as well as the sounds of water, and with giving accurate answers about the future. He is also described as a formidable fighter.

He is often shown first as a thrush, a black bird, and then in human form holding a sharp sword. When he answers questions he seems to stand on glowing ashes or embers. Other writers present him as a Prince of Hell, dressed in elegant clothes, with the head and wings of a blackbird.

Legacy

He appears in occult illustrations of later centuries, usually as a black bird or as a man with a sword, and is part of the cast of demons that Renaissance and modern demonology made popular.

Trivia

A president, not a king. The title President of Hell has been compared to that of a university or assembly president, though those uses are only premodern and do not clarify the original meaning.
An uncertain etymology. Demonological directories propose that his name comes from the Latin Chamos or Chamus, a name given to Baal, perhaps taken from the Hebrew Chium, an epithet of several Assyrian and Babylonian gods.
