Egyptian heron-like deity linked to the sun and rebirth

Bennu

Bennu is an Egyptian deity with the form of a bird, linked to the sun, creation and rebirth. He may have inspired the phoenix legends that later developed in Greek tradition.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Bennu was a self-created being. Some accounts place his origin at Heliopolis, where he came into being from the fire of a sacred tree burning in a court of the temple of Ra. Other versions say he arose from the bursting heart of Osiris.

In the cosmogonic story, Bennu flew over the waters of Nun that existed before creation, landed on a rock and gave a cry that determined the nature of what would exist. He was regarded as the ba, the personality component of the soul, of Ra, and he was also associated with Atum and Osiris. One of his titles, "Lord of Jubilees", referred to his periodic renewal, much like the sun was thought to renew itself.

Worship and religion

He was probably worshipped at Heliopolis, the cult centre of Atum and Ra. The temple there was famous for its water clocks and other devices for measuring time. Bennu also appears on funerary scarab amulets as a symbol of rebirth.

Legacy

In the fifth century BC, the Greek historian Herodotus described an eagle-like bird with red and gold plumage, which the people of Heliopolis told him was the phoenix. The name "phoenix" may derive from Bennu. A giant extinct heron species, discovered in the United Arab Emirates in 1977, was named the Bennu heron (Ardea bennuides) because of its resemblance to the deity's iconography.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts of the Old Kingdom and the Book of the Dead; Herodotus records the tradition in his Histories while describing Egyptian customs.

Trivia

A giant heron. Remains of a human-sized heron, thought to have died out around 1500 BC, were found in the United Arab Emirates and gave the species the name Ardea bennuides.
The phoenix bird. Herodotus claimed the sacred bird lived five hundred years and that, when it died, its son built an egg of myrrh to carry the father's body to the temple of the Sun at Heliopolis.
