Freyr's ship in Norse mythology

Skíðblaðnir

Skíðblaðnir is the ship of the god Freyr in Norse mythology, regarded as the finest and most ingenious of all ships. It matters because its story shows how the gods obtained their most prized treasures, often through the craft of the dwarfs and the mischief of Loki.

The myth

According to tradition, Skíðblaðnir was crafted by the sons of Ivaldi, dwarfs who also made other divine treasures. It first belonged to Loki, but he gave it to Freyr as a peace offering after a prank: he had cut off the hair of Sif, wife of Thor. Furious, Thor threatened to break every bone in Loki's body, so Loki promised that the dwarfs would make Sif a new head of golden hair, along with other marvels.

The ship is so large that all the gods can board it with their weapons, and once its sail is raised a fair wind carries it wherever it needs to go, over sea or land. When not needed, it can be folded like a cloth and tucked into a pocket, as the Prose Edda and Heimskringla describe. All sources agree it is the finest of ships, though Heimskringla presents it as a magical possession of a euhemerized Odin.

Legacy

The ship appears in the 2018 video game God of War, where it is named but never seen, and in its 2022 sequel God of War Ragnarök, where Kratos uses it as a magical boat. It also lent its name to a digital submarine in the animated series Code Lyoko, chosen for its efficiency.

Ancient sources: Poetic Edda (Grímnismál), Prose Edda by Snorri Sturluson, and Heimskringla, also by Snorri.

Trivia

A ship that fits in a pocket. According to several sources, the ship can be folded like cloth and kept in a pocket when it is not needed at sea.
A name on a submarine. The digital submarine in the series Code Lyoko takes its name from this Norse ship because of its efficiency and usefulness.
