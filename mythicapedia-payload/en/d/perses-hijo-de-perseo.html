Perses (son of Perseus)

Perses is the hero whom Greek mythology presents as the legendary ancestor of the Persians. As the son of Perseus and Andromeda, his story explains the origin of a people's name and links Greek tradition with the East.

The myth

According to the tradition, Perses was the firstborn of Perseus and Andromeda, and the only one of their seven children born in Ethiopia, in the kingdom of Cepheus, Andromeda's father. Since Cepheus had no male heirs, Perseus and Andromeda set off for Argos to claim his grandfather's kingdom and left the boy behind.

The story adds that the people living there, previously called Cephenes, took the name Persians from Perses, and that the Persian kings descended from him. The versions differ on the location: Hellanicus placed the Cephenes near Babylon, while Apollodorus and Tzetzes repeat the tale with Ethiopia as Andromeda's homeland.

The legend also had political consequences. In the early fifth century BC, Xerxes tried to use this shared ancestry to bribe the Argives during his invasion of Greece, but he failed. Later, the pseudo-Platonic dialogue First Alcibiades identified Perses with Achaemenes, the legendary founder of the Persians, and stated that both he and Heracles were descendants of Perseus. Pliny the Elder also credited Perses with inventing arrows.

The scholar Daniel Ogden suggests that the link between the myth and the Persians stems from the resemblance between Cepheus and Cephenes, and from the ancient Greek habit of connecting new lands and peoples with their existing mythology.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus (Library), Hellanicus, John Tzetzes, Pliny the Elder, and the pseudo-Platonic First Alcibiades.

Trivia

A failed bribe. Xerxes tried to exploit the shared ancestry with the Greeks to bribe the Argives during his invasion of Greece, without success.
Inventor of arrows. According to Pliny the Elder, Perses was said to have invented arrows.
