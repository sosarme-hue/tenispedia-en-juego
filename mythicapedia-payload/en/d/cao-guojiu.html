Chinese Taoist deity, one of the Eight Immortals

Cao Guojiu

Cao Guojiu is one of the Eight Immortals of Chinese Taoism and the patron deity of acting and theatre. His name means "imperial brother-in-law", and his figure blends the memory of a Song dynasty court figure with tales of reclusion and spiritual transformation.

The myth

According to tradition, he was the maternal uncle of a Song emperor and the younger brother of the empress dowager Cao. His younger brother, Cao Jingzhi, abused the family's position, bullying others and engaging in corrupt practices. Cao tried to make him change his ways without success. Overcome by shame and disappointment, he gave up his official post and withdrew to a cave to meditate and study the Tao. He wore plain clothes and a straw cap, and at times went as long as ten days without eating. The story presents him as a man who, after deep disillusionment, found his path in seclusion.

One day he met two immortals who asked what he was cultivating. He answered that it was the Tao. When he pointed to his heart, they understood that he had reached unity with heaven and the Tao, and they invited him to join them. Another version tells that he found a scroll in a jade box containing the secret of immortality; as he recited it, the cave filled with light and a stork carried him to the land of the immortals.

Worship and religion

He is depicted as a bearded old man in official court robes, holding a jade tablet or a clapper. His image appears in the iconography of the Eight Immortals, and he is venerated as the patron saint of theatre.

Trivia

A kinship name. His name, Guojiu, means "imperial brother-in-law", since the old Chinese word jiu could also mean "brother-in-law".
Extreme fasting. He is said to have gone as long as ten days without eating during his retreat in the cave.
