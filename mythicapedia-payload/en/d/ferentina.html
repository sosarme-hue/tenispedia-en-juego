Roman goddess and protector of Ferentinum

Ferentina

Ferentina was the patron goddess of Ferentinum, a town in Latium, and protector of the Latin commonwealth. Her sacred grove appears in dramatic episodes of Rome's legendary early history.

The myth

Tradition presents her as the guardian of Ferentinum and of the league of Latin towns, a bond that linked her closely to the Roman state. According to the story, at the beginning of the reign of Rome's last king, Lucius Tarquinius Superbus, the leaders of the Latin towns met in her sacred grove. There, on Tarquin's orders, the leading citizen of Aricia, Turnus Herdonius, was drowned in the grove's holy waters.

Years later, in 491 BC, the Volscian leader Attius Tullus Aufidius tried to provoke Rome. After he persuaded the Senate to expel the Volsci from the city during the Great Games, he met the fleeing Volsci at Ferentina's grove and stirred their resentment against Rome. His actions sparked a war between Rome and the Volsci.

Worship and religion

Her grove and its waters were sacred, and the grove served as a meeting place for the Latin peoples. No further details about her rites survive.

Trivia

Deadly waters. Turnus Herdonius was drowned in the sacred waters of the grove, a killing the story presents as ordered by King Tarquin.
A grove at the start of war. The grove was also the setting of the episode that led to war between Rome and the Volsci in 491 BC.
