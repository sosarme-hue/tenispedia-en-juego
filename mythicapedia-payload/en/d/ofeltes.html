son of King Lycurgus of Nemea in Greek myth

Opheltes

Opheltes, also called Archemorus, was an infant in Greek mythology, son of Lycurgus of Nemea. His death at the hands of a serpent is linked to the origin of the Nemean Games.

The myth

According to the tradition, Lycurgus and Eurydice had Opheltes, and the Delphic oracle warned that the child must not touch the ground until he had learned to walk. His nurse, Hypsipyle, set him down on the ground when a group of Argive warriors marching to Thebes asked her where they could find water.

While she was helping them, a serpent strangled the infant. The warriors killed the serpent, and the seer Amphiaraus, one of them, read the death as an omen of the failure of the campaign against Thebes, renaming the child Archemorus, meaning "Beginning of Doom". Funeral games were held in his honour, which according to some traditions gave rise to the Nemean Games. Sources differ on his mother, who is also given as Nemea or Amphithea.

Worship and religion

The games held in his honour were incorporated into the Nemean Games, one of the major Panhellenic competitions. Excavations at Nemea by the University of California, begun in 1973, have probably uncovered his sanctuary, an open-air structure rebuilt several times.

Ancient sources: Euripides, Statius and Apollodorus name him as the son of Lycurgus, and the scholia to Pindar record Aeschylus's version of his mother.

Trivia

Two mountains named after him. According to Tzetzes, one of the two mountains of Euboea was called Opheltes in his honour. The other was called Zarex.
