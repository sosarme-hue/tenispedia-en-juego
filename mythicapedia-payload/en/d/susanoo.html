Japanese god of the sea, storms and battle

Susanoo

Susanoo is the god of the sea, storms and battle in Japanese Shinto. Brother of Amaterasu, the sun goddess, and Tsukuyomi, the moon god, he appears in the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki as a wild, impetuous deity who also slays a monstrous serpent, and whom these texts count among the ancestors of the imperial line.

The myth

According to the tradition, Susanoo was born when Izanagi purified himself in a river after visiting Yomi, the land of the dead, where he had tried in vain to rescue his wife Izanami. Amaterasu came from his left eye, Tsukuyomi from his right eye, and Susanoo from his nose. Izanagi put him in charge of the seas, but the young god, longing for his mother, wept and howled until his beard grew long and the mountains and rivers dried up, and he was expelled. Some versions send him to the underworld, Ne no Kuni.

Before leaving, Susanoo went up to heaven to say farewell to Amaterasu. She suspected his motives, and the two agreed on a contest in which each chewed and spat out objects to create gods. Amaterasu made three goddesses from her brother's sword, and Susanoo made five gods from her beads. He declared himself the winner and, in his rage, ruined his sister's rice fields, defiled the sacred weaving hall and hurled a flayed heavenly horse at the loom, killing one of the weaving maidens. Amaterasu hid in the Ama-no-Iwato cave and the world fell into darkness until the gods coaxed her out. Susanoo was cast out of heaven.

In Izumo, the god met an old man whose seven daughters had been devoured by Yamata-no-Orochi, a serpent with eight heads and eight tails. The eighth daughter, Kushinada-hime, had survived. Susanoo turned her into a comb to hide her in his hair, set up eight gates with sake, and when the serpent drank until it fell asleep, he cut it apart with his sword totsuka. In its fourth tail he found the sword Kusanagi, which he gave to his sister as a gift. Some sources make him the father or ancestor of Ōkuninushi, lord of the land.

Worship and religion

In Shinto, Susanoo was especially linked to Izumo, the region where many of his deeds are set. After Buddhism arrived in Japan, the Gion cult merged him with deities of pestilence and disease, a change that reflects how his figure evolved over the centuries. The sources do not describe specific festivals or rites.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (712) and the Nihon Shoki (720) record his stories, and the gazetteer of Izumo Province, commissioned by the imperial court, contains legends about him and his children.

Trivia

Born from the nose. According to the Kojiki, Susanoo came into being when Izanagi washed his nose after returning from the land of the dead.
The sword in the tail. The sword Kusanagi was found in the fourth tail of the serpent Yamata-no-Orochi, and Susanoo gave it to Amaterasu.
