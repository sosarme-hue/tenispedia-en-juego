heavenly abode of the gods in Japanese mythology

Takamagahara

Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven, is the abode of the heavenly gods (amatsukami) in Japanese mythology, from which they descended to the Earth. It appears in early texts such as the Kojiki and has long been debated as to its location.

The myth

In the Shinto account of creation, the light and pure elements rose to form heaven, while the heavy and turbid ones settled as the earth. Heaven became the home of the amatsukami, and the earth that of the kunitsukami.

According to the Kojiki, Takamagahara is where the gods were born. The islands came into being when the gods, standing on high, stirred the waters with a spear and then descended onto them. When the sun goddess Amaterasu was born, she was entrusted with ruling this High Plain.

In the stories of Susanō, the realm appears as a world much like the human one, with a heavenly river, a rock house, rice paddies and sacred looms. Later, Amaterasu sent her grandson Ninigi down to rule the Central Land of Reed Plains, and the imperial line traces its descent from him.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki is the main source describing it as the birthplace of the gods. The Nihon Shoki mentions it only briefly, and it also appears in the Hitachi Fudoki and the Kogo Shūi.
