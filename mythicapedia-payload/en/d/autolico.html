Autolycus

Autolycus is a hero of Greek mythology, a son of Hermes and a famous thief who could transform whatever he stole so that its owners could not recognise it. He matters chiefly as the maternal grandfather of Odysseus, whom he named.

The myth

Most accounts make his mother Chione, also called Philonis or Telauge, who was joined by Hermes. Ovid tells his conception this way, while Pausanias claims his real father was Daedalion. Authors also disagree about his wife, who was Amphithea, Mestra or Neaera depending on the source, and about his children.

Among his children, Anticlea stands out: she married Laertes and became the mother of Odysseus in the Homeric version. Hesiod instead names Polymede as the mother of Jason. Of his other sons, only Aesimus, the father of Sinon, is named.

Autolycus inherited from Hermes the gift of altering what he stole. He often rustled Sisyphus's herd without being caught, until Sisyphus marked the hooves of his cattle and exposed him. In revenge, Sisyphus lay with Anticlea, and some writers call Odysseus Sisyphid for his cunning.

Autolycus also appears in the cycle of Heracles, whom he is said to have taught wrestling. Later he stole mares from Eurytus and sold them to the hero. When Iphitus was accused of the theft, Heracles killed him by throwing him from a tower, and for that death he served three years of penance.

When Odysseus was born, his nurse Eurycleia asked Autolycus to name the child. He chose Odysseus, after the hatred (odyssamenos) he had met while travelling the land.

Legacy

Plato mentions him in the Republic, where Socrates uses irony to discuss with Polemarchus what it means to be just. Thomas De Quincey recalls him in On Murder Considered as One of the Fine Arts (1827). The lunar crater Autolycus, by contrast, honours a Greek astronomer of the same name, not this figure.

Ancient sources: Homer's Odyssey makes him Odysseus's grandfather, Hesiod names Polymede, and Ovid narrates his conception; Pausanias and Plato also mention him.

Trivia

A name with a double meaning. The name Odysseus is linked to a Greek verb meaning to be hated, which Autolycus himself explains when he chooses it.
A crater with another namesake. The lunar crater Autolycus is named after a Greek astronomer and mathematician, not the mythical thief.
