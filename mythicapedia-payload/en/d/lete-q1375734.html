Greek goddess of oblivion

Lethe

Lethe is the personification of forgetfulness and oblivion in Greek mythology. Hesiod makes her a daughter of Eris, Strife, and her name was also given to the river of oblivion in the Underworld.

The myth

According to Hesiod's Theogony, Lethe is the daughter of Eris, with no father named, and belongs to the long line of Strife's children alongside figures such as Ponos or Algos. Like her siblings, she is more an abstraction than a character with her own story, standing for one of the harms thought to spring from discord.

Later literature linked her with Death (Thanatos) and Sleep (Hypnos). An Orphic hymn calls Sleep a "true brother" to Death and Oblivion. In Hellenistic times she became the counterpart of Mneme, or Memory, and a passage in the Greek Anthology presents the two as the good and bad sides of remembering.

Worship and religion

At the oracle of Trophonios in Livadeia, according to Pausanias, a visitor first drank from the fountain of Lethe to clear the mind of worries, then from the fountain of Mnemosyne to recall what the oracle revealed.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the river Lethe of the Underworld, the river of forgetting, which has endured as a lasting image of oblivion in Western literature.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Theogony; a fragment of Sophocles; the Orphic Hymns; the Greek Anthology; Pausanias, Description of Greece.

Trivia

A river of forgetting. In the Underworld, souls drank from the waters of Lethe to forget their earlier lives before being reborn.
Forgetting and memory. At Trophonios, Lethe and Mnemosyne were consulted as a pair, like complementary fountains.
