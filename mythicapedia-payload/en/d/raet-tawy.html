Egyptian solar goddess, wife of Montu

Raet-Tawy

Raet-Tawy, also spelled Raet or Rattaui, is an ancient Egyptian solar goddess, the female counterpart of Ra. Her longer name means "Raet of the Two Lands," referring to Upper and Lower Egypt, and she is best known as the wife of the god Montu.

The myth

Her name is simply the feminine form of Ra's, which suggests she was not thought of as an independent deity but as a female reflection of the sun god. She is attested from the Fifth Dynasty onward, and she received titles such as "lady of heaven, mistress of the gods" that mirror those of Ra. Some traditions made her Ra's companion from the beginning, yet she never reached the prominence of Hathor, who was also regarded as Ra's wife or, in other myths, his daughter.

Images of her are rare. She appears as a woman with the cow horns of Hathor holding a sun disk, with a uraeus on her brow and sometimes two feathers.

Worship and religion

As Montu's wife she formed a triad with him and Harpocrates at Medamud, and at Karnak too. At Armant she was often shown beside Iunit, another wife of Montu. Her festival fell in the fourth month of the harvest season (Shemu), and her main cult centers were Medamud, El-Tod and Thebes. Fragments survive of a Roman-period demotic manual containing hymns to her.
