first age of humankind in Greek mythology

Golden Age

The Golden Age is the first of the ages of humankind in Greek mythology, a period of peace, abundance and harmony in which people lived without labour or suffering. The idea, first recorded by Hesiod, became one of the most enduring themes in Western literature and thought.

The myth

In Hesiod's Works and Days, the Golden Age is the first of five ages, followed by the Silver, Bronze, Heroic and Iron ages. The men of the golden race lived like gods, free from worry and toil, and the earth gave them fruit in abundance without being tilled. When they died, it was as if they fell into a peaceful sleep, and their spirits lived on as guardians of the living.

In Hesiod's version, the age ended when Prometheus gave humanity fire and the arts. Zeus punished Prometheus and sent Pandora, whose box released all evils. In the Latin tradition, the Golden Age belonged to the reign of Saturn, whom Jupiter overthrew to begin the Silver Age. Ovid reduced the ages to four: gold, silver, bronze and iron.

According to the tradition, justice left the earth during the Bronze Age. The goddess Astraea fled to the heavens and appears as the constellation Virgo, holding the scales of justice. Virgil, in his fourth Eclogue, announced the return of a new golden age of peace and justice.

Legacy

Ovid retold the myth in his Metamorphoses, and Virgil evoked it in the Georgics. European pastoral literature placed its shepherds and nymphs in Arcadia, a region of Greece linked to the Golden Age and to the god Pan. In the Renaissance, painters took up the theme, often using the laurel as its symbol. Jean-Jacques Rousseau drew on the idea of a virtuous state of nature to criticise civilisation. Today the phrase is the title of a novel by Gore Vidal and of a film by Luis Buñuel.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Works and Days (lines 109 ff.); Plato, Cratylus; Ovid, Metamorphoses; Virgil, Georgics and Eclogue IV.

Trivia

Gold is not metal. In the Cratylus, Plato clarifies that Hesiod did not mean the people were literally made of gold, but that they were good and noble.
A constellation of justice. According to tradition, the goddess Astraea fled to the sky when men became violent and greedy, and became the constellation Virgo.
