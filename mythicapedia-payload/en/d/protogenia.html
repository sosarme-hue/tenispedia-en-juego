heroine of Greek myth, daughter of Deucalion

Protogeneia

Protogeneia is a figure of Greek myth, daughter of Deucalion and Pyrrha, linked to Zeus and mother of Aetlius and Opus. Her lineage stands at the origin of the genealogies of Locris, Elis and Aetolia.

The myth

According to tradition, Protogeneia was the daughter of Deucalion and Pyrrha, although one scholium makes her a daughter of Prometheus. Zeus seduced her, as he had earlier seduced her mother, and from that union came Aetlius, later king of Elis. Other versions say she bore Opus, the eponymous hero, also fathered by Zeus. One source presents her as a sister of Pandora and gives her four sons by Zeus: Aetelius, Doros, Melera and Pandoros.

Pindar tells a different story. An unnamed daughter of Opus lived in the land of the Epeans. Zeus carried her off in Opuntian territory, took her to Mount Maenalus and, after lying with her, handed her to Locrus, who was childless. Locrus adopted the son born of that union, who was also called Opus. Later texts call her Cambyse or Cabia, and commentators identify her with Protogeneia.

Ancient sources: Pindar mentions her in his praise of the Locrians, and a scholium makes her a daughter of Prometheus and sister of Pandora.

Trivia

Another name. In later texts, the unnamed daughter of Opus appears under the names Cambyse or Cabia, which commentators identify with Protogeneia.
Sister of Pandora. One source presents her as a sister of Pandora, the woman of the jar in other versions of the myth.
