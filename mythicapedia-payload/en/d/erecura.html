Celtic goddess of the underworld

Erecura

Erecura (also Herecura) is an underworld goddess known only from Latin inscriptions of the Roman period, generally considered Celtic in origin. She matters chiefly as the partner of the god Dis Pater, in which role she takes the place of Proserpina.

The myth

No ancient author tells her stories, so her role has to be inferred from inscriptions and other evidence. As an underworld goddess she appears most often beside Dis Pater, forming an infernal couple. In the tradition these texts reflect, she occupies the place that Proserpina, the queen of the dead abducted by Pluto, holds in classical myth, and she is repeatedly identified with that figure.

Her character also links her to the earth and its fertility. On two inscriptions from Tibilis in Numidia she is equated with Terra Mater, the Earth Mother, and some scholars suggest she may originally have been an earth goddess later drawn into the underworld. In her iconography she is shown seated, with a basket or tray of fruit in her lap, like the Matronae.

Worship and religion

Her cult is attested mainly in Upper Germany, with about 21 inscriptions, and also in Italy, the Danubian and Alpine provinces, and by isolated finds in Gaul, Dacia, Britain and North Africa. Votive altars honour her together with Dis Pater at places such as Aquileia, Carnuntum and Sulzbach, and she was also worshipped alone, as at Perusia, where a married couple restored her shrine in fulfilment of a vow. Several Latin curse tablets from the 2nd to 4th centuries AD call on her to hand the cursed over to the powers below.

Ancient sources: She is known only from Latin inscriptions of the Roman period; no ancient author mentions her.

Trivia

A Roman tomb. The Tomb of Vibia, a Roman hypogeum of the second half of the 4th century on the Appian Way, shows her in painted panels beside Dis Pater, presiding over the tribunal of the dead.
A name with many spellings. The inscriptions record many different spellings, such as Eracura, Aercura or Aeracura, and the origin of her name has still not been securely established.
