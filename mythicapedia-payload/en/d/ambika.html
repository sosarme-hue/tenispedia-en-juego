Hindu goddess, epithet of Parvati and Durga

Ambikā

Ambikā is a Hindu goddess generally regarded as an epithet of Mahadevi, Parvati or Durga. She appears in Vedic texts and later accounts, where she is presented as the sister or the wife of Rudra-Śiva depending on the text, and her figure shows how a single divinity can take on different roles.

The myth

According to the Devi Mahatmya, after Durga slew the asura Mahishasura, the gods set out on a pilgrimage to the Himalayas and sang a hymn in honour of the supreme goddess. Parvati, who had come to the source of the Ganges to bathe, heard the chant and asked to whom it was addressed. Before the gods could answer, she shed her outer bodily form and revealed her true, auspicious form, which has since been called Ambikā.

In the Vedic tradition, Ambikā appears as the sister of Rudra (Śiva). The Yajurveda invokes Rudra "together with his sister Ambikā", and the Shatapatha Brahmana states explicitly that Ambikā is the name of that sister. Other ritual texts, such as the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa, identify her again in the same context.

However, in the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka and the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad of the Atharvaveda, Rudra is called the "husband of Ambikā" (Ambikāpati). The commentator Sāyaṇa then identifies Rudra's consort with Parvati, mother of the whole universe. Thus Ambikā becomes a wife rather than a sister, depending on the text, a shift that shows how her figure was reinterpreted.

Ancient sources: The Yajurveda, the Shatapatha Brahmana, the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa, the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka and the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad mention her; the Devi Mahatmya narrates her origin.

Trivia

Sister or wife. In the Yajurveda she appears as Rudra's sister, while the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka calls her Rudra's wife.
A shared name. The Lakshmi Tantra, a sectarian text from the 12th century CE, also gives Lakshmi the name or epithet Ambikā.
