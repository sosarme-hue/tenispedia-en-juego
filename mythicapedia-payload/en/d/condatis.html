Celtic god of river confluences

Condatis

Condatis was a Celtic god worshipped in northern Roman Britain, linked to the meeting of rivers and, in Roman eyes, to healing. His name comes from the Celtic word condate, 'confluence', which appears in many place-names.

The myth

Very little narrative survives about Condatis. Almost everything we know comes from a handful of Latin votive altars, so his mythology must be reconstructed largely from his name and from where he is found.

His name is usually understood as 'the one of the confluence' or 'watersmeet', which would make him a god of joining rivers. Xavier Delamarre proposed another reading, 'the reunited one' or 'the meeting', perhaps alluding to myths that can no longer be recovered.

Worship and religion

Condatis is attested only in Roman Britain, through five votive altars found in the north, four from the Tees basin and one from Cramond. On all of them he is identified with Mars through the interpretatio romana, so his native name survives only as a byname of the Roman god. Two altars were found where two watercourses meet, supporting his link with rivers and healing waters.

Legacy

His name survives in the Celtic word condate, very common in Gaulish place-names. Condate gave its name to Rennes, and the same element appears in Condom, Candes and other places across Europe.

Ancient sources: No literary texts from antiquity mention him; his only documentation is Latin votive inscriptions from Roman Britain.

Trivia

A god with almost no stories. Condatis is known almost only from small votive altars, with no surviving mythic narratives.
A name on the map. The element condate appears in place-names such as Condom and Rennes, formerly Condate.
