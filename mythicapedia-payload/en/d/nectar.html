drink of the Greek gods

Nectar

In Greek mythology, nectar is the drink of the gods of Olympus, a red wine that was mixed with water before drinking. It matters because its relationship with ambrosia shows how the Greeks imagined divine food and drink.

The myth

According to the earliest poets, such as Homer and Ovid, nectar was the wine of the gods, red in colour, served to them at their feasts by Hebe or Ganymede. Like mortal wine, it was mixed with water before being drunk. In the Odyssey, Odysseus brings wine to the cave of Polyphemus, and the Cyclops calls it "cream of nectar", a way of praising its quality.

The texts do not always keep nectar and ambrosia apart. Usually nectar was the drink and ambrosia the food of the gods, but in Alcman nectar appears as food, and in Sappho and Anaxandrides ambrosia is the drink. This confusion suggests that, originally, the two words may have referred to the same thing.

Later writers gave nectar another use: a fragrant balm that slowed the decay of bodies. In the Iliad, Thetis anoints the body of Patroclus with ambrosia and nectar so that it will not rot. According to a modern interpretation, both words may reflect an earlier substance, honey, seen as dew fallen from the sky, a manna that served as both food and drink.

Indian tradition offers a parallel in amrita, mentioned in the Upanishads and the Puranas, whose name means something like "deathless".

Ancient sources: Homer (Iliad and Odyssey), Ovid, Alcman, Sappho and Anaxandrides mention nectar or ambrosia; in India, the Upanishads and the Puranas speak of amrita.
