Etruscan goddess of love, springtime and possibly the underworld

Alpanu

Alpanu is an Etruscan goddess linked to love, friendship and springtime, and possibly also to the underworld. She appears mainly on bronze mirrors showing scenes of love.

The myth

Alpanu, also written Alpnu, Alpan or Alpena, and shortened to Alp, appears in abbreviated form on the Piacenza Liver and on seven mirrors, mostly in love scenes. On one of these mirrors, Alpan is shown as male. She usually wears jewellery, a loose cloak and sandals, and holds leaves and flowers.

She is often shown beside the love goddess Turan and beside embracing couples, and once in an embrace with the deities Thanr and Achvizr. According to the tradition, she makes plant life bloom after Adonis in springtime. Her name is read as "willing, gladness", and she is described as the personification of generosity and gladness. She is considered a member of the Lasas. Some scholars believe Alpanu is a separate goddess from Alpena, the goddess of springtime, having split off from her as "an inferior form of Venus".

Ancient sources: Her name appears on the Piacenza Liver and on seven Etruscan bronze mirrors.

Trivia

A male Alpan. On one of the Etruscan mirrors, Alpan is depicted as a man, which differs from most of her images.
