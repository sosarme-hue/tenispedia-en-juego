hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo

Linus

Linus is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo and Psamathe, daughter of King Crotopus of Argos. His tragic death gave rise to a funeral song and a ritual festival in the city.

The myth

According to the tradition, Psamathe gave birth to Linus and, fearing her father, abandoned him. Shepherds raised him, but as an adult he was torn apart by the shepherds' own dogs.

When Psamathe learned of the boy's death, she confessed her secret. Crotopus refused to believe the father was a god and condemned her to death. Apollo punished Argos with a plague that killed children, and the oracle urged the Argives to appease Psamathe and Linus. The plague did not end until Crotopus left the city.

Worship and religion

To appease the god's anger, the matrons and virgins of Argos offered sacrifices and sang funeral dirges called linoi. The month when the rite was held was called arneios, and the festival itself arnis, because Linus had grown up among lambs.

Trivia

Poene, punishment personified. An alternate version says Apollo sent Poene, the personification of punishment, who stole children from their mothers until Coroebus killed her.
A name tied to lambs. The name of the festival, arnis, is linked to the lambs among which Linus was said to have grown up.
