daughter of Macareus and lover of Apollo in Greek myth

Amphissa

Amphissa, also called Issa, is a figure of Greek myth, a daughter of Macareus and one of Apollo's lovers. She gave her name to the city of Amphissa in Ozolian Locris, which ties her story to a real place in ancient Greece.

The myth

According to tradition, Amphissa was a daughter of Macareus, son of Aeolus. Some assume she was born from the incestuous union of Macareus and his sister Canace. Ovid, however, speaks of a male child who was exposed by Aeolus and torn apart by wild beasts, so her parentage remains unclear.

Apollo courted her in the disguise of a shepherd, a ruse that, according to ancient storytellers, other gods also used to seduce women and nymphs. Amphissa is likely the same figure as the Issa mentioned by Ovid. Tradition holds that she gave her name to the city of Amphissa, where her memory was kept alive by a splendid monument.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions her as a lover of Apollo in the story woven by Arachne during her contest with Athena, and Hyginus mentions Euboea, daughter of Macareus, who may be an alternative version of her story.

Trivia

A tapestry of disguises. Ovid tells that Arachne wove into her tapestry Apollo's disguise as a shepherd, alongside other disguises used by Zeus, Poseidon and Dionysus to seduce mortal women and nymphs.
A city bearing her name. The city of Amphissa in Ozolian Locris takes its name from this figure, and a monument to her memory was kept there.
