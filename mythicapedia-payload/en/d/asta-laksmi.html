group of eight Hindu goddesses of wealth

Ashta Lakshmi

The Ashta Lakshmi (also Ashtalakshmi) are a group of eight Hindu goddesses, manifestations of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, each presiding over one source of wealth. In temples they are always depicted and worshipped together as an octet.

The myth

According to Hindu tradition, Gaja Lakshmi recovered the wealth that Indra, king of the devas, had lost during the Samudra Manthana, the churning of the ocean. Vasudha Narayanan reads her name as "the one worshipped by elephants", and in iconography she is shown seated between two elephants that bathe her with water pots.

The group has no single story, but a set of attributes. Adi Lakshmi, the primeval form, is said to be the daughter of the sage Bhrigu and to guide the seeker towards the atman. Dhana Lakshmi gives money and gold, Dhanya Lakshmi grain, and Santana Lakshmi children. Vidya Lakshmi grants knowledge of the arts and religion, Vira Lakshmi courage in battle, and Jaya Lakshmi victory, including victory over obstacles. Vira and Jaya Lakshmi are linked with Durga.

Worship and religion

Worship is especially popular today in South India, among Sri Vaishnava and other Hindu communities. Its spread is tied to the Ashtalakshmi Stotra, a hymn published around the 1970s by the theologian UV Srinivasa Varadachariar. The Ashtalakshmi Temple in Besant Nagar, Chennai, built in 1974, is the first shrine devoted exclusively to the group: it has eight small shrines and a ninth for Vishnu and Lakshmi together. Other temples exist in Hyderabad, Sydney, Houston and California. In Kerala, it is believed that Maha Lakshmi appears as the eight forms on eight days of the year.

Legacy

Prayer books, pamphlets and a booming audiocassette market, sold outside temples in South India, have popularised the octet. Their images also appear on traditional silver household objects and decorative jars.

Ancient sources: The Narayana Pancharatra mentions Gaja Lakshmi as a giver of animal wealth and royal splendour, and the Skanda Purana is indirectly implied in the iconography of Santana Lakshmi.

Trivia

A temple with nine shrines. The Chennai temple arranges its eight shrines clockwise and dedicates the ninth to Vishnu and Lakshmi together, unlike the traditional layout of separate shrines.
Statues that hold up the roof. In Madurai, a hall at the entrance to the Meenakshi Temple is dedicated to the Ashta Lakshmi, and their statues support the roof on either side.
