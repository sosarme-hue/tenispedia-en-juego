primordial mother goddess of South Indian Hinduism

Ammavaru

Ammavaru is an ancient mother goddess worshipped in Hinduism in South India, especially in Karnataka. She is presented as a primordial deity who existed before the beginning of time and is linked to the energy that gives rise to the world.

The myth

According to one version of Hindu cosmology, Ammavaru is a primordial deity who existed before time began and laid the cosmic egg from which Prajapati emerged. Over time she came to be associated with Brahma and later with the Trimurti, the triad of Brahma as creator, Vishnu as preserver and Shiva as destroyer.

The name means "mother" in Kannada, and tradition regards her as a mother goddess equivalent to Prakriti, the matter and energy from which the world arises.

Worship and religion

Her most notable shrine is the Dharmasthala Temple in Dharmasthala, Dakshina Kannada, Karnataka, where she is revered alongside a form of Shiva and the Jain tirthankara Chandraprabha. Every year, women of South India who worship her perform a ritual in her honour. A metal pot filled with rice represents her body and is dressed in a traditional sari, with a painted coconut as its head and other objects shaping the eyes, ears and nose.
