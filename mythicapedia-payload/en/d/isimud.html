two-faced Mesopotamian messenger god

Isimud

Isimud is a messenger god of ancient Mesopotamia, known for having two faces that look in opposite directions. He served as the sukkal, or attendant and messenger, of the god Enki.

The myth

In the Enki and Ninhursag myth, Isimud advises his master, who wants to lie with the goddess's beautiful daughters, to start with Ninsar, continue with Ninkurra and finish with Uttu, and takes him to see each of them by boat.

In Inanna and Enki he tells Enki that he handed the me to the goddess while drunk, and then passes on Enki's order that Inanna return them. He also appears in the Enuma Elish and the Atrahasis, where he must warn the protagonist of the fate awaiting the world.

Worship and religion

He is attested from the Early Dynastic period to the Late Assyrian period, although no temples dedicated to him are known. He received offerings in building rituals, had a cella in the Bit Res complex in Uruk, and appears in the akitu festival alongside Nuska and Papsukkal. His worship does not survive today.

Legacy

His two-faced figure has been compared to the Roman god Janus.

Ancient sources: Cuneiform texts such as the Myth of Enki and Ninhursag, Inanna and Enki, the Enuma Elish and the Atrahasis, as well as the Hurrian Song of LAMMA.

Trivia

A sukkal recognized by his face. Most sukkal are identified in art by their staff of office. Isimud is an exception, recognized by his two faces, although he does not always hold the staff.
A god who travelled beyond Mesopotamia. He was incorporated into the Hurrian pantheon and was also worshipped by the Hittites, who offered him meat, bread and libations during festivals.
