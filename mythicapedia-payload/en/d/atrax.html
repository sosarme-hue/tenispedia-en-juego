hero who founded a city in Thessaly

Atrax

Atrax (or Atrace) is, in Greek mythology, the eponymous hero said to have founded the city of Atrax in Pelasgiotis, Thessaly. He matters mainly for his genealogy: he was the son of the river god Peneus and Bura, and the father of several daughters whose stories vary from one author to another.

The myth

According to Stephanus of Byzantium, Atrax founded the city that bore his name, and its female ethnic, Atracia; other writers give the name as Atrago. He was said to be the son of the river god Peneus and Bura, though the same author also mentions a Bura who was a daughter of Ion and Helice, without specifying whether she was Atrax's mother.

The sources disagree about his daughters. Ovid makes him the father of Hippodamia, wife of Pirithous. Antoninus Liberalis names Caenis, the girl Poseidon turned into a man. Pseudo-Plutarch speaks of Damasippe, who was married to Cassandrus, king of Thrace. Damasippe fell in love with her stepson Hebrus, the son Cassandrus had with his first wife Crotonice. When he rejected her, she falsely accused him of seducing her; Cassandrus believed her and tried to kill him, and Hebrus threw himself into the river Rhombus, which was afterwards renamed Hebrus.

Ancient sources: Stephanus of Byzantium, Ovid, Antoninus Liberalis and Pseudo-Plutarch record his figure and genealogy, though with variations among them.

Trivia

Three daughters, three versions. The ancient sources do not agree on the name of Atrax's daughter: Ovid mentions Hippodamia, Antoninus Liberalis Caenis and Pseudo-Plutarch Damasippe.
A river with a new name. In the story of Damasippe and Hebrus, the river Rhombus was renamed Hebrus after the young man threw himself into its waters.
