mythical bird of Persian legend that never touches the ground

Huma

The huma is a legendary bird of Iranian tradition, a recurring motif in Persian art and in Sufi and diwan poetry. It is seen as a symbol of good fortune, and its name lives on in Iran, where it appears in the logo of the national airline.

The myth

According to Iranian legends, the huma flies endlessly high above the earth and never settles on the ground; in some versions it has no legs. Its shadow or touch is considered auspicious, and it was said that whoever it alighted upon would become a king. For this reason, tradition held that the feathers decorating kings' turbans came from this bird.

In other variants the huma resembles the phoenix, burning itself every few hundred years and rising from the ashes, and it is said to combine male and female natures. In Persian literature it acts as messenger and protector of a hero, who may burn one of its healing feathers to summon it back. In Attar of Nishapur's Sufi allegory The Conference of the Birds, it appears as a pupil who refuses to set out on a journey.

Legacy

The huma motif appears in the griffin-like column capitals at Persepolis, which locals call 'homa birds'. It inspired the logo of Iran Air. Herman Melville alludes to it in Moby-Dick with the phrase 'the bird that never alights', and the name inspired the hero Huma Dragonbane in the Dragonlance series. In 2025, the song 'Homay' by Ay Yola was dedicated to its Bashkir version.

Trivia

Shadow of fortune. It was believed that merely catching a glimpse of its shadow would make a person happy for the rest of their life.
A name in the airline. Iran Air's logo depicts the homa, a bird inspired by the statues at Persepolis.
