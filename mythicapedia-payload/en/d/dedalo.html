legendary craftsman and architect of Greek mythology

Daedalus

Daedalus is an architect and craftsman of Greek mythology, traditionally born in Athens, best known for building the Labyrinth of Crete and for the wings he made to escape with his son Icarus. His name became a byword for skilled craftsmanship.

The myth

According to the stories, Daedalus belonged to the Athenian royal house, though his parents vary by author: some make him son of Metion, others of Eupalamus or Palamaon, and his mother appears as Alcippe, Iphinoe, Phrasmede or Merope. He had a son, Icarus, and some accounts add a second, Iapyx.

His nephew Perdix, son of his sister, was a gifted pupil who invented the saw and the compass. Jealous of him, Daedalus threw him from the Acropolis. Athena saved the boy by turning him into a partridge. Daedalus was exiled and took refuge in Crete, in the service of King Minos.

In Crete he built the Labyrinth to imprison the Minotaur, born from Pasiphae's passion for a bull, for which he made a hollow wooden cow. When Minos imprisoned him for helping Theseus, Daedalus made wings of feathers bound with thread and wax. Icarus ignored his father's warnings, flew too close to the sun, the wax melted, and he fell into the sea. Daedalus reached Sicily, under King Cocalus, where he remained after Minos's death.

Worship and religion

At Plataea in Boeotia, the Daedala festival was held, in which an oak was felled and carved into an image dressed as a bride, carried on a cart to the river Asopus. The ritual was explained by a myth about Hera and Zeus. Daedalus is also linked to a temple of Apollo in Sicily, where he hung his wings as an offering.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the term Daedalic, applied to an early style of Greek sculpture. Icarus and Daedalus's flight recur in literature and art, and the Cretan Labyrinth has become a universal symbol of complexity.

Ancient sources: He appears on Linear B tablets from Knossos, in Homer's Iliad and Odyssey references, in Ovid's Metamorphoses, in Pliny's Natural History and in Pausanias's Description of Greece.

Trivia

A name that became an adjective. Daedalus gave his name to Daedalic sculpture, an early style of Greek sculpture.
A sea named after him. The sea where Icarus fell was called the Icarian Sea.
