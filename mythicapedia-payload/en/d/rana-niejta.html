Sami goddess of spring and fertility

Rana Niejta

Rana Niejta is a goddess in Sami mythology associated with spring and fertility. Her name is usually understood as «the green» or «the green, fertile fields», and it can be freely rendered as «daughter of the earth».

The myth

According to Sami tradition, she turned the southern slopes of the mountains green so that hungry reindeer would have food.

Her role is distinguished from that of two other goddesses: Sala Niejta, «daughter of the Sun», who could end snow and cold, and Saivo Niejta, «daughter of the underworld». Sami poems often mentioned them together, and outsiders sometimes confused them, although sources from 1700 show that they were three separate goddesses. Rana Niejta thus stands for the renewal of spring, making trees and herbs grow and flourish again every year.

Legacy

Some scholars think her name gave rise to that of the Norwegian Rana Municipality. In 1971 a bronze statue of the goddess was raised in the park in Mo i Rana, made by the artist Arne Durban and financed by the bank Den Norske Bank for its 25th anniversary. In 2003, a similar statue was moved to Nordlandsbanken after the two banks merged.

Trivia
