giant of Norse mythology, father of Þjazi

Olvaldi

Olvaldi is a giant (jötunn) in Norse mythology, father of Þjazi, Gangr and Iði and grandfather of Skaði. He is remembered mainly for his great wealth in gold and for the unusual way his sons shared his inheritance after his death.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson in the Prose Edda, within the Skáldskaparmál, Olvaldi was very rich in gold. When he died, his three sons divided the inheritance in an unusual way: they took turns taking a mouthful of gold, so that each received the same share.

This episode gave rise to the Norse expression "the speech of Þjazi, Gangr and Iði", used as a metaphor for gold. The Hárbarðsljóð, a poem of the Poetic Edda, names Olvaldi (here as Alvadi) as the father of the giant Þjazi. According to philologist Rudolf Simek, the story is probably the remnant of an older myth.

Legacy

One of Saturn's moons is named after him, Alvaldi.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson records him in the Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál), and the Hárbarðsljóð of the Poetic Edda mentions him as the father of Þjazi.

Trivia

A metaphor for gold. The expression "the speech of Þjazi, Gangr and Iði" served in Old Norse as a way of referring to gold, based on the sharing of Olvaldi's inheritance.
A moon of Saturn. One of Saturn's moons is named Alvaldi, after this giant.
