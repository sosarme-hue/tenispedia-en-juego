Sumerian goddess of the earth

Ki

Ki is the Sumerian goddess of the earth, one of the oldest figures in the cosmology of Mesopotamia. Her name means "earth" in Sumerian, and in Akkadian and Babylonian tradition she developed into the goddess Antu, consort of Anu.

The myth

According to Sumerian stories, in the beginning heaven and earth were joined, forming a mountain that rose from the primordial ocean, Nammu, Ki's mother. An, the sky, was her partner, and from their union the Anunnaki were born. Among them the most prominent was Enlil, god of the air, who at his birth separated heaven from earth: An carried off the heavens, while Ki, together with Enlil, kept the earth. Some versions make Ki a sister of An and a daughter of Anshar and Kishar, primordial personifications of sky and earth; others make her a daughter of Nammu.

Tradition also tells that Ki united with her son Enlil, and from this union the plants and animals of the earth came into being. Ki appears in the stories of Enki and Ninmah, the Myth of Enki and Ninhursag, the tale of Ziusudra, and the Akkadian epic poems Atrahasis and Enuma Elish. Samuel Noah Kramer proposed that Ki was the same figure as the mother goddess Ninhursag, although this is not certain.

Worship and religion

There is no evidence of a cult devoted to Ki, and some scholars doubt she was regarded as a deity at all, since her name appears only in a few Sumerian creation texts.

Ancient sources: Sumerian creation texts, Enki and Ninmah, the Myth of Enki and Ninhursag, Ziusudra, and the Akkadian poems Atrahasis and Enuma Elish.

Trivia

A sign with several readings. The cuneiform sign for Ki means "earth", but it was also read as GUNNI, "hearth", or KISLAH, "threshing floor". It shows how Mesopotamian writing combined meanings and sound values.
