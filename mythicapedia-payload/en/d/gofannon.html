Welsh smithing god

Gofannon

Gofannon is the smithing god of Welsh tradition and a great metalworker, son of the goddess Dôn. His figure, paralleled by the Irish Goibniu, shows how the smith's craft was linked with the sacred among the Celtic peoples.

The myth

In medieval Welsh stories, Gofannon appears as a master metalworker and the son of Dôn. One of his most dramatic episodes is the death of his nephew Dylan Ail Don, whom he killed without knowing who he was.

In the tale of Culhwch and Olwen, the hero Culhwch must complete a series of tasks to win Olwen's hand, and one of them is to get Gofannon to sharpen the plough of his brother Amaethon. The story thus shows him as a craftsman serving others rather than as a central character.

In Ireland, his apparent counterpart, Goibniu, has similar roles: besides being a smith, he is an architect and builder, and he is said to have brewed an ale that granted immortality.

Ancient sources: Medieval Welsh tales, such as Culhwch and Olwen, where he appears as a smith and uncle of Dylan Ail Don.

Trivia

A link to sacred fire. His name is related to the Old Irish word gobae, 'smith', and to the Lithuanian gabija, 'sacred home fire', suggesting an ancient link between the craft and the sacred.
An ale of immortality. His Irish counterpart, Goibniu, brewed an ale that granted immortality, and he was also an architect and builder.
