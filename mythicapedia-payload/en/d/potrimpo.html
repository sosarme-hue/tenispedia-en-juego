Prussian god of waters, earth and grain

Potrimpo

Potrimpo was, according to late sources, a god of waters, earth, grain and crops in the pagan religion of the Old Prussians, one of their three main gods.

The myth

Most of what is known about Potrimpo comes from unreliable 16th-century sources, and their accounts do not agree.

According to Simon Grunau, he formed a pagan trinity with the thunder god Perkūnas and the death god Peckols, and he was shown as a cheerful young man wearing a wreath of grain ears. The Sudovian Book described him as god of running water and gave the seas to Autrimpo, while the Constitutiones Synodales compared the pair to Pollux, Castor and Neptune. Later authors merged the two into a single deity of earth and water.

Worship and religion

Jan Sandecki Malecki claimed that the Prussians prayed to him, poured hot wax into water and foretold the future from the shapes it formed. Maciej Stryjkowski mentions a copper idol shaped like a snake in the temple of Romuva, and Grunau says that snakes regarded as his were given milk. This cult did not survive.

Ancient sources: The Sudovian Book (1520–1530), the Constitutiones Synodales (1530), and the chronicles of Simon Grunau and Maciej Stryjkowski.

Trivia

A name with a stomp. Kazimieras Būga linked the name to the root trimp-, related to the Lithuanian verb trempti, meaning to trample.
A spring rite. Some researchers suggest the name is linked to a stomping ritual meant to scare off evil spirits and wake the earth in spring.
