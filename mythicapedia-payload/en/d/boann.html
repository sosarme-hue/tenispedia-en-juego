Irish goddess of the River Boyne

Boann

Boann is the Irish goddess of the River Boyne (Bóinn), one of the most important rivers in the historic province of Meath. Her name is usually interpreted as "white cow", and her figure links water, fertility and the birth of Aengus.

The myth

According to medieval Irish texts, Boann belonged to the Tuatha Dé Danann and was married to Elcmar, although other versions name Nechtan as her husband. Her lover, the Dagda, made her pregnant, and to hide it he made the sun stand still for a whole day so that Elcmar would not notice the passing of time. Aengus was born.

Another account, preserved in the Dindsenchas, tells how Boann approached the forbidden Well of Segais and walked around it counterclockwise. The waters burst up violently, rushed to the sea and formed the River Boyne. Boann was swept away by the current and lost an arm, a leg and an eye, and finally her life.

In the Táin Bó Fraích she appears as the maternal aunt and protector of the mortal youth Fráech, to whom she gives a magnificent otherworldly outfit: fifty cloaks, fifty spears, dark horses and silver-chained hounds.

Legacy

Some modern writers, especially within Neopaganism, identify her with Brigid or consider her Brigid's mother, although no Celtic source says so. Others have suggested that the birth of Aengus reflects the midwinter sunlight at Newgrange, when the sunbeam enters the inner chamber.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Dindsenchas and the Táin Bó Fraích.

Trivia

A cow in the name. Ptolemy's 2nd-century Geography records the river's ancient name as Bouvinda, which may derive from a Proto-Celtic root meaning "white cow".
A lapdog swept out to sea. In a variant of the story, her lapdog Dabilla was swept out to sea, and its two halves became the rocks known as Cnoc Dabilla.
