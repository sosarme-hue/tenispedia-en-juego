character of the winter carnival of La Vijanera (Silió, Cantabria)

Zarramaco

The zarramaco is one of the characters of La Vijanera, the winter carnival of Silió in Cantabria, where it is seen as a loud, powerful being that drives away evil spirits. It is a figure of Cantabrian folklore tied to the early-January festive cycle.

The myth

The zarramaco has no fixed myth like those of the great ancient traditions; its role is defined by a ritual. Its face is blackened and six to ten cowbells, known as campanos or zumbas, are strapped to its body, which can weigh over forty-five kilos depending on the wearer's stamina.

Dressed this way, it leaps and shakes like a man possessed, ringing the bells to drive away evil spirits. It takes part in the procession that captures the Bear and later, at the boundary line with the neighbouring village, young men ask it whether it wants peace or war. After the coplas and the birth of the Preñá, the zarramacos are the ones who kill the Bear and walk through the village until nightfall.

Worship and religion

Its function is ritual: the din of the bells is meant to drive away malevolent spirits. Today it only appears in Silió, the only Vijanera that has been revived, and in the past the last zarramaco of the night gained the village's recognition.

Trivia

Bells with their own voice. Each bell has a distinctive sound, so the racket, though chaotic, combines a wide range of tones.
Peace or war. At the boundary line, the neighbouring young men asked whether they wanted peace or war. With peace, the money collected was spent in the taverns; with war, old grudges were settled.
