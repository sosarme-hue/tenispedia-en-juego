Trojan princess and queen of Thrace

Ilione

Ilione, also called Iliona, was a Trojan princess, daughter of Priam and Hecuba, who became queen of Thrace. She matters mainly for the role she played in the life of her younger brother, Polydorus.

The myth

According to the myth, Ilione married Polymestor, king of the Thracians, and had a son, Deipylus. Her parents entrusted the infant Polydorus to the couple to keep him safe from the war, and she raised him as her own son.

After the fall of Troy, the Greeks offered Polymestor gold and the hand of Electra in exchange for Polydorus's death. He agreed, but mistakenly killed his own son Deipylus, believing he was killing Polydorus. Polydorus, who had consulted the oracle of Apollo about his parents, returned and asked Ilione for an explanation; she told him the truth, and he blinded and killed Polymestor. Tradition holds that Ilione, grieving her parents' deaths, eventually took her own life.

Legacy

Virgil mentions her in the Aeneid, and the Roman playwrights Pacuvius and Accius wrote tragedies about her story.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid and Hyginus's Fabulae mention her.

Trivia

A crossed upbringing. Ilione raised her brother as if he were her son and her own son as if he were her brother. As a result, Deipylus and Polydorus grew up as brothers.
A fatal mistake. Polymestor killed his own son, believing he was killing Polydorus, the son of Priam he had been asked to hand over to the Greeks.
