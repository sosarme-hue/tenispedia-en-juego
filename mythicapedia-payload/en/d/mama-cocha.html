Inca goddess of water and protector of fishermen

Mama Cocha

Mama Cocha is the Inca goddess of all waters, both the sea and its tides and the lakes, rivers and springs. She guards fishermen and sailors and was one of the Four Elemental Mothers of the Andean pantheon. Her Quechua name, Mama Qucha, can be rendered as “mother sea” or “mother lake.”

The myth

According to the most widespread traditions, Mama Cocha was the wife of Viracocha, the creator god, and the mother of Inti, the sun god, and Mama Quilla, the moon goddess. She dwelt in the Uku Pacha, the world below, alongside gods such as Supay, lord of death, and Pachacamac, who ruled earthquakes and tidal waves.

Other accounts tell a different story. Here she is the daughter of the Sun and the Moon and the sister of the Inca. She was sent down from the Hanan Pacha, the world above, with her brother to teach people to live and work in peace and love. Recognizing her as their protective mother, the people built houses, roads, temples and fortresses, and the tilled earth soon bore fruit. The sources do not entirely agree on where she lived.

Worship and religion

She was especially revered along the coast of what are now Peru, Ecuador, southern Colombia and northern Chile, where fishing was vital to life. People asked her to calm rough waters, as in tidal waves or storms, and to grant good catches. She was also honored in settlements near lakes and lagoons. Colonial chronicles indicate that the sea, a source of food, trade and valuable offerings, was sacred to coastal peoples and that this worship continued under the Tahuantinsuyo.

Legacy

In Quechua, mama means “mother,” and the word appears in the names of other Andean deities, such as Pachamama and Mama Quilla.

Ancient sources: The colonial chroniclers Polo de Ondegardo, Guamán Poma de Ayala and Inca Garcilaso de la Vega document her after the conquest, so their accounts come from writings produced after the arrival of the Spanish.

Trivia

Lunar trinity. According to some sources, Mama Cocha formed with Mama Quilla and Pachamama an Inca lunar trinity, in which the three goddesses were linked.
Rainwater. The Incas linked the sea with rain: they believed seawater replenished the clouds and that the rain then fell over the land.
