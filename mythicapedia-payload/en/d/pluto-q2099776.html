mother of Tantalus in Greek mythology

Pluto

Pluto is, in Greek mythology, the mother of Tantalus. Ancient sources disagree about her origins, and she appears only briefly outside these genealogical accounts.

The myth

In the most common version, Pluto was the mother of Tantalus, usually fathered by Zeus. A scholion on Euripides' Orestes, however, names Tmolos as the father. Hyginus says her father was Himas, while other sources make Cronus her father.

The Clementine Recognitions give a different genealogy: Tantalus's mother, called Plutis or Plute, was said to be a daughter of Atlas. Nonnus calls her Berecyntian Pluto and links her to Mount Berecyntus in Phrygia, sacred to Cybele.

In Nonnus's account, Zeus goes to Pluto's bed to father Tantalus and hides his thunderbolts in a cave. There the monster Typhon finds them and steals them, setting off his great battle with Zeus.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, Nonnus, a scholion on Euripides' Orestes and the Clementine Recognitions.

Trivia

A stolen thunderbolt. In Nonnus's account, Typhon's theft of Zeus's thunderbolts sets off a cosmic battle between the two.
Several fathers. The sources give up to four different fathers for Pluto, showing how variable these genealogies were.
