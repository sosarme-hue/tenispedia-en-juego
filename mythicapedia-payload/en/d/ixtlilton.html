Aztec god of medicine and healing

Ixtlilton

Ixtlilton is the Mexica (Aztec) god of medicine and healing, also linked to dance, festivals and games. His figure joins the care of the sick with the festive life of the community.

The myth

In Mexica tradition, Ixtlilton is closely associated with the Centzontotochtin and is called the brother of Macuilxochitl, god of well-being and good luck. He was a gentle god who emerged from an obsidian mask and brought darkness and peaceful sleep to children in their beds at night. He was also linked to tlílatl, a black water with healing properties that was used as ink for the codices.

Worship and religion

His temple was called Tlacuilocan, 'place of the scribe'. According to Sahagún, he was the patron of professional musicians and dancers: whoever wanted to hold a party called his priests, and the priest dressed in his attire led the dance. After eating, drinking pulque and dancing, the host offered four jars of black water; if dirt was found in them, it was taken as a sign of bad conduct.

Ancient sources: Described by friar Bernardino de Sahagún in his General History of the Things of New Spain, compiled with information from Nahua informants.

Trivia

Codex ink. The black water tlílatl had healing properties and served as ink for making the codices.
Obsidian mask. Ixtlilton emerged from an obsidian mask and was said to bring peaceful sleep to children.
Place of the scribe. His temple, the Tlacuilocan, means 'place of the scribe', reflecting his link to writing.
