princess of Iolcus, wife of Admetus

Alcestis

Alcestis is a princess of Iolcus, daughter of King Pelias, known for her devotion to her husband Admetus. According to the tradition, she agreed to die in his place and was brought back from the underworld by Heracles. Her story, staged by Euripides, is one of the best-known Greek tales of marital loyalty.

The myth

In the myth, Alcestis was the fairest of the daughters of Pelias, king of Iolcus, and of Anaxibia, although other accounts name Phylomache as her mother. When suitors came to the palace, Pelias set a condition: whoever wanted her had to yoke a lion and a boar to a chariot. Admetus, king of Pherae, managed it with the help of Apollo, who was serving him as a shepherd during a year of banishment from Olympus.

After the wedding, Admetus forgot to make an offering to Artemis, and when he opened the bridal chamber he found the bed full of coiled snakes, a sign of an early death. Apollo intervened again, getting the Fates drunk so they would spare Admetus if someone agreed to die in his place. No one volunteered, not even his elderly parents, until Alcestis stepped forward and died.

Shortly afterwards Heracles arrived as a guest in the house and learned what had happened. He wrestled with Thanatos and returned Alcestis to the living. When she came back she could not speak for three days, until she was purified of her debt to the chthonic gods.

Legacy

Euripides staged her in his tragedy Alcestis, and the legend inspired operas by Lully and Gluck, Pérez Galdós's play Alceste, and paintings by Angelica Kauffmann and Frederic Leighton.

Ancient sources: Homer calls her the fairest of Pelias's daughters, and Diodorus Siculus mentions her in his account of her father's death; Euripides made her the title character of his tragedy Alcestis.

Trivia

Honored in antiquity. Her sacrifice was widely celebrated in antiquity, and she appears on the Chest of Cypselus in a scene showing the funeral rites of Pelias.
A rationalist reading. Ancient and modern critics have explained her return as a recovery from a serious illness, treated by a physician named Heracles.
