Lelegian king of Pedasus

Altes

Altes was a Lelegian king who ruled at Pedasus, in or near the Troad. He matters because the Iliad makes him the father of Laothoe, one of King Priam's wives, linking him to the royal house of Troy.

The myth

According to Homer's Iliad, Altes was the father of Laothoe, one of the many wives of King Priam. Through this marriage the Lelegian king became related to the Trojan royal family, and his daughter joined the household of the ruler whose city fought the long war against the Greeks.

Later traditions also make Altes the father of Ancaeus of Samos, one of the Argonauts who sailed with Jason in search of the Golden Fleece. The likely reason is that Ancaeus was also of Lelegian descent, which would explain why the two were linked in the stories.

The ancient mythographers do not give any parentage for Altes himself, so his own origins remain unrecorded.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, which names him as the father of Laothoe; other traditions link him to the Argonaut Ancaeus of Samos.

Trivia

Priam's father-in-law. Altes was the father of Laothoe, one of many wives of the Trojan king Priam, which makes him the father-in-law of one of the best-known rulers of the Trojan War.
An Argonaut of the same people. Some traditions make him the father of Ancaeus of Samos, an Argonaut. This is usually explained by their shared Lelegian descent.
