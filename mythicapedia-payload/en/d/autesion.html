Theban king in Greek mythology

Autesion

Autesion is a king of Thebes in Greek mythology, father of Theras and grandfather, through his daughter Argeia, of the twins Eurysthenes and Procles. His story turns on an oracle that ordered him to leave Thebes and settle in the Peloponnese.

The myth

According to tradition, Autesion was the son of Tisamenus and succeeded his father as king of Thebes. His ancestry reached back into the city's legendary past: his grandfather was Thersander, and his great-grandparents were Polynices and Argeia.

Autesion had two children, Theras and Argeia. Argeia married the Heraclid Aristodemus, making Autesion the grandfather of the twins Eurysthenes and Procles. The best-known episode of his life is an oracle that ordered him to leave Thebes. He obeyed, travelled to the Peloponnese, and settled in Sparta.

Trivia

A warrior named Autesion. In the epic poem Dionysiaca, another warrior named Autesion is killed by Corymbasos, a chief of the Indians.
