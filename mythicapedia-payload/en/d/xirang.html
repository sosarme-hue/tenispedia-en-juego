magical substance in Chinese mythology

Xirang

Xirang is a magical substance in Chinese mythology, a soil able to swell and keep growing without limit. Gun and Yu the Great used it to hold back the waters of the Great Flood.

The myth

In some versions of the story, Gun stole the xirang from Shangdi, who sent the deity Zhu Rong to punish him on Feather Mountain. Yu, by contrast, climbed up to Heaven and begged Shangdi, who granted him as much xirang as his magical black tortoise could carry on its back. With it he managed to block the 233,559 springs that fed the flood. Other versions credit the Primordial Divinity with the theft or the gift, or name a different executioner for Gun.

Some scholars read the myth as history: Gun would stand for a society farming on a small scale, raising plots above the marshes, and Yu for a later one able to turn wetlands into fields on a large scale. The xirang may correspond to a kind of raised garden made of earth and brushwood. Another explanation points to expansive clays, common in Shaanxi, which swell when wet and puff up when dried.

Trivia

An echo in the Americas. The story resembles the earth-diver creation myths of Indigenous peoples of the Americas, in which a creature brings up from the primordial waters a little magical soil that later expands into dry land.
A name with many readings. The word combines xí, meaning breathe, cease, rest, grow or multiply, with rǎng, meaning soil or earth. Translators have rendered it as breathing earth, self-renewing soil or swelling mold.
