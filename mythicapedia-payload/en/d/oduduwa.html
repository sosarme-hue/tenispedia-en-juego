Yoruba deity, first king of Ile-Ife and ancestor of the Yoruba

Odùduwà

Odùduwà is the first divine king of Ile-Ife, the Yoruba holy city, and one of the great deities (òrìṣà) of the Yoruba religion. He is regarded as the progenitor who founded the royal line of Ife and, by extension, the Yoruba peoples.

The myth

According to tradition, Odùduwà came from the east, from the village of Oke Ora, and gained control over the thirteen communities of Ife, which had previously been led by Obàtalá. The rivalry lasted for generations until a large smallpox outbreak forced a truce, and the groups were eventually united under his rule.

In the cosmological account, Odùduwà is a creator sent from heaven by Olódumare to create the earth upon the waters. In the most widespread version, the task belonged to Obàtalá, who carried a snail shell filled with sand and a rooster to scatter it, but Odùduwà took it from him. Some traditions describe Odùduwà as Obàtalá's wife, others as a figure whose gender is fluid. His descendant Ọ̀rànmíyàn, said in some versions to be a grandson, spread the Ife model of kingship as far as Oyo and Benin. Some Muslim traditions link him with Mecca, though scholars usually attribute this to later Islamic influence.

Worship and religion

Ile-Ife is his spiritual centre, and the Óòni of Ife, regarded as a direct descendant of the òrìṣà, is his living embodiment. The Itapa festival commemorates the reconciliation with Obàtalá's group. Within the Gelede society, initiates receive a shrine to Odùduwà, who is revered as the mother of the Yoruba. His cult remains alive in the Yoruba religion and, in Santería, he is syncretized with Jesus Christ, most often as Saint Manuel.

Legacy

The Yoruba call themselves Ọmọ Odùduwà, descendants of Odùduwà. The "crown of Odùduwà" became a symbol of political prestige and power in the region, and his praise names Olófin Adimulà and Olófin Ayé are still remembered.

Ancient sources: His figure is transmitted mainly through Yoruba oral tradition and the verses of the Odu Ifá, such as Osa Meji, which recount his rivalry with Obàtalá.

Trivia

A tool of creation. In the cosmological version, the land is formed from sand scattered from a snail shell with a rooster, and Obàtalá and Odùduwà are symbolically represented by a calabash, one taking the top and the other the bottom.
A name with meaning. His name is explained as Odu-ti-o-da-iwa, literally meaning "the great repository which brings forth existence".
