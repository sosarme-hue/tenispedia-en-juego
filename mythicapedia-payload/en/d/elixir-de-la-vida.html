legendary potion of immortality

Elixir of life

The elixir of life is a legendary potion that, according to tradition, granted eternal life or everlasting youth and cured all diseases. It appears in the lore of several cultures and was one of alchemy's great goals, closely linked to the philosopher's stone.

The myth

One of the oldest accounts comes from Mesopotamia. In the Epic of Gilgamesh, the hero, fearing old age after the death of his companion Enkidu, seeks out Utnapishtim, the survivor of a great flood whom the gods granted immortality. Utnapishtim tells him of a plant at the bottom of the sea, but Gilgamesh loses it to a serpent before he can eat it. The story explains why snakes shed their skin and seem to renew themselves, while humans are doomed to die.

In ancient China, legend says that Qin Shi Huang commissioned the alchemist Xu Fu to find the elixir. Xu Fu sailed east toward the legendary Penglai Mountain and never returned from his second voyage; some versions claim he reached Japan. In India, Hindu scriptures place amrita, the nectar of immortality, in the churning of the ocean of milk by gods and demons.

Legacy

The elixir has inspired many works of fiction, including Natalie Babbitt's novel Tuck Everlasting, Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, the manga Fullmetal Alchemist and the video game Touhou Project. In Scotland and Ireland, the Gaelic name for whiskey, uisce beatha, means "water of life".

Ancient sources: The Epic of Gilgamesh, from the second millennium BC, is the oldest known reference to a plant of immortality.

Trivia

Death by mercury. According to the account, Emperor Jiajing of the Ming dynasty died after ingesting a lethal dose of mercury in a supposed elixir of life prepared by alchemists.
Water of life. The elixir has many names; in Persian, aab-i-hayat means "water of life", and in Sanskrit, amrita means "immortality".
