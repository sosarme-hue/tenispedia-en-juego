Mahavishnu

Mahavishnu is the first of the purusha avatars of Vishnu in Vaishnava Hinduism: the Vishnu who lies in a corner of Vaikuntha, the spiritual world, on an ocean of material cause. His name means 'the great Vishnu', and his figure expresses the belief that every material universe emerges from his body.

The myth

According to Vaishnava tradition, Vishnu has several forms, each with a different role in sustaining the universe. Among them are three Vishnus who rest on cosmic oceans. Mahavishnu is the only one of his kind: he lies in Vaikuntha, the spiritual world, on an ocean of material cause.

Innumerable universes emanate from his pores, likened to seeds or cosmic eggs. The material stuff prepared to form the worlds is personified as Mahamaya. Mahavishnu merely gazes at her and, as purusha, impregnates her with the sleeping souls, who wait to enter the cycle of countless births and deaths. At his feet rests the goddess Lakshmi, sized to suit him.

Ancient sources: The Satvata Tantra is the text that, according to the sources, first describes the existence of these three Vishnus.

Trivia

Cosmic pores. Tradition holds that many universes sprout from Mahavishnu's pores, like seeds that later become cosmic eggs.
Lakshmi at his feet. The goddess Lakshmi rests at Mahavishnu's feet, sized to match him.
