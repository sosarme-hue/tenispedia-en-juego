Hrímnir

Hrímnir is a jötunn, a giant of Norse mythology, whose name has been linked to frost and hoarfrost. He appears mainly as the father of Hljóð in the Völsunga saga, and his presence in several sources shows that he was a recognised giant in Scandinavian tradition.

The myth

In the Völsunga saga, Hrímnir is the father of Hljóð, a young woman whom Frigg sends to Rerir as a "wish-maid". Frigg gives her an apple of fertility that will allow Rerir's wife to bear children. Later, Hrímnir himself sends his daughter to become the wife of one of Rerir's sons, Völsung.

The Prose Edda lists him among the jǫtnar in the þulur section. The Hyndluljóð gives him the children Heiðr and Hrossþjófr, a parentage that may serve the poem's alliteration more than a fixed genealogy. In the Skírnismál he appears briefly, probably as a typical jötunn. The Gríms saga loðinkinna makes him the father of the giantesses Feima and Kleima, and gives him a wife named Hyrja.

Ancient sources: Völsunga saga, Prose Edda (the þulur section), Hyndluljóð, Skírnismál and Gríms saga loðinkinna.

Trivia

A name of frost. His name has been translated as "frosty", "the one covered with hoarfrost" or "the sooty one". It probably evokes the frost giants, the hrímþursar.
An unreliable parentage. In the Hyndluljóð he is given children who may exist only because of the poem's alliteration, so they should not be taken as a firm genealogy.
