mythical king of Argos in Greek mythology

Criasus

Criasus was a legendary king of Argos in Greek mythology, son of Argus and, according to other accounts, of the Oceanid Peitho. He is remembered mainly through the genealogies and chronologies that ancient writers preserved.

The myth

According to tradition, Criasus was the son of Argus and Evadne, daughter of Strymon, although some authors made him the son of the Oceanid Peitho. He had five brothers: Ecbasus, Jasus, Peiranthus or Peiras, Epidaurus and Tiryns. With his wife Melantho he fathered Phorbas, Ereuthalion and Cleoboea. He was said to have reigned for fifty-four years, and during his reign Callithyia, daughter of Peiranthus, became the first priestess of Hera.

Eusebius of Caesarea placed his reign alongside that of rulers in Assyria and Sicyon, and even noted that Moses was born in Egypt during those years. In the end, the throne of Argos passed to his son Phorbas.

Ancient sources: Eusebius of Caesarea mentions his reign when fixing the chronologies of other ancient kingdoms.

Trivia

A striking synchronism. Eusebius of Caesarea placed Criasus's reign at the same time as the birth of Moses in Egypt, showing how chronographers linked the histories of different peoples.
