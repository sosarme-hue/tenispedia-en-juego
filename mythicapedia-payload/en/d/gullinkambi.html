rooster of Norse mythology

Gullinkambi

Gullinkambi, whose Old Norse name means "golden comb", is a rooster who lives in Valhalla and wakes the einherjar, the warriors who fell in battle, every morning. He is one of three roosters whose crowing is foretold to mark the beginning of Ragnarök, the final battle of Norse mythology.

The myth

According to Norse tradition, Gullinkambi lives in Valhalla, the hall of Odin where the einherjar, the warriors slain in battle, rest. Each morning his crowing wakes them so they can prepare for the fight. He is also known as Salgofnir, another name under which he appears in the sources.

In the Völuspá, part of the Poetic Edda, a prophecy says that three roosters will crow at the start of Ragnarök, the final clash between the gods and the jötnar, led by Loki. Gullinkambi is one of them. The other two are Fjalar, who crows in the wood Gálgviðr, and a soot-red rooster who lives in Hel, whose name is unknown.

Legacy

Gullinkambi appears in the manga and anime Kobayashi-san Chi no Maid Dragon, where the character Tohru uses his eggs to make omurice. A modern interpretation also suggests that the bird at the top of the central tree in the Överhogdal tapestries is Gullinkambi, and that the tree represents the world tree Yggdrasil. This is a scholarly suggestion, not something stated in the ancient texts.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Völuspá, one of the poems of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A rooster on a tapestry. The Överhogdal tapestries show a central tree with a bird at the top. Some researchers propose that the bird is Gullinkambi and that the tree is Yggdrasil.
Eggs in the kitchen. In the manga Kobayashi-san Chi no Maid Dragon, the dragon maid Tohru makes omurice with Gullinkambi's eggs.
