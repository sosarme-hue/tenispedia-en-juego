Greek personification of battle noise

Homados

Homados is, in Greek mythology, the personification of battle noise: the shouts, cries and confusion of men mingled with the clash of weapons. He appears as one of the many figures engraved on the shield of Heracles.

The myth

Homados has no stories of his own; he appears in a descriptive passage of the poem The Shield of Heracles, attributed to Hesiod. The hero's shield is crowded with fearsome figures: at its centre is Phobos, Fear, with fiery eyes and a mouth full of teeth, and above him hovers Eris, Strife, who stirs up the throng of men.

Around them are also shown Proioxis, Pursuit, and Palioxis, Flight, alongside Phobos and Androktasia, Slaughter. Among them is Homados, Tumult, who completes the scene of battle. Kydoimos, Uproar, also rushes about, and Ker, deadly Fate, drags a fallen man by the feet through the tumult.

Ancient sources: He appears in The Shield of Heracles, a poem attributed to Hesiod, which describes the hero's shield.
