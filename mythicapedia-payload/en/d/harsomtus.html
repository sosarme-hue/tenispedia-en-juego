Egyptian child god, son of Hathor and Horus

Harsomtus

Harsomtus, also spelled Harsomptus, is a child god of ancient Egyptian religion, son of Hathor and Horus, whose name means Horus who unites the two lands. Researchers regard him as a form of the sun god, linked to sunrise and to the emergence of the world from primordial matter.

The myth

According to tradition, Harsomtus formed with his mother Hathor and with Horus Behedety the Edfu triad. He is most often shown as a naked child seated on a lotus flower. Egyptian theologians linked this flower to the daily course of the sun: it opens at dawn, facing east, and at evening it closes and sinks beneath the waters.

In Thebes, during the late New Kingdom, he was presented as the firstborn son of Amun and Hathor, lady of Thebes. Over time he was identified with other child gods, such as Ihy, with Shed, who protected people from dangerous animals, and with Harpocrates.

Worship and religion

He was honoured in agricultural rites and lunar festivals, and his main cult places were at Dendera and Edfu. His worship is documented from the Old Kingdom into Graeco-Roman Egypt.

Trivia

Varied forms. Harsomtus appears as a child, as a mummy, as a man with a falcon or serpent head, and as a serpent rising from a lotus, an image seen in reliefs at the temple of Dendera.
A birth shrine. In the Ptolemaic period, Harsomtus was worshipped in the mammisi, or birth house, of the Hathor temple at Deir el-Medina.
