Chinese deity of examinations and students

Kui Xing

Kui Xing, whose name means Chief Star, is a Chinese deity linked to the Pole Star and a patron of students and scholars. Because of his connection with the imperial examinations, he is regarded as the protector of those who aspire to government posts.

The myth

In Daoist tradition, Kui Xing is the personification of the Pole Star, which was believed to split yin and yang. He is described as bent and hunchbacked, as if he were a calligraphy character, and came to be venerated as a saint of human fortune, especially regarding the imperial examinations. He appears as the helper of Wenchang Wang, identified with the Big Dipper.

Worship and religion

For centuries, students offered incense, prayers and food to Kui Xing in hope of good results in their exams. The custom continues today: students make the same request at the temples of Wenchang Wang, burning incense and leaving offerings.

Legacy

His image developed in popular art: he stands with his right foot on an ao, a giant turtle or fish, in reference to the saying standing alone on the ao's head, meaning coming first in an examination, and holds a brush and an inkpot. His torso is often formed from calligraphy of Confucian adages.

Ancient sources: Sima Qian, the Grand Historian, records how the Big Dipper was used to locate the Pole Star; references to Kui Xing come mainly from Daoist and popular tradition.

Trivia

The ao. His right foot rests on an ao, a giant turtle or fish. The expression standing alone on the ao's head means coming first in an examination.
A character turned into a god. Since the star character 奎 could not be sculpted, his name was changed to 魁, and when that could not be depicted either, the character was split into its radicals, 鬼 and 斗.
