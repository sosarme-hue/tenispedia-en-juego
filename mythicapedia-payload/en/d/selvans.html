Etruscan god of woodlands and boundaries

Selvans

Selvans is the Etruscan god of woodlands and boundaries, including sacred ones. His name may be related to the Roman Silvanus, although his image and role belong to Etruscan tradition.

The myth

Ancient texts about Selvans are scarce and do not preserve long stories about his life or deeds. He is known mostly through inscriptions and ritual objects.

He was depicted as a naked youth wearing a cap made of bear or cat hide and high boots. This image contrasts with that of Silvanus, who is usually shown as a bearded man.

Worship and religion

Votive inscriptions found on the Piacenza Liver, a bronze model used for divination, show that he was a popular god in Etruria. One dedication calls him Selvans Tularia, "of the boundaries", and a stone probably from Bosnia names him Selvans Sanchuneta, an epithet that may link him to Sancus, the Italic god of sacred oaths. On the rim of that same liver his name appears tenth among sixteen gods.

Ancient sources: The Piacenza Liver, a bronze divination model with inscriptions, is his main ancient witness, alongside dedications and a boundary stone.

Trivia

An unusual cap. His only certain depiction shows him wearing a cap of bear or cat hide, a detail that sets him apart from his possible Roman counterpart.
A god on a bronze liver. His name is inscribed on the rim of the Piacenza Liver, a bronze model of a sheep's liver.
