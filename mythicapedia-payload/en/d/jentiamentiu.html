Egyptian god of the westerners and guardian of the necropolis

Khenti-Amentiu

Khenti-Amentiu is an ancient Egyptian deity, shown with a jackal's head, who guarded the city of the dead at Abydos. His name, meaning "Foremost of the Westerners", was also used as a title for Osiris and Anubis, so his figure is often confused with theirs.

The myth

In Egyptian tradition, Khenti-Amentiu was a guardian of the necropolises, alongside Anubis and Upuaut, and in the Old Kingdom he is sometimes identified with them. The "Westerners" of his name were the justified dead who dwelt in the Amenti, the western realm of the afterlife.

Over time the name became a title of Osiris and Anubis, and Osiris eventually took over Khenti-Amentiu's role as patron of Abydos, under the form Osiris-Khentiamentiu. Some scholars argue that Khenti-Amentiu was simply Osiris under another name before the middle of the Old Kingdom, when Osiris's name first appears in the texts. Others, such as Terence DuQuesne, suggest he was originally an aspect of Anubis and only became independent in the Fifth Dynasty.

Worship and religion

He was a local god attested early at Abydos, with agricultural, fertility and funerary traits, even before Egypt was unified. His name appears on the necropolis seals of the First Dynasty pharaohs Den and Qa'a, and a temple from predynastic times was founded in his honour at Abydos. That temple later became dedicated to Osiris, who made it his major cult centre during the First Intermediate Period.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts, whose earliest known copy dates to the end of the Fifth Dynasty, apply the title Khenti-Amentiu to Anubis rather than to Osiris.

Trivia

Seals of the first pharaohs. His name appears on the necropolis seals of the First Dynasty pharaohs Den and Qa'a, where each predecessor is named with the title "Horus Khenti-Amentiu", starting with Narmer.
A jackal-headed god. He was depicted with a black jackal's head at Abydos in Upper Egypt, where he stood guard over the city of the dead.
