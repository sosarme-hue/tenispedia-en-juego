civilizing god of the Muisca

Bochica

Bochica is a civilizing hero or god in the mythology of the Muisca, who lived on the Altiplano Cundiboyacense in present-day Colombia. He taught his people to spin, weave and live by moral rules.

The myth

According to the tradition, Bochica arrived as a man with white hair and a long beard, wearing a cloak, barefoot and carrying a golden staff. He taught the Muisca to spin cotton, weave blankets and live by moral and social principles, and he is also credited with creating the Tequendama Falls. He had different names depending on the region, such as Nemquetheba or Sugunsua.

Colonial chroniclers, mostly Spaniards, do not agree. Alonso de Medrano (1599) describes a foreigner with white skin who came with a camel, opened roads through the mountains and died in Sogamoso. Juan de Castellanos (1601) mentions a long beard and does not consider him either an apostle or a holy man.

Worship and religion

Sogamoso was regarded as a sacred place. Caciques consulted him in the Toya cave, and festivities were organised around his arrival near the Temple of the Sun.

Legacy

Today he is studied mainly through the colonial chronicles, which the researcher Andrés Camilo Bohórquez Roa compiled in a book.

Ancient sources: Colonial chronicles by Alonso de Medrano (1599), Bernardo Vargas Machuca (1599), Juan de Castellanos (1601) and Lucas Fernández de Piedrahita (1688).

Trivia

Mysterious roads. Medrano claimed the Muisca had long, straight roads that, according to tradition, Bochica had cleared as he walked. The people did not dare to use them because they revered them.
A debated beard. The beard described by the chroniclers has been much debated. Some saw it as proof of European influence, while others link it to Mesoamerican representations.
