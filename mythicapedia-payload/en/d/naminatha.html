twenty-first tirthankara of Jainism

Naminatha

Naminatha is the twenty-first tirthankara of Jainism, a spiritual teacher who, according to his tradition, attained liberation on Mount Shikharji. He matters because his story embodies the Jain ideal of nonviolence.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, he was born in Mithila, son of King Vijaya and Queen Vipra of the Ikshvaku dynasty. The stories say that while his mother was pregnant, the city was besieged by a coalition of rival kings. His calming aura overcame them: they laid down their arms, entered the city and surrendered to Vijaya without battle. This is said to explain his name, linked to bowing down or surrendering.

As an adult he gave up his throne and became a monk. Tradition holds that he attained omniscience while meditating under a bakula tree and founded a monastic order led by Suprabha. He lived ten thousand years and reached moksha on Shikharji, in today's Jharkhand.

Worship and religion

His footprints are venerated in a shrine on Mount Shikharji, a pilgrimage site for both major Jain sects. Temples once stood in Mithila, and a restored site in Sitamarhi, Bihar, commemorates his birthplace.

Legacy

Temples dedicated to him at Khajuraho, from the 10th century, and in Jaisalmer Fort, with its marble idol, show his lasting presence in architecture.
