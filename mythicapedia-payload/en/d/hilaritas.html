Roman goddess of joy

Hilaritas

Hilaritas is the Roman personification of joy, gladness and merriment, associated with happy times. She is known mainly from imperial coinage, where she appears from Hadrian onwards with legends such as Hilaritas Augusti, and also on coins of empresses.

The myth

The sources record no myth of her own. She is known chiefly through her image: a crowned matron wearing a laurel wreath, standing, holding a palm branch, a patera, a sceptre or a cornucopia, sometimes accompanied by one or two children. She belonged to a group of personifications of human virtues and feelings, such as Patientia, Laetitia or Clementia.

Worship and religion

The sources barely document her cult. Her presence is best understood within traditional Roman religion, and the green branches that decorated streets, temples, doors and houses on festive occasions expressed the same feeling of joy.

Legacy

Her name lives on in the asteroid (996) Hilaritas, which takes it from this personification.

Trivia

Branches of joy. In antiquity streets, temples, doors, houses and even entire cities were decorated with green branches and leaves as a sign of joy.
An asteroid named after her. The asteroid (996) Hilaritas takes its name from this Roman personification.
