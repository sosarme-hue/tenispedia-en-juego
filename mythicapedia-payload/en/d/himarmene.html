Greek goddess of fate

Heimarmene

Heimarmene is a goddess of Greek mythology who personifies fate: the orderly chain of cause and effect and, more broadly, the destiny of the universe as a whole, as opposed to the destiny of individual beings. The concept carried great weight in Stoicism and Gnosticism.

The myth

According to the Orphic accounts, the demiurge Zeus was raised by Adrasteia, united with Ananke, and fathered Heimarmene. In this way the goddess is placed within a lineage of powers that govern the order of the world.

The name Heimarmene was apparently used as a synonym for other Greek words for fate, such as Aesa, Moira, Moros, Ananke, Adrasteia or Pepromene. For this reason, her figure is often blended with those of these other deities of fate.

Legacy

The concept of Heimarmene was central to Greek Stoicism, where it described the chain of causes that governs the world, and it also played a major role in Gnosticism and other religious sects. In Hermeticism it was understood as the fate imparted by the influence of the stars or of the deity who ruled them.

Ancient sources: The ancient references come mainly from the Orphic tradition and from Stoic thinkers, who used the term in their discussions of fate.
