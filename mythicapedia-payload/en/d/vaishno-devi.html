Hindu goddess, manifestation of the Mother Goddess on Trikuta Mountain

Vaishno Devi

Vaishno Devi is a Hindu goddess, a manifestation of the Mother Goddess (Devi) and of divine energy (Shakti), worshipped at the sacred cave of Trikuta Mountain in the Jammu region. She is revered as a divine Mother who protects her devotees and grants them boons.

The myth

According to tradition, the goddess was born in southern India from the combined tejas of Mahasaraswati, Mahalakshmi and Mahakali. As the daughter of the devoted Ratnakar Sagar, she withdrew into the forest to meditate and win Vishnu as her husband. When he incarnated as Rama and passed through the forest, she asked him to marry her, but Rama explained that in that incarnation he could have only one wife, Sita. He promised to return as Kalki in the Kali Yuga and told her to settle in the Trikuta Hills.

Centuries later, in the village of Hansali near present-day Katra, she appeared as a girl to the Brahmin Pandit Shridhar and asked him to host a vegetarian feast. When the Tantric disciple Bhairon Nath demanded meat and liquor and tried to seize her, she fled into the mountains. Along the way she created the Banganga stream, left her footprints on the rock of Charan paduka, and hid for nine months in the Garbhjoon cave. Finally, in the holy cave at the summit, she took the form of Kali or Chandika and beheaded Bhairon Nath. Repentant, he won the right that a pilgrimage to her shrine would only be complete after visiting his temple. The goddess then gave up her human form and remained as three rock pindis representing Mahakali, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati.

Worship and religion

Pilgrims visit the sacred cave, where the three pindis are worshipped. The shrine is looked after by the Baridars, descendants of Pandit Shridhar, who keep the tradition alive. She is venerated as a virgin, vegetarian and ascetic goddess, and devotees also visit Bhairon Nath's temple to complete the pilgrimage.

Legacy

Her popular iconography, a young woman in a red sari with eight arms, riding a lion or tiger, often overlaps with that of Durga in posters, calendars and statues.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata mentions her in Arjuna's invocation, the Devi Mahatmya lists the name Vaishnavi among the 108 names of the Mahadevi, and the Rig-Veda refers to Trikuta Mountain.

Trivia

Three colours, three energies. The pindis differ in colour: white stands for Sattva, yellowish red for Rajas and black for Tamas, each representing a different cosmic energy.
An invocation in the Mahabharata. According to the epic, Arjuna invoked the goddess with the phrase Jambookatak Chityaishu Nityam Sannihitalaye, which is interpreted as a reference to Jammu.
