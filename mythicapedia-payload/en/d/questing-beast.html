Arthurian beast with a baying belly

Questing Beast

The Questing Beast, or Glatisant ('the barking beast'), is a mixed-animal monster from Arthurian legend that King Pellinore and his family hunt in vain. It matters because it condenses the incest and chaos that, according to tradition, bring about the ruin of Arthur's realm.

The myth

According to the French prose cycles, later gathered by Malory in Le Morte d'Arthur, the beast has a serpent's head, a leopard's body, a lion's hindquarters and a deer's feet. Its name comes from the noise it makes from its belly, a barking like thirty hounds.

Arthur sees it drinking from a pool just after a troubling dream that foretells the ruin of his realm at Mordred's hands. According to Merlin, it was born of a princess who, infatuated with her brother, lay with a devil; he manipulated her into accusing the boy of rape, and their father had him torn apart by dogs. Before dying, the young man prophesied that his sister would give birth to an abomination that would howl like the pack.

Palamedes pursues it in vain until, now converted to Christianity, he slays it during the quest for the Grail.

Legacy

It appears in modern works inspired by the Arthurian legend.

Ancient sources: The French prose cycles (Post-Vulgate, Suite du Merlin, Perlesvaus) and Thomas Malory's Le Morte d'Arthur.

Trivia

A medieval giraffe. Some Arthurian scholars see the beast as a reflection of medieval ideas about giraffes, whose Latin name, camelopardalis, suggests a hybrid of camel and leopard.
A barking name. Glatisant is related to the French word glapissant, meaning yelping or barking, especially of small dogs or foxes.
