legendary Swedish king of the Yngling house

Ingjald Illråde

Ingjald Illråde was a legendary Swedish king of the Yngling house, son of Anund and father of Olof Trätälja. The Norse sagas remember him for his cruelty and for the nickname his reputation earned him.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson's Ynglinga saga, Ingjald was the son of King Anund and grew up at Gamla Uppsala. One winter, after he found himself weaker than Alf in a game, he was shamed, and his foster-father Svipdag made him eat the roasted heart of a wolf. From then on he was fierce and ill-tempered.

When he inherited the throne, he invited the Swedish chieftains to a feast in a hall with seven high seats. Instead of swearing over the ritual beaker, he vowed with a bull's horn to enlarge his kingdom twofold towards all four quarters or die. When the guests were drunk, he burned the hall with them inside. He later attacked Granmar and Hjörvard, whom he trapped in a farmhouse, and for these betrayals he earned the nickname Illråde, meaning ill-ruler. His own end came at the hands of Ivar Vidfamne, who was avenging his father; according to the story, Ingjald and his daughter Åsa perished in the flames at Ræning.

Legacy

His sagas are still studied, and the Rällinge hill fort in Södermanland shows traces of a fire dated to around 600-650 AD, which some researchers link to Ræning.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Ynglinga saga and Snorri Sturluson's Heimskringla, the Historia Norvegiæ, the Hervarar saga, Íslendingabók and other medieval texts.

Trivia

Wolf's heart. According to the saga, the remedy Svipdag gave to Ingjald's shame was a roasted wolf's heart, a symbolic cure remembered as the origin of his fierce temper.
Legal arson. The burning of his guests is linked to hús-brenna, a form of legal offensive in medieval Scandinavia in which a house was set on fire with its occupants inside.
