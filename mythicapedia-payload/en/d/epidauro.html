eponymous hero of the Greek city of Epidaurus

Epidaurus

Epidaurus is the hero whom tradition named as the eponym of the city of Epidaurus in the Argolid, in ancient Greece. He matters because his parentage differed from one community to another, showing how each Greek people shaped the myth to fit its own history.

The myth

According to Pausanias, Epidaurus's ancestry depended on which community told the story. The Argives considered him a son of Argus, himself a son of Zeus, and of Evadne. The people of Elis believed he was a son of Pelops, while the Epidaurians themselves held that he was a son of Apollo.

Pausanias adds that he knew of no native of Epidaurus who claimed descent from the hero, and that nobody knew who had lived in the region before his arrival. The last king before the Dorians reached the Peloponnese was reportedly Pityreus, a descendant of Ion, who handed the land over without a fight to Deiphontes and the Argives.

Ancient sources: Pausanias records these different versions in his Description of Greece, and mentions the Great Eoiai poem as the source of the Argive genealogy.

Trivia

A hero without known descendants. Pausanias notes that no inhabitant of Epidaurus claimed to descend from the hero who gave the city its name.
A handover without war. According to tradition, Pityreus gave up his land to Deiphontes and the Argives without resistance, just before the Dorians arrived.
