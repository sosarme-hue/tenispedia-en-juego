Hegemone

Hegemone is one of the Charites (the Graces) of Greek mythology, worshipped at Athens alongside Auxo. Her name, from hegemon meaning 'leader' or 'guide', suggests the idea of one who leads, and it was also applied as an epithet to Artemis and Aphrodite.

The myth

Ancient sources say very little about her as a figure with a story. Her name appears mainly in texts describing the cults of Athens, where she is counted among the Charites, deities associated with grace and beauty.

The geographer Pausanias mentions her alongside Auxo as the two Charites honoured in the city, and notes that the name Carpo belongs to one of the Horae rather than to a Charis. This detail shows how carefully the Athenians arranged these divinities, though no tales of Hegemone's origins or deeds have survived.

Worship and religion

Hegemone was invoked, along with Auxo and other deities such as Ares and Zeus, as a witness to the civic oath sworn by Athenian ephebes. As an epithet, the name was also given to Artemis, who had a temple at Lycosura in Arcadia and a sanctuary at Sparta, and to Aphrodite, whose cult is attested at Rhamnus. No living religious practice dedicated to her exists today.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his Description of Greece, names her among the Charites of Athens. The third-century BC poet Callimachus seems to apply the epithet to Artemis as guide of the colonists who founded Miletus.

Trivia

The ephebes' oath. Young Athenians in civic and military training swore loyalty to the city, invoking Hegemone among others.
A guide in several places. The epithet was applied to Artemis at Lycosura and Sparta and, according to Callimachus, to the guide of the colonists who founded Miletus.
