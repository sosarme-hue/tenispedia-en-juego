primordial principle of the waters in Orphic cosmogony

Hydros

Hydros (Greek for "water") is the primordial principle of the waters in Orphic cosmogony, more a philosophical concept than a deity with its own cult. Its status as a god is doubtful, since the sources do not attest it as a divinity.

The myth

According to the Orphic theogony preserved by Damascius and Athenagoras, in the beginning there was water and the mud from which the earth solidified, and above both stood a single first principle that the poem passes over in silence as ineffable. Because the sources render mythical names as abstract concepts, the water and the mud might correspond to Oceanus and Gaia, or to Oceanus and Tethys, though these identifications are conjectural.

From water and earth, sexless elemental beings that form no couple, arises a first being in the shape of a dragon, with heads of a bull and a lion, a divine face in the middle, and wings on its shoulders. It was called Time, unaware of old age, and also Heracles.

Ancient sources: The Orphic theogony, transmitted indirectly through Hieronymus and Hellanicus, Damascius and Athenagoras, and linked to the Orpheus tradition.

Trivia

A principle, not a god. Athenagoras makes clear that these are not distinct primordial beings but a single concept: water charged with what would later settle as earth.
Echoes of the Presocratics. Some features of the text recall Thales, who held water to be the arche, and Anaximenes, with his idea of rarefaction and condensation.
