lost desert city or oasis of Egypt and Libya

Zerzura

Zerzura is a legendary city or oasis which, according to tradition, lay hidden in the desert west of the Nile, in Egypt or Libya. Its long search, especially in the twentieth century, made it one of the great enigmas of the desert.

The myth

The earliest references are Arabic. A thirteenth-century tax register, Villages of the Fayyum, compiled by Abū ‘Uthmān al-Nābulusī, lists a village called Zarzura among those abandoned. A lost Arabic manuscript, the Kitab al-Kanuz, describes it as a white town like a dove, with palm trees, springs and riches, whose gate opens when you take a key from the beak of a carved bird.

In 1835 the English Egyptologist John Gardner Wilkinson recorded an account from a man who said he had found an oasis while searching for a lost camel. It lay five days west of the track between Farafra and Bahariya, with palms, springs and some ruins. No one has reliably found it again.

The best-known version tells of a camel herder, Hamid Keila, who in 1481 was supposedly rescued in the desert by tall, fair, blue-eyed men with straight swords, who lived in a white city. The story comes from a 1969 book by Emile Schurmacher and has no documentary support, so it is most likely a modern invention.

Legacy

Ralph Bagnold and László Almásy led expeditions in 1929-1930 using Ford trucks. They did not find the city, but on their return they founded the Zerzura Club in a bar in Wadi Halfa, and its members later served as officers in the British Army during the Second World War. Michael Ondaatje's novel The English Patient was loosely inspired by Almásy. Zerzura also appears in W. E. Johns's Biggles Flies South, Michael Asher's The Eye of Ra, and the 2012 video game Zerzura: The Lost City.

Ancient sources: Its earliest known mention is in Villages of the Fayyum, a thirteenth-century register, and in the lost Arabic manuscript Kitab al-Kanuz.

Trivia

A lost camel. The first European account arose from the search for a stray camel, a common theme in tales of secret desert oases.
A club born in a bar. The Zerzura Club was founded in a bar in Wadi Halfa after the 1929-1930 expedition.
