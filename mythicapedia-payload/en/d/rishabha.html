Rishabha

Rishabha is a figure of Hindu mythology, regarded by the Bhagavata Purana as a partial incarnation of Vishnu. Jains also revere him as their first tirthankara, making him a shared figure between both traditions.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, King Nabhi and his wife Merudevi, after long austerities to obtain an heir, were blessed with a son in whom Vishnu incarnated. From childhood he showed divine powers, such as making rain fall when Indra withheld it out of envy. Nabhi handed him the throne, and Rishabha married Jayanti, with whom he had one hundred sons, the eldest Bharata, after whom the earth is said to be named. He later taught his sons detachment, crowned Bharata and renounced the throne to wander as an avadhuta, naked, with unbound hair and in silence.

Worship and religion

According to scholar Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, he was already being worshipped by the first century BCE, and Jains continue to honour him as their first tirthankara.

Legacy

The eighth-century Vaikuntha Perumal Temple, from the Pallava era, carries reliefs showing the sacrifice of Nabhi and Merudevi and the birth of Rishabha.

Ancient sources: He appears in early Vedic texts such as the Rigveda, the Atharvaveda and the Taittiriya Brahmana, and above all in the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

One hundred sons. The Bhagavata Purana gives him one hundred sons, including Kukshi and Nimi, and the eldest, Bharata, is said to have given the earth its name.
One figure, two religions. Jains regard him as their first tirthankara, while Hinduism includes him as an avatar of Vishnu.
