Kasuga Daimyōjin

Kasuga Daimyōjin is a group of four Shinto kami worshipped at Kasuga-taisha in Nara, Japan. Founded as the shrine of the Fujiwara family, it is one of the country's most important shrines.

The myth

According to tradition, the shrine's first kami, Takemikazuchi, rode a white deer from Kashima Jingū to Mount Mikasa in 768 to protect Nara, which is why deer are regarded as sacred messengers. The four main kami are Ame-no-Koyane, Himegami, Futsunushi and Takemikazuchi.

Worship and religion

The shrine gained imperial favour in the Nara period and, from 1871 to 1946, ranked among the government-supported shrines of the first rank. Over three thousand lanterns are lit together during the Setsubun and Chūgen Mantoro festivals, and the buildings are rebuilt every twenty years in the shikinen zōtai tradition.

Legacy

The kasuga-zukuri style takes its name from the shrine's main hall, and its torii influenced many across Japan. Together with the Kasugayama Primeval Forest it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Nara, and it appears in paintings of the Nanboku-chō period.

Trivia

Sacred deer. In the park leading to the shrine, deer roam freely and are considered messengers of the kami.
Stone and bronze lanterns. More than three thousand lanterns, made of stone and bronze, line the path and the shrine grounds.
