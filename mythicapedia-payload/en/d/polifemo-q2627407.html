Lapith hero of Greek mythology

Polyphemus

Polyphemus is a Lapith hero of Greek mythology, a native of Larissa in Thessaly and one of the Argonauts who sailed with Jason. He should not be confused with the Cyclops of the same name, to whom some ancient authors wrongly attributed his lineage.

The myth

According to tradition, Polyphemus was the son of Elatus, chief of the Lapiths, and Hippea. His name is linked to the centaurs: in the Iliad, Nestor recalls him among an earlier generation of heroes from his youth, the strongest men the earth had bred, who fought the centaurs and utterly destroyed them. That oral tradition, which Homer's audience would have recognized in Nestor's allusion, did not survive in literary epic.

Years later, as a grown man, he joined the Argonauts. During their stay in Bithynia, he was the one who heard Hylas crying as the nymphs carried the youth off. When he helped Heracles search for Hylas, both were left behind by the crew of the Argo. Polyphemus settled in Mysia, where he founded the city of Cius and became its king. Later he set out to find his fellow Argonauts and died in the land of the Chalybes, where he was buried by the seashore under a poplar tree. Only one source names his wife, Laonome, the sister of Heracles. According to some accounts, he was also the beloved of Heracles.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him in Nestor's speech; other ancient authors, such as Euphorion and Socrates, mistakenly called him a son of Poseidon, a detail that belongs to the Cyclops Polyphemus.

Trivia

Two Polyphemuses. Euphorion and Socrates mistakenly called him a son of Poseidon, confusing him with the Cyclops who bears the same name.
A lost tradition. Nestor's allusion in the Iliad points to an oral tradition about the centaurs that did not survive in written epic.
