Onibaba

Onibaba is an oni from Japanese folklore who takes the form of an old woman. She is also known as kijo or Kurozuka, and her most famous legend is set in Adachigahara.

The myth

The legend traces her origin to a woman who lived in a cave or a small house in Adachigahara and died nearby. According to tradition, she became an oni through karma and resentment. She is described as a wrinkled old woman with a wild, unkempt look and an enormous mouth. She sometimes hides her demonic appearance to lull travelers into a false sense of safety, and she is shown carrying a kitchen knife or seated at a spinning wheel.

The Tosa Obake Zōshi, by an unknown author, tells of a kijo with very long hair who ate the fetus of a pregnant woman. The story comes from the Adachigahara legend, spread to Tosa, and became attached to local tales there.

Legacy

She appears in Japanese legends, folktales and performing arts, alongside figures such as Momiji and Suzuka Gozen.

Ancient sources: The anonymous Tosa Obake Zōshi recounts her story under the title "Kijo".

Trivia

A local museum. A small museum in Adachigahara says it holds the remains of the woman behind the legend, along with the pot and knife she used on her victims.
Kijo as an insult. The word kijo is also used as a general term for women with hideous hearts like those of an oni.
