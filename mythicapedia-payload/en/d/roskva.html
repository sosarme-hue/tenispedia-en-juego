servant of Thor in Norse mythology

Röskva

Röskva is a young servant of the god Thor in Norse mythology, sister of Þjálfi. She appears mainly in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, where her story is woven together with her brother's.

The myth

According to the Prose Edda, Thor and Loki spend a night at the farm of a peasant family. Thor slaughters his two goats, Tanngnjóstr and Tanngrisnir, eats them, and the next morning brings them back to life by striking their skins with his hammer Mjöllnir. One goat is lame, because Þjálfi had cracked a leg bone to reach the marrow. Furious, Thor takes the two children as servants in compensation, and Röskva and her brother enter his service.

Later, Thor, Loki and the two young servants travel toward Jötunheimr, the land of the giants. Röskva accompanies the group to the castle of Útgarða-Loki, where each member must perform a feat. In the tale, Röskva takes no part in the contests, while Þjálfi loses three races to Hugi, whose name means 'thought'.

Ancient sources: She appears in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (13th century), in the books Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál. Her brother Þjálfi is also mentioned in the Poetic Edda poem Hárbarðsljóð.
