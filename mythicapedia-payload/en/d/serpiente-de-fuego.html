demon of Slavic folklore

Fiery serpent

The fiery serpent is a demon of Slavic folklore, especially Russian, that flies through the air trailing sparks and is linked to bolides and meteorites. It matters because it joins beliefs about celestial phenomena with tales of deception, loss and the struggle against evil.

The myth

According to Russian folk belief around Tula, when the archangel Michael cast down the fallen angels, some of these devils never reached the earth and stayed flying in the air as fiery serpents. At night they visited widowed or abandoned women, taking the shape of the absent husband. The women did not recognise the deception, and the victims wasted away, lost their minds or died. To expose the impostor, people said the false husband had no spine.

In other tales, the creature leaves gifts that turn to horse manure at sunrise, or fathers children with human women. One of these sons, the fiery wolf, fought his father and defeated him.

Legacy

It appears in Serbian epic songs, Russian byliny and folk tales, and in the poetry of Fet.

Ancient sources: The Primary Chronicle (Povest vremennykh let) records a sighting in 1092 of a three-headed fiery serpent.

Trivia

A dragon in the sky in 1091. Prince Vsevolod Yaroslavich reportedly saw a dragon-meteorite fall near Vyshgorod, as recorded in the chronicle. Today it is read as a meteorite fall.
An egg that hatches wealth. According to Slavic tradition, a wealth-bringing demon can be hatched from a hen's egg kept for several days. In return it is fed scrambled eggs or omelettes.
