hero of Tanagra in Greek mythology

Eunostus

Eunostus was a hero of the Boeotian city of Tanagra, honoured in a sacred precinct from which women were barred. His story, told mainly by Plutarch, combines unrequited love, a false accusation and a family tragedy.

The myth

According to tradition, Eunostus was the son of Elieus, who was a son of Cephissus, and of Scias. He was said to have been named after the nymph Eunosta, who reared him. He was a noble, just and austere young man.

Ochne, a daughter of Colonus and his cousin, fell in love with him. Eunostus rejected her and was about to report her to her brothers Echemus, Leon and Bucolus. Ochne forestalled him by claiming he had taken her by force. The brothers ambushed and killed him. Elieus imprisoned the killers, but Ochne, overcome with remorse, confessed the truth. Colonus exiled his sons, and Ochne threw herself off a height, as the poet Myrtis of Anthedon sang.

Worship and religion

Tanagra kept a sanctuary and sacred precinct for the hero, which no woman could enter. Plutarch notes that this rule held even in emergencies such as earthquakes; during droughts or other omens, the Tanagrans feared a woman might have slipped in and searched carefully for signs of it.

Ancient sources: Plutarch recounts his death and mentions the poet Myrtis of Anthedon; Diocles, in his compendium on hero shrines, records a Tanagran decree about the matter.

Trivia

A forbidden precinct. Plutarch says the ban on women entering held even in emergencies such as earthquakes.
A name with meaning. His name is linked to the nymph Eunosta, who according to tradition raised him.
