legendary king of Hindu tradition

Yayati

Yayati is a legendary king of Hindu tradition, regarded as an ancestor of the Kuru and Yadu dynasties of the lunar lineage. His story, which mixes desire, power and penance, is one of the best-known episodes of the Mahabharata.

The myth

According to the myth, Yayati was the son of King Nahusha, who had taken Indra's throne before being cursed to become a serpent. Yayati ruled the world and was a great scholar of the four Vedas. He married Devayani, daughter of the priest Shukra, and, without her knowledge, also took Sharmishtha, daughter of the asura king Vrishaparvan, as a second wife.

When Devayani discovered the affair and returned to her father's house, Shukra cursed the king to lose his youth at once. The only remedy was for one of his sons to take on his old age in exchange. One by one his sons refused; only Puru agreed. Yayati enjoyed his youth again for a time, and Puru became his successor.

Later Yayati realised the emptiness of his excesses, returned Puru's youth and withdrew with him to the forest to do penance. The tradition says he attained wisdom and heaven. Another account has him expelled from Indra's heaven for boasting of his sacrifices, and saved afterwards by his grandsons, who shared their merit so he could return to glory.

Legacy

In present-day India, taking advantage of others' youth is known as 'Yayati syndrome'. In 1978 V. S. Khandekar published the award-winning historical novel Yayati, based on this legend from the Mahabharata.

Ancient sources: The Rig-veda hymns attributed to him, the Mahabharata (Adi Parva), the Bhagavata and Matsya Puranas, and the Harivamsa.

Trivia

Two lineages. From Iadu descended the Yadava clan and from Puru the Puru clan, both of the lunar race.
Vedic hymns. He is credited with hymns 4 to 6 of chapter 101 of Mandala 9 of the Rig-veda, placing him among the earliest authors of the Indian tradition.
