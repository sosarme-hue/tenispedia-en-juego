Greek Hora of dawn

Anatole

Anatole is one of the Horae of Greek mythology, the figure who presides over the hour of dawn. Her name means "eastern," referring to the direction where the sun rises.

The myth

According to tradition, Anatole was the second of the Horae, a group of twelve sisters who each oversaw a part of the daily cycle. Her siblings included Auge, First Light; Musica, the Hour of Music; Gymnastica, the Hour of Exercise; Nymphe, the Hour of Bath; Mesembria, Noon; Sponde, Libation; Elete, the Hour of Prayer; Acte, the Hour of Pleasure; Hesperis, Evening; Dysis, Sunset; and Arctos, the Night Sky.

Accounts differ on their father: some name Helios, the Sun, and others Chronos, Time. Anatole, along with Dysis, Mesembria and Arctos, attended the goddess Harmonia in her halls in heaven. Like her sisters, she was assigned to one of the four gates of the Winds.

Trivia

An eastern name. Also called Anatolia or Antolia, her name in Greek means "eastern," referring to the place where the sun rises.
Guardians of the winds. Like her sisters, Anatole was assigned to one of the four gates of the Winds.
