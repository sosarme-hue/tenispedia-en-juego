the celestial bow of Shiva

Pinaka

The Pinaka is the celestial bow of the Hindu god Shiva. It is a mythical weapon tied to some of his feats and to several of his epithets.

The myth

In popular legend, Shiva used this bow in his avatar as Tripurantaka to destroy the three cities of Tripura, built by the asura Mayasura. One of his epithets, Pinākapāṇi, "the wielder of the Pinaka", comes from it. The oldest Vedic texts, the Taittirīya and Vājasneyi Samhitas of the Yajurveda, already mention it, calling Rudra Pinākahasta, the one holding the bow, and Pinākavāsa, the one who wears it as an ornament.

In the Shiva Purana, Shiva used it in a duel against Ganesha, who was guarding the entrance while Parvati bathed. In the Harivamsa, Shiva and a human incarnation of Nandi wielded it to disrupt the sacrifice of Daksha, and Hari (Vishnu) confronted them both. In the Padma Purana, the bow pitted Shiva against Krishna, whose bow Sharanga prevailed in the battle of Sonitapura. According to Valmiki's Ramayana, the hero Rama received it from the sage Vishvamitra during his exile.

Ancient sources: The Yajurveda (Taittirīya and Vājasneyi Samhitas), Valmiki's Ramayana, the Harivamsa, the Shiva Purana and the Padma Purana.

Trivia

A bow with its own name. Pinaka is the name of Shiva's bow and the basis of one of his epithets, which literally means "the wielder of the Pinaka".
A weapon passed from hand to hand. According to Valmiki's Ramayana, the bow reached Rama from the sage Vishvamitra, making it an object that changes owners within the epic.
