king of Olenus in Greek mythology

Dexamenus

Dexamenus is a figure of Greek mythology, the king of Olenus in Achaea, best known for his link to Heracles. His story survives in several versions, and his name is also tied to a son of the hero and to a region of Ambracia.

The myth

According to tradition, Dexamenus ruled Olenus and welcomed Heracles as a guest. When the hero returned after cleaning the stables of Augeas, Dexamenus asked for his help, because his daughter Mnesimache was about to be forced into marriage with the centaur Eurytion. Heracles killed the centaur.

The stories vary. In one version the young woman was Deianira, whom Heracles had promised to marry after taking her virginity; after Eurytion's death, he carried her off. In another she was Hippolyte, attacked during the wedding feast with Azan, and once again Heracles killed the centaur. Other texts make him a son of Mesolus and Ambracia, and father of Ambrax, who ruled Ambracia, where his arrival is still remembered.

Ancient sources: The playwright Iophon gave one of his tragedies his name, and ancient tradition preserves his story and that of his children.

Trivia

A region named after him. A part of the region of Ambracia was called Dexamenas, in his honor.
A lost tragedy. The playwright Iophon wrote a tragedy titled Dexamenus.
