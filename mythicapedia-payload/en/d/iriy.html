place of bliss in Slavic mythology

Iriy

Iriy (also called Vyrai or Irij) is a place of exceptional happiness in Slavic mythology, sometimes identified with paradise. It is one of the oldest Slavic ideas about the afterlife.

The myth

According to the stories, Vyrai lay far away, beyond the sea, at the end of the Milky Way. Early Slavs believed there was only one Vyrai, linked to the deity Rod. It was imagined as a garden behind an iron gate that barred the living, set in the crown of the cosmic tree, where birds nested, and the birds were identified with human souls. Its gates were guarded by Veles, who sometimes took the form of a raróg and held the keys to the otherworlds in its claws.

According to some folk tales, the soul left the earth for Vyrai during the cremation of the body on a pyre. It did not stay there forever, however. It returned to the womb of a pregnant woman, carried by a stork or a nightjar, which is why storks were said to bring souls to earth.

Worship and religion

Pagan Slavic peoples linked the cremation of the dead with the soul's departure toward this place.

Legacy

After the Christianization of Kievan Rus' and Poland, the idea of Vyrai split in two: a heaven for birds and an underworld for snakes and dragons, analogous to the Christian hell.

Trivia

Storks carrying souls. In Slavic tradition, storks brought to earth the souls that had waited in Vyrai before birth.
Uncertain origin of the name. The name has been linked to Proto-Slavic rajъ or Persian rayí, but none of these theories is widely accepted.
