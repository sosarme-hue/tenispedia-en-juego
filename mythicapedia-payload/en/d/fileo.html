Elean prince, son of Augeas

Phyleus

Phyleus is an Elean prince and son of King Augeas, best known for testifying against his father in the dispute over the Augean Stables. His story links Heracles to the royal house of Elis and, through his son Meges, to the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the myth, Phyleus was the elder son of King Augeas of Elis. He married Eustyoche, and their son Meges later became a hero. Other traditions give Meges different mothers, such as Hagnete, Ctimene or Ctesimache, and one tradition says that Timandra, daughter of King Tyndareus of Sparta, had an affair with Phyleus and abandoned her husband Echemus. Phyleus was also remembered as the father of Eurydameia, who married Polyidus.

The key episode came during the fifth labour of Heracles, the cleaning of the Augean Stables. Augeas promised a tenth of his cattle if the hero finished in a single day, and Phyleus witnessed the oath. When Heracles completed the task and the king refused to pay, Phyleus testified at the hearing and described the bargain as it had really happened. Augeas, who had expected his son's support, was furious. He exiled both Phyleus and Heracles, arguing that the rivers Alpheus and Peneus had done the work.

Phyleus went to Dulichium. In one version, Heracles returned with an army gathered across the Peloponnese, defeated Augeas, and restored Phyleus to his father's throne as a reward for his testimony. Pausanias, however, reports that Heracles spared Augeas and restored him to the throne, while Phyleus settled permanently in Dulichium. There he led a colony of Epeans, and his son Meges later led the Dulichian contingent to the Trojan War.

Ancient sources: Pausanias gives the version in which Heracles spares Augeas and leaves Phyleus settled in Dulichium; the other details about his family come from ancient traditions recorded by classical authors.

Trivia

A decisive oath. Phyleus witnessed the oath between Augeas and Heracles, and that role is what led him to testify at the hearing.
Disputed mothers. Ancient sources do not agree on Meges's mother, and Eustyoche, Hagnete, Ctimene and Ctesimache are all named.
