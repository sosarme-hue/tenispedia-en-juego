Greek nymph linked to the Corinth fountain

Pirene

Pirene is a nymph of Greek mythology, associated with a fountain in Corinth. Her story connects her to Poseidon, Pegasus and the Muses, and explains the origin of the spring that bears her name.

The myth

In different traditions, Pirene was the daughter of the river god Asopus and Metope, although other accounts make her the daughter of the river god Achelous or of Oebalus, king of Laconia. Poseidon became her lover, and from their union were born two sons, Lecheas and Cenchrias, who gave their names to the Corinthian harbours of Lechaeum and Cenchreae.

When Cenchrias was unintentionally killed by Artemis, Pirene grieved so deeply that she became nothing but tears and turned into a fountain outside the gates of Corinth. There, according to the legend, Bellerophon found Pegasus drinking from its waters and tamed him after many hardships, with the help of a bridle given by Athena. The Corinthians were proud enough to claim that the city of Pirene was Corinth itself.

Worship and religion

Beside the fountain stood a small sanctuary dedicated to Pirene, and the Corinthians also had an image of Apollo near it. In the dry months of early summer, honey-cakes were offered to her. The fountain was sacred to the Muses. There is no evidence confirming whether this cult survived to the present day.

Trivia

Cakes for a nymph. During the dry months of early summer, honey-cakes were offered to her at the small sanctuary beside the fountain.
A harbour named after a son. Her two sons by Poseidon gave their names to the Corinthian harbours of Lechaeum and Cenchreae.
