celestial abode of Shiva in Hinduism

Kailasha

Kailasha is the celestial abode of the Hindu god Shiva, a mountain where he lives with Parvati and their children Ganesha and Kartikeya. It is identified with Mount Kailash in western Tibet, one of Hinduism's most revered sacred sites.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Shiva and Parvati discuss Hindu philosophy on Kailasha, and the gods gather there to watch Shiva dance the cosmic dance as Nataraja. The mountain is guarded by ganas led by Nandi, Shiva's vahana. The Mahabharata places it in the Himavat range, and tells how the Pandavas tried to reach its summit to enter heaven; only Yudhishthira, accompanied by a dog, succeeded.

In the Ramayana, Ravana tried to uproot the mountain, and Shiva trapped him by pressing down on it with his big toe. The Vishnu Purana describes Kailasha as the pillar of the world, at the centre of six mountain ranges shaped like a lotus, with four lakes and four rivers that flow from the Ganges.

Worship and religion

Hindu pilgrims undertake the yatra, which includes the trek to Lake Manasarovar and a clockwise circumambulation of the mountain on foot, a practice believed to bring merit and purification. The pilgrimage remains alive today.

Ancient sources: The Puranas, the Mahabharata, the Ramayana, and Kālidāsa's Kumārasambhava and Meghadūta describe Kailasha.
