Vedic artisan god

Tvashtr

Tvashtr, also written Tvashta, is a Vedic Hindu artisan god and fashioner. He is the earliest craftsman of the divine world, and his role was later partly absorbed by other deities, especially Vishvakarma.

The myth

In the Rigveda, Tvashtr is a skilled craftsman who made famous objects, including Indra's bolt and a cup for divine food and drink. He is described as the father of Saranyu, mother of the twins Yama and Yami, and of Vishvarupa, the three-headed son. Indra killed Vishvarupa, and in revenge Tvashtr created the demon Vritra. In later Brahmanas, Tvashtr made a mispronunciation in the incantation that brought Vritra into being, and this error let Indra defeat him.

Their relationship was strained. According to the Taittiriya Samhita and the Brahmanas, Tvashtr refused to let Indra attend his soma sacrifice, and Indra drank the soma anyway. Some texts even call Tvashtr Indra's father. In the Mahabharata and the Harivamsa he appears as a solar deity, son of Kashyapa and Aditi, said to have made the worlds from pieces of the Sun.

Worship and religion

He was invoked to ask for offspring, and was called garbha-pati, lord of the womb. The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to him.

Legacy

His name lives on in Tvashtar Paterae, a volcanic region on Io, one of Jupiter's moons.

Ancient sources: Rigveda, Taittiriya Samhita, Brahmanas and Mahabharata.

Trivia

A mispronounced spell. According to the Brahmanas, the mistake in the incantation used to create Vritra is what allowed Indra to defeat the dragon.
A volcano named after him. The volcanic region Tvashtar Paterae on Io takes its name from this Vedic artisan god.
