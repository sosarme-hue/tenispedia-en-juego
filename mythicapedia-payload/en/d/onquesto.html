Boeotian hero, son of Poseidon

Onchestus

Onchestus is a hero of Boeotia in Greek myth, the eponymous founder of the city that bears his name and, according to the stories, a son of Poseidon. He matters mainly because of the sanctuary the city dedicated to the god.

The myth

Very little is known about him. Only two ancient writers, Plutarch in his Greek Questions and Pausanias in his Description of Greece, give him any real role. In their accounts he is a Boeotian son of Poseidon and the father of Megareus and of Abrota, wife of King Nisos.

Onchestus's son and son-in-law are listed as kings of Megara. Some traditions instead call him a son of Boeotus, a sign that the stories did not always agree.

Worship and religion

The city of Onchestus was home to a famous sanctuary of Poseidon, with a temple and a statue of the god worshipped under the epithet Onchestian.

Legacy

His name remained tied to the city and to the god's sacred grove, which ancient texts mention often as one of the best-known places in the region.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions the grove of Poseidon in the Catalogue of Ships in the Iliad, and it also appears in one of the Homeric Hymns to Apollo. Plutarch and Pausanias are the sources that describe his genealogy.

Trivia

A famous grove. Homer calls Poseidon's grove at Onchestus 'bright' in the Catalogue of Ships in the Iliad.
A royal line in Megara. His son and son-in-law are listed as kings of Megara in the accounts preserved by ancient texts.
