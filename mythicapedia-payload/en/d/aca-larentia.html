foster mother of Romulus and Remus in Roman mythology

Acca Larentia

Acca Larentia is a figure of Roman mythology who, in one tradition, was the wife of the shepherd Faustulus and the foster mother of the twin founders, and who was later worshipped as a goddess of fertility. Her figure is linked to the Lares and the Arval Brothers, so she holds an important place in Roman religion.

The myth

Accounts of Acca Larentia contradict one another. One tradition presents her as the wife of the shepherd Faustulus, who raised Romulus and Remus after rescuing them from the Tiber. She had twelve sons; when one died, Romulus took his place, and with the other eleven he founded the Arval Brothers, identified with the goddess Dea Dia.

Another version, recorded by Licinius Macer, tells of a beautiful courtesan in the time of Ancus Marcius, won by Hercules in a game of dice. Married to the wealthy Etruscan Tarutius, she inherited his fortune and bequeathed it to the Roman people. After her death she was buried in the Velabrum.

A third tradition identifies her with the lupa, a Latin word meaning both 'she-wolf' and 'prostitute', said to have nursed the twins.

Worship and religion

Her festival, the Larentalia, was held on 23 December, when sacrifices were offered to the Lares. According to tradition, Ancus Marcius established it in gratitude for her generosity.

Legacy

Scholar T. P. Wiseman links her figure to Lara and Larunda. She also appears as a character in David Drake's story To Bring the Light, set at the beginnings of Rome.

Ancient sources: Licinius Macer and Plutarch record the different versions of her story.

Trivia

Twelve sons. In one tradition she had twelve sons, the same number as the country Lares, a link that reinforces her connection to their cult.
A word with two meanings. Latin lupa means 'she-wolf' but colloquially also 'prostitute', which feeds the tradition of the she-wolf who nursed the twins.
