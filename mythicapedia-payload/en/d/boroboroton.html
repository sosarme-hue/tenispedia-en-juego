tattered futon yōkai of Japanese folklore

Boroboroton

The boroboroton is a tsukumogami, a yōkai born from an everyday object: a tattered futon that comes alive at night. It is one of the most feared spirits of Japanese household life.

The myth

According to Japanese tradition, the boroboroton is a tattered futon that, when night falls, rises into the air and throws its former owner out of bed. It then coils around the head and neck of the sleeper, intending to strangle them.

Its fury comes from neglect. When it feels ignored or unneeded, the object wakes up and, out of spite, drifts through the rooms of the house at night looking for anyone asleep. At other times it prefers to join other tsukumogami for noisy parties, or it leaves the house to wander in search of companions.

Trivia

What a tsukumogami is. These are yōkai born from everyday objects, such as kitchenware or tools, that come alive through possession by spirits or through constant use for around 90 to 100 years.
Objects that look new. Tsukumogami that arise from long use often appear new or in pristine condition despite their age.
