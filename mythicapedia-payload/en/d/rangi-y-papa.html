primordial couple in Māori mythology

Rangi and Papa

Rangi and Papa are the primordial couple of Māori mythology in Aotearoa New Zealand: the Sky Father and the Earth Mother, whose union and later separation bring the world and their descendants into being, among them gods such as Tāne and Tāwhirimātea.

The myth

According to tradition, Ranginui and Papatūānuku lay locked in a tight embrace, so their children lived in the cramped darkness between them. When they longed to see light, Tūmatauenga proposed killing the parents, but Tāne preferred to pry them apart. After the failed attempts of Rongo, Tangaroa and Haumia-tiketike, he lay on his back and pushed with his strong legs until they were separated. The Sky Father rose above and the Earth Mother stayed below, and the world of light appeared.

Tāwhirimātea, god of storms and winds, never forgave the separation. He gathered his children, winds and clouds, and attacked his brothers: Tāne's forests were flattened, Tangaroa's seas were churned, and his offspring, fish and reptiles, fled. Only Tūmatauenga stood firm, and in the story he ended up eating his brothers, except Tāwhirimātea, whose storms still lash humankind. Other traditions give the separating role to Tangaroa, and the tale adds that Rūaumoko, a child never born, still lives within the Earth Mother and causes earthquakes when he kicks.

Worship and religion

The sources do not describe temples or rites dedicated to Rangi and Papa. Their concept of Sky Father and Earth Mother is, however, deeply rooted in Polynesian religion: in Samoa, Hawaii, Tahiti and Rarotonga they had their own names and roles, such as Atea or Wakea, who represented the Sky Father.

Legacy

The tradition remains alive in Aotearoa: every rainfall is said to be Rangi crying for Papa, and the mist rising from the forests is the Earth Mother's sighs as she yearns for her partner. Their many names and epithets live on in the Māori language and in traditional chants.

Ancient sources: Traditional Māori chants and oral accounts from the Te Arawa and Taranaki tribes; the sources do not cite ancient written works.

Trivia

Rain as tears. According to tradition, each rainfall is Rangi crying for Papa, from whom his children have parted him.
The child never born. Rūaumoko, god of earthquakes and volcanoes, was never born and still lives inside the Earth Mother.
