one of the nine daughters of Ægir and Rán, wave goddess in Norse mythology

Dúfa

Dúfa is one of the nine daughters of Ægir and Rán, personifications of the waves of the sea in Norse mythology. Her name belongs to a set of poetic terms for ocean waves.

The myth

In Norse tradition, Rán, goddess of the sea, and Ægir, a jötunn who personifies the sea, had nine daughters who embodied the waves. Their names are poetic words for waves, and the sources remember them mainly as a group rather than as characters with individual stories.

The Poetic Edda mentions them in the poem Helgakviða Hundingsbana I, where the hero Helgi sails through raging seas and Ægir's daughters batter his ships. The Prose Edda lists the nine sisters in Skáldskaparmál, with a small variation: Dröfn is replaced by Bára.

Some scholars have identified these sisters with the nine mothers of Heimdallr, although the idea is debated because the names do not match.

Ancient sources: Appears in the Poetic Edda (Helgakviða Hundingsbana I) and the Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál).

Trivia

A sea vocabulary. The names of Ægir and Rán's daughters are poetic terms for waves, so the list works almost as a glossary of the sea.
Sisters of Heimdallr?. Some scholars believe Ægir and Rán's daughters and Heimdallr's mothers are the same group, which would make the god born from the waves.
