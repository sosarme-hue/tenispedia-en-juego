Greek god of toil and exertion

Ponos

Ponos is the Greek god of toil, exertion and hard labour, personified as a son of Eris, Strife. He matters because he shows how the Greeks imagined the pain of work as a force with a divine face.

The myth

In Hesiod's Theogony, Ponos was born to Eris, Strife, with no father named. His mother, herself a daughter of Night, had a large family of personified abstractions, and Ponos was one of her siblings alongside Algos (pain), Limos (hunger), Lethe (forgetfulness) and Horkos (oath). Hesiod describes him as a 'painful' son, and his name carries the sense of weariness and effort.

Hesiod tells no deeds of Ponos. He is an abstraction that sums up how harmful strife can be. For Hesiod, toil is one of the evils that spring from discord, not a virtue in its own right.

Other accounts change the lineage. Some make him a child of Night and Erebus, the primordial darkness. Cicero gives the same parentage to the personification of the Latin word labor, which shows how the idea of work kept different genealogies in the two languages.

A later nuance follows. In a poem by Lucian, from the 2nd century AD, Ponos takes on a positive meaning: he leads people to a virtuous life. The figure who stands for an evil in Hesiod thus becomes a teacher of virtue.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Theogony (lines 226-232), where he is listed among the children of Eris.

Trivia

A work with a Latin genealogy. Cicero gives a similar parentage, from Erebus and Night, to the personification of the Latin concept labor, suggesting a parallel between the two traditions.
From evil to virtue. In a 2nd-century AD poem by Lucian, the toil that is an evil in Hesiod appears as a path to a virtuous life.
