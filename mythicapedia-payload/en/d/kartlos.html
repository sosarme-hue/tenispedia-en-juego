legendary progenitor of the Georgians

Kartlos

In Georgian tradition, Kartlos is the legendary progenitor of all Georgians and the founder of Kartli, known in classical antiquity as the Kingdom of Iberia. His story, which dates from the medieval period, explains the Georgians' kinship with other Caucasian peoples and with figures from the Old Testament.

The myth

According to the tale, Kartlos was a descendant of Japheth, son of Targamos (Togarmah), who took possession of the Caucasus after the collapse of the Tower of Babel and divided his lands among eight principal sons. Kartlos was one of them and had seven brothers, among them Haos, patriarch of the Armenians, Lekos, of the Lezgins, and Egros, of the Egrisians.

Kartlos united his people, became their chieftain and founded the country of Kartli. Together with his brothers he took part in a war to free himself from the rule of Nebrot (Nimrod), a giant and Titan king. He later had several sons, such as Mtskhetos, Gardabos, Kakhos and Kukhos, who gave their names to places such as Mtskheta, Gardabani, Kakheti and Javakheti.

Legacy

Modern historians have read the myth in different ways. Ivane Javakhishvili saw it as a political tool of the Georgian rulers to justify unification, while Nikoloz Berdzenishvili considered it an extension of the Togarma tradition passed on by Moses of Khorene. W.E.D. Allen linked it to the ancient cult of Armazi, the chief god of the pre-Christian pantheon, and to the veneration of Saint George.

Ancient sources: The Georgian Chronicles, where he first appears around the 11th century, are the main source; medieval Jewish texts mention Togarmah and his sons without connecting them to Kartlos.

Trivia

An ignored ancestor. The Moktsevay Kartlisay chronicle and the cleric Arsen of Iqalto actively ignored and omitted him, alongside the Ancient Hebrews.
Togarmah in Jewish tradition. Medieval Jewish texts such as the Josippon and the Sefer haYashar mention Togarmah and his sons, but do not link them to the Caucasian peoples or to Kartlos.
