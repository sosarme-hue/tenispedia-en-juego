Yavana king in Hindu scripture

Kalayavana

Kalayavana is a king in the Hindu tradition, leader of the Yavanas, who marched on Mathura with an enormous army to confront Krishna. His story appears in several Sanskrit texts and ends in a cave, where a fatal mistake costs him his life.

The myth

According to the Brahmanda Purana, Kalayavana was born out of a cruel joke. A woman called Vrukkka-Devi wanted to test the virility of Gargya, a sage descended from Garga, who could not ejaculate; the Yadavas laughed at him and insulted him. Gargya then performed a twelve-year penance, during which he ate iron filings that darkened his skin to iron-black, kāla. Mahadeva granted him a son able to destroy the Yadavas. Gargya went to Mathura, lay in disguise with an apsara of Indra, and from that union Kalayavana was born. Gargya crowned him king of the Yavanas and retired to the forest.

The Vishnu Purana and the Harivamsa describe him as a partial incarnation of Krodha, anger. Jarasandha, Kamsa's father-in-law and ruler of Magadha, attacked Mathura seventeen times and was defeated by Krishna each time. Unable to beat Krishna alone, Jarasandha allied with Kalayavana, who had received a boon from Shiva making him unbeatable on the battlefield. Krishna moved the people of Mathura to the city of Dvaraka. When Kalayavana marched on Mathura with thirty million Yavanas, Krishna fled and led him to a cave where Muchukunda, a king of the Treta Yuga and ancestor of Rama, lay asleep after helping the devas against the asuras. Muchukunda had obtained a boon from Indra that anyone who woke him would be reduced to ashes. Krishna covered Muchukunda with his shawl; Kalayavana, mistaking him for Krishna, kicked him, woke him, and was burned to ashes. Muchukunda, seeing Krishna, was advised to perform penance to attain liberation, moksha, which he achieved at Badrikashrama.

Legacy

In local tradition, the hills and the cave where Muchukunda rested are placed at Ranchodji teerth, in Lalitpur district, Uttar Pradesh, India.

Ancient sources: The Brahmanda Purana, the Vishnu Purana and the Harivamsa recount his origin and story.

Trivia

Ashes from a kick. Kalayavana died after kicking a sleeping man he believed was Krishna. Muchukunda's sleep was protected by a boon from Indra that turned anyone who woke him to ashes.
Iron skin. According to the story, Gargya turned iron-black by eating iron filings during his penance, and this gave his son the name kāla.
