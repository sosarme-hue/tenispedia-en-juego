sign of the Chinese zodiac, twelfth and last animal

Pig

The Pig is the twelfth and last animal of the Chinese zodiac, the sign linked to the Earthly Branch hai (亥). In the Chinese calendar it closes the twelve-year cycle and remains a familiar part of popular horoscopes.

The myth

According to legend, the Jade Emperor summoned all the animals to a great gathering, and the Pig was the last to arrive. Another version credits Buddha with the summons, made just before he left the Earth, with the same outcome. A third tradition tells of a race the Emperor organised to fix the order of the animals: the Pig, far behind, was about to give up when an oink and a squeal were heard. It had stopped for a feast and fallen asleep, but it finished the race and took the last place, which gave rise to the phrase lazy pig.

Other accounts blame its stout body: as a poor swimmer, it could not keep up with the others or, conversely, it was so fast that it took the wrong side of the river. As a yin sign, the Pig takes only the negative aspects of the five elements, so there are five kinds: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water. According to tradition, its hour is 9 to 11 p.m., when it sleeps and enjoys the good life.

Legacy

The zodiac has also inspired lunar new year stamps: for the 2019 Year of the Pig, the USC U.S.-China Institute collected stamps from 56 jurisdictions. In Malaysia, a Muslim-majority country, businesses avoid pig imagery to avoid controversy, although no law requires it. Some Chinese Muslims say they were born in the year of the hai to avoid saying the word pig, since pork is forbidden in Islam.

Trivia

A year in five variants. Each sixty-year cycle includes five Pig years, one for each element: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water.
Pig or boar. In China the animal is called pig or boar, while in Japan the word for pig and the word for boar are distinguished.
