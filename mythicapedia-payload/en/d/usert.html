minor Egyptian goddess of Thebes

Wosret

Wosret, also written Waset or Wosyet, meaning "the powerful female one", was an Egyptian goddess whose cult centred on Thebes in Upper Egypt. She was a minor deity, yet her name gave rise to the royal names of several pharaohs of the Twelfth Dynasty.

The myth

Wosret appears early in the Amduat, where she is linked to the was sceptre, the symbol of power that also gave her name, and to one of the forms of the Eye of Horus. She is considered a possible first wife of Amun at Thebes, though no extended narrative about her survives.

Her role as protector of the king was so prominent that she watched over expeditions to Sinai in the Twelfth Dynasty. Later her functions passed to Mut, and some scholars think Mut may simply be a later name for her. She was usually shown wearing a tall crown with the was sceptre, sometimes as a lioness or a woman with a lioness head, and she could carry weapons such as spears and a bow.

Worship and religion

Her cult was centred on Thebes, where she appears in the temple of Luxor as the wife of Amun. Depending on the locality she was linked with other goddesses: with Neith at Hermonthis, with Maat at Medamud and with Tait at Tod, and she was also regarded as a form of Isis and Sekhmet. No temples to her have been identified, and her cult did not survive as a living practice.

Legacy

Her name lives on in those of the pharaohs Senusret I, Senusret II and Senusret III, meaning "man of Wosret". The Greek writers called Senusret III Sesostris, a form of the name that entered the later tradition.

Ancient sources: The Amduat is the Egyptian text that mentions her as a symbol of the was sceptre and a form of the Eye of Horus.

Trivia

A name shared by kings. Three pharaohs of the Twelfth Dynasty took her name in the form Senusret, and one ruler of the Second Intermediate Period also incorporated it.
The girl next door. Egyptologist John Ray called her "the theological equivalent of the girl next door", a description that reflects her modest place in the pantheon.
