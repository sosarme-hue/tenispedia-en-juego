Chinese god of death and ruler of the underworld

King Yan

King Yan, also called Yanluo Wang, is the god of death and ruler of Diyu, the Chinese underworld. He judges the dead and decides whether they receive reward or punishment before their next existence, which makes him a central figure in Chinese popular religion.

The myth

According to tradition, King Yan rules Diyu from its capital, Youdu, and oversees the Ten Kings of Hell. His name comes from a transcription of the Sanskrit Yama Rāja, "King Yama", and he is the fifth judge of the underworld court. He is usually portrayed as a burly man with a scowling face, bulging eyes and a long beard, dressed in robes and a judge's cap or a crown bearing the Chinese character for "king".

The classic novel Journey to the West features him in one of its best-known episodes. After a night of drinking, Sun Wukong dreams that Ox-Head and Horse-Face have come to take his soul; furious, he storms the Court of Hell. When he meets King Yan, the king replies that the arrest must have been a mistake caused by people sharing the same name. Sun Wukong then erases his name from the Book of Life and Death, and the Jade Emperor orders his capture.

In his judgments, merit is rewarded with a good future life or even revival in the previous one, while misdeeds bring suffering or miserable lives to come. Some versions divide Diyu into eight, ten or eighteen courts; in the ten-king version, each king governs one hell, such as King Chujiang, who rules over thieves and murderers. After judgment, spirits enjoy a period of rest or endure punishment, but neither fate is permanent, and they return to Earth in new bodies.

Worship and religion

In Chinese popular religion, King Yan is a prominent figure in beliefs about death. Families offer him hell money, on which he appears in the place that political figures occupy on ordinary currency. His interpretation varies widely: some regard him as a Buddhist deity, others as a Daoist counterpart of the Bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha.

Legacy

He appears in the classic Chinese novel Journey to the West, and his episode with Sun Wukong is among the most remembered. His image remains widespread in popular iconography, especially on hell money.
