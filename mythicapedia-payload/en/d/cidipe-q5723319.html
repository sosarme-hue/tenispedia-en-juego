Athenian maiden in Greek myth, wife of Acontius

Cydippe

Cydippe is an Athenian maiden in Greek myth, at the centre of a love story bound by an oath she never meant to make. Her tale, passed down by Callimachus and Ovid, shows how a promise spoken before an altar could bind a person for life.

The myth

According to the tradition, Acontius, a young man from Ceos, met Cydippe on Delos while she was making sacrifices to Artemis. He threw an apple inscribed with the words «I swear I will marry Acontius». Cydippe picked it up and read the inscription aloud before the altar, and so she was bound by it.

Back in Athens, her father arranged a marriage with a wealthy nobleman, but the girl fell ill three times and the wedding could not take place. Worried, the father consulted the oracle at Delphi, which blamed the illnesses on Artemis, punishing his daughter for breaking her oath. In the end, Cydippe married Acontius.

Legacy

Her story reached later literature through the poems of Callimachus and Ovid's Heroides, where a love trick becomes material for poetry.

Ancient sources: Callimachus tells her story in his poem Aitia, and Ovid in the Heroides.

Trivia

An apple as a message. Acontius did not declare himself out loud. He wrote his oath on an apple and threw it to the girl during a sacrifice to Artemis.
Three illnesses. According to the story, Cydippe fell ill three times, each time just as her wedding to the Athenian nobleman was about to take place.
