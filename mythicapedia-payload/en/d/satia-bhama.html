wife of Krishna, goddess of Hindu mythology

Satyabhama

Satyabhama is the third wife of Krishna in Hindu mythology. Many traditions regard her as an incarnation of Lakshmi, Vishnu's consort, while others identify her with Bhumi, the goddess of the earth. She is remembered for her proud, jealous temperament and for fighting beside her husband against the demon Narakasura.

The myth

According to the legend, she was the daughter of Satrajit, a warrior who had received the Syamantaka jewel from the sun-god Surya. When Krishna asked for the gem, Satrajit refused. Soon afterwards, his brother Prasena was killed by a lion while wearing the jewel, and Krishna was accused of the murder. To clear his name, Krishna tracked the jewel to Jambavan, the ancient figure who had slain the lion. After a fight lasting twenty-eight days, Jambavan recognised Krishna as the god Rama and returned the gem. Repentant, Satrajit offered the jewel and his daughter Satyabhama in marriage. Krishna accepted her as his wife but refused the jewel.

In another episode, the demon Narakasura had abducted sixteen thousand women and seized the earrings of Aditi. Satyabhama joined Krishna in battle. When he was wounded and fell unconscious, she fought with renewed fury and slew the demon. In the folk tale of the tulabharam, the sage Narada persuades her to weigh Krishna against her gold and jewels. Only a single tulsi leaf offered by Rukmini balances the scales.

Worship and religion

Narakasura's defeat is commemorated every year on Naraka Chaturdashi with feasts and fireworks. The tulabharam is a folk tale from Odisha that does not appear in the major scriptures about Krishna's life.

Legacy

In Jain literature she appears as Krishna's chief queen, and in the Pradyumna narratives her pride fuels rivalries with Rukmini.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Harivamsa, the Puranas and the Bhagavata Purana, which portrays her as passionate, rebellious and deeply jealous.

Trivia

A weighty offering. In the tulabharam, the scales do not move under all the wives' gold. Only a small tulsi leaf, offered with devotion, balances them.
A festival of lights. The victory over Naraka is celebrated each year with feasts and fireworks during Naraka Chaturdasi.
