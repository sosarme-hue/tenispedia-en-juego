monstrous being of Maya mythology

Zipacna

Zipacna is a giant monstrous being in Maya mythology, son of Vucub Caquix and brother of Cabrakan. His story, recorded in the Popol Vuh, mixes arrogance, deceit and a revenge carried out by the divine twins.

The myth

According to the Popol Vuh, Zipacna was a huge caiman who boasted that he had created the mountains. One day, while basking on the beach, he was disturbed by four hundred boys trying to raise the post of their hut. He lifted it with ease, but his rivals, fearing such strength, plotted to kill him by having him dig a hole where they planned to drop the massive column.

Zipacna escaped by digging a side tunnel and faked his death. Three days later he brought the house down on the sleeping boys, who all died and, in the telling, became the Pleiades. The Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque sought revenge: they hid a fake crab in a canyon, and Zipacna, starving, fell into the trap. A mountain collapsed on top of him.

Legacy

He appears as a Goa'uld in the television series Stargate SG-1, as a hostile monster in the video game Final Fantasy XI, and as the chief antagonist in Louis L'Amour's novel The Haunted Mesa. He also appears, spelled Sipacna, in J. C. Cervantes's Storm Runner series.

Ancient sources: The Popol Vuh recounts his story and that of the Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque.

Trivia

The boys in the sky. According to the Popol Vuh, the four hundred boys were placed in the sky as the Pleiades, a star cluster visible to the naked eye.
A sexual reading. Many scholars see Zipacna's entry into the crab as a sexual metaphor, and some argue that desire rather than hunger brought about his downfall.
