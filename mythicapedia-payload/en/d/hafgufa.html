sea monster of Norse mythology

Hafgufa

The hafgufa is a massive sea monster from Norse tradition, described as a giant fish as large as an island. Its name means "sea steam", and its image helped shape the legend of the kraken.

The myth

According to the Konungs skuggsjá, a Norwegian didactic work from the mid-13th century, the hafgufa is a fish so huge it looks like an island. It attracts prey by belching out a mass of food; when fish crowd into its mouth and belly, it snaps its jaws shut and devours them all at once. The king narrating the text believes only one or two of them must exist, or the seas would be full of them.

In the saga of Örvar-Oddr, from the late 14th century, the hafgufa appears alongside the lyngbakr off Greenland. The crew sails between what they take for two rocks, which are actually the nostril and lower jaw of the monster, rising briefly above the water as the tide turns.

Legacy

In the 2022 video game God of War Ragnarök, hafgufas are giant flying jellyfish that drift through the skies above the deserts of Alfheim.

Ancient sources: The Konungs skuggsjá (mid-13th century), the Örvar-Oddr saga and Snorri's Prose Edda, which lists it among the whales.

Trivia

A whale that traps fish. In 2023 scientists observed whales that stay still at the surface with their jaws open, waiting for fish to swim in. They propose this behaviour may have inspired the hafgufa myth.
An island that is a fish. In the legend of St Brendan, an island turns out to be a giant fish called Jasconius. Some 17th-century writers identified that beast with the hafgufa.
