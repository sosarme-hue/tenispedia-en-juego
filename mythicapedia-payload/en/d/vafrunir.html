wise giant of Norse mythology

Vafþrúðnir

Vafþrúðnir is a wise jötunn, or giant, in Norse mythology, known for his knowledge of the past, present and future of the world. He matters because he stars in one of the most famous contests of wisdom in Norse literature.

The myth

According to the Poetic Edda poem Vafþrúðnismál, Odin decides to visit this giant to test whether his reputation for wisdom is deserved. His wife Frigg warns him, since she knows no other giant equal to him in might. Odin travels in disguise, and the two face off in a contest of questions and answers about the beings and events of the Nine Worlds.

Vafþrúðnir opens by quizzing his guest about the horses that pull Day and Night across the sky, Skinfaxi and Hrimfaxi, and about Ymir, from whose body heaven and earth were formed. Odin answers correctly, then asks his own questions, about the origin of the Sun and Moon, children of the giant Mundilfari, or what will follow the end of the world. Both show extensive knowledge.

Finally Odin breaks the rules and asks what he whispered into the ear of his son Baldr before Baldr was burned on the funeral pyre. Only Odin himself could know that, and Vafþrúðnir recognizes his guest. He accepts defeat calmly and declares that Odin will always be wiser than the wisest.

Legacy

The same pattern of a riddle contest appears in the Hervarar saga, where King Heidrek faces Gestumblindi, again Odin in disguise, and the contest ends with the same question about Odin's last words to Baldr. In J. R. R. Tolkien's The Hobbit, the chapter «Riddles in the Dark», in which Bilbo confronts Gollum, follows a similar structure and ends with a question about what is in Bilbo's pocket.

Ancient sources: Vafþrúðnismál, a poem of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A name with two readings. His name is usually translated as «mighty weaver», though some interpret it as «mighty in riddles».
A question with no way out. Odin breaks the usual order of the contest with a question that only he can answer, a device later repeated in The Hobbit.
