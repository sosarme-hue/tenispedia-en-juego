golden land, legendary place in Southeast Asia in Indian and Buddhist texts

Suvarnabhumi

Suvarnabhumi, or "golden land", is the name of a distant place mentioned in Indian, Buddhist and Greek texts, located to the east of India. It matters because its location remains debated and several peoples of Southeast Asia claim it as their own.

The myth

Indian texts describe Suvarnabhumi as a land rich in gold reached by sea by merchants and monks. The Ramayana names it as Suvarnadvipa, "golden island", or Suvarnabhumi, "golden land". The Arthashastra tells how the brahmin Shanka sailed from Varanasi to it, and the Katha Kosha narrates how Naga Datta set out for it with five hundred ships to carry out a profitable trade.

In Buddhist tradition it appears in the Mahavamsa, in several Jataka stories and in the Milinda Panha. According to the Sinhalese chronicle, after the third Buddhist council the monks Sona and Uttara were sent to Suvannabhumi as missionaries.

For Greek and Roman geographers the land was linked to the island of Chryse and to the Golden Chersonese, mentioned by Ptolemy. Today specialists believe it was not a specific place but an idealised territory to which distant riches were attributed.

Legacy

The name has passed into the identity of several countries and cities in Southeast Asia, and Bangkok's international airport is called Suvarnabhumi. The debate over its origin has fuelled nationalist disputes between Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia and Indonesia.

Ancient sources: The Ramayana, the Arthashastra, the Katha Kosha, the Mahavamsa, the Milinda Panha and the Jataka tales, along with Greek and Latin texts such as the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea.

Trivia

An airport bearing its name. Bangkok's international airport, opened in 2006, is called Suvarnabhumi.
A name without a fixed place. Scholars agree it cannot be marked on a map; the term changed meaning over time.
