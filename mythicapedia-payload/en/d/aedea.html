Greek Muse of song

Aoide

Aoide, also called Aoede or Aede, is one of the Muses of Greek mythology, linked to song and sung poetry. Her name, meaning 'song' in Greek, ties her to the poetry that was recited and sung. In some accounts she is one of the three original Boeotian Muses, and she belongs to the group of the earliest Muses, whose number and names vary from one author to another.

The myth

Ancient accounts do not agree on how many Muses there were at first. According to Pausanias, the 2nd-century travel writer, the Aloadae considered them three and named them Melete, Mneme and Aoede, all placed on Mount Helicon. Later, the Macedonian Pierus is said to have replaced them with the nine Muses known today.

Cicero, by contrast, in On the Nature of the Gods, says the first Muses were four: Thelxinoe, Aoede, Arche and Melete. In this version they were born of a second Jupiter, so the genealogy clearly changed from one author to another. Aoede and Melete appear in both lists, but the others do not.

She is also presented as a daughter of Zeus, with sisters Meletea and Mnemea, though ancient authors do not agree on her parentage.

Legacy

Her name lives on in Aoede, one of the moons of Jupiter, also designated Jupiter XLI, which orbits the planet. The Muse's name thus reaches modern astronomy.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, in his 2nd-century AD description of Greece, and Cicero, in On the Nature of the Gods, mention Aoede among the earliest Muses.

Trivia

A moon of Jupiter. One of Jupiter's moons bears her name. It is called Aoede and is also known as Jupiter XLI.
Shifting names of the Muses. Pausanias and Cicero give different lists of the earliest Muses, and only Aoede and Melete appear in both.
