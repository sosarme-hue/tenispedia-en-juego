national emblem of Zimbabwe carved in soapstone

Zimbabwe Bird

The Zimbabwe Bird is a stone-carved emblem that gave modern Zimbabwe part of its identity, appearing on its flag and coat of arms. The original carvings come from the ruined city of Great Zimbabwe, built by ancestors of the Shona people, and their meaning remains debated.

The myth

The birds were found at Great Zimbabwe, a city built by the ancestors of the Shona from the 11th century and inhabited for over three hundred years. These soapstone sculptures stand about 40 centimetres tall on columns over 90 centimetres high, and were originally installed on the walls and monoliths of the city. No comparable sculptures have been found anywhere else.

Several explanations for their symbolism have been proposed. One suggests each bird marked a new king, but this would have required improbably long reigns. More probably they represent sacred or totemic animals of the Shona: the bateleur eagle (chapungu), held to be a messenger of Mwari, God, and the ancestors, or the fish eagle (hungwe), which may once have been the people's original totem.

In 1889 the European hunter Willi Posselt reached Great Zimbabwe and found one of the birds beside an apparent altar. He acquired it in exchange for blankets and other goods, but since it was too heavy to carry, he hacked it off and hid its pedestal, intending to return. He later sold the bird to Cecil Rhodes, who displayed it in his Cape Town house.

Legacy

The bird appears on the flag, coat of arms and coins of Zimbabwe and former Rhodesia, and has been a symbol of those states since 1924. A 2001 study counted more than one hundred state, corporate and sporting organisations that include it in their emblems. Today it is the most recognisable icon of the country.

Trivia

Stone replicas. Rhodes commissioned stone replicas three times the size of the original to decorate the gates of his house in England.
Late return. In April 2026 South Africa returned the last of Zimbabwe's soapstone birds, along with eight ancestral human remains.
