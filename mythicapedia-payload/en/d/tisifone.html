Erinys, avenging goddess of Greek mythology

Tisiphone

Tisiphone is one of the Erinyes, the avenging goddesses of Greek mythology who pursue murderers and those who commit bloody crimes. The Romans associated her with the Furies, and her name is usually interpreted as "avenger of murder" or "destructive wrath".

The myth

According to Hesiod, the Erinyes were born from the blood spilled when Uranus was castrated, which would make Uranus and Gaia their parents. Later sources make them daughters of Acheron and Nyx. Together with her sisters Alecto and Megaera, she dwells in the underworld and rises to earth to hunt the wicked.

Ovid describes her as covered in blood, carrying a torch and snakes, and accompanied by Pavor, Terror and Insania. Claudian and Statius stress her poisonous appearance, with fire in her mouth that brings sickness, famine and death to peoples.

In one story, Tisiphone fell in love with the handsome young Cithaeron, who tended his flocks on Mount Asterion. Spurned, she tore one of the serpents from her hair and set it upon him, and it coiled around his neck and strangled him. By the will of the gods, the mountain has been called Cithaeron ever since.

Legacy

Virgil places her as a guardian of the gates of Tartarus in the Aeneid, and Dante includes her in the Inferno, where she threatens to unveil Medusa before the gates of Dis. Chaucer calls upon her at the start of Troilus and Criseyde. In astronomy, the asteroid 466 Tisiphone bears her name, as does the fire ship HMS Tisiphone, launched in 1781.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Ovid, Virgil, Claudian and Statius, among other authors who mention her.
