vessel or mead of poetry in Norse mythology

Óðrerir

Óðrerir is, in Norse mythology, one of the vessels that held the mead of poetry, or the name of the mead itself. It appears in the Hávamál and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda.

The myth

According to Snorri Sturluson, the mead of poetry was born from the killing of Kvasir, a wise being who travelled the earth teaching people. Invited to the home of the dwarves Fjalar and Galar, he was killed by them, who let his blood run into two vats and a kettle. The kettle was called Óðrerir, and the vats Són and Boðn. Mixed with honey, the blood became a mead that gave whoever drank it the learning of a skald or scholar.

In skaldic poetry, however, Óðrerir is usually a synonym for the mead itself, so many scholars think the vessel is Snorri's invention. Its name, which may mean 'stirrer of inspiration', points to the drink rather than the container.

Ancient sources: It appears in two ambiguous passages of the Hávamál in the Poetic Edda, and in Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Skáldskaparmál.

Trivia

A name with several readings. The name can be understood as 'stirrer of inspiration' or 'stirrer of fury', which suggests it refers to the mead.
The names of the vats. Boðn probably means 'vessel' and Són 'reconciliation' or 'blood'. Another hypothesis, based on Danish words, gives Boðn the sense of 'penance'.
