Chinese mythological figure who tried to stop the Great Flood

Gun

Gun was, according to Chinese tradition, the ruler of the state of Chong tasked with halting the Great Flood, and the father of Yu the Great, founder of the Xia dynasty. His failure made him one of the Four Criminals.

The myth

According to the Bamboo Annals, during the era of the Youyu clan, a catastrophic flood struck the lands of the Taotang clan in the time of Emperor Yao. The Four Mountains recommended Gun to control the waters, although Yao distrusted him for his disobedient nature. For nine years Gun built dykes up to three ren high, yet he could not hold back the flood.

The accounts differ on his end. According to the Han Feizi, Emperor Shun had him executed after he protested against his appointment. Other traditions say he was imprisoned for life on Feather Mountain. The Classic of Mountains and Seas claims he stole xirang, a self-renewing soil, from the gods to raise his dykes; when they collapsed and many people died, Zhurong executed him. Wang Chong adds that his spirit became a yellow bear.

Worship and religion

The Book of Rites records that the Xia dynasty offered jiao sacrifices to Gun, as the man who received the Mandate of Heaven and passed it on to Yu. The sources do not say whether his cult survives today.

Ancient sources: The Bamboo Annals, the Book of Rites, the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the Lüshi chunqiu, the Han Feizi, Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian and the Chu Ci, where his figure is treated with more sympathy.

Trivia

An unusual name. The character used for his name is rarely found in personal names. In historical dictionaries it literally means «big fish».
A mythical fish. Schuessler links his name to a giant fish in the Zhuangzi, and Wolfram Eberhard suggests that descriptions of Gun as a «naked one» or «dark fish» fit the eel.
