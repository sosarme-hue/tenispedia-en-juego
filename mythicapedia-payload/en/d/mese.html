Muse of the middle string of Apollo's lyre

Mese

Mese is one of the three Delphic Muses of the lyre, personifying the middle string of Apollo's instrument in Greek mythology. Her note marks the centre of the scale.

The myth

According to tradition, Mese was one of three Muses linked to Apollo's lyre. Together with her sisters Nete and Hypate, she stood for one of its strings, and Greek compositions were believed to have often begun on her note.

Some authors also call her Apollonis and make the three sisters daughters of Apollo. Plutarch, in his Quaestiones Convivales, gave the Muses an astronomical role: the universe was divided into fixed stars, planets, and everything beneath the Moon, and Mese watched over the planets.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped at Delphi, where the Temple of Apollo and the Oracle were located, and the sisters were honoured together there.

Ancient sources: Plutarch, in his Quaestiones Convivales, gives her an astronomical role.

Trivia

An astronomical role. Plutarch gave these Muses a place in the order of the cosmos: Mese looked after the region of the planets.
Other names. Some authors give the three Muses other names, such as Cephisso, Apollonis and Borysthenis, and make them daughters of Apollo.
