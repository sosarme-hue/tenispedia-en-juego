Greek hero, eponym of the city of Lepreum

Lepreus

Lepreus is a hero of Greek myth, a son of Poseidon in one account and the eponymous founder of the city of Lepreum. He is remembered mainly for his rivalry with Heracles, which ended with his death.

The myth

According to the tradition, Lepreus advised King Augeas to bind Heracles rather than pay the reward promised for cleaning the stables, and the hero never forgave him. After completing his twelve labors, Heracles came to the house of Caucon, where Astydameia urged the two to reconcile. Lepreus proposed friendly contests: throwing the discus, drawing water, and an eating contest in which each had to consume a whole bull. He lost them all, and also a drinking contest. He then challenged Heracles to single combat and was killed in the match.

Pausanias, who mentions only the eating contest, says that Lepreus matched Heracles there. He was said to have been buried in Phigalia, though the locals could not show his tomb. He was also credited with founding Lepreum, although some thought the city was named after Leprea, daughter of Pyrgeus, or after the skin disease leprā that once struck its people.

Ancient sources: Pausanias records the eating contest, in which Lepreus matched Heracles, and other ancient accounts give the outcome of the final combat.

Trivia

An eating contest. In one of the contests, Lepreus and Heracles competed to see who could eat a whole bull faster. Pausanias is the source that says Lepreus matched the hero.
A name with several explanations. The city of Lepreum may take its name from Lepreus, from Leprea, daughter of Pyrgeus, or from the skin disease leprā that had struck its inhabitants.
