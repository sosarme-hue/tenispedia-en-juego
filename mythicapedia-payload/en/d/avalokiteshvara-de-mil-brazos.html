Thousand-Armed Avalokiteshvara

Avalokiteshvara is the bodhisattva of great compassion in Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism. His thousand-armed form, often with many eyes as well, is one of his most recognisable iconographic representations.

The myth

His name is usually understood as 'the lord who looks down', although Chinese translators read it as 'the one who perceives the world's lamentations'. According to Chapter 25 of the Lotus Sutra, he hears the cries of beings who suffer and comes to those who call his name, taking thirty-three manifestations, some of them female, to suit each being.

Other traditions hold that he is already a Buddha who, out of compassion, remained in the world as a bodhisattva.

Worship and religion

In Sri Lanka, where he is called Natha-deva, many Theravada Buddhists worship him alongside the Buddha in the shrine room, and most believe he is the future Buddha Maitreya. In China he is venerated as Guanyin, a female figure also honoured in Taoism and folk religion. His worship remains alive.

Legacy

His female form, Guanyin, is one of the most popular religious images in East Asia, and his mantra is the most widespread in Tibetan Buddhism.

Ancient sources: The Lotus Sutra (chapter 25, the Universal Gate of Bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara) and the Nīlakaṇṭha Dhāraṇī Sūtra, as well as the accounts of the travellers Faxian and Xuanzang.

Trivia

A name with several readings. When the name reached Chinese, translators such as Kumārajīva read it as 'the one who perceives the world's lamentations', a reading that helped shape the figure of Guanyin.
A future Buddha in Sri Lanka. Many Buddhists in Sri Lanka regard him as the future Buddha Maitreya, although his iconography, with an image of Amitabha on the crown, identifies him as Avalokiteshvara.
