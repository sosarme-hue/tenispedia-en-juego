king of the Taurians in Greek mythology

Thoas

Thoas is the king of the Taurians, a barbaric people of Crimea, in Greek mythology. He matters mainly for his role in the story of Iphigenia and Orestes.

The myth

According to tradition, Thoas was the son of Borysthenes, god of a great northern river that the Greeks knew by that name and that is now identified with the Dnieper. When Orestes and Pylades arrived in Tauris to take the statue of Artemis, the king had them seized and ordered them sacrificed at the goddess's temple. Iphigenia, Orestes' sister and a priestess of Artemis there, managed to save them.

In Euripides' play, Iphigenia persuades the king that Orestes must be purified in the sea because of his matricide. Thoas believes her and lets the young man go, but soon sends men in pursuit, until Athena intervenes and convinces him to let them leave. According to Hyginus, the king chased them to the island of Chryses, son of Chryseis and Agamemnon, who turned out to be the half brother of Orestes and Iphigenia, and Chryses killed him.

Ancient sources: Euripides in his play Iphigenia among the Taurians and Hyginus in his fables, as well as Antoninus Liberalis, who records a version by Nicander about his parentage.

Trivia

Two Thoases. Some authors identify this king with another Thoas, king of Lemnos, son of Dionysus and Ariadne and father of Hypsipyle.
A father of the Dnieper. The poet Nicander, of the 2nd century BC, made him a son of Borysthenes, god of the river now called the Dnieper.
