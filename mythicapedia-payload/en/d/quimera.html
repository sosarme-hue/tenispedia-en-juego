Chimera

The Chimera is a hybrid monster of Greek mythology, made of parts from several animals and famous for breathing fire. Its myth, set in Lycia in Asia Minor, made it a symbol of the hero's triumph over the monstrous, and its name now describes any creature built from parts of different beings.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, the Chimera was the offspring of Typhon and Echidna, although Hesiod leaves the mother ambiguous, possibly meaning the Lernaean Hydra or Ceto. Her siblings included Cerberus and the Hydra. She roamed Asia Minor terrifying settlements and devouring animals and entire herds. She was a single creature of great strength, in whom three animals were combined.

Accounts differ. Homer describes a lion at the front, a serpent at the rear and a goat in the middle, breathing a terrible blaze. Later versions show three heads, a lion, a goat rising from the back and a dragon or serpent at the tail, and some add a fourth head and wings. In certain tales, her union with Orthus produced the Sphinx and the Nemean lion.

King Iobates of Lycia ordered Bellerophon to kill her, hoping the monster would destroy him. The hero, mounted on the winged horse Pegasus, struck her down from above. The manner of her death varies: some say he pierced her with his spear, while others say the spearhead was coated in lead that melted in the Chimera's fiery breath.

Legacy

The word "chimera" now names any fantastic creature made of parts of different animals and, figuratively, an impossible idea or scheme. In medieval art, chimerical figures came to stand for hypocrisy and fraud, and Dante placed a monster with a human face and a scaly tail in the Inferno. Western scholars of Chinese art use the word for the winged lion-like creatures of Chinese tradition, such as the bixie.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions her in the Iliad, Hesiod in the Theogony, and Apollodorus, Hyginus and Ovid also record her story.

Trivia

A fire mountain. Some rationalising versions say the Chimera was a mountain with burning gas vents that sailors used as landmarks.
A ship's name. Virgil gives the name Chimera to a gigantic ship in a boat race in the Aeneid.
