Uther Pendragon

Uther Pendragon is a legendary king of post-Roman Britain and the father of King Arthur in Arthurian tradition. His story was fixed in the twelfth century by Geoffrey of Monmouth, whose account became the basis for most later versions.

The myth

According to the tradition, Uther is the youngest son of King Constantine. After their father's death, his eldest brother Constans takes the throne, but he is murdered on the orders of Vortigern, who seizes the crown. Uther and his brother Aurelius Ambrosius, still children, flee to Brittany. Years later they return as adults: Aurelius burns Vortigern alive in his castle and becomes king.

With Aurelius on the throne, Uther leads an army to Ireland to help Merlin bring the stones of Stonehenge back to Britain. Later, while Aurelius is ill, he defeats Vortigern's son and his Saxon allies. On the way to the battle he sees a comet shaped like a dragon, which Merlin reads as a sign of Aurelius's death and of Uther's glorious future. This is how he earns the epithet Pendragon and the dragons on his standards.

Uther becomes obsessed with Igraine, wife of Gorlois, Duke of Cornwall. Gorlois hides her in the impregnable castle of Tintagel, and Merlin transforms Uther into Gorlois's likeness so that he can enter the fortress. That very night Arthur is conceived, and in the morning Gorlois is found dead. Uther then marries Igraine, and they have a daughter, Anna. Later he falls ill, and after defeating Octa, son of Hengist, he dies from poison in a spring near Verulamium.

Legacy

Wolfram von Eschenbach offers an alternative origin for Uther's lineage in his Parzival, and Robert de Boron credits him with the creation of the Round Table. In modern literature he appears in T. H. White's The Once and Future King and in Valerio Massimo Manfredi's novel The Last Legion (2002), where the name is taken by Romulus Augustulus.

Ancient sources: He appears in poem XXXI of the Black Book of Carmarthen, in the Welsh poem Pa gur yv y porthaur?, in The Death-song of Uther Pen from the Book of Taliesin, and in the Welsh Triads.

Trivia

A dragon-shaped comet. The origin of the epithet Pendragon lies in a comet that, according to tradition, Uther saw on the way to battle. Merlin read it as an omen.
The Half-Dead King. The Saxons called him the « Half-Dead King », because he insisted on leading his troops on horseback despite his illness.
