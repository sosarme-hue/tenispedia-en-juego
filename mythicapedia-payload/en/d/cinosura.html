Cynosura

Cynosura is one of the nurses of Zeus in Greek mythology, a nymph who, in some accounts, was placed in the sky as a constellation. Her name, meaning "dog's tail", also refers to the Little Dipper and its brightest star, Polaris.

The myth

According to tradition, the young Zeus was hidden in the fragrant cave of Mount Dicte, near Mount Ida on Crete, to escape his father Cronus. There he was raised for a year by nymphs, Cynosura among them, while the Curetes drowned out the infant's cries to deceive Cronus.

Cynosura is often paired with her sister Helice. Some writers say both were rewarded for their care by being set among the stars as the two Bears. Helice gave her name to the Great Bear and Cynosura to the Little Bear, which turn together around the pole. The writer Aglaosthenes, author of the Naxica, counted Cynosura among the Idaean nymphs and also among the Curetes who attended Zeus.

Aglaosthenes also linked her to the city of Histoe, where the harbour and much of the land were named Cynosura.

Legacy

The name Cynosura survives in astronomy for the Little Dipper and the star Polaris, which guided sailors for centuries. The Bears gave their name to the Septentriones, the Latin term for the seven stars of the Great Bear.

Ancient sources: Hesiod and other ancient writers preserve the accounts of Zeus raised on Crete; Aglaosthenes' Naxica is cited by Hyginus.

Trivia

A guiding star. Polaris, the brightest star of the Little Dipper, lies near the north celestial pole and was for centuries a reference for navigation.
Two sisters in the sky. Cynosura and Helice are linked to the two Bears, whose joint movement led some ancient texts to call them the Wagons.
