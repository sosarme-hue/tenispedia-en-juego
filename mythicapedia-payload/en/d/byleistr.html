giant of Norse mythology, brother of Loki

Býleistr

Býleistr is a giant in Norse mythology, the brother of Loki and, according to Snorri Sturluson, also of Helblindi. He barely appears in the ancient sources, so he is known mainly through his kinship with the trickster god.

The myth

Very little is known about Býleistr, and nearly everything comes through his connection to Loki. No ancient source credits him with any action of his own: there are no episodes in which he takes part and no deeds that can be assigned to him. He appears only in the context of the god's family.

The most cited reference is a poetic formula, the kenning "Byleist's brother" (bróðir Býleists), by which skalds referred to Loki. It appears in the Völuspá and Hyndluljóð of the Poetic Edda, and in the Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda. The figure was known enough to serve as a poetic reference, even though he was given no real prominence.

Snorri Sturluson, in the Gylfaginning and the Skáldskaparmál, is the author who explicitly names Loki's two brothers: Býleistr and Helblindi. Much of what is said about him therefore depends on his testimony.

The link to Fárbauti and Laufey, Loki's parents, is an inference drawn by several scholars from that brotherly bond, not a specific story. For this reason, Býleistr's exact role in the mythic complex surrounding Loki's family remains unclear.

His name has an uncertain meaning. The most popular proposals connect it with the Old Norse word for storm, in compounds meaning something like "storm-relieving", "storm-flasher" or "violent storm". Some scholars have suggested it may refer to a weather phenomenon, though this is not certain. Manuscripts of the Prose Edda preserve variants such as Blýleistr or Býleiptr.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Völuspá, Hyndluljóð and Poetic Edda, and in Snorri Sturluson's Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál of the Prose Edda.

Trivia

Name variants. Manuscripts of the Prose Edda record the name in different forms, such as Býleistr, Blýleistr or Býleiptr.
Loki, Býleistr's brother. In Norse poetry, Loki could be referred to by the periphrasis "brother of Býleistr", a phrase that remembers the giant more than any tale about him.
