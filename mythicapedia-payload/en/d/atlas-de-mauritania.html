Atlas of Mauretania

In Greek tradition, Atlas is a legendary king of Mauretania whose name became tied to northwest Africa and, eventually, to maps. He is often identified with the Titan who holds up the sky, although ancient texts do not always keep the two figures apart.

The myth

According to the account Plato gives in the Timaeus, the first king of Atlantis was also named Atlas, and he was the son of Poseidon and the mortal Cleito. This link to the sea god places him in a remote land beyond the western ocean.

Over time, tradition connected his kingdom with Mauretania, a region roughly corresponding to modern Morocco and Algeria. In Roman times the Atlas Mountains were already firmly associated with his home, which strengthened the identification, and the king came to be confused with the Titan who supported the heavens.

Legacy

In the sixteenth century, Gerardus Mercator dedicated his work to the "King of Mauretania", and the word "atlas" for a collection of maps grew out of it. The name of the Atlantic Ocean also derives from the "Sea of Atlas".

Ancient sources: Plato, in the Timaeus, is the source that makes this Atlas a son of Poseidon and Cleito.

Trivia

An ocean named after a king. The Atlantic Ocean takes its name from the "Sea of Atlas", a designation that links the ocean to this monarch.
From king to map book. The word "atlas" came to mean a collection of maps thanks to the work Mercator dedicated to this king.
