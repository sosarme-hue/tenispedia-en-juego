mythical ancestor of the Edonians

Edonus

Edonus is, in Greek mythology, the ancestor of the Edonians, a people who lived in Thrace and in Thracian Macedonia. Son of Ares, god of war, and of a muse, his figure explains the origin of a name that later came to be linked with the worship of Dionysus.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Edonus was the son of Ares, the god of war, and of Calliope, the muse of epic poetry. The stories present him as the forefather of the Edonians, who settled in Thrace, the region north of the Aegean Sea, and his name gave rise to the terms Edonian and Edonic.

Over time, the term came to mean more than just Thracian. Because Thrace was one of the main centres of the worship of Dionysus, the adjective Edonic also came to mean "Dionysiac" or "Bacchantic", in reference to the god's followers.

Trivia

A name that changed meaning. The adjective Edonic came to mean "Thracian" and, by extension, "Dionysiac" or "Bacchantic", because of Thrace's importance in the worship of Dionysus.
