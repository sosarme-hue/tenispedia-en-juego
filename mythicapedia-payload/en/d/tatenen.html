Egyptian god of the primordial mound

Tatenen

Tatenen is an Egyptian god who embodied the primeval mound that rose from the watery chaos at the beginning of the world, and who was called father of all the gods in the Memphite tradition. He matters because he was linked to creation, fertility and the life that sprouts beneath the earth.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Tatenen, whose name means risen land, is the primeval mound that emerged from the watery chaos at the dawn of the world. The texts portray him as a creator who shaped everything like a potter, and for this reason he was identified with Khnum, the god who made all that exists on his wheel. From this came titles such as father of all the gods and creator and mother who gave birth to all the gods.

He was both feminine and masculine, and in some texts he appears as Tanenu or Tanuu, the inert land, a chthonic deity with two snakes on his head. Lord of the deep realms from which everything emerges, he was a source of food, plants and minerals, and was linked to rebirth and the flooding of the Nile. He also helped the dead on their journey to the afterlife and, with a staff, kept the serpent Apep away from the primeval mound.

Worship and religion

In Memphis, where he merged with Ptah to form Ptah-Tatenen, he was venerated, and he was also worshipped at This, Asyut and Hermopolis. His presence was constant in temples and tombs, where shrines and mounds supported the sarcophagus, symbolizing the risen earth. He was called Lord of Jubilees in the ceremony that renewed the pharaoh's rule, the Heb Sed.

Ancient sources: He appears in coffin inscriptions from the First Intermediate Period and the Middle Kingdom, and the Great Hymn of Khnum from the Third Intermediate Period identifies him with that creator god.

Trivia

A name centered on the earth. His name means risen land, and in ancient inscriptions he appears as Tanenu or Tanuu, the inert land.
Ram's horns. He is often shown wearing a crown of two ram's horns, a sun disk and two ostrich feathers.
Green skin. As a mummy and as a god of vegetation, he is often shown with green skin, a symbol of fertility.
