Japanese god of grain and agriculture

Ame no Hohi

Ame no Hohi is a male deity in Japanese mythology, the second son of the sun goddess Amaterasu. He is venerated as a protector of agriculture, rice, sericulture and textiles, and the lineages of the ancient rulers of Izumo are said to descend from him.

The myth

According to the accounts recorded in Japan's major chronicles, Ame no Hohi is a son of Amaterasu and a brother of Ōkuninushi and Amenooshihomimi. In some versions he was the first sent down to earth to rule, but he refused, or he did not return after three years, so the gods sent another deity in his place.

His sons, Ame-no-Hinadori and Takehi-Nateru, continue his line. Tradition regards him as the ancestor of the Izumo no Omi clan, of the priests of Izumo and the Sugawara clan, and of the Haji clan. Nomi no Sukune is said to be among his descendants.

Worship and religion

Ame no Hohi is worshipped across Japan as a god of agriculture, rice ears, sericulture and cotton cloth, among other things. Many shrines are dedicated to him, including Izumo-taisha in Shimane Prefecture and several in Shiga, Osaka and Fukuoka. His worship remains alive within Shinto.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, the chronicles that record Japan's mythology and legendary history, where he is called Ame no Hohi no Mikoto.
