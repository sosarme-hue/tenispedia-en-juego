Greek personification of excuse

Prophasis

Prophasis is the personification of excuse or pretext in Greek mythology, known only from a brief mention in ancient literature. Her main interest lies in how later writers made her the daughter of Epimetheus.

The myth

The only ancient mention of Prophasis comes from Pindar, who names her as the daughter of Epimetheus, the Titan whose name suggests "afterthought". Centuries later, the Second Vatican Mythographer named her mother as Pandora, linking her genealogy to the story of the first woman created.

No stories of her deeds or actions survive. The Byzantine encyclopedia Suda defines the word without linking it to any character, and some later Byzantine scholiasts seem to have taken it as the name of a minor figure. Many modern scholars believe she is an allegorical personification devised by medieval compilers rather than a goddess with her own cult.

Ancient sources: Pindar names her as the daughter of Epimetheus, and the Second Vatican Mythographer calls her the daughter of Epimetheus and Pandora.

Trivia

Two spellings. The Second Vatican Mythographer uses the Latinized form Prophasis, while the Greek original is written Πρόφασις.
A play on names. Epimetheus, "the one who thinks afterwards", and his daughter, "the later excuse", form a pair that many scholars see as a wordplay.
