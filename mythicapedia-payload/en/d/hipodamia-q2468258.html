wife of Pirithous in Greek mythology

Hippodamia

Hippodamia is a figure of Greek mythology, the bride of the Lapith king Pirithous and the central figure of the Centauromachy, the battle between Lapiths and centaurs that broke out at her wedding. The attempted abduction by the centaur Eurytion made her one of the most frequently depicted episodes of classical tradition.

The myth

The sources disagree about her parentage. According to different accounts she was a daughter of Butes, Atrax or Adrastus, and she was also known as Deidamia, Laodamia, Hippoboteia, Dia or Ischomache. Apollodorus links her to the centaurs, whom he says descended from Centaurus, son of Ixion and a cloud.

She married Pirithous, king of the Lapiths. At the wedding feast, to which he had also invited the centaurs, they drank wine for the first time and became drunk. Eurytion became fixated on the bride, overturned the tables and dragged her by the hair, while the other centaurs tried to seize the other women and even the young men present.

Theseus, Pirithous's close friend, rescued Hippodamia and killed Eurytion. The fight that followed, the Centauromachy, ended with the centaurs defeated and driven out to Mount Pholoe in Arcadia. Hippodamia gave birth to a son, Polypoetes, who would fight at Troy, and died shortly afterwards.

Legacy

Her abduction was a common subject in Western art of the classical tradition. Works depicting it include the sculpture The Abduction of Hippodamia by Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse and a painting by Rubens.

Ancient sources: Ovid recounts her story in Book XII of his Metamorphoses, where Nestor tells it at a victory feast after Achilles killed Cycnus.

Trivia

Meaning of her name. Hippodamia means she who masters horses, from the Greek hippos (horse) and damazein (to tame).
Many names. Besides Hippodamia, she was called Deidamia, Laodamia, Hippoboteia, Dia or Ischomache, reflecting the variety of ancient versions.
