Sixteen Arhats

The Sixteen Arhats are a legendary group of disciples of the Buddha, venerated in East Asian Buddhism and in Tibet. The grouping came from India, and in Japan they are regarded as models of behaviour.

The myth

They are legendary figures of Buddhism, gathered into a group of sixteen. In Tibetan they are called neten chudrug, also known as sthaviras, meaning 'elders'. Their tradition travelled from India to China and then to Tibet, where the Kashmiri teacher Shakyahribhadra (1127-1225) composed a liturgical practice linked to the festival of the Buddha's birth.

Worship and religion

In China, the expanded group of eighteen arhats became more popular, yet devotion to the sixteen continues in China, Japan and Tibet. Tibetans honour them during the festival of the Buddha's birth.

Legacy

In Japan, where they are much loved in Zen, they appear in paintings and objects such as a lacquered box shaped like a begging bowl. Tibetan art often depicts them too, and China has famous painted sets, such as those held by the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York.

Trivia

Bowl-shaped box. A Japanese box with a cover shaped like a begging bowl displays the sixteen arhats.
Painted series. The Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York holds Chinese sets of these arhats attributed to Ming and Qing painters such as Wu Bin and Shitao.
