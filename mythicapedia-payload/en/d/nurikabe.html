Japanese yōkai that blocks night travelers' paths

Nurikabe

The nurikabe is a yōkai, or spirit, from Japanese folklore that appears as an invisible wall blocking travelers at night. Its name means literally "plastered wall", and it is one of the best-known creatures in the yōkai tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, the nurikabe takes the form of a wall, usually invisible, that blocks the path of anyone walking at night. Going around it is futile, since the wall seems to stretch endlessly in both directions and upward.

The spell is broken by striking the lowest part of the wall, after which the obstacle vanishes and the traveler can continue. Most of the early legends recorded by folklorist Kunio Yanagita in 1938 come from Kyūshū, in Fukuoka and Ōita prefectures. Some have suggested the tale may have arisen to explain why travelers lost their bearings on long journeys.

In Ōita, related accounts describe not a wall but a sudden blindness, attributed to a tanuki. Superstition says the creature spreads its scrotum like a blind; the remedy is to sit down, smoke a puff of tobacco, and regain one's sight.

Legacy

The nurikabe appears in manga and anime, such as GeGeGe no Kitarō, where it is the hero's ally, and in Takashi Miike's film The Great Yokai War (2005). It has also appeared in video games including Ōkami and Super Mario 64, as Whomp. The name is also used for a popular Japanese logic puzzle.

Trivia

A recent discovery. In 2007 a white creature labeled as nurikabe was identified in a yōkai picture scroll held by Brigham Young University, which changed its popular image.
A wall with a face. In an Edo-period story about the Inō household, a face with eyes and a mouth appears on a wall, which some have linked to the nurikabe.
