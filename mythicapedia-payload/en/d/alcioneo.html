Alcyoneus

Alcyoneus is one of the giants of Greek mythology, best known for his fight with Heracles. He ranks among the most powerful of the Giants, and his story explains how an opponent who was immortal in his homeland could still be defeated.

The myth

According to tradition, Alcyoneus was one of the Giants, offspring of Gaia. Some authors say they were born from Uranus, others from Earth and Tartarus. Apollodorus places him among the greatest, alongside Porphyrion, and says he was immortal as long as he fought in his native land, Pallene. During the Gigantomachy, the cosmic battle of the Giants against the Olympian gods, he drove off the cattle of Helios from Erythia.

The gods had been told they could not defeat the Giants without a mortal's help. Zeus prevented Gaia from obtaining a protective potion and called on Heracles with Athena's help. Heracles shot Alcyoneus with an arrow, and he fell to the ground, but revived on touching the earth. On Athena's advice, the hero dragged him out of Pallene, and there he died.

Pindar tells a different version. He describes Alcyoneus as a Thracian herdsman, as huge as a mountain, who attacked Heracles at the Isthmus of Corinth as the hero was returning with the cattle of Geryon. He hurled a rock that smashed chariots and men, but Heracles deflected it with his club and killed him. In some accounts, his seven daughters, the Alkyonides, threw themselves into the sea when their father died and were turned into birds.

Legacy

Alcyoneus appears in the Gigantomachy frieze of the Pergamon Altar, where he is usually identified as the winged Giant fighting Athena. In the tradition recorded by the Suda, his daughters are transformed into halcyons, the birds that give their name to the kingfishers.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Pindar, Claudian, Philostratus, Nonnus of Panopolis (Dionysiaca) and the Suda.

Trivia

A rock at the isthmus. According to the sources, the boulder Alcyoneus hurled at Heracles could still be seen at the Isthmus of Corinth until fairly recent times.
A giant nine cubits tall. Nonnus of Panopolis describes him as nine cubits tall and has him fight using mountains as weapons.
