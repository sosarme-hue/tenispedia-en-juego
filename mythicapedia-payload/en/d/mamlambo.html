South African river goddess, serpent-like

Mamlambo

Mamlambo is a deity of South African and Zulu tradition, known as the "goddess of rivers" and described as a large, snake-like creature. Her name became known well beyond her tradition in 1997, when South African newspapers reported sightings of a monster in a river near Mount Ayliff.

The myth

In Zulu belief, Mamlambo dwells in rivers and carries an ambiguous character tied to water and wealth. Some accounts present her as a seductive figure who offers material prosperity, but who can bring terrible ruin.

In 1997 several South African newspapers described a monster in the Mzintlava River with a horse's head, a fish's lower body, short legs and a snake's neck, which locals said glowed green at night. Nine deaths were blamed on the creature. Police said the bodies had been in the water for a long time and that crabs had eaten the soft tissue around the heads and necks; local villagers, however, insisted that Mamlambo ate faces and sucked out brains, which is why she is also called "the Brain Sucker".

Legacy

Mamlambo appears in the SyFy programme Destination Truth, which mounted an expedition in search of her and found no evidence of her existence. She also inspired a cryptid in the Cartoon Network animated series Secret Saturdays.

Trivia

A failed expedition. The SyFy programme Destination Truth sent an expedition to search for Mamlambo, which found no evidence that the creature existed.
Her nicknames. She is known as "the Brain Sucker", a nickname that came from local villagers' explanations of the deaths attributed to the creature.
