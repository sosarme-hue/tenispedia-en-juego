Heorot

Heorot is the mead-hall and seat of the Danish king Hrothgar in the Anglo-Saxon epic poem Beowulf. Its name means "stag" in Old English, and it is the main setting where the monster Grendel and the hero who defeats him meet.

The myth

The poem describes Heorot as so large that Hrothgar could give Beowulf eight horses with gold-plated headgear. It was both the seat of government and the residence of the king's warriors, and it stood for civilisation and the might of the Danish kings.

Each night the monster Grendel, who lived in the dark marsh waters, attacked the hall and devoured its inhabitants. Beowulf, the Geatish hero, came to defend it and eventually killed Grendel. Later, Grendel's mother attacked the hall, and Beowulf defeated her too.

Legacy

J. R. R. Tolkien used Heorot as the model for King Théoden's Golden Hall, Meduseld, in Rohan. It also gave its name to The Legacy of Heorot, a 1987 science fiction novel by Larry Niven, Jerry Pournelle and Steven Barnes, and to a story in the Dresden Files collection Side Jobs.

Ancient sources: Appears in the Old English poem Beowulf.

Trivia

A stag as a royal symbol. Its name means "stag". In Anglo-Saxon finds, such as the sceptre from Sutton Hoo, stags are associated with royalty.
A real hall?. Some researchers have proposed Lejre in Denmark as a possible inspiration for the hall, where large Viking-age buildings have been found.
