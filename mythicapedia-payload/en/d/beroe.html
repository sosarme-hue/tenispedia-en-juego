Phoenician nymph of the Beirut spring

Beroe

Beroe is a nymph linked to the spring that supplied water to Berytus, the modern Beirut, on the Phoenician coast. Accounts present her as a daughter of Aphrodite and Adonis and as the prize in a rivalry between Dionysus and Poseidon.

The myth

According to tradition, Hermes attended her birth, and the Virgin Astraia, lady of justice, raised her on wise milk, taught her laws and washed her in sacred water. She briefly served Artemis and grew up famed for her beauty, though she never wore ornaments or make-up and never looked into a mirror.

Both Dionysus and Poseidon fell in love with her. Fearing for her daughter, Aphrodite proposed that they fight for her hand, promising that the loser would bear no grudge against the city. Poseidon won, made her his wife and placed the sea kingdom at her disposal. Some authors also call her Amymone and make her a daughter of Oceanus and Tethys, while others consider her a daughter of Aphrodite and Adonis.

Legacy

The story explains the name of Berytus, the modern Beirut, which according to tradition was given in her honour.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis describes her in his Dionysiaca, where she appears as a Lebanese nymph tied to the city's founding.

Trivia

Raised by Astraia. According to the story, the Virgin Astraia, lady of justice, fed her honey and taught her words of law.
No vanity. Beroe wore no ornaments or make-up and never looked in a mirror, though her beauty was compared to that of the goddesses.
