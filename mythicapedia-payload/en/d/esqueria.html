land of the Phaeacians in the Odyssey

Scheria

Scheria is the island of the Phaeacians in Homer's Odyssey, the last stop of Odysseus before he returns to Ithaca. It is considered one of the earliest descriptions of a utopia.

The myth

After leaving the island of Ogygia, where the nymph Calypso had held him, Odysseus built a raft and sailed by the stars for eighteen days. Poseidon, seeking revenge for his son Polyphemus, whom Odysseus had blinded, unleashed a storm that threw him onto the shore of Scheria after three days of struggling with the waves.

There he was found by the princess Nausicaa, daughter of King Alcinous, who was washing clothes by the sea at Athena's urging. Her maids fled at the sight of the naked, leaf-covered man, but Nausicaa stayed, gave him clothes, food and drink, and pointed him toward the palace. With Athena's help, disguised as a young girl, Odysseus slipped inside unseen and pleaded with Queen Arete. An elderly Phaeacian, Echeneus, persuaded the king to welcome him.

The palace had doors guarded by dogs of gold and silver made by Hephaestus, bronze walls and an orchard that bore fruit all year. Odysseus told his story of the adventures after the Trojan War, and the Phaeacians, whose ships were steered by thought, carried him home to Ithaca. According to local tradition on Corfu, a rock off the coast is the ship turned to stone by Poseidon as punishment for helping his enemy.

Legacy

Since ancient times, people have tried to locate Scheria. Thucydides identified it with Corcyra (Corfu), and Strabo placed it in the Atlantic Ocean, an idea some authors connect with Plato's Atlantis. Other scholars compare it to Minoan Crete, while some believe it is purely imaginary. Its picture of a remote, happy land makes it one of the first examples of utopia.

Ancient sources: Homer describes it in the Odyssey; Thucydides and Strabo tried to place it on the map.

Trivia

Dogs of gold and silver. According to Homer, the dogs guarding the entrance to Alcinous's palace were forged by Hephaestus, the god of fire and metalworking.
Ships steered by thought. The Phaeacian ships were steered by the minds of their crews and could carry a passenger home within a single day.
