Chinese goddess of fertility

Songzi Niangniang

Songzi Niangniang, "the Maiden Who Brings Children", is a Taoist goddess of fertility venerated in the folk religion of southeast China. She is often depicted as Guanyin or as one of her attendants, and sometimes in the form of an empress.

The myth

According to tradition, her figure grew out of three goddesses in the Ming novel Investiture of the Gods, the younger sisters of the wealth god Zhao Gongming: Zhao Yunxiao, Zhao Qiongxiao and Zhao Bixiao. Over time they were merged into a single triple deity known as Zhusheng Niangniang.

She often appears as an attendant of Bixia Yuanjun, and different parts of China have told different stories about fertility goddesses. She has also been identified with several historical figures.

Worship and religion

In Taoist painting and sculpture she is a goddess of birth and childcare, in charge of pregnancy, delivery and the protection of mother and child. She holds a book and a brush, alluding to the recording of newborns in family lineage records. Many temples have a shrine to her in one of the side halls, and she sometimes appears alongside other fertility goddesses.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Ming novel Investiture of the Gods, which contains the three sisters from whom her figure derives.

Trivia

The book and the brush. Her most characteristic attribute is a book with a brush, a symbol of recording newborns in the family genealogy.
Three sisters in one. According to tradition, three sisters of a wealth god were merged into a single triple deity.
