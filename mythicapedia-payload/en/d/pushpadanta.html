ninth Jain tirthankara of the present age

Pushpadanta

Pushpadanta, also known as Suvidhinatha, is the ninth tirthankara of Jainism in the present age. For Jains he is an arihant who attained moksha and became a siddha, a liberated soul that has destroyed all of its karma.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, he was born in Kakandi, identified with modern Khukhundoo in Uttar Pradesh, into the ancient Ikshvaku dynasty, as the son of King Sugriva and Queen Rama. After ruling his kingdom he renounced worldly attachments, attained omniscience and taught. His great achievement was re-establishing the four-part monastic order first founded by Rishabhanatha.

Jain texts give him a symbolic lifespan of 200,000 years and a height of 100 bows. His liberation is said to have taken place on the peaks of Mount Shikharji, in today's Jharkhand.

Worship and religion

He is recognised by his white complexion and meditative posture, and by the crocodile carved on the pedestal of his images. He is venerated across the Indian subcontinent, and Kakandi and the sacred footprints on Shikharji still draw pilgrims today.

Legacy

The Thirupanamur Jain Temple, in southern India, is a major Jain centre dedicated to him.

Trivia

A crocodile as emblem. His emblem is the crocodile or alligator, carved or stamped on the pedestal of his images.
Guardians that differ by sect. His male guardian is Ajita in both traditions, but sects disagree about his female guardian: Mahakali for some and Sutaraka for others.
