Mother goddess of Hinduism, *shakti* of Vishnu

Vaishnavi

Vaishnavi is one of the Matrikas (Mothers) of Hinduism, regarded as the feminine energy, or shakti, of Vishnu. She belongs to the groups of seven or eight mother goddesses associated with childhood.

The myth

According to tradition, each Matrika emerges from a major deity: Vaishnavi from Vishnu, Brahmani from Brahma, Maheshwari from Shiva and Indrani from Indra. Originally, they were the seven stars of the Pleiades star cluster.

The sources link them to conception, birth and childhood diseases. For that reason they were seen as goddesses of peril, who had to be appeased. Over time they took on a protective role, and in the traditional account of Durga's fight against demons they help the goddess defeat them.

Worship and religion

They were worshipped to avoid childhood illnesses and early deaths. Their cult appears in temples from the ninth century onwards. In South India the worship of the seven mothers predominates, while in Nepal eight are venerated.

Ancient sources: The Matrikas appear in the Mahabharata, and the Devi Mahatmya describes the group of eight.

Trivia

Starry origin. The Matrikas were originally the seven stars of the Pleiades, before becoming goddesses linked to the gods.
A ninth mother. In Bhaktapur, a city in the Kathmandu Valley, a ninth Matrika is added to represent the centre.
