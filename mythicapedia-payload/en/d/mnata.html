mythical Bohemian prince

Mnata

Mnata is the second of the seven mythical princes of Bohemia who, according to tradition, ruled before the first historical prince, Bořivoj. His name belongs to the legendary genealogy of the Přemyslid dynasty.

The myth

His name first appears in the chronicle of Cosmas and from there spread into many nineteenth-century history books. In the list he is the son of Nezamysl and the father of Vojen, placed between Přemysl the Ploughman and Bořivoj.

Some believe his name comes from the Czech word mníti, "to remember". Záviš Kalandra suggested that the seven names hid the old Slavic days of the week, which would make Mnata Monday, similar to German Montag. Another theory, by Vladimír Karbusický, holds that Cosmas may have invented the names while misreading a lost Latin copy of an old Slavic message, possibly linked to the Battle of Zásek around 849.

Legacy

The frescoes in the Rotunda at Znojmo, in Moravia, have been used to debate how many princes they show. Anežka Merhautová argued that they depict all members of the dynasty, including the junior Moravian princes.

Ancient sources: The chronicle of Cosmas, written in Bohemia, is the first to record the names of the princes.

Trivia

Days of the week. Záviš Kalandra believed the names of the princes were disguised ancient Slavic days of the week.
A lost message. By joining the ancestral names, Karbusický obtained a sentence with a tone of warning and peace, possibly addressed to the Franks.
