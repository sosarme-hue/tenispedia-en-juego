Seven Fairies

The Seven Fairies are the seven daughters of the Jade Emperor and the Queen Mother of the West in Chinese mythology. They are best known for their roles in the legend of Dong Yong and the Seventh Fairy, and they also appear in the novel Journey to the West.

The myth

According to tradition, one of the earliest tales about the sisters is "The Bird Maidens", recorded in the fourth century in the collection In Search of the Supernatural and, in a very similar version, in Guo Pu's Mythical Tales. In the story, a man in Xinyu, in Jiangxi Province, finds six or seven young maidens in a field wearing feathered garments. He marries one of them after hiding her robe so she cannot fly away, but she eventually recovers it, returns, and flies off with their three daughters. The tale may have been meant to explain why some large birds carried off children.

In the legend of Dong Yong, a devoted son is helped by a celestial maiden sent from heaven to assist him at the loom. For more than a thousand years she was identified as the Weaver Girl (zhinü). In late imperial times she was replaced by the Seventh Fairy, partly to avoid hints of infidelity, since the Weaver Girl also features in the older legend of The Cowherd and the Weaver Girl. Later, the other six fairies were also written into the plot. In Journey to the West, the Queen Mother plans a peach party and sends the fairies to the peach garden, where one of them wakes the Monkey King, who freezes them in place with a spell.

Legacy

Today, Xinyu has a reservoir and tourist resort called Fairy Lake, which claims to be the hometown of the legend of the Seven Fairies of China.

Ancient sources: In Search of the Supernatural (fourth century) and Guo Pu's Mythical Tales, as well as the novel Journey to the West, where they appear in the peach party episode.
