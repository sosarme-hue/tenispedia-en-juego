faithful companion of Aeneas in Roman mythology

Achates

Achates is the loyal companion of Aeneas in Virgil's Aeneid, so inseparable from his hero that his name became a byword for a very close friend. He belongs to the Roman tradition, although his name is Greek in origin.

The myth

In Virgil's poem, Achates accompanies Aeneas through all his adventures. When the pair scout the Libyan coast, Achates goes with him in disguise to Carthage, and later he leads Aeneas to the Sibyl of Cumae, the prophetess who allows him to descend to the underworld.

Virgil presents him as a model of fidelity. The epithet fidus Achates, or faithful Achates, evokes the Roman virtue of fides, the strict keeping of one's word. Yet, although he is the most important Trojan after Aeneas, he has very little development: he speaks only four lines in the whole epic. As a result, Aeneas stands out as a lone protagonist, with few allies on his quest.

According to one tradition, Achates was the one who killed Protesilaus, the first Greek to set foot on Trojan soil.

Legacy

His name became a byword for a faithful companion, and the phrase fidus Achates, or faithful Achates, appears in works by Proust, Rousseau, Proudhon, Walter Scott, James Joyce and Sheridan Le Fanu. In Joyce's Ulysses, Buck Mulligan and Leopold Bloom are suggested as possible fidus Achates to Stephen Dedalus. The Jupiter trojan asteroid 5144 Achates, discovered in 1991, was named in his honour.

Ancient sources: He appears in Virgil's Aeneid, where he accompanies Aeneas from the fall of Troy onward.
