Daoist goddess of war and longevity

Xuannü

Xuannü, the Dark or Mysterious Lady, is a goddess of Chinese mythology linked to war, longevity and the Daoist arts of the bedchamber. She is best known as the ally who helped the Yellow Emperor defeat Chiyou.

The myth

According to the story, the Yellow Emperor and Chiyou fought nine rounds without a clear winner. After the emperor withdrew to Mount Tai, which lay hidden in mist for three days, Xuannü appeared with a human head and a bird's body, knelt before him and offered a strategy that brought victory.

In later Daoist texts, especially those attributed to Du Guangting, she becomes the Yellow Emperor's teacher and a disciple of Xi Wangmu. She rides a cinnabar phoenix and grants him objects that help him win. Her power works through the Six Ding Jade Maidens, who conceal the body, destiny and spirit of those who practise the ritual.

Worship and religion

The ancient Chinese worshipped her, and she was gradually absorbed into Daoism, especially during the Tang dynasty. Several texts also link her to longevity practices and, from the 3rd century onward, to alchemy.

Ancient sources: The Taiping Yulan, compiled by Li Fang in the Song dynasty, recounts her appearance before the Yellow Emperor. Du Guangting's Yongcheng Jixian Lu gives her biography.

Trivia

Kingfisher-feather robes. According to Du Guangting's text, she wears garments of nine colours made of kingfisher feathers and rides a cinnabar phoenix.
Invisibility as military strategy. For Daoist texts, learning to conceal the body is a first step toward expelling evil and protecting the state.
