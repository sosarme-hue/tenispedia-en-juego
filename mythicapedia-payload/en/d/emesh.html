Sumerian god of summer and vegetation

Emesh

Emesh is a Sumerian god of vegetation, associated with summer, abundance and the fields. He is best known from a contest with his brother Enten, the god of winter and of farmers.

The myth

According to Sumerian tradition, Enlil fathered the two brothers after mating with a hill, or hursag. Enki had created Emesh to look after forests and fields. Emesh founded towns and villages with plentiful harvests, while Enten brought the spring floods.

The brothers carried their gifts to Enlil's house of life, the E-namtila, and argued over which was more valuable. In the end Enlil declared Enten the winner, since winter brought the water essential to farming in Mesopotamia's hot climate, and the brothers were reconciled.

Legacy

Modern scholars have compared this debate with the biblical story of Cain and Abel, which Samuel Noah Kramer considered the closest Sumerian parallel, and with an Aesop fable about winter and spring. It is one of the best-known examples of the Sumerian disputation genre.

Ancient sources: A Sumerian debate poem between summer and winter, preserved on clay tablets from the mid to late third millennium BC, found at Nippur.

Trivia

A biblical parallel. Samuel Noah Kramer considered this myth the closest Sumerian parallel to the biblical story of Cain and Abel.
A genre of debates. This story belongs to a group of Sumerian debates in which elements of nature argue with each other, such as sheep and grain or bird and fish.
