legendary king of Sicyon

Apis

Apis is a legendary king of Sicyon, in the northeastern Peloponnese, the fourth in the line of primeval kings of the city. His name became attached to the old name of a vast region in southern Greece.

The myth

According to tradition, Apis was the son and heir of King Telchis, a descendant of Aegialeus, the city's founder. He reigned for 25 years and grew so powerful that, according to the stories, the territory south of the Isthmus came to be called Apia after him, long before Pelops arrived at Olympia.

Yet the myth portrays him as a tyrant, and he was overthrown by his son Thelxion, who succeeded him on the throne. A namesake from Argos is also linked to the name, since some sources credit him with giving the whole Peloponnese the name Apia.

Trivia

A name that spread. According to the stories, Apis's name was applied to the whole territory south of the Isthmus, a region far larger than his kingdom of Sicyon.
