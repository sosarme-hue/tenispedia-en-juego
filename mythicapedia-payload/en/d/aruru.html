Mesopotamian goddess of vegetation

Aruru

Aruru is a Mesopotamian goddess originally associated with vegetation who was later identified with birth goddesses such as Ninhursag. She matters because she shows how several deities came to blend into one another in Sumerian and Akkadian religion.

The myth

Hymns portray her as a powerful and violent deity, whose behaviour was compared to that of a bull or a viper, and tradition calls her the elder sister of Enlil. Her name, of uncertain origin, could also serve as an epithet of other goddesses, such as Nisaba.

In the Epic of Gilgamesh she is the creator of Enkidu. Compositions dedicated to her mention plants such as poplar, date palm and cedar.

Worship and religion

Her cult centres were most likely the cities of Kesh, Adab and Irisaĝrig. The oldest evidence of her worship is a theophoric name, Ur-Aruru, from the Ur III period.

Ancient sources: Hymns in emesal, the Epic of Gilgamesh, the text of Lugal-e and god lists such as the one from Nippur.

Trivia

A disputed name. The meaning of the name is uncertain. The translation "outpour of water" is now considered a mistake, because the homophone arūru means "outlet of a canal".
Bull lyres. In the god list An = Anum, Nin-Aruru appears as a minor deity of Ninhursag's court, one of her six gud-balaĝ, literally "bull lyres".
