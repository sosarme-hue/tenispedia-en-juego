Thai goddess of rice

Phosop

Phosop is the Thai rice goddess, belonging more to ancient folklore than to an organized religion. Popular belief holds that she ensures there is enough food for everyone.

The myth

In the Laotian version, recorded in a manuscript at Wat Si Saket, a thousand-year famine ended when a young man freed a golden fish in exchange for Nang Khosop, the maiden who was the soul of rice. A greedy king hoarded the grain and caused another famine. A hermit, seeing a starving couple of servants, asked the goddess to feed them; when she refused, he killed her and cut her into pieces. Her fragments became the varieties of rice.

Worship and religion

Offerings are made at each stage of rice cultivation, and in 2008 Queen Mother Sirikit gave royal patronage to the custom. Each year the Royal Ploughing Ceremony scatters seeds that people gather to increase their luck. Her worship, however, is becoming rarer.

Legacy

Phosop appears in the logo of the Rice Department of Thailand's Ministry of Agriculture and was the lead of a 2021 fantasy romantic-comedy TV drama on Channel 3.

Trivia

A stolen golden idol. Phosop's idol at Wat Siri Wattanaram in Bang Phrom was once believed to be made of gold, but it was stolen in the 1970s by three men from outside the area and never recovered.
From rice paddies to city. About a century ago Bangkok was filled with rice fields; today only a few remain in eastern districts such as Min Buri or Lat Krabang.
