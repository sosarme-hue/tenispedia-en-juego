Hindu goddess of rain, smallpox and health

Karumariamman

Karumariamman is a Hindu goddess of rain, smallpox, health and healing, widely worshipped in the villages of South India. She is regarded as a form of other goddesses, including Parvati, Kali, Mariamman and Renuka.

The myth

In Hindu tradition, Karumariamman has no single long narrative of her own, as other deities do. Instead, she is understood as a manifestation of broader goddesses. She is associated with Parvati or Kali, and is also considered another form of Mariamman and Renuka.

Her abode is placed at Thiruverkadu, a place where devotees believe she lives. From there, according to belief, she looks after the community during illness and brings rain.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped mainly in the villages of South India. The temple at Tiruverkadu is the most important, as it is believed to be her dwelling. Her worship has also spread abroad, with temples dedicated to her in Penang, Malaysia, and in Singapore.

Trivia

Temple at Tiruverkadu. The Karumariamman Temple in Tiruverkadu, India, is considered her abode.
Presence in Singapore. The Sri Mariamman Temple in Singapore houses an image of the goddess, showing that her worship reached the diaspora.
