mythical paradise of the Mexica tradition

Tamoanchan

Tamoanchan is a mythical paradise in Mexica tradition, said to lie above the heavens and linked to the origin of humanity and of pulque. Its Nahuatl name is read as "the home of mortals" or "the house of those who descend."

The myth

According to various accounts, Tamoanchan was a heavenly garden inhabited by gods, where pulque was invented and humans were created. Some texts place it "above the thirteen heavens," while others identify it with Tlalocan, the paradise of Tlaloc, or with a flowering tree within it whose blossoms made whoever touched them fall in love.

The goddess Xochiquetzal lived there. According to one myth, the flowers bled when they were cut; according to another, this happened because of her sin with Tezcatlipoca. Tonacatecuhtli and Tonacacihuatl then expelled the gods, sending some to the earth and others to the underworld. Later, Quetzalcoatl and Quilaztli carried there the sacred bones from which the first humans were formed.

Ancient sources: It appears in Book X of Sahagún's Florentine Codex and in the Codex Chimalpopoca, and in codices such as the Borgia, where it is shown as a split tree.

Trivia

A split tree. In the codices, its glyph is a cleft tree that flowers and bleeds, and from which a person falls.
Two mountains for the sun and moon. Sahagún writes that people went from Tamoanchan to sacrifice in Teotihuacan, where two mounds were built in honor of the sun and the moon.
