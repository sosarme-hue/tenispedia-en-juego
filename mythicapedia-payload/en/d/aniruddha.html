grandson of Krishna and hero of Hindu mythology

Aniruddha

Aniruddha is a hero of Hindu mythology, son of Pradyumna and grandson of Krishna. His love story with the daitya princess Usha, told in the Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana, makes him one of the best-loved figures in the tales of the Yadu dynasty. The myth

The myth

According to tradition, the apsara Tilottama was cursed by the sage Durvasa to be born among the daityas after a love affair in the forest. Reborn as Usha, daughter of the asura Bana, she dreamed of a young man and fell in love with him. Her friend Chitralekha, using magical powers, located him from sketches of Vrishni heroes and brought him to the palace in Sonitapura.

Bana discovered Aniruddha and bound him with magical ropes. Krishna, Balarama and Pradyumna came to rescue him, and a great battle followed. Bana was a devotee of Shiva, who intervened on his behalf, but Krishna cut off the asura's extra arms, leaving him four. Aniruddha returned to Dvaraka with Usha as his wife. Their son Vajra was the only Yadava to survive the fratricidal destruction of his people.

Worship and religion

In Vaishnavism, Aniruddha is associated with the boar, his theriomorphic aspect known as Varaha, and appears as a boar in some Caturvyuha statues.

Legacy

The story of Aniruddha and Usha is told in the Okhaharan, an 18th-century Gujarati akhyana by Premanand Bhatt, and in Usha Parinayam, a 1901 Telugu play by Vedam Venkataraya Sastry. His name is also one of Vishnu's names, meaning "unstoppable."

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana recount his exploits and his romance with Usha.

Trivia

A name of Vishnu. Aniruddha, meaning "unstoppable" or "ungovernable," is also one of the names of Vishnu.
A dream and a sketch. Usha fell in love with Aniruddha in a dream, and her friend Chitralekha drew several Vrishni heroes until she found the face of the young man.
