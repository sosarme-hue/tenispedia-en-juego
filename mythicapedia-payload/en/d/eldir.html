servant of the giant Ægir in Norse mythology

Eldir

Eldir, whose name means 'fire-stoker', is a servant of the giant Ægir in Norse mythology. He matters because he is the first verbal opponent of Loki in the poem Lokasenna from the Poetic Edda.

The myth

According to Lokasenna, Ægir had two servants who were greatly praised by the guests and who cooked by a fire of bright gold. During a feast in Ægir's hall, Loki killed one of them, Fimafeng, and the gods drove him out of the hall.

When Loki came back, he met Eldir at the entrance and asked him what the Æsir were discussing in his absence. Eldir replied that they were judging their weapons and their prowess, and added that no one was a friend of Loki in words. Loki then announced that he was about to enter and blend mead with harmful power, and the exchange turned into insults against the gods until Thor threw him out for good.

Ancient sources: Lokasenna, in the Poetic Edda, where he is Loki's first interlocutor.

Trivia

Guardian at the threshold. The scholar John Lindow compares him to the outer guardian type, like the herdsman in Skírnismál, whom a character must confront before the main confrontation.
