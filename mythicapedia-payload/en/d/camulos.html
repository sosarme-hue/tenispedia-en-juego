Celtic god of war, identified with Mars

Camulos

Camulos was a god of war worshipped by the Gaulish and Brittonic peoples, especially by the Remi of the Reims region. The Romans identified him with Mars, and his name gave rise to the place-name Camulodunum, today Colchester.

The myth

Myths about Camulos are scarce, as he is known mainly from Latin votive inscriptions dating from the 1st to the 3rd century AD. They present him as a protective deity, regularly equated with Mars under the interpretatio romana. Scholars have proposed several etymologies for his name, linking it to Celtic words meaning 'champion' or 'servant', though the question remains open.

The name was also very common as a personal name across the Celtic-speaking world, from Iberia to the Danube, which has led some scholars to think it may have been an honorific epithet rather than the name of a single deity. Julius Caesar mentions a Gaulish commander called Camulogenus, meaning 'descendant of Camulus'.

Worship and religion

His cult centred on the region of Reims, capital of the Remi, whose citizens built a temple at Rindern and set up altars at Rome and elsewhere. Dedications spread across northern Gaul, the German provinces, Britain and Dacia. No details of festivals or rites survive, and there is no evidence that his cult continues today.

Legacy

The god's name left its mark on British place-names in Camulodunum, and the root has been proposed as the source of Camelot, King Arthur's seat in medieval romance, first attested in the late 12th century in Chrétien de Troyes's Lancelot.

Ancient sources: Known only from Latin votive inscriptions of the 1st to 3rd centuries AD, and from Caesar, who names the commander Camulogenus.
