creative principle and "Great Mystery" in Lakota spirituality

Wakan Tanka

Wakan Tanka is the Lakota term for the sacred or the divine, usually translated as "Great Spirit" or "Great Mystery". For the Lakota it is the creative principle of the universe, who made everything and gives spirit to whatever possesses it.

The myth

According to Lakota mythology, before creation Wakan Tanka existed in a dark void called Han. Feeling alone, he decided to create companions for himself. He concentrated his energy into a powerful force that formed Inyan, the stone. With Inyan he created Inyan Maka, the earth; from their union came Skan, the sky. Skan, Inyan and Maka brought forth Wi, the sun. These four beings were distinct and powerful, yet all were part of Wakan Tanka.

Everything comes from him: he placed the stones and minerals in the ground, made plants rise from the earth, and governs the change of seasons and the weather.

Worship and religion

Although he is not a personal being, the Lakota address him as "Ate" (Father) and "Tunkašila" (Grandfather). The sacred, or wakan, is not limited to him: it extends to animals, people, objects and acts that deserve respect because of their mysterious power. The sources consulted do not describe specific temples or rites.

Legacy

The term has been used to translate the Christian "God". Other related Siouan languages have similar words: in Omaha-Ponca, Ioway-Otoe-Missouri, Kansa and Osage it is wakanda, and in Quapaw, wakatakeh. Activist Russell Means promoted the translation "Great Mystery" and the view that Lakota spirituality is not monotheistic.

Trivia

A meaning hard to pin down. One of the Oglala informants of ethnographer James Riley Walker defined wakan as "anything hard to understand" or "that someone does not understand".
A related name. In the Omaha-Ponca, Ioway-Otoe-Missouri, Kansa and Osage languages, the same root appears as wakanda.
