Lycian captain in the Trojan War

Glaucus

Glaucus was a captain of the Lycian army, son of Hippolochus and grandson of the hero Bellerophon, who fought for Troy during the Trojan War. His face-to-face meeting with the Greek hero Diomedes in the Iliad is one of the poem's best-known scenes, since the two men discover that their families were bound by ties of hospitality.

The myth

According to the tradition, Glaucus was a son of Hippolochus and a grandson of Bellerophon. Together with his close friend and cousin Sarpedon, he commanded the Lycian contingent, which was allied with Troy. In the sixth book of the Iliad he met Diomedes in combat, but when they learned that their grandfathers had been friends and had exchanged gifts, they chose not to fight. As a sign of friendship they swapped armour: Glaucus gave up his golden set, while Diomedes gave up his bronze one.

Glaucus later fought alongside Hector, both in the battles against the Greeks and when Hector killed Patroclus. When Hector fell to Achilles, Glaucus took command of the Trojan army. During the assault on the Greek wall he was struck by an arrow shot by Teucer and had to withdraw from the fighting. When he saw Sarpedon dying, he prayed to Apollo to help him rescue his friend's body. The god healed his wound and allowed him to rally the Trojans around the corpse until the gods carried it away.

Later, in the struggle over the body of Achilles, Glaucus was killed by Ajax. Aeneas saved his body, and Apollo carried it back to Lycia, where it received funeral rites.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad is the main source, where he appears in books VI and XVII.

Trivia

Golden and bronze armour. Glaucus gave up golden armour, said to be worth one hundred oxen, in exchange for Diomedes' bronze armour, valued at nine oxen.
Wounded and healed by Apollo. Teucer wounded him with an arrow, and Apollo healed the wound so that he could keep fighting over the body of Sarpedon.
