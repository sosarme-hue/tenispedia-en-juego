mythical king of Orchomenus in Greek mythology

Minyas

Minyas was a legendary king of Orchomenus, in Boeotia, whom Greek tradition regards as the founder of the city and the ancestor of the Minyans, a people whose name was later linked to the Argonauts. He is best remembered for his wealth and for the treasury he had built.

The myth

According to the myth, Minyas grew so rich that he surpassed his predecessors and was the first to build a treasury to hold his riches. Apollonius Rhodius and Pausanias credit him with this work, a round stone structure with a great capstone at the top, whose ruins were still standing in Pausanias' day.

His parentage is the most disputed part of the story, since no strong tradition ever settled it. The most common version makes him a son of Poseidon, but his mother changes from author to author: Tritogeneia, daughter of Aeolus; Hermippe, daughter of Boeotus; Chrysogone, daughter of Almus; or Callirhoe, an Oceanid. Other traditions tie him to the family of Sisyphus, or make him a son of Orchomenus, of Ares or of Aleus. Apollonius calls him Aeolid, and a scholiast places him in the line Aeolus, Sisyphus, Almus, Chrysogone and Poseidon.

His wives vary too: Tritogeneia, Clytodora or Phanosyra, daughter of Paion. Among his children, the accounts agree most on Orchomenus, who gave his name to the city and its people, and on Clymene, who married a famous hero, which is why the Argonauts were sometimes called Minyans. Other children named include Athamas, Presbon and Diochthondas. His daughters, the Minyades, gave in to Bacchic frenzy and committed terrible deeds; in some versions they were turned into bats.

Ancient sources: Apollonius Rhodius and Pausanias describe his treasury and his lineage, and Homer mentions him indirectly through Orchomenus, the city of the Minyans.

Trivia

The treasury of Minyas. Pausanias describes it as a round stone building with a not very pointed top, whose ruins could still be seen in his time.
Bats as punishment. In some versions, the daughters of Minyas, the Minyades, were turned into bats after their Bacchic frenzy.
