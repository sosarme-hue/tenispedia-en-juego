eleventh Tirthankara of Jainism

Shreyansanath

Shreyansanath is the eleventh Tirthankara, or spiritual ford-maker, of the present age in Jainism. According to this tradition he attained liberation from the cycle of rebirth, becoming a Siddha, and he remains one of the most venerated figures in Jain devotion in India.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, Shreyansanath was born at Simhapuri, near Sarnath, in the Ikshvaku dynasty, to King Vishnu and Queen Vishna. The accounts give him a lifespan of 8,400,000 years and a height of 80 bows, figures that belong to Jain cosmology rather than to historical chronology.

The tradition recounts that after ruling his kingdom he renounced worldly attachments and became an ascetic. After a long period of preaching nonviolence and the shedding of karma, he attained final liberation on the peaks of Mount Shikharji, in present-day Jharkhand. His teachings stressed nirjara, the discharge of karma, and penance as the path to it.

Worship and religion

His chief centre of veneration is Simhapuri, identified as his birthplace, where a Jain temple stands today. The Sarnath Jain temple commemorates his early life, renunciation and first sermons. On Shikharji, a shrine venerates his footprints at the site of his liberation. In art he is recognised by his golden complexion, meditative posture and the rhinoceros emblem.

Legacy

His image appears in Jain sculpture, such as the 9th-century idol at the Bhand Dewal temple, and in regional paintings from Gujarat. Sarnath, linked to his memory, is a pan-Indian pilgrimage destination.

Ancient sources: His story appears in Jain tradition and its sacred texts, which record his biography, lineage and teachings.

Trivia

The rhinoceros emblem. In Jain art he is identified by the rhinoceros, which is usually carved on the pedestal of his images.
Footprints at Shikharji. Pilgrims venerate his footprints in a shrine on the peaks of Mount Shikharji, marking the site of his liberation.
