Hindu goddess of the Thiyya community of Kerala

Poomala Bhagavathy

Poomala Bhagavathy, also known as Aryapoomala, is a goddess worshipped in the North Malabar region of Kerala, India. She is the kuladevata of the Thiyya community, the protective deity of its lineage.

The myth

According to tradition, Poomala Devi was strolling with her friends in a heavenly garden when divine guards stopped her from picking flowers. She asked for help from one of them, of Shiva origin, who was resting inside a blossom in the form of wind. She named him Poomaruthan (from poo, 'flower', and maruthan, 'wind' in Malayalam), and he became her brotherly companion.

They descended to earth at Aryapoonkavu, in Arya Nadu. There the king's daughter, Aryapoonkani, gathering flowers as an offering to Kamadeva, suddenly fainted: the goddess had possessed her. The astrologer revealed that the deity wished to see the beauty of Malanadu, so the king had a great wooden ship built, and the goddess sailed to the Oriyara estuary. Another version has her landing at Kuruvanthata, in Ezhimala, where she was so charmed by the countryside that she stayed.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped mainly by the Thiyya community, and her main temples are in the Kannur and Kasaragod districts. During the Pooram festival, held in the Malayalam month of Meenam, girls who have not yet reached puberty worship Kamadeva, god of love and fertility, and Poorakkali is performed. Her brother Poomaruthan appears in theyyam form; Poomala does not.

Trivia

A brother in theyyam form. Her brother Poomaruthan is performed in the theyyam ritual, but the goddess herself has no theyyam form.
