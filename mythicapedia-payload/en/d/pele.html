Hawaiian goddess of fire and volcanoes

Pele

Pele is the Hawaiian goddess of fire and volcanoes, credited with creating the Hawaiian Islands. Still honored today as Tūtū Pele, she remains a powerful presence in island culture and in the hula tradition.

The myth

According to tradition, Pele is a daughter of Haumea and has many siblings, among them Nāmaka and Hiʻiaka, who is often considered the first hula dancer. Versions differ. In one, Pele leaves the mythical land of Kahiki, in Tahiti, and sails by canoe to Hawaiʻi; her older sister Nāmaka pursues her to end her life. After the fight, Pele dies, yet her spirit lives on in the crater Halemaʻumaʻu, at the summit of Kīlauea.

In another, she is a rival of Poliʻahu, the snow goddess of Mauna Kea. Jealous, Pele hurls fire at her, but Poliʻahu throws her burning snow mantle over the mountain, chilling the lava and dividing the island: snow to the north, Pele to the south.

In the story of Hiʻiaka and Lohiʻau, Pele sends a lava flow over Hōpoe, Hiʻiaka's friend, and the sisters' anger leads to tragedy before a reconciliation.

Worship and religion

Many Hawaiians believe Pele governs Kīlauea and controls its lava flows, and she is still honored with respect as Tūtū Pele. Her domain covers all volcanic activity on the Big Island, and she is considered the goddess of hula, a dance dedicated to her and her family. Her character shares features with Guayota, the demon of the Guanches of the Canary Islands, said to dwell in Teide.

Legacy

The International Astronomical Union officially named a volcano on Jupiter's moon Io after Pele. Her figure also appears in many hula dances and chants dedicated to her and her family.

Ancient sources: Traditional Hawaiian chants name her, and the first published stories about her were written down by William Ellis.

Trivia

A volcano on a moon. A volcano on Jupiter's moon Io bears Pele's name, officially approved by the International Astronomical Union.
Many forms. According to the stories, her body is the lava and steam of the volcano, but she can appear as a white dog, an old woman or a beautiful young woman.
