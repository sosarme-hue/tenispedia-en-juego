local Greek heroine of Melite

Aspalis

Aspalis was a local heroine of Melite in Phthia, Greece, whose story seems to explain a local epithet and a cult of Artemis. Her tale is known mainly from Antoninus Liberalis.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Antoninus Liberalis, Melite was ruled by a tyrant so cruel that the citizens would not dare speak his real name and called him Tartarus. When he summoned Aspalis, daughter of Argaeus, she hanged herself rather than suffer violation.

Her brother Astygites swore to avenge her. He dressed in his sister's clothes, hid a sword beneath them, entered the palace and killed the tyrant. The citizens threw the body into a river, which from then on was called Tartarus, and crowned Astygites in gratitude.

When they came to bury Aspalis, they found her body had vanished, and in its place they found a wooden statue of Artemis; it is believed the goddess turned her into it out of pity.

Worship and religion

The statue became a cult object, invoked as "Aspalis Ameilete Hekaerge". Every year the city's maidens sacrificed a young she-goat to the goddess by hanging it, imitating Aspalis' suicide.

Ancient sources: Antoninus Liberalis is the main ancient source that recounts her story.

Trivia

A river with a hellish name. According to the story, the river where the tyrant's body was thrown became known as Tartarus, the same name the citizens had given the tyrant.
A ritual imitating a suicide. Each year the maidens sacrificed a young she-goat by hanging it, a ritual imitation of Aspalis' suicide.
