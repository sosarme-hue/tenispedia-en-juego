monstrous cat of Welsh and Celtic legend

Cath Palug

Cath Palug was a monstrous cat from Welsh tradition, later linked to the Arthurian cycle. Its legend spread to France, where it became known as Chapalu, one of the monsters associated with King Arthur.

The myth

According to the Welsh Triads, it was born to Henwen, a huge white sow, and was cast into the sea. It survived, reached the isle of Anglesey and was raised by the sons of Palug, who did not realise how dangerous it was. As it grew, it spread death until Cai (Sir Kay) slew it. In a poem from the Black Book of Carmarthen, the cat has killed nine score warriors.

In other versions, especially French ones, the monster lives by a lake or marsh and it is Arthur who is defeated. Some sources describe it as a fish with the form of a cat, and the medieval Chapalu has a cat's head, a horse's body and a lion's tail.

Legacy

Guillaume Apollinaire revived it in 1898 in L'Enchanteur pourrissant, where Chapalu is always hungry, and Max Jacob later wrote a tribute version. It also appears as one of the beasts in the mobile game Fate/Grand Order, under the name Fou.

Ancient sources: The Welsh Triads (late 13th century), the poem in the Black Book of Carmarthen (before 1250), the Vulgate Estoire de Merlin and La bataille Loquifer.

Trivia

A cat born at sea. In some versions, the creature begins life as a kitten swept away by the waves, linking it to the aquatic realm.
A disputed reputation. The poet André de Coutance criticised French writers for claiming the cat had killed Arthur, calling it an outright lie.
