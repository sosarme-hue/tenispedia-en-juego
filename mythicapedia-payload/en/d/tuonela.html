Finnish realm of the dead

Tuonela

Tuonela is the realm of the dead in Finnish mythology, the counterpart of the Greek Hades. It is where souls go after death, set apart from the world of the living by a dark river.

The myth

According to tradition, Tuonela lies in the far north of the world, beyond a great divide that separates it from the land of the living. It is ruled by Tuoni, god of the dead, and his wife Tuonetar, although runic songs also name the icy Loviatar as sovereign. The dead must cross the river of Tuoni by a thread bridge, by swimming, or in a boat steered by Tuoni's daughter, while a black swan that sings death spells guards the current.

In the Kalevala, the shaman-hero Väinämöinen travels there to learn the secret of death. The ferrywoman takes him across, but on Tuoni's island he fails to get the spell he wants and barely escapes by turning into a snake. Back among the living, he curses anyone who tries to enter the realm alive. Lemminkäinen, another hero of the poem, dies after trying to kill the swan of Tuonela; his mother gathers the pieces of his body and, with the help of a bee, restores him to life.

Worship and religion

Shamans were believed to visit Tuonela in a trance, tricking its guards to gain spells and knowledge. Those who entered alive could not leave, and Tuonetar would offer them a beer that erased their former lives.

Legacy

Finnish Bible translations used the name Tuonela for the Greek Hades, and Finnish Christianity came to see it as the resting place of the dead before the Last Judgement. Its appearance in the Kalevala has made it one of the best-known images in Finnish myth.

Ancient sources: The Kalevala, the Finnish national epic, gathers Finnish and Karelian mythology, and it features Tuonela, Tuoni and Lemminkäinen.

Trivia

A swan that sings spells. The black swan guarding the river of Tuonela sings death spells, and hunting it is the task that costs Lemminkäinen his life.
Other names for the realm. Tuonela, Tuoni, Manala and Mana are used as synonyms. In Estonian mythology the realm is also called Toonela.
