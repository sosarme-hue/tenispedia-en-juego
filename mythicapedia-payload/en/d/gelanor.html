legendary king of Argos

Gelanor

Gelanor was a legendary king of Argos, son of Sthenelas, whom tradition places at the origin of the Danaans. His story is intertwined with that of Danaus, who arrived in the city fleeing the sons of Aegyptus and contested the throne with him.

The myth

According to the myth, Gelanor was ruling Argos when Danaus arrived. To decide who should govern, the people heard both men and postponed their verdict until the next day. That morning, a wolf attacked a herd of cattle and defeated the bull that led it. The Argives compared Gelanor to the bull and Danaus to the wolf, and Danaus gained the throne. Another version says that Gelanor handed over the kingdom on the advice of an oracle.

Worship and religion

The sanctuary of Apollo Lycius in Argos is credited to Danaus, who founded it to thank the god for the omen of the wolf.

Ancient sources: Pausanias includes him in the genealogy of the kings of Argos, Aeschylus in The Suppliants calls him Pelasgus, and Plutarch records the version of the wolf and the bull.

Trivia

A name meaning laughter. According to Robert Graves, the name Gelanor would mean "laughter".
Gelanor and Pelasgus. In Aeschylus' tragedy he appears only as "king", identified with Pelasgus, son of Pelector, and given a much more ancient origin.
