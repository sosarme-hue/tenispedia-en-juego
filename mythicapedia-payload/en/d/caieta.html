Aeneas's nurse in Roman mythology

Caieta

Caieta is, in Roman mythology, the nurse of Aeneas, the Trojan hero whose lineage tradition links to the origins of Rome. Her name became attached to a coastal bay in Latium.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Virgil, Caieta was buried on the bay of Gaeta, which took her name. Ovid, a generation later, composed an epitaph for her in which Caieta herself thanks her pious son for cremating her with fitting fire after she was snatched from the Greek flames.

The fourth-century commentator Servius notes that there was no agreement about whom she had nursed. Besides Aeneas, she was also assigned to Creusa or Ascanius.

Legacy

Her name remained linked to the bay of Gaeta, according to the Virgilian tradition.

Ancient sources: Virgil places her on the bay of Gaeta, Ovid writes her an epitaph, and Servius records the versions about whom she nursed.

Trivia

A disputed nurse. Servius reports that there was no agreement about whom Caieta had nursed: besides Aeneas, she was also assigned to Creusa or Ascanius.
An epitaph in her own voice. Ovid wrote an epitaph spoken in Caieta's own voice, a fictional inscription that makes the nurse the narrator of her own death.
