female spirit in Native American traditions

Deer Woman

Deer Woman, also called Deer Lady, is a female spirit in several Indigenous American traditions who can take the form of a beautiful woman or of a deer. She is best known as a seductress who lures men to their deaths, but within her communities she also serves as a warning against harmful behaviour.

The myth

In the stories, Deer Woman appears as a beautiful young woman, often beside the path or hidden in the bushes, calling men toward her. Her telltale sign is her deer hooves, and her eyes are the soft brown of a deer. Men who give in to her often realise what she is too late and die. She can be driven off with tobacco and song, or her spell can be broken by looking at her hooves; once someone recognises her for what she is, she flees.

In Lakota tradition, Deer Woman is one form of Anukite, the young woman Ite, who wanted to become a goddess, was punished by the Sky and was left with half a disfigured face. She appears in dreams or visions, and her two sides stand for proper and improper sexual relations. In this version, men who sleep with her go insane. Other stories treat her sighting as a sign of personal change or a warning, and say she loves dancing and joins communal dances until the drums fall silent.

Legacy

Deer Woman appears in the episode Deer Woman, directed by John Landis, from the horror anthology series Masters of Horror. She is also a recurring character in the series Reservation Dogs, played by Kaniehtiio Horn. In recent decades her story has been retold in the context of violence against Indigenous women, presenting her as a figure who defends herself.

Ancient sources: Oral traditions of peoples such as the Lakota, Ojibwe, Ponca, Omaha, Cherokee, Muscogee, Seminole, Choctaw, Otoe, Pawnee and Haudenosaunee include stories about her, but no ancient written texts mention her.

Trivia

Hooved feet. Her most distinctive trait is that her feet are deer hooves, which usually give her away. It is said that looking at them breaks her spell.
Two deer. In the Lakota version she may appear as a single deer or as two deer women, one white-tailed and one black-tailed.
