the talking mongoose of the Isle of Man

Gef

Gef is an allegedly talking mongoose said to have lived on a Manx farm in the 1930s, according to the family who claimed to share their home with it. It is one of the most famous cases in modern British folk paranormal lore.

The myth

According to the Irvings, in 1931 they heard scratching and voices behind the wooden panels of their farmhouse. A creature called Gef introduced itself as a mongoose born in New Delhi in 1852, about the size of a rat. The family said it guarded the house, warned of visitors and put out forgotten fires. Many people heard its voice, but almost no one saw it, and the footprints and hair attributed to Gef turned out to belong to the family's sheepdog.

The investigators Harry Price and Rex Lambert visited the farm in 1935 and concluded that only the most credulous would be convinced. Many skeptics suspect the daughter, Voirrey, invented the hoax using ventriloquism.

Legacy

The musician Lemon Demon dedicated the song “Eighth Wonder” (2009) to him, quoting many of his alleged sayings. The book Price and Lambert published on the case, “The Haunting of Cashen's Gap”, remains a standard reference.

Trivia

A very particular diet. The Irvings said they left biscuits, chocolates and bananas on a tray hung from the ceiling, and that Gef took them when he thought no one was watching.
A lawsuit with consequences. In 1937 Richard Lambert won a slander case against Sir Cecil Levita, who had said he was “off his head” for believing in the mongoose. He received £7,600, an exceptional sum for such a case.
