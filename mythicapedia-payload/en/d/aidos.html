Greek personification of shame and modesty

Aidos

Aidos, sometimes spelled Aedos, was the Greek personification of shame, modesty and reverence. Rather than a goddess with a cult of her own, she embodied the inner restraint that keeps people from wrongdoing, which is why she appears often in tragedies and didactic poems.

The myth

In myth, Aidos is an almost allegorical figure. Pindar called her a daughter of Prometheus, while Plato explains that Zeus sent her, together with justice, through Hermes, because humans, after receiving fire from Prometheus, harmed one another out of selfishness. Zeus wanted everyone to have a moral sense and a sense of justice, which made city life possible.

Hesiod tells that, at the end of the fifth age of men, Aidos and Nemesis will leave the earth wrapped in white robes and rise to Olympus, leaving mortals alone with their suffering. She is thus considered the last deity to leave the earth after the Golden Age, and a close companion of Nemesis. In another episode, Aidos is linked to Penelope, who covered her face with a veil when Odysseus asked her to go with him, a gesture her father Icarius understood as her choice to leave with her husband.

Worship and religion

Aidos had an altar near the old temple of Athena on the Athenian Acropolis, and there were altars to her in Sparta too. Athenians dedicated images to her alongside Fame and Impulse. According to tradition, Icarius set up a statue of Aidos on the spot where Penelope veiled herself. In Rome there were two shrines to Pudicitia, and her figure was associated with other virtues such as Sobriety and Piety.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions her in his account of the ages of man, and the tragedies of Aeschylus, Euripides and Sophocles refer to her.
