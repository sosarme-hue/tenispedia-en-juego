Celtic god identified with Mercury

Visucius

Visucius was a Celtic god worshipped in Gaul and the German provinces of Rome, known almost only from Latin votive inscriptions in which he usually appears as a Roman Mercury. His name, read as 'the knowing one' or possibly 'the raven', makes him one of the most characteristic native epithets of that god.

The myth

No ancient narrative tells stories about Visucius. He is absent from classical literature, and no episodes of his life, family or deeds are recorded. What we know of him comes from stone dedications, which usually name him as Mercurius Visucius.

In a few inscriptions he appears alone, without Mercury, which suggests that he may have been a local deity with some independence rather than only a byname of the Roman god.

Worship and religion

His worship was concentrated in Germania Superior, with a group of dedications among the Treveri and Mediomatrici of Gallia Belgica and one isolated case at Burdigala. At Grinario he was honoured together with the goddess Visucia, his divine partner, following a common pattern in which the god takes a Roman name and the goddess keeps a native one. The sources do not say whether his cult survived antiquity.

Legacy

His legacy is slight. The scholar Raffaele Pettazzoni read his name as 'the knowing one' and linked him to an all-knowing solar deity, a view that Jan de Vries rejected.

Ancient sources: He is absent from classical literature and is known only from Latin votive inscriptions from Gaul, the German provinces and, once, Hispania.

Trivia

A divine couple. At Grinario, Visucius appears beside the goddess Visucia, with whom he forms a couple. This kind of pairing, with a god bearing a Roman name and a goddess keeping a native one, was common in Gaul and the German provinces.
A name with several readings. His name may derive from a Gaulish root meaning 'knowledge', or from one meaning 'raven', compared with Old Irish fiach. Scholars do not agree.
