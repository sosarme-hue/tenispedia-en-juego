king of the Lapiths, companion of Theseus

Pirithous

Pirithous was a king of the Lapiths of Larissa in Thessaly and the closest friend of Theseus. His wedding to Hippodamia gave rise to the famous Centauromachy, one of the most repeated themes in Greek art.

The myth

According to tradition, Pirithous was the son of Dia, whom Zeus seduced in the form of a stallion, though others said his father was Ixion. The Iliad remembers Pirithous and Theseus as heroes of an earlier generation. In one story, Pirithous wanted to test Theseus' fame by stealing his cattle at Marathon, but when the two met they admired each other and swore an oath of friendship. Together they joined the hunt for the Calydonian Boar and the voyage of the Argonauts.

Pirithous married Hippodamia, and at the wedding the drunken centaurs tried to abduct the women. The Lapiths defeated them and drove them out of Thessaly. Later the two friends resolved to marry daughters of Zeus: Theseus chose Helen, still a child, and Pirithous chose Persephone. They descended into the underworld, where they became stuck to a rock. Heracles freed Theseus, but when he tried to lift Pirithous the earth shook, and Pirithous remained there forever. Some say Hades fed him to Cerberus for his impiety.

Worship and religion

In Athens he was worshiped as a hero alongside Theseus. The sources do not describe any shrines or festivals of his own.

Legacy

The wedding of Pirithous and the battle with the centaurs decorated one of the pediments of the temple of Zeus at Olympia. The scene appears on many Attic vases and later paintings, such as those by Pelagio Palagi and Angélique Mongez. In Attic comedy the failed rescue became a joke: Theseus left part of his body stuck to the rock, which gave rise to the epithet Hypolispos.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Iliad, in Hesiod and in Ovid, as well as in scholia on Homer and in a lost play by Euripides or Critias.

Trivia

A name with a story. A folk etymology linked his name to peritheein, the verb that Zeus supposedly used to seduce Dia.
A half rescue. Heracles managed to free only Theseus. In Attic comedy, the episode was told for laughs.
