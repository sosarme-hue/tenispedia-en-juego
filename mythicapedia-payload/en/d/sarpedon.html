Lycian hero of Greek mythology, son of Zeus and Europa

Sarpedon

Sarpedon is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Zeus and Europa, associated with Lycia, the region of Asia Minor where tradition places him as king. He matters mainly for his role in the Trojan War and for the versions that make him a brother of Minos.

The myth

According to tradition, Europa had Minos and Rhadamanthus by Zeus. Hesiod, Hellanicus and Bacchylides add Sarpedon as a third brother.

Raised by Asterion, king of Crete, the three brothers quarrelled over the young Miletus. Minos won, and Sarpedon, driven out of Crete, allied himself with Cilix against the Lycians and ended up ruling Lycia. Zeus granted him a life spanning three generations.

According to Herodotus, the Lycians originally came from Crete. Some authors identify this Sarpedon with the hero of the Trojan War, although the chronology does not fit. Diodorus resolves the problem with a later Sarpedon, son of Evander.

Worship and religion

There was a temple of Sarpedon at Xanthus in Lycia, probably linked to his tomb. Tertullian also mentions a sanctuary and an oracle of Sarpedon in the Troad, though he may have confused it with the one in Cilicia.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, Hesiod, Hellanicus, Bacchylides, Herodotus, Ephorus, Apollodorus, Diodorus and Tertullian mention him, with differing versions of his parentage and role.

Trivia

Two Sarpedons. Some authors believed that Minos's brother and the Trojan hero were the same person, but the difference in generations forced explanations such as the long life granted by Zeus.
Lycians from Crete. Herodotus held that the Lycians had Cretan origins, a tradition that links Sarpedon with Minos's island.
