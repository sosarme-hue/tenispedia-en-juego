seer and augur of the Argonauts

Mopsus

Mopsus is a seer and augur in Greek mythology, son of Ampyx and a nymph whose name varies between sources. He matters for his role among the Argonauts, where he shared the gift of prophecy, and for the tragic story of his death in the Libyan desert.

The myth

According to tradition, Mopsus was born at Titaressa in northern Thessaly and was a Lapith. Accounts agree that he was a seer and augur. Some name the nymph Chloris as his mother, others Aregonis. In Thessaly, the place name Mopsion recalled his own.

The myth says he learned augury from Apollo and could understand the language of birds. He was one of two seers who sailed with the Argonauts on Jason's quest, alongside the other prophet of the crew. Earlier he had competed in the boxing contest at the funeral games for Jason's father and had fought the centaurs among the Lapiths.

His end is the best-known episode. While fleeing across the Libyan desert from the enraged sisters of the Gorgon Medusa, he was bitten by a viper born from a drop of Medusa's blood. Medea could not save him, not even by magic. The Argonauts buried him by the sea under a monument, and a temple was later built on the spot. Ovid also places him at the hunt of the Calydonian Boar, although that hunt took place after the Argonauts returned, when Mopsus was supposedly already dead.

Worship and religion

According to tradition, he was honoured with a temple built on the site of his burial by the sea. The sources give no further details about his cult.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions him in the account of the Calydonian Boar hunt, and different ancient authors give varying versions of his parentage and life.

Trivia

An early inscription. The earliest known evidence of his name is an inscription on the strap of a soldier's shield, found at Olympia and dated to around 600–575 BC.
