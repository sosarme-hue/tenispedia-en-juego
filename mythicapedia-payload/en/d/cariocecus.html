Lusitanian god of war

Cariocecus

Cariocecus was the god of war in the mythology of the Lusitanians and other Iberian tribes of Hispania. Through conquest and cultural overlay, he came to be identified with the Roman Mars and the Greek Ares.

The myth

Few stories about him survive, so we know more about his rituals than his myths. Strabo does not name him, but he describes how the Lusitanians offered goats, horses and prisoners of war to their gods, along with Greek-style hecatombs.

Human victims held a central place in the cult, and prisoners of war were the most frequent sacrifices.

Worship and religion

The Lusitanians practiced human sacrifice and made predictions from the innards of victims or from the way they fell after a priest wounded them in the stomach. The right hand of prisoners was consecrated to Cariocecus. There is no information on whether his cult survived.

Legacy

Under Roman rule, his figure was merged with Mars and, in Greek contexts, with Ares.

Trivia

Divination with victims. The Lusitanians read the future in the innards of their victims and in the way they fell after being wounded.
The consecrated hand. They cut off the right hand of prisoners and offered it to the god as part of his worship.
