female creature of Cantabrian mythology

Ojáncana

The ojáncana is the female of the ojáncanu, a bloodthirsty being from Cantabrian mythology. Even more wicked than her mate, she is feared above all for devouring children who get lost in the forest.

The myth

According to oral tradition, she is a creature of great strength, with a flat face, piercing eyes, and huge, twisted teeth, and her dark hair is wild and tangled. Her breasts are long, and she slings them over her back when she runs. She lives in dark, dirty, foul-smelling caves and feeds mainly on raw meat, preferably that of children, though she also steals other food, milk or blood. She fears and hates the rámila, or stone marten, alone.

Their reproduction is unusual, because no birth takes place. When an ojáncanu grows old, the others kill him, open his belly and bury him under an oak. Nine months later, yellow, slimy worms emerge from his corpse. For three years an ojáncana nurses them with her own blood, until they become new ojáncanos and ojáncanas. Unlike the gentle, kindly anjanas, the ojáncanas represent the extreme opposite.
