Danaid of Greek mythology

Pirene

Pirene is one of the Danaids, daughters of Danaus in Greek mythology. Her story, marked by a wedding and the murder of her husband, is told as an eternal punishment in Tartarus.

The myth

According to the tradition, Pirene was a daughter of Danaus and an Ethiopian woman. Like her sisters, she was forced to marry one of the sons of her uncle Aegyptus, called Agaptolemus or Dolichus. Danaus had consented to this wedding in order to destroy his brother's sons and seize the kingdom of Egypt, which the two brothers had fought over for a long time.

Danaus gave each of his daughters a dagger and ordered them to kill their husbands on the wedding night. Pirene obeyed her father, as did all her sisters except Hypermnestra. The Danaids solemnly buried the heads of their husbands in the city of Lerna.

Although Hermes and Athena, acting on the orders of Zeus, purified them of the crime, the sisters were punished in Tartarus. There they were condemned to try forever to fill a leaking jar with water.

Trivia

Heads in Lerna. The Danaids buried the heads of their husbands in the city of Lerna, a place that appears in the story as the scene of the crime.
A leaking jar. The punishment of the Danaids in Tartarus is to fill a jar full of holes with water forever, so they never finish the task.
