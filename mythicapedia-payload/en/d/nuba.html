Chinese goddess of drought

Nüba

Nüba, also called Ba or Hanba, is the Chinese goddess of drought and one of the earliest goddesses attested in Chinese literature. She was the daughter of the Yellow Emperor, and her dry power proved decisive in his battle against Chiyou.

The myth

According to the tradition, when Chiyou sent the wind god Feng Bo and the rain god Yu Shi against the Yellow Emperor, Ba descended from heaven and used her drought power to defeat them. After the battle she did not return to heaven but wandered north, and wherever she appeared, drought followed.

She already appears in the Shijing and the Shanhaijing, where she is described as dressed in dark clothes. The Book of the Later Han links her with Yinglong in the fight against Chiyou, which gave rise to a mythological romance in later centuries.

Worship and religion

Until the mid-twentieth century, rain ceremonies were held in many regions of China and could last several hours. Their aim was to drive Ba out of the area, since the drought was thought to leave with her.

Legacy

She is considered one of the four ancestors of the jiangshi, the reanimated corpses of Chinese folklore.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Shijing, the Shanhaijing and the Book of the Later Han, and the Shenyijing of the Han period describes her as a small figure who ran naked, as fast as the wind.

Trivia

One of the earliest goddesses. Nüba is counted among the first goddesses to appear in Chinese literature, already in the Shijing, an ancient collection of poetry.
A goddess who wanders. After the battle, Ba did not return to heaven but wandered to the northern parts of the earth.
