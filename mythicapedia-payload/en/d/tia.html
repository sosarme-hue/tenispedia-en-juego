daughter of Deucalion and mother of Macedon

Thyia

Thyia is a figure of Greek mythology linked to the origins of Macedonia. According to an ancient genealogy, she bore two sons to Zeus, and one of them gave his name to that region.

The myth

According to the genealogy handed down by Hesiod, Thyia was the daughter of Deucalion, the survivor of the flood, and of Pyrrha. With Zeus she had two sons, Magnes and Macedon.

Macedon was regarded as the eponym of Macedonia, the figure from whom the country took its name. In this way, Thyia is tied to the lineage that the Greeks attributed to the peoples of the region.

Ancient sources: She appears in Hesiod's lost Catalogue of Women, whose content survives in Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus's De Thematibus.
