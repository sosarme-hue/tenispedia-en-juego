Hindu deity, teacher of the Pashupata sect

Lakulisha

Lakulisha is a Shaivite teacher and reformer whom Hindu tradition regards as the 28th incarnation of Shiva and the founder or first formal preceptor of the Pashupata doctrine, one of the oldest sects of Shaivism.

The myth

According to the Puranas, Shiva chose to appear as a wandering monk called Lakulin or Nakulisha, accompanied by four disciples (Kushika, Garga, Mitra and Kaurushya) who would restore the worship of Pashupati. The tradition says he was born in the village of Karavan on the banks of the Narmada in Gujarat, and his name is usually read as lord of the staff or club.

Some texts place him as a contemporary of Vyasa and Krishna, and several claim he restored hatha yoga and aspects of Samkhya cosmology. Another tradition says he and his disciples installed a linga at Mahakalavana, formerly known as Kayavarohaneshvara.

Worship and religion

From the 4th century CE his images show him as a naked yogi holding a staff in his left hand and a citron (matulinga) in his right, either standing or seated. He is associated with celibacy and the retention of vital energy, symbolised by the upward-pointing linga.

Ancient sources: The Linga, Vayu, Kurma and Skanda Puranas speak of him, and the Pashupata Sutra is traditionally attributed to him, though its authorship is debated.
