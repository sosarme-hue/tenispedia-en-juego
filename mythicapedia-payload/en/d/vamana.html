dwarf avatar of Vishnu

Vamana

Vamana, the dwarf, is an avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu who appears as a small brahmin to restore the three worlds to the gods after the asura king Bali has seized them. He is counted as the fifth avatar of Vishnu in some Puranas and as the first of the Dashavatara in the Treta Yuga in others.

The myth

The tradition tells how the asura king Bali, though born into a demonic lineage, was devoted to Vishnu and defeated Indra, taking over the heavens. The gods sought refuge with Vishnu, who agreed to restore Indra. He took birth as Vamana, the son of the sage Kashyapa and Aditi, and approached Bali as a small brahmin.

During a grand sacrifice, Bali agreed to give him as much land as he could cover in three steps. Vamana then grew into a giant: one step covered the earth and the underworld, and the second covered the sky. With no room left for the third, Bali offered his own head. Vishnu blessed him, sent him down to the netherworld of Patala, and allowed him to rule there, while Indra regained his realm.

The Bhagavata Purana and other texts repeat the episode with variations. Vedic verses about Vishnu's three strides across the cosmos are often seen as its earliest root, which is why this form is also called Trivikrama, the one who took three steps.

Worship and religion

Vamana is worshipped as a form of Vishnu. The Brahma-vaivarta Purana describes the vamana-ekadashi, also called parivartini-ekadashi, observed on the bright half of the month of Bhadrapada (August-September). Devotees honour the dwarf form of the god and seek liberation, and the day is also said to mark the moment when Vishnu turns over in his sleep.

Ancient sources: He is first mentioned in the Vedas, in the Rigvedic verses about Vishnu's three strides, and is developed in the Brahmanas, the epics and the Puranas, such as the Garuda Purana and the Bhagavata Purana.
