protective deity of Palmyra, twin of Azizu

Arsu

Arsu was a protective deity worshipped mainly in Palmyra, and also across Syria and northern Arabia. He was paired with his twin brother Azizu and was linked to the protection of caravans and to war.

The myth

The surviving accounts are few and are known mostly from inscriptions. Arsu could be depicted as male or female, although the male form was the most common. According to tradition, he was the evening star, while his brother Azizu was the morning star.

On the ceiling of the great temple of Baal in Palmyra he appears as a planet orbiting Jupiter/Baal. He is often shown riding a camel and accompanied by Azizu, and both were regarded as protectors of caravans.

Worship and religion

He had a temple in Palmyra and was among the four most important cult sites in the city. His worship is also confirmed at the Temple of Adonis in Dura-Europos, where a relief shows him on a camel with an inscription calling him 'Arsu the camel-rider'.

Legacy

In pre-Islamic Arabia he was equated with Ruda, an important protective deity whose cult spread across northern Arabia. In Edessa he was called Monimos. The sources record no later presence in art or language.

Ancient sources: Inscriptions from Palmyra and Dura-Europos, and references from Edessa. No ancient literary texts describing him are cited.

Trivia

A camel-rider in stone. In a relief from the Temple of Adonis at Dura-Europos, Arsu appears on a camel. The inscription calls him 'Arsu the camel-rider' and was made by a sculptor named Oga for the life of his son.
Evening and morning star. Arsu and his twin Azizu shared the two stars that mark night and day: Arsu was the evening star and Azizu the morning star.
