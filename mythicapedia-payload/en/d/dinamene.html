Greek nereid, sea nymph

Dynamene

Dynamene is a Greek Nereid, one of the fifty daughters of Nereus and Doris, sea nymphs associated with the power of great ocean swells. Her name means 'the capable one', and she appears in Homer's Iliad as a companion of the goddess Thetis.

The myth

According to tradition, Dynamene was one of the fifty Nereids, daughters of the Old Man of the Sea, Nereus, and the Oceanid Doris. Her name, a participle, means 'she who can, the capable one'. Together with her sister Pherusa, she was linked to the might of great ocean swells. One of her distinctive traits was her ability to appear and vanish rapidly.

In Homer's Iliad, Dynamene appears with her sisters to comfort Thetis when she cries out in grief over Achilles' sorrow at the death of his friend Patroclus.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad, Book XVIII, where she appears with Thetis.

Trivia

Name variants. Her name also appears as Dyomene and Dinamene, two older forms of the same figure.
A nymph who fades away. She was said to be able to appear and disappear very quickly, a trait less common among the Nereids.
