legendary king and personification of snow in Norse mythology

Snær

Snær is, in Norse mythology, a personification of snow, although ancient texts also present him as a legendary Scandinavian king. His name means 'snow' in Old Norse.

The myth

According to Icelandic tradition, Snær is the son of Frosti, 'frost', or of Jökul, 'icicle', depending on the account, and he descends from Kári, lord of the wind. His son is Thorri, and his daughters are Fön, Drífa and Mjöl, names that refer to snow in different forms. The Ynglinga saga tells how Vanlandi, ruler of Sweden, visited Snær in Finland, married his daughter Drífa and left in the spring, never to return.

In Danish tradition, Snær comes to the throne after the tale of the dog king sent by Hākun of Sweden. Through riddling talk he gets the king himself to confirm the dog's death, but he then rules cruelly. His end comes when his servant Roth, sent to the giant Lǣ, returns with a pair of gloves; when he puts them on in an assembly, lice devour him. Saxo Grammaticus places him much later, as son of King Sivald, and tells how he abducted the daughter of a king of Götaland.

Ancient sources: The Orkneyinga saga, the Hversu Noregr byggdist account in the Flatey Book, the Ynglinga saga, the Sturlaugs saga, the Annales Ryensis, the Chronicon Lethrense, the Annals of Lund and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

Daughters named after snow. Snær's three daughters have names describing forms of snow: Fön means snowdrift, Drífa snowfall and Mjöl powdered snow.
A dog as king. In the Danish account, the Danes receive a small dog sent from Sweden as their king, and its death opens the way to Snær's throne.
