horse demon of Hindu mythology

Keshi

Keshi is a horse-shaped demon in Hindu mythology, defeated by Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu. His slaying is one of the best-known episodes of Krishna's youth.

The myth

According to the Bhagavata Purana, the tyrant Kamsa, king of Mathura and Krishna's maternal uncle, learned he would die at his nephew's hands. After other demons failed, he sent Keshi to kill Krishna and Balarama. Keshi appeared as a gigantic horse whose neighing terrified everyone. Krishna challenged him, thrust his arm into the beast's mouth, and as the arm grew, the demon choked to death. The gods and the sage Narada praised Krishna, who has been called Keshava ever since.

Worship and religion

At Vrindavan, the Keshi Ghat on the Yamuna river is a bathing site linked to Krishna's victory.

Legacy

He appears in the Bhagavad Gita, where Arjuna calls Krishna the slayer of Keshi, and the name entered the Vishnu sahasranama.

Ancient sources: Bhagavata Purana, Vishnu Purana and Harivamsa; the Atharvaveda describes him as a demon that attacks fetuses.

Trivia

A demon of the womb. In the Atharvaveda, Keshi appears as a demon that attacks female fetuses, before he is linked to Krishna.
Split in two. In the Vishnu Purana, Krishna not only chokes Keshi but tears his body into two halves; the Bhagavata Purana does not mention this.
