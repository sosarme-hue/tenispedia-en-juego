Andean god of death and lord of the underworld

Supay

Supay is an Andean god of death and lord of the underworld, an ambivalent figure who could protect or harm. He matters because he embodies the balance between good and evil in Inca religion, and because he was later identified with the Christian devil.

The myth

According to tradition, Viracocha created him to show humanity that there would always be evil in the world, but never enough. The Incas therefore saw him as the being who balanced the scales between good and evil, and he was considered a brother of Inti, Illapa, and Mama Quilla.

He was unpredictable. He could favor those he liked or who had died a worthy death, and punish the rest; he could turn a holy man wicked and a wise man mad. He appeared in any form and place, reminding gods and humans that everything beautiful could vanish in an instant. Accounts also credit him with creating humans, who later became demons of mountains, waters, and caves.

As guide of the dead, his role recalls that of Anubis. According to Guamán Poma de Ayala, deceased Inca rulers turned into Supay and demanded veneration; their mummies were kept in caves called machay and carried out in ceremonies. The 1560 lexicon of Domingo de Santo Tomás linked the bad angels to the term manaalliçupay, a reading later questioned by the clergyman Bartolomé Álvarez.

Worship and religion

Worshippers offered gifts, built altars, and performed rituals to obtain favors, for good or for ill. According to the sources, some people still believe in his power and venerate him today.

Legacy

The Spanish identified him with the Devil, and in Quechua the word supay eventually came to mean the Christian demon.

Ancient sources: The 1560 lexicon of Domingo de Santo Tomás and the Nueva corónica y buen gobierno by Guamán Poma de Ayala.

Trivia

A trickster. He is classed as a trickster, a deceiver who can protect travelers on their way to the afterlife or push them to reveal the worst of themselves.
A demonic look. He is described with long horns, glassy star-like eyes, and a feline face with long fangs and ears.
