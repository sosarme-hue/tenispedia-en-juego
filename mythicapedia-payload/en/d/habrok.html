hawk from Norse mythology

Hábrók

Hábrók is a hawk from Norse mythology, named in the poem Grímnismál of the Poetic Edda as the best of hawks. Its name, usually translated as "High Pants", is nearly all that is known about it.

The myth

Ancient texts say very little about Hábrók. It appears in Grímnismál, a poem that lists names representing the best of each kind of thing: the best tree is Yggdrasil, the best ship Skíðblaðnir, the best steed Sleipnir and the best hound Garm. Among them is Hábrók, named as the best of hawks. Snorri Sturluson repeats the same list in his Gylfaginning, but he offers no stories about this bird either.

No deeds, family or ending are recorded for Hábrók. Its name, understood as "High Pants", may refer to the bird's long legs, although this is only one possible reading.

Ancient sources: Grímnismál in the Poetic Edda and Gylfaginning by Snorri Sturluson, in the Prose Edda.
