nymph of the island of Aegina in Greek mythology

Aegina

Aegina is, in Greek mythology, the nymph who gave her name to the island of the same name in the Saronic Gulf. Mother of Aeacus, and thus ancestor of Peleus and Achilles, her story is central to myths about the origins of Greek heroic lineages.

The myth

According to tradition, Aegina was the daughter of the river god Asopus and the nymph Metope, although the sources do not fully agree on her parentage or on the number of her sisters. Zeus fell in love with her and carried her off to an island near Attica that was then called Oenone and afterwards took her name. Apollodorus and Pindar tell that the god came to her as a flame, while other accounts speak of an eagle.

Asopus set out to look for her and reached Corinth, where King Sisyphus told him that Zeus was the abductor. As he pursued the god, Zeus drove him back to his own riverbed with thunderbolts, and it was said that for this reason his waters still carried embers. From her union with Zeus came Aeacus, who became king of the island, then uninhabited. Jealous Hera poisoned the waters and sent a serpent to torment the child, according to Hyginus, who adds further variants.

Aegina also bore Menoetius to Actor, who ruled at Opus and was the father of Patroclus, Achilles' companion. Other accounts name Damocrateia, daughter of Zeus, married to Menoetius. Some authors also give Aegina a son by Ares, Sinope, though she is usually considered the sister of Sinope.

Legacy

Aegina gave her name to the island and, in modern astronomy, to one of the asteroids.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus, Pindar, Hyginus and Ovid recount her abduction and descendants, and scholia add variants about her genealogy.
