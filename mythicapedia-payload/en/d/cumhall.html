leader of the Fianna and father of Fionn mac Cumhaill

Cumhall

Cumhall, son of Trénmhoir, is a leader of the Fianna in the Fenian Cycle of Irish mythology and the father of Fionn mac Cumhaill. His story of love, abduction and war opens the legend of his son.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cumhall was the son of a petty king and served the high king Conn Cét-Chathach, to whom he was also related. He wanted to marry Muirne, daughter of the druid Tadg mac Nuadat, but Tadg refused him, so Cumhall carried her off by force. Tadg appealed to Conn, who gave Cumhall the choice of returning the young woman or going into exile. Cumhall refused, and Conn made war on him.

In the Battle of Cnucha, at the site of today's Castleknock, Cumhall was killed by Goll mac Morna, who then took over leadership of the Fianna. Muirne was pregnant, and after her father ordered her burned, she sought Conn's protection. In exile she gave birth to a boy, Demni, who would later become known as Fionn.

Ancient sources: The earliest account is the Fotha Catha Chnucha (The Cause of the Battle of Cnucha), preserved in the 12th-century Lebor na hUidre, and the Macgnímartha Finn also covers him in a 15th-century manuscript.

Trivia

Burial place. Cumhall is reputedly buried within the grounds of Castleknock College, supposedly under a hill where an old water tower now stands.
