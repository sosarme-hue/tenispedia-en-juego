Nubian and Egyptian god of the Kingdom of Kush

Arensnuphis

Arensnuphis, also known as Arihesnefer or Arsnufis, is a deity of the Kingdom of Kush in ancient Nubia, widely worshipped at Meroe and later in the part of Nubia under Egyptian control. He matters because he shows how Egyptian and Nubian beliefs blended around the First Cataract of the Nile.

The myth

His exact role in mythology is unknown, but his epithets suggest a benevolent deity. He is shown as a lion or as a man wearing a crown of feathers and sometimes carrying a spear. As a lion he wears the atef crown, and at Esna he has a crocodile's head.

Tradition linked him with other gods. With Shu or Onuris, at Kalabsha and Dakka, he took part in the myth of the Distant Goddess, bringing the lioness Tefnut from Nubia to Egypt; she was Shu's sister and wife. With the Nubian god Dedun he received the epithet Lord of Punt, and at Dendur he is the Great God, Lord of Abatos, associated with Osiris.

His origin is debated. Some think he came from Egypt because he resembles Onuris; others see a Nubian deity, perhaps with two forms, an Egyptian one in Lower Nubia and a Meroitic one in Upper Nubia.

Worship and religion

His worship peaked under Ptolemy IV, especially at Philae, Kalabsha and Dakka. At Philae he was called the 'companion' of Isis, and a small kiosk-type temple was built in his honour there, a joint project with the Meroitic king Arqamani. At Dendur he formed a triad with Isis and Harpocrates. Over time, Osiris gradually displaced him in Nubia. Today no living cult surrounds him.

Legacy

His image appears on a relief from the temple of Dendur, now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, and in the chapel of Adikhalamani in the temple of Debod, which stands in Madrid.

Ancient sources: He is documented mainly in temple inscriptions and reliefs at Philae, Kalabsha, Dendur and Debod, as well as a Saite-period priest mentioned alongside Khnum.

Trivia

A temple in Madrid. The temple of Debod, which contains the chapel where Arensnuphis appears, was moved from Egypt and now stands in Madrid.
A relief in New York. The Dendur relief showing him is held in the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York.
