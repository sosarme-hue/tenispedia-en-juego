Japanese god of the Suwa region

Moreya

Moreya (also Moriya) is a Japanese god of the Suwa region in Nagano Prefecture, best known for his battle with Takeminakata, the deity of the Grand Shrine of Suwa. He is regarded as the mythical ancestor of the Moriya clan, a priestly family linked to the shrine.

The myth

According to local tradition, Moreya was the original lord of the Lake Suwa region, and he resisted the arrival of Takeminakata, who wanted to settle there or conquer it. In the best-known version, the Suwa deity, or his companion the boy Arikazu, defeated Moreya in battle. Moreya, armed with an iron ring or hook, was beaten with a wisteria (fuji) branch, and he fled to the mountain that now bears his name.

The story appears in the Suwa Daimyōjin Ekotoba (1356), where it explains the origin of an auxiliary shrine. Other medieval and modern texts tell it differently, and some call the god Moriya Daijin, a title more often used for the historical Mononobe no Moriya. Many historians think the myth preserves an ancient political conflict, though this is an interpretation rather than a documented event.

Worship and religion

Moreya is venerated as a local tutelary deity at Moriya Shrine in Okaya City. His priestly line, the Moriya clan, held the office of Jinchō at the Upper Suwa Shrine, where they presided over ceremonies and summoned the Mishaguji spirits.

Ancient sources: The Suwa Daimyōjin Ekotoba (1356), the Suwa Nobushige Gejō and Matsuzawa Yoshiakira's Ken'yū Hongi record versions of the myth.
