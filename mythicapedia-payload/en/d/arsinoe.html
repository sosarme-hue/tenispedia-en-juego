daughter of Phegeus and wife of Alcmaeon

Arsinoë

Arsinoë is a figure of Greek myth, daughter of Phegeus, king of Psophis in Arcadia. Her story belongs to the cycle of Alcmaeon, shaped by the pursuit of the Erinyes and by the family feud over Eriphyle's necklace and robe.

The myth

According to the myth, Alcmaeon came to the court of Phegeus fleeing the Erinyes, who pursued him for killing his mother, Eriphyle. Phegeus offered him shelter, and Alcmaeon married his daughter Arsinoë.

Advised by the Delphic oracle, Alcmaeon sought land purified by the river Achelous, newly formed soil from a meander that was free of the curse. There he took Callirrhoe, the river's daughter, as wife and had two sons with her. Callirrhoe, fearing she would lose her beauty, demanded the necklace and robe of Eriphyle that Alcmaeon had given Arsinoë. To obtain them, he returned to Psophis and deceived Phegeus, promising to dedicate the jewels at Delphi.

When Phegeus discovered the deception, he ordered his sons to kill Alcmaeon. Arsinoë reproached her brothers for the deed, and they shut her in a chest and took her to Tegea, where she was given as a slave to Agapenor. Callirrhoe asked Zeus to let her sons grow up at once so they could avenge their father; once granted, they killed Arsinoë's sons, who were travelling to Delphi with the necklace and robe, and then went to Psophis and killed Phegeus too.
