Chinese money toad of folklore and feng shui

Chan Chu

The Jin Chan, or Money Toad, is a three-legged Chinese charm linked to wealth and prosperity. Part of the folklore of the Han people and of feng shui practice, it is placed in homes and businesses to attract money and guard against bad luck.

The myth

In the best-known tale, a village boy threw a stone at a frog and left it with three legs. Years later, regretting it, he fed it every day without ever seeing it come out. When his sick son needed costly medicine the family could not afford, the frog appeared croaking, with a gold coin in its mouth, and the father bought the cure. Another version identifies it as the wife of one of the Eight Immortals, turned into a three-legged frog for her greed and sent out each night to gather coins.

Worship and religion

The charm is placed on the floor near the door, facing inward and never directly toward the entrance, since it might leave and never return. It should not sit on high furniture or in front of a mirror. Some traditions link it to the Sheng Chi direction or to the southeast. It remains a household charm used in homes and shops.

Trivia

Seven spots. In its traditional description, the toad often displays seven diamond-shaped spots on its back.
Forbidden places. Feng shui advice holds that it should never be kept in the bathroom, bedroom, dining room or kitchen.
