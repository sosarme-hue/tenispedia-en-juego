mythical weapon of Norse mythology

Lævateinn

Lævateinn is a magical weapon in Norse mythology, briefly mentioned in the Eddic poem Fjölsvinnsmál. Its nature remains debated: scholars have read it as a sword, a dart or a magic wand, and its name is usually rendered as 'wand of destruction' or 'wounding wand'.

The myth

According to the lore preserved in the Poetic Edda, the weapon was forged by Loki and later passed through the hands of Surtr and then Freyr. In the poem Fjölsvinnsmál it is presented as the only weapon able to defeat the rooster Viðofnir, who perches atop the world tree, so whoever seeks to complete the quest must obtain it.

Tradition links it with fire, through its connection to Loki and Surtr, and some scholars compare it with the spear of Lugh in Celtic mythology. Some interpretations, which are not unanimous, also connect it with the mistletoe that killed Balder, a view other scholars reject.

Legacy

The weapon appears in many video games, series and card games, such as Persona 5, Touhou Project, God of War (2018), where the protagonist's weapon is linked to fire, and Yu-Gi-Oh!, among others. It has also given its name to Japanese and Korean fictional works.

Ancient sources: It appears in the poem Fjölsvinnsmál of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A name with three readings. The term can be understood as 'cunning', 'deception' or 'injury', which explains the different translations proposed.
