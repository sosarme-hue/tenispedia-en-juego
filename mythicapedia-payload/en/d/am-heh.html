minor god of the Egyptian underworld

Am-heh

Am-heh was a minor god of the Egyptian underworld, whose name is usually rendered as “devourer of millions” or “eater of eternity”. His image, a man with the head of a hunting dog, makes him one of the most unsettling figures of the Egyptian afterlife.

The myth

In Egyptian mythology, the underworld was a realm the deceased had to travel through before reaching eternal life, full of gates, guardians and trials. Within that world, Am-heh was a feared figure. Accounts describe him as a man with the head of a hunting dog who lived in a lake of fire, a setting that reinforces the idea of punishment and destruction common in Egyptian beliefs about the afterlife.

His name suggests a being that consumes everything given to it, which is why he is linked to total annihilation. Some traditions present him as an aspect of Ammit, the personification of divine retribution, who punished those who failed the judgment of the dead. The connection is better understood as a shared role than as a full identity: both embody the harshest side of the fate awaiting souls.

According to the tradition, Am-heh could only be repelled by the god Atum, a creator deity and solar figure associated with Heliopolis. Atum's power was the only safeguard against a being that embodied the extreme danger of judgment and the fire of the underworld.
