mythical forefather of the Macedonians in Greek mythology

Makedon

Makedon is the name of several eponymous figures in Greek mythology, regarded as the founder of the Macedonian people and the namesake of their land. His genealogy mattered because ancient writers used it to argue whether the Macedonians belonged to the Hellenic world.

The myth

In Hesiod's Catalogue of Women, Makedon was the son of Zeus and Thyia, daughter of Deucalion and Pyrrha, and brother of Magnes. His uncle was Hellen, progenitor of the Greeks. After marrying a local woman, he had two sons, Pierus and Amathus, who gave their names to the cities of Pieria and Amathia. Makedon conquered the land then belonging to Thrace and called it Macedonia after himself. He lived in a palace near Mount Olympus and Pieria.

Other traditions make him a son of Aeolus, according to Hellanicus, or a son of Osiris. Diodorus Siculus, who blends Greek and Egyptian myth, says Makedon accompanied his father on campaigns alongside Anubis and fought wearing a wolf's skin. In a later tradition he appears as one of the fifty sons of the impious Lycaon, king of Arcadia.

Legacy

The name of Makedon gave rise to the name of the region of Macedonia. Ancient genealogists used him to debate whether the Macedonians were Greek, a question that still engages modern historians.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Catalogue of Women, Hellanicus of Lesbos, Diodorus Siculus and Apollodorus.

Trivia

A wolf in the armour. Diodorus describes Makedon fighting with the forequarters of a wolf's skin, an animal the Egyptians held in honour.
A name with many versions. Ancient sources give Makedon different fathers, including Zeus, Aeolus, Osiris or Lycaon, depending on the author.
