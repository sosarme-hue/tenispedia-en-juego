giantess in Norse mythology

Þökk

Þökk (also Thökk) is a giantess in Norse mythology whose name means 'thanks'. She is often identified with Loki, because she refused to weep for Baldr, which kept him from returning from Hel.

The myth

After Baldr was killed, Hermóðr rode to Hel to ask for his return. The ruler of that realm agreed on one condition: that everything in the world weep for him. The Æsir sent messengers across the earth, and everything wept except a giantess they found in a cave. She called herself Þökk and answered that she would weep waterless tears, since she had never loved Baldr, living or dead. So he stayed in Hel.

The Prose Edda adds that many believed the giantess was in fact Loki, son of Laufey, the god who had done the most harm among the Æsir.

Ancient sources: Prose Edda (Gylfaginning, 49)

Trivia

A name meaning thanks. Þökk means 'thanks' in Old Norse and Icelandic, a name that contrasts with her refusal to help.
Waterless tears. The giantess promises to weep waterless tears, an ironic reply that is enough to leave Baldr in Hel.
