Japanese god of the sea

Watatsumi

Watatsumi is the most important of the sea gods in Japanese Shinto, ruling fish, tides and the creatures of the ocean. He is often identified with the dragon god Ryūjin, and his name came to mean simply "the sea" in Japanese.

The myth

According to tradition, Watatsumi arose when Izanagi washed himself in the sea after returning from Yomi, the land of the dead. The Kojiki counts him among the deities born to the creating pair, Izanagi and Izanami, and calls him by the honorific title Ōwatatsumi, "the great sea god". Accounts describe him as a green serpent-dragon, though he can also appear as an old man with gills. He lives in a vast undersea palace, Ryūgū-jō, and regulates the tides so regularly that people can predict them. He is generally benevolent and treats visitors to his palace as a good host would.

He also appears in stories of heroes and lineages. His daughter Otohime married Hoori, who had lost his brother Hoderi's fishhook. Watatsumi summoned the fish to search, the hook was found in the throat of a tahi, and he advised Hoori how to deal with his brother. A sea monster called Wani then carried the couple back to land. Their child Toyotama-hime is presented as an ancestor of the imperial line.

Worship and religion

His main shrine is Shikaumi, on Shika Island in Fukuoka Prefecture. His messages usually arrive through Wani, but they may also come as violent or strange tides.

Legacy

The name Watatsumi is used in Japanese as a synecdoche for the sea, the ocean or the main body of water. His identification with Ryūjin has linked him with the sea dragon in popular Japanese imagination.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki (c. 712) and the Nihongi (c. 720) mention him in the creation account, in the story of Hoori and Otohime, and in legends of Emperors Keikō and Jimmu.

Trivia

A princess sacrificed. In the Nihongi, the concubine Oto-tachibana-hime throws herself into the sea to calm a storm believed to be the work of the sea god. The storm stops and the ship reaches the shore.
A hook in the throat. In the Kojiki, Hoori's lost fishhook is found in the throat of a tahi fish that could not swallow, after a search ordered by Watatsumi.
