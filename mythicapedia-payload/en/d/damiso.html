swift giant of Greek mythology

Damysus

Damysus is, according to Greek tradition, the fastest of all the giants, a son of Uranus and Gaia. His story, preserved by a single author, links him to Achilles, the one of swift feet.

The myth

In the account preserved by Photius and attributed to Ptolemy Hephaestion, Thetis secretly burned the children she had with Peleus in the fire. When her seventh son was born, Peleus snatched him from the flames; the boy, Achilles, survived, though his ankle bone was burnt. Peleus entrusted him to the centaur Chiron, who dug up the body of the giant Damysus, buried at Pallene, took his ankle bone and fixed it into Achilles' foot.

That borrowed bone later failed the hero when Apollo pursued him during the Trojan War: it broke, and Achilles could be killed. Photius also explains that the epithet Podarces is a pun on 'feet' and Arce, a sister of Iris whose wings Zeus tore off to give as a wedding gift at the marriage of Thetis and Peleus.

As for the giants' origin, the tradition says Gaia, angered by the Titans, bore them with Uranus. They were enormous, with thick hair and dragon-scale feet, and hurled burning oak trees and rocks. Some placed their birth at Phlegra, others at Pallene.

Ancient sources: His story appears in the New History of Ptolemy Hephaestion, known through Photius' summary.
