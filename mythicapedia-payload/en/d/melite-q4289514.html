heroine of Greek mythology

Melite

Melite is a figure of Greek mythology, daughter of Apollo in the most common version, who gave her name to the Attic deme of Melite. Her story matters because it links her to Heracles and to the local cult of this hero in Attica.

The myth

According to a scholiast on Aristophanes, Melite was a lover of Heracles during his stay in Attica, where she was initiated into the lesser mysteries.

Tradition presents her as a daughter of Apollo, though she is also given the alternative name Myrmex, showing that her origin was not fixed. She was also said to have been a companion of Poseidon.

Worship and religion

In the deme of Melite stood a temple of Heracles Alexikakos, meaning Heracles the Protector from Evil, which places the memory of the heroine within a sanctuary dedicated to the hero.

Legacy

Some modern scholars have recognized Melite and Heracles among the figures beside Demeter on the right half of the west pediment of the Parthenon, though this remains an interpretation.

Ancient sources: A scholiast on Aristophanes is the main ancient source that names her as a lover of Heracles.

Trivia

A deme named after a heroine. The Attic deme of Melite took its name from this figure, and it housed a temple of Heracles Alexikakos.
A possible presence on the Parthenon. Some scholars believe she can be recognized beside Demeter on the west pediment of the Parthenon.
