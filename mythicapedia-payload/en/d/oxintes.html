mythical king of Athens, son of Demophon

Oxyntes

Oxyntes is a mythical king of Athens, the thirteenth ruler in the traditional list of the city's kings and a member of the line of Theseus, as he was the son of Demophon and grandson of the hero. His interest lies mainly in leading the final stretch of that royal line.

The myth

According to tradition, Oxyntes was the son of Demophon and Phyllis and, therefore, a grandson of Theseus. He held the thirteenth place in the succession of Athenian kings, a sequence that ancient chroniclers arranged to offer a chronology of the city. Like other names in those lists, he has hardly any stories of his own: his role is to link his predecessors with those who came after him.

No concrete deeds from his reign survive. He had two sons, Apheidas and Thymoetes, who reigned one after the other, so the throne passed from father to son and then from brother to brother. Thymoetes is regarded as the last descendant of Theseus on the Athenian throne.

A late testimony, attributed to Eusebius, gives Thymoetes a reign of twelve years and places during that period a striking episode: the Amazons burned down the temple of Artemis at Ephesus. This is a late chronological claim rather than a historical event, and the story shows how the myths of Athens were woven together with the traditions of other peoples of the Aegean and Asia Minor.

Trivia

Twelve years of rule. According to Eusebius, Thymoetes ruled for twelve years, a detail that appears only in late chronologies.
The Amazons at Ephesus. During his reign, according to the same tradition, the Amazons set fire to the temple of Artemis at Ephesus.
