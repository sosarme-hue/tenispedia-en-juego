Amycus

Amycus (Ancient Greek: Ἄμυκος) is a centaur, son of Ophion, who appears in the Centauromachy, the battle between the centaurs and the Lapiths. He is best remembered as the first centaur to kill a Lapith in the fight.

The myth

Little is known about Amycus, but tradition places him among the centaurs who attended the wedding of the Lapith king Pirithous and Hippodamia. In Ovid's Metamorphoses, the wine drove the centaurs wild, and Eurytus tried to seize the bride, sparking an assault on the Lapith women.

After Theseus killed Eurytus, Amycus, enraged, smashed the skull of the innocent Lapith Celadon with a candlestick, the first Lapith death of the battle. Pelates of Pella clubbed him down with a leg from a maple table and sent him to Tartarus. The Lapiths eventually won and drove the centaurs out of Thessaly to the northwest.

Legacy

The clash of centaurs and Lapiths was a popular subject in art, and two of its best-known versions are the Parthenon metopes by Phidias and a Renaissance sculpture by Michelangelo. The asteroid 55576 Amycus is named after him.

Ancient sources: Ovid mentions him in the Metamorphoses while narrating the wedding of Pirithous and Hippodamia.

Trivia

An asteroid named after him. The asteroid 55576 Amycus is named after this centaur.
A furniture weapon. According to the story, Pelates brought Amycus down with a leg from a maple table, a detail that highlights how improvised the fight was.
