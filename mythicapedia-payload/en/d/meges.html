Greek hero of Dulichium and the Echinades

Meges

Meges, son of the charioteer Phyleus, was a Greek hero who led the Epeans along with the contingents of Dulichium and the Echinades in the Trojan War. He appears in several episodes of the Trojan story, and ancient authors disagree about how he died.

The myth

According to tradition, Meges was the son of Phyleus, a favourite of Zeus, but he left his homeland after quarrelling with his father and settled in Dulichium, which is why he is often called Meges Phyleides. His mother has no fixed name: authors give Timandra, Ctimene, Eustyoche, Ctesimache or Hagnete. He had a sister, Eurydameia, who married the seer Polyidus.

At Troy he is credited with killing as many as seven opponents, among them Pedaeus, Croesmus and Agelaus. Dolops tried to strike him with a spear, but a breastplate that Euphetes of Ephyra had given to his father saved his life. He was also one of Helen's suitors, one of the warriors hidden in the Trojan Horse, and he helped Odysseus gather gifts to win over Achilles.

The accounts of his end differ: some say he fell at Troy, while others say he died in Euboea, together with Prothous.

Ancient sources: Dictys Cretensis, who places his death at Troy; Pausanias, who describes a painting of his wound; and Tzetzes, who relates his death in Euboea.

Trivia

A family breastplate. The breastplate that saved his life had been a gift from Euphetes of Ephyra to his father, Phyleus.
A seer for a brother-in-law. His sister Eurydameia married the seer Polyidus, linking him to a figure of prophecy in the tradition.
