female sea spirit of Slavic folklore

Moryana

Moryana is a female sea spirit in Slavic folklore, possibly a goddess, linked to storms and to the winds that blow off the sea.

The myth

In Slavic tradition, Moryana was a vodyanitsa, daughter of the Sea Tsar, and in some beliefs she also ruled the winds. She swam in the form of a large fish and came ashore only on calm evenings. When the Sea Tsar grew angry and a storm rose, she calmed him down. In another version she was a stern, very tall woman who controlled the southeast winds at the mouth of the Volga and sank ships in the Caspian. Her great enemy was Ded Shapka Dranaya, lord of the northwest wind, whom she almost always defeated.

Legacy

Her name, built on more ("sea"), gave rise to a regional Russian term, morianka, for the cold winds that blow from the sea toward the land. Because of the resemblance of her name, she was sometimes confused with the death goddess Morana.

Trivia

Golden canoe. She was said to ride the sea in a golden canoe, and her beauty was so dazzling that no one could look at her all at once.
A family of spirits. When the moryany were imagined as a family, they were giant maidens who waited for ships beside coastal cliffs and rocked them until they were wrecked.
