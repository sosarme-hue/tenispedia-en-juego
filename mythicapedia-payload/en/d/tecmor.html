primordial god of Greek mythology

Tecmor

Tecmor is a primordial god or concept in Greek mythology, known only from a cosmogonic fragment attributed to the poet Alcman. His name means "goal" or "limit", which makes him a useful example of how the Greeks imagined the origin of the cosmos.

The myth

According to the fragment, at the beginning there was a confused, mixed substance, from which emerged Poros, the path or way, and Tecmor, the goal at the end of that way.

Later, Tetis, whom the commentary understands as Thesis, meaning creation or order, brought organisation to a disordered cosmos. From the combined power of these three concepts, Thesis, Poros and Tecmor, came a new generation of primordial deities: day, the moon, darkness, the glimmers of the stars and the sun.

Tecmor appears in no other known story, so his role can only be reconstructed from this single text.

Ancient sources: A cosmogonic fragment attributed to Alcman, preserved in an ancient commentary that quotes it.

Trivia

A name with several forms. The word appears as tékmor or tékmar in Greek, two variants of the same term meaning end, limit or goal.
A god known from a single fragment. Tecmor is known only from a quotation in one text, so almost no other information about him survives.
