afterlife tale that closes Plato's Republic

Myth of Er

The Myth of Er is the tale that concludes Plato's Republic: a soldier from Pamphylia who dies in battle and comes back to life on his funeral pyre to describe what he saw in the afterlife. It matters for its vision of the fate of souls, with rewards, punishments and rebirth, which shaped religious, philosophical and scientific thought for centuries.

The myth

In Plato's account, told through Socrates, Er, son of Armenios, dies in battle, and his body does not decay for ten days. When he is already on the pyre, he revives and tells how his soul had travelled with others to a marvellous place where judges sent the just along a path to the sky and the unjust down into the earth, where they paid tenfold for each wrong they had committed.

After seven days of travel, the souls reach the Spindle of Necessity, where the Fates and the Sirens accompany the turning of the heavens. Each soul chooses its next life in the order of the lots drawn: a tyrant picks a dictatorship without foreseeing its crimes, while Odysseus, weary of fame, chooses a quiet, ordinary existence. They then drink from the River of Forgetfulness, which erases memory, and are reborn. Er, unable to drink, remembers everything and returns to his body.

Legacy

The tale served Plato to explain the cosmos: the Spindle of Necessity carries eight orbits, which are identified with the spheres of the fixed stars, the five classical planets, the Sun and the Moon. Its vision of justice and the fate of souls shaped philosophy and religious thought for centuries. Some scholars have compared it with the Armenian legend of King Ara the Handsome.

Ancient sources: Plato, Republic, Book X (614b-621b), where the tale closes the dialogue.

Trivia

A spindle with eight orbits. Plato describes the Spindle of Necessity with eight circles, which scholars identify with the spheres of the fixed stars, the five classical planets, the Sun and the Moon.
A body that does not decay. Er's body remains undecomposed for ten days and he revives two days later, when he is already on the funeral pyre.
