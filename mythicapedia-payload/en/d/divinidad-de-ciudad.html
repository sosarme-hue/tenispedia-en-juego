City God

The City God (Chenghuangshen, 城隍神) is a tutelary deity in Chinese folk religion. Believers hold that he protects the people and affairs of a village, town or large city, as well as its counterpart in the afterlife.

The myth

Its origins go back over two thousand years, when worship centred on a protective spirit of a city's walls and moats. Over time the post came to be held by the town's deified leader, often a deceased official who kept authority over the souls of his townsfolk and took part in the affairs of the living alongside other divine beings.

In imperial times City Gods were appointed for everything from small villages to whole provinces. According to tradition, three times a year the god accompanied processions and oversaw the local spirits of the dead: releasing them from their winter quarters, taking their census and feeding them, and on the tenth lunar month gathering them, providing winter clothing and settling them for the cold season.

Worship and religion

In official religion, worship followed written law and was carried out by officials and scholars. In popular religion, believers come to ask for good health, rain or protection in disasters, and on the god's birthday towns hold festivals with opera, fireworks and processions. Tradition held that if the god failed to bring rain when asked, his image could be exposed to the sun or punished.

Legacy

His figure remains present in temples in Hong Kong, Taiwan and Thailand, some of which draw many visitors today.
