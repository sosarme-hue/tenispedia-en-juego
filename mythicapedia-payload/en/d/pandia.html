Greek goddess of the full moon

Pandia

Pandia is a Greek goddess, daughter of Zeus and Selene, often identified with the full moon. Her name means "all brightness" or "all radiance". She is a minor figure, known mainly from the Homeric Hymn to Selene and from an Athenian festival that may have been connected to her.

The myth

According to the Homeric Hymn to Selene, the Son of Cronos joined with the goddess in love, and she conceived and bore a daughter, Pandia, exceptionally lovely among the deathless gods. The stories say little more about her: she has no deeds or episodes of her own.

Other sources are briefer. A scholion suggests that Pandia may originally have been only a local epithet of Selene, and Hyginus names her as a daughter of Jupiter and Luna without further detail. An Athenian tradition made her the wife of Antiochus, the eponymous hero of one of the ten Athenian tribes. Other sources say that Zeus and Selene produced Nemea or Herse, perhaps different versions of the same figure.

Worship and religion

The Pandia, a festival about which very little is known, was probably held at the full moon and may have been dedicated to Zeus, though it could also have been connected to her. Photius is the source who records it. The sources do not show whether she had a cult of her own or temples dedicated to her.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymn to Selene, a scholion, Hyginus and Photius, late authors who record her name and her festival.

Trivia

Wife of an Athenian hero. An Athenian tradition made her the wife of Antiochus, the hero who gave his name to one of the ten tribes of Athens.
Perhaps only an epithet. A scholion suggests Pandia was originally a local epithet of Selene that later became a separate daughter.
