wife of Deucalion, survivor of the flood in Greek mythology

Pyrrha

Pyrrha is, in Greek mythology, the wife of Deucalion and one of the two people who repopulated the earth after the great flood. In the most common account she was the daughter of Epimetheus and Pandora, though some sources make her the daughter of Prometheus.

The myth

According to the myth, Zeus decided to end the Bronze Age with a great deluge. Prometheus, who foresaw the catastrophe, warned his son Deucalion, who built an ark in which the couple survived. When the waters subsided, they consulted the oracle of Themis on how to repopulate the world and were told to throw the bones of their mother over their shoulders. They understood the mother to be Gaia and the bones to be stones: those Deucalion threw became men, and those Pyrrha threw became women.

In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Jove takes pity on the pair for their devotion and ends the flood. Pyrrha hesitates at first out of respect for her mother, until Deucalion points out that the oracle means the earth.

The genealogies vary. According to the Library of Apollodorus, their children were Hellen, Amphictyon and Protogeneia; other sources name Orestheus or Melantho, and some claim that Hellen was the son of Pyrrha and Zeus.

Legacy

Horace and Ovid both allude to her blonde or reddish hair. Achilles, hidden as a maiden at the court of King Lycomedes, received the name Pyrrha because of his hair colour. According to some authors, Thessaly was formerly called Pyrrhaea in her honour.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Catalogue of Women), Ovid (Metamorphoses), Hyginus, Pindar and the Library of Apollodorus.

Trivia

A name for Achilles. Pyrrha was also the name under which Achilles was known while he hid, disguised as a maiden, at the court of King Lycomedes.
The first mortal. Hyginus says Pyrrha was the first mortal woman ever born, while other versions name Niobe, daughter of Phoroneus, as the first mortal loved by Zeus.
