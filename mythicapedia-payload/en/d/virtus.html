Roman goddess of bravery and military strength

Virtus

In Roman religion, Virtus was the personification of bravery and military excellence, the virtus that Romans regarded as essential for a citizen and a soldier. She was worshipped alongside Honos, the god of honour, and her image later passed into funerary art and modern political iconography.

The myth

Ancient sources say little about independent myths of Virtus; her importance lies mainly in what she represented. As an abstract divinity she was linked with Honos, and the two shared a cult. According to Valerius Maximus, around 210 B.C.E. Marcus Claudius Marcellus planned a single temple for both, but the College of Pontiffs objected: if a miracle happened in the shrine, the priests would not know which of the two to thank with a sacrifice.

Marcellus solved the problem by building two separate temples, funded with spoils from the sack of Syracuse and the defeat of the Gauls. The shrine of Virtus stood near the Porta Capena in Rome and was later restored by Vespasian. In another account, one entered the temple of Virtus before that of Honos, symbolising that honour could only be won through military success.

Worship and religion

Virtus was worshipped in Rome in her own temple near the Porta Capena, paired with that of Honos. Her veneration was tied to military careers and civic prestige. The sources contain no information on any modern worship of her.

Legacy

Roman art showed her as a matron, an old man or a young man, sometimes with a javelin or helmet, and on funerary reliefs she always appeared beside a male figure. In 1776 she became the central figure of the Seal of Virginia and of the state flag, and she also appeared on the Virginia four-dollar note.

Ancient sources: Valerius Maximus records the story of Marcellus's temple, and Roman tradition associates her with the cult of Honos.

Trivia

A temple in two parts. According to one account, one had to pass through the temple of Virtus before reaching that of Honos, symbolising that honour is earned through military success.
