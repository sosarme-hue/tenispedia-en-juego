legendary king of Sparta of the Eurypontid dynasty

Polydectes of Sparta

Polydectes was a king of Sparta from the Eurypontid dynasty, a figure who belongs more to tradition than to documented history. He is notable because ancient authors disagree about his lineage, and his reign is remembered as a time of peace.

The myth

Stories about Polydectes are few and mostly genealogical. According to Herodotus, he was the son of Pritanis and the father of Eunomus, placing him in a line of succession from father to son within the Eurypontid house.

Pausanias gives a different version, making him the son of Eunomus, which would place him a generation earlier in the chain of kings. Despite this disagreement, both authors agree that his rule brought calm to Sparta, and tradition remembers him as a peaceful king.

He is named as the successor of Eunomus, and Charilaus is also associated with him among his descendants. The dates assigned to him, around 835 to 805 BC, are a convention, since the chronology of these kings cannot be checked against contemporary documents. For this reason he is studied more as part of Spartan memory than as a securely historical figure.

Ancient sources: Herodotus and Pausanias mention him, giving different genealogies for the same king.

Trivia

Two genealogies. Herodotus makes him the son of Pritanis, while Pausanias presents him as the son of Eunomus. The difference shows how lists of Spartan kings varied among ancient authors.
A peaceful reign. Pausanias notes that peace prevailed in Sparta during his rule, one of the few things consistently attributed to him.
