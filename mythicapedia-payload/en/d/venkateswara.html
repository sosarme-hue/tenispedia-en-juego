form of Vishnu worshipped at Tirupati

Venkateswara

Venkateswara is a form of Vishnu worshipped as the presiding deity of the Venkateswara Temple in Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh. He belongs to the Hindu Vaishnava tradition and is one of the most visited deities in the world, receiving donations that run into enormous sums each year.

The myth

Tradition tells how the sage Bhrigu was sent to determine which of the three great gods deserved a sacrifice. Ignored by Brahma and Shiva, he reached Vaikuntha, where Vishnu was meditating beside Lakshmi. Furious, Bhrigu kicked Vishnu's chest; Vishnu, rather than retaliating, asked for his forgiveness and massaged the sage's feet, crushing the extra eye on his sole and thereby ending his pride. Lakshmi, offended, left Vaikuntha. Vishnu withdrew to the Venkata Hill, where he performed penance in an anthill beneath a tamarind tree, and was reborn as Srinivasa, son of Vakula Devi.

Srinivasa fell in love with Princess Padmavati, daughter of King Akasha, after chasing a wild elephant into a royal garden. After several turns of events, the wedding was financed with a loan from Kubera, the god of wealth. Soon afterwards, Brahma and Shiva explained to Lakshmi that Vishnu wished to remain on the seven hills to free humanity from the troubles of the Kali Yuga; he and his consorts became stone idols, with Lakshmi resting on his left side and Padmavati on his right.

Worship and religion

The Tirupati temple receives hundreds of thousands of devotees every year, and the image is believed to be swayambhu, or self-manifested. In the twelfth century Ramanuja reorganised the rituals according to the Vaikhanasa tradition and founded the Tirupati Jeeyar Matha in 1119, an institution that still oversees them. For believers, the worship remains very much alive.

Legacy

In northern India he is popularly known as Balaji. His mantra, "Om Namo Venkateshaya", is widely recited by devotees.

Ancient sources: Sri Venkatachala Mahatmyam, the Padma and Skanda Puranas, and Ramanuja's work on the temple's ritual practice.
