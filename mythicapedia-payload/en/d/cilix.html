eponymous hero of Cilicia, son of Agenor

Cilix

Cilix is a figure of Greek myth, a Phoenician prince son of King Agenor and the eponymous hero of Cilicia, a rugged region in southeastern Asia Minor that took its name from him. He matters because his story explains how a real ancient land got its name.

The myth

According to the tradition, Cilix was the son of King Agenor and Telephassa, and brother of Cadmus, Phoenix and Europa. Sources disagree about his parents: the oldest version makes him a son of Phoenix and Cassiope, while later writers made him a son of Agenor and Tyre. When Zeus, disguised as a white bull, carried Europa off to Crete, Agenor sent his sons to find her, ordering them not to come home without her.

The search failed. Cadmus went on to found Thebes in Boeotia, while Cilix gave up and settled in Asia Minor, near Phoenicia, by the river Pyramus. The land was named Cilicia in his honour.

Apollodorus tells of a daughter, Thebe, who married Corybas, son of Iasion and Cybele. Pherecydes considered Thasus, the eponymous hero of the island of Thasos, to be his son. Ancient authors never fully agreed on these genealogies.

Legacy

The name of Cilicia lasted as a Roman province, and its chief city, Tarsus, was the birthplace of Saint Paul, linking the region to the beginnings of Christianity. Nathaniel Hawthorne made Cilix a character in his Tanglewood Tales, where the hero, weary of searching for Europa, decides to stay and build a kingdom.

Ancient sources: Apollodorus (Library) and Pherecydes, who name him as father of Thasus and Thebe.

Trivia

An island with his kin. Thasus, whom some sources make a son or nephew of Cilix, gave his name to the island of Thasos in the northern Aegean.
A land yet to be excavated. In 1932 the scholar Martin P. Nilsson noted that Cilicia was almost unknown archaeologically, though Mycenaean colonization there had been suggested.
