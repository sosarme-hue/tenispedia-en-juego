Orestes

Orestes is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, whose story of revenge, madness and purification fed much of Athenian tragic theatre. Above all in Aeschylus' Oresteia, he became the model of a guilty man whom justice manages to save.

The myth

According to Homer, Orestes was away from Mycenae when Agamemnon returned from Troy, and it was Aegisthus, his wife's lover, who killed the king. Years later Orestes came back from Athens and avenged his father by slaying Aegisthus and his own mother. Pindar tells instead that as a child he was rescued by his nurse Arsinoe or by his sister Electra, and grew up in Phanote on Mount Parnassus under King Strophius, father of his friend Pylades. When he turned twenty, the Delphic oracle ordered him to avenge his father.

After the matricide the Erinyes hunted him until Athens tried him before the Areopagus. In Aeschylus, Athena presided; the votes were tied and she, with her deciding vote, acquitted him, and the Erinyes became the Eumenides. In Euripides, Apollo sends him to Tauris to steal a statue of Artemis; there he is captured and recognises his sister Iphigenia, the priestess who was to sacrifice him, and the three flee with the image.

Back in Greece, Orestes killed Aletes, son of Aegisthus, and ruled Mycenae, Argos and Laconia. He had been promised to his cousin Hermione, but Menelaus gave her to Neoptolemus; Orestes killed him, married her and came to rule the Peloponnese. His son Tisamenus was later killed by the Heracleidae. He is said to have died of a snakebite in Arcadia.

Worship and religion

His body was taken to Sparta, where he received worship. Herodotus reports that the Delphic oracle told the Spartans to move his bones before they could defeat Tegea, and that the tomb was found in a Tegean blacksmith's yard. The sources do not say whether this cult survived.

Legacy

Orestes is the protagonist of Jean-Paul Sartre's play The Flies (1943) and of Álvaro Cunqueiro's novel El hombre que se parecía a Orestes (1968), which won the Premio Nadal. He also gives his name to the first part of Thomas Wolfe's Of Time and the River (1935), to a 2000 song by A Perfect Circle, and to two concept albums by Virgin Steele. He appears too in the 1984 game Gift from the Gods.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in Homer's Odyssey, Pindar, Aeschylus' Oresteia, the Electras of Sophocles and Euripides, and Herodotus' Histories.

Trivia

A giant in Tegea. Herodotus reports that the remains found in Tegea measured seven cubits, which would make Orestes a giant.
An imperial relic. The grammarian Servius counts his ashes among the seven pignora imperii of Rome, kept in the Temple of Jupiter on the Capitoline.
