Maya god of wind, storm and fire

Huracán

Huracán is the Maya god of wind, storm and fire, also known as the Heart of Sky. He was one of the creator deities who took part in the attempts to shape humanity, and his name gave rise to the word for hurricanes.

The myth

According to Maya tradition, Huracán was one of the gods who took part in creating human beings, who in some accounts were made from maize. However, the first people, made of wood, angered the gods.

To punish them, Huracán unleashed the Great Flood, a universal deluge meant to destroy the wooden men. While the earth lay under water, he lived in the windy mists above the floodwaters and repeatedly called out for earth until land rose from the seas, pushing the water back to its original shores.

He is shown with a serpent's tail and a reptile-like appearance, with a single leg that let him cover great distances in a short time, a large crown, and a smoking object, possibly a torch. He is also described as walking on his hands, upside down.

Legacy

His name gave us the word hurricane for the weather phenomenon. Some sources suggest the term may come from a Carib word, huracan, which is also the source of the English hurricane and the European term orcan, a windstorm.

Trivia

Origin of the word. The name Huracán may come from a Carib word, and it is the source of the English words hurricane and orcan, a European windstorm.
A one-legged god. His name is understood as One-Leg and recalls god K of Maya iconography, a lightning deity with one human leg and one serpent-shaped leg.
Related deities. Related deities include Tohil in K'iche' mythology, Bolon Tzacab in Yucatec mythology, Cocijo in Zapotec mythology and Tezcatlipoca in Aztec mythology.
