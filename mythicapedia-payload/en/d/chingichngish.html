deity of the Mission Indians of Southern California

Chinigchinix

Chinigchinix is a central figure in the mythology of the Mission Indians of Southern California, including the Luiseño, Tongva and Acjachemem peoples. Most scholars treat him as a deity, although he has also been seen as a culture hero, lawgiver or prophet.

The myth

According to tradition, Chinigchinix appeared after the death of Wiyot, a tyrannical ruler of the first beings who was poisoned by his sons. That murder brought death into the world, and the creator Night divided the first human ancestors into distinct peoples, giving them languages and territories.

Accounts of his origin vary. Some say his parents were Tacu and Auzar, others that he was born of Mother Earth, and others that he had neither father nor mother. His most distinctive feature is a set of Chinigchinix avengers, such as Raven, Rattlesnake, Bear and Mountain Lion, who spied on people and enforced the moral code.

Worship and religion

His beliefs were linked to initiation ceremonies for adolescent boys, during which the hallucinogenic plant Datura (toloache, jimsonweed) was ingested. Mortars and winnowing trays were sacred to him. Many of these ritual elements were shared more widely than belief in the specific figure of Chinigchinix.

Legacy

His alternative name, Quaoar, was given to a large trans-Neptunian object and ringed dwarf planet discovered in 2002 and catalogued as 50000 Quaoar.

Ancient sources: The earliest accounts come from descriptions written in the 1820s by the Franciscan missionary Gerónimo Boscana about the people of Mission San Juan Capistrano.

Trivia

A name with many spellings. The name appears in many spellings, such as Chinigchinix or Quaoar, and its exact pronunciation is not known for certain.
A dwarf planet named after him. The trans-Neptunian object 50000 Quaoar, discovered in June 2002, was named after this deity.
