scene of the divine wedding of Shiva and Parvati

Kalyanasundara

Kalyanasundara ('beautiful wedding') is the iconographic depiction of the wedding of the Hindu gods Shiva and Parvati, a theme widely repeated in Indian art. It shows the panigrahana rite, in which the groom takes the bride's hand.

The myth

After the death of his first wife Sati, Shiva withdrew into deep meditation. The asura Tarakasura obtained a boon from Brahma that only a son of Shiva could kill him, and he spread terror. Parvati, Sati's reincarnation and daughter of the Himalayan god Himavan, performed severe austerities to win Shiva. To test her devotion, he appeared as an old ascetic who disparaged himself; only when she was about to leave did he reveal his true form and agree to marry her. Their son Kartikeya later slew Tarakasura.

Worship and religion

No sect is built around this icon, but in South Indian temples such as Chidambaram and Tiruvenkadu, bronze images are brought out during annual festivals and kept put away the rest of the year. At the Meenakshi Amman Temple, women worship it to find husbands.

Legacy

The scene decorates caves, sculptures and temple gopurams across India, including at Ellora and Elephanta.

Ancient sources: The Agamic texts Amsumadbhedagama, Uttara-kamaikagama and Purva-Karanagama prescribe its iconography.

In art

Trivia

Children before birth. Some scenes show Ganesha and Kartikeya, who are not yet born. They appear in the Rameshvara Cave at Ellora and in a 9th-century sculpture from Uttar Pradesh.
