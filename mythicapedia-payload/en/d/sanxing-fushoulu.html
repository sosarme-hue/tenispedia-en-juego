the three star gods of fortune, prosperity and longevity in Chinese folk religion

Sanxing (Fushoulu)

The Sanxing, also known as Fu, Lu and Shou, are three benevolent gods of Chinese folk religion who embody fortune, prosperity and longevity. They are shown together because each began as a celestial body, and their familiar human image took shape during the Ming dynasty.

The myth

According to tradition, each of the three gods began as a star. Shou, the star of longevity, was linked to Canopus, which Chinese astronomers called the Old Man of the South Pole. He is shown as a bald old man with white eyebrows, holding a peach of immortality and accompanied by a crane and a tortoise. He is said to decide how long each person lives, recording the age on a tablet, and the figure can be changed through offerings; in one account, an age is altered from 18 to 81.

Fu, the god of happiness and good fortune, was associated with Jupiter. A Taoist version identifies him with Yang Cheng, an official who risked his life to free dwarfs kept as slaves at court. After his death the people built him a temple, and over time he became the personification of good luck. He is often shown in scholar's robes, sometimes holding a child, and is frequently confused with Cai Shen, the god of wealth.

Lu, the god of salary and rank, was linked to Mizar. He is believed to be Zhang Xian, a bureaucrat who served the founder of the Han dynasty. People invoked him to pass imperial examinations and advance in the bureaucracy, and he is depicted in a mandarin's dress.

Worship and religion

Shou has no temple of his own, yet he is deeply respected, since a long life is considered an honour in China. The three gods appear on the altars of folk temples, ancestral shrines, homes and shops, especially at Chinese New Year, with offerings such as a glass of water or an orange. They are arranged from right to left and are linked to feng shui as auspicious presences in the home.

Legacy

In Japan, Shou became Jurōjin, one of the Seven Gods of Fortune.

Ancient sources: Sima Qian recorded the southern polar longevity star in the Shiji, noting that its appearance was held to herald peace.

Trivia

A god who extends lives. According to one account, Shou can change a person's age recorded on his tablet, for example from 18 to 81, if offerings are made to him.
Stars and the calendar. Jupiter, linked to Fu, was called the Year Star because its orbit takes about twelve years, matching the twelve-year cycle of the Earthly Branches.
