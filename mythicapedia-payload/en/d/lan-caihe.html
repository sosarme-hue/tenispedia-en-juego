Chinese Taoist immortal, patron of florists

Lan Caihe

Lan Caihe is one of the Eight Immortals of Chinese Taoism, the least known of the group and the only one whose gender is ambiguous. Patron of florists and gardeners, Lan is recognized by a basket of flowers.

The myth

Tradition says Lan lived during the Tang dynasty, although Lan is not generally considered a historical figure. Lan was a homeless street performer who wandered China singing philosophical songs to the rhythm of clappers, in a style called stomping songs. Lan wore a tattered blue gown and a single shoe, leaving one foot bare.

Lan's gender is a mystery. Some writers portray Lan as a man, others as a woman or intersex; according to the Xiu Xiang Ba Xian Dong You Ji, Lan was a man who could not understand how to be one. Lan gave earnings to the poor and, when drunk, improvised songs about immortality that no one could understand. Accounts of how Lan attained immortality vary.

Worship and religion

Taoists revere Lan as one of the Eight Immortals and as patron of florists and gardeners. The sources do not describe specific temples, festivals or rites.

Legacy

The flower basket is a common motif in Chinese New Year decorations, linked to wealth and abundance. Lan also appears on a 19th-century Qing dynasty imperial dragon robe and in Chinese theatre, where Lan is dressed as a woman. The bare foot is sometimes confused with that of Bodhidharma.

Ancient sources: The Liexian Zhuan (Huan Chu version) describes Lan's attire and life as a wandering beggar, and the Xiu Xiang Ba Xian Dong You Ji addresses Lan's gender identity. The earliest stories about Lan date back at least to the 10th century.

Trivia

Dressed as a woman, voiced as a man. In Chinese theatre, Lan appears in feminine clothes, but the actor speaks in a masculine voice without attempting a feminine style of speech.
A single shoe. Lan wore one shoe and left the other foot bare, a detail also associated with the monk Bodhidharma.
