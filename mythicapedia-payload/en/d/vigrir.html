battlefield of Ragnarök in Norse mythology

Vígríðr

Vígríðr, also called Óskópnir, is the vast field where, according to Norse tradition, the gods will clash with the forces of Surtr during Ragnarök. It matters because it is the setting of the final battle that precedes the world's destruction and rebirth.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Vígríðr is a plain one hundred leagues in each direction where the forces of Muspelheim will gather at the start of Ragnarök. In the poem Vafþrúðnismál of the Poetic Edda, the god Odin, disguised as Gagnráðr, answers the wise giant Vafþrúðnir in a contest of wits and reveals the plain's name and size. In Fáfnismál, the dying serpent Fafnir tells the hero Sigurd that the island of battle is called Óskópnir.

Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda gives a fuller account. The wolf Fenrir and the serpent Jörmungandr arrive, along with the giant Hrym and the frost giants and Loki with the people of Hel. Heimdall blows his horn Gjallarhorn, Odin consults Mímir, and the gods and einherjar advance to the field. Odin confronts Fenrir and is devoured, but his son Víðarr avenges him by tearing open the wolf's jaws. Thor kills Jörmungandr and dies from the venom after nine steps, Freyr falls to Surtr for lacking his sword, and Tyr and the hound Garmr kill each other. Finally Surtr hurls fire over the earth and the whole world burns.

Ancient sources: The Vafþrúðnismál and Fáfnismál of the Poetic Edda, which mention it briefly, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which describes the battle in detail.
