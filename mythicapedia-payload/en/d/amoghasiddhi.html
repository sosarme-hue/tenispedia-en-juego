Buddha of wisdom in Tibetan Buddhism

Amoghasiddhi

Amoghasiddhi is one of the Five Wisdom Buddhas of Buddhism, especially in the Vajrayana tradition. He is linked to the completion of the Buddhist path and to the destruction of envy, and he often appears in Buddhist art in China and Tibet.

The myth

His name means Unfailing Accomplishment. According to tradition, he belongs to the karma family, whose symbol is the double vajra, and his consort is Tara, whose name means Liberator or Noble Star. His mounts are garudas. He is associated with the conceptual skandha, that is, the conceptual mind as opposed to the non-conceptual mind of the senses.

His work is to pacify evils, and he is symbolised by the moon. His mudra is that of fearlessness, expressing his courage and that of his devotees in the face of poisons and delusions. He is usually shown green, linked to the air or wind element and to autumn; his heavenly quarter is the northern buddha-kṣetra called Prakuta.

Worship and religion

Chinese Buddhist temples kept sets of statues of the Five Tathagatas, Amoghasiddhi among them, such as the Jin-dynasty figures at Shanhua Temple and the Ming-dynasty ones at Huayan Temple in Datong, Shanxi. The sources do not describe specific rites.

Legacy

In the Śūraṅgama mantra, very influential in the Chinese Chan tradition, Amoghasiddhi is named as lord of the Karma Division of the north, one of five major divisions that control the vast demon armies of the five directions.

Ancient sources: The Śūraṅgama sutra, a Buddhist scripture taught in China, and its mantra name him among the rulers of the divisions that command the demon armies.

Trivia

An imperial figurine. A Ming figurine of Amoghasiddhi was made during the reign of the Yongle Emperor, in the early 1400s.
A green, lunar Buddha. He is usually depicted with green skin, and his symbol is the moon.
