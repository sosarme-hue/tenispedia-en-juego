Roman goddess of discord

Discordia

Discordia is the Roman goddess of discord and strife, the equivalent of the Greek Eris. She was mainly a literary personification, linked to Rome's civil wars.

The myth

According to the Fabulae, she was the daughter of Nox (Night) and Erebus, although Hesiod made Eris the daughter of Night with no father. Unlike Eris, she had no children. Her best-known episode is the Judgement of Paris: she was not invited to the wedding of Peleus and Thetis, so she tossed in an apple meant for the most beautiful. Juno, Venus and Minerva each claimed it, and the quarrel led to the Judgement of Paris and, eventually, the Trojan War.

Legacy

In Latin literature she appears often in epic poetry about civil strife. Christian writers turned her into a symbol of heresy. Today she is a central figure in Discordianism, a modern religious movement.

Ancient sources: She appears in Ennius's Annales, Virgil's Aeneid, Lucan's Pharsalia, Silius Italicus's Punica, Statius's Thebaid, Valerius Flaccus's Argonautica, Petronius's Satyricon, Augustine's City of God and Prudentius's Psychomachia.
