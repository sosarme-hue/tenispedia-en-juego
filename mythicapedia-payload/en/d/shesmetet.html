lioness goddess of the sky in ancient Egypt

Shesmetet

Shesmetet is an ancient Egyptian goddess of the sky, depicted as a lioness or as a woman with a lion's head. Her name means “She of the shesmet”, a ritual sash, and she matters because ancient texts present her as the mother of the deceased king.

The myth

The Pyramid Texts mention her in several formulas, where the deceased king is said to have been conceived by Sekhmet and born to Shesmetet, making her his mother.

Later spells in the Coffin Texts and the Book of the Dead also invoke her. According to a temple ritual, the body of Osiris was guarded by four lioness goddesses: Sekhmet, Bastet, Uadyet and Shesmetet.

Her epithet “Lady of Punt” sets her apart from Sekhmet and Bastet and suggests a possible African origin.

Worship and religion

She appears in depictions of Old Kingdom rulers and of the sky god Sopdu, wearing the shesmet. An inscription in the funerary temple of King Sahura at Abusir mentions her explicitly.

Ancient sources: Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts and Book of the Dead.

Trivia

A name from a sash. Her name comes from the shesmet, a sash decorated with beads, shells or small heads of Hathor, which may originally have been worn by unmarried young women.
Lady of Punt. Her epithet “Lady of Punt” points to a possible African origin, reaching Egypt through trade.
