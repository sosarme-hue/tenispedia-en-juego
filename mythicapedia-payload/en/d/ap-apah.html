the waters, Vedic divinity

Āp (Apah)

Āp is the Vedic Sanskrit word for 'water', and in Indian tradition it names the waters as divine beings. It appears in several hymns of the Rigveda and later in philosophy, astrology and Hinduism.

The myth

According to the Rigveda, several hymns are addressed to the waters, in the plural. In the oldest of them, 7.49, the waters are linked to the drought caused by Indra. Agni, the god of fire, has a close bond with water and is called Apām Napāt, offspring of the waters.

In later tradition, Varuna is presented as a personification of water and appears, in most Puranic lists, among the Vasus.

Legacy

The word survives in Persian āb and in the name Punjab, from panj-āb, 'five waters'. In Vedic astrology, the goddess Apah presides over the Purva Ashadha asterism. In Hindu philosophy, water is one of the five great elements, the Panchamahabhuta.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda hymns addressed to the waters (7.49, 10.9, 10.30 and 10.137).

Trivia

Name of a region. Punjab comes from panj-āb, 'five waters', a word related to the Vedic term for water.
A goddess in the sky. In Vedic astrology, the goddess Apah presides over the Purva Ashadha asterism, whose name means 'the first of the aṣāḍhā'.
