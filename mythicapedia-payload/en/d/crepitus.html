alleged Roman god of flatulence

Crepitus

Crepitus is an alleged Roman god of flatulence, a deity not attested in reliable ancient sources. His name comes from the Latin crepitus, meaning «noise», and he probably arose as a satirical device in Christian writing.

The myth

No myth about Crepitus is known. The few references come from a single ancient source of doubtful reliability, which mocks him as the «god of flatulence».

The story is little more than a joke: the figure serves as a target of ridicule rather than as a character in a religious narrative. For this reason he is regarded as an invented deity rather than a real god of Roman religion.

Legacy

The name survived mainly in French literature, where the idea of a god of intestinal noise, called crepitus ventris, appears in several important works. In medical jargon, crepitus also refers to the creaking or popping sounds made by joints.

Ancient sources: A single ancient source of doubtful reliability, written in Christian satirical tone, calls him the «god of flatulence».

Trivia

A medical term. In medicine, crepitus refers to the creaking or popping sounds made by the joints.
A missing verb. The Latin verb for «to fart», pēdere, does not appear in the sources that mention Crepitus.
