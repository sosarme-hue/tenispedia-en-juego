Lusitanian goddess of the home, family and war

Trebaruna

Trebaruna, also known as Treborunnis, was a Lusitanian deity linked to the home, family, battles and death. Her name survives in inscriptions from western Iberia, making her one of the few Lusitanian divinities documented by name.

The myth

Ancient sources preserve no story about Trebaruna's origin, family or deeds. Almost everything known comes from inscriptions, which show her as a protector of the home, property and community.

Some scholars link her to war, partly because a devotee who dedicated an altar to her also honoured the Roman goddess Victoria. Others read her as a protector of the group or tribe. These are modern interpretations of a figure whose myth has not survived in full.

Worship and religion

She was venerated through small votive altars, such as the one from Lardosa, which once held a statue of the goddess that has since been lost. At Fundão, a man named Toncius Toncetanus invoked her in a dedication, and at Lardosa a devotee called Oconus fulfilled a vow made to her. No information survives about festivals or priests, and her cult is not known to have continued.

Legacy

In 1895, the announcement of her discovery by José Leite de Vasconcelos inspired a poem that compared her to the Roman Victoria. Today, among neopagans, she is a goddess of battles and alliances, and the Portuguese metal band Moonspell wrote a song called «Trebaruna» in her honour.

Ancient sources: Lusitanian epigraphic inscriptions from Lardosa, Idanha-a-Velha, Coria and Fundão, with no ancient literary texts mentioning her.

Trivia

A metal song. The Portuguese band Moonspell composed a song titled «Trebaruna» celebrating the goddess.
An altar to Victoria. The same devotee who invoked her at Fundão dedicated another altar to the Roman goddess Victoria, which led some to think Trebaruna had a warlike role.
