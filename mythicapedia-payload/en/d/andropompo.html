hero of Greek mythology, father of King Melanthus

Andropompus

Andropompus is a figure of Greek mythology, a descendant of Neleus and father of King Melanthus of Athens. He matters because he links the Pylian lineage with the Athenian monarchy after the Dorian invasion of the Peloponnese.

The myth

According to tradition, Andropompus was the son of Penthilus and Anchirhoe, and therefore a descendant of Neleus, king of Thessaly. Pausanias credits him with a feat: he defeated Janthus, king of Thebes, in single combat, an episode said to have shifted Thebes from monarchy to oligarchy.

Andropompus is best known as the father of Melanthus. After the Heraclids invaded the Peloponnese, Melanthus and other exiles reached Athens, where he overthrew Timetes and became king. According to the Suda, a border dispute between Athens and Boeotia pitted Janthus against Timetes; when Timetes refused the duel, Melanthus fought in his place and killed Janthus through a ruse. His son Codrus was the last king of Athens.

Ancient sources: Pausanias, Strabo and Pherecydes of Leros, cited by Strabo, plus the Byzantine Suda, which records the dispute with Janthus.

Trivia

A duel settled by trickery. According to the Suda, Melanthus killed Janthus by using a ruse, fighting in place of Timetes.
