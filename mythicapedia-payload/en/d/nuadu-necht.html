legendary high king of Ireland

Nuadu Necht

Nuadu Necht is, according to medieval Irish tradition, a high king of Ireland who took the throne by killing his predecessor. He matters for his place in the genealogies of the heroes of the Fianna.

The myth

According to the stories, Nuadu Necht seized the throne by killing Eterscél and ruled for only six months. He was killed by Conaire Mór, Eterscél's son. Medieval sources place him in very different chronologies: the Lebor Gabála Érenn makes him a contemporary of Augustus, while the Annals of the Four Masters date him to the 1st century BC and combine his reign with others to fill a single year.

In the Fenian Cycle he is an ancestor of Fionn mac Cumhaill through his son Baoisgne. Some versions confuse the father of the druid Tadg, Fionn's maternal grandfather, with Nuadu Necht, when the figure is really Nuada Airgetlám of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Ancient sources: Medieval Irish texts such as the Lebor Gabála Érenn and the Annals of the Four Masters, which record his reign and genealogy.

Trivia

Very different dates. Keating places his reign in 64–63 BC, while the Four Masters date it to 111–110 BC.
A confused name. His name is sometimes confused with that of Nuada Airgetlám, a figure of the Tuatha Dé Danann.
