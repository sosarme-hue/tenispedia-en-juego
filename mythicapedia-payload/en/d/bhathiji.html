folk deity of Gujarat, protector of cows

Bhathiji

Bhathiji, also called Bhathiji Maharaj, is a folk deity of Gujarat remembered as a warrior who protects cattle. Rooted in Hindu tradition, his figure blends heroic courage with local devotion.

The myth

According to folk tradition, Bhathiji was the second son of Thakore Rathod Takhatsinhji of Fagvel, of the Rathod branch of the Kshatriya clan. While he was marrying Kankuba and completing the fourth of the seven circuits, he learned that the Muslim king of Kapadvanj had impounded the village's sacred cow after receiving a complaint against him.

Bhathiji rode off at once on horseback, sword in hand, leaving the wedding unfinished. He freed the cow and defeated the army, but was beheaded. He died a martyr, yet had saved the cattle. Folk songs tell how his headless body kept fighting the raiders until every one of them was destroyed.

Worship and religion

He is worshipped as a protector of cows, and believers say those who pray to him are shielded from bites of snakes, cobras and scorpions. The Rathod Rajputs of Saurashtra honour him as their kuladevata. In Devada village in Kheda district, Hindus and Muslims alike gather every Friday to seek his blessing and vow to give up alcohol. Four other fairs are held in his honour on the second day of the bright fortnight of Kartika.

Legacy

Films in Gujarati about him were released in 1980 and 1990, and there are albums of bhajans and songs in his honour performed by local artists and well-known singers such as Alka Yagnik. A association of Kshatriya devotees, Bhathiji Sena, carries his name.
