stone monster of Hurrian mythology

Ullikummi

Ullikummi is a giant stone monster from Hurrian mythology, son of the god Kumarbi. His story, known mainly from the Song of Ullikummi, belongs to a cycle of tales from ancient Anatolia that scholars have compared with Greek myths about Zeus and Typhon.

The myth

In the myth, Kumarbi, who wanted to replace the storm god Teshub and destroy the city of Kummiya, fathered Ullikummi on a rock cliff. He hid the monster in the netherworld and set him on the shoulder of Upelluri, who, lost in his meditations, never noticed him.

Ullikummi grew until he reached the heavens. Teshub's thunder and rain did no harm to him, and the storm god fled and gave up his throne. He then asked Ea for help. Ea visited Upelluri and cut off the monster's feet, toppling him.

Legacy

Scholars have pointed to parallels between this story and the Greek myth of Typhon, the ancient rival of Zeus. From its first rediscovery, the Song was regarded as a precursor of some Greek myths.

Ancient sources: Hittite texts found at Bogazköy, with some Hurrian fragments of the Song of Ullikummi.

Trivia

A creation by separation. The Hittite creation myth holds that in the primordial past heaven and earth were united and later separated.
A monster without senses. According to the story, Ullikummi had no gender and was deaf and blind, yet still sentient.
