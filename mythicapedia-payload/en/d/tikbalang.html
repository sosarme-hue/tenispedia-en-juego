horse-headed creature of Filipino folklore

Tikbalang

The tikbalang is a creature of Filipino folklore: a tall, bony being with the head and hooves of a horse that haunts mountains and forests. Its image blends local beliefs with Hindu-influenced ideas that arrived through Asian trade routes.

The myth

According to tradition, the tikbalang roams the forest at night, leading travelers astray until they walk in circles and reach nowhere. Some tales say it poses a riddle: whoever answers correctly receives a pot of gold, while whoever fails is lost in the woods.

To protect yourself, you can wear your shirt inside out or ask aloud for permission to pass. Whoever manages to pull three golden hairs from its mane turns it into a loyal servant who helps lead them out of the forest.

Worship and religion

It has no formal cult, but it is treated with respect in everyday life: tikbalang are believed to guard sacred trees such as the balete, and people avoid making loud noise in the woods so as not to disturb them.

Legacy

It appears in Arnold Arre's graphic novel The Mythology Class and in the children's book Tikbalang Kung Kabilugan ng Buwan. The propagandist Mariano Ponce used Tikbalang as a pen name in the newspaper La Solidaridad.

Ancient sources: The earliest written accounts come from Spanish friars such as Juan de Plasencia (1589), who describe them as forest spirits, still without a horse form.

Trivia

Punk band. A Filipino punk rock band from the 1990s took the name Tame the Tikbalang and became part of the country's underground music scene.
Television series. A Tikbalang appears as a fae in the episode Delinquents of the series Lost Girl.
