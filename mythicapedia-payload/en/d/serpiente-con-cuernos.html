Horned Serpent

The Horned Serpent is a figure from the oral traditions of many Native peoples of the Southeast and Great Lakes, linked to water, rain, lightning and thunder. It also appears in Celtic art and European folklore.

The myth

According to Muscogee accounts, it is an underwater serpent with iridescent scales and a large crystal in its forehead, whose horns and scales were used in divination and medicine. For the Cherokee, the uktena is enormous and can only be wounded in the seventh spot from its head; its breath is deadly, and seeing it asleep kills the hunter's family.

The Sioux spoke of the Unhcegila, reptilian water monsters destroyed by the Thunderbirds, except for small species such as snakes and lizards.

Worship and religion

The Yuchi made effigies of this being from stuffed deerhide as late as 1905, and their Big Turtle Dance honors its spirit, tied to storms and disease.

Legacy

Its variants live on in Northern and Central European folklore, such as the Icelandic Lagarfljót Worm.

Trivia

Medicinal horns. The Muscogee used the horns of this being, called chitto gab-by, in medicine.
Fossils and monsters. It has been suggested that the Sioux belief in the Unhcegila may have been inspired by dinosaur fossil finds in their territory.
