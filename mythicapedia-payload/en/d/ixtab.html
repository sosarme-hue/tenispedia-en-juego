Maya goddess of suicide by hanging (disputed figure)

Ixtab

Ixtab, also called Xtab or Ix Tab ("Rope Woman"), was long presented as the Maya goddess of suicide by hanging. Most researchers today consider her a figure that grew out of misreadings, and her current interest lies in what she reveals about how the history of the Maya has been told.

The myth

According to the traditional picture that took shape from missionary accounts, Ixtab protected those who took their own lives by hanging and accompanied them to a special paradise, a role known as psychopomp or guide of souls. She was depicted as a partly decomposed corpse with closed eyes, hanging from a tree.

In that tradition, suicide was seen as an honorable death, on a level similar to that of sacrifice victims, warriors fallen in battle, women who died in childbirth, or priests. Over time she was also made the wife of Chamer or Ah Puch, the god of death, and a later version turned her into a seductress who lured men in the form of a beautiful woman and killed them when her demonic face was revealed. However, the reading of the Dresden Codex does not confirm her existence, and recent studies attribute the idea to a misinterpretation.

Worship and religion

The writings of the sixteenth-century missionary Diego de Landa are the basis for the idea of a cult of Ixtab, but codices, murals, vessels and ethnohistorical documents offer no consistent evidence of temples, festivals or offerings dedicated to her. No living cult of this goddess exists today.

Legacy

Her figure has entered popular culture, where she is linked to the legend of the Xtabay, a seductive woman who kills her victims. According to the historian Alberto Ramírez, the use of the name "Ixtab" reflects racist prejudices among part of the population of the Yucatán peninsula, which keeps spreading the idea without evidence.

Ancient sources: Accounts by sixteenth-century missionaries, especially Fray Diego de Landa's work on Yucatán, are the ones that present her as a goddess of suicide.

Trivia

A mistaken reading. The idea of Ixtab came from interpretations of Maya writing made before it had been deciphered. Researchers who reviewed them concluded that the codices do not support it.
A political motive. According to the researcher Sara Álvarez, Fray Diego de Landa presented this information during his inquisitorial campaign and took it back to Spain to explain a sudden rise in suicides.
