wolf who chases the Sun in Norse mythology

Sköll

Sköll is a wolf in Norse mythology who, according to Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, chases the chariot of the Sun, driven by the goddess Sól, and is destined to devour her at Ragnarök. He is the son of the wolf Fenrir and the brother of Hati, who pursues the Moon.

The myth

According to the tradition, Sköll and his brother Hati are the sons of the wolf Fenrir and an unnamed giantess. Sköll runs after Sól, who crosses the sky every day in her chariot, while Hati chases Máni, the Moon. In the Prose Edda, Snorri Sturluson describes the goddess fleeing with nowhere to turn, since the wolf is close behind her and she fears him most.

A prophecy foretells that at Ragnarök the wolves will catch up with the celestial bodies and devour them. The name Sköll has been translated as "treachery" or "mockery", and it is linked to the sound of barking. In verse 46 of the Vafþrúðnismál, Sköll appears as a heiti, a poetic synonym for Fenrir, and the verse gives Fenrir the aim of catching the Sun instead of his son.

Legacy

The scholar Rudolf Simek suggests that Sköll, Hati and Fenrir may be one and the same figure, inspired by the Hyades star cluster in the constellation Taurus. Its stars form a V shape that, seen with some imagination, resembles a wolf's jaws. In the Scandinavian languages, halos around the Sun and Moon are called sun dogs and moon dogs, and in Norwegian and Swedish they are known as sun-wolves, which links to this myth. This is a modern interpretation rather than an ancient fact.

Ancient sources: Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda and the Vafþrúðnismál of the Poetic Edda.

Trivia

A name with double meaning. Sköll is usually translated as "treachery" or "mockery". The noun is the feminine form of a word meaning bark or a far-carrying sound.
A wolf with a brother. Hati, Sköll's brother, chases the Moon while Sköll chases the Sun. Both are sons of Fenrir.
