sage and disciple of Shukra in Hindu mythology

Kacha

Kacha is a sage in Hindu mythology, the son of Brihaspati, the priest of the gods. His story appears in the Mahabharata and the Matsya and Agni Puranas, and centres on the knowledge of how to restore the dead to life.

The myth

According to the tradition, the devas send Kacha, a handsome young man, to the hermitage of Shukra, teacher of the asuras, to learn the Mṛtasañjīvanī vidyā mantra, the knowledge that restores life after death. Shukra accepts him as a disciple in return for a thousand years of service, and his daughter Devayani falls in love with him.

Suspicious of his intentions, the asuras kill him twice: they feed him to wolves deep in the forest, and they pound his body into paste mixed with seawater. Shukra revives him both times at his daughter's insistent pleas. On the third attempt, the asuras burn his body, mix the ashes with wine and give it to Shukra to drink. At nightfall, the teacher senses that Kacha is in his belly; he teaches him the mantra, and when the disciple bursts out and kills him, he brings him back to life with it.

Having completed his education, Kacha takes his leave to return to Devaloka. Devayani asks him to marry her, but he reveals that, having emerged from Shukra's belly, he is regarded as the teacher's son and therefore her brother. Furious, Devayani curses him so that he can never use the resurrection mantra himself. Kacha answers with a curse: no son of a sage will ever marry her. They part and never meet again.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata and the Matsya and Agni Puranas.
