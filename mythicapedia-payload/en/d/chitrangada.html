princess of Manipura and wife of Arjuna in the Mahabharata

Chitrangada

Chitrangada is a warrior princess in the Mahabharata, the great Hindu epic, daughter of King Chitravahana of Manipura. Her romance with the Pandava prince Arjuna and the son they share, Babhruvahana, make her a key figure in the epic's closing episodes.

The myth

According to the tradition, King Chitravahana of Manipura had no sons, so he raised his only heir, Chitrangada, to rule and fight. During Arjuna's exile he visited the kingdom, fell in love with her and asked for her hand. The king explained that his ancestors had received a boon from Shiva granting each generation a single descendant, and that Chitrangada had therefore been made a 'putrika': only her son could succeed him. Arjuna accepted the condition and stayed with her for three years.

When their son Babhruvahana was born, Arjuna took leave and returned to Hastinapura, promising to take her to his kingdom one day. After the war against the Kauravas, Yudhishthira performed the ashvamedha, the horse sacrifice. While Arjuna guarded the horse, a young man blocked his way and defeated him in battle. Arjuna was struck down by his own son, but Ulupi, another of Arjuna's wives, brought the nagamani gem, which restored him to life. This also lifted a curse of the Vasus, who had decreed that Arjuna would die at his son's hand.

Arjuna then took Chitrangada and their son to Hastinapura, where she served Gandhari. At the onset of the Kali Yuga, when the Pandavas began their final pilgrimage, Chitrangada returned to Manipura.

Legacy

The Indian writer Rabindranath Tagore adapted her story in his play Chitra.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Mahabharata, the Sanskrit epic traditionally attributed to Vyasa.
