legendary king and founder of Ephesus

Androclus

Androclus is a hero of Greek myth, son of the Athenian king Codrus and the legendary founder of the city of Ephesus in Asia Minor. His story explains why Ephesus held pre-eminence among the cities of the Ionian League.

The myth

According to tradition, Androclus was sent with his brothers to conquer Ionia, the Aegean coast of Asia Minor. He drove out the Leleges and Carians who had settled the Ephesian region, and he also conquered the island of Samos.

The oracles told the colonists to found their city where a boar and a fish would indicate. The sign came during the preparation of a dinner: a fish leaped from the cooking pot, carrying embers that set a bush alight. A boar emerged from the thicket and Androclus killed it at once, and there he founded Ephesus.

As leader of the Ionian expedition, Ephesus afterwards enjoyed primacy among the twelve cities of the Ionian League, a confederation that tradition attributes to the illegitimate sons of Codrus.
