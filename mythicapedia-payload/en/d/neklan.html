legendary prince of Bohemia

Neklan

Neklan is one of the seven mythical princes of Bohemia, placed between the founder Přemysl the Ploughman and the first historical prince, Bořivoj. He is remembered as a peaceful ruler, and his story is preserved in the chronicle of Cosmas of Prague.

The myth

According to the tradition, Neklan preferred to avoid war. When Vlastislav, leader of the Lučans, besieged his castle of Levý Hradec, Neklan wanted to make peace. His adviser Tyr, a warrior of Viking origin, borrowed his armour and horse to fight in his place, an episode that recalls Patroclus standing in for Achilles in the Iliad.

Tyr and Vlastislav both died in the battle, but the Bohemians won and invaded the land of the Lučans. Neklan then ruled wisely for a long time and was succeeded by his son Hostivít. According to the Chronicle of Dalimil, he also had another son, Děpolt.

Ancient sources: The chronicle of Cosmas of Prague and the Chronicle of Dalimil mention him.

Trivia

An echo of Homer. The episode of Tyr fighting in Neklan's armour is strongly inspired by Homer, like Patroclus in the Iliad.
Days of the week. The historian Záviš Kalandra thought the names of the seven princes were hidden names of ancient Slavic days of the week, with Vojen as Friday, though the evidence was very weak.
