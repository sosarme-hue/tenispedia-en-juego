sixteenth tirthankara of Jainism

Śāntinātha

Śāntinātha, also known as Śānti, is the sixteenth tirthankara of Jainism in the present age. Jains revere him as a deity of peace and he is one of the five most worshipped tirthankaras.

The myth

According to Jain tradition, he was born in Hastinapur to King Vishvasena and Queen Aćira, of the Ikshvaku dynasty. Before his final life he accumulated merit through earlier existences, including that of a king who saved a dove by offering his own flesh to a hawk.

As a monarch, the accounts say, he reigned for thousands of years and possessed the fourteen jewels and nine treasures that symbolize material dominion. He later renounced the throne, became a monk and, after sixteen years of penance, attained omniscience beneath a nandi tree. He finally achieved moksha at Sammed Shikharji, where he became a siddha.

Worship and religion

Devotees pray to him to ward off calamities and epidemics, and his hymns are recited at funeral rites and in the Shanti Snaatra Puja, a celebration of universal peace. His worship remains alive among Jains today.

Legacy

His image appears in famous temples such as the one at Khajuraho, and in the work of the 10th-century poet Ponna.

Ancient sources: Medieval Jain biographies, such as that of Hemachandra, which narrate his previous lives and his reign.

Trivia

His emblem. His icon is the deer, which gives him his name in iconography and distinguishes him from other tirthankaras.
A birth that calmed an epidemic. According to tradition, his presence in the womb pacified an epidemic, and this is why he is named Lord of Peace.
