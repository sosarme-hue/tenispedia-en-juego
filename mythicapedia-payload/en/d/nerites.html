minor sea god of Greek mythology

Nerites

Nerites is a minor sea god of Greek mythology, son of Nereus and Doris and the only male among the Nereids. We know him mainly through the account of the writer Aelian, who describes him as a boy of extraordinary beauty, and through the link between his name and a spiral-shelled sea snail.

The myth

According to Aelian, the only ancient author who mentions him, Nerites was so beautiful that gods such as Poseidon, Aphrodite and Helios competed for his favour. He never appears in Homer or Hesiod, and his figure belonged mostly to sailors' folklore.

Aelian records two versions of his fate. In the first, Aphrodite fell in love with him before rising from the sea to Olympus and wanted him to come along. Nerites preferred to stay with his family, and even the promise of a pair of wings did not change his mind. The scorned goddess turned him into a shellfish, gave the wings to Eros and chose the god as her companion and servant.

In the second, Poseidon loved him and was loved in return; from their mutual love came Anteros, the personification of reciprocated love. Poseidon made Nerites his charioteer, and the youth drove so fast that tritons and dolphins marvelled at him. Helios, for reasons Aelian does not specify, changed him into a mollusc. Aelian suggests that the gods were rivals for the boy, or that Helios wanted to see him made a constellation rather than left among the sea monsters.

Legacy

The name nerites was applied to several species of sea snail, according to Aristotle. R. S. P. Beekes proposed a Pre-Greek origin for the word. In the nineteenth century, the German archaeologist Karl Bernhard Stark identified several terracotta figurines from the Greek island of Aegina as depictions of Aphrodite with Nerites.

Ancient sources: Aelian, On the Characteristics of Animals, the only ancient source that mentions him.
