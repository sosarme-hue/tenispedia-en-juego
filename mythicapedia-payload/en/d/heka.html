Egyptian god of magic

Heka

Heka is the personification of magic and vital force in Egyptian mythology. His name is the Egyptian word for "magic", and his role reached from the creation of the world to the afterlife.

The myth

According to the tradition, Heka existed from the beginning of time, even before duality came into being. Some accounts make him a son of Atum; others name Khnum and Menhit as his parents, or Khnum and a succession of goddesses, with Neith eventually becoming his mother in some later texts. A Coffin Texts spell says that Heka came into being of himself and, by his power, created the mountains and joined the sky together.

Magic was a power the gods possessed, and the Pyramid Texts describe Heka as an energy even the gods feared. According to the tradition, the pharaoh had to devour other gods to take that power. Heka rode in the solar barque with the sun god and protected Osiris in the underworld, where he could blind crocodiles. In the Ptolemaic period he proclaims the pharaoh's enthronement as a son of Isis.

Worship and religion

Heka had no regular cult, yet his role in religion was considerable. His priests were physician-magicians trained in schools at Heliopolis, Memphis and Esna. He was invoked in many magical and religious contexts, and in the Roman period a statue of the god was carried in procession from the temple of Esna to the surrounding fields to secure fertility.

Legacy

Heka gave his name to the Egyptian word for magic and was incorporated into personal names such as Hekawy or Hekaf. In the Late Period he was also depicted as a child, accompanying divine couples as their son.

Ancient sources: The Pyramid Texts and the Coffin Texts mention him, along with the Book of the Dead in its references to magic.

Trivia

A name with ka. His name was written with the sign for ka, the Egyptian concept of vital force, suggesting the link between magic and that energy.
