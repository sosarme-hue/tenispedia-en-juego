supreme deities of Chinese Taoism

Three Great Officials

The Three Officials (Sanguan) are three of the highest deities in some branches of religious Taoism, subordinate only to the Jade Emperor. They administer all phenomena in the three realms of heaven, earth and water, and were believed able to pardon sins.

The myth

According to the Ming text Comprehensive Collection of Deities from the Three Religions, the Three Officials are three sons born to Chen Zidao and the Dragon Girl. Yuanshi Tianzun, the Celestial Worthy of Primordial Beginning, enfeoffed them by rank: the eldest became the Heavenly Official, the second the Earthly Official, and the youngest the Water Official.

The Qing text Comprehensive Mirror of Immortals offers a different account, identifying them with the ancient sage-kings Yao, Shun and Yu, with Yao as Heaven, Shun as Earth and Yu as Water.

Worship and religion

They have been worshipped since the second century CE. The Heavenly Official was thought to bring happiness to believers, the Earthly Official to take away sin, and the Water Official to avert misfortune. Chinese playwrights spread their worship by staging a short skit before performances, called The Official of Heaven Brings Happiness.

Ancient sources: The Ming text Comprehensive Collection of Deities from the Three Religions and the Qing text Comprehensive Mirror of Immortals record their origins and identities.
