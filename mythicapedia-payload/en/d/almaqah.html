Sabaean sun god of Yemen and Ethiopia

Almaqah

Almaqah was the national god of the ancient Sabaean kingdom of Saba, in present-day Yemen, and was also worshipped in Dʿmt and Aksum, in Ethiopia and Eritrea. Scholars debate whether he was a sun or moon god, though he is now mostly regarded as a solar deity.

The myth

Almaqah was the protector of the ruling dynasty, which regarded itself as his descendants. He was shown as a cluster of curved rays, like a sickle or the letter "S", and the bull was his sacred animal. He was credited with the fertility of the land and with overseeing the artificial irrigation that was vital in such an arid region. Although he held an important place in the Sabaean pantheon, in invocations he always came after Athtar.

Worship and religion

His main centre was the Awwam Temple in Ma'rib, the Sabaean capital, which served as his oracular seat. Several hundred inscriptions have been found there, mostly from the first three Christian centuries, and during the month of ḏū-Abhī the sanctuary drew pilgrims from across South Arabia. Other temples stood at Sirwah, Nashq and Yeha in Ethiopia. Worship lasted until the fourth century AD, and the sources give no evidence that it survives today.

Ancient sources: Sabaean inscriptions found at the Awwam Temple, from the pre-Islamic period, which name him as the god of Ma'rib.

Trivia

A god with sacred bulls. Bulls were sacred to Almaqah, which is why he was known as the "Bull of the land of the Lord".
Children of a god. The dynasty ruling Saba regarded itself as descended from Almaqah, which gave royal power a divine basis.
