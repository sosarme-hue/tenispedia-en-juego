Hel's guardian hound in Norse mythology

Garm

Garm, also spelled Garmr, is the hound that guards the gates of Hel's dwelling in Helheim, within Niflheim. A figure of Norse and Germanic tradition, he is linked both to the realm of the dead and to the final battle of Ragnarök.

The myth

According to Norse sources, Garm is a terrible dog or wolf, bloodstained across the chest, who guards the entrance to Hel's realm. In the Baldrs draumar, Odin rides down to Hel to seek answers about the fate of his son Baldr. On the way he meets a dog that howls at the enchanter from afar. The poem does not name it, but it is often identified with Garm.

In the Poetic Edda, the Völuspá uses his howling as a sign that Ragnarök is near: Garm howls before the cave of Gnipahellir, his fetters burst and the wolf runs free. The Prose Edda describes him bound before that cave and loosed at the world's end, when he will fight Týr and each will slay the other. The Grímnismál names him the greatest of hounds.

Legacy

In the folklore of Twente, in the eastern Netherlands, Garm appears in Low Saxon tradition as the helhond, or kardoes, companion of Tamfana. His red glowing eyes are said to have been seen near Oldenzaal, and his howl is taken to announce illness and death. His figure is also linked to the sound of the midwinter horn, which communities used to call upon the ancestors.

In popular culture, the name appears in the video game Ace Combat Zero: The Belkan War, where the Galm Team takes its name from Garm through an English mistransliteration. He is also a boss in Boktai, Hellblade: Senua's Sacrifice and God of War Ragnarök. In 2026, the giant zombie dogs of Resident Evil: Requiem are dubbed Garmr.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Grímnismál, Völuspá, Baldrs draumar) and the Prose Edda (Gylfaginning).

Trivia

A name in a video game. The Galm Team squadron in Ace Combat Zero takes its name from Garm. The form Galm is a mistransliteration into English, due to the single liquid sound in Japanese.
A dog on Odin's journey. In the Baldrs draumar, Odin meets a bloodstained dog on his way to Helheim. The poem does not call it Garm, though it is often identified with him.
