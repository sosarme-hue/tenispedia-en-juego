Mesopotamian god of war

Zababa

Zababa was a Mesopotamian god of war and the tutelary deity of the city of Kish. His importance rose and fell across the centuries, and he was worshipped in both Babylonia and Assyria.

The myth

No myths centred on Zababa survive. According to tradition, he was first regarded as a son of Enlil, but in Assyria, during the reign of Sennacherib, he came to be seen as a son of Ashur, a way of linking the Assyrian god to Enlil's family.

Bau, a goddess associated with healing, was regarded as his wife from the time her cult reached Kish in the Old Babylonian period. The couple appear together in oath formulas and on kudurru stones in both Babylonia and Assyria. His court included the vizier Papsukkal and Udulu, a minor god who enforced his will and could be shown in the form of an eagle. Zababa also appears in legends about rulers of the Akkadian Empire and in texts such as Urash and Marduk.

Worship and religion

His worship is documented from the Early Dynastic period, with the temple Edubba in Kish as its main centre. He was venerated in Babylonia and Assyria through the first millennium BCE. With the end of Mesopotamian civilisation, his cult disappeared.

Legacy

Several rulers bore names built on his, such as Ur-Zababa of Kish, early patron of Sargon of Akkad, and Zababa-shum-iddina, a Kassite king of Babylon. Mesopotamian astronomers recognised a constellation named after him, whose stars were identified with parts of his body and clothing, such as the "Eye of Zababa". The Hittites also used his name for a war god of Anatolian origin.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Zame Hymns from Abu Salabikh, in the god list An = Anum, in the Lament for Sumer and Ur and in Enmesharra's Defeat.

Trivia

A constellation named after him. Mesopotamian astronomers recognised a constellation named Zababa, whose stars were linked to parts of his body. The "Crown of Zababa" and the "Eye of Zababa" are two of the documented examples.
A king who took his name. Ur-Zababa, ruler of Kish, was the early patron of Sargon of Akkad, founder of the Akkadian Empire.
