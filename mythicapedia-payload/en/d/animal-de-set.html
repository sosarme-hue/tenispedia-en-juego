Set animal

The Set animal, or sha, is the creature affiliated with the god Set in ancient Egypt. Its hieroglyph was used to write words linked to chaos, violence and storms.

The myth

Set was the god of disorder, violence and storms, though he also defended Ra and the king. He was shown as a man with the head of a canine-like animal, with squared upright ears, a long snout and a stiff, forked tail. In the Osirian tradition, Set murdered his brother Osiris and became the eternal enemy of Horus, an image reinforced during the Second Intermediate Period. The animal, of uncertain species, has no mythology of its own apart from Set.

Worship and religion

The animal had no cult of its own. Set was venerated mainly in the New Kingdom, but during the Third Intermediate Period his worship was abandoned and many of his images were destroyed.

Legacy

The Greeks identified Set with Typhon, which is why the animal is also called the Typhonic beast. The was-sceptre, a symbol of power carried by gods and pharaohs, bears its head and forked tail.

Trivia

Earliest appearances. The animal already appears on the ceremonial macehead of the ruler Scorpion II, and later on the serekhs of the Second Dynasty kings Peribsen and Khasekhemwy.
An unidentified animal. Some Egyptologists believe it is a fantastic animal that never existed. The jackal, by contrast, is mainly associated with Anubis.
