woodland spirit of Turkic mythology

Archura

The archura is a shapeshifting woodland spirit from Turkic mythology. It protects wild animals and forests, and its appearance mixes human, plant and animal traits.

The myth

According to Turkic tradition, the archura can change its size from that of a blade of grass to that of a very tall tree. It usually appears as a man resembling a peasant with glowing eyes, with hair and a beard of living grass, and sometimes with a tail, hooves and horns. It has a close bond with the gray wolf, wears a red scarf, has its left shoe on its right foot and casts no shadow.

Its main role is to guard the animals and birds of the forest, telling them when to migrate. It is, however, a mischievous being: it lets out horrible cries and copies the voices of people wanderers know, luring them back to its cave, where it tickles them to death. It also removes the signs on roadposts. It is not wholly evil, since it keeps grazing cattle from straying too deep into the forest and getting lost. When more than one archura lives in the same forest, they fight over territory, knocking down trees and scaring off animals.
