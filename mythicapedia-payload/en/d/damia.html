Greek goddess of fertility

Damia

Damia is a Greek fertility deity, associated with Auxesia and worshipped in Troezen, Epidaurus, Aegina, Thera and Sparta. She is often linked to Demeter, and sometimes described as one of her epithets.

The myth

Accounts of Damia and Auxesia vary from city to city. The Epidaurians and Aeginetans tell one story, but in Troezen the tale is of two virgins who came from Crete and were stoned to death by the opposing faction during a civil conflict.

Herodotus explains that a feud between Athens and Aegina had left the land of Epidaurus barren. The Epidaurians consulted the Delphic oracle, and the Pythia advised them to set up images of Damia and Auxesia. When they asked what material to use, they were forbidden bronze and marble: the images had to be carved from cultivated olive wood.

Worship and religion

In Troezen, festivals called Lithobolia, or stone-throwing, were held in memory of the virgins. The command to make the images from olive wood links their worship to the fertility of the land.

Ancient sources: Herodotus, in his Histories, recounts the Epidaurians' consultation of the Delphic oracle and the order to make the images from olive wood.

Trivia

Lithobolia. The festivals at Troezen were called Lithobolia, meaning stone-throwing, in reference to the death of the virgins.
Olive-wood images. The Delphic oracle forbade bronze and marble and required the images to be made of cultivated olive wood.
