king of Percote, interpreter of dreams

Merops

Merops was a king of Percote, a city in the Troad, known in Greek tradition for his reputation as an interpreter of dreams. He matters mainly for the tragic fate of his sons in the Trojan War.

The myth

According to the tradition recorded by Homer, Merops had two sons, Amphius and Adrastus, and two daughters, Clite and Arisbe. Clite married Cyzicus, while Arisbe became the first wife of Priam, king of Troy. Homer presents him as one of Troy's allies who came from the Troad.

Merops had a reputation as a dream interpreter and warned his sons not to go to war, but they ignored his words and marched into battle. Both died at the hands of Diomedes during the fighting, fulfilling their father's prediction. The story highlights the irony of a man able to read the future who could not persuade those he cared about most.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him among Troy's allies and recounts the deaths of his sons at the hands of Diomedes.
