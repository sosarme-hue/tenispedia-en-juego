naiad of Greek mythology

Drosera

Drosera is a naiad in Greek mythology, one of the three ancestors of the Tyrians along with Abarbarea and Callirrhoe. Her story, preserved by Nonnus of Panopolis, explains how the Tyrian people descended from the union of nymphs and earth-born men.

The myth

According to Nonnus' Dionysiaca, Eros grew angry with the naiads because they remained chaste and rejected love. He drew his bow and loosed three shafts: one at Abarbarea, warning her that she too would feel the dart; another at Callirrhoe, for whom he prepared the bridal chamber; and a third at Drosera, whose wedding hymn he announced.

Thus, in a watery bower, the god joined the nymphs in love with earth-born men, the autochthonous sons of the soil, and sowed the divine race of Dionysos' ancestors, from whom the Tyrians descend. Drosera, then, is not merely a spring nymph: her union marks the origin of a people.

Ancient sources: Nonnus of Panopolis mentions her in his Dionysiaca, the poem recounting the story of Dionysos and the main source for her.

Trivia

Ancestor of the Tyrians. Drosera and her two companions are presented as ancestors of the Tyrian people, the lineage to which Dionysos' ancestors belong.
Three arrows, three unions. Eros shoots three arrows in a single scene, one for each naiad, and each shot leads to a different union.
