Greek personification of prudence

Epiphron

Epiphron is, in Greek mythology, the personification of prudence and good sense, one of the divine abstractions that the ancients imagined as beings with their own lineage. He barely appears in the sources, almost only in the fables of the Latin writer Hyginus.

The myth

According to Hyginus, Epiphron was the son of Erebus, Darkness, and Nox, Night, the Latin equivalent of the Greek Nyx. Like other figures of that lineage, he embodied a human quality: good sense.

His presence in the text raises doubts. He appears alongside Lysimeles and Hedymeles, and some scholars believe these names are not independent deities but epithets of Eros, Love, who would be the child one would expect in that list. Hyginus kept the Greek form of the name rather than translating it, perhaps inspired by Sophrosyne, Moderation.

Ancient sources: Hyginus, in his Fabulae, is the only ancient source that mentions him.

Trivia

Untranslated name. Hyginus left the name in its Latinized Greek form, unlike other abstractions that he did translate into Latin.
A possible epithet of Eros. Lysimeles would mean the one who loosens the limbs, and Hedymeles the one of sweet limbs, readings that suggest these names describe Love.
