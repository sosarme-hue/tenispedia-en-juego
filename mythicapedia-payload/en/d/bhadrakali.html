fierce form of the supreme goddess in Hinduism

Bhadrakali

Bhadrakali is the fierce form of Adi Shakti, the supreme goddess revered in Hinduism. She is worshipped mainly in the Indian state of Kerala and in Nashik, and her name is linked to the ideas of auspiciousness and cosmic illusion.

The myth

According to the Brahma Purana, Shiva, enraged on learning that his consort Sati had immolated herself at her father Daksha's sacrifice, beat the earth with his matted hair. From this act emerged Virabhadra and Bhadrakali, whom he commanded to lay waste to the yajna in vengeance.

In the Tamil Puranas, Lankalakshmi, guardian of Lanka, tries to stop Hanuman from entering the city. He strikes her down, and she regains her true form as Bhadrakali and returns to Kailasha. There she asks to witness the war between Rama and Ravana, and Shiva sends her to the Dravida region, to a temple with a self-manifested lingam. He promises to compose the Ramayana in Tamil. He is born as the son of Cinkaravalli, a widow devoted to the temple's deity, who abandons him out of fear of scandal. A local chieftain adopts him and names him Kambar.

Under a Chola king, Kambar and the poet Ottakkuttar are commissioned to write the Ramayana in Tamil. Kambar delays until the final night, when the goddess Saraswati is said to write the verses for him. The next day the poem astonishes the court, and the story of the war is performed before the goddess, who watches and dances while Shiva, in the form of Kambar, recites.

Worship and religion

In Kerala she is worshipped as a kuladevata of Nair clans, the family's protective deity. She is seen as a protector of women's honour and a bestower of all spiritual knowledge. According to Kerala traditions, her slaying of the demon Darika, which freed the universe from evil, took place near Madayi in Kannur District.

Ancient sources: The Brahma Purana, the Markandeya Purana and the Tamil Puranas mention her or recount episodes in which she takes part.

Trivia

A name with two meanings. One etymology splits «bhadra» into «bha» and «dra», which can mean «delusion» and «the greatest». The name could thus be read as «great delusion».
A goddess who watches Kambar. In the Tamil account, Saraswati writes the verses of the Ramayana for Kambar on the last night, and the goddess Bhadrakali watches the work recited before the court.
