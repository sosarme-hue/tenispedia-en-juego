Egyptian god of medicine and patron of scribes

Imhotep

Imhotep was an Egyptian sage of the Third Dynasty, chancellor to the pharaoh Djoser (Zoser) and, according to later attribution, the architect of the Step Pyramid at Saqqara. Centuries after his death he was deified as a god of medicine and wisdom, and his story shows how a historical figure could become an object of worship in ancient Egypt.

The myth

According to later tradition, Imhotep was the son of the god Ptah and the mortal Khereduankh, although in other versions his mother is the goddess Sekhmet. A Demotic papyrus from the temple of Tebtunis, dating to the 2nd century AD, tells how Djoser fell in love with Imhotep's younger sister, Renpetneferet, and how Imhotep disguised himself to try to rescue her.

Another legend, recorded on the Upper Egyptian Famine Stela from the Ptolemaic period, credits him with ending a seven-year famine. One of his priests explained the link between the god Khnum and the rise of the Nile to the pharaoh, who then dreamed that the river god promised to end the drought. Some tales even pit him against an Assyrian sorceress in a magical duel, an anachronism that shows how much his fame grew.

Worship and religion

His main cult centre was in Memphis. In the Ptolemaic period he was also venerated at Thebes, Philae and Deir el-Medina, alongside Hathor, Maat and Amenhotep, son of Hapu. The Greeks identified him with Asklepios, whom the Romans called Aesculapius. The first references to his healing powers appear some 2,200 years after his death. There is no evidence that his cult survives today.

Legacy

His name has appeared in many works of fiction. Boris Karloff played him in The Mummy (1932), and Arnold Vosloo played the character in the 1999 film and its 2001 sequel, though these bear little relation to the historical man. Agatha Christie made him a priest in the novel Death Comes as the End, and he appears in the game Pharaoh, where the player must build his step pyramid. The American thrash metal band Iced Earth wrote a song titled "Imhotep (The Pharaoh's Curse)".

Ancient sources: The Song of the Harper, a Middle Kingdom composition, mentions him, and the Demotic papyrus from Tebtunis, dating to the 2nd century AD, recounts his family story.

Trivia

A name that reached history. Imhotep is the earliest scientist whose name has survived to the present day, according to sources on his historical figure.
From vizier to god. His name means "the one who comes in peace", and about two thousand years after his death his status had risen to that of a god of medicine and healing.
