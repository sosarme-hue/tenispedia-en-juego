hero of Russian fairy tales

Anika the Warrior

Anika the Warrior is a hero of Russian fairy tales, a knight errant who confronts Death. His story spread through a religious ballad and gave rise to a proverb about hopeless struggles.

The myth

According to a religious ballad widely known in Russia, Anika was a violent and sacrilegious man who set out to destroy the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem. A half-beast being called Wonder stopped him, and Anika challenged Wonder to a duel. Wonder defeated him, since it was really Death in disguise.

Anika begged for more time and tried to bribe Death, but no mercy was given. He also appears in folk plays such as Tsar Maximilian and King Herod.

Legacy

His name became a proverb for anyone engaged in a battle as hopeless as a fight against death. He is mentioned in Maxim Gorky's Childhood, and the plot of his tale is loosely based on the Byzantine epic of Digenes Akritas.

Trivia

A popular proverb. In Russian, "Anika the Warrior" describes someone who takes on something as futile as fighting death.
A character in Gorky. Maxim Gorky mentions Anika in his autobiographical novel Childhood.
