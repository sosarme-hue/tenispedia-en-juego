yōkai with eyes on the palms of its hands

Tenome

The tenome is a Japanese yōkai that looks like a blind man, with eyes set in the palms of its hands. It appears in Toriyama Sekien's Gazu Hyakki Yagyō and is one of the most recognisable monsters of modern Japanese folklore.

The myth

In the stories, the tenome looks like a blind man who searches the darkness with the eyes in his hands. In a tale from the Shokoku Hyaku Monogatari (1677), a young man who dares himself to spend the night in a graveyard is chased by an old figure with eyes on its palms. He flees to a temple, where a priest hides him in a large chest; the monster sucks the bones from his body, leaving only skin.

In Iwate Prefecture, the tradition tells of a blind man robbed and killed on the plains who, dying, wished he could see his attackers with the eyes of his hands. That resentment turned him into a tenome.

Legacy

The tenome inspired the character Krumm in Aaahh!!! Real Monsters, the Blind Specter boss in Cuphead and a monster in the Pathfinder roleplaying game. The cover of Destruction's album Release From Agony shows one hooked up to an IV. Guillermo del Toro echoes it in Pan's Labyrinth through the Pale Man.

Ancient sources: It appears in Toriyama Sekien's 18th-century Gazu Hyakki Yagyō and in the Edo-period kaidan collection Shokoku Hyaku Monogatari (1677).

Trivia

A blind man as model. The tenome takes the form of a zatō, a masseur or biwa tutor, trades that in the Edo period were often reserved for blind people through guilds.
A play on words. According to researcher Katsumi Tada, the drawing plays on expressions such as teme o ageru, meaning to reveal someone's tricks.
