princess of Kabul in Persian mythology, wife of Zal and mother of Rostam

Rudaba

Rudaba is a princess of Kabul in Ferdowsi's Persian epic Shahnameh. She is the mother of Rostam, the poem's main hero, and her love story with Zal is one of the best-known episodes of the work.

The myth

According to the poem, Rudaba was the daughter of Mehrab Kaboli, lord of Kabul, and of Sindukht. Her beauty was legendary: the text describes her as paradise gilded from top to toe. This description drew Zal to her before they had ever met.

Zal came to the walls of her palace, and Rudaba let down her tresses to him as a rope. He declined the help and climbed a rope of his own, and the two talked for a long time on the roof. Sam and the priests opposed the match, since her father descended from the wicked Zahhak, but the astrologers predicted that the union would produce a conqueror of the world, and the marriage was approved.

During the birth, the baby was so large that Rudaba nearly died. Zal burned a feather of the Simurgh in the sacred fire, and the bird taught him how to perform a caesarean that saved mother and child. The boy was Rostam.

Legacy

An English version of the story appears in The Story-Book of the Shah, a prose collection of legends of old Persia. Folktale collectors have noted the resemblance between Rudaba's long hair, the climbing scene and the German tale of Rapunzel.

Ancient sources: She appears in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, the great Persian epic.

Name with meaning. According to the Dehkhoda dictionary, her name means shining child, from rud (child) and āb (shining).
