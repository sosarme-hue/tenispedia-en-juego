androgynous Hindu deity, ancestor of the lunar dynasty

Ila

Ila is a deity in Hindu legend known for changing sex: depending on the telling, he is born or becomes a man and a woman. Ila is regarded as the chief progenitor of the lunar dynasty of Indian kings, also called the Aillas, which gives the figure a key place in the genealogy of ancient India.

The myth

According to tradition, Ila was the son or daughter of Vaivasvata Manu, the progenitor of mankind, and sibling of Ikshvaku, founder of the solar dynasty. The accounts differ on the birth: in some, the parents long for a son and the gods Mitra and Varuna change the child's sex; in others, a ritual error produces a daughter who is later transformed by Vishnu or the gods into a man named Sudyumna.

As an adult, Ila wanders by mistake into the sacred grove of Sharavana, dedicated to Parvati, where every male being except Shiva turns female. Depending on the text, a curse makes Ila change sex every month, while some versions present the change as Shiva's deliberate act to found the lunar dynasty. As a woman, Ila marries Budha, the god of the planet Mercury and son of the moon god Chandra, and bears Pururavas, the first king of the lunar dynasty. Later Shiva restores Ila's masculinity permanently, and as a man Ila fathers three sons.

The endings vary: in the Skanda Purana, Ila wishes to remain a woman, but the goddesses dissuade him and he returns to male form.

Ancient sources: In the Vedas, Ila appears as Idā, goddess of speech and mother of Pururavas. The story is told in the Puranas, the Ramayana and the Mahabharata.

Trivia

One figure, several names. Depending on the telling, the same figure is called Ila as a man or Ilā as a woman, and in some versions is named Sudyumna.
A deity who founds dynasties. The Aillas, descendants of Ilā, form the lunar dynasty, set against the solar dynasty founded by his brother Ikshvaku.
