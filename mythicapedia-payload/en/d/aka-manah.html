Zoroastrian demon of evil mind

Aka Manah

Aka Manah is the daeva of "evil mind" in Persian Zoroastrian tradition, a being that embodies sensual desire and wicked intent. He matters because his eternal rival, Vohu Manah, stands for good mind and the moral struggle at the heart of Zoroastrianism.

The myth

According to tradition, Aka Manah is the sensual desire sent by Ahriman to seduce the prophet Zoroaster. In the Gathas, the term akem manah describes a state of mind that leads to deceit, and the daevas are said to be its offspring. In later texts he becomes a demon, an ally of Angra Mainyu, who will battle Vohu Manah at the final renewal of the world.

In the Vendidad he tries to weaken Zoroaster's faith with ninety-nine questions, but the prophet does not fall for the trick. The Pahlavi texts place him as the second creature of Ahriman and credit him with the crying of newborn infants and the inability to tell good from evil.

Legacy

In Ferdowsi's Shahnameh he appears as Akvan, a demon with an elephant's head who abducts the hero Rostam and is finally beheaded by him.

Ancient sources: The Gathas, Yasht 19, the Vendidad, the Bundahishn and the Denkard, as well as the Epistles of Zadspram.

Trivia

Elephant head. In the Shahnameh, Akvan has long hair, blue eyes and an elephant's head with tusks in place of teeth.
A lost stone. Another story mentions a "Stone of Akvan", suggesting that more legends about him once existed but have not survived.
