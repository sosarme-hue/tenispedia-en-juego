Roman earth goddess and consort of Vulcan

Maia

Maia is a Roman goddess of Italic origin, probably linked to spring. She is best known as the consort of Vulcan and the mother of Mercury.

The myth

According to tradition, Maia was the wife of Vulcan and the mother of Mercury. After the Hellenization of Rome she was conflated with the Greek goddess Maia, and so became the daughter of the Titan Atlas and the mother of Mercury by Jupiter. Her functions are unclear: she may have represented growth, through a link between her name and maius, meaning "greater."

Worship and religion

She was sometimes called Maia Volcani and was worshipped at the Volcanal, at the foot of the Capitoline Hill, during the Vulcanalia on August 23rd. On May 1st, sacred to her, one of Vulcan's flamens sacrificed a pregnant sow. At Pompeii, a college of priests honoured her alongside Mercury.

Legacy

The month of May (Maius) may owe its name to this goddess.

Ancient sources: Varro, Gellius, Macrobius and Cornelius Lebeo mention her, and she appears on the Piacenza Liver and in the Fasti Antiates and Fasti Arvalium calendars.

Trivia

An inscription on a liver. The Piacenza Liver, a bronze model used in divination, bears the inscription "Uni Mae," which is linked to Juno.
A shared festival. Maia was also worshipped during the Vulcanalia, the festival of Vulcan held on August 23rd.
