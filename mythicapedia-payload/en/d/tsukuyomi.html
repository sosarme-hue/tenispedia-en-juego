Japanese god of the Moon

Tsukuyomi

Tsukuyomi is the moon kami of Japanese mythology and Shinto. Brother of Amaterasu, the sun goddess, his story explains why the Sun and the Moon never meet in the sky.

The myth

According to tradition, Tsukuyomi was born when Izanagi, after escaping the underworld, cleansed himself by bathing in water. He emerged from Izanagi's right eye; in another version he was born from a mirror of white copper held in Izanagi's right hand. He was the second of the three noble children, alongside Amaterasu and Susanoo.

The central episode is recorded in the Nihon Shoki. Amaterasu sent Tsukuyomi to a feast given by Ukemochi, the goddess of food. To prepare the meal, Ukemochi drew food from her own body or spat it out. Utterly disgusted, Tsukuyomi killed her. From her body grew the staple foods: rice from her eyes, millet from her ears, wheat, red beans from her nose and soybeans from her rear. Amaterasu, furious, refused ever to look at her brother again, and so the Sun and Moon never appear together. The Kojiki tells a similar tale but assigns it to Susanoo, who kills the food goddess Ōgetsuhime, often conflated with Ukemochi.

Worship and religion

He is venerated at auxiliary shrines such as the Tsukiyomi-no-miya, attached to the Inner Shrine of Kotaijingu (Naiku) at Ise, and the Tsukuyomi Shrine at Matsunoo-taisha in Kyoto. He is also one of the kami of Tenrikyo. His worship remains alive within contemporary Shinto.

Legacy

His name appears mostly in popular culture. In the anime Naruto it is the signature genjutsu of Itachi Uchiha, an illusion played out beneath a blood-red moon. He is a playable character in SMITE, a recurring demon in the Shin Megami Tensei series, a character in Persona 4 Arena Ultimax, and the stage name of the child god in Oshi no Ko. He also appears in Age of Mythology: Retold as one of the three main deities of the Japanese civilization.

Ancient sources: The Nihon Shoki records the feast and the killing of Ukemochi, and the Man'yōshū also mentions his name, while the Kojiki assigns a similar tale to Susanoo.

Trivia

A genjutsu with his name. In Naruto, Itachi Uchiha's most famous illusion technique bears Tsukuyomi's name and takes place under a blood-red moon.
