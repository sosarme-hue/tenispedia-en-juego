Greek goddess of healing

Aceso

Aceso is a minor goddess of Greek mythology, daughter of Asclepius and Epione, associated with the process of healing. Her name, meaning "the healer," places her among the medical deities who accompanied the god Asclepius.

The myth

Aceso has no mythology of her own; she is known mainly through her family tree. According to the Suda, a Byzantine lexicon, she was one of five daughters of Asclepius and Epione, along with Hygieia, Aegle, Iaso and Panacea. The sources attribute no deeds or narrative episodes to her.

Within the family, each sister stood for a different aspect of health. While Panacea embodied the cure-all remedy, Aceso embodied the act of healing itself, the path to recovery rather than the final result.

Worship and religion

Aceso was worshipped at least in Epidaurus and Athens, as statues found in both places suggest. Unlike her sisters, however, she does not appear in inscriptions as an object of cult, which suggests a limited religious role. Her presence in the sanctuaries of Asclepius reflects the importance of healing in Greek religion.

Ancient sources: The Suda, a Byzantine lexicon, is the only ancient written reference that names her as a daughter of Asclepius and Epione.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Aceso derives from a Greek verb meaning to cure an illness or ailment. Her name is therefore a description of her role.
A male counterpart. A male counterpart, Acesis, existed; in Epidaurus, Telesphorus, another minor healing god, was known by this local name.
