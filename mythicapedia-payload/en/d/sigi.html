legendary Viking chieftain, son of Odin

Sigi

Sigi is a legendary chieftain of Norse tradition, regarded as a son of Odin and an ancestor of the Völsung line. His story links the figure of the Viking warrior with the origins of a heroic dynasty.

The myth

According to tradition, Sigi was outlawed after killing a slave who had outdone him in hunting. With Odin's help he escaped and found success in Viking raids, eventually becoming king of Húnaland, a territory that some sources connect with the Frankish realm and others with the land of the Huns.

In his old age he was murdered by his wife's brothers, who wanted the throne. His son Rerir avenged his death and succeeded him as ruler. The Prose Edda presents him as monarch of Frankland, the first of a line that gave rise to the house of the Völsungs.

Ancient sources: The Völsunga saga and the Prose Edda mention him, and the Nafnaþulur list includes him among Odin's sons.

Trivia

An ambiguous name. Húnaland could refer both to the territory of the Franks, also called Hugones or Hugas, and to the land of the Huns.
A king of Frankland. The Prose Edda places him as the first ruler of what is now called Frankland.
