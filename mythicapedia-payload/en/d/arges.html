Cyclops of Greek mythology, forger of Zeus's lightning

Arges

Arges is one of the three Cyclopes of Greek mythology, brother of Brontes and Steropes, to whom myths credit the forging of Zeus's thunderbolts. His name means bright, a nod to the flash of lightning.

The myth

According to tradition, Arges was a son of Uranus and Gaia. Hesiod tells that Uranus, fearing his children, locked Arges and his brothers in Tartarus soon after their birth, together with the Hundred-Handed Ones. Freed during the war between the Titans and the Olympian gods, the three Cyclopes forged thunder and lightning for Zeus; according to Apollodorus they also made the Helmet of Invisibility for Hades and the trident for Poseidon. These weapons were decisive in the fall of the Titans.

The sources do not agree on names or lineage. Some authors make the Cyclopes sons of Neptune and Amphitrite, and in some versions Arges appears as Pyracmon: Virgil calls him Piracmon and Ovid calls him Acmonides. They are described as skilled, tireless craftsmen who worked on the island of Lipari under Vulcan.

His end is also told differently. In Hesiod's Catalogue of Women, the three Cyclopes are killed by Apollo in revenge for the death of his son Asclepius, struck down by a lightning bolt. This clashes with the Theogony, where they seem immortal; the mythographer Pherecydes of Athens resolves the discrepancy by saying Apollo killed the Cyclopes' sons instead. Another version holds that Zeus himself killed them so they would not forge lightning for anyone else.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Theogony and Catalogue of Women), Apollodorus, Virgil and Ovid mention him under varying names and versions of his story.

Trivia

A helmet for Hades. Apollodorus credits Arges and his brothers with making the Helmet of Invisibility for Hades, as well as Poseidon's trident.
Disputed deaths. Hesiod's Catalogue of Women says Apollo killed the Cyclopes, yet in the Theogony they seem immortal, a contradiction ancient writers tried to resolve.
