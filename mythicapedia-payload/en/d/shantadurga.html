form of the goddess Durga worshipped in Goa

Shantadurga

Shantadurga is the most popular form of the Hindu goddess Durga in Goa, and is also revered in parts of Karnataka. She is regarded as a form of the ancient mother goddess Santeri, and her name means "peaceful Durga".

The myth

According to local tradition, the goddess appeared before a sage named Shantamuni, who lived in Nagavya, today's Nagoa, and this is how she came to bear her name. The chapter that mentions her belongs to the Sahyadrikhanda of the Skanda Purana, although the epithet appears only in the chapter title.

Another story tells of a terrible war between Shiva and Vishnu that left the world in distress. Brahma asked Shakti to intervene, and the goddess took each god by one hand and reconciled them, bringing peace. Many scholars read this episode as a symbolic reflection of the sectarian conflicts between Vaishnavas and Shaivas in Goa.

Worship and religion

She is worshipped in almost every village in Goa and in Sindhudurg district of Maharashtra, often in the form of an ant hill that is regarded as her presence. Many shrines keep an ant hill, a stone or metal image, or a consecrated kalasha inside the sanctum. Her worship continues today among Hindus in the region.

Ancient sources: The Sahyadrikhanda, part of the Skanda Purana, is the ancient text that mentions her, though only in the title of the chapter.

Trivia

An ant hill as the goddess. In many Goan temples, the ant hill inside the sanctum is considered the goddess herself rather than a mere representation of her.
A goddess with snakes. At the Kavale temple, the four-armed image holds two male figures representing Vishnu and Shiva in its lower hands, while snakes appear in its upper hands.
