folk Hindu goddess of southern India

Isakki

Isakki is a folk Hindu goddess widely venerated in southern India, especially in Tamil Nadu. She is counted among the village deities (kaval deivam) and is believed to act as a guardian spirit for her devotees, particularly women who wish to have children.

The myth

According to the most common legend, Ambika was a housewife living peacefully with her husband Somasharman and their two sons. When the tarpanam ritual for the family ancestors was due, she gave food to a starving sage who begged for it before the rites had been performed. When he returned, Somasharman flew into a rage and drove his wife and children out of the house.

Ambika wandered until she found a calm place to rest. Later, regretting his anger, he went to look for her, but fearing him, she gave up her life. After death she took the form of a yakshini, still wanting to care for her children, and was able to regain her life for their benefit. In doing so she became the goddess Isakki.

Worship and religion

Her shrines are humble sanctuaries decorated with a cactus-like plant called paalkalli, whose milky sap is taken as a sign of the goddess. Near them grows a banyan or bo tree, where women hoping for children leave small wooden cribs or tie pieces of their saris. Festivals involve cooking food at the shrine and offering rice, cakes and fruit to large painted terracotta figures of the goddess, a cult that remains popular today in several southern districts.

Trivia

Link to Jain Ambika. Isakki corresponds to the Jain yakshini Ambika, who is usually shown with one or two children beneath a tree.
Cactus sap. When the cactus at her shrines is damaged, it oozes a milk-like sap that is regarded as a sign of the goddess.
