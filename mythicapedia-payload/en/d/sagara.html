legendary Indian king

Sagara

Sagara is a legendary Indian king of the solar dynasty, best known from the Mahabharata and the Ramayana. His story is tied to the descent of the Ganges river to Earth.

The myth

According to tradition, Sagara was born in the ashram of the sage Aurva, where his mother Yadavi had taken refuge. His name means 'with poison', because a rival wife had supposedly poisoned her during her pregnancy.

His sixty thousand sons, born to Sumati, and Asamansha, born to Keshini, went searching for a sacrificial horse that Indra had stolen. When they accused the sage Kapila of the theft, his furious gaze reduced them to ashes.

His great-grandson Bhagiratha persuaded the goddess Ganga to descend to Earth. Shiva softened her fall with his matted hair, and her waters purified the ashes of the brothers. Bhagiratha led the river to the sea, which he named Sagara in honour of his ancestor.

Legacy

His story lives on in Hindu and Jain traditions. In Jain sources he is the younger brother of the second Tirthankara, Ajitanatha, and a chakravartin, a universal ruler.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata and the Ramayana mention him; the Vishnu Purana tells of the horse sacrifice and the story of his sons.

Trivia

A name with poison. In Sanskrit, sa means 'with' and gara 'poison', which gives the king his name.
A sea named after a king. According to the story, Bhagiratha was the first Aryan to see the Bay of Bengal, and he named the sea after his great-grandfather.
