elephant-headed Hindu goddess

Vinayaki

Vinayaki is an elephant-headed Hindu goddess, often associated with Ganesha, the god of wisdom. Her mythology and iconography are loosely defined, and she rarely appears in Hindu scripture, which makes her a good example of the many feminine forms linked to Ganesha.

The myth

Hindu texts say little about her, and her story varies by tradition. The Puranas mention several elephant-headed women, usually demonesses or cursed goddesses. According to the Matsya Purana, Shiva created her as one of the Matrikas, the divine mothers, to defeat the demon Andhaka; in that setting she seems more a shakti of Shiva than of Ganesha, although her name, meaning belonging to Vinayaka, hints at the link. The Devi Purana, by contrast, names her as Ganesha's shakti and counts her as the ninth Matrika. The medieval Gorakshasamhita describes her with an elephant head, a pot belly, three eyes and four arms.

In Buddhist and Jain traditions she is an independent goddess. Buddhist works call her Ganapatihridaya, heart of Ganesha, and the Aryamanjusrimulakalpa presents her as a siddhi of Vinayaka and daughter of the god Ishana, an aspect of Shiva.

Worship and religion

Her worship is poorly documented. The earliest known images are a badly damaged terracotta plaque from Rairh, Rajasthan, dated between the first century BCE and the first century CE. From the tenth century onwards more distinct sculptures appear, such as Sri-Aingini, the forty-first yogini at the Chausath Yogini Temple in Bhedaghat. The sources do not clearly document festivals or rites devoted to her.

Ancient sources: The Puranas (Matsya, Devi, Agni, Linga and Skanda), the Harivamsa, the Gorakshasamhita and the Buddhist Aryamanjusrimulakalpa.

Trivia

Sri-Aingini. At the Chausath Yogini Temple in Bhedaghat, one of her best-known images shows her with a bent left leg resting on an elephant-headed figure seated at her feet.
Ganapatihridaya. Buddhist texts call her heart of Ganesha, a name that underlines her bond with the god even though she is treated as an independent goddess.
