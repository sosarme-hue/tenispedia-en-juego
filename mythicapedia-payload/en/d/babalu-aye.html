Yoruba orisha of the earth, disease and healing

Babalú-Ayé

Babalú-Ayé is a Yoruba orisha of the earth, linked to smallpox and other diseases but also to healing. In Cuba he is venerated alongside Saint Lazarus.

The myth

In Yoruba tradition, Sopona was old and lame. At a feast in Obatalá's palace he stumbled while trying to dance, and the other orishas laughed at him. Furious, he tried to infect them with smallpox, but Obatalá drove him into the bush, where he lives as an outcast.

In some Candomblé lineages, Nanã Burukú abandoned him on a beach, where crabs scarred him, and Yemoja raised him. Accounts of his parentage vary.

Worship and religion

He is honored with respect and sometimes fear, through offerings made by day and roasted corn. In Cuba, tens of thousands of devotees make a pilgrimage every 17 December to the San Lázaro sanctuary in El Rincón. Those who receive him in ceremony cover his head with a sackcloth cloth.

Legacy

He lives on in Cuban Santería, Palo and Umbanda, and his syncretism with Saint Lazarus has made him known among Cuba's Catholics.

Trivia

Annual pilgrimage. Every 17 December, tens of thousands of devotees visit the San Lázaro sanctuary in El Rincón, on the outskirts of Havana.
A name of respect. Devotees sometimes call him Alápa-dúpé, 'one who kills and is thanked for it'.
