Mesopotamian god, husband of Nanshe

Nindara

Nindara is a Mesopotamian god worshiped in the city of Lagash, known mainly as the husband of the goddess Nanshe. His character is little known because he appears only rarely in the surviving texts.

The myth

The surviving texts barely narrate episodes about Nindara, so his mythical role is almost unknown. Because of his bond with Nanshe, he belonged to her divine circle, along with Nin-MAR.KI, regarded as the goddess's daughter, and the servant god Hendursaga, whom some scholars consider his younger brother. His most common epithet, Lugal-uru16, "the powerful master", might suggest a warlike character.

Worship and religion

His main cult center was Ki'esa, where his temple was the Elaltum, "house producing date syrup". He was also the protective god of Niĝun, his wife's city, and received offerings in Girsu and Lagash. A text from the Ur III period describes offerings during a New Year festival that may have been betrothal gifts for him and Nanshe. Festivals involving boats in his honor are also documented.

Ancient sources: He appears in a royal inscription of Gudea, who credits him with giving the king strength, and in offering lists and administrative texts from Lagash.

Trivia

Lord of the holy sea. Some texts call him "the lord of the holy sea", and in a hymn to Nanshe he is described as "the tax collector of the sea", perhaps because of the port city where he was worshiped.
A sacred bird. Like Nanshe, he was associated with the dar bird (darmušen), possibly a kind of francolin.
