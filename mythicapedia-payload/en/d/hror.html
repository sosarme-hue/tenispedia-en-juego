Hróðr

Hróðr is a jötunn, a giantess of Norse mythology, whose name means "famed" or "glorious". She appears briefly in the poem Hymiskviða, where Thor is called "Hróðr's adversary".

The myth

The poem does not make the context clear, so the name could just as well refer to any giantess who opposed Thor, of which other sources, such as Hárbarðsljóð, mention many.

Some readings of Hymiskviða identify Hróðr as the mother of Týr, a giantess friendly to the Æsir and the wife of the jötunn Hymir. In that case, the poem suggests that Hymir is Týr's father, while the Prose Edda states that his father is Odin. Since giantesses raising hero figures is a common motif in Norse folklore, Hymir could also be a foster-father, an important relationship in Viking culture.

Ancient sources: She appears in the poem Hymiskviða of the Poetic Edda, and the Prose Edda names Odin as Týr's father.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. The Old Norse name hróðr has been translated as "glorious" or "famed".
An allied giantess. According to some readings, she is a giantess friendly to the Æsir, which was unusual among the jötnar.
