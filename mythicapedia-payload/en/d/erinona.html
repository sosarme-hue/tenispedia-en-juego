Cypriot maiden in a local variant of the myth of Adonis

Erinona

Erinona is a maiden from the island of Cyprus who appears in a local variant of the myth of Adonis. Her story survives only in the commentary of Servius, a Latin grammarian of the early fifth century AD.

The myth

In this Cypriot version, Erinona is a girl of great beauty and chastity, favoured by Athena and Artemis. Aphrodite, who disliked her, made Zeus fall in love with her, and Hera, fearing yet another infidelity from her husband, asked the goddess of love to make Adonis desire her too. Wrapped in a cloud, Adonis entered her chamber and raped her.

Zeus struck him down with a lightning bolt, but at Aphrodite's request Hermes guided his shade back to the world, and Hera persuaded Zeus to restore him fully to life. Artemis turned Erinona into a peahen, though she later returned her to human form, and Erinona married Adonis and bore him a son, Taleus.

Legacy

Erinome, also known as Jupiter XXV, is a small retrograde irregular satellite of Jupiter named after her.

Ancient sources: Servius, a Latin grammarian of the early fifth century AD, in a commentary on Virgil.

Trivia

Variable name. Depending on the manuscript, her name may be written as Erinoma, Erínome, Eritoma or Eurínome.
A disguised boar. In some versions, the boar that wounded Adonis was actually the god Ares in disguise.
