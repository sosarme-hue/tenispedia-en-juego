asura princess in Hindu mythology

Usha

Usha is the daughter of the asura king Banasura, and her love story with Aniruddha, grandson of Krishna, is one of the best-known episodes in Hindu mythology. The tale, told in the Bhagavata Purana, shows the clash between the asuras and the descendants of Vishnu.

The myth

Usha is the daughter of Banasura, who ruled from the city of Sonitapura. Banasura, son of Bali and grandson of Prahlada, was a devotee of Shiva, who granted him a thousand arms. According to the Bhagavata Purana, Usha dreamed of a young man and fell in love with him. Her friend Chitralekha, a talented artist with yogic powers, sketched portraits of the Vrishnis until Usha recognised Aniruddha, grandson of Krishna. Chitralekha then secretly brought him to the palace, shrinking him to the size of a doll.

Banasura discovered the lovers and bound Aniruddha with the mystical ropes of Varuna. The war that followed pitted the Yadus against Banasura's forces. Shiva came to defend his devotee, and Krishna faced him with divine weapons. In the end Krishna cut off Banasura's extra arms, leaving him four, since Vishnu had promised Bali not to kill his descendants. Banasura repented, and the couple married in Dvaraka.

Legacy

The story inspired the eighteenth-century Gujarati poem Okhaharan by Premanand Bhatt, the Telugu play Usha Parinayam (1901), and two films: a Telugu film from 1961 and the Tamil film Usha Kalyanam (1936).

Ancient sources: The Bhagavata Purana recounts her story, and the Bengali Manasamangala Kavya tradition describes her rebirth in a later life.

Trivia

A later life. According to the Bengali Manasamangala Kavya, Usha and Aniruddha are reborn as Behula and Lakshmindara and marry again.
