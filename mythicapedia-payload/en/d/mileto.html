Miletus

Miletus is a figure of Greek mythology, the legendary founder and eponym of the city of Miletus in Caria, in Asia Minor. His birth and ancestry vary from one author to another, and that variety is much of what makes him interesting.

The myth

According to tradition, Miletus was a son of Apollo, although his mother changes from account to account: Areia, Acacallis, daughter of Minos, or Deione. Some texts make him the son of Euxantius, himself a son of Minos. In one version, his mother hid him in a bed of smilax, and the shepherd Cleochus found him there and named him after the plant. In another, Minos abandoned him in a forest in Crete, but Apollo ordered she-wolves to nurse him until shepherds took him to their hut and raised him.

Minos and Sarpedon both loved him, but Miletus preferred Sarpedon, and this rivalry led to Sarpedon's expulsion from Crete. Following Sarpedon's advice, Miletus sailed to Caria and founded the city that bears his name. Some accounts add that he first killed Asterius, son of Anax, and that the region was then called Anactoria.

In Caria he married Eidothea, daughter of King Eurytus, and on his father-in-law's death he inherited the throne. By Eidothea, or by another woman depending on the version, he had twins, Byblis and Caunus. Byblis fell in love with her brother and, after her misfortunes, was transformed into a nymph or a spring from her tears. Nonnus of Panopolis, however, makes him a son of Asterius, with Caunus and Byblis as his siblings.

Legacy

His name remains attached to the city of Miletus, the capital of a kingdom and one of the most important cities of the Greek world in Asia Minor.

Ancient sources: The Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus and the Dionysiaca of Nonnus of Panopolis.

Trivia

A plant's name. In one account, Cleochus found him hidden in a bed of smilax and named him after the plant.
Anactoria. Some myths say the region later called Miletus was once known as Anactoria, and that the city was founded after the death of Asterius, son of Anax.
