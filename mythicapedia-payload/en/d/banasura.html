asura, king of Sonitpur in Hindu mythology

Banasura

Banasura is an asura, son of the king Bali, who ruled from the city of Sonitpur in Hindu tradition. He is remembered for his devotion to Shiva, for his daughter Usha and for the war that pitted his kingdom against the god Krishna.

The myth

According to tradition, Banasura was a demon with a thousand arms, feared even by human kings and devas. He was a devotee of Shiva, whom he accompanied by playing the mridanga drum while the god performed the tandava dance. As a reward, Shiva granted him a boon that made him nearly invincible, but over time he grew cruel and arrogant.

His daughter Usha dreamed of Aniruddha, Krishna's grandson. With the help of her friend Chitralekha, who abducted him by supernatural means, the young man was brought to Sonitpur, where Banasura captured him. The Yadu family attacked the city to rescue him, and a great battle followed in which Shiva, leading Banasura's forces, faced Krishna.

The conflict ended when Krishna, who had no intention of killing Banasura because he was Bali's son and a descendant of Prahlada, cut off his extra arms until only four remained. Banasura admitted his mistake and bowed before Krishna, arranging a chariot for the wedding of Aniruddha and Usha.

Worship and religion

In local tradition in Assam, the Mahabhairav temple is attributed to King Bana and linked to a Shiva lingam. According to belief, at the Rudrapada temple on the Brahmaputra, Shiva revealed his true form to the king.

Legacy

Banasura's name survives in Assam in the place name of the Agnigarh hillock, which local belief says was built to isolate Usha, and in the Banasura Sagar Dam in Kerala.

Ancient sources: His war with Krishna is narrated in the Bhagavata Purana, and his story appears in the Mahabharata and in Tamil works such as the Manimekalai.

Trivia

A name with meaning. Banasura means 'the demon Arrow', combining bāṇa (arrow) and ásura (demon, enemy of the devas).
