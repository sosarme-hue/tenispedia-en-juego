Nereid of Greek mythology

Thaleia

Thaleia, also spelled Thalia, is one of the Nereids, the sea-nymph daughters of Nereus, the Old Man of the Sea, and the Oceanid Doris. Her name comes from the Greek verb thallein, meaning "to flourish" or "to be green."

The myth

In Homer's Iliad, thirty-three Nereids, Thaleia among them, rise from the depths of the sea to the coast of Troy to join Thetis in mourning. Her son Achilles is overwhelmed with grief over the death of his beloved Patroclus, and they cry out in sympathy with him.

In some accounts, Thaleia travelled in the train of the nymph Cyrene along with her sisters Cymodoce, Nesaea and Spio. Later, these four, together with Thetis, Melite and Panopea, came to the aid of the hero Aeneas and his crew during a storm at sea.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions her in the Iliad, where the Nereids join Thetis in mourning Patroclus.

Trivia

A telling name. Her Greek name points to abundance and flourishing life, fitting for a sea nymph.
Help for Aeneas. In some accounts, the Nereids who accompanied her helped Aeneas and his crew survive a storm at sea.
