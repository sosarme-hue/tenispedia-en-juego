warrior of the Fianna in Irish mythology

Caílte mac Rónáin

Caílte mac Rónáin is a warrior of the Fianna, the band of heroes of the Irish Fenian Cycle, and a nephew of Fionn mac Cumhaill. He is remembered for his remarkable speed, his way with animals and his reputation as a great storyteller.

The myth

In the Fenian Cycle, Caílte is said to run at remarkable speed and to communicate with animals. Tradition remembers him above all as a storyteller, and some poems of the cycle are attributed to him.

In the Middle Irish tale Finn and Gráinne, his ancestry is given as son of Oisgen or Conscen, the son of the Smith of Múscraige Dobrut, and a son of Cumall's daughter. The Battle of Gabhra (Cath Gabhra) says that Caílte and Oisín are the only members of the Fianna to survive the final battle. In the tale Acallam na Senórach (Colloquy of the Ancients), both survive into Christian times and recount tales of the Fianna to Saint Patrick.

Another story places him beside the hero Cas Corach, with whom he killed three werewolf-like daughters of Airitech, who came out of the Cave of Cruachan every Samhain to destroy sheep. Cas Corach distracted them with music and persuaded them to take human form, and Caílte speared all three. His sword was Cruadh-Chosgarach. He also loved Scathniamh, daughter of Bodb Dearg, but they were parted and did not meet again until he was old and one of the last of the Fianna.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Middle Irish tale Finn and Gráinne, in the poem Cath Gabhra and in the medieval Acallam na Senórach, a text about the Fianna.

Trivia

A hero who survives. According to Cath Gabhra, Caílte is one of only two members of the Fianna to survive the final battle, along with Oisín.
A poem by W. B. Yeats. The poet W. B. Yeats portrays Caolte in his poem The Hosting of the Sidhe.
