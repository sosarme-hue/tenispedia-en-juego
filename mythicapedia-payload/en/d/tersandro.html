Theban king, one of the Epigoni

Thersander

Thersander is a hero of Greek mythology, son of Polynices and Argia, who took part in the Epigoni, the younger generation that attacked Thebes to avenge their fathers' deaths. He matters mainly because, according to tradition, he became king of Thebes after that victory. According to the myth, the Epigoni's assault relied on a deception: Thersander is said to have bribed Eriphyle with Harmonia's robe so that she would send her son Alcmaeon to fight beside him. His father had done something similar with Harmonia's necklace to persuade her to send her husband with the original attackers, the Seven against Thebes. The expedition succeeded and Thersander took the Theban throne. Thersander wished to fight with the Greeks in the Trojan War, but according to the account he was killed by Telephus before the war began, when the Greek fleet had mistakenly landed in Mysia. He was succeeded by his son Tisamenus, whose mother was Demonassa. Pindar praises him as a figure deserving honor after Polynices' death, for preserving the house of Adrastus for future generations. Cult: no information in the sources. Legacy: no information in the sources.

The myth

Ancient sources: Pindar praises him as the preserver of the house of Adrastus, and Virgil's Aeneid names him among the warriors hidden in the Trojan Horse, a mention that contradicts his death before the war.
