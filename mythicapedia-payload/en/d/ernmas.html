Irish mother goddess of the Tuatha Dé Danann

Ernmas

Ernmas is an Irish mother goddess, one of the Tuatha Dé Danann and the mother of many deities. She matters because she links the sovereignty goddesses and the war goddesses in a single lineage.

The myth

According to the Lebor Gabála Érenn, Ernmas was the daughter of Etarlam and belonged to the Tuatha Dé Danann. She is named as the mother of many: the three eponymous goddesses of Ireland, Ériu, Banba and Fódla, and the war goddesses Badb, Macha and Morrigan, also called Anann. Some sources say the war goddesses had a different father, the wizard Cailitin.

Her other children include three sons, Glonn, Gnim and Coscar, and two more, Fiacha and Ollom. Ernmas was killed during the first battle of Mag Tuired. The Lebor Gabála Érenn calls her 'the she-farmer', and her children share an affinity with the land, fertility and agriculture.

Ancient sources: She appears in the Lebor Gabála Érenn and the Cath Maige Tuired, medieval Irish texts that preserve the traditions of the Tuatha Dé Danann.

Trivia

A farmer among goddesses. The Lebor Gabála Érenn describes her as 'the she-farmer', an epithet not expected for the mother of war goddesses.
Goddesses who name Ireland. Ériu, Banba and Fódla are eponymous goddesses, meaning they give their names to the island in tradition.
