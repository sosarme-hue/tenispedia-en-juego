Turkic hero of Turkic tradition

Alp Er Tunga

Alp Er Tunga (also Alp Er Tonga) is a mythical hero of Turkic tradition, whose name is understood to mean «brave», «man» and «Siberian tiger». He matters because he appears in several medieval texts and because the Karakhanids claimed descent from him.

The myth

According to tradition, Alp Er Tunga is the son of Pashang and belongs to the Turkic heroic epic. Accounts remember him above all for his courage, and his name, combining the senses of brave, warrior and Siberian tiger, sums up that character.

His figure was passed down in Turkic epic, which gives its name to a cycle of tales known as the Alp Er Tunga Epic. The sources cited, however, do not preserve detailed accounts of his deeds or the episodes of his life.

Legacy

The Karakhanids, a Turkic dynasty, claimed descent from this hero, so his figure helped lend prestige and legitimacy to a political lineage. In this way the myth moved from epic into dynastic propaganda.

Ancient sources: He is mentioned in Mahmud al-Kashgari's Dīwān Lughāt al-Turk, in Yusuf Balasaguni's Kutadgu Bilig, and in a Vatican manuscript of the Oghuznama by an unknown author.

Trivia

A name with several meanings. Alp means «brave» or «warrior», Er means «man» or «soldier», and Tunga or Tonga means «Siberian tiger». The name thus condenses the character of the hero.
A lineage that claimed his blood. The Karakhanids presented themselves as descendants of Alp Er Tunga, a link that gave them a heroic origin in Turkic memory.
