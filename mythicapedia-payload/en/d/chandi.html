fierce goddess of Hinduism, form of Durga

Chandi

Chandi, whose name means "the fierce", is a Hindu goddess and a manifestation of Durga, closely linked to the Shakta tradition. She is revered as one of the principal forms of the Supreme Goddess, especially in eastern India and Bengal.

The myth

According to the Devi Mahatmya, Chandi emerges from the combined energies of the gods, who unite into a dazzling light that takes female form when the devas cannot win their long war against the asuras. She is described as three-eyed, adorned with the crescent moon, with many arms holding weapons and emblems, and riding a lion.

Among her deeds, tradition recalls her victory over the demon generals Chanda and Munda, and over the buffalo demon Mahishasura, whom she slew as Mahishasuramardini. In other accounts she assists Kali against Raktabija, whose spilled blood produced new demons; Kali drank it before it touched the ground. The Skanda Purana retells this story and adds Mahakali's killing of Chanda and Munda.

Worship and religion

The Chandi Homa, a fire sacrifice in which verses of the Durga Saptashati are recited, is one of the most popular rites in Hinduism and is performed across India, especially during Navaratri. Her mantra is one of the principal mantras of Shakti worship and is chanted during recitations of the Devi Mahatmya. In Bengal, many local cults are identified with her, such as Mangal Chandi, linked to good fortune, or Olai Chandi, invoked against disease. The Indian city of Chandigarh is named after the goddess.

Legacy

In Bengal, numerous Bengali poems called Chandi Mangala Kavyas were written between the 13th and early 19th centuries, merging local cults with mainstream Hinduism. The city of Chandigarh, joint capital of Punjab and Haryana, bears her name.

Ancient sources: The Devi Mahatmya, included in the Markandeya Purana, presents her as a primordial form of the Goddess; the Skanda Purana and the Narada Purana also mention her.

Trivia

Chandigarh. The city of Chandigarh, joint capital of Punjab and Haryana, takes its name from the goddess.
Many local cults. Many goddesses of Bengali villages and tribes bear her name followed by the place, such as Mangal Chandi or Olai Chandi.
