Guanche sun god

Magec

Magec was the name by which the pre-Hispanic peoples of Gran Canaria and Tenerife called the Sun, which they regarded as a deity in their religion. The figure shows how central the star was to the worldview of the islands' ancient inhabitants.

The myth

According to Guanche tradition, Magec was captured by Guayota and held prisoner inside Teide. He was later freed by Achamán, who restored him to his place in the sky.

The sources do not clearly establish the gender of this deity. Although it is traditionally considered male, in ancient Amazigh mythology the Sun could be perceived as female.

Worship and religion

The islanders revered the stars, and the Sun held a leading place: it was invoked in their sacred oaths. The inhabitants of Gran Canaria believed that human souls were 'daughters of Magec'.

The winter solstice, called Tabburt n Tagrest or 'Gate of Winter', marked the rebirth of the Sun on the longest night of the year, and was accompanied by household rites to bring luck, abundance and health. At the summer solstice, known as Ašu n Magheq or 'Triumph of the Sun', bonfires were lit and the tislit baqqiḍ, or 'vegetal bride', was burned. At dawn, goat's milk was poured from a gánigo while the name of the star was shouted three times.

Legacy

The word mago, used disparagingly for Tenerife's farmers, may derive, according to some researchers, from the cult these farmers paid to the Sun to secure good harvests.

Trivia

The gate of winter. The winter solstice was called Tabburt n Tagrest, literally 'Gate of Winter'. It marked the yearly opening of a solar gate.
A vegetal bride. At the summer solstice the tislit baqqiḍ, or 'vegetal bride', was burned as part of the rituals that accompanied the bonfires.
