legendary hero of Hawaiian mythology

Nana-Ula

Nana-Ula, also written Nanaula, is the hero who, according to Hawaiian tradition, led his people from Tahiti to Hawaii and is regarded as the first king of the island's royal dynasty. He matters because the stories about him link the Polynesian origins of the archipelago with the history of its ruling lines.

The myth

According to traditions recorded in the nineteenth century, Nana-Ula was a distinguished chief who arrived in Hawaii from the southern islands. It is not known whether he discovered the archipelago after being blown north by adverse winds or whether he deliberately sailed far into the ocean in search of new lands.

He came with his gods, priests, prophets and astrologers, along with a large body of followers and retainers. He also brought dogs, pigs and fowl, and seeds of useful plants. It is believed that the islands had no human inhabitants when he arrived.

In his lifetime, other chiefs of lesser rank arrived from Tahiti or Samoa in large double canoes, bringing priests, gods and the earliest Polynesian traditions. According to the same account, none of the pioneers of his era ever returned to the southern islands.

Legacy

His name became associated with the Hawaiian royal genealogy. King Kalākaua, the last monarch of Hawaii, compiled these legends in his work on the mythology of the archipelago.

Ancient sources: The legends were passed down orally and written down in the nineteenth century, chiefly in Kalākaua's compilation.
