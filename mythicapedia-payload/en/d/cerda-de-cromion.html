Crommyonian Sow

The Crommyonian Sow is a wild beast of Greek mythology that ravaged the land of Crommyon, between Megara and Corinth. It is best known for being slain by Theseus during his early adventures.

The myth

According to the tradition, the sow was a fierce and hard-to-tame creature that terrorised the region of Crommyon. Theseus, on his way to Athens, confronted and killed it, not out of obligation but because he believed a hero should face the most dangerous beasts.

The sources do not agree on its origin. The Bibliotheca attributed to Apollodorus makes it a daughter of Echidna and Typhon, and says it was named after the old woman who raised it. Strabo presents it as the mother of the Calydonian Boar, while Hyginus claims it was really a boar and came from Cremion. Diodorus stresses its ferocity and size, though he gives it no monstrous features.

Plutarch records another version: Phaia was a murderous female robber, nicknamed "sow" for her coarse manners, and she was the one Theseus killed.

Legacy

In Rick Riordan's Percy Jackson and the Olympians series, a flying sow called the Clazomenian Sow clearly recalls the Crommyonian Sow, although the character does not belong to the ancient tradition.

Ancient sources: The Bibliotheca of Pseudo-Apollodorus, Strabo, Hyginus, Diodorus Siculus and Plutarch, who recount it in their accounts of Theseus and other heroes.

Trivia

Phaia, the sow and the robber. Plutarch relates that the sow was really a woman named Phaia, a thief and criminal who was nicknamed after the animal because of her character and way of life.
A boar in another version. Hyginus states that what Theseus killed at Crommyon was a boar, and that it came from Cremion rather than Crommyon.
