dark forest of Norse mythology

Myrkviðr

Myrkviðr is the name of a forest in Norse mythology, meaning «dark wood» in Old Norse. Its location shifts from source to source, appearing in eastern Europe, in Sweden, or between the realm of the gods and the realm of fire beings.

The myth

The sources place it in very different regions. In the Hervarar saga, it was a forest region north of the Ukrainian steppe, where Goths and Huns clashed, and in other tales it separated the Huns from the Burgundians.

Sagas and heroic legends, such as that of Helgi Hundingsbane, place it in Kolmården, in Sweden. In Styrbjarnar þáttr it lay south of Uppsala, and its remnant was known as Lunsen. In the Poetic Edda, as in Lokasenna, it appears between Asgard and Muspelheim, through which Muspell's sons ride during Ragnarök.

Legacy

The name entered English as Mirkwood, first through Walter Scott in Waverley and then William Morris. J. R. R. Tolkien popularized it in his fiction. The Old Norse myrkr is related to the English words mirky and murky.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda (Lokasenna), Atlakviða, Helgakviða Hundingsbana I, Hlöðskviða and the Hervarar saga.

Trivia

A name with European echoes. Related forms of the name appear elsewhere in Europe, such as the Black Forest (Schwarzwald), suggesting it may have been a general term for dark, dense forests.
From ancient forest to Tolkien's world. Tolkien popularized the anglicized name Mirkwood, though Walter Scott and William Morris had used it earlier.
