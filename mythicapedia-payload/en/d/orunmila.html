Yoruba god of wisdom and divination

Orunmila

Orunmila, also Orula in Latin America, is the orisha of wisdom, divination and fate in the Yoruba religion. Tradition holds that he witnessed the creation of the universe and is considered the originator of the Ifá divination system.

The myth

According to a patakí, Obatalá ordered all his sons killed after learning that Ogun had tried to sleep with his own mother. Eleguá hid Changó with Dadá and buried Orula at the foot of a ceiba tree, bringing him food every day. When Obatalá fell ill, Changó healed him, and Eleguá seized the moment to plead for Orula's pardon. Grateful, Changó felled the ceiba, carved a fine tablet and gave it to his brother along with the secret of divination. Since then Orula has been master of the tablet, the diviner of the future and counselor of humankind.

Worship and religion

Babalawos, the priests of Ifá, invoke him through suyeres and orikis before consulting the oracle. His attributes include the Ifá divination tablet, the opele chain and kola nuts. Offerings include goat, snapper, black hen, dove and deer, and his feast falls on October 4. His worship remains very much alive today.

Trivia

A pact with Death. According to tradition, Orunmila was the only deity who made a pact with Ikú, Death, so that she would spare his children.
A tablet from Changó. The divination tablet, or até, once belonged to Changó, Orunmila's brother, who handed it over with the permission of Olofi.
