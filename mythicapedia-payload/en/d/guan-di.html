Chinese god of war and wealth, deified form of Guan Yu

Guan Di

Guan Di, the "Emperor Guan", is the deification of the general Guan Yu, a loyal companion of Liu Bei during the late Han dynasty and the Three Kingdoms period. He is one of the most venerated deities in China, associated with war and, in popular devotion, with commerce.

The myth

According to the historical accounts, Guan Yu came from Shanxi and fought with Liu Bei in the Yellow Turban Rebellion. After being captured by Cao Cao, who treated him respectfully, he eventually returned to his former lord.

At the Battle of Boma, the tradition says, he killed the general Yan Liang after recognising his parasol in the middle of combat, and Cao Cao rewarded him with a noble title before he left. Later, while attacking at Fancheng, Sun Quan broke his alliance with Liu Bei and seized Jing Province. Guan Yu, with no time to react, was ambushed and executed in early 220.

Tradition turned this tragic end into a model of loyalty and righteousness, above all through popular storytelling and the Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which enlarged his image into that of an almost legendary hero.

Worship and religion

After his death he was deified, a process that, according to the sources, began in the Sui dynasty. He is worshipped under the names "Emperor Guan" (Guān Dì) and "Lord Guan" (Guān Gōng). He is venerated in Chinese folk religion, popular Confucianism, Taoism and Chinese Buddhism. Small shrines to him are almost ubiquitous in traditional shops and restaurants, and many Chinese still worship him today.

Legacy

His figure inspired storytelling, drama and the novel Romance of the Three Kingdoms, which cemented his reputation for loyalty and martial courage. The airport in Yuncheng, his hometown, bears his name.

Ancient sources: His principal biography appears in Chen Shou's Records of the Three Kingdoms in the third century, expanded by Pei Songzhi in the fifth century.

Trivia

Peerless beard. According to the Records of the Three Kingdoms, Zhuge Liang described him as having a "peerless beard". His traditional red face may derive from the novel or from opera, where red symbolized loyalty.
His weapon. In the novel, his weapon was a guandao named Green Dragon Crescent Blade, said to weigh 82 catties.
