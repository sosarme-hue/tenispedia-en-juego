Lithuanian god of the sea and storm

Bangpūtys

Bangpūtys is a masculine deity of Lithuanian mythology, reconstructed by some mythologists from very scanty sources as a god of the sea and storms. According to these reconstructions, he is austere and unrelenting.

The myth

Modern reconstructions portray him with a beard, wings and two faces, holding a fish in his left hand, a utensil in his right, and with a rooster on his head. His sons are the wind gods: Rytys (easterly wind), Pietys (southerly), Šiaurys (northerly) and Vakaris (westerly).

The stories describe him as a very vindictive god. In one of them, Auštaras, son of Aušrinė and Mėnuo (the other god of the easterly wind), was swimming in the sea and stirred up a storm, and Bangpūtys wanted to drown him. He is sometimes associated with Vėjopatis.

Trivia

A two-faced god. He is usually shown with two faces, a beard and wings, and his usual attributes are a fish, a utensil and a rooster.
A vengeful god. In one story, Bangpūtys tries to drown Auštaras after he stirs up a storm at sea.
