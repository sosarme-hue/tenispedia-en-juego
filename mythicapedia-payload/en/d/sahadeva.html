hero of the Mahabharata, youngest of the Pandavas

Sahadeva

Sahadeva is the youngest of the five Pandava brothers, the heroes of the Mahabharata, the great epic of India. He is remembered for his skill with the sword and for his calm, discreet and virtuous character.

The myth

Sahadeva and his twin brother Nakula were born to Madri, the second wife of King Pandu. Pandu was cursed never to father children, so Madri invoked the divine twins, the Ashvins, to have them. After Pandu's death, Madri threw herself onto his funeral pyre and entrusted her sons to Kunti, who raised them with the other Pandavas in Hastinapura.

Draupadi married all five brothers following an unexpected order from Kunti, and Sahadeva also wed Vijaya, princess of Madra, with whom he had a son named Suhotra. When Yudhishthira sought to display his power through the Rajasuya, Sahadeva was sent south to subdue kingdoms. During the exile, he disguised himself as a cowherd at the court of Virata and went unnoticed. In the Kurukshetra War he fulfilled his vow to kill Shakuni, the maternal uncle of the Kauravas, who had rigged the dice game.

At the end of the epic, the Pandavas set out on a final journey to the Himalayas from which none would return. One by one they fell, and Sahadeva was the second to die, after Draupadi, because of his excessive pride in his wisdom. Only Yudhishthira reached the summit.

Legacy

Today his name is applied to someone who is reserved and does not reveal what they understand. In southern India he is also regarded as a skilled astrologer and a master of intuition.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Harivamsa and the Bhagavata Purana, as well as the Rig-veda, which mentions a sage of the same name.

Trivia

A namesake sage. The Rig-veda mentions a sage named Sahadeva, with the patronymic Varsha-Guira, meaning voice of a bull.
A meaningful name. His name combines saha, meaning with, and deva, meaning god, so it means with the gods or protected by the gods.
A birth by invocation. Madri invoked the Ashvins only once and obtained two sons, Nakula and Sahadeva.
