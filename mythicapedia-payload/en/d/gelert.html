faithful hound of Welsh folklore

Gelert

Gelert is the hunting hound of the prince Llywelyn ab Iorwerth in a Welsh legend linked to the village of Beddgelert in North Wales. His story, of a faithful dog wrongly killed, is one of the best-known versions of the 'faithful hound' folk motif.

The myth

According to the tradition, Llywelyn ab Iorwerth, a medieval ruler of North Wales, returns from hunting to find his baby's cradle overturned and Gelert with a blood-smeared mouth. Convinced the dog has attacked the child, he kills it with his sword.

The dog's dying yelp is answered by the cry of the baby, unharmed beneath the cradle. Nearby lies a dead wolf, which Gelert had killed to protect the child. Overcome with remorse, Llywelyn buries the dog with great ceremony, and the legend says the village took its name from this act. From that day the prince never smiles again, and the story adds that he still hears the dog's cry.

Legacy

The tale inspired several English poems, including William Robert Spencer's 'Beth Gêlert; or, the Grave of the Greyhound', written around 1800, and Francis Orray Ticknor's 'Gelert'. It appears in Disney's Lady and the Tramp (1955), where the rescuing dog is Tramp. There have also been ballet versions and a short film from 2002.
