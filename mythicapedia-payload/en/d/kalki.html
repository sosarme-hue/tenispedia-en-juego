tenth incarnation of Vishnu in Hinduism

Kalki

Kalki is the tenth and final avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu. According to tradition, he will appear at the end of the Kali Yuga to restore dharma and inaugurate a new age of truth.

The myth

The stories place his arrival at the close of the Kali Yuga, the darkest and most corrupt age of the cosmic cycle. According to the Bhagavata Purana, he will ride a white horse from the Sind region, wield a sword and lead a great army to destroy corruption. His two daughters will give rise to the solar and lunar dynasties, and a new Satya Yuga will begin.

Kalki first appears in the Mahabharata, though his role there differs: he seems to be an extension of the Parashurama legend, in which a Brahmin warrior destroys abusive Kshatriyas and restores justice without ending the cycle of existence. The Puranas expand the story in different ways. The Kalki Purana, a late text probably composed in Bengal, has him born in the village of Shambala, trained in arms by Parashurama, and given by Shiva the white horse Devadatta, a sword and a parrot who knows all. After defeating an impious army, he founds a new age and ascends to Vaikuntha.

Worship and religion

In the regional tradition of the Vaishno Devi shrine on Trikuta mountain, the goddess waits in a cave for Kalki's arrival, having been refused by Rama, and believers hold that she will marry him in his future incarnation.

Legacy

The word kalkin appears in the Vaishnava grammar Harinamamrta-vyakarana as a technical name for the general future tense, reflecting Kalki's unique status as the only major avatar belonging entirely to the future. His figure reappears in the Buddhist Kalachakra Tantra, where righteous kings bear the name, and in Sikh texts such as the Dasam Granth, which counts him as the twenty-fourth incarnation.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Mahabharata (Markandeya section) and in the Bhagavata, Garuda, Agni and Devi Bhagavata Puranas; the Kalki Purana is later.

Trivia

A shrine in waiting. In the Vaishno Devi tradition, the goddess waits in a cave on Trikuta mountain for Kalki's arrival.
A future in grammar. A Vaishnava grammar uses his name to designate the general future tense.
