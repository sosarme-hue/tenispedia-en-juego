king of Mycenae in Greek mythology, master of Heracles' twelve labours

Eurystheus

Eurystheus is a king of the Argolid in Greek mythology and the cousin of Heracles, who set the hero his famous twelve labours. Overshadowed by his cousin, he matters chiefly as the instrument of Hera's plans and as the frame for much of the hero's story.

The myth

According to the tradition, Eurystheus was the son of King Sthenelus and Nicippe, though other accounts name Archippe or Antibia as his mother, and he was a grandson of Perseus. His wife was Antimache, daughter of Amphidamas, and their children included Admete, Alexander, Eurybius and Eurypylus.

The myth says that Hera hastened his birth and delayed that of Alcmene's son, so that Eurystheus, born prematurely at seven months, could rule Mycenae before his cousin. After Heracles committed a terrible crime in a fit of madness, the Delphic oracle ordered him to serve Eurystheus for twelve years, promising immortality if he completed the tasks.

Frightened by the Nemean lion's skin, Eurystheus barred Heracles from the city and hid in a bronze jar whenever the hero approached, passing his orders through the herald Copreus. Because he did not count the Hydra, which Heracles completed with the help of Iolaus, nor the Augean stables, cleaned for payment, the labours numbered twelve rather than ten. After Heracles was made a god, Eurystheus pursued his children, who fled to Athens; the war that followed ended in an Athenian victory after the sacrifice of Macaria, and Eurystheus and his sons died.

Accounts of his end differ. In one version Hyllus caught him by the Scironian rocks and killed him, and his head was given to Alcmene; other sources credit Iolaus. Pausanias places his tomb along the Scironian Way, while Strabo speaks of a burial at Gargettus and of a head buried near Tricorinthus.

Legacy

In Euripides' play Heracleidae, Eurystheus pursues Heracles' children and, at the end, prophesies that his grave in Attica will protect the Athenians. On screen he appears in the 1958 film Hercules, though there he is a minor villain far removed from his mythological role.

Ancient sources: Homer and Euripides mention him; Euripides makes him a central character in Heracleidae, while other ancient writers present him as ruler of Argos.

Trivia

Born early. According to the myth, Hera hastened his birth and delayed Heracles', giving Eurystheus the throne of Mycenae.
A labour not counted. Eurystheus did not count the Lernaean Hydra because Heracles had help from Iolaus, which brought the labours to twelve rather than ten.
