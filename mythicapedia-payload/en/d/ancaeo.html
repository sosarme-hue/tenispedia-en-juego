Greek hero, Argonaut and hunter of the Calydonian Boar

Ancaeus

Ancaeus is a hero of Greek mythology, a son of King Lycurgus of Arcadia. He is known for taking part in the voyage of the Argonauts and in the hunt for the Calydonian Boar, where he met his end.

The myth

According to the tradition, Ancaeus was a son of King Lycurgus of Arcadia, though ancient accounts disagree on his mother: some name Cleophyle, others Eurynome or Antinoe. He married Iotis and became the father of Agapenor, who later led the Arcadian forces during the Trojan War. His lineage thus linked a royal house of Arcadia to one of the great episodes of Greek heroic myth.

Ancaeus is remembered for two great heroic ventures. He sailed with the Argonauts, the crew that, according to the myth, accompanied Jason in his quest for the Golden Fleece; the voyage was one of the most famous in the Greek world and gathered many heroes of the age. Later he joined the hunt for the Calydonian Boar, a collective hunt against a monstrous beast sent to punish the land, and there he met his end.

Before setting out, the story tells of an unsettling detail: his weapons were left hidden at home, as an omen of what was to come. He nevertheless went forth dressed in a bearskin and armed only with a labrys, a double-bladed axe.

Trivia

Hidden weapons. His arms were left hidden at home, a detail the story presents as an ominous sign of his fate.
