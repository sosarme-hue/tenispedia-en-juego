Egyptian child god of joy and music

Ihy

Ihy is the child god of joy and music in Egyptian mythology, son of the goddess Hathor. His cult centred on Dendera, where he formed a sacred triad with his mother and Horus.

The myth

According to Egyptian tradition, Ihy is the son of Hathor, although in some legends his mother is given as Isis, Sekhmet or Neith. He is most often presented as the son of Horus, but in some accounts his father is the sun god Ra, which links him to the beginning of creation.

Texts mention him only occasionally, as in the Book of the Dead. The Coffin Texts offer meanings for his name such as "the sistrum player" or "calf", alluding to Hathor, the cow goddess. He was also called "god of hearts", which gave him a role in the funerary rites of the pharaoh and linked him with Anubis.

Worship and religion

Ihy was not very popular among ordinary people, as he belonged to the last generation of gods. His main cult site was Dendera, where he was worshipped alongside Hathor and Horus. In the temple's mammisi, or "birth house", annual rituals commemorated his birth, and there mother and child awaited the arrival of Horbehedety, the Horus of Edfu. The walls of this building still bear his images.

Ancient sources: The Book of the Dead mentions him occasionally, and the Coffin Texts record interpretations of his name.

Trivia

A name with several readings. The Coffin Texts offer two readings of his name: "the sistrum player" and "calf".
Fertility mysteries. According to the sources, mystery plays in thirteen acts on fertility were performed in his temples.
