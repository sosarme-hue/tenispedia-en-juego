Trojan princess, daughter of Laomedon

Aethilla

Aethilla is a Trojan princess, daughter of King Laomedon and sister of Priam. In her story she sets fire to the ships of her Greek captors, a tale that explains the origin of several place names and a collective nickname.

The myth

After the fall of Troy, Aethilla became the captive of Protesilaus, who took her, along with other prisoners, on his voyage home. When he landed in Thrace to collect fresh water, he went inland, and she persuaded her fellow captives to set fire to the ships. Left without vessels, the Greeks were forced to stay there and founded the town of Scione.

Other authors place the event in Italy. The nearby river was named Nauaethus ("of the burning ships"), and Aethilla, Astyoche and Medesicaste were called the Nauprestidai ("those who set fire to ships"). In other versions the instigator was a Trojan captive named Setaia, who was crucified by the Greeks, yet a town and a rock near Sybaris were named after her.

Ancient sources: Strabo records a variant of the episode involving the river Neaethus in Sicily, and Virgil's Aeneid tells a very similar story of ship-burning by other Trojan women.

Trivia

A story that repeats. In the Aeneid, Virgil tells an almost identical ship-burning in Sicily, but with other protagonists, such as Beroe and Pyrgo.
A crucified woman. In some versions, the captive Setaia was crucified by the Greeks for inciting the fire, yet a place near Sybaris was named after her.
