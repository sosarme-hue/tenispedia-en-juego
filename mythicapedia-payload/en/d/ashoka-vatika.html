Ravana's garden in Lanka, in Hindu mythology

Ashoka Vatika

Ashoka Vatika is a grove in Lanka, in the kingdom of the rakshasa king Ravana. Hindu tradition places it as the site where Ravana held Sita, the wife of Rama, captive.

The myth

In the Ramayana of Valmiki, Ravana keeps Sita in this garden, but she refuses to stay in the palace and prefers to sit beneath a simsapa tree. Ravana's wife Mandodari visits her there, and Hanuman first meets her, identifying himself by showing her Rama's ring.

During his first visit to Lanka, Hanuman destroys much of the garden, including the Pramda Vana at its centre. Sita remains there until the battle between Rama and Ravana ends, with Ravana and most of his clan destroyed. The garden houses around it were built by Vishvakarma himself.

Worship and religion

Its traditional location is identified with the Seetha Eliya area in Sri Lanka, where the Hindu Sita Amman temple commemorates the episode.

Legacy

The site has drawn media interest in recent years because of its connection with Hindu mythology.

Ancient sources: It appears in the Vishnu Purana, in Valmiki's Ramayana and its later versions, such as Tulsidas's Ramacharitamanas, in the Sundara Kanda.

Trivia

Hakgala Botanical Garden. The Ashoka Vatika is believed to be located at today's Hakgala Botanical Garden, at the base of the Hakgala rock formations near the resort city of Nuwara Eliya.
Sita Jharna. Near Sita Eliya is a stream called Sita Jharna, where tradition says Sita used to bathe.
