messenger squirrel of Norse mythology

Ratatoskr

Ratatoskr is a squirrel in Norse mythology who runs up and down the world tree Yggdrasil, carrying messages between the eagle perched in its crown and the dragon Níðhöggr, who dwells among its roots. His name is usually read as "drill-tooth", although its etymology is still debated.

The myth

In the Poetic Edda poem Grímnismál, the god Odin, disguised as Grímnir, describes Ratatoskr running up and down Yggdrasil. His task is to carry messages between the eagle perched at the top and Níðhöggr, who lives beneath one of the roots.

In chapter 16 of Gylfaginning, Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda adds that the squirrel carries more than words, spreading slanderous gossip that provokes both of them.

This role fits a tree that, according to the sources, is gnawed by four harts and by Níðhöggr, and that also endures the hostility of the creatures that live in it.

Legacy

Ratatoskr appears in the Marvel comic The Unbeatable Squirrel Girl, where Ryan North introduces her as a female character with shapeshifting powers and the ability to stir up discord. She is a recurring boss in the video game La-Mulana 2, a playable character in Smite, and a prominent side character in God of War Ragnarök, where a ghostly version had already appeared as an ally of Atreus in God of War. The Icelandic viking metal band Skálmöld and the Swedish band Brothers of Metal have written songs about her, and in One Piece the warhammer Ragnir can take the form of a squirrel thanks to a Devil Fruit based on Ratatoskr.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda poem Grímnismál and the Prose Edda's Gylfaginning by Snorri Sturluson, both from the 13th century.

Trivia

A disputed name. Sophus Bugge proposed that the name came from Old English and means "rat-tooth". Albert Sturtevant rejected this hypothesis for the element rata-.
A squirrel with a real echo. Richard W. Thorington Jr. and Katie Ferrell suggest that her role may come from the habit of the European red squirrel of giving a scolding alarm call when danger appears.
