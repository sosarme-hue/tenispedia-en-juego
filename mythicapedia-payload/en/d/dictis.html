fisherman of Greek myth, brother of King Polydectes

Dictys

Dictys is a fisherman in Greek myth, brother of King Polydectes of Seriphos, and is remembered above all for taking in Danaë and her son Perseus. His role as protector of mother and child makes him one of the kindest figures in the Perseus cycle.

The myth

According to the most common genealogy, Dictys and Polydectes were sons of Magnes, son of Aeolus, and of an unnamed Naiad. Other versions make them sons of Peristhenes and Androthoe, or of Poseidon and Cerebia. Unlike his brother, Dictys is portrayed as a simple fisherman, although his lineage was illustrious.

One day he hauls in a drifting chest holding Danaë and the infant Perseus. He takes them in, raises the boy as his own son, and brings them before Polydectes. Later the violent king abuses Dictys and Danaë, who take refuge in a temple. Perseus returns after slaying Medusa, turns the king and his courtiers to stone with the Gorgon's head, and leaves Dictys as the new ruler of Seriphos.

Worship and religion

Pausanias reports that in Athens there was an altar to Dictys and Clymene, called the saviours of Perseus, within a sacred precinct of the hero. Little is known about Clymene, or whether she was Dictys's wife, and some identify her with the Oceanid Clymene of Hesiod's Theogony. There is no evidence of a widespread cult.

Legacy

His name survives mainly in Euripides' tragedy bearing his title, now lost apart from a few fragments, and on some Greek vases that show him dressed as a fisherman.

Ancient sources: Aeschylus shows him fishing with other fishermen in a tragedy, Euripides wrote a lost play named after him, and Pausanias and Hesiod's Theogony add details about his family and his cult.

Trivia

A name that is a trade. Dictys means 'net' in Greek, a term that fits his role as a fisherman.
A fisherman among heroes. Despite his illustrious lineage, Greek vases show him dressed as a humble fisherman.
