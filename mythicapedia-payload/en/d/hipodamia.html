Hippodamia

Hippodamia is, in Greek myth, the daughter of King Oenomaus of Pisa and the wife of Pelops. Her story joins beauty, a deadly chariot contest for her hand and a chain of crimes and curses that reached her descendants.

The myth

According to tradition, Hippodamia was a young woman of great beauty who attracted many suitors. Her father, Oenomaus, feared a prophecy that he would die at the hands of his son-in-law, so he set a condition: any suitor had to beat him in a chariot race to the Isthmus of Corinth, and whoever lost would be executed. Many men died this way, and Pausanias preserves a list of their names.

Pelops, son of Tantalus, came to seek her hand. With the help of Myrtilus, Oenomaus's charioteer, he won: Myrtilus replaced the bronze linchpins of the chariot with wax ones, and the chariot broke apart during the race. Oenomaus was dragged to death by his horses. When Myrtilus asked for his reward, Pelops threw him off a cliff into the sea, and the charioteer cursed the couple.

The marriage produced many children, among them Atreus and Thyestes. According to a scholium, Hippodamia plotted the death of Chrysippus, an illegitimate son of Pelops; when her sons refused, she attacked him herself. Pelops banished her, and different versions say she withdrew to Midea or took her own life. Another tradition says her bones were later moved to Olympia by oracle's command. Myrtilus's curse would afterwards haunt Atreus, Thyestes and their descendants, including Agamemnon and Orestes.

Worship and religion

At Olympia stood a site called the Hippodameion, within the Altis, where according to Pausanias women offered sacrifices to Hippodamia once a year. Pausanias also says that Hippodamia, with sixteen women, first organised the Heraean Games, reserved for women, at the temple of Hera. Pelops honoured the dead suitors as heroes, sacrificing to them every year.

Legacy

The name Hippodamia, meaning "she who tames horses", was given to an asteroid discovered in 1901.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions her in the Catalogue of Women and the Great Eoiae, and Pausanias in his Description of Greece.

Trivia

Women's games. Pausanias credits Hippodamia with founding the Heraean Games, competitions reserved for women and held in honour of Hera.
A list of suitors. Pausanias records the names of many suitors killed by Oenomaus, and Pelops erected a monument in their memory.
