location in Norse mythology linked to the world tree

Hoddmímis Holt

Hoddmímis holt (Hoddmímis holt) is a place in Norse mythology where, according to a prophecy, Líf and Lífthrasir will survive Fimbulvetr, the long winter that heralds the end of the world. Many scholars identify it with the world tree Yggdrasil, although this remains a hypothesis.

The myth

In the Poetic Edda, the god Odin asks the jötunn Vafþrúðnir who among humans will survive Fimbulvetr. Vafþrúðnir answers that they will be Líf and Lífthrasir, who will hide in Hoddmímis holt, feed on the morning dew, and become the ancestors of future generations.

Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in chapter 53 of the Gylfaginning, repeats the idea: the god High tells King Gylfi, disguised as Gangleri, that the couple will lie hidden during Surt's fire and that their descendants will repopulate the world.

Scholars have linked the grove to Mímameiðr, Mímir's tree, usually taken to mean Yggdrasil. Rudolf Simek argues that it should not be read literally as a forest but as another name for the world tree, so that the creation of humankind from tree trunks would be repeated after Ragnarök.

Ancient sources: The Poetic Edda, in the poem Vafþrúðnismál, and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in the Gylfaginning.

Trivia

A tree-man. Simek compares the story with Örvar-Oddr, who becomes young again after living as a tree-man, according to Örvar-Odds saga.
A shepherd in a tree. Simek also cites a Bavarian legend in which a shepherd lives inside a tree and his descendants repopulate the land after a plague.
