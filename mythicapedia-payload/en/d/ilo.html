king of Dardania, son of Dardanus

Ilus

Ilus is, in Greek tradition, the eldest son of Dardanus and the successor of his father on the throne of Dardania. His name is linked to Ilion, the city later known as Troy, whose lineage the myths trace back to him.

The myth

According to the stories, Ilus was the son of Dardanus and Batea, although some versions name a different mother, Olizone, daughter of Phineus. After Dardanus died, Ilus inherited the throne and reigned for a long time.

He died without children, so the kingdom passed to his brother Erichthonius, ancestor of the later Trojans. In some traditions the two brothers are called Erichthonius and Zacynthus. The Iliad mentions Ilus's tomb in the middle of the Trojan plain, a detail the ancients took as a landmark.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Ilion, another name for Troy, and by extension to the Iliad, the poem named after the city.

Ancient sources: Homer mentions him in the Iliad when placing his tomb on the Trojan plain; other ancient sources record his genealogy with variations.
