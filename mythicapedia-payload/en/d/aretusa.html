Greek nymph linked to the Fountain of Arethusa in Syracuse

Arethusa

Arethusa is a nymph of Greek mythology whose name is borne by the famous spring on the island of Ortygia, in Syracuse, Sicily. Her story, of a nymph pursued by the river god Alpheus until their waters finally merge, is one of the best-known metamorphosis tales in the classical tradition.

The myth

According to the myth, Arethusa was a nymph who lived in Arcadia. One day she was bathing in a clear stream, unaware that the river god Alpheus, who flowed from Arcadia through Elis to the sea, was watching her. He fell in love with her, but Arethusa, who had vowed to remain a chaste companion of Artemis, fled. After a long chase, she asked the goddess for protection, and Artemis hid her in a cloud. Overcome with fear, she sweated profusely until she turned into a stream. Artemis then broke open the ground so she could escape again, and the stream travelled underground and beneath the sea to the island of Ortygia, where it emerged as a fountain. Alpheus followed her across the sea and mingled his waters with hers.

In another episode, Arethusa spoke to Demeter during her search for Persephone. She told the goddess that, travelling beneath the earth in her stream, she had seen Demeter's daughter become queen of the Underworld, and this led Demeter to lift the punishment she had placed on Sicily.

Ancient sources also offer a different version. In it, Arethusa lay with Poseidon in the Boeotian town of Euripus, and Hera turned her into a spring in Chalcis. Before that, she had given birth to Abas, leader of the Abantes of Euboea. Hyginus accepts this parentage but also describes her as one of the Nereids, and the Catalogue of Women names her as a daughter of Hyperenor, son of Poseidon and Alcyone. The genealogies therefore vary between authors.

Worship and religion

In Syracuse, the nymph was revered by the city's inhabitants as a protective figure. She was depicted on coins surrounded by dolphins, and the spring on Ortygia became one of the emblems of the city. Pastoral poetry was also dedicated to her.

Legacy

The myth inspired poets and artists during the Renaissance and Romanticism, among them Percy Bysshe Shelley and John Keats. The Syracusan coins showing her head surrounded by dolphins are counted among the most famous and beautiful coins of ancient Greece. Karol Szymanowski titled the first of his three violin and piano pieces Myths as "The Fountain of Arethusa", and Ottorino Respighi composed a tone poem called "Aretusa".

Ancient sources: Hyginus and the Catalogue of Women name her as the daughter of Hyperenor, and Ovid calls her "Alpheias" because of the underground link between her spring and the river Alpheus.
