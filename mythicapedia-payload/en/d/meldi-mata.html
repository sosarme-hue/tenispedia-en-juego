Hindu goddess protecting the fields of Gujarat

Meldi Mata

Meldi Mata is a Hindu goddess who protects farmland and serves as a household deity, most revered in the western state of Gujarat. She is especially cherished by Hindu farmers and by the Chunvalia Koli community.

The myth

Tradition holds that Meldi Mata can grant any wish. One origin story links her to the goddess Parvati, who gave her a goat as her mount and bestowed her powers. She is depicted with eight arms holding a trident, sword, discus and mace.

She is believed to protect people from spirits and other dangers.

Worship and religion

Worship varies between temples and communities, and includes arti, bhajans, garbas, prasad offerings and havana rites. At the Jasalpur temple, annual festivals feature garba singing and dancing, and a procession in which women carry lamp-lit clay pots to the shrine. Devotees pray for fertility and prosperity and give thanks with offerings or pilgrimages.

Legacy

She appears in devotional images of the Mata ni Pachedi textile tradition and in popular lithographs, where she is shown with many arms.

Trivia

A distinctive mount. Meldi Mata stands apart from other Gujarati goddesses by her mount: Bahuchar Mata rides a rooster and Khodiyar Mata a crocodile.
Procession of lamps. At the Jasalpur festivals, women walk toward the shrine carrying clay pots holding lamps.
