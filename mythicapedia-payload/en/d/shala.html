Mesopotamian goddess of grain and fertility

Shala

Shala is a Mesopotamian goddess linked to fertility, grain and agriculture, and the wife of the weather god Adad. First attested in the Old Babylonian period, she is also associated with the constellation Virgo.

The myth

Her genealogy is unknown. She almost always appears beside Adad, and texts call her his "great wife" or "beloved wife who gladdens the heart." In a balag song from the library of Ashurbanipal, she describes herself as the righteous wife of the god.

She and Adad had several daughters, among them Shubanuna, Namashmash and Minunesi, and some incantations make her the mother of the fire god Gibil. She is typically shown holding an ear of barley or a grain winnowing fan, beside a recumbent ram.

Worship and religion

In 1763 BCE Hammurabi built a storehouse, the bit shu-tum, for her and commissioned images of her. She was especially worshipped in Karkar and Zabban, cult centres of her husband as well.

Legacy

In Mesopotamian astronomy, the main star of the constellation The Furrow of Sowing was Spica, called "the barley ear of Shala." Today she is sometimes confused with Shalash, a Syrian goddess and wife of Dagan.

Ancient sources: The astronomical catalogue MUL.APIN, the god list An = Anum and a balag song from the library of Ashurbanipal.

Trivia

Ear in the sky. Mesopotamian astronomers called Spica "the barley ear of Shala," linking the goddess to the sky of the sowing season.
Many names. The god list An = Anum gives her several alternative names, including Medimsha and Shuzabarku, meaning roughly "she of lovely limbs" and "she with a shining bronze hand."
