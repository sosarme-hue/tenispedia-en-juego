son of Poseidon and Cleito in Plato's Atlantis

Autochthon

Autochthon is one of the ten sons of Poseidon and Cleito who, in Plato's account of Atlantis, ruled a portion of the island. His name means «sprung from the land itself», and he is one of the few figures in that story to have a personal name.

The myth

In Plato's Critias, Poseidon fathered five pairs of twin boys with Cleito. Autochthon was the younger twin of the third pair, alongside his brother Mneseus, and all of them were counted among the Atlantean princes. After Poseidon divided the island into ten parts, each brother received the rule of one of them.

Although each ruled his own territory, all were subject to the supreme dynasty of Atlas, the eldest of the ten. Tradition links Autochthon's name to his grandfather Evenor, regarded as one of the original inhabitants of the land, which explains the meaning of his name.

Legacy

Autochthon has little presence outside Plato's account. A map in an atlas by Vaugondray proposed identifying Atlantis with South America and assigned Autochthon the territories of Brazil, a cartographic hypothesis showing how the myth was reinterpreted for geographical purposes.

Ancient sources: Plato, in the Critias, where he appears as one of the ten sons of Poseidon who ruled Atlantis.

Trivia

Five pairs of twins. In the Critias, the offspring of Poseidon and Cleito include five pairs of male twins, an unusual detail even among mythological tales.
Atlantis in Brazil. A Vaugondray atlas proposed placing Atlantis in South America and gave Autochthon the territory of Brazil.
