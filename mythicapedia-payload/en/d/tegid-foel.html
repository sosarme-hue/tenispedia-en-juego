husband of Ceridwen in Welsh mythology

Tegid Foel

Tegid Foel is the husband of Ceridwen in Welsh mythology, a figure of the Celtic tradition of Wales. He is linked to Llyn Tegid, in Gwynedd, and may have been the guardian deity of that lake.

The myth

According to Welsh tradition, Tegid lived by Llyn Tegid, in the region of Penllyn, with his wife Ceridwen, a sorceress. Together they had a beautiful daughter, Creirwy, and a son, Morfran, called Afagddu, "utter darkness", because of his dark skin and hideous looks.

He is best known from the story of Taliesin's birth, first written down in the sixteenth century, though it dates from much earlier. His name also appears in several Welsh texts, including the Mabinogion tales Culhwch and Olwen and The Dream of Rhonabwy, and in Welsh Triads 24 and 41. The Welsh genealogies give other children, such as Dwywai, and one version links Afan Buellt to his line.

Ancient sources: His name appears in the Mabinogion tales and the Welsh Triads, and medieval Welsh genealogies record his children.
