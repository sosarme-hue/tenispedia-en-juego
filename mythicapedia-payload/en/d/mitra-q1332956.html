Vedic god of friendship, covenants and the dawn sun

Mitra

Mitra is a Hindu god and one of the Adityas, the sons of the goddess Aditi. He is known as the guardian of treaties, oaths and friendship, and he is usually invoked together with his brother Varuna.

The myth

According to Vedic accounts, Mitra is a son of Aditi and Kashyapa and one of the Adityas, whose number varies between sources: some name seven or eight brothers, others as many as thirty-one. In the Rigveda he appears mostly in the pair Mitra-Varuna, to whom cosmic order (ṛta), the keeping of covenants and the punishment of falsehood are attributed. Mitra is linked to the light of dawn and the gentle morning sun, while Varuna is associated with evening and night. In later texts his figure fades, and he becomes above all the patron of friendship.

In the Puranas he appears in minor episodes: it is told that, together with Varuna, he became enamoured of the nymph Urvashi, and from that confusion the seers Vashishtha and Agastya were born. According to the Bhagavata Purana, his wife is Revati and they have three sons. He also appears in the legend of Prithvi, where he milks the earth goddess in the form of a cow.

Worship and religion

In the Vedic period Mitra received hymns and sacrificial invocations, and was linked to contracts. Today his worship is minor: Hindus address him in the sunrise prayers, and he is honoured at the Mitrotsavam festival of the sun god Surya. In Bengal a month-long observance in his honour takes place in Agrahayan, with fasting and offerings.

Legacy

His name gave rise to Sanskrit mitra, 'friend', which is now common in many Indian languages. Many scholars relate the root of his name to the sense of 'pact' or 'contract'.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda is where he appears most often, alongside Varuna; the Atharvaveda, the Brahmanas and the Mahabharata also mention him.
