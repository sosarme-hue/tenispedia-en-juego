Hindu goddess, mother of Parashurama

Renuka

Renuka is a goddess of Hindu mythology, wife of the sage Jamadagni and mother of Parashurama, the sixth avatar of Vishnu. Today she is venerated above all as Yellamma Devi in southern and western India, where she is one of the most beloved popular deities.

The myth

According to the tale, Renuka lived with her husband Jamadagni in an ashram beside the Malaprabha River. One day, while fetching water, she saw the gandharva king Chitraratha with his wives and lost her concentration. The power her chastity gave her faded, and the water she had gathered was lost. Jamadagni knew what had happened through his yogic insight and cursed her. He ordered his sons to kill her, but four refused and were reduced to ashes. He then asked his youngest son, Parashurama, to behead his mother, and Parashurama obeyed with his axe.

Pleased by his son's obedience, Jamadagni granted him a boon, and Parashurama asked that his mother and brothers be brought back to life. This was done. Later, King Kartavirya killed Jamadagni to seize the celestial cow, and Renuka endured four days of widowhood before her husband was restored. Some South Indian versions tell of a low-caste woman who tried to save her and was beheaded alongside her; when both were revived, they became goddesses, possibly the origin of Yellamma and Mariamman.

Worship and religion

Her temple on Yellammagudda hill near Savadatti is a major pilgrimage site. Devotees bathe in ponds whose water is considered healing and offer camphor, salt, oil, coconuts and bananas. Every Thursday and Friday evening, her image is carried around the temple in a silver palanquin. Some women still perform the ritual of begging for rice from house to house, and the transgender community regards her as its protector. Her worship remains very much alive in Karnataka and other states.

Legacy

In Sanskrit, Renuka means 'fine grains of sand'. The poet Ramdhari Singh, known as Dinkar, wrote a poem titled Renuka. A pond called Ramsarover, in local tradition, is now venerated as Renuka Sanctuary.

Ancient sources: Her legend is recorded in the Mahabharata, the Harivamsa and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A name made of sand. In Sanskrit, Renuka means 'fine grains of sand', a simple name for a goddess who is so widely worshipped.
A temple with a Jain past. The Yellamma temple is believed to have originally been a Jain shrine, which became Hindu around 1250.
