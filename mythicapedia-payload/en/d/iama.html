Hindu god of death and justice

Yama

Yama is the Hindu god of death, lord of the departed and judge of souls in the underworld. Rooted in the Vedas, his figure later became the moral judge who weighs the deeds of each life.

The myth

According to Vedic tradition, Yama was the first mortal to die and, having reached the heavens before anyone else, found the path that others would follow. For this reason he became ruler of the dead and guide of the ancestors, the pitris. He was the son of the sun god Surya (Vivasvat in earlier texts) and Saranyu, and he had a twin sister, Yami. Some hymns say she proposed that they have children together; he refused, and the episode is remembered as the first incest. After his death, Yami mourned so deeply that the gods created the night to ease her grief.

The Vedas describe him as the gatherer of people who leads them with kindness. His path is guarded by two four-eyed dogs, sons of Sarama, who keep the living from entering his realm. In the Puranic period his image grows sterner: he appears as Yamaraja, king of prohibition, and as Dharmaraja, king of duty. He dwells in Yamapura, the forbidden city, where the scribe Chitra Gupta records each soul's actions, and Yama delivers a final, unappealable verdict that sends the soul to heaven, hell or rebirth. He is shown with green skin, red garments, riding a buffalo and carrying a noose and a mace.

Worship and religion

In Vedic rituals Yama received offerings of soma and ghee, and he was invoked to grant long life and to guide the sacrificers. Several temples across India are dedicated to him. He is also venerated in Tibetan Buddhism as a guardian of spiritual practice.

Legacy

His figure spread into Buddhism, Chinese religion and Japanese mythology, where he appears as Enma, king of hell. In modern India he has served as the face of road safety campaigns.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Rigveda, the Atharvaveda and the White Yajurveda; also in the Katha Upanishad, the Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

A name with two meanings. In Sanskrit, yama means 'twin' but also 'prohibition' and 'restraint', which explains his later role as king of prohibition.
The first incest. According to the Rigveda, his twin sister proposed having children together, but he refused.
