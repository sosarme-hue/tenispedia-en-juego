Slavic god of uncertain functions

Khors

Khors is a Slavic god whose functions remain unclear, mentioned in sources since the 12th century. He is usually interpreted as a sun god, though he is sometimes linked to the moon, and he matters mainly because so much about early Slavic religion remains unknown.

The myth

References to Khors are scarce and do not describe his deeds, family or stories in any detail. For that reason, there is no true myth of Khors in the strict sense. What survives are brief mentions that present him as a divine figure in the Slavic world, without a narrative that would allow us to reconstruct his role in the lives of his worshippers.

His name has also been the subject of long debate. For a long time, scholars proposed an Iranian origin, linking it to the Persian xoršid, meaning sun, or to the Ossetian xor. Modern linguists strongly reject this etymology, and other explanations rooted in Slavic languages have been proposed and are still debated.

The figure of Khors therefore remains one whose exact function can be guessed at but not known. The solar interpretation is the most widespread, while the lunar one is a minority alternative that has never replaced it.

Ancient sources: Khors is mentioned in medieval Slavic texts from the 12th century onward, although the sources offer no detailed description of his figure.

Trivia

A disputed name. The Iranian etymology of the name, which links it to the Persian word for sun, is rejected today by linguists.
