Greek hero, founder of Oaxus and son of Apollo

Oaxes

In Greek myth, Oaxes is the founder of the Cretan town of Oaxus, a place known to writers such as Herodotus and Servius. He is said to be a son of the god Apollo.

The myth

According to tradition, Oaxes was a son of Apollo, though the sources disagree about his mother: some name the Cretan nymph Anchiale, others Acacallis, daughter of King Minos.

Tradition links him to the founding of the town of Oaxus on Crete and to the river Oaxes, which some authors credited with giving the town its name. Apollonius of Rhodes wrote in the Argonautica that Crete was the Oaxian land, while Vibius Sequester recorded that the river gave its name to the city of Oaxia.

Ancient sources: Apollonius of Rhodes, in the Argonautica, calls Crete the Oaxian land; Herodotus and Servius knew the town of Oaxus.
