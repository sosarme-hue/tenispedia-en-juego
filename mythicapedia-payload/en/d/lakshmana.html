younger brother of Rama in Hindu tradition

Lakshmana

Lakshmana is the younger brother and inseparable companion of Rama in the Ramayana, the Sanskrit epic of Hindu tradition. He is remembered for his absolute devotion to his brother, and Puranic texts regard him as an incarnation of the serpent Shesha.

The myth

According to the Ramayana attributed to Valmiki, Lakshmana was born in Ayodhya to King Dasharatha and Sumitra. He was bound to Rama from childhood and is portrayed as his loyal shadow. Puranic texts describe him as an incarnation of Ananta Shesha, the thousand-headed serpent on which Vishnu rests, and say he accompanied Vishnu through his incarnations: in the treta yuga as Lakshmana and in the dvapara yuga as Balarama, Krishna's elder brother.

He accompanied Rama and Sita during their fourteen-year exile, built them a hut and kept watch over them. Sita asked Rama to hunt a golden deer, which was really the demon Maricha. When a voice imitating Rama's cry for help was heard, Sita insisted that Lakshmana go to his aid. Before leaving, Lakshmana drew a protective line with his bow, the lakshmana rekha, which Sita must not cross. She crossed it to give alms to a beggar who was really Ravana, and was abducted.

In the war in Lanka, Lakshmana killed Indrajit, son of Ravana, and many demons. Wounded by a mystic weapon of Indrajit, he was revived with the sanjivani herb, which Hanuman sought in the Himalayas. Unable to recognise it, the monkey carried the whole mountain to Lanka. After the war Lakshmana declined to be crown prince, since Bharata was the elder. Later, keeping a promise, he interrupted Rama's meeting with Yama to attend the sage Durvasa, and resolved to renounce the world on the banks of the Saryu river.

Worship and religion

Hindus worship him alongside Rama and Sita at various places in India, and his devotion as a brother remains a model for the faithful.

Legacy

The Malay word laksamana, the title for an admiral in the modern Malaysian and Indonesian navies, derives from his name through the Hikayat Seri Rama, which compares the admiral's loyalty to that of Lakshmana towards his lord. His figure as the loyal brother remains present in popular versions of the Ramayana across South Asia.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Ramayana attributed to Valmiki and in Puranic texts such as the Bhagavata Purana, which present him as an incarnation of Shesha.

Trivia

Lakshmana's limit. The line he drew around the hut, the lakshmana rekha, has given its name to a common expression in South Asia for a boundary one should not cross.
A wife who sleeps for him. According to one tradition, his wife Urmila agreed to sleep through the fourteen years of exile so that Lakshmana could stay awake and keep watch, a sacrifice known as Urmila Nidra.
