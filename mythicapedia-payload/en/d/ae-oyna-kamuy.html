Ainu god and culture hero

Ae-oyna-kamuy

Ae-oyna-kamuy, also known as Ainurakkur or Okikurmi, is a kamuy (god) and culture hero in Ainu mythology. He is credited with teaching humans domestic skills and community life, which is why he is called the father of humanity.

The myth

According to Ainu tradition, the hero was born to Kannakamuy, a thunder deity, and Princess Chikisani, spirit of the Japanese elm. Other versions name the sun or thunder as his father, and some place his origin on Mount Oputateshike. As a child he taught women to weave and men to carve, and taught his people fishing, hunting, gathering, house building, medicine and religious ritual.

On his sixteenth birthday, the god Tokapchkamuy charged him with protecting humankind and marrying the swan princess Retacchir. After defeating a giant deer, he saw the princess kidnapped by the sorceress Uesoyoma and carried to the Underworld, and his sight was taken by her magic. He descended to rescue her, struck down the king of the Underworld and his followers with a heavenly sword, and set the realm burning for twelve days. Some tellings say he later returned to the sky, where the gods sent him back to earth because he smelled of humans, leaving his old sandals behind, which became the first squirrels.

Trivia

Sandals turned into squirrels. According to one tellings, when he went back to the sky he left his old sandals on earth, and they became the first squirrels.
A name meaning human scent. Ainurakkur roughly means 'he who smells of humans', which suggests he was half god and half human.
