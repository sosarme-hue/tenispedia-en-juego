bodhisattva guardian of Buddhist monasteries

Weituo

Weituo, also known as Skanda or Idaten, is a Mahayana bodhisattva and devoted protector of monasteries and of the Buddha's teachings. Depicted as a young Chinese general in armor, he is a familiar figure in temples across China, Japan, Korea and Southeast Asia.

The myth

According to tradition, Skanda was the son of a virtuous king, and when the Buddha entered nirvana he was charged with guarding the Dharma. The Sutra of Golden Light tells how a demon stole the Buddha's ashes and how Skanda chased him to the summit of Mount Meru to recover the relics, a feat that gave him a reputation for extraordinary speed.

Chinese accounts also present him as a general who loved the princess Miao Shan, the future Guanyin. After both died, he became a bodhisattva to protect her. Some identify him with the Hindu god of war Kartikeya.

Worship and religion

In Chinese temples he usually stands facing the Buddha statue in the main shrine, often unarmed and with hands in anjali. He is honoured as a protector of monasteries and the monastic community, and as a guardian against fire and theft. His birthday falls on the third day of the sixth lunar month.

Legacy

His image as an armoured general is one of the most common sights in Buddhist temples in China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Malaysia.

Ancient sources: The Sutra of Golden Light and the Ming-era Xiangshan Baojuan preserve his principal stories.

Trivia

Lunar birthday. His birthday falls on the third day of the sixth month of the lunar calendar.
Pose in the temple. In many Chinese shrines he appears unarmed, with hands joined, facing the Buddha statue.
