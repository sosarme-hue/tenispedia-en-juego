sea god of Hong Kong and Macau

Tam Kung

Tam Kung is a sea deity of Chinese origin worshipped in Hong Kong and Macau. Believed able to forecast the weather and to heal, he is especially venerated by fishing communities and by emigrants from southern China.

The myth

In Chinese folk legend, Tam Kung was one of the gods who could foretell the weather. He was born in Huizhou Prefecture, and as a child he was said to be able to cure the sick. At the age of twenty he became an immortal in heaven, from Nine-Dragon Mountain in Huizhou.

He was formally deified during the Qing dynasty. His most devoted worshippers are people whose ancestral homes lie in Huizhou or Chaoshan, in Guangdong province.

Worship and religion

Fishermen and residents of Hong Kong built temples in his honour, such as the one at Shau Kei Wan, rebuilt many times since its origins as a small shrine and managed by the Chinese Temples Committee since 1928. His birthday, the eighth day of the fourth lunar month, is marked with kung fu shows, parades and dragon dances. Temples also stand in Happy Valley, on Ping Chau island, in Coloane (Macau), in Victoria (Canada) and in Sandakan (Malaysia), the last several founded largely by Hakka immigrants.
