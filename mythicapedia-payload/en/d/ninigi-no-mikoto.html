Japanese god, grandson of Amaterasu

Ninigi no Mikoto

Ninigi no Mikoto is a god in Japanese mythology, grandson of the sun goddess Amaterasu. According to tradition, he is the great-grandfather of Japan's first emperor, Jimmu, so his line founded the Japanese imperial family.

The myth

According to the myths, Amaterasu sent him to rule the earth with three sacred treasures: the sword Kusanagi, the mirror Yata no kagami, and the jewel Yasakani no magatama. Other gods accompanied him on his descent. Sarutahiko blocked his path until Uzume persuaded him to let Ninigi pass. He came down on Mount Takachiho in the Kirishima range of Kyushu.

In one story about finding a wife, the mountain god Ōyamatsumi offered him two daughters, Konohanasakuya and her elder sister Iwanagahime. Ninigi chose the younger and rejected Iwanagahime because of her appearance. As a result, the lives of the rulers became as brief as blossoms. Konohanasakuya became pregnant after a single night, and Ninigi doubted her faithfulness. She proved her innocence by giving birth amid a fire, and her sons Hoderi, Hosuseri and Hoori emerged unharmed. Ninigi died and was buried at Ena no Goryō.

Worship and religion

He has few temples dedicated to him, though shrines such as Amatsu and Kirishima-jingū are consecrated to him. At Ise he is venerated alongside Kuni no Tokotachi. According to the Imperial Household Agency, his official burial site is Nitta Shrine in Satsumasendai, Kagoshima.

Legacy

The three sacred treasures he is said to have brought are regarded as the foundation of the legitimacy of the Japanese imperial house. His name is tentatively interpreted as "beloved jeweled mallet."

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki recount his descent to earth and his main episodes.

Trivia

A rejected daughter. Iwanagahime, the daughter Ninigi rejected, was later venerated as a protective deity of longevity.
An official tomb. The Imperial Household Agency designates Nitta Shrine as his tomb, and the shrine came under its direct administration in 1914.
