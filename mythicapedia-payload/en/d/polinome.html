Nereid of the sea caverns in Greek mythology

Polynome

Polynome is one of the Nereids, sea nymphs and daughters of the old sea god Nereus and the Oceanid Doris, associated with undersea caverns. She belongs to Greek mythology.

The myth

In Greek tradition, Polynome was one of the fifty Nereids, the sea nymphs who made up the retinue of her father Nereus and her mother Doris. Her name, in Ancient Greek Πολυνόμη, means roughly "the one with many pastures," a meaning often read as a link to the sea's abundance. Sources place her as a mistress of the sea caverns, a realm of shadow and echo where the Nereids were said to dwell.

Hesiod's Theogony lists the Nereids, Polynome among them, but the surviving stories give her little individual prominence. Her role amounts to belonging to the chorus of sea nymphs who attend the gods of the sea and those who travel upon it.

Ancient sources: Hesiod's Theogony includes her in its list of the Nereids.
