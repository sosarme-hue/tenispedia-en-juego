Supreme being of Yoruba religion

Olodumare

Olodumare is the supreme God and creator of the universe in Yoruba religion, a unique, omnipotent being with no father or mother. He matters because, according to tradition, he created the world but left the relationship with humans to the orishas.

The myth

According to Yoruba tradition, Olodumare created the universe but not life on Earth directly. He gave the necessary tools to Obatalá, chief of the orishas, and entrusted the other deities with creating the forces that sustain the world.

Yoruba religion regards him as the origin of virtue and mortality, and believes he grants each person knowledge at birth.

Worship and religion

The Yoruba do not worship him directly: there are no shrines, altars, images or sacrifices dedicated to him. He is invoked when the orishas cannot or will not help, and it is traditional to entrust one's life to his care each night.

Legacy

His name gave rise to the Afro-Brazilian percussion group Olodum. He also appears in songs such as Paul Simon's The Rhythm of the Saints and in the video game Smite.
