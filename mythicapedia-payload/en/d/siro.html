son of Apollo in Greek mythology

Syrus

A hero of Greek mythology, son of Apollo and Sinope, to whom the Syrians trace their name. In another version he appears as a son of King Agenor of Tyre.

The myth

According to the most widespread version, Syrus was the son of Apollo and Sinope, daughter of the river Asopus and Metope. The Syrians were named after him, and tradition regards them as his descendants.

Another account makes him a son of King Agenor of Tyre and brother of Cadmus, Phoenix, Cilix and Europa. When Agenor was about to die, he divided his kingdom among three sons: Phoenix, Syros and Cilix. Syros received the land he named Syria, while his brothers got Phoenicia and Cilicia. He was described as a wise man who wrote arithmetic philosophy in Phoenician letters.

Trivia

A sage with Phoenician letters. Syrus is said to have written arithmetic philosophy using the Phoenician alphabet.
A dog with the same name. Syrus is also the name of one of the hunting dogs of Actaeon, a different figure.
