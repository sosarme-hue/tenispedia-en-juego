minor hero of Greek mythology, son of Ares

Calydon

Calydon is a minor figure of Greek mythology, the son of the god Ares and the mortal Astynome. His story explains how a mountain by the Achelous river came to bear his name, in the region of Calydon in Aetolia.

The myth

According to tradition, Calydon was the son of Ares, god of war, and a mortal woman named Astynome. He came from Calydon, an ancient city in Aetolia, in western Greece, and his name reflects that origin.

The best-known tale says that one day Calydon accidentally came upon the virgin goddess Artemis while she was bathing naked. As punishment, she turned him into rock. The mountain Gyrus, beside the Achelous river, then took his name and has been called Calydon ever since.

Some modern scholars read the scene as a sexual intrusion, and point out that blinding was a common punishment for sexual crimes in Greek myth, so turning him to stone fits that logic.

Ancient sources: Pseudo-Plutarch, in his Treatise on Rivers and Mountains (De fluviis), a second-century work not written by the real Plutarch.

Trivia

An author who was not Plutarch. The story survives only in a treatise attributed to Plutarch, but it is now known to have been written by another author in the second century. It is classed as paradoxography, or perhaps a parody of it, and may have been meant humorously.
