giant-headed Japanese yōkai

Ōkubi

The ōkubi is a Japanese yōkai in the form of a huge head, usually female, with teeth blackened like those of a married woman. It is one of the monsters of Japanese folklore linked to misfortune.

The myth

According to tradition, it may be a vengeful human spirit or an obsession turned into a yōkai. Some believe it was invented to satirize monks who broke their vows.

In the Tōtei Bukkairoku, a mid-Edo collection of monster stories, a man finds a huge old woman's face in his storeroom. He prods it with tongs without any response, and when he touches it, it feels sticky. It can also make people fall ill.

The haiku poet Hori Bakusui describes another ōkubi, about two metres tall, rising above a wall after the rain. Its breath turns the skin it touches yellow and swollen, and the doctor prescribes a medicated bath.

Legacy

Natsuhiko Kyōgoku includes it in the story «Ōkubi», from the collection Hyakkiyakō-yō, as a vision in a morgue. Shigeru Mizuki makes it a villain in GeGeGe no Kitarō, where it has a sunken eye and powers over bones and air.

Ancient sources: Tōtei Bukkairoku, a mid-Edo collection of monster stories, and the haiku of Hori Bakusui.

Trivia

A head that sickens. According to the tales, the ōkubi can make people ill when they come near. Its breath turns the skin it touches yellow and swollen.
A satire of monks. Some argue the ōkubi is not an ancient monster but was created to satirize monks who broke their precepts.
