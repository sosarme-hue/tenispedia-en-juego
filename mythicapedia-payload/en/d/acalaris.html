Acallaris

Acallaris is, in Greek mythology, the daughter of Eumedes and the wife of the Trojan king Tros. She matters chiefly for her place in the genealogy of the royal house of Troy, since through her son Assaracus the line leads down to Aeneas.

The myth

According to some accounts, Acallaris married Tros, king of Troy, and bore him a son, Assaracus, who also became king of the city. Not all writers agree: some give Callirrhoe, daughter of the river god Scamander, as Tros's wife, and others name further children of the king, among them Ilus, Ganymede, Cleopatra and Cleomestra.

Dionysius of Halicarnassus preserves a genealogy in which Assaracus, son of Tros and Acallaris, fathers Capys with Clytodora, daughter of Laomedon; Capys fathers Anchises with the naiad Hieromnemê, and Anchises fathers Aeneas with Aphrodite. In this way Acallaris stands among the roots of the line that leads to the hero regarded as the founder of Rome.

Ancient sources: Dionysius of Halicarnassus records her descendants in his writings on the origins of Rome.
