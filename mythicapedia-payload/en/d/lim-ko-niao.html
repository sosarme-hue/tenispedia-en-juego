Chinese goddess of filial piety in southern Thailand

Lim Ko Niao

Lim Ko Niao is a goddess worshipped by the Chinese community of southern Thailand, especially in Pattani. She is considered the sister of Lim Toh Khiam, and her cult is associated with filial piety and patriotism.

The myth

According to the Patani legend, Lim Ko Niao set out to find her brother when their mother fell ill, vowing not to return without him. She found him in Patani, married to the sultan's daughter, converted to Islam and building a mosque. He refused to come back and defeated her in combat. She hanged herself from a janggus tree and, before dying, cursed the mosque so it could never be completed.

Another version says she died after fighting alongside her brother against rebels.

Worship and religion

Her devotees carved an image from wood of the tree where she died. Today it stands in the Leng Chu Kiang temple in Pattani, decorated with pearl necklaces, with which she is associated. Each year, on the 15th day of the Chinese New Year, her image is carried in procession across the Pattani River, and the carriers walk over fire.

Legacy

Her temple is now a tourist destination for visitors from Malaysia and Singapore, and her worship has spread to neighbouring provinces such as Songkhla and Yala.

Trivia

A disputed tomb. Her gravestone is dated 1574, but it may have been made in the early 20th century.
A renamed temple. The original temple, dedicated to Master Qingshui, was renamed Leng Chu Kiang when Lim Ko Niao's image was moved there.
