lantern yōkai of Japanese folklore

Chōchin-obake

The chōchin-obake is a yōkai from Japanese folklore: a paper lantern that comes to life with one eye and a long tongue. It became a stock character among ghosts from the Edo period and earned a place in the illustrated demonology of 1784.

The myth

Hardly any oral legends about it exist. Tradition describes an old lantern that splits upward and downward; the separated part becomes a mouth with a long tongue, and it usually has a single eye in its upper half. Sometimes it also grows a face, hands, a torso or wings.

Its image comes mainly from prints. Toriyama Sekien included it in the Gazu Hyakki Yagyō, and Hokusai and Kuniyoshi also drew it. It is commonly believed to have been inspired by a performance of Yotsuya kaidan (1825), in which the spirit of Oiwa appeared from a lantern. In a story from Yamagata Prefecture, an old object at a shrine frightened people until it was put away.

Legacy

It appears in toys, children's books and haunted house attractions from the Meiji and Taishō eras, and in card games still in use today. The manga artist Mizuki Shigeru wrote a story in which it sucked out people's souls, though it cites no primary sources.

Ancient sources: Gazu Hyakki Yagyō (1784) by Toriyama Sekien, which lists it among the yōkai.

Trivia

No ancient precedents. No mentions have been found in works older than the Edo period, so its appearance is relatively late.
A theatre ghost. It is thought that its image grew out of a scene in Yotsuya kaidan in which the spirit appeared from a lantern.
