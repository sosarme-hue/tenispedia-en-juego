Celtic goddess of the home and prosperity

Nantosuelta

Nantosuelta was a goddess of Gaul linked to the home, prosperity and, in some readings, the realm of the dead. She is most often shown as the companion of the hammer-god Sucellus, and her distinctive emblem is a house-shaped object mounted on a pole.

The myth

No classical author names her, so her figure is known only from inscriptions and Gallo-Roman reliefs, concentrated among the Mediomatrici of eastern Gaul and their neighbours. Her name, a Celtic compound, is usually rendered as 'valley' or 'winding river', though scholars disagree on its exact meaning.

The best-known relief, from Sarrebourg, shows her holding a long pole topped by a house beside Sucellus, who carries a pot and a mallet. A raven appears below them. Later interpreters have seen in the house a beehive or a funerary urn, which would point to a role in the afterlife.

Worship and religion

She was worshipped alongside Sucellus as a divine couple with native names, and occasionally appears alone, suggesting an independent status. No temples or festivals linked to her are known.

Legacy

Scholars such as Jan de Vries and Anne Ross have reinterpreted her, the latter comparing her with Irish war-goddesses such as the Morrígan. She has no documented presence in later culture.

Ancient sources: She is not mentioned in classical literature; she is attested only in Gallo-Roman inscriptions, such as the altar dedication from Sarrebourg.

Trivia

A house on a pole. Her emblem, a house or shrine atop a long pole, is her most distinctive symbol and appears only in a specific area of Gaul.
A raven companion. The raven accompanying the goddess is read as a symbol of war by some scholars and of the underworld by others.
