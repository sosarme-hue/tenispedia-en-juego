Sumerian god of wisdom and magic

Enki

Enki, known as Ea in Akkadian and Babylonian tradition, was the Mesopotamian god of wisdom, magic, crafts and fresh subterranean waters. Tradition credits him with creating humankind, and he ranked among the three great gods of Mesopotamia, alongside Anu and Enlil.

The myth

According to tradition, Enki was a son of Anu and a half-brother of Enlil. He gave humans the arts, crafts and farming techniques, and urged other gods to create them. In the Atrahasis epic, the great gods slaughter a lesser god and mix his flesh and blood with clay to shape people. Depending on the version, both the sacrificed god and the mother goddess who helps shape humans change. Enki then teaches humans purification rituals and, together with the mother goddess, fashions fourteen lumps of clay into seven men and seven women.

Annoyed by the noise of humans, Enlil tries to wipe them out three times. In the last attempt he unleashes a flood, but Enki advises the hero, called Atrahasis or Ziusudra depending on the account, to build a huge boat and load it with seeds and animals. The other gods rebuke Enlil, since they depend on human offerings for food. When the waters recede, the hero makes a sacrifice that the starving gods eagerly receive. In the end, Enki asks the mother goddess to create new human beings.

Worship and religion

His main temple, the E-Abzu, stood in Eridu, which the earliest texts regard as his home. His cult soon spread beyond southern Mesopotamia. In the Ur III and Old Babylonian periods he was part of a triad with Anu and Enlil, and he was worshipped in almost all the important cities of Babylonia, as well as in Assyria and Anatolia, where he joined the Hittite pantheon. When Babylon and its god Marduk rose to power, his importance declined, though he remained a major deity. His symbols included the goat-fish and the ram-headed staff. His cult did not survive, as it disappeared together with Mesopotamian religion.

Legacy

His symbol, the goat-fish, later merged into a single creature, the sea-goat, which is why he is linked to the constellations Capricorn and Aquarius. He is also associated with the planet Mercury. In art he appears as a male figure pouring water or holding a vessel from which it flows, an image that expresses his dominion over the waters.

Ancient sources: The Atrahasis epic, the myth of Enki and Ninmah and the Babylonian Epic of Creation place him at the centre of the creation of humankind and of his conflicts with Enlil.

Trivia

Two different Enkis. Sources distinguish between Enki of Eridu and a primordial god with a similar name, written differently in the Emesal dialect. Some scholars think Enki was originally an epithet that eventually replaced the god's original name.
Nudimmud, the creator. One of his most common epithets is Nudimmud, linked to creation and the shaping of clay figures. It appears mainly in literary texts and, less often, in prayers and royal inscriptions.
