queen of Lanka, wife of Ravana

Mandodari

Mandodari is the wife of Ravana, king of Lanka, in the Hindu epic Ramayana. She is portrayed as beautiful, pious and righteous, and her loyal love for her husband sits alongside her repeated attempts to steer him toward a better path.

The myth

According to the Uttara Kanda of the Ramayana, Mayasura, king of the asuras, received the apsara Hema from the gods, and together they had two sons, Mayavi and Dundubhi, and a daughter, Mandodari. Other versions tell a very different origin: the apsara Madhura, who had made love with Shiva while Parvati was away, was turned into a frog for twelve years and, once restored to human form, was adopted by Mayasura and Hema.

Ravana fell in love with her at Mayasura's house, and they married with Vedic rites. Mandodari advised him in vain: she urged him not to subdue the navagraha and not to seduce Vedavati, who would be reborn as Sita and bring about his downfall. When Hanuman reached Lanka, Ravana threatened to behead Sita, and Mandodari caught his hand, arguing that killing a woman is a grave sin. Before the final battle she tried once more to dissuade him, and although she told her son Indrajit not to fight Rama, she stood by her husband to the end. Some versions add that it was she who advised Vibhishana to join Rama.

Worship and religion

Her name is part of the recitation of the Panchakanya, five women whose remembrance is believed to dispel sin. The sources do not describe a cult devoted to her.

Ancient sources: Valmiki's Ramayana and its Uttara Kanda, which recount her origins and her role at the court of Lanka.

Trivia

Origin with a frog. Several traditions explain her name, meaning "soft-bellied", through a story in which a frog plays a central role in her birth.
A temple to Ravana. Mandore, a town about 9 km north of Jodhpur, is believed to be her birthplace, and it has a temple dedicated to Ravana.
