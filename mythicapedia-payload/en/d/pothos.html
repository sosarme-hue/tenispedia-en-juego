Greek personification of amorous longing

Pothos

Pothos is, in Greek mythology, the personification of amorous longing and one of the Erotes, the retinue of Aphrodite. His name means yearning or lovesick nostalgia.

The myth

According to tradition, Pothos was a son of Aphrodite, although another version has him born from the allegorical union of Zephyrus and Iris. As one of the Erotes he accompanied the goddess, alongside Persuasion, Harmonia and Charis.

He was depicted beside Eros and Himeros as a winged youth, the gods of love.

Worship and religion

He had a place in the temple of Aphrodite at Megara, which housed a sculpture by Scopas. Pliny the Elder reports that on Samothrace he was worshipped with very solemn rites. There is no evidence about whether his cult survived.

Legacy

In Neoplatonism, Plotinus and Proclus turned the term into a spiritual force: the soul's longing to return to its origin. Jean-Pierre Vernant studied it as a desire for what is absent, a desire that is also linked to mourning.

Ancient sources: Pliny the Elder mentions him in connection with the sculptures of Pasiteles, and Roman poets rendered his name as Ardor or Desiderium.

Trivia

Worship on Samothrace. Pliny writes that on the island of Samothrace Pothos was worshipped with especially solemn rites.
Longing for the dead. In Greek, having pothos for the dead meant remembering them constantly and being enthralled by their memory.
