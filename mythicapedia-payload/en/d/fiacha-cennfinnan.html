legendary Fir Bolg high king of Ireland

Fiacha Cennfinnán

Fiacha Cennfinnán is a legendary king from Irish mythology, a member of the Fir Bolg lineage, who according to tradition became High King of Ireland. His name, meaning 'Fiacha of the little white head', places him among the rulers of Ireland's earliest times.

The myth

According to the stories, Fiacha was the son of Starn, who was the son of Rudraige, and he belonged to the Fir Bolg people, whom tradition presents as one of the first groups to dominate Ireland. He overthrew his great-uncle Sengann and became High King, a position he held for five years.

His reign ended when Rinnal, son of Genann, drove him from the throne. Some sources link the Milesian king Fíachu Findoilches to the same name, showing how different tales overlapped in Irish tradition and how the names of its kings could repeat.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the Annals of the Four Masters and Seathrún Céitinn's Foras Feasa ar Érinn, the last written in the seventeenth century.

Trivia

Meaning of the name. The name Fiacha Cennfinnán translates as 'Fiacha of the little white head', an epithet that describes the figure.
