eponymous hero of the Eleans in Greek myth

Eleus

Eleus is a figure of Greek myth, regarded as the eponymous ancestor of the Eleans, the people who gave their name to the region of Elis. His story explains why the inhabitants of the area came to be called Eleans rather than Epeans.

The myth

According to Pausanias, the land near the river Achelous took its name from Aetolus, son of Endymion, who fled to that part of the mainland. Later, Eleus, son of Eurycyda (also a daughter of Endymion) and of Poseidon, gained power over the Epeans, and the inhabitants came to be called Eleans instead of Epeans. Pausanias adds with some skepticism that the divine paternity is something each reader may or may not believe.

Augeas is said to have been Eleus's son, though his wife is not named. Those who glorify the story change the account and make Augeas a son of Helios. Conon calls the hero Elis and the mother Eurypile, differences that are merely spelling variants. The ethnonyms Eleans and Epeans are often confused in mythography, since each had an eponymous hero, Eleus and Epeus respectively.

Ancient sources: Pausanias and Conon mention him, with differing names for his parents.

Trivia

Two names for the mother. Pausanias calls her Eurycyda, while Conon names her Eurypile. The two forms are treated as spelling variants of the same figure.
A hero and a people sharing a name. The ethnonyms Elean and Epean are often confused, because each had its own eponymous hero, Eleus and Epeus.
