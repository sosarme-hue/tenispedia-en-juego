supreme celestial weapon of Hinduism

Brahmastra

The brahmastra is the supreme celestial weapon of Hinduism, created and owned by the god Brahma. Its more powerful variant, the brahmashirastra, is considered the most destructive weapon in the whole tradition.

The myth

According to Sanskrit texts, only a handful of gods and warriors knew how to invoke it, among them Rama, Arjuna, Karna and Drona. When it is unleashed, all of nature trembles, the sky fills with fire and smoke, rivers evaporate and entire armies fall in a single blow. Unless the target has a counter-weapon, defeat is certain.

The brahmashirastra, with four heads of Brahma, is even more devastating: it is said to dry up rivers, stop grass from ever growing again and withhold rain for immeasurable ages.

In the Mahabharata, Ashvatthama hurls the weapon at Arjuna, who answers with his own; Narada and Vyasa make them withdraw. Ashvatthama, not knowing how to recall his, aims it at the womb of Uttarā, but Krishna saves the unborn Parikshit and condemns Ashvatthama to wander until the end of time.

Ancient sources: Sanskrit texts, including the Mahabharata and the Ramayana, where the brahmastra is used or its use is threatened.

Trivia

A weapon no one knew how to recall. Ashvatthama launched the brahmastra without knowing the full teaching needed to recall it, which led to the tragedy in the tale.
A rod that swallows it. According to the tale, Vasishtha's Brahma Danda swallowed the brahmastra that Vishvamitra hurled at him in a fit of anger.
