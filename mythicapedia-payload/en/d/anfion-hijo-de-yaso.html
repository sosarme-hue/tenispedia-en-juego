Amphion (son of Iasus)

Amphion, son of King Iasus, was an ancient ruler of the Minyans of Orchomenus in Boeotia. He matters because he appears in texts as old as those of Homer and Hesiod, though he remains a little-known figure.

The myth

According to tradition, Amphion was the son of King Iasus and grandson of Minyas. Some sources also connect him with Persephone, a descent that remains unclear.

He ruled the Minyans of Orchomenus in Boeotia. His two daughters, Chloris and Philomache, were joined to two prominent figures in the story: Chloris married Neleus and Philomache married Pelias.

This figure has an identity problem, since he is often confused with another Amphion, son of Zeus and Antiope, who is far better known. The Amphion of Orchomenus is older, and his presence in the tradition is modest.

Ancient sources: Homer and Hesiod mention him among the ancient figures of the Greek tradition.

Trivia

A different Amphion. He is easy to confuse with Amphion, son of Zeus and Antiope, whose story is much better known. They are two different characters.
Two daughters, two marriages. His daughters married into the story of two other figures: Chloris wed Neleus and Philomache wed Pelias, names that also appear in other tales.
