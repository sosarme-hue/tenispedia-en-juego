legendary Persian king of the Shahnameh epic

Kay Kavus

Kay Kavus is a legendary king of Iran's mythical history and a central figure in several episodes of the Shahnameh, the great epic by Ferdowsi. He matters because he embodies both the military champion and the guardian of Persian cultural continuity.

The myth

According to the tradition preserved in the Shahnameh, Kay Kavus is the son of Kay Qobad and the father of Prince Siyavash. He ruled Iran for 150 years and, for much of that time, was aided by the hero Rostam, although that help became increasingly grudging over the years. He was succeeded by his grandson Kay Khosrow.

The stories credit him with extravagant deeds. According to al-Masudi, he built a tower to challenge God and invaded Yemen, where he was held captive for four years until Rostam freed him. He returned to Persia with Soda, the daughter of the Yemeni king, who had fallen in love with him. The Shahnameh also tells how he commissioned a flying throne pulled by hungry eagles that carried him as far as China; there the birds tired, the throne came down, and Rostam had to rescue the king, who miraculously survived. According to the Bundahishn, he owned five mansions, one of gold, two of glass and two of steel, from whose springs flowed a water that restored youth.

Legacy

His figure lives on mainly through the Shahnameh, a work that the historian Dariush Zolfaghari reads as a defence of Iranian cultural identity. In that analysis, Kay Kavus stands both as a military champion and as a protector of Persian customs, heritage and political legitimacy.

Ancient sources: Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, which places him in Iran's mythical history, and the Bundahishn, which describes his mansions.

Trivia

The flying throne. The Shahnameh describes a wooden and gold throne with four long poles at its corners, tipped with meat that eagles chained to its feet strained to reach, lifting the structure into the air.
Five mansions. The Bundahishn credits him with five mansions of gold, glass and steel, whose springs gave waters that restored youth and held off death.
