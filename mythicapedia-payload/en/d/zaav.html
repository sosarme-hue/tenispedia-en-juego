mythical king of Persia, tenth shah of the Pishdadian dynasty

Zaav

Zaav is a mythical king of Persia, the tenth shah of the Pishdadian dynasty according to the Shahnameh. His reign is remembered as a time of justice, prosperity and rebuilding after the war against Afrasiab.

The myth

According to the Shahnameh, Zaav descended from Nowzar and ruled Iran for about five years, during the war with Afrasiab, when military abuses were rampant across the land. Facing widespread famine, Afrasiab wanted to end the conflict as soon as possible, and an agreement was reached: Turan would receive all the lands across the Oxus in exchange for peace.

The rest of his reign brought renewed prosperity. Al-Tabari says Zaav directed the rebuilding of key sites such as Khunarath and Babylon, repaired destroyed fortresses and cleared blocked channels to restore irrigation. He also granted a seven-year remission of the land tax. The 11th-century historian Gardizi adds that he dug a canal from Armenia toward the Tigris and created channels in the Sawad, the fertile plain of Iraq, which came to be called the Zab River. He died at the age of 86.

Ancient sources: Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, along with the chronicles of al-Tabari and the work of Gardizi, presents him as a king of the Persian tradition.

Trivia

The Zab River. According to Gardizi, the channels dug in the Sawad were called the Zab River, in reference to this king.
