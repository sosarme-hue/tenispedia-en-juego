Chinese goddess of dragons and motherhood

Longmu

Longmu is a goddess of Chinese mythology, revered as the mother of five dragons. Her story is a model of filial devotion and maternal love, two virtues highly valued in Chinese culture.

The myth

According to tradition, Longmu was once a woman named Wen Shi, born near the Xi River in Guangdong during the Qin dynasty. Her family was poor. One day she found a smooth white stone by the river and took it home, only to discover it was an egg from which five snakes hatched. Wen Shi shared her best food with them and fed them by hand.

The snakes grew into dragons, strong swimmers who helped her catch fish. Since dragons command water and weather, they summoned rain during a drought, and the grateful villagers gave her the title "Mother of Dragons."

When Emperor Qin Shihuang asked her to come to his capital, Xianyang, her dragon children resisted: they hid under her boat and dragged it backward so it could never pass Guilin. The officials eventually gave up. After Wen Shi died, the grieving dragons took human form as the "Five Scholars" and buried her north of Zhu Mountain.

Worship and religion

In the early Han dynasty the Xiaotong Temple was built in her honor, later known as the Longmu Ancestral Temple in Deqing County, Guangdong. It has been renovated thirteen times and remains very popular. Her festival falls in the first week of the fifth lunar month. She is a patron of parents and children and still a widely honored deity across China and in Hong Kong, where Lung Mo temples are dedicated to her.

Legacy

Her story of filial devotion and the dragons who protect her is a touchstone in Chinese culture on the bonds between mothers and children.

Trivia

An imperial temple. Emperor Hong Wu of the Ming dynasty wrote a calligraphy dedicated to the goddess for the Xiaotong Temple.
An egg in the river. According to the story, Wen Shi found the stone that turned out to be an egg while washing clothes and fishing in the Xi River.
