sun god of ancient Mesopotamia

Utu

Utu, known as Shamash in Akkadian, was the sun god of ancient Mesopotamia. Son of the moon god Sin and Ningal, he was believed to see everything happening in the world each day, which made him a guardian of justice and of travelers.

The myth

According to tradition, Utu crossed the sky from east to west each day and returned at night in the opposite direction through a nether sky above the underworld. The earliest sources, however, show him resting at night, and the night journey only appears later.

His parents were Sin and Ningal, and his twin sister was Inanna. He married Aya, the dawn goddess, and texts describe their daily reunions on a mountain where the sun went down. Their children included Kittum, the personification of truth, and the god Ishum. No myths focus on him, but he often appears as an ally of others: he protected Dumuzi when the galla demons tried to drag him to the underworld, helped Gilgamesh defeat the monster Humbaba, and supported Inanna in acquiring the temple Eanna.

Worship and religion

He was one of the principal gods of the pantheon and was especially venerated in Sippar and Larsa. He was often invoked outside temples as an astral body and appears frequently in prayers and incantations. He was also a god of divination, usually alongside the weather god Adad. Wilfred Lambert suggested that a tradition made him the supreme god, but it never won official support and remained mainly among the clergy of Sippar and, to a lesser degree, Larsa.

Legacy

His name lives on in Shamish, the Mandaean word for the sun, derived from Akkadian Shamash, and in personal names such as Samsu-iluna, meaning Samsu is our god. His name was also used to write the names of many foreign solar deities.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Epic of Gilgamesh, in Sumerian myths such as Inanna and An and How Grain Came to Sumer, and in god lists from Fara, An = Anum and Nippur.

Trivia

Four animals of light. His solar chariot was pulled by four animals whose Sumerian names mean the abundant light of heaven, the terrifying great light of heaven, the dreadful great light and the noble light of heaven. The sources do not agree on their species, though equids are often mentioned.
A number as a sign. His name could also be written with the numeral 20, which was associated with him.
