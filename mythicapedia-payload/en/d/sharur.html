enchanted talking mace of Ninurta in Sumerian mythology

Sharur

Sharur is the enchanted, talking mace of the Sumerian god Ninurta, whose name means "smasher of thousands". It is one of the best-known magical objects in Mesopotamian mythology.

The myth

According to the myth, Sharur plays a prominent part in Ninurta's battle against Asag, a monstrous demon. The weapon can fly unhindered across vast distances and communicate with its wielder. Its fullest telling appears in the epic Lugal-e, known in English as The Exploits of Ninurta.

In that tale, Sharur is far more than a weapon: it acts as an emissary between the god Enlil and Ninurta, relaying his father's will. It commands him to slay Kur, an ancient serpent deity venerated in Babylon and linked to mountains and primordial elements, and it suggests the strategy for defeating Asag. It can also take the form of a winged lion, a motif common in Sumerian and Akkadian lore.

Legacy

Sharur has been proposed as a possible precursor to similar objects in Arthurian lore, though this remains a hypothesis rather than a proven connection.

Ancient sources: The Sumerian epic Lugal-e, which recounts the exploits of Ninurta.

Trivia

Expressive name. Its Sumerian name, šar₂-ur₃, means "smasher of thousands", a direct description of its power in battle.
A weapon with an animal form. Besides flying and speaking, Sharur can transform into a winged lion, an image common in Sumerian and Akkadian lore.
