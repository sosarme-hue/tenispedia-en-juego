Japanese legendary hero, god of sweets

Tajimamori

Tajimamori is a legendary figure of Japan's Kofun period, also worshipped as the god of sweets. His story, recorded in ancient chronicles, explains why he became the patron of confectionery.

The myth

According to the myth, Emperor Suinin sent him to fetch a marvelous fruit from a magical land across the sea. After ten years he returned with branches bearing leaves and fruit, but the emperor had already died. He gave half of the branches to the emperor's widow and offered the rest at the tomb, and then died himself, overcome with grief.

The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki give different ancestries for him, so the family ties do not agree between the two sources.

Worship and religion

The fruit he brought back is identified in the records as a tachibana. He is worshipped mainly by people in the confectionery industry, at a shrine in Hyōgo Prefecture and at other shrines across the country through bunrei. Imari in Saga and Kainan in Wakayama keep traditions linked to his return and to the planting of the branches.

Legacy

The last character of the fruit's name originally meant simply "fruit", but it now refers to "sweets". He also appears as a character in the video game Rin: Daughters of Mnemosyne.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki and the Nihon Shoki, Japan's ancient chronicles, mention him under differently written names.

Trivia

A fruit whose meaning changed. The last character in the fruit's name once meant simply "fruit". Over time it came to refer to sweets.
Port of return. Imari, in Saga Prefecture, is traditionally considered the place where Tajimamori landed on his return to Japan.
