river god of Greek mythology

Apidanus

Apidanus is a river god of Greek mythology, linked to the river of the same name in Thessaly that flows into the Enipeus before it reaches the Peneus. He matters because he belongs to the large family of river deities born from Oceanus and Tethys, the waters that Greeks imagined flowing from the great stream encircling the earth.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Apidanus was one of the children of Oceanus and Tethys, one of the river gods who gave rise to the freshwater streams of the world. The account places him in a large family of rivers, all considered tributaries of the mythical river Oceanus.

Ovid mentions him among the neighbouring rivers who came to console his brother Peneus when his daughter Daphne was turned into a laurel. In this way the tradition presents him as part of a web of river kinship, in which the rivers respond like related people.

In another passage he appears in the heroic geography: the Argonaut Asterion, son of Cometes, lived at Pieria beside the waters of this river, near Mount Phylleion.

Ancient sources: Hesiod names him among the children of Oceanus and Tethys, and Ovid mentions him among the rivers who consoled Peneus after Daphne's metamorphosis.
