legendary king of Sparta

Prytanis

Prytanis is a legendary king of Sparta from the Eurypontid dynasty, according to the tradition transmitted by Pausanias. His reign is remembered mainly because the rivalry between the Lacedaemonians and the Argives is said to have begun during it.

The myth

Prytanis was the son of Eurypon and inherited the throne of Sparta from his father within the Eurypontid dynasty. Sparta had two kings at the same time, one from each line, a feature that set the city apart from most Greek poleis. The surviving accounts are brief and attribute few personal deeds to him, so his figure works more as a link in the royal genealogy than as the hero of any story.

What is recorded is an episode with long consequences: during his reign the enmity between the Lacedaemonians and the Argives is said to have arisen, a rivalry that would shape relations between these two cities of the Peloponnese for centuries. Pausanias, in his description of Greece, passes on this report as part of the history of the Spartan kings.

He was succeeded by Polydectes. His sons include Polydectes and Eunomus. Modern chronologies place his reign between 865 and 835 BC, approximate dates that do not come from the tradition itself.

Ancient sources: Pausanias mentions him in his Description of Greece (Book III, chapter 7), where he records the succession of the Spartan kings and the origin of the enmity with Argos.
