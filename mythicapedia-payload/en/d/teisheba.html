Urartian god of storms and thunder

Theispas

Theispas, also called Teisheba, was the storm and thunder god of the ancient kingdom of Urartu, and was sometimes regarded as a god of war. He formed a divine triad with Ḫaldi and Shivini, and his name was given to several places in the kingdom.

The myth

According to Urartian tradition, Theispas was the son of Jabli and the husband of the goddess Huba, whom scholars match with the Hurrian goddess Ḫepat. His power over thunder and lightning made him a protector in war, and he was usually shown as a man standing on a bull, and less often on a lion, holding a handful of thunderbolts.

He usually appears alongside Ḫaldi, the supreme and warlike god, and Shivini, the sun god. Together they formed a triad that was called upon before the other gods. Comparisons with other traditions are common: he is linked with the Assyrian god Adad, the Vedic god Indra and the Hurrian and Hittite god Teshub, showing how the figure of a storm god recurred across the ancient Near East and beyond.

Worship and religion

In royal inscriptions, the triad was invoked before the other gods to organise sacrifices. Some royal curse formulas also named the triad to punish anyone who might damage the monument bearing the text. The 8th-century BCE fortress of Odzaberd was dedicated to him. Several cities took his name, among them Teyseba, founded by Rusa I, and Teishebaini, meaning 'city of the god Teisheba', founded by Rusa II.

Ancient sources: Urartian royal inscriptions, which invoke the divine triad and mention cities named after the god.

In art

Trivia

A city bearing his name. Teishebaini means 'city of the god Teisheba' and was founded by King Rusa II. It shows that the god held an important place in the organisation of the kingdom.
A wife with a Hurrian counterpart. His wife, the goddess Huba, is matched with the Hurrian goddess Ḫepat, which reveals the cultural links between Urartu and the Hurrian world.
