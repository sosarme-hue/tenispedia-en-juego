imaginary animal of Iberian popular culture

Gamusino

The gamusino is an imaginary animal of Iberian popular culture, used to play practical jokes on newcomers, especially children and young people. Its nighttime hunt is a typical prank at summer camps and in rural villages.

The myth

No ancient text mentions the gamusino; it is an oral joke passed down through generations. The victim is sent out at night with a bag or trap to catch an animal that does not exist, while the others leave and abandon them in the dark. The creature is said to have four legs, but its appearance is never clearly described, and that vagueness is part of the trick.

The name varies from region to region and language to language, showing how old and widespread the joke is.

Legacy

The Royal Spanish Academy defines it as an imaginary animal whose name is used to tease novice hunters. In Cuba the word gave its name to the fish genus Gambusia. The Galician writer Eduardo Blanco Amor mentions a similar fabulous animal in his 1962 story collection Os biosbardos.

Trivia

The French dahu. France has an equivalent imaginary animal, the dahu, and the chasse au dahu is a traditional joke.
A fish with a borrowed name. In Cuba, the word came to mean a tiny fish and gave its name to the genus Gambusia.
