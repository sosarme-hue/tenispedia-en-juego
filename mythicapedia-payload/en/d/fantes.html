Egyptian prince of Greek mythology, son of Aegyptus

Phantes

Phantes was, in Greek mythology, one of the fifty sons of King Aegyptus and an Egyptian prince. He is remembered mainly for the tragedy of his wedding to the Danaids, the daughters of King Danaus of Libya.

The myth

According to the tradition, Phantes was a son of Aegyptus and the naiad Caliadne, and so a full brother of Eurylochus and ten other brothers, among them Peristhenes, Hermus, Dryas, Potamon, Bromius and Chthonius. In some accounts his mother was not Caliadne but Eurryroe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre.

The sons of Aegyptus married the daughters of Danaus, but Danaus ordered his daughters to kill their husbands on the wedding night. Phantes, like nearly all his brothers, was slain in this way; only Lynceus was spared. Phantes had married the Danaid Theano, daughter of Danaus and the naiad Polyxo.

Trivia

A name meaning visible. Phantes comes from the Greek Φάντης, meaning visible, a name that suggests something that can be seen.
Two possible mothers. Some accounts do not make him a son of Caliadne, but of Eurryroe, daughter of the river-god Nilus, or of Isaie, daughter of King Agenor of Tyre.
