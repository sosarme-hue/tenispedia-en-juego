companion of Odysseus in the Aeneid

Achaemenides

Achaemenides is a character in Virgil's Aeneid, a native of Ithaca and a member of Odysseus' crew who was left behind on Sicily. His rescue by Aeneas makes him one of the episodes that show the Trojan hero's mercy.

The myth

In Virgil's account, Achaemenides was a son of Adamastus of Ithaca and served in Odysseus' crew. When Odysseus fled the Cyclops Polyphemus, the Greeks left this man behind on the island of Sicily, where he was stranded.

Years later, Aeneas arrived on the island with the Trojans fleeing the ruin of Troy and took him aboard to carry him to Italy with his company of refugees. Homer does not mention him in the Odyssey, which the Aeneid continues. The episode gives Virgil a chance to show Aeneas' magnanimity, since he bears no grudge against a member of the crew of the man chiefly responsible for the fall of Troy.

His name comes from the Persian Achaemenes, which Virgil treats as Greek, giving it the sense of «he who waits with affliction». In literature, Achaemenides is one of two known members of Odysseus' crew to return to Ithaca, along with Macareus, since the other ships were destroyed by the Laestrygonians and the men left on the last ship drowned after eating the sacred cattle of Helios.

Legacy

He appears in the Aeneid as an example of clemency, and his rescue has been read as a sign of Aeneas' generosity toward former enemies.

Ancient sources: Virgil's Aeneid, Book III.

Trivia

A name of Persian origin. Virgil takes the name Achaemenes, of Persian origin, and reads it as Greek, giving it the meaning «he who waits with affliction».
One of the few survivors. Along with Macareus, Achaemenides is one of the few members of Odysseus' crew who return to Ithaca in literature.
