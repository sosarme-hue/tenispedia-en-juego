norn of the past in Norse mythology

Urðr

Urðr is one of the three Norns of Norse mythology, along with Verðandi and Skuld. Her name means "what has happened" or "fate", and she is usually associated with the past.

The myth

According to the myth, the Norns live by the well of Urðr, beneath the world ash tree Yggdrasil, in Asgard. From there Urðr and her sisters determine the destinies of all beings, both humans and gods. The Eddas place them present at the birth of every child, when they settle its fate.

In the most common image, Urðr spins the threads on the loom, representing what has already happened. Verðandi weaves what is happening now and Skuld what is yet to come, so past, present and future are intertwined. According to tradition, what has already occurred shapes what will come, and her resemblance to the Greek Clotho has been noted because of her role as spinner. From Urðr's well the Norns draw water and clay to nourish the world tree.

Legacy

Her name left a mark on language: the Old English wyrd gave rise to the modern word weird, meaning strange. Shakespeare named the witches in Macbeth The Weird Sisters, who play the role of the Norns. In modern Swedish, öde, meaning fate, comes from the same root.

Ancient sources: She appears in stanza 20 of the Völuspá in the Poetic Edda and in the Gylfaginning of the Prose Edda.
