Shinto kami of mountains and good health

Ōyamakui-no-Kami

Ōyamakui-no-Kami is a kami in Japanese Shinto, associated with mountains, land and good health. He matters because he was closely tied to the Hie shrine and to the Sannō tradition, which blended kami worship with Buddhism.

The myth

According to the Kojiki, this kami dwells on Mount Hiei in Ōmi Province, or on Mount Matsunoo in Yamashiro Province. He is the son of Ōtoshi-no-Kami and grandson of Susanoo, and belongs to a lineage of gods linked to agriculture and land. Some scholars think he may be a folk deity, or a personification of the time and space governed by Ōkuninushi.

His name is read as something like «great mountain stake». Several theories explain the term: that it refers to a guardian of the mountain, to the marker stone of the summit boundary, or to the sacred stake driven into the ground during festivals held on the peak. He is considered androgynous, and possibly hermaphroditic.

Worship and religion

He is worshipped at Hie Shrine, now Hiyoshi Taisha, and its network of affiliated shrines, and at Matsunoo Shrine, now Matsunoo Taisha. At Hie his secondary shrine is the Ninomiya, east of the main precinct. In the medieval period he was known as Sannō Gongen within Buddhist-Shinto syncretism, and after the Meiji era he was worshipped again under his original name. His cult remains alive today.

Ancient sources: The Kojiki, from the 8th century, is the earliest source that mentions him.
