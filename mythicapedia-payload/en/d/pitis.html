Greek nymph of the pine tree

Pitys

Pitys (Greek Πίτυς, "pine") is a nymph of Greek mythology, one of the Oreads, whose affections were contested by Pan and the north wind Boreas. Her story explains the origin of the pine tree.

The myth

According to the myth, Pan and Boreas both courted her. To win her favour, Boreas uprooted pine trees with his breath, while Pan leapt, capered and offered gifts that shepherds had made. Pitys chose Pan, and the spurned Boreas hurled her against rocks with a violent gust. Pitying her, Gaia turned her body into a pine tree.

Libanius gives the only complete narrative. Other versions differ: Nonnus says she reached Hades a virgin after Pan had also abused her, and in another account Pan kills her or the transformation happens as she flees him.

Legacy

A Roman mosaic and an oil painting by Edward Calvert, around 1850, depict her, though scenes of Pan and Syrinx are far more common in art. The humanist Álvaro de Cadaval wrote the Pythiographia, a Latin epyllion on her metamorphosis. The same Greek word, pitys, gave its name to the Pityusic Islands.

Ancient sources: Nonnus (Dionysiaca), Longus (Daphnis and Chloe), Lucian (Dialogues of the Dead) and Libanius mention her.

Trivia

The Pityusic Islands. The Greek word pitys also named these islands in the western Mediterranean.
Pan's pipes. Pan also chased the naiad Syrinx, who was turned into reeds. The god used them to make his musical instrument.
