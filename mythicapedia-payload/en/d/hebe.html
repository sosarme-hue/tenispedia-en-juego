Greek goddess of youth

Hebe

Hebe is a minor goddess of Greek mythology who lives on Mount Olympus and embodies eternal youth. Daughter of Zeus and Hera, she served as cupbearer to the gods and became the wife of Heracles, and her Roman counterpart is Juventas.

The myth

According to Hesiod, Hebe is the daughter of Zeus and Hera, alongside Ares and Eileithyia. After his apotheosis, Heracles took her as his wife on Olympus. Homer presents her as the attendant of the gods: she pours nectar while they hold their assemblies, helps Hera harness the horses to her chariot, and bathes and dresses her brother Ares.

Another tradition, recorded by the First Vatican Mythographer, claims that Hera conceived Hebe after eating a lettuce rather than through Zeus. With Heracles she had two children, Alexiares and Anicetus, of whom little more than the names is known. Callimachus notes that Eileithyia attended her during labour.

Myth also credits her with the power to restore youth. When the aged Iolaus asked the gods to return his strength so he could fight Eurystheus, Hebe granted him his youthful vigour for a few hours. Some accounts say that after she left the post of cupbearer, Ganymede took her place, while the Vatican Mythographers preserve a variant in which she was a daughter of Minos.

Worship and religion

Hebe was worshipped in Athens, where she had an altar at the Cynosarges beside that of Heracles. In Sicyon and Phlius she was honoured under the names Ganymeda or Dia in a sacred grove.

Legacy

Her name gave rise to the medical term hebephrenia, an old name for a form of schizophrenia, and to the word hebetude, meaning dullness or lethargy. In art she appears in a white dress holding a golden jug, and her role as cupbearer remained a staple of depictions of divine banquets.

Ancient sources: Hesiod, Homer, Callimachus, Cicero, Philostratus the Elder, Bacchylides and the First Vatican Mythographer mention her in their works.

Trivia

A lettuce as origin. The First Vatican Mythographer says Hera conceived Hebe after eating a lettuce, a version that contrasts with the usual account of her birth.
Juventas in Rome. Roman boys offered her a coin when they first put on the toga virilis, marking their passage into adulthood.
