Hindu folk goddess of Gujarat and Rajasthan

Khodiyar

Khodiyar is a Hindu folk goddess worshipped in Gujarat and Rajasthan, in western India. She serves as the clan goddess, or kuldevi, of several lineages, and her story helped legitimise local dynasties.

The myth

According to the tradition, Mamadiya Gadhvi, a bard living near Vallabhi, was banished from court after the merchants turned the queen against him, and he prayed for children at a Shiva temple. Shiva granted him seven daughters and a son. The girls, while playing on a hill, developed a craving for buffalo and tore them apart. When the king tried to burn them alive, a voice from heaven cursed his line to remain childless. The youngest, Khodiyar, was lame and could not keep up with her sisters; when she prayed to Jagdamba, the goddesses told her that her limp had a purpose and that she would become the greatest of the sisters. Later, she helped the young Navghan, son of the Chudasama ruler Ra Dayas, reclaim Junagadh, and became the protector of that dynasty.

Worship and religion

She was the kuldevi of the Chudasama and Gohil clans. Her shrines once received animal and blood offerings, but over time many became vegetarian, and animal sacrifice was banned in Gujarat in 1971. She is still venerated in rural shrines.

Legacy

Her story helped legitimise the power of the Chudasama and Gohil dynasties.

Trivia

A vegetarian goddess. Over time, Khodiyar and other goddesses of western India became vegetarian, and their fierce character softened.
The waters of the Rann. According to the tradition, a Charan woman who embodied Khodiyar made the waters of the Rann of Kachh part so that Navghan's army could cross.
