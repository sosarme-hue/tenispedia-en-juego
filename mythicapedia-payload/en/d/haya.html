Mesopotamian god of scribes and grain

Haya

Haya was a minor god of ancient Mesopotamia, linked to the scribal arts and, according to some scholars, to grain. He was considered the husband of Nisaba and the father of the goddess Sud, which made him Enlil's father-in-law.

The myth

According to Mesopotamian texts, Haya was a wise divine accountant and archivist, charged with recording the years of a king's reign on the "tablet of life", a mythical object the gods used to track the deeds of mortals.

In the myth of Enlil and Sud he appears briefly as the father of the goddess who marries Enlil, although unlike Nisaba he takes no part in the consultations before the wedding. Only a single hymn devoted to him survives, from the reign of Rim-Sîn I of Larsa, which presents him as a protector of Ur and asks him to bless the king.

Worship and religion

He was worshipped in Ur, Umma, Kuara, Nippur and Shaduppum, where a temple existed in the Old Babylonian period. His cult declined afterwards in Babylonia, but in Assyria Sennacherib built him a new temple, perhaps as a replacement for the god Nabu, though he soon ceased to be venerated.

Legacy

His name is probably of Semitic origin, and a link with the god Ea has been proposed; the idea is plausible but not universally accepted.

Ancient sources: He appears in god lists such as An = Anum and An = Anu ša amēli, in an Old Babylonian hymn and in the myth of Enlil and Sud.
