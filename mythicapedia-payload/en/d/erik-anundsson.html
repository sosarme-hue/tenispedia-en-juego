semi-legendary Swedish king of the Viking Age

Erik Anundsson

Erik Anundsson, also known as Erik Emundsson, was a Swedish chieftain of the Svear who, according to the Norse sagas, ruled Sweden in the 9th century. He matters because the sagas credit him with expansion across the Baltic and a clash with Harald Fairhair of Norway, although his historical existence remains unconfirmed.

The myth

According to the Hervarar saga, Erik was the son of King Anund of Uppsala and inherited the throne from his father and his uncle Björn of Birka. During his reign, Harald Fairhair rose to power in Norway. The Heimskringla recalls that Erik sent expeditions every summer and conquered Finland, Courland, Estonia and other eastern lands.

The saga of Harald says Erik wanted to extend his realm westward, as large as that of Sigurd Ring and Ragnar Lodbrok. He conquered Värmland, Västergötland and Bohuslän, but Harald defeated him in Värmland and forced him back. When the local chief Åke favoured the Norwegian king, Erik killed him. Harald then pursued Erik and subdued the Swedish king's men. Erik died around 882, and his son Björn III succeeded him.

Ancient sources: The Icelandic sagas Hervarar, Heimskringla and the saga of Harald Fairhair, all from the 13th and 14th centuries, record him; no contemporary sources survive.
