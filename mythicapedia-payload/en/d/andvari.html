dwarf of Norse mythology who guarded a cursed treasure

Andvari

Andvari is a dwarf in Norse mythology who lived beneath a waterfall and could turn himself into a pike at will. He matters chiefly for the cursed gold he owned, which shaped the fates of several heroes in the Germanic legends.

The myth

According to the stories, Andvari was a wealthy dwarf thanks to a magic ring called Andvaranaut, which had helped him gather a great hoard. He lived beneath a waterfall and could change into a pike whenever he wished.

Loki caught him using a net lent by Rán, goddess of the sea. Trapped in pike form, Andvari was forced to hand over his gold and the ring. Before they took it, he placed a curse on the treasure: it would destroy anyone who possessed it. Tradition holds that the curse came true through a long chain of misfortunes.

After the deaths of Brynhild and Sigurd, Gunnar left the gold in a cave. Years later, Andvari discovered the cave and recovered his lost treasure, but the ring had vanished forever.

Legacy

In Richard Wagner's cycle of music dramas Der Ring des Nibelungen, the character Alberich combines traits of Andvari with those of the Alberich of the Nibelungenlied. His figure thus bridged Norse and Germanic tradition in nineteenth-century music and theatre.

Ancient sources: His story appears in the Norse sagas and Eddic material on the cursed hoard that later passed into the Nibelung legends.

Trivia

A name meaning caution. In Old Norse, Andvari is linked to a word meaning careful or cautious, a trait that fits his role as guardian of the treasure.
