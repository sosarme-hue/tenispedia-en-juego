demoness (asura) of Hindu tradition

Holika

Holika is an asura (demoness) in Hindu tradition, sister of the king Hiranyakashipu and aunt of Prahlada. Her burning gives its name to the bonfire rite held the night before Holi, the festival of colours.

The myth

According to the Puranas, King Hiranyakashipu obtained a boon from Brahma that made him nearly invincible: he could not be killed by day or night, indoors or outdoors, by any weapon, on earth, in the sky or in water, nor by man or beast. After conquering the three worlds, he turned against his son Prahlada, a devotee of Vishnu who refused to worship him.

Holika, who according to the Vishnu Purana was immune to fire, offered her help. Prahlada was to sit on her lap atop a burning pyre, but he chanted Vishnu's name, emerged unharmed and Holika burned. In one variant, Holika wore a fireproof shawl that flew off in the blaze and covered Prahlada instead.

Vishnu then appeared as Narasimha, half man and half lion, at dusk, on the threshold of the palace, and slew the king on his lap with his claws, sidestepping every condition of the boon. Cosmic order was restored.

Worship and religion

On the night before Holi, bonfires are lit across North India and Nepal, and in parts of the south, to commemorate the legend. Rituals include circling the fire and throwing coconuts, barley, wheat and cow dung into it. In the south, the same date marks Kama Dahanam, linked to Shiva burning Kamadeva to ashes. The celebration remains very much alive today.

Legacy

The Holika Dahan rite gives its name to the eve of Holi, one of India's most popular festivals, and its message of good triumphing over evil has become a cultural symbol of the celebration.

Ancient sources: The Puranas, particularly the Vishnu Purana, which recount the story of Holika and Prahlada.

Trivia

Kama Dahanam. In South India the same date is linked to Shiva burning Kamadeva with his third eye. In rural Tamil Nadu, mime performances of Kamadeva are staged and his effigies are burnt.
Regional names. In the north the day is called Holika Dahan, while in Purvanchal and the Terai region of Nepal it is known as Sammat Jaarna.
