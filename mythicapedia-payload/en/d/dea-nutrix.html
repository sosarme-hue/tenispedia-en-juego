Gallo-Roman and British goddess of fertility

Dea Nutrix

Dea Nutrix ("nursing goddess") is the name archaeologists give to a fertility deity worshipped in Gaul, Britain and southern Germania during the Roman era. Her original name and mythology are unknown.

The myth

No stories about her survive, so her myth is almost entirely unknown. She is known only from her images: a mature, clothed woman seated in a wicker chair, nursing one or two infants. Scholars place her among the large group of Romano-Gaulish and British mother goddesses, portrayed in metal, clay and stone across the Roman world.

Worship and religion

Cheap statuettes of white pipe clay are common finds at many sites in the United Kingdom and France, both in homes and in graves, which points to a household cult linked to motherhood and the protection of children. They were probably made mainly in Central Gaul and the Rhineland. It has been suggested that her cult grew out of earlier Celtic goddesses associated with fertility and childbirth.
