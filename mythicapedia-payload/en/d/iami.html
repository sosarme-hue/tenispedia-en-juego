river goddess of life in Hinduism, twin sister of Yama

Yami

Yami is a figure from the Vedic tradition who later became the goddess of the Yamuna river in Hinduism. According to the tradition, she was the first woman and the twin sister of Yama, the first mortal, and her story shows the shift from Vedic worship to Hindu devotion.

The myth

In Vedic mythology, which predates Hinduism, Yami ("the twin") and her brother Yama were children of Vivasvat, the sun god, and his wife Saranyu. The tenth mandala of the Rigveda, the oldest text of India, contains a dialogue in which Yami urges Yama to have incestuous relations to continue the human line, but he refuses, fearing Varuna and Mitra, the main gods of the Vedic period.

In later texts such as the Harivamsa and the Markandeya Purana, she appears as Yamuna and as the presiding deity of the river. The Vamana Purana tells how her originally clear waters turned black when Shiva, grief-stricken over the death of Sati, plunged into the river. Another legend links the darkness of her waters to the serpent Kaliya, whom Krishna defeated and banished. In Tibetan Buddhism, by contrast, Yami is a goddess of death who rules the female spirits of the underworld and is the consort of Yama.

Worship and religion

In Hinduism she is worshipped as the river goddess, and her waters are considered sacred. According to the scriptures, bathing in them or drinking them removes sin. Her image appears on temple doorjambs from the Gupta era, paired with that of Ganga. She is depicted with dark skin, standing on a tortoise and holding a water pot.

Legacy

Yami shares the festival of Bhau-beej, in which a sister prays for her brother. As a consort of Krishna, she is counted among his eight principal wives.

Ancient sources: Rigveda (books 10, hymns 10 and 14), Shatapatha Brahmana, Taittiriya Samhita, Maitrayani Samhita, Harivamsa, Markandeya Purana, Vamana Purana and Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Incestuous twins. In the Rigveda, Yami is the first woman and her brother Yama the first mortal. The dialogue in which she asks him to have children with her is one of the most debated passages of the text.
Dark river. According to the Vamana Purana, the waters of the Yamuna turned dark from Shiva's grief after Sati's death, which is one reason for the name Kalindi.
