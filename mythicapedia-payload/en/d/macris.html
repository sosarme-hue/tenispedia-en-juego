nurse of Dionysus and Hera, daughter of Aristaeus

Macris

Macris is a figure from Greek mythology, a daughter of Aristaeus linked to the upbringing of Dionysus and Hera. Her story explains an old name for the island of Corfu and the name of Medea's Cave. Ancient authors give her different roles, depending on the version.

The myth

According to tradition, Macris was a daughter of Aristaeus, a rustic god and son of Apollo, and was also called Nysa. Accounts differ on her role: in some she was the nurse of Dionysus, whom she fed with honey; in others she reared the young goddess Hera on Euboea. When Zeus and Hera hid on Mount Cithaeron and Macris tried to search for them, the mountain king Cithaeron misled her, claiming Zeus was there with Leto. Hera, furious at Macris for helping Dionysus, banished her from Euboea.

She fled to an island that, according to one scholiast, later took her name; the Argonautica call it Drepane and link it with Macris and Demeter. It is generally identified with modern Corfu, the ancient Corcyra. Her cave later became the bridal chamber of Jason and Medea, who consummated their marriage on the pelts of the Golden Fleece, and since then it has been known as Medea's Cave.

Euboea, the island off Boeotia, was sacred to Hera and was one of several places, though far from the only one, that claimed to be the site of Zeus and Hera's first prenuptial union.

Ancient sources: The Argonautica and the comments of an ancient scholiast.
