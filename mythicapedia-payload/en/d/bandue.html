indigenous god or goddess of western Iberia

Bandue

Bandue is the name of a deity worshipped in north-western and western Iberia by the Callaeci and the Lusitanians. It is one of the best-documented indigenous theonyms of the region, and its cult seems to have reached beyond Lusitania.

The myth

No ancient text offers a narrative about Bandue, and almost everything known comes from dedicatory inscriptions. Its name appears in some thirty inscriptions, mostly from Ourense and Cáceres, often paired with epithets that seem to refer to particular places.

Scholars debate whether Bandue named one specific deity or was an epithet used for several gods. Some proposals link the name with water, fords or passageways, while others present it as a protective deity of indigenous settlements, and one suggests it formed a pair with a goddess called Bandua.

Worship and religion

Its worship is known mainly from stone dedications, which point to local devotion among indigenous communities in western Iberia rather than in the large Romanized centres.

Legacy

One researcher has proposed that Saint Torquatus, one of the Seven Apostolic Men linked to the arrival of Christianity in Hispania, whose relics are kept at Santa Comba de Bande, may be a Christian version of Bandue.

Ancient sources: The name is known almost exclusively from votive inscriptions on stone, not from ancient literary texts.

Trivia

Many spellings. The name appears in forms such as Bandi, Bandei, Bandu or Banduae, depending on the region and the language of the inscription.
A misread name. For a long time scholars recorded the name as Bandua, due to a misreading of how it appears in the inscriptions.
