Buddhist goddess of wealth and abundance

Vasudhārā

Vasudhārā, whose name means "stream of gems" in Sanskrit, is the Buddhist goddess of wealth, prosperity and abundance, also known as Gold Tara. She is especially popular among the Newar Buddhists of the Kathmandu Valley and is honored in many Buddhist countries.

The myth

According to the tradition, the impoverished layman Sucandra asked the Buddha Shakyamuni for a way to obtain gold, grain, silver and gems to feed his large family and give to charity. The Buddha taught him a mantra and ritual associated with Vasudhārā. Once Sucandra practiced them he prospered, and the Buddha urged that they be shared "for the good of many."

Worship and religion

Her mantra, which originated in Nepal, is now recited across India and beyond. Devotees count it on a mala (prayer beads) and believe the practice brings seven kinds of prosperity and leads toward enlightenment.

Legacy

She is often compared with the Hindu goddess Bhūmidevī, and both share a golden hue and treasure-vase imagery in art.

Ancient sources: The Vasudhara Dharani, a Buddhist text where her origin in Buddhism appears.
