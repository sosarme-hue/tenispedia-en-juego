Chinese deity of silk and sericulture

Canshen

Canshen, or the «Silkworm God», is the deity of silkworm rearing and sericulture in Chinese folk religion. It groups two deified figures who, according to tradition, helped invent and spread the cultivation of silk.

The myth

The main figure is Canmu, the «Silkworm Mother», also called Cangu, the «Silkworm Maiden». Her cult is linked to that of Houtu, the «Queen of the Earth», and to that of the goddesses of the Three Skies. Some traditions identify her with Leizu, wife of Huangdi, the deity of the cosmic centre and progenitor of all the Chinese.

The other figure is Qingyishen, the «Bluegreen-Clad God», who is said to be the same as Cancong, the «Silkworm Twig». He is presented as the first ruler and ancestor of the Shu kingdom, and as the promoter of sericulture among his people.

Worship and religion

The cult of Canmu is typical of central-northern and eastern China, while Qingyishen was worshipped in Sichuan, the province descended from the ancient Shu kingdom.
