bodhisattva and protective goddess of Tibetan Buddhism

Tara

Tara is a female figure of Vajrayana Buddhism, revered above all in Tibet and Mongolia as a saviouress who hears the cries of beings. Her roots lie in India, where she emerged from the Hindu tradition of the mother goddess.

The myth

According to Buddhist tradition, Tara arose from the compassionate tears of Avalokiteshvara, who wept on seeing the suffering of beings trapped in samsara. His tears turned into a blue lotus, from whose petals Tara emerged. Another tale tells of a princess who, millions of years ago in another world system, made offerings to a buddha and received teachings on compassion. When some monks suggested she pray to be reborn as a man, she replied that only weak-minded worldlings saw gender as a barrier to enlightenment, and vowed to always be reborn as a female bodhisattva until samsara ended.

Texts call her the mother of the buddhas and the embodiment of perfect wisdom joined with compassion, a role she shares with Prajñāpāramitā. Her name comes from the Sanskrit root tṝ, "to cross", and means "the one who saves" or "the one who carries across". Her many forms include Green Tara, the most revered in Tibet; White Tara, linked to healing and long life; Kurukulla, the Red Tara; and Ekajati, the Blue Tara, of wrathful aspect. Tradition speaks of twenty-one Taras, and the Praises to the Twenty-One Taras is recited daily in Tibetan schools.

Worship and religion

Tara's worship grew in India from around the sixth century, and her cult is documented at Nalanda and in the Kānherī and Ellora caves. Under the Pala Empire she became an important bodhisattva, and her veneration spread to Tibet with the transmission of Indian Buddhism. A distinctive feature is that lay people can invoke her directly, without a lama or monk. Her sadhanas include prayers, recitation of her mantra, visualization of the goddess and the dedication of merit to all beings. Her cult remains alive today in Tibet, Mongolia, Nepal, Bhutan and Buddhist communities worldwide.

Legacy

Tara is one of the great female figures of the Tibetan Buddhist pantheon and has inspired countless paintings, sculptures and thangkas. Her mantra, oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā, is recited throughout Tibetan Buddhism and is also shared with Hindus.

Ancient sources: The Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa calls her the noble goddess who is the compassion of Avalokiteshvara, and the Tārā-mūla-kalpa is the main tantra associated with her.

Trivia

Twenty-one Taras. According to some schools, Tara emanates twenty-one forms with different colours and attributes, from the pacifying to the wrathful.
