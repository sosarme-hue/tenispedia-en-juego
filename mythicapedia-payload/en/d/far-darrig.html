solitary faerie of Irish mythology

Far darrig

The far darrig, or fear dearg («Red Man»), is a faerie of Irish mythology classed among the solitary fairies alongside the leprechaun and the clurichaun. He is known for his cruel practical jokes and his link to nightmares.

The myth

The far darrig, or fear dearg, is said to wear a red coat and cap, and is sometimes called a Rat Boy. Accounts describe him as rather fat, with dark, hairy skin, a long snout and a skinny tail. He is generally short, though some tellings make him tall, and he is said to be able to change size. An 1832 description by T. Crofton Croaker, from Munster, gives him a height of about 2.5 feet, a wrinkled face, long grey hair and a sugarloaf hat.

Practical jokes are his favourite pastime, especially gruesome ones, and one example is swapping human babies for changelings. He is also linked to nightmares. Refusing one of his requests brings bad luck, since he is very inventive when it comes to punishments. When he is not playing tricks, he spends much of his time smoking by campfires and thinking. He will eat if offered food, and his presence in a house is said to bring good luck.

Trivia

Rat Boys. His nickname, Rat Boys, refers to his looks: he is said to be fat, with dark, hairy skin, a long snout and a skinny tail.
Luck in the house. Although his jokes are feared, his presence in a house is considered a sign of good luck.
