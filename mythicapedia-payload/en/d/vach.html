Vedic goddess of speech

Vac

Vac is the Vedic goddess of speech, a personified form of divine utterance. She belongs to the Hindu tradition, and her figure matters because she links the sacred word of the Vedas with the divine itself.

The myth

In the Rig Veda, Vac is the daughter of the sage Ambhrina, which is why she appears as Vac Ambhrini. The Vedas describe her as created by Prajapati, a figure later identified with Brahma, and say that he took her as his wife. Elsewhere she is called the mother of the Vedas and the consort of Indra, and the Vishnu Purana makes her a daughter of Daksha and the wife of the sage Kashyapa. The genealogies vary from author to author and from text to text.

Tradition portrays her as a force that enters inspired poets and visionaries, giving expression and energy to those she favours. The Naighantuka and Yaska's Nirukta place her as the voice of the middle spheres. The Padma Purana adds further links: it makes her the wife of Kashyapa, the mother of the emotions and a friend of the gandharvas, the celestial musicians.

Legacy

She is very often identified with Sarasvati, goddess of knowledge, and with Bharati. Sarasvati remains a significant and revered deity in Hinduism today. The name Vac comes from the ancient vak, meaning 'word', and from the same Indo-European root comes the Latin vox, the source of the Spanish word voz.

Ancient sources: Rig Veda, the Vedas, Aitareya Aranyaka, Naighantuka, Yaska's Nirukta, Vishnu Purana and Padma Purana.

Trivia

Voice and word. The Sanskrit vāk, from which Vach derives, means 'word'. The same Indo-European root gave the Latin word vox.
A goddess merged with another. In later Vedic literature and post-Vedic Hindu texts, Vac is identified with Saraswati, the goddess of knowledge who is still revered.
