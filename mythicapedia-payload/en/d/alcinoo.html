king of the Phaeacians in Greek mythology

Alcinous

Alcinous is the king of the Phaeacians, a people who live on the island of Scheria in Greek mythology. He becomes important in the Odyssey, where he welcomes the shipwrecked Odysseus and helps him return to Ithaca.

The myth

According to tradition, Alcinous was the son of Nausithous and brother of Rhexenor. After his brother's death, he married Arete, his brother's daughter and therefore his niece, and they had Nausicaa and several sons, among them Laodamas and Halius. Hesiod says Alcinous and Arete were siblings who married each other, while in some accounts his father was Phaeax, son of Poseidon and Corcyra.

In the Argonaut story, Alcinous rules the island of Drepane, often identified with Corfu. When the Argonauts arrived, he welcomed Jason and Medea. When the Colchians demanded Medea back, Alcinous declared that she would be returned if still a virgin, but protected if she was already Jason's wife; Arete arranged for the Colchians to leave without her.

In the Odyssey, Alcinous receives Odysseus with hospitality, listens to his adventures and provides a ship for his return to Ithaca. Poseidon, angry because Odysseus had blinded his son Polyphemus, turns the ship into a rock on its return and threatens the Phaeacians, who stop sailing to foreign lands.

Worship and religion

Alcinous received a heroic cult in the city of Corcyra, where one of the city's two harbours bears his name.

Ancient sources: Homer presents him in the Odyssey, Hesiod mentions his kinship with Arete, and the sources on the Argonauts include him in their accounts.

Trivia

A harbour named after him. In the city of Corcyra, one of the two harbours bears the name of Alcinous.
A rock as punishment. According to the Odyssey, Poseidon turns the ship carrying Odysseus home into a rock as punishment for the Phaeacians.
