legendary ancestor of the Angles

Angul

Angul is a legendary ancestor of the Angles and brother of Dan, forefather of the Danes. He is known mainly from Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum, which places him among the earliest kings of Denmark's protohistory.

The myth

According to Saxo Grammaticus, Angul and Dan were the sons of Humblus. Both brothers were forefathers and founders of the Danes; with the support of their kinsmen they ruled the realm, though they never took the title of king. Angul was also the ancestor of the Angles, who later migrated to Britain.

Saxo presents these founders as humans rather than gods, unlike the Prose Edda. Some scholars have suggested that Dan and Angul resemble Romulus and Remus, part of a wider set of parallels with Roman tradition. The chronology is also unclear: the Chronicle of Lejre places Dan in the time of Emperor Augustus, while Saxo puts them more than twenty generations before his own time.

Legacy

Saxo says the region of Angeln took its name from Angul, and the land the Angles settled in Britain eventually became England.

Ancient sources: Saxo Grammaticus' Gesta Danorum. The Chronicle of Lejre and Jordanes' Getica also treat the origins of the Danes, though they do not mention Angul.

Trivia

Founding twins. Their parallel with Romulus and Remus suggests Saxo wanted to place the Danes on a par with Rome, as an independent and equal people.
From Angul to England. The Angles carried his name to Britain, and the name England ultimately derives from it.
