Proto-Indo-European goddess of the dawn

Hausos

Hausos is the reconstructed dawn goddess of Proto-Indo-European mythology, the ancestor of Greek Eos, Roman Aurora and Vedic Ushas. Her consistent traits across so many traditions explain her importance.

The myth

According to the reconstruction, she is a daughter of the sky god Dyeus and sister of the divine twins. In the Rigveda, Ushas wakes the world and upholds cosmic order, yet she also makes mortals grow old. In Greek myth, Eos asks Zeus that Tithonus keep his life but not his youth, and he ends up turned into a cricket.

Worship and religion

No temples to the Proto-Indo-European goddess are documented, but the Rigveda dedicates twenty-one hymns to Ushas. Among the Germanic peoples, the goddess Ostara was honoured with a yearly spring festival.

Legacy

Her legacy survives in the Latin name Aurora and in Lucifer, 'light-bringer', the Gothic name for the morning star.

Ancient sources: The Rigveda, with twenty-one hymns to Ushas, and Homeric texts, which use the phrase 'rosy-fingered Dawn'.

Trivia

Aušrinė. In Lithuanian tradition, Aušrinė, personification of the morning star, was said to light a fire for the sun at the start of each day.
From dawn to Easter. The name of the Germanic goddess Ostara gave rise to the word Easter in some West Germanic languages.
