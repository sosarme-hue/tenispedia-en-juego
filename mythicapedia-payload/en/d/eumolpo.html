mythical priest of Demeter and founder of the Eleusinian Mysteries

Eumolpus

Eumolpus is a figure of Greek mythology, the legendary founder of the Eumolpidae, a priestly family linked to the Eleusinian Mysteries. Depending on the source, he appears as a Thracian king, a warrior, a musician or a priest, and he matters because he is remembered as one of the first servants of Demeter's cult.

The myth

According to Apollodorus, Eumolpus was the son of Poseidon and Chione, daughter of Boreas. Chione gave birth to him in secret and, fearing her father, threw the infant into the sea. Poseidon rescued him and took him to Ethiopia, where Benthesikyme raised him. As an adult he tried to force himself on one of his foster family's daughters, and was banished to Thrace with his son Ismarus.

There he plotted against King Tegyrius, fled to Eleusis and made friends among its people. After Ismarus died, he inherited the Thracian throne. When war broke out between Eleusis and Athens, he came to their aid with many Thracian troops. In the battle, according to tradition, Erechtheus killed him, and Poseidon avenged him by destroying Erechtheus and his house.

The Homeric Hymns present him as the first priest of Demeter. Hyginus says he marched on Attica, claiming the land had belonged to his father. Diodorus credits him with the Bacchic songs in honour of Dionysus. The genealogies disagree: some make him a son of Apollo and the nymph Astycome, others a son of Musaeus, or even one of the earth-born.

Worship and religion

As the first priest of Demeter, Eumolpus was the ancestor of the Eumolpidae, the family that guarded the Eleusinian Mysteries. After the war with Athens, the Eleusinians kept the exclusive right to celebrate the Mysteries, and Eumolpus, together with the daughters of Celeus, performed the sacred rites. Tombs attributed to him were shown both at Eleusis and at Athens.

Legacy

Eumolpus is linked to music: he is credited with victories in singing and flute contests, and later writers attached poems and writings on the Mysteries to his name. In modern times, the scholars Vinzenz Brinkmann and Ulrike Koch-Brinkmann identified the 5th-century bronze statue known as Riace B as a likeness of Eumolpus, since its fingers suggest it once held a bow and arrow, typical of Thracian warriors.

Ancient sources: The Homeric Hymns, Hyginus, Diodorus Siculus, Apollodorus (Bibliotheca) and Pausanias.

Trivia

A statue with a bow. A 5th-century bronze statue, Riace B, was identified by two researchers as a likeness of Eumolpus. The position of its fingers suggests it once held a bow and arrow.
A flute-playing witness. Philonome falsely accused Tenes before Cycnus and named as witness a flute player called Eumolpus, though it is unclear whether he was the same figure.
Heracles initiated. Before his twelfth labour, Heracles was purified by Eumolpus and then initiated into the Eleusinian Mysteries, despite being a foreigner.
