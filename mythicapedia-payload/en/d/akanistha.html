the highest of the Buddhist form realms

Akaniṣṭha

In classical Buddhist cosmology, Akaniṣṭha is the highest of the Pure Abodes and therefore the topmost of all the form realms. It matters because it appears across the major Buddhist traditions, each giving it a different meaning.

The myth

In classical Buddhist cosmology, Akaniṣṭha is the realm where devas such as Maheśvara live. In Theravada Buddhism it is one of the 31 planes of existence and the highest of the Brahma realms. Its name means "Nothing Higher" or "Unsurpassed."

In Mahayana Buddhism, Akaniṣṭha is also the name of the Pure Land of the Buddha Vairocana, the setting of the Ghanavyūha Sūtra. Tibetan Buddhism describes three Akaniṣṭhas: the ultimate one, the formless state of the dharmakaya and ultimate reality; the densely arrayed or symbolic one, the realm of the saṃbhogakāya, where, according to tradition, the Buddha Vajradhara taught the Vajrayana; and the mundane one, the highest pure level of the form realm, the sphere of the nirmāṇakāya.

Ancient sources: The Ghanavyūha Sūtra places the Pure Land of Vairocana in this realm, and Tibetan traditions describe it as the abode of the saṃbhogakāya.

Trivia

A name that is a superlative. Akaniṣṭha means "Nothing Higher." The Pali form, Akaniṭṭha, carries the same sense and reflects its status as the summit of the form realms.
Three Akaniṣṭhas. Tibetan Buddhism speaks of three Akaniṣṭhas: the ultimate, the densely arrayed, and the mundane. Each corresponds to a different level of Buddhist reality.
