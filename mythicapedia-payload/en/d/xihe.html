sun goddess of Chinese mythology, mother of the three-legged ravens

Xihe

Xihe is a solar deity in Chinese mythology, wife of Di Jun and mother of the ten suns, depicted as three-legged crows. She matters because she explains the sun's daily journey across the world.

The myth

According to tradition, Xihe was one of two wives of Di Jun, alongside Changxi. She bore ten sons who were suns in the form of three-legged crows, living in a mulberry tree called Fusang in the East Sea. Each day one of these sun birds was assigned to travel across the world on a carriage driven by Xihe.

The best-known story tells that one day all ten suns came out at once and the world began to burn. The archer Houyi saved it by shooting down all but one.

The Huainanzi describes the sun's journey in detail: it rises from the Bright Valley, bathes in the Pool of Xian, rests in the Fusang Tree and passes through a series of stages with poetic names until it sinks into the Vale of Obscurity, from which it rises again at dawn. The Tang poet Li He turned hostility against the dragons that pulled the sun chariot in his poem on the shortness of days.

Legacy

Li He's poem on the shortness of days and the Huainanzi's account of the sun's journey are part of the Chinese literature that preserves her story. Her figure lives mainly in literary tradition rather than in an organised cult.

Ancient sources: The Huainanzi describes the sun's daily journey, and Tang dynasty poetry such as Li He's alludes to its dragons and solar chariot.

Trivia

One sun too many. According to the story, all ten suns came out on the same day and the world began to burn until Houyi shot down nine of them.
A journey with names. The Huainanzi gives poetic names to each stage of the sun's route, from dawn to dusk.
