Ainu goddess of the hearth fire

Kamuy-huci

Kamuy-huci is the Ainu goddess of the hearth fire, from Hokkaido in Japan. She keeps the gateway between the human world and that of the kamuy, and is one of the most powerful divinities in Ainu mythology.

The myth

According to the most common version, she descended from heaven with Kanna Kamuy, god of thunder and lightning, to bring fire to a world without vegetation. Other accounts make her born from the fire drill, or the daughter of an elm, though the traditions do not fully agree.

She taught Ainu women to make kut, sacred girdles, and so earned the name Iresu Kamuy, “People’s Teacher.” One myth tells how her husband was seduced by Wakka-us-kamuy, deity of fresh water. Insulted, she challenged the rival to a duel of sorcery, won with relative ease, and her chastened husband returned home.

Worship and religion

The hearth was the centre of the household and a point of contact with the kamuy, so its fire could never be allowed to go out completely. It was also believed to be the abode of the dead, since the Ainu word for ancestor means those who dwell in the hearth.

Legacy

Her figure may have inspired the Hisuian form of Typhlosion in the video games Pokémon Legends: Arceus, set in a region inspired by Ainu culture, though the link is a possibility rather than a certainty.

Trivia

Full name. Her full name, Apemerukoyan-mat Unamerukoyan-mat, is usually rendered as something like “Rising Fire Sparks Woman”.
Guardian and judge of the home. She punishes those who pollute the hearth or neglect proper family relations, and she is assisted by other kamuy such as Mintakoro-kamuy, guardian of the home’s grounds, and Rukoro-kamuy, the kamuy of the privy.
