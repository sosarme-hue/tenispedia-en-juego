king of Argos in Greek mythology

Alector

Alector is a king of Argos in Greek mythology, son of Anaxagoras and father of Iphis. He is mainly remembered for the division of the kingdom made by his father, which left him with only a part of the territory.

The myth

According to Greek tradition, Alector came to the throne as son and successor of Anaxagoras. Before that, his father had divided the kingdom of Argos into three parts, sharing them with Melampus and Bias, so Alector received only one third of the Argive land.

Some sources also consider Capaneus to be a son of Alector, adding a second descendant to his line, although not all texts record it this way.

When Alector died, the throne passed to his son Iphis, who became king of Argos. The brevity of this succession makes Alector more a link in the line of Argive kings than a protagonist of great deeds.
