Latvian goddess of the land, livestock and dawn

Māra

Māra is the highest-ranking goddess in Latvian mythology, an ancient dawn goddess who watches over women's work, the household economy and even markets. She is linked to the land and to the fertility of livestock.

The myth

According to tradition, Māra is the counterpart of Dievs: while Dievs takes the soul after death, Māra takes the body. She is called 'Mother of Cows' (Govu māte), a title that recalls the Vedic dawn goddess, and the land itself is known as Māras zeme, 'Māra's land'.

In western Latvia she was closely associated with Laima, to the point that the two may have been the same deity. Some scholars distinguish her from Zemes māte, Mother Earth, although the two are often confused.

Worship and religion

The festival Māras was held in her honour every 15 August. That date coincides with the Assumption of Mary, which suggests a blending with Christian devotion. Scholars disagree on whether Māra predates Christianity or arose as a reflection of the Christian Mary among semi-Christianised Livonian peasants.

Trivia

Mother of Cows. One of her titles is Govu māte, 'Mother of Cows', which links her to the care of livestock.
A date shared with Mary. Her festival fell on 15 August, the same day as the Assumption of Mary in Christian tradition.
