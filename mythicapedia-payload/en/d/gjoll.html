river of the dead in Norse mythology

Gjöll

Gjöll is the river that divides the living from the dead in Norse mythology, one of the eleven Élivágar which, according to the sources, rise from the wellspring Hvergelmir. It matters because it forms the boundary that must be crossed to reach Hel, the realm of the dead.

The myth

According to the Gylfaginning, the work in which Snorri Sturluson gathered Norse myths, Gjöll is one of eleven rivers that rise from the wellspring Hvergelmir in Niflheim. These streams, known as the Élivágar, are said to have flowed through Ginnungagap, the primordial void, before the worlds existed.

After crossing that emptiness, the river reaches the worlds of the living, yet it is above all the current that runs closest to the gates of the underworld. Its waters are icy, and knives drift along in them. Across it lies the bridge Gjallarbrú, which the dead must cross to reach Hel. It is guarded by Móðguðr, and it was over this bridge that Hermóðr rode on his quest to retrieve Baldr from the land of the dead. The tale makes clear that even a living visitor could not avoid that crossing.

Gjöll is also the name of the boulder to which the monstrous wolf Fenrir was bound. Scholars compare this river with similar waterways in other Indo-European cultures, such as the Greek Styx and the Hindu Vaitarani, which likewise separate the world of the living from that of the dead. The name has been translated as 'noisy'.

Ancient sources: The main source is the Gylfaginning of Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, which lists the eleven Élivágar rivers.

Trivia

A noisy name. The name has been translated as 'noisy', possibly a reference to the roar of its waters.
A river, a bridge and a rock. In the tale, the name Gjöll refers to the river, to the bridge Gjallarbrú that spans it, and to the rock where Fenrir was bound.
