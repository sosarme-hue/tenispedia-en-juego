Andean god of wind and rain

Kon

Kon is a primordial god of wind and rain in Andean tradition, first venerated by the Paracas and Nazca cultures and later absorbed into the Inca pantheon. He matters because he represents one of the oldest creator figures of the Peruvian coast.

The myth

According to the tradition, Kon emerged from the waters of the sea and moved from the north toward the south, creating the world, the heavenly bodies and the first humans, whom he asked only for worship. When people forgot him, he punished them by withholding the rains, and the fertile lands turned into coastal deserts.

Then Pachacámac, also a son of the Sun, arrived from the south, fought him in a violent battle and banished him. Some versions say Kon vanished into the sky and that Pachacámac transformed his creatures into animals.

Worship and religion

The Paracas and Nazca peoples honored him with ritual dances, and some scholars link him to the geoglyph known as “The Astronaut”. His worship does not survive today.

Legacy

Thor Heyerdahl named his 1947 raft expedition “Kon-Tiki”, and a crater on Saturn’s moon Rhea bears his name.

Trivia

The eyed god. In Paracas and Nazca depictions he appears with a prominent head and large eyes, which is why he is called the “eyed god”.
A crater on Ceres. The dwarf planet Ceres has a crater named Coniraya, a name related to Kon.
