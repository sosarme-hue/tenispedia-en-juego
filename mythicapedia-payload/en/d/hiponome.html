daughter of Menoeceus and wife of Alcaeus in Greek myth

Hipponome

Hipponome is, in Greek mythology, a daughter of Menoeceus of Thebes who appears as the wife of Alcaeus and mother of Amphitryon. Her importance is mainly genealogical, since her name appears only in one variant of Amphitryon's ancestry.

The myth

According to the tradition, Hipponome was a daughter of Menoeceus of Thebes and therefore a sister of Creon and Jocasta, although she does not appear in the usual Theban genealogy. She married Alcaeus, son of Perseus and Andromeda, and had by him Amphitryon and the daughters Anaxo and Perimede.

Pseudo-Apollodorus, in The Library, records this version as one alternative. Other accounts gave the same children to Astydamia, daughter of Pelops, or to Laonome, daughter of Guneus. Pausanias, when describing Pheneus, also makes Laonome the mother of Amphitryon, so Hipponome's name survives as a minority variant.

Ancient sources: The Library attributed to Apollodorus and Pausanias's Description of Greece, which mention her as a genealogical variant.

Trivia

Meaningful name. Her Greek name, Hipponomes, is usually rendered as horse-driver, a meaning that reflects the sense of her name.
A mother with several names. Depending on the version, Amphitryon's mother could be Hipponome, Astydamia or Laonome, showing that ancient authors did not agree.
