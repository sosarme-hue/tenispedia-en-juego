king of Mycenae, son of Perseus and father of Alcmene

Electryon

Electryon is a king of Mycenae or Midea in Greek mythology, belonging to the Perseid house descended from Perseus. He matters chiefly as the father of Alcmene, mother of the hero Heracles, and for the cattle feud that led to his death and to the exile of Amphitryon.

The myth

According to tradition, Electryon was one of the sons Perseus had with Andromeda, and his line was tied to that of Pelops through marriage. The sources disagree about his wife: the Catalogue of Women gives him Lysidice, daughter of Pelops, while the Library names Anaxo, daughter of Alcaeus, and another source names Eurydice, also a daughter of Pelops.

His daughter Alcmene bore Heracles after Zeus visited her following the deaths of her brothers. Electryon also had many sons, among them Licymnius, born to a Phrygian woman named Midea.

The central episode arose from a family quarrel. The six sons of Pterelaus, king of the Taphians and descendants of Electryon's brother Mestor, claimed a share of the kingdom of Mycenae. When refused, they stole his cattle, and Electryon's sons fought them. Nearly all died in the battle, except Licymnius and Everes. Amphitryon, Electryon's nephew and promised husband of Alcmene, recovered the cattle, but accidentally killed his uncle by throwing a club at one of the cows. An older tradition says he killed him in anger over the cattle. Electryon's brother Sthenelus then seized the throne, charged Amphitryon with murder, and exiled him.

Ancient sources: Hesiod mentions him in the Catalogue of Women, and the Library of Apollodorus and Apollonius of Rhodes record different versions of his story.
