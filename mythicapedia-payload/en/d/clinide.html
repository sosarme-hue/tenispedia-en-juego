Babylonian devotee of Apollo, turned into a bird

Clinis

Clinis is a wealthy and pious Babylonian in Greek mythology who, together with his family, was transformed into birds by the gods after a botched sacrifice to Apollo. His story is one of the few Greek tales set in Babylon.

The myth

According to Antoninus Liberalis, Clinis honoured Apollo and Artemis with great devotion, and the two gods repaid him with their favour. Apollo took him to the land of the Hyperboreans, where he saw donkeys sacrificed to the god. Back in Babylon, Clinis wanted to copy the rite, but the god warned him that such offerings were only acceptable from Hyperboreans.

His children disagreed: Lycius and Harpasus insisted on sacrificing the donkeys, while Ortygius and Artemiche urged obedience to Apollo. The first two drove the donkeys to the altar, the god drove them mad, and they attacked the family. The gods took pity and turned them into birds: Clinis into a hypaietos, Lycius into a white raven, Artemiche into a lark and Ortygius into a tit, for having advised sacrificing goats.

Ancient sources: The story survives only in Antoninus Liberalis' Metamorphoses, which cites the lost works Boeus' Ornithology and Symmias of Rhodes' Apollo as its sources.

Trivia

A raven that turned black. According to the story, Lycius was turned into a white raven, which later became black after the incident involving Coronis.
A tit for a piece of advice. Ortygius was turned into a tit because he had advised his father to sacrifice goats instead of donkeys.
