Aztec god of the arts and mischief

Huehuecoyotl

Huehuecoyotl is the Aztec god of music, dance, song and mischief. His name means "very old coyote", which links him to the trickster Coyote of the North American tribes.

The myth

In Aztec mythology, Huehuecoyotl belongs to the family of Tezcatlipoca, from whom he inherited the power to shapeshift. Although he usually appears as a man, he can change gender. He was a benign prankster whose tricks were often aimed at other gods or at humans, but tended to backfire on him. A great party-giver, he was said to stir up wars among people to relieve his boredom.

Those who received bad omens from other gods sometimes appealed to him to soften or reverse their fates. His partners were many: Temazcalteci, goddess of the temazcal steam baths; Xochiquetzal, goddess of love, beauty and flowers; and the male deities Opochtli and Xochipilli. He embodies the duality of good and evil, old and new, worldly and spiritual.

Worship and religion

In the calendar, the day Cuetzpallin, whose sign is a lizard, belonged to him, and the fourth trecena was also associated with him. The Codex Borbonicus shows him as a coyote dancing with human hands and feet, accompanied by cymbals. The sources do not describe temples or festivals dedicated to him.

Legacy

He appears in modern fiction: he inspires the poet king Nezahualcoyotl in J. D. Abrego's surrealist story "Teyolitectiliztli", and he appears as an androgynous long-haired figure in the anime Midnight Occultic Civil Servants. A Mexican ecovillage bears his name.

Ancient sources: The Codex Borbonicus depicts him, and the Codex Telleriano-Remensis preserves stories of his pranks.

Trivia

A respected old coyote. In Aztec culture the coyote symbolized cunning, worldly wisdom and male beauty, so the nickname "old coyote" carried a positive meaning.
A shared trickster. His pranks recall the trickster Coyote of North American peoples, with whom he also shares storytelling and choral singing.
