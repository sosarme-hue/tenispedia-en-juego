Naga princess of Hindu myth

Ulupi

Ulupi is a Naga princess of Hindu tradition, daughter of King Kauravya and second wife of Arjuna. She appears in the Mahabharata and in the Vishnu and Bhagavata Puranas, and is best remembered for restoring Arjuna to life after a curse.

The myth

According to tradition, Ulupi was the daughter of Kauravya, king of the Nagas, who ruled a kingdom of serpents beneath the waters of the Ganges. Half woman and half serpent, she was also a skilled warrior. Before meeting Arjuna she had been widowed, as her first husband died fighting Suparna.

During the twelve-year exile that Arjuna undertook as penance, Ulupi pulled him into the Ganges while he was bathing and carried him to her father's kingdom. There, after he made offerings to the sacred fire, Arjuna declined her proposal, citing his vow of celibacy. She argued that the vow applied only to Draupadi, and he agreed to marry her. Their son Iravan was born, and Ulupi granted Arjuna the power to defeat any amphibious creature.

When the Vasus cursed Arjuna for killing Bhishma by treachery, Ulupi asked her father for help, and he won from the goddess Ganga a remedy: Arjuna would die at the hands of his son Babhruvahana and could be revived by placing the gem Nagamani on his chest. Ulupi urged Babhruvahana to challenge his father when Arjuna arrived in Manipura with the horse for the Ashvamedha sacrifice. Arjuna fell to an arrow from his son, who, filled with remorse, wanted to take his own life. Ulupi stopped him, fetched the gem, and restored Arjuna's life.

With the onset of the Kali Yuga, the Pandavas handed the throne to Parikshit, Arjuna's grandson, and set off for the Himalayas with a dog. Ulupi returned to her kingdom in the Ganges.

Ancient sources: Mahabharata, Vishnu Purana and Bhagavata Purana.

Trivia

Many names. The Mahabharata gives her numerous names, such as Pannagī or Uragātmajā, which refer to her serpent origin.
Part reptile. Wm. Michael Mott describes her as partly reptilian, with a lower body resembling that of a snake or a crocodile.
