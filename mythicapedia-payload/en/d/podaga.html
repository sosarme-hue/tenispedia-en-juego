Polabian Slavic deity

Podaga

Podaga was a deity of the Polabians, a Slavic people of northeastern Germany, whose statue stood in a temple at Plön. He appears only in Helmold's Chronicle, which describes neither his appearance nor his role, and his name remains debated.

The myth

The sources say almost nothing about Podaga: there are no tales of his origin, family or deeds. Helmold mentions him only as an idol, without describing his appearance or function.

The real discussion centres on his name. Some scholars identify him with Dazhbog; others link it to the Slavic root *dag- 'to burn' or to the Polish word potęga, 'power', which would make the god a deified might.

Worship and religion

His statue stood in a temple at Plön. According to Henryk Łowmiański, the cult was promoted by prince Kruto rather than by the tribe, since creating a separate deity and temple required resources a small territory could hardly muster. No records survive of festivals or rites, and no trace of his worship remains today.

Legacy

His name has fed a philological debate among specialists, with no agreement on its original form. Michał Łuczyński proposes that Potąg was first an epithet of Perun before becoming an independent theonym.

Ancient sources: Helmold's Chronicle, the only ancient source that mentions him.

Trivia

A name hard to read. According to Aleksander Brückner, the name Podaga cannot be read with certainty as Helmold wrote it.
A male god?. Brückner noted that had Helmold meant a goddess he would have written dea rather than idolum, which suggests the deity was male.
