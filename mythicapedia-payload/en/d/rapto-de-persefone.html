Greek myth of Persephone's abduction by Hades

Rape of Persephone

The Rape of Persephone is the episode in which Hades carries off Demeter's daughter to the Underworld. It is one of the Greek myths most often represented in Western painting and sculpture.

The myth

Persephone is the daughter of Zeus and Demeter. Hades wanted to make her his wife, so he obtained permission from her father, Zeus, and help from Gaia to take her into the Underworld. While she was picking flowers in a field, Hades emerged in his chariot from a crack in the earth and carried her off against her will.

Only Hecate and Helios witnessed the abduction, and they were the ones who later told Demeter what had happened.

Legacy

The episode has been depicted in very different works: a mural in the Macedonian Tomb I at Vergina, from the mid-4th century BC; a Roman sculpture from the Chiragan villa; a large Baroque marble group by Bernini, carved between 1621 and 1622; and paintings attributed to Rubens, Rembrandt, Allori and Giordano.

Trivia

A misleading translation. The English word rape translates the Latin for «seized» or «carried off», and in this context it refers to the kidnapping of a bride, not to the sexual violence that may have followed.
