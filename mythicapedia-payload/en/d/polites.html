Trojan prince, son of Priam

Polites

Polites was a Trojan prince, son of Priam and Hecuba, known for his swiftness. His death at the hands of Neoptolemus, son of Achilles, is one of the final episodes of the Trojan War.

The myth

Born into the royal house of Troy, Polites grew up among his many siblings, who according to the accounts numbered 49 brothers and 12 sisters. He appears in the Iliad as a minor character: he runs swiftly, acts as a scout and comes to the aid of his brother Deiphobus in battle. As the city fell, he tried to flee towards Latium, but Neoptolemus struck him in the leg with an arrow. Wounded, he dragged himself to his father's palace and died at Priam's feet, and Priam was also killed by Neoptolemus.

Other accounts give a different ending. According to Cato, Polites survived the war and accompanied Aeneas to Italy, where he supposedly founded a city called Politorium. Virgil's Aeneid remembers him as the father of a son named Priam.

Legacy

The asteroid 4867 Polites is named after him.

Ancient sources: Homer's Iliad mentions him as a scout and warrior, and Virgil's Aeneid names him as the father of a son called Priam.

Trivia
