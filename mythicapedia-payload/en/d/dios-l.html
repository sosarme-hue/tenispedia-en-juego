Maya god of trade and the underworld

God L

God L is one of the major pre-Spanish Maya deities, associated above all with trade and travelling merchants. The codices show him as an aged figure with jaguar traits, a feathered hat topped by an owl, and a jaguar mantle.

The myth

In the codices and on pottery he appears as an old man with an owl hat, mantle and staff, often surrounded by merchandise. His attributes tie him to wealth: he carries bundles of goods, and the floating ends of his cloth show footsteps, a sign of merchants on the move. Some scenes link him to cacao orchards and to young women, suggesting that his wealth extends to them as well.

His nocturnal side appears in the cigar he smokes, the owl on his hat, pointing to the underworld and night, and in his jaguar traits. Several scenes connect him with war and with Venus rising from the underworld, hurling spears at his victims. He has been interpreted as one of the principal lords of Xibalba, drawing on the Hero Twins story of the Popol Vuh.

Worship and religion

The Dresden Codex shows him in end-of-year rituals, when the acantun stone shafts were draped with his mantle during the unlucky days known as wayeb. He has been suggested to correspond to Maximón, the Tz'utujil 'Grandfather' venerated during Holy Week, a figure still present in contemporary Maya religion.

Ancient sources: He appears mainly in pre-Spanish codices, such as the Dresden and Madrid codices, and on the Princeton vase.
