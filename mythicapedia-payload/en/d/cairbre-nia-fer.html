legendary king of Tara in Irish mythology

Cairbre Nia Fer

A legendary king of Tara in medieval Irish tradition and father of Erc. His death at the hands of Cú Chulainn in the Battle of Rosnaree is one of the episodes of the Ulster Cycle.

The myth

According to Irish tradition, Cairbre was the son of Rus Ruad and the brother of Ailill mac Máta, king of Connacht. Married to Fedelm Noíchrothach, daughter of King Conchobar, he had a son, Erc, and a daughter, Achall. Medieval sources do not agree on his chronology: the Lebor Gabála Érenn places him in the time of the emperor Augustus.

In the Battle of Rosnaree, Cairbre and his brother Find mac Rossa fought against Conchobar and the Ulaid. Cú Chulainn killed him from a distance with a spear and cut off his head before his body touched the ground. Afterwards, Erc swore allegiance to Conchobar and took the throne of Tara. Later, after avenging Cú Chulainn, Conall Cernach brought Erc's head to Tara, and Achall died of grief for her brother.

Ancient sources: He appears in the Book of Armagh, in the annotations to the Amra Choluim Chille, in the Lebor Gabála Érenn and in the stories of the Ulster Cycle, such as Cath Ruis na Ríg.

Trivia

A giant brought back to life. In a Latin text in the Book of Armagh, Saint Patrick raises from the dead a giant who says he was killed during Cairbre's reign.
Erc's head. After avenging Cú Chulainn, Conall Cernach brought Erc's head to Tara, where his sister Achall died of grief.
