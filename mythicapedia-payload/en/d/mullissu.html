Assyrian goddess, consort of Asshur

Mullissu

Mullissu is an Assyrian goddess of Mesopotamia, the consort of the god Asshur, the chief deity of Assur. Her figure is linked to Ninlil, wife of Enlil, and to Ishtar of Nineveh, which shows how the great goddesses of the region came to overlap.

The myth

Texts present her mainly as the partner of Asshur, sharing with him the role of chief god of the Assyrian pantheon. Some scholars think Mullissu may be identical with Ninlil, which would fit the fact that Asshur himself was modeled on Enlil. The sources offer no detailed stories of her deeds or family life beyond that union.

In the Neo-Assyrian period her name was identified with Ishtar of Nineveh. In Sumerian tradition, Mulliltum appeared as an epithet of Ninlil, and the Babylonian form Mulliltu or Mullitta may lie behind the name Herodotus recorded as Mylitta, whom he equated with Aphrodite. This link remains a proposal among scholars rather than a settled fact.

Worship and religion

Sources mention cults connected with Nippur and with Kish, and in late Babylonia her worship survived under the form Mulit. There is no reliable evidence about particular temples, festivals or rites devoted to her.

Ancient sources: The Sfire inscription, written in Old Aramaic in the eighth century BCE, where she appears as the consort of Asshur, and the Mandaic magical corpus of late antiquity, which preserves her late Babylonian form.

Trivia

A goddess with many names. Her name appears in different forms depending on language and period, such as Mullissu, Mulliltum or Mulit, which makes her harder to trace through the texts.
An Aramaic inscription. The Sfire inscription, written in Old Aramaic in the eighth century BCE, names her beside Asshur, showing that her worship was known beyond Assyria.
