poet of the Tuatha Dé Danann

Abhean

Abhean is a poet of the Tuatha Dé Danann, the divine people of Irish mythology, and in particular a poet of the god Lugh. His name is linked to poetry and perhaps to the act of strumming a harp.

The myth

According to tradition, he was the son of Bec-Felmas. The best-known account of his life is brief: Óengus killed him in front of Midir. This is told in a poem by Fland Mainistreach included in the Lebor Gabála Érenn, the great medieval compilation of Ireland's legendary history and its successive invasions.

Almost everything we know about Abhean comes from that poem, so his deeds and his end remain shrouded in a silence that modern readers cannot fill. Few details survive beyond this brief scene. He is therefore a minor figure, though well defined in his craft.

Ancient sources: Lebor Gabála Érenn, in a poem by Fland Mainistreach.

Trivia

A name with a harp echo. Reconstructed Proto-Celtic lexica at the universities of Leiden and Wales suggest the name may derive from Ad-bej-ānos, possibly meaning something like 'related to striking', perhaps alluding to harp-strumming.
