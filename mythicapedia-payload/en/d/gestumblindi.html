legendary Norse chieftain disguised as Odin

Gestumblindi

Gestumblindi is a legendary chieftain of Norse tradition, the protagonist of the Hervarar saga and of Saxo's Gesta Danorum. Behind his figure hides, in fact, Odin himself.

The myth

According to the Hervarar saga, this powerful chieftain refused to pay tribute to King Heidrek. Faced with the threat of imprisonment, he offered a sacrifice to Odin, and a stranger with the very same name appeared at his farm. After swapping clothes, the newcomer went to the court and challenged the king with riddles, until Heidrek solved them all and posed Odin's question to Vafthrudnir: what did he whisper into Balder's ear before the cremation?

On realising that his rival was the god, Heidrek attacked him with the sword Tyrfing. Odin fled as a hawk and the blow cut off its tail, which the saga gives as the reason hawks have short tails. In Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, Gestiblindus is a king of the Geats who places himself under the protection of the Danish king Frodi.

Legacy

In Scandinavian folklore he survives as Gest Blinde in later folk tales. His name, meaning blind guest, is linked to Odin's wandering nature, as the god often travels disguised as a visitor.

Ancient sources: The medieval texts Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks and Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum.

Trivia

A name with a double meaning. Gestumblindi can be read as gestr blindi, meaning both Gestr the Blind and blind guest. This double reading fits the one-eyed Odin who visits the worlds disguised as a stranger.
The hawk's tail. The saga uses this episode to explain why the hawk has a short tail: Heidrek's sword cut it off as Odin fled.
