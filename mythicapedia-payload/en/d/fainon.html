youth of Greek myth linked to Saturn

Phaenon

Phaenon is a youth in Greek mythology, created by Prometheus and linked to the star of Cronus, that is, the planet Saturn. His name, meaning 'bright' or 'shining', was sometimes used poetically to refer to that planet.

The myth

According to the story, Prometheus shaped the human race and made Phaenon the most beautiful of all the youths. Prometheus meant to keep him back and not present him to Zeus, as he had done with the others. Eros reported this to Zeus, and Zeus sent Hermes, who persuaded Phaenon to come before the god and become immortal. For this reason he was placed among the wandering stars, that is, the planets.

Hyginus, who draws on a tradition from Heraclides Ponticus, says that Phaenon was transformed into the star of Jupiter. Other authors identify him with Phaethon, a name that Hyginus, citing Eratosthenes, gives as another name for the Sun, the character's father.

Legacy

His name survives mainly as a poetic way of referring to Saturn.

Ancient sources: Heraclides Ponticus, as transmitted by Hyginus, and Eratosthenes, also cited by Hyginus, are the sources that relate his variants.

Trivia

A luminous name. Phaenon means 'bright' or 'shining', a fitting word for a star visible to the naked eye.
Confusion with Phaethon. Some authors confuse him with Phaethon. Hyginus records that this name is another name for the Sun itself.
