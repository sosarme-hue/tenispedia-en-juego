Norse goddess of healing and medicine

Eir

Eir is a figure of Norse mythology linked to medicine and healing, whom the sources describe either as a goddess or as a valkyrie. She is interesting because she barely appears in the texts, and scholars debate whether she is one figure or several.

The myth

The sources present Eir above all as a skilled physician. In Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, in chapter 35 of Gylfaginning, she is named third among the goddesses (ásynjur) and described as an extremely good physician, with no further detail.

In the Poetic Edda poem Fjölsvinnsmál she appears among the maidens who attend Menglöð, all seated on the hill Lyfjaberg, the 'hill of healing'. There the hero Svipdagr speaks with Fjölsviðr, who mentions Eir among them. In Skáldskaparmál, however, Eir appears as a valkyrie rather than among the ásynjur, and the thulur also list her as a valkyrie.

Legacy

The name Eir was used in skaldic poetry as a kenning for women, as in Eir aura, 'Eir of riches', found in Gísla saga. It also appears in a runic inscription from Bergen, Norway, from around 1300.

Ancient sources: Poetic Edda (Fjölsvinnsmál), Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda (Gylfaginning and Skáldskaparmál), skaldic poetry and the Bergen runic inscription.

Trivia

A goddess compared to Hygieia. Some scholars propose that Eir is the Norse counterpart of the Greek goddess Hygieia, associated with health.
A verse on a Bergen stick. A runic inscription from Bergen, from around 1300, uses the name Eir to mean a woman, in a verse written by a displeased scribe.
