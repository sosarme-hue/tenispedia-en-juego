nymph of Roman mythology, mother of Latinus

Marica

Marica is a nymph of Roman mythology, linked to the area of Minturnae in Latium, and in some traditions the mother of King Latinus. She matters because through her son she is placed at the origin of the Latin people.

The myth

In Virgil's Aeneid, King Latinus, now old, ruled the fields and cities of Latium in peace. He was said to have been born of Faunus and the Laurentine nymph Marica. Since Faunus was the son of Picus, and Picus boasted of having Saturn as his father, the poet makes Saturn the founder of this line.

Other authors connect her with other figures. Several Roman writers identify her with a form of Diana or Venus, so her personality is not fixed in a single profile. The surviving sources do not describe her end or any deeds of her own in detail; her role centres mainly on the genealogy.

Worship and religion

Near Minturnae there was a sacred grove dedicated to Marica. Livy records that on one occasion lightning struck this grove, as well as the temple of Jupiter and a city wall, and that the inhabitants read it as a portent. A nearby lake also bore her name. The sources do not say that her cult survives today.

Legacy

The consulted sources show no clear presence of Marica in later art or literature. Her memory survives mainly in the name of the lake that bears her name near Minturnae.

Ancient sources: Virgil mentions her in the Aeneid as the mother of Latinus, and Livy, in his History of Rome, links her to the sacred grove at Minturnae when he recounts the city's portents.

Trivia

Omens at Minturnae. Livy reports that several buildings in the city, including Marica's sacred grove, were struck by lightning, and that the inhabitants added an alleged trickle of blood at a gate to make the horror greater.
A lake with her name. According to the sources, a lake near Minturnae was also named after Marica.
