king of the Rutuli in Roman mythology

Turnus

Turnus is, in Roman mythology, the king of the Rutuli, a people of Latium, and the main antagonist of Aeneas in Virgil's Aeneid. His story matters because he embodies resistance to a destiny the poem reserves for the Trojans.

The myth

According to the tradition, Turnus was the son of Daunus and the nymph Venilia, and brother of the nymph Juturna. Because of his lineage he was the natural suitor of Lavinia, daughter of King Latinus, until an oracle foretold that the princess must marry a stranger arriving from the sea, Aeneas. Moved by Juno and by the Fury Allecto, Turnus pressed Latinus to declare war, and ended up leading a rebellion with many Italic allies.

In the fighting, Turnus stood out for his courage and his hot temper. He killed Pallas, son of Evander, and took his sword belt as a trophy. Aeneas pursued him out of a desire for revenge and, after a duel in which Turnus's sword broke, wounded him in the thigh. Turnus begged to be spared or to have his body returned; Aeneas hesitated, but on seeing Pallas's belt on him, he killed him. The poem ends with his soul descending, angry, to the realm of shadows.

Legacy

Later traditions linked him to the city of Tours: the Historia Brittonum presents him as the founder of a city of the Turones. In the medieval poem Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, an unnamed "Ticius" appears as a parallel to Brutus, and scholar Otis Chapman suggests this is a scribal error for Turnus. Milton mentions him in Book IX of Paradise Lost. Barry Powell reads him as a "new Achilles" and as a symbol of the peoples who had to submit to Rome.

Ancient sources: He appears in Virgil's Aeneid; before it, Cato mentions him in his Origines, and later Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus do too.

Trivia

A different name. Dionysius of Halicarnassus uses the name "Tyrrhenus" instead of Turnus, and makes him a nephew of Amata, Latinus's wife.
An ancestor in Tours. The Historia Brittonum says that Turnus, exiled after his death, founded a city of the Turones among the Gauls, called Turnis, identified with Tours.
