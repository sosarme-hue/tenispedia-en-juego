Polish spirit of the drowned

Topielec

The topielec is a Polish water spirit of Slavic folklore, believed to be the soul of a person who drowned and who remains bound to the very water where they died. It belongs to the family of Slavic water beings that includes the Czech and Slovak vodník.

The myth

According to Polish rural tradition, the topielec is the soul of a drowned person who stays tied to the water where they died. From there it lures travelers into swamps and lakes and also kills the animals that come near still waters.

Popular tales from the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries place it in the everyday life of the peasantry, who swapped stories and legends about it. Its female form, the topielica, appears in the same narratives.

Legacy

Władysław Reymont mentions it in The Peasants (Chłopi), the novel for which he won the Nobel Prize. In recent Polish popular culture it appears in the comic series Lil i Put, where a utopiec lives in a pond beside the protagonists' village and is never seen. The drowned monster of the video game Minecraft is called utopiec in its official Polish translation.

Trivia

Minecraft translation. The Drowned monster in the video game Minecraft has the official Polish translation utopiec, the same name used for these spirits.
A legend in The Peasants. Reymont includes a legend about the topielica in chapter ten of book two of The Peasants, during a gathering where the characters exchange stories.
