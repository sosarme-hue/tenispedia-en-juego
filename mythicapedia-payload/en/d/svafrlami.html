legendary Norse king, first owner of Tyrfing

Svafrlami

Svafrlami is a legendary king of Norse tradition, ruler of Garðaríki and first wielder of the magic sword Tyrfing. His story, preserved in the Hervarar saga, explains the origin of one of the most famous weapons in Germanic myth and the curse attached to it.

The myth

According to the H and U versions of the Hervarar saga, Svafrlami was a son of King Sigrlami, who was himself a son of Odin. In the R version, however, the king is called Sigrlami and his parentage is not given.

As the tale goes, while hunting on horseback the king came upon two dwarves, Dvalinn and Durinn, beside a large stone. He held them by swinging his sword over their heads so they could not vanish, and they offered to forge him a weapon in exchange for their freedom. The sword they made would never break or rust, could cut through iron and stone as easily as cloth, and would always grant victory. It was named Tyrfing. As the dwarves vanished into the rock, they cursed it: once drawn, it must kill a man, and it would bring three evil deeds that would be the king's undoing.

Svafrlami met the berserker Arngrim in combat. In the H and U versions, Tyrfing split Arngrim's shield and sank into the ground; Arngrim then cut off the king's hand and slew him with his own sword. He afterwards forced Svafrlami's daughter Eyfura to marry him. In the R version, Arngrim was a war-chief serving the old king and received Tyrfing and Eyfura as rewards for his loyalty.

Legacy

Svafrlami mainly serves a narrative role. His story is the starting point of the legend of Tyrfing, which continues with Arngrim.

Ancient sources: The Hervarar saga, in its H, U and R versions, is the source that recounts his story.
