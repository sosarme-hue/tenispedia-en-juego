Egyptian solar god of midday

Ra-Horakhty

Ra-Horakhty, also written Ra-Harakhte or Re-Horakhty, is an Egyptian sun god born from the fusion of Ra with Horakhty, the Horus of the horizon. He matters because he expresses the sun at its highest point and enhances the power and majesty of Ra.

The myth

According to Heliopolitan theology, Ra-Horakhty represents the sun at its zenith, standing between two moments of the day. At dawn appears Khepri, the new sun, and at evening Atum bids farewell as the old sun sets. Ra-Horakhty occupies the middle moment, when the star reigns over the earth in full glory.

In iconography he is shown as Ra at midday. The usual form is a man with red skin, standing or seated, with a falcon's head crowned by the solar disc surrounded by a uraeus. He also appears with a ram's head, a lion's head or a cat's head, and even with a falcon's head and ram's horns. Under this name, Ra is described as the sun god who rises in the east, and at Edfu he was considered identical to the god Horus.

Worship and religion

His fusion with Horus appears around 2445 BC, in the Fifth Dynasty, in the sun temple of Niuserra at Abusir, at a time when Heliopolitan theology sought to distinguish the solar triad of Khepri, Ra-Horakhty and Atum. He was also worshipped in the temples of Wadi es-Sebua, Amada, Derr and the Great Temple of Abu Simbel, built by Ramesses II. His form also appears in several hymns and sacred prayers. The sources do not say whether his cult survives today.

Legacy

Akhenaten called himself 'High Priest of Ra-Horakhty' and, at the start of his reign, still depicted the god in his traditional form. Later, in the first 'doctrinal name' of the god Aten, he described him as the Horus of the Horizon who rejoices in the Land of Light, showing how this deity entered the religious politics of the pharaoh.
