jötunn of Norse mythology, brother of Suttung

Baugi

Baugi is a jötunn in Norse mythology, brother of the giant Suttung, and appears in the story of how Odin obtained the mead of poetry. His role is minor, but his trickery and his drill are central to the tale.

The myth

In Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál, the mead of poetry was in the possession of Suttung, who had taken it from Fjalar and Galar, the men who had killed his father, Gilling. Baugi, Suttung's brother, owned nine slaves, whom Odin caused to kill one another. To make amends, Odin, calling himself Bölverk ('evil-deed'), offered to do the work of all nine for a summer in exchange for one drink of the mead.

When payment came due, Suttung refused to give up a single drop. Bölverk then asked Baugi to help him bore into the Hnitbjörg mountain with Baugi's drill, Rati, to reach the chamber where Gunnlöð, Suttung's daughter, guarded the mead. When Baugi said the tunnel was finished, Odin blew into the hole and felt that the far end was still blocked, so he realised Baugi was trying to deceive him. Odin turned himself into a snake and slipped through the hole, while Baugi struck at him with the auger and missed.

The sources do not fully agree on the details: in another version, Baugi is a farmer for whom Odin works an entire summer, and it is Gunnlöð who grants him three sips. Baugi is absent from the Hávamál, the source Snorri used for the story, which has led some scholars to suspect that he was an invention or a misreading by Snorri himself.

Ancient sources: He appears in Snorri Sturluson's Skáldskaparmál and in the þulur, but not in the Hávamál.

Trivia

Meaning of his name. His Old Norse name has been translated as 'ring-shaped'.
Absent from a key text. Baugi does not appear in the Hávamál, the source Snorri used for the mead story, and is only attested in the Skáldskaparmál and the þulur.
