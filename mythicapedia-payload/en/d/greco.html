eponymous hero of the Greeks in Greek mythology

Graecus

Graecus is the eponymous hero who gave his name to the Graecians, one of the Hellenic tribes, and by extension to the Greeks. His figure links the origin of the people to Zeus and Pandora.

The myth

According to Hesiod, in the Catalogue of Women, Graecus was born to Pandora, daughter of Deucalion, who had joined Zeus in her father's palace. He is described as a steadfast warrior. John the Lydian quotes the passage and links him to Latinus, whom the same poem presents as a son of Circe and Odysseus, in order to make Greeks and Latins brothers.

Stephanus of Byzantium, by contrast, makes him a son of Thessalus, and adds that the river Granicus took its name from him. According to West, the Graecians were later joined genealogically to the Hellenes of Phthia, since they descended from the daughters of Deucalion, while Hellen, eponym of the Hellenes, was a son of Zeus.

Legacy

The Latin name Graeci comes from this tribe: the Romans used it for all the Hellenes, and from it the Latin and later European forms for the Greeks derive. Magna Graecia also owes its name to the Graecians, according to contemporary authors.

Ancient sources: Hesiod (Catalogue of Women, quoted by John the Lydian), with Alcman and Sophocles as witnesses to the "mothers of the Hellenes"; Pliny and Stephanus of Byzantium also mention him.

Trivia

A river named after him. Stephanus of Byzantium links the river Granicus to Graecus, whom he presents as a son of Thessalus.
Magna Graecia. Contemporary authors hold that the Graecians were among the first tribes to colonize Italy, in the area later known as Magna Graecia.
