Rabbit (Chinese zodiac)

The Rabbit, also called the Hare, is the fourth animal in the twelve-year cycle of the Chinese zodiac. It is linked to grace, good manners, sound advice, kindness and sensitivity, and is regarded as a symbol of longevity.

The myth

The Chinese zodiac arranges the years in a cycle of twelve animals, and the Rabbit holds fourth place. In Chinese tradition it is associated with the Earthly Branch 卯 (mǎo), with the element Wood in the theory of the five elements and, within Traditional Chinese medicine, with Liver Yin and with the emotions and virtues of kindness and hope.

The available sources do not preserve a fixed story explaining how the rabbit came to occupy its place in the cycle, so it is best understood as a symbol rather than the hero of a tale. People born in a Year of the Rabbit are described as carrying its gentle qualities, and each of these years is also paired with one of the five elements.

A striking detail is that the Chinese word 兔 (tù) covers both rabbits and hares. When the zodiac was devised, the hare was the only leporid in China, since rabbits did not arrive there until the sixteenth century.

Legacy

In popular Chinese astrology the Rabbit is one of the most recognisable signs, and its year returns every twelve years. According to this tradition, its ideal partners are the Goat, the Dog and the Pig, while the Rooster and the Dragon are considered poor matches. In neighbouring traditions the animal changes: the Vietnamese zodiac puts the cat in its place, and the Malay zodiac uses the mousedeer.

Trivia

Hares and rabbits. Both are called 兔 in Chinese, although the zodiac was devised when the hare was the only leporid known in China.
A late arrival. Rabbits were not introduced to China until the sixteenth century.
A different animal by country. In the Vietnamese zodiac, the cat takes the place of the rabbit.
