Hindu king who brought the Ganges to Earth

Bhagiratha

Bhagiratha is a legendary king of the Ikshvaku dynasty in Hindu tradition, best known for bringing the sacred river Ganges, personified as the goddess Ganga, down from heaven to the earth. His feat gave rise to the name of the river's upper course and to the phrase bhagiratha-prayatna, meaning "Bhagiratha's labour". The myth begins with the death of the 60,000 sons of his great-grandfather King Sagara, reduced to ash by the fiery gaze of the sage Kapila. To give them funeral rites, Bhagiratha needed the Ganges, and he undertook a long penance until Shiva agreed to receive the river's fall in his hair.

The myth

According to the tale, King Sagara performed the ashvamedha sacrifice, but Indra stole the horse and hid it in Patala, where the sage Kapila was meditating. Sagara's sons found it and, disturbing the sage with their noise, were burned to ash. Without funeral rites their souls wandered, and the duty passed from generation to generation until it fell to Bhagiratha.

After ascending the throne of Ayodhya, Bhagiratha went to the Himalayas to perform austerities. Ganga warned him that her fall would be too violent for the earth and asked him to seek the favour of Shiva. The king performed a penance at Kailasa, and Shiva caught the river in his matted hair, letting it flow for a thousand years before releasing a single drop onto the plain. Bhagiratha then guided the stream to Patala, where its waters purified his ancestors' ashes.

On the way, Ganga flooded the hermitage of the sage Jahnu, who angrily swallowed the river. Only at Bhagiratha's pleading did he release it through his ear, which gave the goddess the epithet Jahnavi. Back on the throne, the king ruled in prosperity and is said to have gifted hundreds of thousands of cows.

Worship and religion

The Ganges is a sacred river for millions of Hindus, and ritual bathing in its waters remains a living practice. On Sagar Island, where the myth places Kapila's hermitage, the Ganga Sagar Mela is held every year with ritual baths on Makara Sankranti. In Nepal, Bhagiratha is sculpted beneath the spouts of traditional stone fountains, and in some Indian temples his figure appears below the gargoyles.

Legacy

Bhagiratha also appears in Bengali literature, where he is born in an unusual way to two mothers, a story found in the Krittivasi Ramayan and other Bengali texts. His name has been given to the stream that forms the upper course of the Ganges.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, the Ramayana and several Puranas, such as the Narada Purana, recount his story.

Trivia

Epithet of the Ganges. The goddess Ganga is called Jahnavi because the sage Jahnu let the river out through his ear.
Two mothers. Some Bengali texts say Bhagiratha was born to two of King Dilipa's widows, and that he was deformed until the sage Ashtavakra transformed him.
