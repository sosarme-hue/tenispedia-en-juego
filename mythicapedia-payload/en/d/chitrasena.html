Gandharva king in the Mahabharata

Chitrasena

Chitrasena is a Gandharva king, a celestial musician and dancer, in Hindu mythology. He appears mainly in the Mahabharata, where he is known for his mastery of music and dance and for his martial skill.

The myth

According to the Mahabharata, Chitrasena was the son of the Gandharva Vishvavasu and served at the courts of Indra and Kubera. During the Pandavas' exile, Indra invited Arjuna to his heavenly abode, where Chitrasena taught him music and dance. In a version the Critical Edition rejects, the apsara Urvashi made advances on Arjuna; when he refused, she cursed him to live as a eunuch for a time, and Chitrasena helped soften the curse by relaying a message from Indra to console him.

In another episode, Duryodhana wanted to show off his wealth before the Pandavas and had pleasure houses built by a lake in the Dvaitavana forest. Chitrasena claimed the lakeside for himself and forbade entry. When Duryodhana's men challenged him, Chitrasena defeated them with magical weapons and captured Duryodhana and his brothers. At Yudhishthira's order, Arjuna freed them after pleading for mercy, and Chitrasena complied.

The Puranas add that he had two wives, Sandhyavali and Ratnavali, and that a spittle of his fell on the sage Galava, who cursed him. Krishna vowed to kill him, and after a clash between Krishna and Arjuna the dispute ended when Chitrasena apologized to Galava.

Legacy

In Buddhism, the Atanatiya Sutta lists him among the gandharvas a practitioner may call upon for protection from demonic forces.

Ancient sources: The Mahabharata, in its Vana Parva and Sabha Parva books, and the Puranas, including the Skanda Purana.
