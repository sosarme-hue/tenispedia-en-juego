Egyptian protective god, form of Horus

Hor-Behedety

Hor-Behedety is a form of the god Horus, known in Egyptian tradition as Horus the Elder. He was a protector of kingship, linked to the sun and to the victory over Seth.

The myth

In Egyptian tradition, Hor-Behedety is a manifestation of Horus the Elder, first documented in the Old Kingdom. From the Third Dynasty he appears connected with the enthronement of the king during the Set festival and as the king's personal protector.

His name means "He who is from Behedet". He is shown as a winged solar disc with multicoloured feathers and two uraei, or as a falcon wearing the double crown. With the epithet "the harpooner" he is remembered for defeating Seth, which made him a guardian of the monarchy. At Edfu he is the husband of Hathor, who travelled each year from Dendera to join him.

Worship and religion

His main cult centre was Edfu, where the Festival of the Good Meeting celebrated his reunion with Hathor. He was also worshipped at Damanhur, Tell Balamun, Filew, Mesen and Tanis. His image appeared on temple lintels and at the top of stelae as an apotropaic deity.

Trivia

Greek name for Edfu. The Greeks linked Hor-Behedety with Apollo, which is why the city of Edfu was called Apollinopolis Magna.
Festival of reunion. Each year Hathor travelled from Dendera to Edfu to join him during the Festival of the Good Meeting.
